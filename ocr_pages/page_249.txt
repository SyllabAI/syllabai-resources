DEHYDRATION OF COPPER(I!) SULFATE CRYSTALS

If you heat blue copper(II) sulfate crystals gently, the blue crystals turn to

a white powder and water is driven off. Heating causes the crystals to lose
their water of crystallisation and white anhydrous copper(|I) sulfate is formed.
‘Anhydrous’ simply means ‘without water’.

CuSO,-5H,O(s) + CuSO,(s) + 5H,O(!) Heat is needed for this
blue white reaction to occur

If you add water to the white solid, it turns blue again; it also becomes very
warm. See Figure 18.3 (page 192).

CuSO,(s) + 5H,O(!) + CuSO,-5H,O(s) Heat is released from this
white blue reaction

The original change has been exactly reversed. Even the heat that you put in
originally has been given out again. This is called a reversible reaction and is

HEATING AMMONIUM CHLORIDE

If you heat ammonium chloride, the white crystals disappear from the bottom
of the tube and reappear further up. Heating ammonium chloride splits it into
the colourless gases ammonia and hydrogen chloride.
