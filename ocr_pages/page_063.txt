CALCULATIONS INVOLVING ONLY MASSES

Typical calculations will give you a mass of starting material and ask you

to calculate how much product you are likely to obtain. You will also find
examples done in reverse, where you are told the mass of the product and are
asked to find out how much of the starting material you would need. In almost
all the cases you will see at International GCSE you will be given the equation
for the reaction.

A PROBLEM INVOLVING HEATING CALCIUM CARBONATE

When calcium carbonate, CaCOs, is heated, calcium oxide is formed. Imagine
you wanted to calculate the mass of calcium oxide produced by heating 25g
of calcium carbonate (A,: C = 12, O = 16, Ca = 40).

CaCO, — CaO + CO,

The M, of CaCO; is 100 and the M, of
CaO is 56.

The method we will use has three steps:

Step 1: Calculate the number of moles using the mass that you have been
given.

Step 2: Use the chemical equation to work out the number of moles of the
substance you are interested in.

Step 3: Convert the number of moles to a mass.

In this reaction we have 25g of CaCO, and the number of moles can be
calculated as:

number of moles = ——4SS_ - 25 0.25mol
mass of1mole 100

The chemical equation for this reaction is
CaCO, — CaO + CO,

We can see from the equation that 1 mole of CaCO, will decompose to
produce 1 mole of CaO. In other words, the equation is telling us that if we
start with a certain number of moles of CaCO, we will obtain the same number
of moles of CaO at the end.

Therefore, we can deduce that 0.25 mol CaCO, will decompose to produce
0.25 mol CaO. Now that we know the number of moles of CaO (M, = 56) we
can convert this to a mass:

mass = number of moles x mass of 1 mole
mass = 0.25 x 56 = 14g

Therefore, the reaction will produce 14g of calcium oxide.
