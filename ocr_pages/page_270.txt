INTRODUCTION TO ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM

Structural isomers are molecules with the same molecular formula, but
different structural formulae.

Examples will make this clear.

ISOMERS OF C;H;.

If you had some molecular models and picked out five carbon atoms and 12
hydrogen atoms, you would find it was possible to join them together in more
than one way. The different molecules formed are known as isomers. All have
the molecular formula C;H,., but different structures.

The straight chain isomer is called pentane. The branched chain isomer in
the middle, which has a four-carbon chain (‘butane’) and a methyl group on
the second carbon, is called 2-methylbutane. The final branched chain isomer,
which has a three-carbon chain (‘propane’) and two methy! groups on the
second carbon, is called 2,2-dimethylpropane. The prefix ‘di’ indicates the
presence of two identical branches in the molecule.
