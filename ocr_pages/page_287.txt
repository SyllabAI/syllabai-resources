HOMOLOGOUS SERIES

The alkanes form a homologous series. A homologous series is a series of
compounds that:

= have the same functional group

® have similar chemical properties

® show a trend (gradation) in physical properties

®@ can be described by the same general formula

® differ from the next by a -CH,- unit.

The alkanes form the simplest homologous series. The alkanes do not really

have a functional group as they just contain single C-C and C-H bonds, which
are the basis of all other organic compounds.

In the case of the alkanes, if there are n carbons, there are 2n + 2 hydrogens.
The general formula for the alkanes is C,H2,,2.

So, for example, if there are three carbons, there are (2 x 3) + 2 = 8 hydrogens.
The formula for propane is C3Hg.

If you want the formula for an alkane with 15 carbons, you could work out that
it is C,;H32 and so on.

The reason that the alkanes and other homologous series can be described by
a general formula is that each member differs from the next by a -CH,- unit.
