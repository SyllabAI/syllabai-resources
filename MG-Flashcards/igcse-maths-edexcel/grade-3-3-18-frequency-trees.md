## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In maths, frequency means [ ___ ].

**Back:** In maths, frequency means how many.

**Choices:**

- how many ✔
  - _why:_ Frequency counts how many times an event, value or result occurs in a set of data, so it is expressed as a number of occurrences.
- how likely
  - _why:_ This confuses frequency with probability, which measures how likely an outcome is. Frequency means how many times the outcome actually occurs.
- how large
  - _why:_ This describes the size of a value rather than its frequency. Frequency means how many times a particular value or outcome occurs.
- how spread
  - _why:_ This confuses frequency with the spread of data, which describes variation. Frequency means how many times an event or value occurs.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In a frequency tree, what goes in the first circle on the left?

**Back:** The total number.

**Choices:**

- The total number. ✔
  - _why:_ The first circle represents the complete group before any splits occur, so it contains the total number of people or items in the frequency tree.
- The largest group.
  - _why:_ The first circle is not determined by which subgroup is largest. It contains the total number before that total is divided into smaller groups.
- The first subgroup.
  - _why:_ A subgroup belongs after the first split, not in the initial circle. The first circle contains the total number in the complete group.
- The final frequency.
  - _why:_ Final frequencies appear at the ends of later branches. The first circle on the left contains the total number before any splitting occurs.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What do branches in a frequency tree show?

**Back:** A split into smaller groups.

**Choices:**

- A split into smaller groups. ✔
  - _why:_ Each pair of branches divides the frequency in its parent circle into smaller groups, whose frequencies must add back to the parent frequency.
- A total of all the values.
  - _why:_ Branches do not show an arithmetic total of data values. They show how the people or items are split into smaller groups.
- A ranking from largest to smallest.
  - _why:_ The position of branches does not rank the frequencies by size. Branches show a split of a parent group into smaller groups.
- A product of two smaller numbers.
  - _why:_ Branch frequencies are not multiplied to produce the parent frequency. They represent smaller groups and must be added to recover the parent total.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a frequency tree, branch-end circles must add up to the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a frequency tree, branch-end circles must add up to the circle they came from.

**Choices:**

- circle they came from ✔
  - _why:_ The branches divide the parent group without changing its total, so the frequencies in the branch-end circles must add up to the circle they came from.
- first circle on the left
  - _why:_ Only the first split adds directly to the initial total. At every later split, the branch-end circles must add up to their immediate parent circle.
- largest circle in the tree
  - _why:_ Circle size is not the rule used to check a split. The branch-end frequencies must add up to the parent circle they came from.
- number of branches shown
  - _why:_ The number of branches does not determine their total frequency. The values at the branch ends must add up to the frequency in their parent circle.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** 100 people split into 45 males and some females. How many females?

**Back:** 55 females.

**Choices:**

- 55 females. ✔
  - _why:_ The male and female frequencies must total 100, so subtracting gives $100-45=55$ females.
- 45 females.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly assumes the numbers of males and females are equal. The missing frequency is found by subtraction: $100-45=55$ females.
- 65 females.
  - _why:_ This results from an inaccurate subtraction of 45 from 100. Since the two groups total 100, there are $100-45=55$ females.
- 145 females.
  - _why:_ This adds the male frequency to the total instead of finding the missing part. Subtracting correctly gives $100-45=55$ females.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** 45 males split into 20 “Yes” and some “No”. How many “No”?

**Back:** 25.

**Choices:**

- 25. ✔
  - _why:_ The “Yes” and “No” groups must total 45 males, so the missing frequency is $45-20=25$.
- 20.
  - _why:_ This assumes the “Yes” and “No” groups are equal. Their frequencies must total 45, so the missing value is $45-20=25$.
- 35.
  - _why:_ This comes from subtracting incorrectly or using the wrong total. The two branches total 45, so $45-20=25$ answered “No”.
- 65.
  - _why:_ This adds 20 to 45 rather than finding the missing branch. The correct calculation is $45-20=25$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find a missing branch when the total and other branch are known?

**Back:** Subtract the known branch from the total.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the known branch from the total. ✔
  - _why:_ The two branch frequencies add to the parent total, so rearranging this relationship means subtracting the known branch from the total.
- Add the known branch to the total.
  - _why:_ Adding would produce a value greater than the parent group. Since the branches add to the total, subtract the known branch from the total.
- Multiply the known branch by the total.
  - _why:_ Multiplication is not used to divide a fixed frequency between two branches. Subtract the known branch from the total to find the missing frequency.
- Divide the total by the known branch.
  - _why:_ Division finds a ratio rather than the missing frequency. Because both branches add to the parent total, subtract the known branch from that total.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a probability fraction, the denominator is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a probability fraction, the denominator is the total number chosen from.

**Choices:**

- total number chosen from ✔
  - _why:_ For equally likely outcomes, probability is the number of favourable outcomes divided by the total number of possible people, items or outcomes chosen from.
- number of favourable outcomes
  - _why:_ The favourable outcomes form the numerator, not the denominator. The denominator is the total number of possible people, items or outcomes chosen from.
- number of unsuccessful outcomes
  - _why:_ The denominator includes every possible outcome, not only the unsuccessful ones. It is the total number in the group being chosen from.
- total number left unchosen
  - _why:_ Probability is based on the full group before the selection, not only what remains afterwards. The denominator is the total number chosen from.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a probability fraction, the numerator is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a probability fraction, the numerator is the number fitting the description.

**Choices:**

- number fitting the description ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator counts the outcomes that satisfy the event being described, while the denominator counts all possible outcomes in the relevant group.
- total number in the group
  - _why:_ The total number in the relevant group forms the denominator, not the numerator. The numerator is the number fitting the description because it counts favourable outcomes.
- number outside the description
  - _why:_ Counting outcomes outside the description gives the complement of the required event. The numerator must be the number fitting the description.
- number of branches in the tree
  - _why:_ The number of branches is part of the tree’s structure, not the probability fraction. The numerator is the number fitting the description.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** If “one of the girls” is chosen at random, what is the denominator?

**Back:** The total number of girls.

**Choices:**

- The total number of girls. ✔
  - _why:_ Choosing from “one of the girls” restricts the sample space to girls only, so the denominator is the total number of girls.
- The total number of students.
  - _why:_ Using all students ignores the restricted sample space. Because the choice is made from the girls, the correct denominator is the total number of girls.
- The total number of boys.
  - _why:_ Boys are excluded by the phrase “one of the girls”. The denominator must count everyone eligible for selection, which is the total number of girls.
- The total number of tennis players.
  - _why:_ Tennis preference is irrelevant unless the selection is specifically restricted to tennis players. Here, the denominator is the total number of girls.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** 150 students include 80 boys. How many girls are there?

**Back:** 70 girls.

**Choices:**

- 70 girls. ✔
  - _why:_ The girls form the remainder after removing the 80 boys from all 150 students: $150-80=70$, so there are 70 girls.
- 230 girls.
  - _why:_ Adding 150 and 80 double-counts the boys because the 150 students already include them. Subtraction gives $150-80=70$ girls.
- 80 girls.
  - _why:_ This copies the number of boys instead of finding the remaining students. The number of girls is $150-80=70$.
- 60 girls.
  - _why:_ This results from subtracting the tens incorrectly. Taking the 80 boys from the total gives $150-80=70$ girls, not 60.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** After completing a frequency tree, what should the far-right circles add up to?

**Back:** The starting total.

**Choices:**

- The starting total. ✔
  - _why:_ The far-right circles represent all final, mutually exclusive groups. Together they account for everyone originally counted, so their frequencies must equal the starting total.
- The final probability.
  - _why:_ A frequency tree’s circles contain counts rather than a single final probability. The terminal counts partition the original group and must add to the starting total.
- The number of branches.
  - _why:_ The number of branches describes the tree’s layout, not the combined frequency of its terminal groups. The far-right circles must add to the starting total.
- The largest frequency.
  - _why:_ The largest frequency represents only one subgroup, so it does not account for every outcome. Adding all far-right frequencies should reproduce the starting total.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the far-right circles do not add to the starting total, there is likely a [ ___ ].

**Back:** If the far-right circles do not add to the starting total, there is likely a subtraction error.

**Choices:**

- subtraction error ✔
  - _why:_ Missing branch frequencies are commonly found by subtracting a known subgroup from its parent total. An incorrect subtraction prevents the final groups from recombining to the starting total.
- rounding error
  - _why:_ Frequency-tree counts are whole numbers, so no rounding should be needed. A mismatch usually indicates a subtraction error when a missing branch was found from its parent total.
- unit conversion error
  - _why:_ Frequency trees use counts directly and normally require no conversion between units. A failure to recover the starting total instead points to a subtraction error.
- sampling error
  - _why:_ Sampling error concerns differences between a sample and its population, not whether recorded branch counts reconcile. A mismatch within the tree usually comes from a subtraction error.

**Back:** $\frac{45}{150}$.

  - _why:_ The numerator 70 counts all the girls rather than boys who play tennis. The relevant frequency is 45 out of the total 150, so the answer is $\frac{45}{150}$.

**Back:** 170.

**Choices:**

- 170. ✔
  - _why:_ The two handedness groups contain all 200 people. Subtracting the 30 left-handed people gives $200-30=170$ right-handed people.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** You only need to simplify a probability fraction if the question asks for its [ ___ ].

**Back:** You only need to simplify a probability fraction if the question asks for its simplest form.

**Choices:**

- simplest form ✔
  - _why:_ A probability fraction may be left unsimplified unless the question specifically requests its simplest form, meaning the numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1.
- decimal form
  - _why:_ Decimal form requires converting the fraction by division, not simplifying it by cancelling common factors. Simplification is only necessary when the question requests the fraction’s simplest form.
- standard form
  - _why:_ Standard form is used to express very large or very small numbers using powers of 10. A probability fraction needs simplifying only when its simplest form is requested.
- mixed-number form
  - _why:_ Mixed-number form applies to improper fractions and is not the usual requirement for probabilities between 0 and 1. The relevant instruction would be to give the fraction in simplest form.
