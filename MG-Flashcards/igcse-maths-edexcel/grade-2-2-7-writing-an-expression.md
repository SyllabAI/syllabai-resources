## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a variable in algebra?

**Back:** A letter that stands for an unknown or changing number.

**Choices:**

- A letter that stands for an unknown or changing number. ✔
  - _why:_ A variable is represented by a letter and can stand for an unknown value or a quantity whose value may change.
- A number that has a fixed and unchanging value.
  - _why:_ A fixed, unchanging number is a constant, not a variable. A variable is a letter representing an unknown or changing number.
- A number placed before a letter to show multiplication.
  - _why:_ The number multiplying a variable is its coefficient. The variable itself is the letter that represents an unknown or changing number.
- A symbol that shows an algebraic operation between numbers.
  - _why:_ Symbols such as $+$ and $-$ are operators, not variables. A variable is a letter standing for an unknown or changing number.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** An [ ___ ] is a combination of numbers, variables and operations, and does not need an equals sign.

**Back:** An expression is a combination of numbers, variables and operations, and does not need an equals sign.

**Choices:**

- expression ✔
  - _why:_ An expression combines numbers, variables and operations without requiring an equals sign, as in $3x+5$.
- equation
  - _why:_ An equation includes an equals sign showing that two expressions have the same value. A combination without an equals sign is an expression.
- identity
  - _why:_ An identity is an equality that holds for every permitted value of its variables. The described combination without an equals sign is an expression.
- inequality
  - _why:_ An inequality compares expressions using a sign such as $$. A combination of numbers, variables and operations alone is an expression.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the key difference between an expression and a formula?

**Back:** A formula contains an equals sign ($=$) to link variables, whereas an expression does not.

**Choices:**

- A formula contains an equals sign ($=$) to link variables, whereas an expression does not. ✔
  - _why:_ A formula states a relationship between variables using an equals sign, while an expression is only a combination of terms and operations.
- An expression contains an equals sign ($=$) to link variables, whereas a formula does not.
  - _why:_ This reverses the definitions. A formula uses an equals sign to connect variables, whereas an expression does not contain an equals sign.
- A formula contains an inequality sign ($<$) to compare variables, whereas an expression does not.
  - _why:_ An inequality sign compares quantities but does not define a formula. A formula links variables using an equals sign, while an expression has no equals sign.
- A formula contains only numbers, whereas an expression contains variables and operations.
  - _why:_ Formulae can contain numbers, variables and operations. Their distinguishing feature is an equals sign linking variables, which an expression does not have.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the multiplication $5 \times x$ correctly written in algebra?

**Back:** $$5x$

**Choices:**

- $$5x$ ✔
  - _why:_ In algebra, multiplication between a numerical coefficient and a variable is written without the multiplication sign, so $5 \times x$ becomes $5x$.
- $$5 + x$
  - _why:_ This changes multiplication into addition. Writing a number directly beside a variable indicates multiplication, so $5 \times x$ is written as $5x$.
- $$x^5$
  - _why:_ This means $x$ raised to the power of $5$, not $x$ multiplied by $5$. The required algebraic form is $5x$.
- $$\frac{x}{5}$
  - _why:_ This represents $x$ divided by $5$, which is not the given multiplication. The product of $5$ and $x$ is written as $5x$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** How is '4 fewer than $m$' written as an algebraic expression?

**Back:** $$m - 4$

**Choices:**

- $$m - 4$ ✔
  - _why:_ The phrase “4 fewer than $m$” means start with $m$ and reduce it by $4$, giving $m-4$.
- $$4 - m$
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of subtraction and means “$m$ fewer than 4”. For “4 fewer than $m$”, start with $m$ and subtract $4$.
- $$m + 4$
  - _why:_ Adding $4$ gives “4 more than $m$”, not “4 fewer than $m$”. Fewer indicates subtraction, so the expression is $m-4$.
- $$4m$
  - _why:_ Placing $4$ beside $m$ means multiplication, giving “four times $m$”. The word “fewer” requires subtraction, so the expression is $m-4$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is '4 fewer than $m$' written as $m-4$ and not $4-m$?

**Back:** Because 'fewer than' means starting with $m$ and subtracting 4 from it.

**Choices:**

- Because 'fewer than' means starting with $m$ and subtracting 4 from it. ✔
  - _why:_ The quantity following “than” is the starting value. Therefore, “4 fewer than $m$” means begin with $m$ and subtract $4$.
- Because 'fewer than' means starting with 4 and subtracting $m$ from it.
  - _why:_ This reverses the order and produces $4-m$, meaning “$m$ fewer than 4”. The correct interpretation starts with $m$ and subtracts $4$.
- Because 'fewer than' means starting with $m$ and adding 4 to it.
  - _why:_ Adding gives “4 more than $m$”, not “4 fewer than $m$”. The word “fewer” signals subtraction, producing $m-4$.
- Because subtraction is commutative, so $m-4$ and $4-m$ are equivalent.
  - _why:_ Subtraction is not commutative, so changing the order generally changes the value. “4 fewer than $m$” specifically means $m-4$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** A tutor earns $p$ pounds per hour. Write an expression for their total pay after 12 hours.

**Back:** $$12p$

**Choices:**

- $$12p$ ✔
  - _why:_ Total pay equals the hourly rate multiplied by the number of hours. At $p$ pounds per hour for 12 hours, the pay is $12 \times p = 12p$.
- $$p + 12$
  - _why:_ Adding the hours to the hourly rate mixes different quantities and does not calculate total pay. The rate must be multiplied by 12, giving $12p$.
- $$\frac{p}{12}$
  - _why:_ Dividing the hourly rate by 12 would produce a smaller rate, not the total earned. Multiply $p$ by the 12 hours to obtain $12p$.
- $$p^{12}$
  - _why:_ Raising $p$ to the power 12 means multiplying twelve copies of $p$ together. Total pay requires multiplying $p$ by 12, which gives $12p$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is 12 hours at $p$ pounds per hour written as $12p$ instead of $p+12$?

**Back:** Because 'per hour' means you must multiply the number of hours by the rate.

**Choices:**

- Because 'per hour' means you must multiply the number of hours by the rate. ✔
  - _why:_ A rate of $p$ pounds for each hour is repeated 12 times, so total pay is calculated as $12 \times p = 12p$.
- Because 'per hour' means you must add the number of hours to the rate.
  - _why:_ Adding 12 to $p$ incorrectly combines a number of hours with a rate in pounds per hour. Total pay is hours multiplied by the rate, giving $12p$.
- Because 'per hour' means you must divide the number of hours by the rate.
  - _why:_ Division is used to recover a rate or quantity from a known total, not to calculate total pay. Here, 12 hours must be multiplied by $p$.
- Because 'per hour' means you must subtract the number of hours from the rate.
  - _why:_ Subtraction would represent a decrease and mixes the number of hours with the hourly rate. Total earnings are found by multiplying 12 by $p$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A juice costs $j$ pounds and a sandwich costs $s$ pounds. Write an expression for the total cost of 4 juices and 3 sandwiches.

**Back:** $$4j + 3s$

**Choices:**

- $$4j + 3s$ ✔
  - _why:_ Four juices cost $4j$ and three sandwiches cost $3s$. Adding these separate costs gives the total expression $4j + 3s$.
- $$4j + 4s$
  - _why:_ This counts four sandwiches instead of three, so the sandwich cost is too large. Three sandwiches contribute $3s$, making the correct total $4j + 3s$.
- $$3j + 4s$
  - _why:_ The coefficients have been swapped, representing three juices and four sandwiches. The stated quantities are four juices and three sandwiches, so the total is $4j + 3s$.
- $$12js$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the quantities and prices together does not combine the costs of two different item types. Calculate each cost separately and add them to get $4j + 3s$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** To write an expression for a total containing two different types of items, you should build the expression in [ ___ ] first, then [ ___ ] them.

**Back:** To write an expression for a total containing two different types of items, you should build the expression in individual chunks first, then add them.

**Choices:**

- To write an expression for a total containing two different types of items, you should build the expression in individual chunks first, then add them. ✔
  - _why:_ Each item type has its own quantity and price, so its cost should be formed separately. Because the question asks for a total, those individual costs are then added.
- To write an expression for a total containing two different types of items, you should build the expression in separate equations first, then add them.
  - _why:_ An expression does not require separate equations, because equations contain equals signs. Build one cost term for each item type as individual chunks, then add those terms.
- To write an expression for a total containing two different types of items, you should build the expression in individual chunks first, then multiply them.
  - _why:_ Multiplying the chunks combines different item costs as factors rather than finding their total. After forming each item type's cost separately, add the individual chunks together.
- To write an expression for a total containing two different types of items, you should build the expression in matching factors first, then subtract them.
  - _why:_ Different item types do not need matching factors, and subtraction finds a difference rather than a total. Form individual cost chunks and add them together.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Concert tickets cost £4 each. If the total money collected is $y$ pounds, write an expression for the number of tickets sold.

**Back:** $$\frac{y}{4}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{y}{4}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The total money equals the number of tickets multiplied by £4. Dividing the total $y$ by the price per ticket gives $\frac{y}{4}$ tickets.
- $$4y$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the total money by £4 makes the result larger and does not recover the number sold. Divide $y$ by the £4 ticket price to get $\frac{y}{4}$.
- $$y - 4$
  - _why:_ Subtracting £4 removes the price of one ticket from the total rather than finding how many tickets were sold. The correct operation is $y \div 4$.
- $$\frac{y}{2}$
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 incorrectly treats each ticket as costing £2. Since every ticket costs £4, the number sold is the total divided by 4, or $\frac{y}{4}$.

**Back:** $$n + 6$

**Choices:**

- $$n + 6$ ✔
  - _why:_ Being 6 years older means the brother's age is 6 more than Lina's age. Adding 6 to $n$ gives $n + 6$.
- $$n - 6$
  - _why:_ Subtracting 6 would make the brother 6 years younger than Lina. Since he is older, 6 must be added to $n$, giving $n + 6$.

  - _why:_ Multiplying by 6 would mean the brother is six times Lina's age, not 6 years older. A fixed age difference is represented by $n + 6$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebra, [ ___ ] have the same variable part and can be collected together.

**Back:** In algebra, like terms have the same variable part and can be collected together.

**Choices:**

- like terms ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms have identical variable parts, including the same variables raised to the same powers. Their numerical coefficients can therefore be added or subtracted.
- unlike terms
  - _why:_ Unlike terms have different variable parts, so they cannot be collected directly. Terms sharing the same variables and powers are called like terms.
- constant terms
  - _why:_ Constant terms contain no variables, although constants can be collected with other constants. The general name for terms with the same variable part is like terms.
- algebraic factors
  - _why:_ Factors are quantities multiplied together within a term, not terms grouped by matching variable parts. Terms that can be collected together are called like terms.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify the age total expression $n + (n + 6) + (n - 3)$ by collecting like terms.

**Back:** $$3n + 3$

**Choices:**

- $$3n + 3$ ✔
  - _why:_ The three $n$ terms combine to give $3n$, while the constants combine as $6 - 3 = 3$. Therefore the simplified expression is $3n + 3$.
- $$3n + 9$
  - _why:_ This comes from treating the subtraction of 3 as addition. The constants must combine as $6 - 3 = 3$, while the variable terms give $3n$, so the answer is $3n + 3$.
- $$2n + 3$
  - _why:_ This misses one of the three $n$ terms in the original expression. All three combine to make $3n$, and the constants give $6 - 3 = 3$, so the answer is $3n + 3$.
- $$3n - 3$
  - _why:_ This keeps only the negative constant and overlooks the positive 6. The constants combine to give $6 - 3 = 3$, so after collecting the three $n$ terms the answer is $3n + 3$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Muffins cost 40p each and juice cartons cost 65p each. Write a formula for the total cost, $C$ pence, of $m$ muffins and $j$ juice cartons.

**Back:** $$C = 40m + 65j$

**Choices:**

- $$C = 40m + 65j$ ✔
  - _why:_ The muffins cost $40m$ pence altogether and the juice cartons cost $65j$ pence. Adding these costs gives the formula $C = 40m + 65j$.
- $$C = 65m + 40j$
  - _why:_ This assigns the prices to the wrong products. Each muffin costs 40p and each juice carton costs 65p, so the correct formula is $C = 40m + 65j$.
- $$C = 0.40m + 0.65j$
  - _why:_ These coefficients express the prices in pounds, but $C$ is defined in pence. Using 40 and 65 as the pence prices gives $C = 40m + 65j$.
- $$C = 105(m + j)$
  - _why:_ This adds the two different prices and charges 105p for every item. Muffins and juice cartons must be costed separately, giving $C = 40m + 65j$.
