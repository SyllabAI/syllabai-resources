# Rounding Flashcards

_Lane: `igcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 1 · 22 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** To round to a chosen place, keep that digit and look at the digit [ ___ ].

**Back:** To round to a chosen place, keep that digit and look at the digit immediately to its right.

**Choices:**

- immediately to its right ✔
  - _why:_ The digit immediately to the right is the deciding digit. Its value determines whether the chosen place-value digit stays the same or increases by 1.
- immediately to its left
  - _why:_ Looking left confuses the deciding digit with a higher place value. The deciding digit is immediately to the right of the digit being kept.
- two places to its right
  - _why:_ This skips over the actual deciding digit and can give the wrong result. You must inspect the digit immediately to the right of the chosen place.
- furthest to its right
  - _why:_ The final digit is not necessarily relevant to the chosen place value. Rounding is determined by the digit immediately to the right of the kept digit.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** If the deciding digit is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, what do you do?

**Back:** Round up: increase the kept digit by 1.

**Choices:**

- Round up: increase the kept digit by 1. ✔
  - _why:_ A deciding digit from 5 to 9 means the number is at or beyond the halfway point, so the kept digit must increase by 1.
- Round down: keep the kept digit the same.
  - _why:_ Keeping the digit unchanged applies when the deciding digit is from 0 to 4. For 5 to 9, round up by increasing the kept digit by 1.
- Round up: increase the deciding digit by 1.
  - _why:_ The deciding digit is discarded after it has been checked, so increasing it has no effect. Rounding up means increasing the kept digit by 1.
- Round down: decrease the kept digit by 1.
  - _why:_ Rounding down never means subtracting 1 from the kept digit. With a deciding digit from 5 to 9, increase the kept digit by 1.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** If the deciding digit is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, what do you do?

**Back:** Round down: keep the kept digit the same.

**Choices:**

- Round down: keep the kept digit the same. ✔
  - _why:_ A deciding digit from 0 to 4 lies below the halfway point. The kept digit therefore remains unchanged, while the digits after it are removed or replaced.
- Round up: increase the kept digit by 1.
  - _why:_ Increasing the kept digit is required only when the deciding digit is from 5 to 9. For 0 to 4, keep the kept digit unchanged.
- Round down: decrease the kept digit by 1.
  - _why:_ The phrase round down can misleadingly suggest subtraction, but the kept digit is not decreased. With a deciding digit from 0 to 4, it stays unchanged.
- Round down: replace the kept digit with 0.
  - _why:_ Zeros replace digits to the right when rounding whole numbers, not the kept digit itself. For a deciding digit from 0 to 4, the kept digit remains unchanged.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** When rounding a whole number, what replaces digits to the right?

**Back:** Zeros replace all digits to the right.

**Choices:**

- Zeros replace all digits to the right. ✔
  - _why:_ Zeros preserve the whole number’s place value after rounding. For example, rounding to the nearest hundred requires the tens and units digits to become zero.
- Nines replace all digits to the right.
  - _why:_ Using nines would increase the rounded number and would not preserve the intended place value. All whole-number digits to the right are replaced by zeros.
- Fives replace all digits to the right.
  - _why:_ The deciding digit may be compared with 5, but it is not used as a replacement. Digits to the right are replaced by zeros.
- Nothing replaces all digits to the right.
  - _why:_ Simply deleting the digits would change the number’s place value. When rounding a whole number, zeros must replace every digit to the right of the kept digit.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Round $468$ to the nearest hundred.

**Back:** $500$

**Choices:**

- $500$ ✔
  - _why:_ The hundreds digit is 4 and the deciding tens digit is 6. Since 6 is at least 5, the hundreds digit increases to give $500$.
- $400$
  - _why:_ This treats the tens digit 6 as though it were below 5. Since 6 requires rounding up, $468$ rounds to $500$ to the nearest hundred.
- $470$
  - _why:_ This is the result of rounding to the nearest ten rather than the nearest hundred. Using the tens digit 6, the correct hundred is $500$.
- $460$
  - _why:_ This truncates the number at the tens place instead of rounding to the requested place. To the nearest hundred, the deciding digit 6 gives $500$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** The halfway point between 300 and 400 is [ ___ ], so 376 rounds to [ ___ ] to the nearest hundred.

**Back:** The halfway point between 300 and 400 is 350, so 376 rounds to 400 to the nearest hundred.

**Choices:**

- The halfway point between 300 and 400 is 350, so 376 rounds to 400 to the nearest hundred. ✔
  - _why:_ The midpoint is $350$. Since $376$ is above this halfway point and closer to $400$ than to $300$, it rounds to $400$.
- The halfway point between 300 and 400 is 350, so 376 rounds to 300 to the nearest hundred.
  - _why:_ Although the halfway point is correctly identified as $350$, the rounding direction is reversed. Since $376$ is above $350$, it rounds up to $400$.
- The halfway point between 300 and 400 is 400, so 376 rounds to 400 to the nearest hundred.
  - _why:_ The rounding result is right, but $400$ is an endpoint rather than the midpoint. Halfway between $300$ and $400$ is $350$, and $376$ rounds to $400$.
- The halfway point between 300 and 400 is 300, so 376 rounds to 300 to the nearest hundred.
  - _why:_ This mistakes an endpoint for the halfway point and then rounds in the wrong direction. The midpoint is $350$, and because $376$ exceeds it, the answer is $400$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** In $3.847$, which digit is the 2nd decimal place?

**Back:** 4

**Choices:**

- 4 ✔
  - _why:_ The first digit after the decimal point is 8, so the second digit is 4. Therefore, 4 occupies the hundredths place.
- 8
  - _why:_ The digit 8 is the first digit after the decimal point, so it is in the tenths place. The second decimal place is occupied by 4.
- 7
  - _why:_ The digit 7 is the third digit after the decimal point, which is the thousandths place. The second decimal place contains 4.
- 3
  - _why:_ The digit 3 is before the decimal point, so it is in the units place. Counting after the decimal point, the second digit is 4.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The 1st decimal place is [ ___ ]; the 2nd decimal place is [ ___ ].

**Back:** The 1st decimal place is tenths; the 2nd decimal place is hundredths.

**Choices:**

- The 1st decimal place is tenths; the 2nd decimal place is hundredths. ✔
  - _why:_ Decimal place values decrease by powers of ten: the first digit after the point represents tenths, and the second represents hundredths.
- The 1st decimal place is hundredths; the 2nd decimal place is tenths.
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the first two decimal places. Moving right from the decimal point gives tenths first and then hundredths.
- The 1st decimal place is units; the 2nd decimal place is hundredths.
  - _why:_ Units are immediately before the decimal point, not after it. The first decimal place is tenths, while the second decimal place is hundredths.
- The 1st decimal place is tenths; the 2nd decimal place is thousandths.
  - _why:_ Thousandths are the third decimal place, not the second. The sequence after the decimal point is tenths, hundredths, then thousandths.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What must an answer correct to 3 decimal places show?

**Back:** Exactly 3 digits after the decimal point.

**Choices:**

- Exactly 3 digits after the decimal point. ✔
  - _why:_ Decimal places count the number of digits written after the decimal point. An answer correct to 3 decimal places must therefore display exactly three such digits.
- Exactly 2 digits after the decimal point.
  - _why:_ Showing two digits would give an answer to 2 decimal places. An answer to 3 decimal places must display exactly three digits after the decimal point.
- Exactly 4 digits after the decimal point.
  - _why:_ Showing four digits would give an answer to 4 decimal places. For 3 decimal places, exactly three digits must appear after the decimal point.
- At most 3 digits after the decimal point.
  - _why:_ The phrase “at most” wrongly allows fewer than three digits. A stated accuracy of 3 decimal places requires exactly three digits, including any necessary trailing zeros.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Why must $5.410$ keep the final zero for 3 decimal places?

**Back:** It shows exactly 3 digits after the decimal point.

**Choices:**

- It shows exactly 3 digits after the decimal point. ✔
  - _why:_ A trailing zero does not change the numerical value, but it records the required accuracy. Writing $5.410$ makes clear that the result is given to 3 decimal places.
- It shows exactly 3 significant figures in the number.
  - _why:_ The number $5.410$ actually has four significant figures because its final zero follows non-zero digits. The zero is needed to show exactly three decimal places.
- It shows exactly 3 digits before the decimal point.
  - _why:_ There is only one digit before the decimal point in $5.410$. The retained zero instead ensures that exactly three digits appear after the decimal point.
- It makes $5.410$ greater than $5.41$.
  - _why:_ Trailing zeros after a decimal do not change a number’s value, so $5.410$ and $5.41$ are equal. The zero communicates accuracy to 3 decimal places.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** Significant figures are counted from the first [ ___ ] digit; zeros before it are [ ___ ].

**Back:** Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit; zeros before it are not significant.

**Choices:**

- Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit; zeros before it are not significant. ✔
  - _why:_ Leading zeros only locate the decimal point and do not indicate measured precision. Counting significant figures begins with the first digit from 1 to 9.
- Significant figures are counted from the first zero digit; zeros before it are significant.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly counts leading zeros as measured digits. Significant figures begin at the first non-zero digit, and any zeros before it are not significant.
- Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit; zeros before it are significant.
  - _why:_ Although counting starts at the first non-zero digit, the statement then wrongly includes earlier zeros. Those leading zeros only set place value and are not significant.
- Significant figures are counted from the first decimal digit; zeros before it are not significant.
  - _why:_ Significant figures do not necessarily begin after the decimal point; whole-number digits may be significant too. Counting starts at the first non-zero digit.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Why does the 0 in $90437$ count as a significant figure?

**Back:** It comes after the first non-zero digit.

**Choices:**

- It comes after the first non-zero digit. ✔
  - _why:_ Once the first non-zero digit has appeared, every following digit is significant, including zeros. Since 0 follows 9, it counts as significant.
- It comes before the first non-zero digit.
  - _why:_ Zeros before the first non-zero digit are leading zeros and do not count as significant. Here, the 0 follows 9, so it is significant.
- It is the final digit in the number.
  - _why:_ The 0 is not the final digit, and being final would not automatically make a whole-number zero significant. It counts because it follows the first non-zero digit.
- It appears to the right of a decimal point.
  - _why:_ There is no decimal point in $90437$, so this cannot explain the zero’s significance. The zero counts because it occurs after the first non-zero digit.

## Card 22 — `cloze`

**Front:** The phrase “correct to” means [ ___ ] that level of [ ___ ].

**Back:** The phrase “correct to” means round to that level of accuracy.

**Choices:**

- The phrase “correct to” means round to that level of accuracy. ✔
  - _why:_ In rounding questions, “correct to” instructs you to round a value to the stated degree of accuracy, such as a given number of decimal places or significant figures.
- The phrase “correct to” means round up that level of accuracy.
  - _why:_ “Correct to” does not mean always increasing the value; rounding may increase or decrease it. It means to round to the specified level of accuracy.
- The phrase “correct to” means round to that level of error.
  - _why:_ The stated level describes the accuracy required, not the amount of error to introduce. “Correct to” means to round to that level of accuracy.
- The phrase “correct to” means truncate to that level of uncertainty.
  - _why:_ Truncation simply removes later digits and is not the same as rounding, while uncertainty describes a range rather than the requested accuracy. The phrase means to round to that level of accuracy.
