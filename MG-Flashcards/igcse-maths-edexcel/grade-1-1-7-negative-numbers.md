## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A negative number is [ ___ ]; a positive number is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A negative number is less than zero; a positive number is greater than zero.

**Choices:**

- A negative number is less than zero; a positive number is greater than zero. ✔
  - _why:_ Negative numbers lie below zero, while positive numbers lie above zero. The comparison with zero is the defining feature that separates the two types of number.
- A negative number is greater than zero; a positive number is less than zero.
  - _why:_ The two definitions have been reversed. Numbers below zero are negative, and numbers above zero are positive, so the correct completion uses those relationships in the opposite order.
- A negative number is less than one; a positive number is greater than one.
  - _why:_ The reference point is zero, not one. Some positive numbers are between zero and one, so they are still positive even though they are not greater than one.
- A negative number is greater than zero; a positive number is greater than zero.
  - _why:_ A negative number cannot be above zero. Numbers greater than zero are positive, whereas negative numbers must be less than zero.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a number line, values get [ ___ ] as you move left and [ ___ ] as you move right.

**Back:** On a number line, values get smaller as you move left and bigger as you move right.

**Choices:**

- On a number line, values get smaller as you move left and bigger as you move right. ✔
  - _why:_ Moving left on a number line decreases the value, while moving right increases it. For example, -5 is smaller than -2 because it is further left.
- On a number line, values get bigger as you move left and smaller as you move right.
  - _why:_ The directions have been reversed. Values increase as you move right and decrease as you move left, so the correct order is smaller, then bigger.
- On a number line, values get smaller as you move left and smaller as you move right.
  - _why:_ Values cannot decrease in both directions. Moving right means increasing values, while moving left means decreasing values.
- On a number line, values get bigger as you move left and bigger as you move right.
  - _why:_ Values cannot increase in both directions from every point. Only movement to the right makes values bigger; movement to the left makes them smaller.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ On a number line, the number further to the left is smaller. Since -8 is to the left of -3, -8 is the smaller number.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** Adding a negative number means moving [ ___ ] on a number line.

**Back:** Adding a negative number means moving left on a number line.

**Choices:**

- left ✔
  - _why:_ Adding a negative moves to a lower value. Lower values are found to the left on a number line, so adding a negative number means moving left.
- right
  - _why:_ Moving right increases the value, which corresponds to adding a positive number. Adding a negative decreases the value, so the movement is to the left.
- upwards
  - _why:_ A standard number line shows values increasing and decreasing horizontally, not vertically. Adding a negative number means moving left to a smaller value.
- towards zero
  - _why:_ Adding a negative always moves left, but that does not always mean moving towards zero. From a negative starting value, it moves further away from zero.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $-6 + 8$.

**Back:** $2$

**Choices:**

- $2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Starting at -6 and adding 8 moves eight places right, landing on 2. Equivalently, 8 - 6 leaves a positive difference of 2.
- $-14$
  - _why:_ This comes from adding the magnitudes while keeping a negative sign, as though both terms were negative. The positive 8 moves right, giving -6 + 8 = 2.
- $14$
  - _why:_ This ignores the negative sign on the first number and adds the magnitudes. Since the signs differ, subtract the magnitudes and keep the sign of the larger magnitude, giving 2.
- $-2$
  - _why:_ The magnitude difference is correct, but the sign is reversed. Because 8 has the larger magnitude and is positive, the result is positive 2, not negative 2.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $-5 + (-7)$.

**Back:** $-12$

**Choices:**

- $-12$ ✔
  - _why:_ Both numbers are negative, so add their magnitudes, 5 + 7 = 12, and keep the negative sign. Therefore the result is -12.
- $-2$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the magnitudes is appropriate when adding numbers with different signs, not when both numbers are negative. Here 5 + 7 = 12, so the result is -12.
- $12$
  - _why:_ The magnitudes have been added correctly, but the common negative sign was lost. Adding two negative numbers gives a negative result, so the answer is -12.
- $2$
  - _why:_ This combines a subtraction of magnitudes with a positive sign, neither of which applies here. Two negative addends combine to give a total of -12.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Subtracting a negative number is the same as [ ___ ] of that number.

**Back:** Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the positive version of that number.

**Choices:**

- adding the positive version ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting a negative reverses the change: removing a negative is equivalent to adding its positive magnitude. For example, 5 - (-3) becomes 5 + 3.
- subtracting the positive version
  - _why:_ This keeps subtraction instead of reversing it. Subtracting a negative is equivalent to adding the positive version, so 5 - (-3) becomes 5 + 3.
- adding the negative version
  - _why:_ Adding the negative version would make the value smaller rather than cancelling the subtraction. The two negative signs reverse the operation, producing addition of the positive version.
- taking the absolute value
  - _why:_ The absolute value removes a number's sign but does not describe the required operation. Subtracting a negative changes into adding the positive version of that number.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For addition or subtraction, which operation changes if the order is swapped?

**Back:** Subtraction

**Choices:**

- Subtraction ✔
  - _why:_ Subtraction is not commutative: swapping the numbers generally changes the result because $a-b$ is not generally equal to $b-a$.
- Addition
  - _why:_ Addition is commutative, so swapping its numbers does not change the result. Subtraction is the operation whose result generally changes when the order is swapped.
- Both operations
  - _why:_ Addition remains unchanged when its terms are swapped because it is commutative. Only subtraction generally changes, since reversing the order reverses the difference’s sign.
- Neither operation
  - _why:_ Although addition is unaffected by swapping its terms, subtraction generally is affected. For example, $5-2=3$ but $2-5=-3$, so the answer is subtraction.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** For multiplication or division, the same signs give a [ ___ ] answer.

**Back:** For multiplication or division, the same signs give a positive answer.

**Choices:**

- positive ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying or dividing two numbers with matching signs gives a positive result, whether both numbers are positive or both are negative.
- negative
  - _why:_ A negative result comes from different signs, not matching signs. Two negative signs cancel in multiplication or division, so numbers with the same sign give a positive answer.
- zero
  - _why:_ Matching signs determine that the result is positive; they do not make it zero. A product is zero only when at least one multiplied factor is zero.
- fractional
  - _why:_ The sign rule determines whether the answer is positive or negative, not whether it is fractional. Multiplication or division of numbers with the same sign gives a positive answer.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** For multiplication or division, different signs give a [ ___ ] answer.

**Back:** For multiplication or division, different signs give a negative answer.

**Choices:**

- negative ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying or dividing two numbers with different signs gives a negative result: one negative sign remains unpaired, making the answer negative.
- positive
  - _why:_ A positive result comes from matching signs, such as two negatives or two positives. With different signs, one negative remains, so the answer is negative.
- zero
  - _why:_ Different signs make the result negative rather than zero. A product equals zero only when a factor is zero, regardless of the signs involved.
- undefined
  - _why:_ Different signs do not make multiplication or division undefined. Except for division by zero, the operation is defined, and differing signs produce a negative answer.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** Division undoes [ ___ ]; multiplication undoes [ ___ ].

**Back:** Division undoes multiplication; multiplication undoes division.

**Choices:**

- Division undoes multiplication; multiplication undoes division. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplication and division are inverse operations. Dividing reverses multiplication by the same number, while multiplying reverses division by that number.
- Division undoes division; multiplication undoes multiplication.
  - _why:_ An operation does not generally undo itself. Division undoes multiplication, and multiplication undoes division, because each operation reverses the effect of the other.
- Division undoes addition; multiplication undoes division.
  - _why:_ This confuses the two inverse-operation pairs. Subtraction undoes addition, whereas division undoes multiplication and multiplication undoes division.
- Division undoes multiplication; multiplication undoes subtraction.
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not reverse subtraction; addition does. Division and multiplication form an inverse pair, so each operation undoes the other.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the temperature difference between $-7$ °C and $4$ °C?

**Back:** $11$ °C

**Choices:**

- $11$ °C ✔
  - _why:_ Temperature difference is the distance between the values. Moving from $-7$ °C to $0$ °C is $7$ degrees, then to $4$ °C is another $4$, totalling $11$ °C.
- $-3$ °C
  - _why:_ This adds the signed temperatures, $-7+4$, rather than finding their separation. The distance from $-7$ °C to $4$ °C is $4-(-7)=11$ °C.
- $3$ °C
  - _why:_ This subtracts the magnitudes, $7-4$, but the temperatures lie on opposite sides of zero. Their distances from zero must be added, giving $7+4=11$ °C.
- $-11$ °C
  - _why:_ This calculates a signed change in the wrong direction. A temperature difference is a non-negative distance, so the separation between $-7$ °C and $4$ °C is $11$ °C.
