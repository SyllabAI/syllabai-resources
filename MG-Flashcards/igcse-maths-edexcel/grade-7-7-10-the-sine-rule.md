## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What essential feature is required to use the sine rule in any triangle?

**Back:** A complete opposite pair (a side and its opposite angle).

**Choices:**

- A complete opposite pair (a side and its opposite angle). ✔
  - _why:_ The sine rule links each side with the angle directly opposite it, so at least one complete opposite pair must be known to set up the proportion.
- A complete adjacent pair (a side and an angle beside it).
  - _why:_ The sine rule does not use a side and an adjacent angle; it requires an opposite pair. The side must be matched with the angle directly across from it.
- Two equal sides and the angle between them.
  - _why:_ Two equal sides and the included angle describe an isosceles-triangle situation, but they are not required for the sine rule. One known opposite side-angle pair is essential.
- A complete right-angle pair (the hypotenuse and a right angle).
  - _why:_ The sine rule applies to any triangle and does not require a right angle. What is essential is a side paired with the angle directly opposite it.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you identify an opposite side for an angle in a triangle?

**Back:** It is the side directly across from the angle (it does not touch the angle).

**Choices:**

- It is the side directly across from the angle (it does not touch the angle). ✔
  - _why:_ An opposite side lies across the triangle from the angle and shares neither endpoint with it. This is the side that must be paired with that angle in the sine rule.
- It is the side directly beside the angle (it touches the angle).
  - _why:_ A side touching the angle is adjacent, not opposite. The opposite side is directly across the triangle and does not touch the angle.
- It is the side directly across from the angle (it touches the angle).
  - _why:_ The side across from an angle cannot touch that angle; touching it would make the side adjacent. The correct description says it does not touch the angle.
- It is the side longest in the triangle (it does not touch the angle).
  - _why:_ The longest side is only opposite the largest angle, so it is not automatically opposite every chosen angle. Opposite means directly across from the specific angle.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** In triangle ABC, the sum of internal angles is $A + B + C = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** In triangle ABC, the sum of internal angles is $A + B + C = \boxed{180^\circ}$.

**Choices:**

- $$180^\circ$ ✔
  - _why:_ The three internal angles of every triangle add to $180^\circ$, so $A + B + C = 180^\circ$.
- $$90^\circ$
  - _why:_ $$90^\circ$ is the angle sum associated with a straight angle split incorrectly here, not a triangle. The internal angles of a triangle always total $180^\circ$.
- $$360^\circ$
  - _why:_ $$360^\circ$ is a full turn, not the sum of a triangle’s internal angles. The correct total is $180^\circ$.
- $$270^\circ$
  - _why:_ $$270^\circ$ may result from confusing a triangle with three right angles, but triangle angles total $180^\circ$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Write the version of the sine rule formula best suited for finding a missing side.

**Back:** $$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ For finding a missing side, pair each side with the sine of its opposite angle. Equating these ratios lets you solve for the unknown side.
- $$$\frac{a}{\cos A} = \frac{b}{\cos B} = \frac{c}{\cos C}$$
  - _why:_ The sine rule uses sines, not cosines. Replacing sine with cosine gives a different and generally invalid relationship, so it will produce the wrong missing side.
- $$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin C} = \frac{c}{\sin B}$$
  - _why:_ The side-angle pairings have been swapped: $b$ must pair with $B$, and $c$ must pair with $C$. Matching each side to its opposite angle gives the correct formula.
- $$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin B}$$
  - _why:_ Both $b$ and $c$ cannot be paired with $B$ in the general sine rule. Each side needs its own opposite angle, so $c$ must be paired with $C$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Write the version of the sine rule formula best suited for finding a missing angle.

**Back:** $$$\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ This is the reciprocal form of the sine rule, arranged with sine values over their corresponding opposite sides. It is convenient when solving for a missing angle.
- $$$\frac{\cos A}{a} = \frac{\cos B}{b} = \frac{\cos C}{c}$$
  - _why:_ The sine rule requires sine values, not cosine values. Using cosine here changes the relationship and gives an incorrect result for the missing angle.
- $$$\frac{\sin A}{b} = \frac{\sin B}{a} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$
  - _why:_ The first two sine values have been matched with the wrong sides. Each angle must be paired with its opposite side: $A$ with $a$ and $B$ with $b$.
- $$$\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin B}{c}$$
  - _why:_ The final term repeats $B$ instead of using angle $C$. The correct pairing is $C$ with side $c$, because they are opposite each other.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What calculator mode must be used for sine rule calculations in IGCSE Maths?

**Back:** Degrees mode.

**Choices:**

- Degrees mode. ✔
  - _why:_ IGCSE triangle angles are normally given in degrees, so the calculator must be in degree mode for sine and inverse-sine calculations to produce the expected answers.
- Radians mode.
  - _why:_ Radians are a different angle measure and would interpret a degree input incorrectly. For standard IGCSE sine rule questions, use Degrees mode.
- Grads mode.
  - _why:_ Grads are another angle unit and are not the unit normally used in IGCSE triangle questions. Degree mode is required when the angles are given in degrees.
- Turns mode.
  - _why:_ Turns measure complete rotations rather than ordinary triangle angles. Entering degree values in turns mode gives incorrect sine values; the correct setting is Degrees mode.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the function of the inverse sine ($\sin^{-1}$) button?

**Back:** It finds the angle when the sine value is already known.

**Choices:**

- It finds the angle when the sine value is already known. ✔
  - _why:_ The inverse sine button reverses the sine operation: given a sine value, it returns the corresponding angle, provided the calculator is using the correct angle mode.
- It finds the side when the angle value is already known.
  - _why:_ Inverse sine does not calculate a side length. It finds an angle from a known sine value; side lengths are found by rearranging the sine rule.
- It finds the sine value when the angle is already known.
  - _why:_ The ordinary sine button finds a sine value from an angle. The inverse sine button works in the opposite direction, finding the angle from the sine value.
- It finds the angle when the cosine value is already known.
  - _why:_ A cosine value must be processed with inverse cosine, not inverse sine. Inverse sine specifically finds an angle from a known sine value.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you avoid rounding the sine value halfway through a calculation?

**Back:** To prevent rounding errors in the final answer; keep the full value until the end.

**Choices:**

- To prevent rounding errors in the final answer; keep the full value until the end. ✔
  - _why:_ Rounding an intermediate sine value changes the number used in later calculations. Keeping full calculator precision reduces accumulated error and gives a more accurate final answer.
- To prevent angle errors in the calculator mode; keep the full value until the end.
  - _why:_ Rounding does not change the calculator’s angle mode. It changes the numerical sine value used later, causing rounding errors in the final answer; keep full precision instead.
- To prevent unit errors in the side length; keep the full value until the end.
  - _why:_ Units are controlled by the measurements and formula, not by rounding the sine value. Early rounding can cause numerical rounding errors, so retain the full value.
- To prevent inverse errors in the angle; keep the full value until the end.
  - _why:_ The inverse sine button is not made more or less valid by delaying rounding. The real issue is that rounding an intermediate value introduces error into the final calculation.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** If you know two angles and one side, but the side is not opposite either angle, what is the first step?

**Back:** Calculate the third angle using $180^\circ - (\text{sum of known angles})$.

**Choices:**

- Calculate the third angle using $180^\circ - (\text{sum of known angles})$. ✔
  - _why:_ The third angle creates an opposite angle-side pair with the known side, allowing the sine rule to be applied correctly. Triangle angles always sum to $180^2$39.
- Calculate the third angle using $360^2 - (\text{sum of known angles})$.
  - _why:_ Using $360^2$ confuses the angle sum of a triangle with the angle sum around a point. The third angle is found using $180^2 - (\text{sum of known angles})$.
- Calculate the missing side using Pythagoras’ theorem.
  - _why:_ Pythagoras applies only to right-angled triangles and cannot generally find this missing side. First calculate the third angle so the known side has a known opposite angle.
- Calculate the missing side using the cosine rule.
  - _why:_ The cosine rule is unnecessary when two angles and one side are known. First calculate the third angle, creating the opposite angle-side pair needed for the sine rule.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the area of a non-right-angled triangle is $\text{Area} = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The formula for the area of a non-right-angled triangle is $\text{Area} = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ ✔
  - _why:_ Two sides and their included angle determine the area: multiplying $a$ and $b$ by $\sin C$ and halving gives the perpendicular-height contribution.
- $$\frac{1}{2}ab\cos C$
  - _why:_ Using cosine confuses the area formula with relationships appearing in the cosine rule. The triangle’s area is $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, where $C$ is the included angle.
- $$ab\sin C$
  - _why:_ Omitting the factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ doubles the calculated area. The correct formula is $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ because a triangle is half the corresponding parallelogram.
- $$\frac{ab}{2\sin C}$
  - _why:_ Dividing by $\sin C$ reverses its role in the perpendicular height. The correct area formula multiplies by the sine: $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** In the area formula $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, what is the relationship between the angle $C$ and sides $a$ and $b$?

**Back:** $$C$ must be the included angle (the angle between sides $a$ and $b$).

**Choices:**

- $$C$ must be the included angle (the angle between sides $a$ and $b$). ✔
  - _why:_ The height relative to one side is obtained from the other side using $\sin C$, so $C$ must be the angle directly between sides $a$ and $b$.
- $$C$ must be an opposite angle (the angle opposite either side $a$ or $b$).
  - _why:_ Choosing an angle opposite either given side confuses the area formula with sine-rule pairings. In $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, $C$ must lie between sides $a$ and $b$.
- $$C$ must be the exterior angle (the angle outside sides $a$ and $b$).
  - _why:_ The area formula uses an interior included angle, not an exterior angle. Specifically, $C$ must be the interior angle between sides $a$ and $b$.
- $$C$ must be the right angle (a $90^2$ angle between sides $a$ and $b$).
  - _why:_ The triangle need not be right-angled, so assuming $C=90^2$ is an unjustified restriction. $C$ is simply the included angle between sides $a$ and $b$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the trigonometric identity linking the sine of an acute angle and its obtuse supplement?

**Back:** $$$\sin \theta = \sin(180^\circ - \theta)$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\sin \theta = \sin(180^\circ - \theta)$$ ✔
  - _why:_ Supplementary angles have equal sine values because they have the same vertical component on the unit circle. Therefore an acute angle and its obtuse supplement share the same sine.
- $$$\sin \theta = -\sin(180^2 - \theta)$$
  - _why:_ This sign change is a common unit-circle error. Sine is positive throughout the first and second quadrants, so $\sin(180^2-\theta)=\sin\theta$, not its negative.
- $$$\sin \theta = \cos(180^2 - \theta)$$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly substitutes cosine for sine. Supplementary angles have equal sine values, giving $\sin\theta=\sin(180^2-\theta)$; their cosine values instead have opposite signs.
- $$$\sin \theta = \sin(90^2 - \theta)$$
  - _why:_ This confuses supplementary-angle and complementary-angle identities. The complementary identity is $\sin\theta=\cos(90^2-\theta)$, while the obtuse supplement gives $\sin\theta=\sin(180^2-\theta)$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Define an obtuse angle.

**Back:** An angle greater than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$.

**Choices:**

- An angle greater than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$. ✔
  - _why:_ An obtuse angle lies strictly between a right angle and a straight angle. Therefore it is greater than $90^2$ but less than $180^2$.
- An angle greater than $0^2$ and less than $90^2$.
  - _why:_ Angles strictly between $0^2$ and $90^2$ are acute, not obtuse. An obtuse angle must instead be greater than $90^2$ and less than $180^2$.
- An angle greater than $180^2$ and less than $360^2$.
  - _why:_ Angles strictly between $180^2$ and $360^2$ are reflex angles. An obtuse angle lies between $90^2$ and $180^2$ instead.
- An angle greater than $90^2$ and at most $180^2$.
  - _why:_ Including $180^2$ is a boundary error because $180^2$ is a straight angle, not an obtuse angle. The upper boundary must be strictly less than $180^2$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate the obtuse version of an angle if your calculator gives an acute result?

**Back:** Subtract the acute angle from $180^\circ$.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the acute angle from $180^\circ$. ✔
  - _why:_ An acute angle and its obtuse supplement have the same sine. Since supplementary angles sum to $180^2$, subtracting the acute result gives the required obtuse angle.
- Subtract the acute angle from $90^2$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting from $90^2$ finds a complementary angle, not the obtuse solution with the same sine. The required supplement is found by subtracting from $180^2$.
- Add the acute angle to $180^2$.
  - _why:_ Adding the acute angle to $180^2$ produces a reflex angle greater than $180^2$. The corresponding obtuse solution is $180^2$ minus the acute angle.
- Subtract the acute angle from $360^2$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting from $360^2$ gives a reflex angle, reflecting full-turn reasoning rather than supplementary angles. The obtuse solution is found by subtracting from $180^2$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate the perimeter of a triangle after using the sine rule to find missing sides?

**Back:** Add all three side lengths together.

**Choices:**

- Add all three side lengths together. ✔
  - _why:_ The perimeter is the total distance around the triangle, so it is found by adding the lengths of all three sides after any missing lengths are calculated.
- Multiply all three side lengths together.
  - _why:_ Multiplying side lengths does not measure the boundary and produces inappropriate squared or cubed units. Perimeter is found by adding all three side lengths.
- Add the two longest side lengths together.
  - _why:_ Adding only two sides omits one part of the triangle’s boundary. The perimeter requires the sum of all three side lengths, including the newly calculated side.
- Average all three side lengths together.
  - _why:_ Dividing the total by three calculates the mean side length rather than the perimeter. The perimeter is simply the sum of all three side lengths.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** When finding a side length using the sine rule, it is easiest to put the [ ___ ] on top of the fractions.

**Back:** When finding a side length using the sine rule, it is easiest to put the side lengths on top of the fractions.

**Choices:**

- side lengths ✔
  - _why:_ Placing side lengths on top gives $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$, making an unknown side the numerator and easier to isolate.
- angles
  - _why:_ Putting angles themselves on top is invalid because the sine rule uses their sine values, not the angles directly. For finding a side, place the side lengths on top.
- sines of the angles
  - _why:_ Putting the sines on top is a valid rearrangement, but it is less direct for finding a side because the unknown side appears in a denominator. Side lengths should go on top.
- opposite angles
  - _why:_ The sine rule pairs each side with its opposite angle, but opposite angles are not placed as raw quantities in the numerators. Put the side lengths on top.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** If side $x$ is opposite $40^\circ$ and side $10$ is opposite $80^\circ$, set up the sine rule to find $x$.

**Back:** $$$\frac{x}{\sin 40^\circ} = \frac{10}{\sin 80^\circ}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{x}{\sin 40^\circ} = \frac{10}{\sin 80^\circ}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ Each side must be paired with the sine of its opposite angle. Thus $x$ pairs with $\sin 40^\circ$, while $10$ pairs with $\sin 80^\circ$.
- $$$\frac{x}{\sin 80^\circ} = \frac{10}{\sin 40^\circ}$$
  - _why:_ This swaps the angles associated with the sides. The correct pairings are $x$ with $40^\circ$ and $10$ with $80^\circ$, giving $\frac{x}{\sin 40^\circ}=\frac{10}{\sin 80^\circ}$.
- $$$\frac{\sin 40^\circ}{x} = \frac{10}{\sin 80^\circ}$$
  - _why:_ This mixes sine-over-side with side-over-sine, so the ratios are inconsistent. Both fractions must use the same orientation: $\frac{x}{\sin 40^\circ}=\frac{10}{\sin 80^\circ}$.
- $$$\frac{x}{10} = \frac{\sin 80^\circ}{\sin 40^\circ}$$
  - _why:_ The sine ratio has been reversed. Dividing the correct sine-rule equation by $10$ gives $\frac{x}{10}=\frac{\sin 40^\circ}{\sin 80^\circ}$, not the opposite ratio.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If $\sin x = 0.5$ and $x$ is known to be obtuse, what is the value of $x$?

**Back:** $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$$180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$

**Choices:**

- $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$$180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$ ✔
  - _why:_ The principal inverse sine gives $30^\circ$. Sine is also positive in the second quadrant, so the required obtuse solution is its supplement, $180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ$.
- $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$$180^\circ + 30^\circ = 210^\circ$
  - _why:_ Adding the acute angle to $180^\circ$ produces a reflex angle whose sine is negative. The obtuse solution is the supplement, $180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ$.
- $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 60^\circ$$180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$
  - _why:_ Confusing the inverse sine with the inverse cosine gives the incorrect principal angle of $60^\circ$. Since $\sin^{-1}(0.5)=30^\circ$, the obtuse solution is $150^\circ$.
- $\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$$90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$
  - _why:_ The second sine solution is found using supplementary angles, not by adding to $90^\circ$. The correct calculation is $180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ$.
