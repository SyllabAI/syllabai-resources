# Circle Theorems Flashcards

_Lane: `igcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 6 · 23 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A line from the centre to the circumference is a [ ___ ].

**Back:** A line from the centre to the circumference is a radius.

**Choices:**

- radius ✔
  - _why:_ A radius is a line segment joining the centre of a circle to any point on its circumference.
- diameter
  - _why:_ A diameter passes through the centre and joins two points on the circumference. A segment from the centre to the circumference is a radius.
- chord
  - _why:_ A chord joins two points on the circumference and does not have to pass through the centre. The required line segment is a radius.
- tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent touches the circumference at exactly one point but does not join that point to the centre. The correct term is radius.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A straight line joining two points on the circumference is a [ ___ ].

**Back:** A straight line joining two points on the circumference is a chord.

**Choices:**

- chord ✔
  - _why:_ A chord is a straight line segment whose two endpoints lie on the circumference of a circle.
- arc
  - _why:_ An arc is a curved section of the circumference, not a straight line segment. A straight segment joining two circumference points is a chord.
- radius
  - _why:_ A radius joins the centre to one point on the circumference. A straight segment joining two points on the circumference is a chord.
- tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent touches the circle at only one point. A straight segment with both endpoints on the circumference is called a chord.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is triangle AOB isosceles when A and B lie on a circle centred at O?

**Back:** OA and OB are equal radii.

**Choices:**

- OA and OB are equal radii. ✔
  - _why:_ OA and OB both run from the centre O to the circumference, so they are radii of the same circle and therefore have equal lengths.
- OA and OB are equal chords.
  - _why:_ A chord has both endpoints on the circumference, whereas OA and OB each have one endpoint at the centre. They are equal radii, making the triangle isosceles.
- OA and OB are parallel sides.
  - _why:_ OA and OB meet at O, so they cannot be parallel. The triangle is isosceles because OA and OB are radii of the same circle and are equal.
- OA and OB are equal tangents.
  - _why:_ OA and OB extend from the centre to the circumference, so they are not tangents. They are equal radii, which gives the triangle two equal sides.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] touches a circle at exactly one point.

**Back:** A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point.

**Choices:**

- tangent ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent is a straight line that meets, or touches, a circle at exactly one point.
- secant
  - _why:_ A secant crosses the circle and meets its circumference at two points. A line touching the circle at exactly one point is a tangent.
- chord
  - _why:_ A chord is a line segment with both endpoints on the circumference, so it meets the circle at two points. The correct term is tangent.
- diameter
  - _why:_ A diameter joins two points on the circumference and passes through the centre. A line meeting the circle at exactly one point is a tangent.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** A radius meets a tangent at its point of contact. What angle is formed?

**Back:** 90°.

**Choices:**

- 90°. ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to its point of contact, so the angle between them is 90°.
- 45°.
  - _why:_ Confusing the diagram with an isosceles right triangle can suggest 45°. The tangent and radius are perpendicular at the point of contact, giving 90°.
- 180°.
  - _why:_ An angle of 180° would place both lines along one straight line. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the contact point, so the angle is 90°.
- 60°.
  - _why:_ A 60° angle may be associated with equilateral triangles, but that fact does not apply here. The tangent-radius theorem gives an angle of 90°.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** The tangent-radius right angle is between the tangent and the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The tangent-radius right angle is between the tangent and the radius to the point of contact.

**Choices:**

- radius to the point of contact ✔
  - _why:_ The tangent is perpendicular specifically to the radius drawn from the centre to the point where the tangent touches the circle.
- radius to a different point
  - _why:_ The perpendicular relationship does not apply to an arbitrary radius. It applies specifically to the radius drawn to the tangent’s point of contact.
- chord through the point of contact
  - _why:_ A chord through the contact point is not necessarily perpendicular to the tangent. The guaranteed right angle is with the radius to the point of contact.
- tangent from another external point
  - _why:_ Two tangents are not generally perpendicular to each other. The tangent-radius theorem concerns the radius drawn to the point where the tangent touches the circle.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** OA = 5 cm. AC is a 12 cm tangent at A. O, B and C are in a straight line, with B on the circle. Find BC.

**Back:** BC = 8 cm.

**Choices:**

- BC = 8 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ OA is perpendicular to tangent AC, so triangle OAC is right-angled. Pythagoras gives $OC=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13$, then $BC=OC-OB=13-5=8$ cm.
- BC = 13 cm.
  - _why:_ This finds $OC$ using the 5-12-13 right triangle but forgets that BC is only the part outside the circle. Subtracting $OB=5$ gives $BC=8$ cm.
- BC = 7 cm.
  - _why:_ This subtracts $OA$ directly from tangent length $AC$, although those perpendicular sides cannot be combined that way. Pythagoras gives $OC=13$, so $BC=13-5=8$ cm.
- BC = 17 cm.
  - _why:_ This adds the radius to the tangent length, treating perpendicular lengths as though they lie on one line. Pythagoras gives $OC=13$, then $BC=13-5=8$ cm.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** If AB and AC are tangents from the same external point A, how do AB and AC compare?

**Back:** AB and AC are equal.

**Choices:**

- AB and AC are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Tangents drawn from the same external point to a circle have equal lengths, so $AB=AC$.
- AB and AC are parallel.
  - _why:_ AB and AC share the endpoint A, so distinct segments cannot be parallel. The tangent theorem states that tangents from the same external point have equal lengths.
- AB and AC are perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Each tangent is perpendicular to its own radius at its contact point, not necessarily to the other tangent. Tangents from A have equal lengths, so $AB=AC$.
- AB and AC are in the ratio 2:1.
  - _why:_ There is no 2:1 relationship between tangents from a common external point. The tangent-length theorem shows that their lengths are equal, giving a ratio of 1:1.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** AB and AC are tangents to a circle centred at O, touching at B and C. If ∠BAC = 40°, find ∠BOC.

**Back:** ∠BOC = 140°.

**Choices:**

- ∠BOC = 140°. ✔
  - _why:_ Radii OB and OC are perpendicular to the tangents, so quadrilateral ABOC has two 90° angles. Therefore ∠BOC = 360° - 90° - 90° - 40° = 140°.
- ∠BOC = 80°.
  - _why:_ Doubling 40° incorrectly applies the centre-angle theorem to the angle between two tangents. The tangent radii form two right angles, giving ∠BOC = 180° - 40° = 140°.
- ∠BOC = 40°.
  - _why:_ The angle at the centre is not equal to the angle between the tangents. Since OB and OC are perpendicular to the tangents, ∠BOC = 180° - 40° = 140°.
- ∠BOC = 320°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 40° directly from 360° ignores the two right angles formed by the radii and tangents. The required central angle is 180° - 40° = 140°.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** If a centre angle and circumference angle stand on the same chord or arc, how are they related?

**Back:** The centre angle is twice the circumference angle.

**Choices:**

- The centre angle is twice the circumference angle. ✔
  - _why:_ The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference when both angles are subtended by the same chord or arc.
- The centre angle is equal to the circumference angle.
  - _why:_ Equal angles occur in the same segment, not when comparing centre and circumference angles. On the same chord or arc, the centre angle is twice the circumference angle.
- The centre angle is half the circumference angle.
  - _why:_ This reverses the circle theorem. The circumference angle is half the centre angle, so the centre angle is twice the circumference angle.
- The centre angle is three times the circumference angle.
  - _why:_ The multiplier is not three. For angles standing on the same chord or arc, the standard circle theorem states that the centre angle is twice the circumference angle.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** ∠BOC = 66° at the centre. ∠BAC stands on the same chord BC at the circumference. Find ∠BAC.

**Back:** ∠BAC = 33°.

**Choices:**

- ∠BAC = 33°. ✔
  - _why:_ The angle at the circumference is half the angle at the centre when both stand on the same chord. Therefore ∠BAC = 66° ÷ 2 = 33°.
- ∠BAC = 66°.
  - _why:_ This treats the centre and circumference angles as equal. The circumference angle is half the 66° centre angle, so ∠BAC = 33°.
- ∠BAC = 132°.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 66° by two reverses the theorem. The centre angle is twice the circumference angle, so ∠BAC is 66° ÷ 2 = 33°.
- ∠BAC = 114°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the centre angle from 180° gives an unrelated supplementary angle. The matching circumference angle is half the centre angle, so ∠BAC = 33°.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A circumference angle is 118°. Its matching centre angle is reflex. Find the smaller centre angle.

**Back:** 124°.

**Choices:**

- 124°. ✔
  - _why:_ The reflex centre angle is twice the 118° circumference angle, giving 236°. The smaller centre angle is therefore 360° - 236° = 124°.
- 236°.
  - _why:_ Doubling 118° correctly gives the reflex centre angle, but the question asks for the smaller centre angle. Subtracting 236° from 360° gives 124°.
- 59°.
  - _why:_ Halving 118° reverses the relationship and does not account for the reflex centre angle. The reflex angle is 236°, so the smaller centre angle is 124°.
- 242°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 118° from 360° incorrectly treats the circumference angle as the reflex centre angle. First double 118° to get 236°, leaving a smaller centre angle of 124°.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** A segment is the part of a circle cut off by a [ ___ ].

**Back:** A segment is the part of a circle cut off by a chord.

**Choices:**

- chord ✔
  - _why:_ A chord is a straight line segment joining two points on a circle’s circumference. It divides the circle into two regions, each called a segment.
- radius
  - _why:_ A radius joins the centre to the circumference and does not cut off a segment by itself. A segment is the region of a circle cut off by a chord.
- diameter
  - _why:_ A diameter is a special chord through the centre, but the general boundary that cuts off a segment is called a chord. A diameter specifically creates two semicircles.
- tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point and does not cut off part of its interior. A chord joins two circumference points and cuts off a segment.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Two angles stand on the same chord and are on the same side of that chord. What can you conclude?

**Back:** The angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- The angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles subtended by the same chord in the same segment are equal. Being on the same side of the chord ensures both vertices lie in the same segment.
- The angles are supplementary.
  - _why:_ Supplementary angles arise as opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral, not as angles in the same segment. Angles on the same chord and side are equal.
- The angles are complementary.
  - _why:_ There is no rule requiring these angles to total 90°. The same-segment theorem states that angles subtended by the same chord on the same side are equal.
- The angles are perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular describes lines meeting at 90°, not a relationship between two angle sizes here. Angles standing on the same chord in the same segment are equal.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** A quadrilateral is cyclic when all four vertices lie on the [ ___ ].

**Back:** A quadrilateral is cyclic when all four vertices lie on the circumference.

**Choices:**

- circumference ✔
  - _why:_ A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circumference of one circle. Its opposite angles consequently add to 180°.
- diameter
  - _why:_ The vertices do not all need to lie on a diameter, which is only one chord through the centre. A quadrilateral is cyclic when all vertices lie on the circumference.
- radius
  - _why:_ A radius is a line segment from the centre to the circumference, so four vertices cannot define cyclicity by lying on it. They must lie on the circumference.
- centre
  - _why:_ A quadrilateral cannot have all four distinct vertices at the circle’s centre. The defining condition is that all four vertices lie on the circumference.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** In a cyclic quadrilateral, what do opposite angles add to?

**Back:** 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ Opposite angles in any cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so each opposite pair has a total of 180°.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ A total of 90° describes complementary angles, not opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral. Opposite angles are supplementary and therefore add to 180°.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ All four interior angles of any quadrilateral total 360°, but the question asks about one pair of opposite angles. In a cyclic quadrilateral, that pair totals 180°.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ This can result from subtracting a right angle from the quadrilateral’s 360° total, but it is not the cyclic rule. Opposite angles add to 180°.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠ADC = 83°. Find ∠ABC.

**Back:** ∠ABC = 97°.

**Choices:**

- ∠ABC = 97°. ✔
  - _why:_ Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°. Therefore, ∠ABC = 180° - 83° = 97°.
- ∠ABC = 83°.
  - _why:_ This assumes opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are equal. They are supplementary, so ∠ABC = 180° - 83° = 97°.
- ∠ABC = 107°.
  - _why:_ This results from subtracting 83° from 190° rather than 180°. Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral total 180°, giving ∠ABC = 97°.
- ∠ABC = 7°.
  - _why:_ This comes from subtracting 83° from 90°, as if the angles were complementary. Opposite cyclic-quadrilateral angles are supplementary, so ∠ABC = 97°.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A tangent makes an angle with a chord. Which circle angle is equal to it?

**Back:** The angle in the opposite segment.

**Choices:**

- The angle in the opposite segment. ✔
  - _why:_ The alternate segment theorem states that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle subtended by that chord in the opposite segment.
- The angle in the same segment.
  - _why:_ Angles in the same segment are equal to each other, but that is a different theorem. A tangent-chord angle equals the angle in the opposite segment.
- The angle at the centre of the circle.
  - _why:_ The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference subtended by the same arc. The tangent-chord angle instead equals the angle in the opposite segment.
- The angle in the nearest semicircle.
  - _why:_ An angle in a semicircle is 90°, but it is not generally equal to a tangent-chord angle. The equal angle lies in the opposite segment.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A, B and C lie on a circle. A tangent touches at C. If ∠ABC = 61° and ∠ACB = 73°, find the angle between the tangent at C and chord CB.

**Back:** 46°.

**Choices:**

- 46°. ✔
  - _why:_ The third angle of triangle ABC is ∠CAB = 180° - 61° - 73° = 46°. By the alternate segment theorem, this equals the tangent-chord angle.
- 61°.
  - _why:_ The 61° angle is subtended by chord AC, not chord CB. Chord CB subtends ∠CAB = 46°, which equals the required tangent-chord angle.
- 73°.
  - _why:_ The 73° angle is at the point of contact and is not the opposite-segment angle for chord CB. The required angle equals ∠CAB = 46°.
- 119°.
  - _why:_ This is the supplement of 61°, but the tangent-chord theorem does not require that supplement. The opposite-segment angle is ∠CAB = 46°.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** If chords AB and CD intersect at E, then [ ___ ].

**Back:** If chords AB and CD intersect at E, then AE × BE = CE × DE.

**Choices:**

- AE × BE = CE × DE ✔
  - _why:_ For two chords intersecting inside a circle, the products of the two segment lengths on each chord are equal: AE × BE = CE × DE.
- AE × CE = BE × DE
  - _why:_ This incorrectly multiplies segments from different chords. The intersecting chords theorem multiplies the two parts of each complete chord, giving AE × BE = CE × DE.
- AE × DE = BE × CE
  - _why:_ This pairs diagonal segments rather than the two segments belonging to each chord. The correct relationship is AE × BE = CE × DE.
- AE + BE = CE + DE
  - _why:_ Intersecting chords do not generally have equal total lengths. Their segment products are equal, so the correct relationship is AE × BE = CE × DE.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Chords AB and CD intersect at E. AE = 5 cm, BE = 9 cm, CE = 6 cm and DE = x. Find x.

**Back:** x = 7.5 cm.

**Choices:**

- x = 7.5 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ The intersecting chords theorem gives 5 × 9 = 6 × x. Dividing 45 by 6 gives x = 7.5 cm.
- x = 10.8 cm.
  - _why:_ This comes from treating the segment lengths as a direct proportion, 5/9 = 6/x. Intersecting chords require equal products, so 5 × 9 = 6x and x = 7.5 cm.
- x = 8 cm.
  - _why:_ This equates the total chord lengths, using 5 + 9 = 6 + x. Chord lengths need not be equal; their segment products are, giving x = 7.5 cm.
- x = 3.33 cm.
  - _why:_ This uses 6 × 5 ÷ 9, pairing and dividing the wrong segment lengths. The correct equation is 5 × 9 = 6x, so x = 7.5 cm.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** For two secants from the same external point, which product is equal for each secant?

**Back:** External part × whole length from the external point.

**Choices:**

- External part × whole length from the external point. ✔
  - _why:_ For two secants drawn from one external point, each secant’s external segment multiplied by its entire length from that point gives the same product.
- External part × internal part inside the circle.
  - _why:_ Using only the internal section omits part of the required secant length. The equal product is external part × whole length from the external point.
- Whole length × whole length from the external point.
  - _why:_ The whole secant length is not squared or multiplied by itself. Each product uses the external part once and the whole length once.
- External part + whole length from the external point.
  - _why:_ The secant theorem equates products, not sums. For each secant, multiply its external part by its whole length measured from the external point.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** From E, one secant has CE = 4 cm and CA = x. Another has DE = 3 cm and DB = 10 cm. Find x.

**Back:** x = 5.75 cm.

**Choices:**

- x = 5.75 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ The whole lengths are 4 + x and 3 + 10 = 13. Thus 4(4 + x) = 3 × 13, giving x = 5.75 cm.
- x = 7.5 cm.
  - _why:_ This uses external part × internal part, producing 4x = 3 × 10. The theorem requires whole lengths, so 4(4 + x) = 3 × 13 and x = 5.75 cm.
- x = 3.5 cm.
  - _why:_ This treats DB = 10 cm as the whole second secant, using 4(4 + x) = 3 × 10. Its whole length is 13 cm, giving x = 5.75 cm.
- x = 9.75 cm.
  - _why:_ This treats x as the whole first secant length rather than its internal part, leading to 4x = 39. The whole first secant is 4 + x, so x = 5.75 cm.
