# Pie Charts Flashcards

_Lane: `igcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 2 · 2 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the angle of a sector represent in a pie chart?

**Back:** It is directly proportional to the quantity or frequency.

**Choices:**

- It is directly proportional to the quantity or frequency. ✔
  - _why:_ A larger quantity or frequency occupies a larger fraction of the complete circle, so its sector angle increases in direct proportion to that value.
- It is inversely proportional to the quantity or frequency.
  - _why:_ This reverses the relationship: a larger frequency does not produce a smaller angle. The angle is directly proportional to the quantity or frequency represented.
- It is equal in size for every quantity or frequency.
  - _why:_ Equal sector angles would represent equal frequencies, not different ones. Each sector angle is directly proportional to its corresponding quantity or frequency.
- It is independent of the quantity or frequency.
  - _why:_ Sector angles are not chosen independently of the data. Each angle is calculated from the relevant share of the total, making it directly proportional to frequency.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the sum of all sector angles in a pie chart?

**Back:** $$360^\circ$

**Choices:**

- $$360^\circ$ ✔
  - _why:_ A pie chart represents one complete circle, and the angle around the centre of a complete circle is $360^\circ$, so all sectors must total this.
- $$180^\circ$
  - _why:_ This is the angle in a semicircle, which represents only half of a pie chart. A complete pie chart is a full circle totalling $360^\circ$.
- $$90^\circ$
  - _why:_ This is the angle in one quarter of a circle, not the whole pie chart. All sector angles in the complete circle sum to $360^\circ$.
- $$100^\circ$
  - _why:_ This confuses a circle’s angle total with a percentage total of 100%. Pie chart frequencies total 100%, but their sector angles total $360^\circ$.
