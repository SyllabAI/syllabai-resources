## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the two steps of the unitary method to solve proportion problems?

**Back:** Find the value for 1 unit (divide).Multiply that value to find the required amount.

**Choices:**

- Find the value for 1 unit (divide).Multiply that value to find the required amount. ✔
  - _why:_ The unitary method first uses division to find the value of one unit, then multiplication to scale this value to the required number of units.
- Find the value for 1 unit (multiply).Divide that value to find the required amount.
  - _why:_ The operations have been reversed. Finding one unit requires division by the known number of units, and finding the required amount then requires multiplication.
- Find the difference between the two amounts (subtract).Add that difference to find the required amount.
  - _why:_ Subtraction and addition assume a constant difference, not a proportional relationship. The unitary method divides to find one unit, then multiplies by the required number.
- Find the total for all units (multiply).Divide that total by the required amount.
  - _why:_ This does not establish the value of a single unit and can reverse the intended scaling. Divide first to find one unit, then multiply for the required amount.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In direct proportion, the relationship is expressed by the formula [ ___ ].

**Back:** In direct proportion, the relationship is expressed by the formula $y = kx$.

**Choices:**

- $$y = kx$ ✔
  - _why:_ In direct proportion, the ratio $y/x$ is the constant $k$. Rearranging $y/x=k$ gives the defining relationship $y=kx$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x}$
  - _why:_ This formula describes inverse proportion, where $xy$ remains constant and $y$ decreases as $x$ increases. Direct proportion is represented by $y=kx$.
- $$y = x + k$
  - _why:_ Adding a constant gives a straight line that generally does not pass through the origin, so it is not direct proportion. The correct formula is $y=kx$.
- $$y = kx^2$
  - _why:_ This represents proportion to the square of $x$, so multiplying $x$ by a factor squares that factor for $y$. Direct proportion uses $y=kx$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What happens to quantity $y$ in a direct proportion if quantity $x$ is divided by 5?

**Back:** $$y$ is also divided by 5.

**Choices:**

- $$y$ is also divided by 5. ✔
  - _why:_ Directly proportional quantities change by the same scale factor. Since $x$ is multiplied by $\frac{1}{5}$, $y$ is also multiplied by $\frac{1}{5}$.
- $$y$ is instead multiplied by 5.
  - _why:_ Changing in opposite directions is associated with inverse proportion. In direct proportion, dividing $x$ by 5 also divides $y$ by 5.
- $$y$ is also divided by 25.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 25 incorrectly squares the scale factor, as though $y$ were proportional to $x^2$. Direct proportion means $y$ is divided by 5.
- $$y$ remains exactly the same.
  - _why:_ A constant value of $y$ would mean it does not vary with $x$. In direct proportion, both quantities share the scale factor, so $y$ is divided by 5.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What mathematical ratio remains constant for any two variables in direct proportion?

**Back:** $$\{y\}/\{x\}$ (or $\{x\}/\{y\}$)

**Choices:**

- $$\{y\}/\{x\}$ (or $\{x\}/\{y\}$) ✔
  - _why:_ For direct proportion, $y=kx$, so $y/x=k$ is constant. Its reciprocal $x/y=1/k$ is also constant, provided the values are non-zero.
- $$xy$ (or $x \times y$)
  - _why:_ A constant product is the defining feature of inverse proportion, not direct proportion. For directly proportional variables, the ratio $y/x$ or $x/y$ remains constant.
- $$y+x$ (or $x+y$)
  - _why:_ Direct proportion does not preserve the sum of the variables; both values scale together. Their ratio $y/x$, or equivalently $x/y$, remains constant.
- $$y-x$ (or $x-y$)
  - _why:_ A constant difference describes an additive relationship and does not establish direct proportion. Directly proportional variables have a constant ratio $y/x$ or $x/y$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** A straight line graph only represents direct proportion if it passes through the [ ___ ].

**Back:** A straight line graph only represents direct proportion if it passes through the origin (or $(0,0)$).

**Choices:**

- origin (or $(0,0)$) ✔
  - _why:_ A direct proportion has equation $y=kx$, which gives $y=0$ when $x=0$. Its straight-line graph must therefore pass through the origin.
- $$y$-intercept (or $(0,c)$)
  - _why:_ A non-zero $y$-intercept gives an equation such as $y=kx+c$, which is linear but not directly proportional. Direct-proportion graphs pass through the origin $(0,0)$.
- $$x$-intercept (or $(c,0)$)
  - _why:_ Passing through a non-zero $x$-intercept does not establish direct proportion. The equation $y=kx$ requires the graph to pass specifically through the origin $(0,0)$.
- turning point (or $(a,b)$)
  - _why:_ A turning point is associated with a curved graph, such as a quadratic, rather than a direct-proportion line. The required point is the origin $(0,0)$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** 6 oranges cost £1.20. Calculate the cost of 9 oranges using the unitary method.

**Back:** £1.80 (1 orange = £0.20; $0.20 \times 9 = 1.80$)

**Choices:**

- £1.80 (1 orange = £0.20; $0.20 \times 9 = 1.80$) ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing £1.20 by 6 gives £0.20 per orange. Multiplying this unit cost by 9 gives the correct total of £1.80.
- £10.80 (1 orange = £1.20; $1.20 \times 9 = 10.80$)
  - _why:_ This treats the cost of six oranges as the cost of one orange. Divide £1.20 by 6 first, giving £0.20, then multiply by 9 to get £1.80.
- £0.80 (1 orange = £0.20; $0.20 \times 4 = 0.80$)
  - _why:_ The unit cost is correct, but multiplying by the difference between 9 and 5 does not find the cost of 9 oranges. Multiply £0.20 by 9 to get £1.80.
- £2.40 (1 orange = £0.20; $0.20 \times 12 = 2.40$)
  - _why:_ The unit cost is correct, but it has been multiplied by 12 rather than the required 9 oranges. Using $0.20\times9$ gives £1.80.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** In inverse proportion, the relationship is expressed by the formula [ ___ ].

**Back:** In inverse proportion, the relationship is expressed by the formula $y = \frac{k}{x}$.

**Choices:**

- $$y = \frac{k}{x}$ ✔
  - _why:_ In inverse proportion, the product $xy$ equals a constant $k$. Rearranging $xy=k$ by dividing by $x$ gives $y=\frac{k}{x}$.
- $$y = kx$
  - _why:_ This formula represents direct proportion, where $y/x$ is constant and both variables scale in the same direction. Inverse proportion uses $y=\frac{k}{x}$.
- $$y = \frac{x}{k}$
  - _why:_ Dividing $x$ by a constant still makes $y$ increase with $x$, so this is a direct linear relationship. Inverse proportion requires $y=\frac{k}{x}$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x^2}$
  - _why:_ This represents inverse-square proportion, where doubling $x$ quarters $y$. Ordinary inverse proportion has a constant product $xy$ and formula $y=\frac{k}{x}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** What mathematical product remains constant for any two variables in inverse proportion?

**Back:** $$xy$ (or $x \times y$)

**Choices:**

- $$xy$ (or $x \times y$) ✔
  - _why:_ Inverse proportion is described by $y=k/x$. Multiplying both sides by $x$ gives $xy=k$, so the product of the variables remains constant.
- $$\frac{y}{x}$ (or $y \div x$)
  - _why:_ A constant ratio is the defining feature of direct proportion, where $y=kx$. For inverse proportion, the constant quantity is the product $xy$.
- $$x+y$ (or $y+x$)
  - _why:_ A constant sum means one variable falls by the same amount that the other rises, which is not inverse proportion. In inverse proportion, the product $xy$ remains constant.
- $$x-y$ (or $y-x$)
  - _why:_ A constant difference describes an additive relationship rather than inverse scaling. Inversely proportional variables change reciprocally, keeping their product $xy$ constant.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** If 3 painters take 8 days to paint a house, the total amount of work is [ ___ ] painter-days.

**Back:** If 3 painters take 8 days to paint a house, the total amount of work is 24 painter-days.

**Choices:**

- 24 ✔
  - _why:_ Work in painter-days is found by multiplying the number of painters by the number of days: $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.
- 11
  - _why:_ Adding the painters and days gives $3 + 8 = 11$, but total work requires multiplication. The correct amount is $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.
- 5
  - _why:_ Subtracting the number of painters from the days does not measure total work. Multiplying gives $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.
- 8/3
  - _why:_ Dividing days by painters calculates days per painter, not the total work completed. Total work is $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** 3 painters take 8 days to paint a house. How long would 2 painters take at the same rate?

**Back:** 12 days ($24 \div 2$)

**Choices:**

- 12 days ($24 \div 2$) ✔
  - _why:_ The job requires $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days. With 2 painters, the time is $24 \div 2 = 12$ days.
- 16 days ($8 \times 2$)
  - _why:_ Multiplying the original time by the new number of painters ignores the constant total workload. Using 24 painter-days gives $24 \div 2 = 12$ days.
- 6 days ($24 \div 4$)
  - _why:_ This incorrectly divides the workload by 4 rather than by the 2 available painters. The correct calculation is $24 \div 2 = 12$ days.
- 5 days ($8 - 3$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting the original number of painters from the days does not model inverse proportion. The 24 painter-days shared by 2 painters require 12 days.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** The relationship for inverse square proportion is expressed as [ ___ ].

**Back:** The relationship for inverse square proportion is expressed as $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.

**Choices:**

- $$y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ In inverse square proportion, $y$ is proportional to the reciprocal of $x^2$, so the relationship is $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x}$
  - _why:_ This represents ordinary inverse proportion, where doubling $x$ halves $y$. In inverse square proportion, doubling $x$ quarters $y$, so $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.
- $$y = kx^2$
  - _why:_ This represents direct square proportion, where $y$ increases with $x^2$. Inverse square proportion instead uses the reciprocal: $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.
- $$y = \frac{k^2}{x}$
  - _why:_ Squaring the constant does not create inverse square proportion. The variable $x$ must be squared in the denominator, giving $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** In the formula $F = \frac{80}{d^2}$, what happens to $F$ if $d$ is doubled?

**Back:** $$F$ becomes one quarter as large (divided by 4).

**Choices:**

- $$F$ becomes one quarter as large (divided by 4). ✔
  - _why:_ Because $F$ is inversely proportional to $d^2$, doubling $d$ multiplies the denominator by $2^2 = 4$, making $F$ one quarter as large.
- $$F$ becomes one half as large (divided by 2).
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 treats the relationship as ordinary inverse proportion and overlooks the square. Doubling $d$ multiplies $d^2$ by 4, so $F$ becomes one quarter.
- $$F$ becomes four times as large (multiplied by 4).
  - _why:_ This reverses the inverse square relationship. Doubling $d$ makes the denominator four times larger, so $F$ becomes one quarter as large, not four times larger.
- $$F$ becomes twice as large (multiplied by 2).
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats $F$ as directly proportional to $d$. Since $d$ is squared in the denominator, doubling it makes $F$ one quarter as large.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the graph of direct proportion ($y = kx$).

**Back:** A straight line passing through the origin.

**Choices:**

- A straight line passing through the origin. ✔
  - _why:_ For $y = kx$, the gradient is the constant $k$ and $y = 0$ when $x = 0$, producing a straight line through the origin.
- A straight line crossing the $y$-axis above the origin.
  - _why:_ A non-zero $y$-intercept would give a formula such as $y = kx + c$. Direct proportion has no added constant, so its line passes through the origin.
- An upward curve passing through the origin.
  - _why:_ An upward curve suggests a non-linear relationship such as $y = kx^2$. Direct proportion has a constant gradient, so its graph is a straight line through the origin.
- A hyperbola approaching both coordinate axes.
  - _why:_ A hyperbola approaching the axes represents inverse proportion, $y = \frac{k}{x}$. Direct proportion, $y = kx$, gives a straight line through the origin.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the graph of inverse proportion ($y = \frac{k}{x}$).

**Back:** A hyperbola with branches in the 1st and 3rd quadrants.

**Choices:**

- A hyperbola with branches in the 1st and 3rd quadrants. ✔
  - _why:_ For positive $k$, $x$ and $y$ have the same sign in $y = \frac{k}{x}$. This produces hyperbola branches in the 1st and 3rd quadrants.
- A hyperbola with branches in the 2nd and 4th quadrants.
  - _why:_ Branches in the 2nd and 4th quadrants occur when the constant of proportionality is negative. For the standard positive inverse proportion graph, they lie in the 1st and 3rd quadrants.
- A straight line with branches in the 1st and 3rd quadrants.
  - _why:_ Inverse proportion is non-linear because its gradient changes as $x$ changes. Its graph is a hyperbola with branches in the 1st and 3rd quadrants.
- A parabola with branches in the 1st and 2nd quadrants.
  - _why:_ A parabola is associated with a quadratic relationship such as $y = kx^2$. The reciprocal relationship $y = \frac{k}{x}$ produces a hyperbola in the 1st and 3rd quadrants.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** An [ ___ ] is a line that a curve approaches but never actually meets.

**Back:** An asymptote is a line that a curve approaches but never actually meets.

**Choices:**

- asymptote ✔
  - _why:_ An asymptote is a straight line that a curve approaches increasingly closely. In inverse proportion graphs, the coordinate axes act as asymptotes.
- tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent is a line that touches a curve at a point and matches its gradient there. A line approached by a curve without being met is an asymptote.
- chord
  - _why:_ A chord is a line segment joining two points on a curve, particularly a circle. A line that a curve approaches without meeting is called an asymptote.
- intercept
  - _why:_ An intercept is a point where a graph crosses a coordinate axis. A line that the curve approaches increasingly closely but does not meet is an asymptote.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the graph of direct square proportion ($y = kx^2$).

**Back:** An upward parabola with its lowest point at the origin.

**Choices:**

- An upward parabola with its lowest point at the origin. ✔
  - _why:_ For positive $k$, $y = kx^2$ is non-negative and equals zero when $x = 0$. Its graph is an upward parabola with vertex at the origin.
- A downward parabola with its highest point at the origin.
  - _why:_ A downward parabola would require a negative coefficient, as in $y = -kx^2$. For positive direct square proportion, the parabola opens upward from the origin.
- An upward parabola with its lowest point above the origin.
  - _why:_ A vertex above the origin would require an added positive constant. Since $y = kx^2$ gives $y = 0$ at $x = 0$, the lowest point is the origin.
- An upward straight line with its lowest point at the origin.
  - _why:_ Treating $x^2$ as though it were $x$ produces a straight-line misconception. Squaring $x$ creates an upward parabola whose lowest point is at the origin.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** How does the graph of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ differ from $y = \frac{k}{x}$ regarding the y-axis?

**Back:** Both branches of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ are above the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis).

**Choices:**

- Both branches of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ are above the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis). ✔
  - _why:_ Because $x^2$ has the same positive value for $x$ and $-x$, both branches have positive $y$-values when $k>0$ and are symmetric about the y-axis.
- Both branches of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ are above the x-axis (symmetric about the x-axis).
  - _why:_ Confusing the axes gives the wrong symmetry. Since replacing $x$ with $-x$ does not change $x^2$, the graph is symmetric about the y-axis, not the x-axis.
- Both branches of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ are on opposite sides of the x-axis (symmetric about the origin).
  - _why:_ This describes $y=\frac{k}{x}$ when $k>0$. Squaring $x$ makes the denominator positive on both sides, so both branches are above the x-axis and y-axis symmetric.
- Both branches of $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ are below the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis).
  - _why:_ This sign error overlooks that $x^2$ is positive for every non-zero $x$. For positive $k$, both branches are above the x-axis and symmetric about the y-axis.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a fixed job, doubling the number of workers [ ___ ] the time required.

**Back:** For a fixed job, doubling the number of workers halves the time required.

**Choices:**

- halves ✔
  - _why:_ The number of workers and completion time are inversely proportional for a fixed job. Doubling the workforce therefore divides the required time by two.
- doubles
  - _why:_ This applies direct rather than inverse proportion. For a fixed amount of work, doubling the number of workers halves the time required, assuming all workers have equal productivity.
- quadruples
  - _why:_ Quadrupling the time incorrectly treats the relationship as involving a square. Workers and time are inversely proportional, so doubling the workers halves the required time.
- quarters
  - _why:_ Quartering the time would correspond to multiplying the workforce by four, not two. Doubling the number of workers halves the time needed for the fixed job.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** 2 packets of rice and 5 tins of soup cost £6.10. If 4 packets of rice cost £3.20, what is the cost of 1 tin of soup?

**Back:** £0.90 (2 rice = £1.60; 5 soup = £4.50; 1 soup = £0.90)

**Choices:**

- £0.90 (2 rice = £1.60; 5 soup = £4.50; 1 soup = £0.90) ✔
  - _why:_ Four rice packets cost £3.20, so two cost £1.60. The soups therefore cost £6.10 - £1.60 = £4.50, and £4.50 ÷ 5 = £0.90.
- £0.58 (2 rice = £3.20; 5 soup = £2.90; 1 soup = £0.58)
  - _why:_ This uses the price of four rice packets as the price of two. Two packets cost £1.60, leaving £4.50 for five soups, so one soup costs £0.90.
- £1.22 (2 rice = £0.00; 5 soup = £6.10; 1 soup = £1.22)
  - _why:_ This divides the full bill by five and ignores the cost of the rice. Subtracting £1.60 for two rice packets leaves £4.50, so each soup costs £0.90.
- £1.80 (2 rice = £1.60; 5 soup = £4.50; 1 soup = £1.80)
  - _why:_ This divides the soup total by an incorrect number of tins. There are five tins, so £4.50 must be divided by 5, giving £0.90 per tin.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the formula $m = 24l$, the value 24 represents the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In the formula $m = 24l$, the value 24 represents the constant of proportionality.

**Choices:**

- constant of proportionality ✔
  - _why:_ In a direct proportion formula of the form $m=kl$, the multiplier $k$ is the constant of proportionality. Here, its value is 24.
- variable of proportionality
  - _why:_ The multiplier 24 is fixed rather than variable. In $m=24l$, it is the constant of proportionality linking the two variables $m$ and $l$.
- gradient of inverse proportion
  - _why:_ The equation $m=24l$ represents direct proportion, not inverse proportion. The fixed multiplier 24 is therefore called the constant of proportionality.
- reciprocal of proportionality
  - _why:_ No reciprocal is being used because $l$ is multiplied directly by 24. The number 24 is the constant of proportionality in the direct proportion $m=24l$.
