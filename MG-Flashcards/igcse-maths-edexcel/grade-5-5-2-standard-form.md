## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the two constraints on $a$ and $n$ for the expression $a \times 10^n$ to be in standard form?

**Back:** $1 \le a $n$ is an integer

**Choices:**

- $1 \le a $n$ is an integer ✔
  - _why:_ Standard form requires the coefficient $a$ to be at least $1$ but less than $10$. The exponent $n$ must be an integer, including zero or a negative integer.
- $0 $n$ is an integer
  - _why:_ This reverses the endpoint restrictions: coefficients below $1$ and a coefficient of $10$ are not permitted. The correct constraint is $1 \le a < 10$, with integer $n$.
- $1 \le a $n$ is a positive integer
  - _why:_ Restricting $n$ to positive integers excludes zero and negative powers, which are valid in standard form. The correct requirements are $1 \le a < 10$ and integer $n$.
- $1 $n$ is a whole number
  - _why:_ This wrongly excludes $a=1$, allows $a=10$, and prevents negative exponents by requiring a whole number. Standard form needs $1 \le a < 10$ and integer $n$.
