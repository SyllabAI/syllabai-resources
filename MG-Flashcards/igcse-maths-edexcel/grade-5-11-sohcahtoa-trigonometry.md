## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Before using SOHCAHTOA, what type of triangle must the diagram contain?

**Back:** A right-angled triangle.

**Choices:**

- A right-angled triangle. ✔
  - _why:_ SOHCAHTOA defines trigonometric ratios using the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse sides of a right-angled triangle, which must contain a $90^\circ$ angle.
- An acute-angled triangle.
  - _why:_ An acute-angled triangle has no $90^\circ$ angle, so the standard SOHCAHTOA side relationships do not apply directly. The diagram must contain a right-angled triangle.
- An obtuse-angled triangle.
  - _why:_ An obtuse-angled triangle contains an angle greater than $90^\circ$, rather than a right angle. Basic SOHCAHTOA requires a right-angled triangle.
- An equilateral triangle.
  - _why:_ An equilateral triangle has three $60^\circ$ angles and therefore no hypotenuse. Basic SOHCAHTOA requires a right-angled triangle.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** If a side is opposite the $90^\circ$ angle, what is its label?

**Back:** The hypotenuse.

**Choices:**

- The hypotenuse. ✔
  - _why:_ The side directly opposite the $90^\circ$ angle is always the hypotenuse. It is also the longest side of a right-angled triangle.
- The opposite.
  - _why:_ The opposite side is identified relative to the chosen acute angle, not the $90^\circ$ angle. The side opposite $90^\circ$ is the hypotenuse.
- The adjacent.
  - _why:_ The adjacent side lies beside the chosen acute angle and is not the hypotenuse. The side opposite the $90^\circ$ angle is the hypotenuse.
- The base.
  - _why:_ “Base” may describe a side’s position in a drawing, but it is not the required trigonometric label. The side opposite $90^\circ$ is the hypotenuse.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Changing the focus angle can swap [ ___ ], but the [ ___ ] stays the same.

**Back:** Changing the focus angle can swap opposite and adjacent, but the hypotenuse stays the same.

**Choices:**

- Changing the focus angle can swap opposite and adjacent, but the hypotenuse stays the same. ✔
  - _why:_ Opposite and adjacent are defined relative to the selected acute angle, so they can exchange labels. The hypotenuse remains opposite the fixed $90^\circ$ angle.
- Changing the focus angle can swap hypotenuse and adjacent, but the hypotenuse stays the same.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse is fixed as the side opposite $90^\circ$, so it cannot swap with the adjacent side. Only opposite and adjacent depend on the focus angle.
- Changing the focus angle can swap opposite and adjacent, but the opposite stays the same.
  - _why:_ Although opposite and adjacent can swap when the focus angle changes, the opposite side does not remain fixed. The hypotenuse always stays the same.
- Changing the focus angle can swap opposite and hypotenuse, but the adjacent stays the same.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse cannot become an angle-dependent side, and the adjacent side does not remain fixed. Opposite and adjacent swap, while the hypotenuse stays unchanged.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** $$\sin\theta$ uses [ ___ ] over [ ___ ].

**Back:** $$\sin\theta$ uses opposite over hypotenuse.

**Choices:**

- $$\sin\theta$ uses opposite over hypotenuse. ✔
  - _why:_ The mnemonic SOH means sine equals opposite divided by hypotenuse, so $\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$.
- $$\sin\theta$ uses adjacent over hypotenuse.
  - _why:_ Adjacent over hypotenuse is the cosine ratio, represented by CAH. Sine follows SOH, so it uses opposite over hypotenuse.
- $$\sin\theta$ uses opposite over adjacent.
  - _why:_ Opposite over adjacent is the tangent ratio, represented by TOA. Sine follows SOH, so its denominator is the hypotenuse.
- $$\sin\theta$ uses hypotenuse over opposite.
  - _why:_ This reverses the sine ratio, producing the reciprocal of sine rather than sine itself. SOH gives opposite over hypotenuse.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Angle $35^\circ$, opposite $20\text{ cm}$, hypotenuse $x$: what expression gives $x$?

**Back:** $x=\frac{20}{\sin 35^\circ}$

**Choices:**

- $x=\frac{20}{\sin 35^\circ}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Using $\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ gives $\sin35^\circ=\frac{20}{x}$. Rearranging by multiplying by $x$ and dividing by $\sin35^\circ$ gives this expression.
- $x=20\sin 35^\circ$
  - _why:_ This multiplies by the sine value instead of dividing by it. From $\sin35^\circ=\frac{20}{x}$, rearrangement gives $x=\frac{20}{\sin35^\circ}$.
- $x=\frac{20}{\cos 35^\circ}$
  - _why:_ Cosine relates the adjacent side to the hypotenuse, but the given side is opposite the angle. Sine is required, giving $x=\frac{20}{\sin35^\circ}$.
- $x=\frac{\sin 35^\circ}{20}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the quantities when rearranging and would not produce a length of the correct size. The hypotenuse is $x=\frac{20}{\sin35^\circ}$.

  - _why:_ Using $\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$ gives $\tan42^\circ=\frac{11}{x}$. Rearranging by multiplying by $x$ and dividing by $\tan42^\circ$ produces this expression.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If the angle is unknown, what type of trig button is usually needed?

**Back:** An inverse trig button.

**Choices:**

- An inverse trig button. ✔
  - _why:_ When a side ratio is known and the angle is required, an inverse sine, cosine or tangent function reverses the relevant trigonometric relationship.
- An ordinary trig button.
  - _why:_ An ordinary trig button converts a known angle into a side ratio rather than recovering an angle. An inverse trig button is needed when the angle is unknown.
- A logarithm button.
  - _why:_ A logarithm button reverses exponentiation, not a trigonometric ratio. To recover an angle from a side ratio, the corresponding inverse trig button is required.
- A square root button.
  - _why:_ A square root button reverses squaring and cannot recover an angle from a trigonometric ratio. The required operation is an inverse trig function.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Adjacent $16\text{ cm}$, hypotenuse $24\text{ cm}$, angle $x$: what inverse expression finds $x$?

**Back:** $x=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{16}{24}\right)$

**Choices:**

- $x=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{16}{24}\right)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Cosine uses adjacent over hypotenuse, so $\cos x=\frac{16}{24}$. Applying inverse cosine to both sides gives the required expression for $x$.
- $x=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{16}{24}\right)$
  - _why:_ Sine uses opposite over hypotenuse, but $16$ is the adjacent side. Cosine is therefore required: $x=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{16}{24}\right)$.
- $x=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{24}{16}\right)$
  - _why:_ This reverses adjacent over hypotenuse, producing a ratio greater than $1$, for which inverse cosine has no real angle. The ratio must be $\frac{16}{24}$.
- $x=\cos\left(\frac{16}{24}\right)$
  - _why:_ Ordinary cosine converts an angle into a ratio; it does not convert a ratio back into an angle. The inverse expression is $x=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{16}{24}\right)$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** Inverse trig works backwards from a side ratio to find an [ ___ ]; $\tan^{-1}$ does not mean [ ___ ].

**Back:** Inverse trig works backwards from a side ratio to find an angle; $\tan^{-1}$ does not mean one divided by tangent.

**Choices:**

- Inverse trig works backwards from a side ratio to find an angle; $\tan^{-1}$ does not mean one divided by tangent. ✔
  - _why:_ An inverse trigonometric function converts a known side ratio back into its corresponding angle. The superscript $-1$ denotes an inverse function, not a reciprocal.
- Inverse trig works backwards from a side ratio to find a side length; $\tan^{-1}$ does not mean one divided by tangent.
  - _why:_ A side ratio and inverse trig function determine an angle, not a side length. The notation $\tan^{-1}$ means inverse tangent rather than one divided by tangent.
- Inverse trig works backwards from a side ratio to find an angle; $\tan^{-1}$ does not mean inverse tangent.
  - _why:_ Inverse trig does recover an angle, but the second statement wrongly denies the meaning of the notation. Here, $\tan^{-1}$ means inverse tangent, not a reciprocal.
- Inverse trig works backwards from a side ratio to find a side length; $\tan^{-1}$ does not mean inverse tangent.
  - _why:_ Both completions confuse inverse functions with finding sides. Inverse trig finds an angle from a ratio, and $\tan^{-1}$ means inverse tangent rather than one divided by tangent.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $B$, $BC=20\text{ cm}$ and angle $C=35^\circ$. What equation finds $AB$?

**Back:** $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$

**Choices:**

- $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Relative to angle $C$, $AB$ is the opposite side and $BC=20$ is the adjacent side. Therefore, tangent gives opposite over adjacent: $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$.
- $\sin 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$
  - _why:_ Sine compares opposite with the hypotenuse, but $20$ is the adjacent side here. The correct ratio uses tangent: $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$.
- $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{20}{AB}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the tangent ratio, using adjacent over opposite instead of opposite over adjacent. The correct equation is $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$.
- $\cos 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$
  - _why:_ Cosine compares adjacent with the hypotenuse, whereas $AB$ is opposite angle $C$ and $20$ is adjacent. The correct equation is $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{AB}{20}$.

  - _why:_ The sides have been placed in the reverse order, giving adjacent over opposite. Tangent uses opposite over adjacent, so the correct equation is $\tan\theta=\frac{2}{15}$.

  - _why:_ Cosine compares adjacent with hypotenuse, not opposite with adjacent. Here the relevant ratio is tangent, giving $\tan\theta=\frac{2}{15}$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** If the angle to the horizontal is $7.6^\circ$, what is the angle to the vertical?

**Back:** $82.4^\circ$

**Choices:**

- $82.4^\circ$ ✔
  - _why:_ The horizontal and vertical directions are perpendicular, so their angles add to $90^\circ$. Thus the angle to the vertical is $90-7.6=82.4^\circ$.
- $97.6^\circ$
  - _why:_ Adding $7.6^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ gives the reflex-style value $97.6^\circ$, but the acute complementary angle is required. It is $90-7.6=82.4^\circ$.
- $7.6^\circ$
  - _why:_ The angle to the vertical is not equal to the angle to the horizontal because the reference directions are perpendicular. The two angles are complementary, giving $90-7.6=82.4^\circ$.
- $172.4^\circ$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $7.6^\circ$ from $180^\circ$ finds a supplementary angle, not the angle between the line and the vertical. The required complementary angle is $82.4^\circ$.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ Keeping only one decimal place discards useful calculator accuracy before the calculation is finished. Keep the full calculator value, then round only at the final line.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** A right triangle gives both shorter sides and asks for the hypotenuse. Which method is quickest?

**Back:** Pythagoras.

**Choices:**

- Pythagoras. ✔
  - _why:_ When both shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are known, Pythagoras’ theorem finds the hypotenuse directly using $a^2+b^2=c^2$.
- Sine rule.
  - _why:_ The sine rule is mainly used when the triangle is not necessarily right-angled and includes known opposite side-angle pairs. For two shorter sides of a right triangle, use Pythagoras.
- Cosine rule.
  - _why:_ The cosine rule can find a missing side but is unnecessary here and is usually used for non-right-angled triangles. With both shorter sides known in a right triangle, use Pythagoras.
- SOHCAHTOA.
  - _why:_ SOHCAHTOA relates a side to a known acute angle, but no angle is needed when both shorter sides are given. Pythagoras is the quickest method for finding the hypotenuse.
