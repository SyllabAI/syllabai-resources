## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** A digit's place value depends on its [ ___ ] in a number.

**Back:** A digit's place value depends on its position in a number.

**Choices:**

- position ✔
  - _why:_ A digit’s position determines which column it occupies, such as units, tens or hundreds, and therefore determines its place value.
- size
  - _why:_ The size of a written digit does not affect its value. The correct term is position, because the column occupied by the digit determines its place value.
- shape
  - _why:_ A digit’s shape identifies which digit it is, but not its place value. Its position in the number determines whether it represents units, tens, hundreds or more.
- colour
  - _why:_ Colour has no mathematical effect on a digit’s value. The correct term is position, because moving a digit between columns changes its place value.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Back:** The further left a digit is, the larger its place value.

**Choices:**

- left ✔
  - _why:_ Moving a digit one place to the left multiplies its place value by 10, so digits further left generally have larger place values.
- right
  - _why:_ Moving a digit to the right divides its place value by 10 rather than increasing it. The correct direction is left, where place values become larger.
- up
  - _why:_ Place-value columns run horizontally, so moving up does not describe a change in place value. Moving further left gives a digit a larger place value.
- down
  - _why:_ Moving down is not a place-value direction within a written number. Place values increase from right to left, so the correct term is left.

  - _why:_ Thousandths is the third column after the decimal point, not the first. The first two columns are tenths followed by hundredths.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** In a 5 digit number, why can the first digit not be 0?

**Back:** Then it would not be a 5 digit number.

**Choices:**

- Then it would not be a 5 digit number. ✔
  - _why:_ A five-digit number must have a non-zero digit in the ten-thousands place. If the first digit were 0, that zero would not count as a place, leaving only four digits.
- Then it would be an even number.
  - _why:_ The first digit does not determine whether a number is even; the last digit does. A leading zero would instead mean the number is not a five-digit number.
- Then it would have a decimal point.
  - _why:_ A leading zero does not create a decimal point. It simply does not count as a significant place, so the number would have fewer than five digits.
- Then it would be larger than 99,999.
  - _why:_ A leading zero makes the written number smaller in place-value terms, not larger. It would mean the number is not a five-digit number.

## Card 25 — `cloze`

**Front:** Adding zeros at the [ ___ ] of a decimal does [ ___ ].

**Back:** Adding zeros at the end of a decimal does not change its value.

**Choices:**

- Adding zeros at the end of a decimal does not change its value. ✔
  - _why:_ Zeros placed after the final decimal digit are placeholders. For example, $0.5$, $0.50$, and $0.500$ all represent the same value.
- Adding zeros at the end of a decimal does multiply its value by 10.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by $10$ moves every digit one place to the left; merely adding trailing zeros does not. Zeros at the end of a decimal do not change its value.
- Adding zeros at the middle of a decimal does not change its value.
  - _why:_ A zero inserted between decimal digits changes their place values, as $0.25$ and $0.205$ show. Only zeros added at the end leave the value unchanged.
- Adding zeros at the beginning of a decimal does divide its value by 10.
  - _why:_ Division by $10$ requires moving every digit one place to the right. The rule is that adding zeros at the end of a decimal does not change its value.

## Card 27 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is 1.06 smaller than 1.6?

**Back:** 1.06 = 1.060, while 1.6 = 1.600.

**Choices:**

- 1.06 = 1.060, while 1.6 = 1.600. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding trailing zeros makes the place values easier to compare without changing either number. At the tenths place, $1.060$ has $0$ while $1.600$ has $6$.
- 1.06 = 1.600, while 1.6 = 1.060.
  - _why:_ This swaps the values: trailing zeros cannot turn $1.06$ into $1.6$. The correct comparison is $1.06=1.060$ and $1.6=1.600$, making $1.06$ smaller.
- 1.06 = 1.006, while 1.6 = 1.060.
  - _why:_ Inserting zeros between digits changes their place values, so these equalities are false. Add zeros only at the end: $1.06=1.060$ and $1.6=1.600$.
- 1.06 = 1.600, while 1.6 = 1.600.
  - _why:_ Although $1.6=1.600$, the first equality confuses hundredths with tenths. In fact, $1.06=1.060$, whose tenths digit is smaller than that of $1.600$.
