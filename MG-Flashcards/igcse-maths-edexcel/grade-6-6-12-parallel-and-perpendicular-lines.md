## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the equation $y=mx+c$, what do m and c represent?

**Back:** m: gradient (steepness)c: y-intercept

**Choices:**

- m: gradient (steepness)c: y-intercept ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient m determines the line’s gradient or steepness, while the constant c gives the y-coordinate where the line crosses the y-axis.
- m: y-interceptc: gradient (steepness)
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the two values. In $y=mx+c$, m is the gradient, while c is the y-intercept.
- m: x-interceptc: y-intercept
  - _why:_ The coefficient m does not directly give the x-intercept; it gives the gradient. The constant c gives the y-intercept.
- m: gradient (steepness)c: x-intercept
  - _why:_ Although m correctly represents the gradient, c is the y-intercept, not the x-intercept. Finding the x-intercept requires setting $y=0$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** State the gradient and y-intercept of $y=4x-7$.

**Back:** Gradient: 4y-intercept: -7 (or (0,-7))

**Choices:**

- Gradient: 4y-intercept: -7 (or (0,-7)) ✔
  - _why:_ Comparing $y=4x-7$ with $y=mx+c$ gives $m=4$ and $c=-7$, so the line crosses the y-axis at $(0,-7)$.
- Gradient: -7y-intercept: 4 (or (0,4))
  - _why:_ This swaps the coefficient and constant. In $y=mx+c$, the coefficient of $x$ is the gradient, so the gradient is $4$ and the y-intercept is $-7$.
- Gradient: 4y-intercept: 7 (or (0,7))
  - _why:_ This loses the negative sign on the constant term. Since the equation contains $-7$, the y-intercept is $-7$, while the gradient remains $4$.
- Gradient: -4y-intercept: -7 (or (0,-7))
  - _why:_ The negative sign belongs to the constant term, not the coefficient of $x$. Therefore, the gradient is $4$ and the y-intercept is $-7$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the requirement for two straight lines to be parallel?

**Back:** They must have the same gradient ($m_1 = m_2$).

**Choices:**

- They must have the same gradient ($m_1 = m_2$). ✔
  - _why:_ Parallel straight lines have equal steepness and direction, so their gradients must be equal. They may have different y-intercepts, provided they are distinct lines.
- They must have negative reciprocal gradients ($m_1m_2 = -1$).
  - _why:_ Negative reciprocal gradients are the condition for perpendicular lines, not parallel lines. Parallel lines must instead have equal gradients, so $m_1=m_2$.
- They must have the same y-intercept ($c_1 = c_2$).
  - _why:_ Equal y-intercepts only show that two lines pass through the same point on the y-axis. Parallel lines are identified by having the same gradient.
- They must have reciprocal gradients ($m_1m_2 = 1$).
  - _why:_ Gradients with product $1$ are generally neither parallel nor perpendicular. Parallel lines require the gradients themselves to be equal, so $m_1=m_2$.
