## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does $T_n$ represent in a sequence?

**Back:** The term in position $n$.

**Choices:**

- The term in position $n$. ✔
  - _why:_ $$T_n$ denotes the term whose position in the sequence is $n$, so it identifies the value found at that particular position.
- The term in position $n+1$.
  - _why:_ This confuses the position with the next position in the sequence. $T_n$ means the term in position $n$, whereas $T_{n+1}$ is the following term.
- The sum of the first $n$ terms.
  - _why:_ This is the meaning of $S_n$, not $T_n$. The notation $T_n$ refers to one term at position $n$, while $S_n$ represents a sum.
- The difference between consecutive terms.
  - _why:_ A difference between consecutive terms describes a common difference or first difference, not $T_n$. $T_n$ specifically represents the term in position $n$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What does $S_n$ represent in a sequence?

**Back:** The sum of the first $n$ terms.

**Choices:**

- The sum of the first $n$ terms. ✔
  - _why:_ $$S_n$ represents the sum of the first $n$ terms of a sequence, found by adding every term from the first through to term $n$.
- The term in position $n$.
  - _why:_ This is the meaning of $T_n$, not $S_n$. The letter $S$ indicates a sum, so $S_n$ is the total of the first $n$ terms.
- The product of the first $n$ terms.
  - _why:_ $$S_n$ uses addition rather than multiplication. The product of terms would require a different notation, while $S_n$ means the sum of the first $n$ terms.
- The difference between the first and $n$th terms.
  - _why:_ Subtracting terms gives a difference, not $S_n$. The notation $S_n$ refers to adding all the terms from position 1 to position $n$.
