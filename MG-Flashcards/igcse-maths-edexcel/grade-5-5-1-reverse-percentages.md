## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In percentage change, what is the starting amount treated as?

**Back:** 100%.

**Choices:**

- 100%. ✔
  - _why:_ The starting amount represents the whole original quantity, so it is treated as 100%. Any percentage increase or decrease is measured relative to this baseline.
- 0%.
  - _why:_ Treating the starting amount as 0% confuses the baseline with no quantity. The complete original amount is always represented by 100%.
- 1%.
  - _why:_ This confuses the whole with one hundredth of the whole. The starting amount is the complete original quantity, so it is treated as 100%.
- 50%.
  - _why:_ Treating the starting amount as 50% incorrectly assumes it is half of another quantity. The original amount is the whole baseline and therefore represents 100%.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A reverse percentage question asks for the [ ___ ], before the percentage change.

**Back:** A reverse percentage question asks for the original amount, before the percentage change.

**Choices:**

- original amount ✔
  - _why:_ Reverse percentage problems work backwards from an amount after a percentage change to find the original amount before that increase or decrease occurred.
- percentage change
  - _why:_ The percentage change is normally given and is used to reverse the calculation. The unknown quantity being found is the original amount before the change.
- final amount
  - _why:_ The final amount is usually supplied in a reverse percentage question, not sought. The calculation uses it to determine the original amount before the change.
- change multiplier
  - _why:_ The multiplier is calculated from the stated percentage and used as part of the method. The quantity the question asks for is the original amount.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** An increase makes the final amount [ ___ ] of the original; a decrease makes it [ ___ ].

**Back:** An increase makes the final amount more than 100% of the original; a decrease makes it less than 100%.

**Choices:**

- An increase makes the final amount more than 100% of the original; a decrease makes it less than 100%. ✔
  - _why:_ An increase adds to the original 100%, making the final percentage greater than 100%. A decrease removes part of the original, making it less than 100%.
- An increase makes the final amount less than 100% of the original; a decrease makes it more than 100%.
  - _why:_ This reverses the effects of increases and decreases. An increase produces more than 100% of the original, while a decrease produces less than 100%.
- An increase makes the final amount equal to 100% of the original; a decrease makes it less than 100%.
  - _why:_ An unchanged amount would equal 100%, but an increase must exceed it. The correct relationship is more than 100% after an increase and less after a decrease.
- An increase makes the final amount more than 100% of the original; a decrease makes it equal to 100%.
  - _why:_ A decrease cannot leave the amount at exactly 100% unless the decrease is zero. It makes the final amount less than 100% of the original.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** After an increase of 6%, what percentage of the original is the final amount?

**Back:** 106%.

**Choices:**

- 106%. ✔
  - _why:_ The original amount is 100%, and a 6% increase adds 6 percentage points. Therefore, the final amount is 100% + 6% = 106%.
- 94%.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 6% gives the result for a decrease rather than an increase. Adding 6% to the original 100% gives a final amount of 106%.
- 6%.
  - _why:_ This gives only the percentage increase and omits the original 100%. The final amount includes both, so it is 100% + 6% = 106%.
- 160%.
  - _why:_ This treats 6% as 60%, a place-value error. A 6% increase adds 6 percentage points to 100%, producing 106%.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** After a decrease of 25%, what percentage of the original is the final amount?

**Back:** 75%.

**Choices:**

- 75%. ✔
  - _why:_ The original amount is 100%, and a 25% decrease removes 25 percentage points. The final amount is therefore 100% - 25% = 75%.
- 125%.
  - _why:_ Adding 25% calculates an increase instead of a decrease. A 25% decrease requires subtracting from 100%, leaving 75% of the original.
- 25%.
  - _why:_ This gives the portion removed rather than the portion remaining. Subtracting the 25% decrease from the original 100% leaves 75%.
- 85%.
  - _why:_ This subtracts 15% rather than the stated 25%. The correct calculation is 100% - 25%, so the final amount is 75%.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What multiplier applies an increase by $p\%$?

**Back:** Increase multiplier: $1 + \frac{p}{100}$.

**Choices:**

- Increase multiplier: $1 + \frac{p}{100}$. ✔
  - _why:_ The $1$ represents the whole original amount, while $\frac{p}{100}$ represents the fractional increase. Adding them gives the multiplier for increasing by $p\%$.
- Increase multiplier: $1 - \frac{p}{100}$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting $\frac{p}{100}$ creates a decrease multiplier. An increase adds that fraction to the original whole, so the correct multiplier is $1 + \frac{p}{100}$.
- Increase multiplier: $\frac{p}{100}$.
  - _why:_ This represents only the fractional increase and omits the original amount. The multiplier must include the original whole, giving $1 + \frac{p}{100}$.
- Increase multiplier: $1 + \frac{100}{p}$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the percentage fraction. Converting $p\%$ to a decimal gives $\frac{p}{100}$, so the increase multiplier is $1 + \frac{p}{100}$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What multiplier applies a decrease by $p\%$?

**Back:** Decrease multiplier: $1 - \frac{p}{100}$.

**Choices:**

- Decrease multiplier: $1 - \frac{p}{100}$. ✔
  - _why:_ The $1$ represents the whole original amount, and $\frac{p}{100}$ is the fraction removed. Subtracting them gives the multiplier for decreasing by $p\%$.
- Decrease multiplier: $1 + \frac{p}{100}$.
  - _why:_ Adding $\frac{p}{100}$ creates an increase multiplier rather than a decrease multiplier. A decrease removes this fraction, so the correct multiplier is $1 - \frac{p}{100}$.
- Decrease multiplier: $\frac{p}{100}$.
  - _why:_ This gives the fraction removed, not the fraction remaining. The multiplier must subtract that fraction from the original whole, giving $1 - \frac{p}{100}$.
- Decrease multiplier: $1 - \frac{100}{p}$.
  - _why:_ This inverts the fraction used to convert a percentage. Since $p\% = \frac{p}{100}$, the correct decrease multiplier is $1 - \frac{p}{100}$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** An increase multiplier is [ ___ ]; a decrease multiplier is [ ___ ].

**Back:** An increase multiplier is bigger than 1; a decrease multiplier is smaller than 1.

**Choices:**

- An increase multiplier is bigger than 1; a decrease multiplier is smaller than 1. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying by a number greater than 1 increases a positive amount, while multiplying by a positive number below 1 retains only part of it and causes a decrease.
- An increase multiplier is smaller than 1; a decrease multiplier is bigger than 1.
  - _why:_ This reverses the two effects. A multiplier above 1 makes a positive quantity larger, while a positive multiplier below 1 makes it smaller.
- An increase multiplier is equal to 1; a decrease multiplier is smaller than 1.
  - _why:_ A multiplier of exactly 1 leaves an amount unchanged, so it cannot represent a genuine increase. An increase multiplier must be bigger than 1.
- An increase multiplier is bigger than 1; a decrease multiplier is equal to 1.
  - _why:_ A multiplier of exactly 1 causes no change, not a decrease. A positive decrease multiplier must be smaller than 1, while an increase multiplier is bigger than 1.

- 1.8.
  - _why:_ Treating 8% as 0.8 creates an 80% increase rather than an 8% increase. Since 8% is 0.08, the correct multiplier is 1.08.
- 1.008.
  - _why:_ Converting 8% to 0.008 divides by 1,000 instead of 100, producing a 0.8% increase. An 8% increase uses the multiplier 1.08.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For a normal percentage change, how are original, multiplier and final linked?

**Back:** Forward link: $\text{original} \times \text{multiplier} = \text{final}$.

**Choices:**

- Forward link: $\text{original} \times \text{multiplier} = \text{final}$. ✔
  - _why:_ The multiplier expresses the final amount as a proportion of the original. Multiplying the original amount by that multiplier therefore produces the final amount.
- Forward link: $\text{original} + \text{multiplier} = \text{final}$.
  - _why:_ A percentage multiplier is a scale factor, not an amount to add directly. The correct forward link is original multiplied by the multiplier equals final.
- Forward link: $\text{original} \div \text{multiplier} = \text{final}$.
  - _why:_ Division reverses a completed percentage change when the final amount is known. In the forward direction, the original must be multiplied by the multiplier to obtain the final.
- Forward link: $\text{original} \times \text{final} = \text{multiplier}$.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the final amount as a scale factor. The final amount is found by multiplying the original amount by the percentage multiplier.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a known final amount in reverse percentages: [ ___ ] = [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a known final amount in reverse percentages: original = final ÷ multiplier.

**Choices:**

- For a known final amount in reverse percentages: original = final ÷ multiplier. ✔
  - _why:_ The forward calculation multiplies the original by the multiplier. Its inverse is division, so dividing the known final amount by the multiplier recovers the original.
- For a known final amount in reverse percentages: original = final × multiplier.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the multiplier repeats the percentage change rather than reversing it. To recover the original from the final, divide the final amount by the multiplier.
- For a known final amount in reverse percentages: final = original ÷ multiplier.
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the known and unknown amounts. When the final is known, the original is found using original = final ÷ multiplier.
- For a known final amount in reverse percentages: multiplier = final ÷ original.
  - _why:_ This formula calculates the multiplier when both amounts are known, rather than recovering the original. Reverse percentages use original = final ÷ multiplier.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you not subtract the percentage from the final amount in reverse percentages?

**Back:** The percentage is based on the original, not the final.

**Choices:**

- The percentage is based on the original, not the final. ✔
  - _why:_ A stated percentage increase or decrease is calculated from the original amount. Applying that percentage directly to the final uses the wrong base and gives an incorrect change.
- The percentage is based on the final, not the original.
  - _why:_ This reverses the percentage base: percentage changes are defined using the original amount. Therefore, subtracting a percentage of the final does not correctly undo the change.
- The percentage is based on the multiplier, not the final.
  - _why:_ The multiplier represents the proportion remaining after the change; it is not the monetary or numerical base. The percentage change is calculated from the original amount.
- The percentage is based on the amount changed, not the final.
  - _why:_ The amount changed is itself calculated as a percentage of the original, so it cannot be the percentage base. Reverse calculations must use the original-based multiplier.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a price is reduced by 20%, the sale price is [ ___ ] of the original, not [ ___ ].

**Back:** If a price is reduced by 20%, the sale price is 80% of the original, not 20%.

**Choices:**

- If a price is reduced by 20%, the sale price is 80% of the original, not 20%. ✔
  - _why:_ A 20% reduction removes 20% from the original 100%, leaving 80%. The sale price is therefore 80% of the original price.
- If a price is reduced by 20%, the sale price is 20% of the original, not 80%.
  - _why:_ This confuses the percentage removed with the percentage remaining. Reducing 100% by 20% leaves 80%, so the sale price is 80% of the original.
- If a price is reduced by 20%, the sale price is 120% of the original, not 20%.
  - _why:_ A sale reduction decreases the price; 120% would represent a 20% increase. Subtracting 20% from 100% shows that the sale price is 80% of the original.
- If a price is reduced by 20%, the sale price is 98% of the original, not 2%.
  - _why:_ This treats 20% as 2%, usually from an incorrect decimal conversion. A genuine 20% reduction leaves 100% - 20% = 80% of the original.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** When should you use 0.2 for a 20% sale reduction?

**Back:** Only when given the amount reduced.

**Choices:**

- Only when given the amount reduced. ✔
  - _why:_ The decimal 0.2 represents the 20% removed from the original. It is therefore appropriate when the given value is the amount of the reduction.
- Only when given the original amount.
  - _why:_ Knowing the original does not make 0.2 the sale-price multiplier; multiplying by 0.2 finds only the reduction amount. The remaining sale price uses 0.8.
- Only when given the sale price.
  - _why:_ The sale price is 80% of the original, so it corresponds to 0.8 rather than 0.2. Use 0.2 only when the given value is the amount reduced.
- Only when given the amount remaining.
  - _why:_ After a 20% reduction, the amount remaining represents 80% and uses 0.8. The multiplier 0.2 applies only to the amount removed.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** If given the amount of change, how do you find the original amount?

**Back:** Amount of change ÷ percentage change as a decimal.

**Choices:**

- Amount of change ÷ percentage change as a decimal. ✔
  - _why:_ The amount of change equals the original multiplied by the decimal percentage change. Reversing this relationship requires dividing the amount of change by that decimal.
- Amount of change × percentage change as a decimal.
  - _why:_ Multiplication finds a percentage of an amount rather than reversing the calculation. To recover the original, divide the amount of change by the decimal percentage change.
- Final amount ÷ percentage change as a decimal.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the final amount as the amount changed. When the change amount is given, divide that change by the decimal percentage to find the original.
- Amount of change ÷ percentage change as a whole number.
  - _why:_ Using 20 instead of 0.2 makes the result 100 times too small. Convert the percentage to a decimal, then divide the amount of change by it.
