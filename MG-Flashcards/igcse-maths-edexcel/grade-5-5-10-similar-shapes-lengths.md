## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What two conditions must be met for shapes to be mathematically similar?

**Back:** Matching angles are equalMatching sides are in the same ratio

**Choices:**

- Matching angles are equalMatching sides are in the same ratio ✔
  - _why:_ Equal corresponding angles give both shapes the same form, while proportional corresponding sides allow their sizes to differ by one consistent scale factor.
- Matching angles are in the same ratioMatching sides are equal
  - _why:_ Similarity requires corresponding angles to be equal, not proportional, and corresponding sides to be in the same ratio. Requiring equal sides incorrectly restricts the shapes to congruent examples.
- Matching angles are supplementaryMatching sides have the same difference
  - _why:_ Supplementary angles add to $180°$ but need not match, and equal side differences do not preserve shape. Similarity requires equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides.
- Matching angles are equalMatching sides have the same difference
  - _why:_ Although equal corresponding angles are required, comparing side differences is an additive misconception. Corresponding side lengths must have one constant ratio, called the scale factor.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The scale factor is the [ ___ ] used to get from one shape to the similar shape.

**Back:** The scale factor is the multiplier used to get from one shape to the similar shape.

**Choices:**

- multiplier ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor is the number by which every corresponding length is multiplied to transform one similar shape into the other.
- divisor
  - _why:_ A divisor may be used when reversing an enlargement, but it does not define the scale factor itself. The scale factor is the multiplier connecting corresponding lengths.
- difference
  - _why:_ Similar shapes are related multiplicatively rather than by adding a constant difference. The scale factor is the multiplier applied to every corresponding length.
- percentage
  - _why:_ A percentage can describe a relative change, but the scale factor is specifically the multiplier used to convert corresponding lengths from one shape to the other.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Instead of using eyesight, how should you determine which sides of two triangles correspond?

**Back:** Use matching angles or the order of the letters in the triangle names.

**Choices:**

- Use matching angles or the order of the letters in the triangle names. ✔
  - _why:_ Corresponding vertices have equal angles and occupy matching positions in correctly ordered triangle names, so either method reliably identifies the corresponding sides.
- Use the positions on the page or the directions of the sides in the diagram.
  - _why:_ Similar triangles may be rotated or reflected, so page position and side direction can mislead. Use equal angles or the order of vertices in the triangle names.
- Use the matching side lengths or the size of the drawing in the diagram.
  - _why:_ Corresponding sides need not have equal lengths, and diagrams may not be drawn to scale. Match equal angles or use the ordered letters in the triangle names.
- Use the matching line thicknesses or the order of the side lengths in the diagram.
  - _why:_ Line thickness has no geometric meaning, and estimated length order can be unreliable. Corresponding sides should be found from matching angles or ordered triangle names.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a missing length on a larger shape, [ ___ ] the corresponding smaller length by the scale factor.

**Back:** To find a missing length on a larger shape, multiply the corresponding smaller length by the scale factor.

**Choices:**

- multiply ✔
  - _why:_ The larger shape’s corresponding length is found by multiplying the smaller length by the scale factor, since an enlargement increases every length by the same ratio.
- divide
  - _why:_ Dividing reverses an enlargement and would find the smaller corresponding length. To move from the smaller shape to the larger shape, multiply by the scale factor.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding the scale factor treats enlargement as an additive change, but similar lengths change proportionally. Multiply the smaller length by the scale factor instead.
- subtract
  - _why:_ Subtracting would reduce the length and does not preserve proportionality. For the larger similar shape, multiply the corresponding smaller length by the scale factor.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a missing length on a smaller shape, [ ___ ] the corresponding larger length by the scale factor.

**Back:** To find a missing length on a smaller shape, divide the corresponding larger length by the scale factor.

**Choices:**

- divide ✔
  - _why:_ Moving from the larger shape to the smaller shape reverses the enlargement, so the larger corresponding length is divided by the scale factor.
- multiply
  - _why:_ Multiplication moves from the smaller shape to the larger one. To reverse that enlargement and find the smaller corresponding length, divide by the scale factor.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding the scale factor assumes a fixed increase rather than a proportional relationship. Find the smaller length by dividing the larger length by the scale factor.
- subtract
  - _why:_ Subtracting the scale factor is an additive shortcut that does not preserve similarity. The enlargement is reversed by dividing the larger length by the scale factor.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** A small triangle is inside a large triangle. What geometric feature ensures they are similar?

**Back:** A side of the small triangle is parallel to a side of the large triangle.

**Choices:**

- A side of the small triangle is parallel to a side of the large triangle. ✔
  - _why:_ Parallel sides create equal corresponding angles, while the triangles also share the angle at the common vertex. Two equal angle pairs establish similarity by angle-angle.
- A side of the small triangle is perpendicular to a side of the large triangle.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular sides create a right angle between the lines but do not establish equal corresponding angles. Parallel sides produce the angle relationships needed for triangle similarity.
- A side of the small triangle is equal to a side of the large triangle.
  - _why:_ One pair of equal sides is insufficient to prove similarity and may occur in unrelated triangles. A parallel pair of sides establishes the required matching angles.
- A side of the small triangle is bisected by a side of the large triangle.
  - _why:_ Bisecting a side concerns equal segments and does not guarantee matching angles or proportional sides. The relevant feature is that one pair of corresponding sides is parallel.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** In nested triangles $ABE$ and $ACD$ where $BE \parallel CD$, the ratio of the sides is: $$\frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{AE}{AD} = \frac{\text{[...]}}{CD}$$

**Back:** In nested triangles $ABE$ and $ACD$ where $BE \parallel CD$, the ratio of the sides is: $$\frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{AE}{AD} = \frac{\boxed{BE}}{CD}$$

**Choices:**

- $$BE$ ✔
  - _why:_ Side $BE$ of the smaller triangle corresponds to side $CD$ of the larger triangle because both lie opposite the shared vertex $A$ and are parallel.
- $$CE$
  - _why:_ The segment $CE$ is not a side of the smaller triangle $ABE$, so it cannot be its numerator. The side corresponding to $CD$ is $BE$.
- $$DE$
  - _why:_ The segment $DE$ joins points on different sides of the nested construction and is not a corresponding triangle side. The smaller side parallel to $CD$ is $BE$.
- $$AB$
  - _why:_ Side $AB$ corresponds to $AC$, as already shown by the first ratio. Since $CD$ corresponds to the parallel side of the smaller triangle, the numerator is $BE$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** In nested triangles $ABE$ and $ACD$ where $BE \parallel CD$, the ratio of the sides is: $$\frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{AE}{AD} = \frac{BE}{\text{[...]}}$$

**Back:** In nested triangles $ABE$ and $ACD$ where $BE \parallel CD$, the ratio of the sides is: $$\frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{AE}{AD} = \frac{BE}{\boxed{CD}}$$

**Choices:**

- $$CD$ ✔
  - _why:_ Side $CD$ of the larger triangle corresponds to $BE$ of the smaller triangle because the sides are parallel and join matching vertices.
- $$AD$
  - _why:_ Side $AD$ corresponds to $AE$, as shown by the middle ratio, rather than to $BE$. The larger side corresponding to $BE$ is $CD$.
- $$AC$
  - _why:_ Side $AC$ corresponds to $AB$, as shown by the first ratio. The side paired with $BE$ must instead be the parallel side $CD$.
- $$BE$
  - _why:_ Using $BE$ in both numerator and denominator would always give $1$, ignoring the scale difference. Its corresponding side in the larger triangle is $CD$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** When calculating the scale factor for nested triangles (one inside another), what must you do if a side is split into two parts (e.g., $AB$ and $BC$)?

**Back:** Add the two parts together to find the full side length of the larger triangle.

**Choices:**

- Add the two parts together to find the full side length of the larger triangle. ✔
  - _why:_ The larger triangle’s side consists of both adjacent segments, so their lengths must be added before it can be compared with the corresponding side of the smaller triangle.
- Subtract the smaller part from the larger part to find the full side length of the larger triangle.
  - _why:_ Subtraction finds a difference rather than the complete side. The two adjacent parts must be added because together they form the full side of the larger triangle.
- Use only the longer part as the full side length of the larger triangle.
  - _why:_ Using one segment ignores the other section of the larger triangle’s side. Add both adjacent parts to obtain the full corresponding length before finding the scale factor.
- Multiply the two parts together to find the full side length of the larger triangle.
  - _why:_ Multiplying lengths produces square units and cannot give the length of the complete side. Add the two adjacent segments to find the larger triangle’s full side length.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** In a bow-tie diagram where two lines cross at $P$, which sides typically correspond?

**Back:** Sides that lie on the same straight line passing through $P$.

**Choices:**

- Sides that lie on the same straight line passing through $P$. ✔
  - _why:_ Each side of one triangle corresponds to the side of the other triangle continuing along the same intersecting line through $P$, even though they point in opposite directions.
- Sides that lie on parallel straight lines on opposite sides of $P$.
  - _why:_ The parallel sides are usually the triangles’ bases, not the paired sides passing through the crossing. Corresponding sloping sides lie on the same straight line through $P$.
- Sides that have the same numerical length on opposite sides of $P$.
  - _why:_ Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional, but they need not have equal lengths. In a bow-tie diagram, correspondence is identified by sides lying on the same line through $P$.
- Sides that meet at the same parallel base away from $P$.
  - _why:_ The triangles have separate parallel bases, so their sides do not meet at one common base. Corresponding sloping sides are those on the same straight line through $P$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are triangles in a bow-tie diagram with parallel bases mathematically similar?

**Back:** Vertically opposite angles at the crossing point are equalParallel lines create equal corresponding (or alternate) angles

**Choices:**

- Vertically opposite angles at the crossing point are equalParallel lines create equal corresponding (or alternate) angles ✔
  - _why:_ These two angle relationships provide equal pairs of angles in the triangles. Therefore, the triangles satisfy the angle-angle similarity condition and must be mathematically similar.
- Adjacent angles at the crossing point are equalParallel lines create equal co-interior angles
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles at an intersection are generally supplementary, while co-interior angles between parallel lines sum to $180^\circ$. Similarity follows from vertically opposite and corresponding or alternate angles being equal.
- Vertically opposite sides at the crossing point are equalParallel lines create equal side lengths in both triangles
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite describes angles, not sides, and parallel lines do not guarantee equal lengths. The triangles are similar because vertically opposite and corresponding or alternate angles are equal.
- Supplementary angles at the crossing point are equalParallel lines create equal opposite angles
  - _why:_ Supplementary angles add to $180^\circ$ but are not necessarily equal, and “opposite angles” is not the relevant parallel-line rule. Use vertically opposite and corresponding or alternate angles.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the ratio of two corresponding sides $AB:AC$ is $2:5$, the scale factor to get from $AB$ to $AC$ is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If the ratio of two corresponding sides $AB:AC$ is $2:5$, the scale factor to get from $AB$ to $AC$ is $\boxed{2.5}$.

**Choices:**

- $$2.5$ ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor from $AB$ to $AC$ is the target-to-original ratio, $5 \div 2 = 2.5$. Multiplying the length of $AB$ by $2.5$ gives $AC$.
- $$0.4$
  - _why:_ This comes from reversing the division and calculating $2 \div 5$. That is the scale factor from $AC$ to $AB$; from $AB$ to $AC$, use $5 \div 2 = 2.5$.
- $$1.5$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses the difference between the ratio numbers rather than dividing corresponding lengths. The required scale factor is $5 \div 2 = 2.5$.
- $$3.5$
  - _why:_ Adding or otherwise combining the ratio numbers does not produce a scale factor. Divide the target ratio part by the original part: $5 \div 2 = 2.5$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If you know the total length $AD$ and the small length $AE$, how do you find the length of the remaining segment $ED$?

**Back:** $$$ED = AD - AE$$ (Subtract the smaller triangle side from the larger triangle side).

**Choices:**

- $$$ED = AD - AE$$ (Subtract the smaller triangle side from the larger triangle side). ✔
  - _why:_ Since $AD$ is the complete length and consists of $AE$ plus $ED$, rearranging $AD = AE + ED$ gives $ED = AD - AE$.
- $$$ED = AD + AE$$ (Add the smaller triangle side to the larger triangle side).
  - _why:_ Adding $AE$ to the total $AD$ counts that section twice and produces a length greater than the whole side. The remaining segment is $ED = AD - AE$.
- $$$ED = AE - AD$$ (Subtract the larger triangle side from the smaller triangle side).
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction would give a negative value because $AD$ is the complete, longer side. Subtract the known small segment from the total: $ED = AD - AE$.
- $$$ED = AD \div AE$$ (Divide the larger triangle side by the smaller triangle side).
  - _why:_ Division gives a dimensionless ratio or scale factor, not the length of the missing segment. Remove the known part from the total using $ED = AD - AE$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are all rectangles not necessarily similar, even though their angles are all $90^\circ$?

**Back:** Their length-to-width ratios (or the scale factors for length and width) might be different.

**Choices:**

- Their length-to-width ratios (or the scale factors for length and width) might be different. ✔
  - _why:_ Similarity requires corresponding side lengths to share one scale factor. Rectangles all have equal corresponding angles, but differing length-to-width ratios mean their sides are not proportional.
- Their opposite side lengths (or the pairs of parallel sides in each shape) might be different.
  - _why:_ Similar shapes may have different actual side lengths, so this alone does not prevent similarity. Rectangles are dissimilar when their length-to-width ratios, or corresponding scale factors, differ.
- Their total interior angles (or the number of right angles in each shape) might be different.
  - _why:_ Every rectangle has four right angles and an interior-angle sum of $360^\circ$, so these cannot distinguish similarity. The deciding condition is whether corresponding side lengths are proportional.
- Their areas and perimeters (or the overall sizes of the two shapes) might be different.
  - _why:_ Similar rectangles can have different areas and perimeters because similarity does not require equal size. They fail to be similar only when their length-to-width ratios are different.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** To show two rectangles are not similar, show that the scale factor for the [ ___ ] is different from the scale factor for the widths.

**Back:** To show two rectangles are not similar, show that the scale factor for the lengths is different from the scale factor for the widths.

**Choices:**

- lengths ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle’s length and width are corresponding linear dimensions. If they require different scale factors, the sides are not proportional and the two rectangles cannot be similar.
- areas
  - _why:_ Area uses a squared scale factor, so it should not be compared directly with a width’s linear scale factor. Compare the scale factors for the lengths and widths instead.
- angles
  - _why:_ Angles do not have a linear scale factor, and all rectangles already have four $90^\circ$ angles. Compare the scale factor for the lengths with that for the widths.
- volumes
  - _why:_ A rectangle is two-dimensional and has no volume, so a volume scale factor is not relevant. To test similarity, compare the linear scale factors for its lengths and widths.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If the scale factor from triangle $A$ to triangle $B$ is $1.5$, and a side on triangle $B$ is $24$ cm, how do you find the matching side on triangle $A$?

**Back:** $$$24 \div 1.5 = 16\text{ cm}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$24 \div 1.5 = 16\text{ cm}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor from $A$ to $B$ is $1.5$, so reversing the enlargement requires division by $1.5$. Therefore, the matching side on triangle $A$ is $16$ cm.
- $$$24 \times 1.5 = 36\text{ cm}$$
  - _why:_ Multiplying applies the scale factor from triangle $A$ to triangle $B$, but the question asks for the reverse direction. Dividing $24$ by $1.5$ gives the correct length of $16$ cm.
- $$$24 \div 1.5 = 18\text{ cm}$$
  - _why:_ The correct operation has been selected, but the division is inaccurate. Since $16 \times 1.5 = 24$, the matching side is $16$ cm, not $18$ cm.
- $$$24 - 1.5 = 22.5\text{ cm}$$
  - _why:_ A scale factor represents multiplication, not an amount to subtract from a length. Reverse the enlargement by calculating $24 \div 1.5$, which gives $16$ cm.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the scale factor from a length of $140$ mm to a length of $180$ mm?

**Back:** $$$\frac{180}{140} = \frac{9}{7} \approx 1.29$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{180}{140} = \frac{9}{7} \approx 1.29$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor is the new length divided by the original length. Thus $180 \div 140$ simplifies to $9/7$, which is approximately $1.29$.
- $$$\frac{140}{180} = \frac{7}{9} \approx 0.78$$
  - _why:_ This reverses the division and finds the scale factor from $180$ mm to $140$ mm. From $140$ mm to $180$ mm, calculate $180/140 = 9/7 \approx 1.29$.
- $$$\frac{180}{140} = \frac{8}{7} \approx 1.14$$
  - _why:_ Dividing both numerator and denominator by $20$ gives $9/7$, not $8/7$. Therefore, the scale factor is approximately $1.29$, rather than $1.14$.
- $$$\frac{180}{140} = \frac{9}{5} = 1.8$$
  - _why:_ The fraction has been simplified inconsistently, because numerator and denominator must be divided by the same number. Dividing both by $20$ gives $9/7 \approx 1.29$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** In back-to-back triangles meeting at point $C$ with $AB \parallel DE$, if $AB=8$ and $DE=12$, what is the scale factor from the small triangle to the large one?

**Back:** $$$\frac{12}{8} = 1.5$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{12}{8} = 1.5$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor from the small triangle to the large triangle is corresponding large length divided by small length. Therefore, $12 \div 8 = 1.5$.
- $$$\frac{8}{12} = \frac{2}{3}$$
  - _why:_ This division gives the scale factor from the large triangle to the small triangle, reversing the requested direction. Small to large requires $12/8 = 1.5$.
- $$$\frac{12}{8} = 2$$
  - _why:_ The correct corresponding lengths have been divided in the correct order, but the arithmetic is wrong. Since $12$ is one and a half times $8$, the factor is $1.5$.
- $$$\frac{12-8}{8} = 0.5$$
  - _why:_ This calculates the proportional increase of $0.5$, not the enlargement scale factor. The scale factor compares the complete lengths, giving $12/8 = 1.5$.
