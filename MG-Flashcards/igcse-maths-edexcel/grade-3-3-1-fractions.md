## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In $\frac{3}{5}$, 3 is the [ ___ ] and 5 is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In $\frac{3}{5}$, 3 is the numerator and 5 is the denominator.

**Choices:**

- In $\frac{3}{5}$, 3 is the numerator and 5 is the denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator is the number above the fraction line, so 3 is the numerator. The denominator is below the line, so 5 is the denominator.
- In $\frac{3}{5}$, 3 is the denominator and 5 is the numerator.
  - _why:_ The two labels have been reversed. The numerator is above the fraction line and the denominator is below it, so the correct order is numerator, then denominator.
- In $\frac{3}{5}$, 3 is the factor and 5 is the multiple.
  - _why:_ Factor and multiple describe relationships between numbers, not the positions in a fraction. The number above the line is the numerator and the number below is the denominator.
- In $\frac{3}{5}$, 3 is the divisor and 5 is the quotient.
  - _why:_ Divisor and quotient belong to division terminology and do not name the parts of a fraction. Here, 3 is the numerator and 5 is the denominator.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor apart from [ ___ ].

**Back:** A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor apart from 1.

**Choices:**

- 1 ✔
  - _why:_ The only positive common factor that every pair of integers can share without making the fraction reducible is 1. A fraction with no larger common factor is in simplest form.
- 0
  - _why:_ Zero is not a factor of non-zero numbers, because multiplying by zero always gives zero. The fraction is in simplest form when its only common factor is 1.
- 2
  - _why:_ A common factor of 2 would mean both terms can still be divided by 2, so the fraction is reducible. Simplest form allows no common factor apart from 1.
- the numerator
  - _why:_ The numerator is one part of a fraction, not the permitted common factor. The numerator and denominator must have no common factor other than 1.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{12}{18}$.

**Back:** $\frac{2}{3}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{3}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The highest common factor of 12 and 18 is 6. Dividing both numerator and denominator by 6 gives $\frac{12}{18}=\frac{2}{3}$ in simplest form.
- $\frac{2}{6}$
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator by 6 but the denominator by 3 uses different divisors, so the fraction is changed incorrectly. Dividing both by 6 gives $\frac{2}{3}$.
- $\frac{3}{2}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the simplified fraction, making a value greater than 1 even though $\frac{12}{18}$ is less than 1. Dividing both terms by 6 gives $\frac{2}{3}$.
- $\frac{6}{12}$
  - _why:_ This may result from subtracting 6 from both terms rather than dividing by their common factor. Fractions are simplified by dividing both terms by 6, giving $\frac{2}{3}$.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** To add or subtract fractions, make the [ ___ ] the same, then add/subtract the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To add or subtract fractions, make the denominators the same, then add/subtract the numerators.

**Choices:**

- To add or subtract fractions, make the denominators the same, then add/subtract the numerators. ✔
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions must first have a common denominator. Once the parts are the same size, add or subtract the numerators while keeping the common denominator.
- To add or subtract fractions, make the numerators the same, then add/subtract the denominators.
  - _why:_ The denominator determines the size of each part, so it must be made common before combining fractions. Then add or subtract the numerators, not the denominators.
- To add or subtract fractions, make the denominators the same, then add/subtract the denominators.
  - _why:_ Making the denominators equal is necessary, but the numerators count the parts being combined. Add or subtract those numerators and retain the common denominator.
- To add or subtract fractions, make the numerators the same, then add/subtract the numerators.
  - _why:_ Matching numerators does not make the fractional parts equal in size. The denominators must be made the same, after which the numerators are added or subtracted.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** A good common denominator is often the [ ___ ] of the denominators.

**Back:** A good common denominator is often the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

**Choices:**

- lowest common multiple ✔
  - _why:_ The lowest common multiple is the smallest positive number that each denominator divides into exactly. Using it usually keeps the equivalent fractions and calculations as simple as possible.
- highest common factor
  - _why:_ The highest common factor divides both denominators rather than being a shared multiple of them. A common denominator must be a multiple of each denominator, preferably the lowest common multiple.
- greatest common divisor
  - _why:_ The greatest common divisor is another name for the highest common factor, so it is generally used for simplifying, not finding a common denominator. The required term is lowest common multiple.
- product of the denominators
  - _why:_ Multiplying the denominators always produces a common multiple, but it may not be the smallest one. The usual efficient choice is the lowest common multiple.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{4}$ not $\frac{2}{7}$?

**Back:** The denominators must be made the same before adding.

**Choices:**

- The denominators must be made the same before adding. ✔
  - _why:_ Thirds and quarters represent different-sized parts, so their numerators cannot be added directly. Making the denominators equal first allows the fractions to be combined correctly.
- The numerators must be made the same before adding.
  - _why:_ Matching numerators does not make the fractional parts equal in size. The denominators must be made the same first, then the numerators can be added.
- The denominators can be added directly after adding the numerators.
  - _why:_ Adding denominators changes the unit size and is not the rule for fraction addition. Use a common denominator, giving $\frac{1}{3}=\frac{4}{12}$ and $\frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12}$.
- The fractions should be multiplied before adding.
  - _why:_ Multiplication is not needed to combine these fractions and would produce a different operation. The denominators must be made equal before adding the numerators.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5}$.

**Back:** $\frac{3}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Both fractions already have denominator 5, so add their numerators: $1+2=3$. The denominator stays 5, giving $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{10}$
  - _why:_ The denominator has been doubled even though the fractions already have a common denominator. Add the numerators only, so $1+2$ gives $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{2}{5}$
  - _why:_ This misses one of the fifths by effectively using only one numerator. Since $1+2=3$ and the denominator remains 5, the answer is $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{1}{5}$
  - _why:_ This keeps only the first numerator instead of combining both fractions. With equal denominators, add $1+2$ while keeping 5, giving $\frac{3}{5}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{1}{8}+\frac{3}{4}$.

**Back:** $\frac{7}{8}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{7}{8}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Convert $\frac{3}{4}$ to $\frac{6}{8}$. Then add the numerators: $\frac{1}{8}+\frac{6}{8}=\frac{7}{8}$.
- $\frac{4}{8}$
  - _why:_ This treats $\frac{3}{4}$ as though it were $\frac{3}{8}$ or adds incorrectly after changing the denominator. Since $\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{8}$, the answer is $\frac{7}{8}$.
- $\frac{5}{8}$
  - _why:_ This usually comes from adding 1 and 3 after changing only the denominator, rather than doubling the numerator as well. The equivalent fraction is $\frac{6}{8}$, giving $\frac{7}{8}$.
- $\frac{7}{12}$
  - _why:_ Using 12 as a common denominator without converting both fractions correctly leads to this result. Using eighths is simpler: $\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{8}$, so the sum is $\frac{7}{8}$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4}$.

**Back:** $\frac{7}{12}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{7}{12}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The lowest common denominator is 12, so $\frac{5}{6}=\frac{10}{12}$ and $\frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12}$. Subtracting gives $\frac{10}{12}-\frac{3}{12}=\frac{7}{12}$.
- $\frac{2}{5}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the numerators and adding the denominators is not a valid fraction rule. Using a common denominator gives $\frac{10}{12}-\frac{3}{12}=\frac{7}{12}$.
- $\frac{3}{8}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the numerators and denominators separately does not preserve the fractions’ values. Convert both fractions to twelfths first, giving the correct result $\frac{7}{12}$.
- $\frac{4}{12}$
  - _why:_ This subtracts the original numerators after choosing 12 as the denominator, without converting them. The equivalent numerators are 10 and 3, so the answer is $\frac{7}{12}$.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** To multiply fractions, multiply the [ ___ ] together and the [ ___ ] together.

**Back:** To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

**Choices:**

- To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. ✔
  - _why:_ Fraction multiplication combines the top numbers to form the new numerator and the bottom numbers to form the new denominator. Any common factors can then be cancelled.
- To multiply fractions, multiply the denominators together and the numerators together.
  - _why:_ This reverses the positions of the resulting numerator and denominator, producing the reciprocal of the intended product. Numerators multiply together, and denominators multiply together.
- To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the numerators together.
  - _why:_ Using the numerators for both parts ignores the original denominators and changes the fractions’ values. Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.
- To multiply fractions, multiply the denominators together and the denominators together.
  - _why:_ Using the denominators for both parts ignores the original numerators, so it cannot give the product. Multiply the numerators together for the top and denominators for the bottom.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{9}{10}$.

**Back:** $\frac{3}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying gives $\frac{2\times9}{3\times10}=\frac{18}{30}$. Dividing the numerator and denominator by 6 simplifies this to $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{10}$
  - _why:_ This results from cancelling factors without applying the cancellation to both relevant parts correctly. The product is $\frac{18}{30}$, which simplifies to $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{6}{5}$
  - _why:_ This effectively introduces an unnecessary reciprocal, a rule used when dividing fractions rather than multiplying them. Direct multiplication and simplification give $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{11}{13}$
  - _why:_ Adding the numerators and denominators is not the rule for multiplying fractions. Multiply corresponding parts to get $\frac{18}{30}$, then simplify to $\frac{3}{5}$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Can you cancel in $\frac{3}{4}+\frac{8}{9}$?

**Back:** No - cancelling is for multiplication.

**Choices:**

- No - cancelling is for multiplication. ✔
  - _why:_ Cancellation divides factors that are multiplied in a numerator and denominator. Because these fractions are being added, they must first be rewritten using a common denominator.
- Yes - cancel any common factors.
  - _why:_ Factors cannot be cancelled across an addition sign because the terms are not being multiplied. Use a common denominator to add the fractions; cancellation applies only to multiplied factors.
- No - cancelling is for subtraction.
  - _why:_ Cancellation is not a subtraction rule; it simplifies common factors joined by multiplication. These fractions cannot be cancelled because they are being added.
- Yes - cancel before finding a denominator.
  - _why:_ Cancelling across the addition sign would change the value of the expression. Find a common denominator first, then add; only common factors within multiplied parts may be cancelled.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** To divide by a fraction, multiply by its [ ___ ].

**Back:** To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

**Choices:**

- reciprocal ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal. The reciprocal is formed by interchanging the fraction’s numerator and denominator.
- denominator
  - _why:_ Multiplying only by the denominator ignores the numerator of the divisor. Division by a fraction requires multiplying by its reciprocal, which swaps its numerator and denominator.
- numerator
  - _why:_ Multiplying only by the numerator does not account for the whole divisor. The correct method is to multiply by the fraction’s reciprocal.
- opposite
  - _why:_ The opposite changes a number’s sign and is associated with addition, not division. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the reciprocal of $\frac{5}{8}$?

**Back:** $\frac{8}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{8}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A reciprocal is found by interchanging the numerator and denominator. Flipping $\frac{5}{8}$ therefore gives $\frac{8}{5}$.
- $\frac{5}{8}$
  - _why:_ Leaving the fraction unchanged does not produce its reciprocal. The numerator and denominator must swap places, so the reciprocal is $\frac{8}{5}$.
- $-\frac{8}{5}$
  - _why:_ Taking a reciprocal does not change the sign of a positive number. Swap the numerator and denominator only, giving $\frac{8}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{8}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the numerator from the denominator finds neither a reciprocal nor an equivalent fraction. A reciprocal swaps the two numbers, giving $\frac{8}{5}$.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** In keep, change, flip: change divide to [ ___ ] and flip the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In keep, change, flip: change divide to multiply and flip the second fraction.

**Choices:**

- In keep, change, flip: change divide to multiply and flip the second fraction. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal. Therefore, keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, and flip the second fraction.
- In keep, change, flip: change divide to add and flip the second fraction.
  - _why:_ Changing division to addition does not create an equivalent calculation. Division must change to multiplication, while the second fraction is replaced by its reciprocal.
- In keep, change, flip: change divide to multiply and flip the first fraction.
  - _why:_ Flipping the first fraction changes the dividend rather than replacing the divisor with its reciprocal. Change division to multiplication and flip only the second fraction.
- In keep, change, flip: change divide to subtract and flip the both fractions.
  - _why:_ Division does not become subtraction, and flipping both fractions changes both numbers unnecessarily. It becomes multiplication, with only the second fraction flipped.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What is division by zero?

**Back:** Undefined

**Choices:**

- Undefined ✔
  - _why:_ No number multiplied by zero can recover a non-zero dividend, so division by zero has no defined value in ordinary arithmetic.
- Zero
  - _why:_ Zero is the result when zero is divided by a non-zero number. Dividing a number by zero is undefined because no valid quotient exists.
- Infinity
  - _why:_ Infinity describes unbounded growth, but it is not a number that can be assigned as the result. Division by zero is undefined.
- Indeterminate
  - _why:_ Division by zero is not automatically indeterminate. In ordinary arithmetic it is undefined; indeterminate forms arise in limits, such as zero divided by zero.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What type of fraction is $\frac{7}{4}$?

**Back:** An improper fraction.

**Choices:**

- An improper fraction. ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator, 7, is greater than the denominator, 4. A fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator is improper.
- A proper fraction.
  - _why:_ A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator. Since 7 is greater than 4, the fraction is an improper fraction.
- A mixed number.
  - _why:_ A mixed number combines a whole number with a proper fraction, such as $1\frac{3}{4}$. The given single fraction is an improper fraction.
- An equivalent fraction.
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions have the same value but different numerators and denominators. The requested classification is improper because the numerator exceeds the denominator.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Back:** Multiply the whole number by the denominator.Add the numerator.Keep the same denominator.

**Choices:**

- Multiply the whole number by the denominator.Add the numerator.Keep the same denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying the whole number by the denominator and adding the numerator gives the new numerator. The denominator remains unchanged because the size of each fractional part stays the same.
- Multiply the whole number by the numerator.Add the denominator.Keep the same numerator.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the numerator reverses the roles of the fraction’s parts. Multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and retain that denominator.
- Add the whole number to the denominator.Multiply by the numerator.Keep the same denominator.
  - _why:_ Adding the denominator to the whole number does not count how many fractional parts the whole numbers contain. Multiply by the denominator before adding the numerator.
- Multiply the whole number by the denominator.Add the numerator.Add one to the denominator.
  - _why:_ Changing the denominator alters the size of the fractional parts and is unnecessary. The new numerator is found by multiplying the whole number by the denominator, then adding the numerator.

**Back:** $\frac{13}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{13}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying 2 by 5 gives 10, and adding 3 gives the new numerator, 13. The denominator remains 5, producing $\frac{13}{5}$.
- $\frac{5}{5}$
  - _why:_ Adding 2 and 3 ignores that each whole contains five fifths. Calculate $2\times5+3=13$ for the numerator and keep the denominator 5.

  - _why:_ Adding the whole number to the denominator incorrectly changes the size of the fractional parts. The denominator stays 5 and the correct numerator is 13.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Before multiplying or dividing with a mixed number, what should you do?

**Back:** Change it to an improper fraction.

**Choices:**

- Change it to an improper fraction. ✔
  - _why:_ Converting a mixed number to an improper fraction makes it possible to multiply numerators and denominators directly, or to apply the reciprocal correctly when dividing.
- Change it to a proper fraction.
  - _why:_ A proper fraction cannot represent a mixed number’s value when that value exceeds one. It should first be converted to an improper fraction.
- Change it to a terminating decimal.
  - _why:_ Converting to a decimal may introduce rounding and is unnecessary for fraction operations. The standard exact method is to change the mixed number to an improper fraction.
- Change it to an equivalent mixed number.
  - _why:_ Finding an equivalent mixed number does not put the value into a form suitable for direct multiplication or division. Convert it to an improper fraction instead.

- $1\frac{1}{20}$
  - _why:_ Inverting the second fraction changes the multiplication into division. Multiply the improper fractions directly: $\frac{7}{5}\times\frac{4}{3}=\frac{28}{15}$, which simplifies to $1\frac{13}{15}$.
