## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the reciprocal of 8?

**Back:** $$\frac{1}{8}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{1}{8}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The reciprocal of a non-zero number is found by dividing 1 by that number. Therefore, the reciprocal of $8$ is $\frac{1}{8}$.
- $$\frac{8}{1}$
  - _why:_ Writing $8$ as a fraction over 1 does not invert it. Swapping the numerator and denominator gives the reciprocal $\frac{1}{8}$.
- $$\frac{1}{64}$
  - _why:_ This comes from squaring 8 rather than finding its reciprocal. A reciprocal multiplies by the original number to make 1, so it is $\frac{1}{8}$.
- $$-\frac{1}{8}$
  - _why:_ Finding a reciprocal does not change the sign of a positive number. Since $8 \times \frac{1}{8}=1$, the reciprocal is $\frac{1}{8}$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the reciprocal of $\frac{4}{9}$?

**Back:** $$\frac{9}{4}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{9}{4}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The reciprocal of a fraction is found by interchanging its numerator and denominator. Reversing $\frac{4}{9}$ gives $\frac{9}{4}$.
- $$\frac{4}{9}$
  - _why:_ Leaving the fraction unchanged does not produce its reciprocal. The numerator and denominator must be interchanged, giving $\frac{9}{4}$.
- $$-\frac{9}{4}$
  - _why:_ Taking a reciprocal does not introduce a negative sign. Interchanging the numerator and denominator of $\frac{4}{9}$ gives the positive fraction $\frac{9}{4}$.
- $$\frac{9}{8}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly doubles the original numerator instead of reversing the fraction. A reciprocal swaps 4 and 9, so the correct result is $\frac{9}{4}$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What operation does a negative index indicate?

**Back:** Taking the reciprocal of the base.

**Choices:**

- Taking the reciprocal of the base. ✔
  - _why:_ A negative index means the corresponding positive power is moved to the denominator. Equivalently, the reciprocal of the base is taken before applying the positive index.
- Taking the negative of the base.
  - _why:_ A negative index does not make the base negative; it indicates a reciprocal. For example, $a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}$ for any non-zero $a$.
- Multiplying the base by the index.
  - _why:_ Indices represent repeated multiplication or related roots, not multiplication by the exponent. A negative index specifically indicates taking the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power.
- Subtracting the index from the base.
  - _why:_ An exponent is not subtracted from its base. A negative index means taking the reciprocal, so $a^{-n}$ is equal to $\frac{1}{a^n}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Evaluate $5^{-2}$.

**Back:** $$\frac{1}{25}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{1}{25}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The negative index gives the reciprocal of the positive power: $5^{-2}=\frac{1}{5^2}$. Since $5^2=25$, the result is $\frac{1}{25}$.
- $$\frac{1}{10}$
  - _why:_ This treats the exponent as multiplication by 2 rather than a power. The correct calculation is $5^{-2}=\frac{1}{5^2}=\frac{1}{25}$.
- $$-\frac{1}{25}$
  - _why:_ A negative exponent creates a reciprocal; it does not make the value negative. Since $5^2=25$, the correct value is the positive fraction $\frac{1}{25}$.
- $$25$
  - _why:_ This evaluates $5^2$ but ignores the negative sign on the index. The negative index requires the reciprocal, so $5^{-2}=\frac{1}{25}$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** For any non-zero base $a$, the negative index rule is $a^{-n} = \text{[...]}.

**Back:** For any non-zero base $a$, the negative index rule is $a^{-n} = \boxed{\frac{1}{a^n}}.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{1}{a^n}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A negative index means taking the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power. Thus, for non-zero $a$, $a^{-n}$ is equal to $\frac{1}{a^n}$.
- $$-a^n$
  - _why:_ A negative index affects the position of the power, not its sign. The base’s positive power belongs in the denominator, giving $a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}$.
- $$\frac{1}{a^{-n}}$
  - _why:_ This takes the reciprocal while incorrectly keeping the exponent negative, producing $a^n$. The negative index rule instead gives $a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}$.
- $$\frac{-1}{a^n}$
  - _why:_ The minus sign in the index does not make the fraction negative. It indicates a reciprocal, so the numerator is positive and the result is $\frac{1}{a^n}$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** The fractional index $a^{\frac{1}{n}}$ is equivalent to the radical expression $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The fractional index $a^{\frac{1}{n}}$ is equivalent to the radical expression $\boxed{\sqrt[n]{a}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\sqrt[n]{a}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A denominator of $n$ in a fractional index indicates an $n$th root. Therefore, $a^{\frac{1}{n}}$ is equivalent to $\sqrt[n]{a}$.
- $$n\sqrt{a}$
  - _why:_ The denominator $n$ specifies the order of the root; it is not a coefficient multiplying a square root. The equivalent expression is $\sqrt[n]{a}$.
- $$\sqrt{a^n}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats $n$ as a power inside a square root. In $a^{\frac{1}{n}}$, the denominator specifies an $n$th root, giving $\sqrt[n]{a}$.
- $$\frac{1}{\sqrt[n]{a}}$
  - _why:_ A reciprocal would require a negative fractional index, such as $a^{-\frac{1}{n}}$. Since the index is positive, the correct expression is $\sqrt[n]{a}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In the fractional index $a^{\frac{m}{n}}$, what do the numerator and denominator represent?

**Back:** Denominator ($n$): rootNumerator ($m$): power

**Choices:**

- Denominator ($n$): rootNumerator ($m$): power ✔
  - _why:_ In $a^{\frac{m}{n}}$, the denominator gives the order of the root and the numerator gives the power: $a^{\frac{m}{n}}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
- Denominator ($n$): powerNumerator ($m$): root
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the numerator and denominator. In a fractional index, $n$ specifies the root and $m$ specifies the power.
- Denominator ($n$): rootNumerator ($m$): coefficient
  - _why:_ The numerator is not a multiplier placed before the radical. It specifies the power, while the denominator specifies the root, as in $\sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
- Denominator ($n$): reciprocalNumerator ($m$): power
  - _why:_ A denominator in a fractional index indicates a root, not a reciprocal; reciprocals are associated with negative indices. The numerator still represents the power.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $144^{\frac{1}{2}}$ not equal to 72?

**Back:** The index $\frac{1}{2}$ means square root ($\sqrt{144} = 12$), not dividing by 2.

**Choices:**

- The index $\frac{1}{2}$ means square root ($\sqrt{144} = 12$), not dividing by 2. ✔
  - _why:_ A denominator of 2 in a fractional index represents a square root. Therefore $144^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{144}=12$, whereas dividing 144 by 2 gives 72.
- The index $\frac{1}{2}$ means divide the square root by 2 ($\sqrt{144} \div 2 = 6$), not dividing 144 by 2.
  - _why:_ The denominator of the index specifies the root; it is not an additional divisor. Thus $144^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{144}=12$, with no subsequent division by 2.
- The index $\frac{1}{2}$ means square then divide ($144^2 \div 2 = 10368$), not dividing 144 by 2.
  - _why:_ A fractional index does not instruct you to square the base and divide. The denominator 2 means take the square root, so $144^{\frac{1}{2}}=12$.
- The index $\frac{1}{2}$ means halve the square root ($\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{144} = 6$), not dividing 144 by 2.
  - _why:_ Treating the index as a coefficient incorrectly multiplies the square root by one-half. It represents the square-root operation itself, giving $144^{\frac{1}{2}}=12$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** The combined rule for negative fractional indices is $a^{-\frac{m}{n}} = \text{[...]}.

**Back:** The combined rule for negative fractional indices is $a^{-\frac{m}{n}} = \boxed{\frac{1}{(\sqrt[n]{a})^m}}.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{1}{(\sqrt[n]{a})^m}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The fraction $\frac{m}{n}$ means take the $n$th root and raise it to the power $m$, while the negative sign takes the reciprocal.
- $$\frac{1}{(\sqrt[m]{a})^n}$
  - _why:_ This swaps the numerator and denominator roles. In $a^{-\frac{m}{n}}$, $n$ determines the root and $m$ determines the power, so the result is $\frac{1}{(\sqrt[n]{a})^m}$.
- $$(\sqrt[n]{a})^m$
  - _why:_ This handles the fractional index but ignores the negative sign. A negative index requires the reciprocal, so the correct expression is $\frac{1}{(\sqrt[n]{a})^m}$.
- $$\frac{1}{\sqrt[n]{a^n}}$
  - _why:_ This replaces the numerator $m$ with $n$, causing the root and power to cancel. The correct rule is $a^{-\frac{m}{n}}=\frac{1}{(\sqrt[n]{a})^m}$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the most efficient first step to evaluate $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n}$?

**Back:** Invert the base to $\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$ to remove the negative index.

**Choices:**

- Invert the base to $\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$ to remove the negative index. ✔
  - _why:_ A negative index means take the reciprocal of the base. Inverting $\frac{a}{b}$ changes the exponent from $-n$ to $n$, giving $\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$.
- Keep the base as $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n$ and simply remove the negative index.
  - _why:_ Removing a negative sign without taking a reciprocal changes the value. The base must be inverted, so $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n}=\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$.
- Invert the index to $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}$ to remove the negative index.
  - _why:_ A negative exponent does not become a reciprocal exponent; that would introduce an $n$th root. Instead, invert the base to obtain $\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$.
- Negate the base to $\left(-\frac{a}{b}\right)^n$ to remove the negative index.
  - _why:_ The negative sign belongs to the exponent and does not make the base negative. It requires taking the reciprocal, giving $\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** The index law for a power of a power is $(a^p)^q = \text{[...]}.

**Back:** The index law for a power of a power is $(a^p)^q = \boxed{a^{pq}}.

**Choices:**

- $$a^{pq}$ ✔
  - _why:_ When a power is raised to another power, the exponents multiply. Therefore $(a^p)^q=a^{p\times q}=a^{pq}$.
- $$a^{p+q}$
  - _why:_ Adding exponents applies when multiplying powers with the same base, not when raising a power to another power. Here the exponents multiply, giving $a^{pq}$.
- $$a^{p-q}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting exponents applies when dividing powers with the same base. A power raised to another power requires multiplication, so $(a^p)^q=a^{pq}$.
- $$a^{\frac{p}{q}}$
  - _why:_ Dividing exponents is associated with reversing a power operation, not applying another power. For $(a^p)^q$, multiply the exponents to obtain $a^{pq}$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the general strategy for finding an unknown index $n$ in an equation like $2 \times \sqrt{32} = 2^n$?

**Back:** Rewrite both sides using the same base and equate the indices.

**Choices:**

- Rewrite both sides using the same base and equate the indices. ✔
  - _why:_ Powers with the same base are equal only when their indices are equal, so expressing both sides as powers of 2 allows $n$ to be found directly.
- Rewrite both sides using the same index and equate the bases.
  - _why:_ Matching indices does not provide a valid general method for solving this equation. Both sides should be rewritten with the same base, after which their indices can be equated.
- Rewrite both sides using different bases and multiply the indices.
  - _why:_ Indices cannot be compared directly when the bases differ, and multiplication is not the relevant index operation. Use the same base on both sides and equate the indices.
- Evaluate both sides using decimal approximations and round the indices.
  - _why:_ Rounding decimal approximations can produce an inaccurate index and is unnecessary here. Rewriting both sides with the same base gives an exact result by equating the indices.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $0^{-1}$ undefined?

**Back:** It requires division by zero ($\frac{1}{0}$), which is impossible.

**Choices:**

- It requires division by zero ($\frac{1}{0}$), which is impossible. ✔
  - _why:_ A negative index means taking a reciprocal, so $0^{-1}=\frac{1}{0}$. Division by zero has no defined value, making the expression undefined.
- It requires multiplication by zero ($0 \times 1$), which is impossible.
  - _why:_ Multiplication by zero is valid and always gives zero, so that is not the problem. A negative index creates the reciprocal $\frac{1}{0}$, which is undefined.
- It requires taking a square root of zero ($\sqrt{0}$), which is impossible.
  - _why:_ The square root of zero is defined and equals zero. The index $-1$ instead means taking a reciprocal, producing $\frac{1}{0}$, which is undefined.
- It gives a negative result ($-1$), which is not permitted.
  - _why:_ A negative index does not make the value negative; it indicates a reciprocal. Thus $0^{-1}$ would be $\frac{1}{0}$, and division by zero is undefined.
