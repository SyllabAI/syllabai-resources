## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step when calculating the median or quartiles from raw data?

**Back:** Put the values in order (smallest to largest).

**Choices:**

- Put the values in order (smallest to largest). ✔
  - _why:_ Ordering the values reveals their positions in the data set, allowing the middle value and the points one-quarter and three-quarters through the data to be identified.
- Add the values to find the total of the data set.
  - _why:_ Adding the values is a step used when calculating the mean, not the median or quartiles. The values must first be ordered from smallest to largest.
- Subtract the smallest value from the largest value.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the extremes calculates the range, which measures spread. To calculate the median or quartiles, first arrange all values from smallest to largest.
- Group the values into equal intervals of the same width.
  - _why:_ Grouping data into intervals can help create a grouped frequency table, but it is unnecessary for raw data. First put the individual values in ascending order.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The interquartile range (IQR) is calculated using the formula: $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The interquartile range (IQR) is calculated using the formula: $\boxed{Q_3 - Q_1}$.

**Choices:**

- $$Q_3 - Q_1$ ✔
  - _why:_ The IQR is the difference between the upper quartile and lower quartile, so subtracting $Q_1$ from $Q_3$ gives the spread of the middle half.
- $$Q_3 + Q_1$
  - _why:_ Adding the quartiles does not measure the distance between them. The IQR is found by subtracting the lower quartile from the upper quartile: $Q_3 - Q_1$.
- $$Q_1 - Q_3$
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and normally produces a negative value, although spread cannot be negative. The correct order is upper quartile minus lower quartile: $Q_3 - Q_1$.
- $$\frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}$
  - _why:_ Dividing the difference by two gives half the interquartile range, not the IQR itself. The complete distance between the quartiles is $Q_3 - Q_1$.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The IQR measures the spread of the [ ___ ] of the data.

**Back:** The IQR measures the spread of the middle half of the data.

**Choices:**

- middle half ✔
  - _why:_ The lower and upper quartiles enclose the central 50% of the ordered values. Therefore, the IQR measures the spread of the middle half of the data.
- middle quarter
  - _why:_ The interval from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$ contains 50%, not 25%, of the ordered data. Therefore, the IQR measures the spread of the middle half.
- outer half
  - _why:_ The outer half lies outside the quartiles and is not measured by their difference. The IQR measures the spread of the middle half between $Q_1$ and $Q_3$.
- whole set
  - _why:_ The range measures the spread of the whole data set using its maximum and minimum. The IQR instead measures the spread of the middle half.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a smaller IQR indicate about a data set?

**Back:** The results are more consistent (less spread out).

**Choices:**

- The results are more consistent (less spread out). ✔
  - _why:_ A smaller IQR means the middle half of the values occupies a narrower interval, so those results show less variation and are therefore more consistent.
- The results are less consistent (more spread out).
  - _why:_ This describes a larger IQR, where the middle half covers a wider interval. A smaller IQR means the results are more consistent and less spread out.
- The results are more accurate (closer to the true value).
  - _why:_ IQR measures consistency rather than accuracy, so it cannot show closeness to a true value. A smaller IQR indicates that the results are more consistent.
- The results have a higher median (greater typical value).
  - _why:_ IQR describes spread and does not determine whether the median is high or low. A smaller IQR means the results are more consistent and less spread out.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the median calculated if there are two middle values?

**Back:** Find the mean of the two middle values.

**Choices:**

- Find the mean of the two middle values. ✔
  - _why:_ With an even number of ordered values, neither middle value alone is central. Adding the two middle values and dividing by two gives the median.
- Find the range of the two middle values.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the middle values gives the distance between them, not their central value. The median is the mean of the two middle values.
- Choose the lower of the two middle values.
  - _why:_ Selecting the lower middle value ignores the upper one and places the median too low. The correct median is the mean of both middle values.
- Choose the higher of the two middle values.
  - _why:_ Selecting the higher middle value ignores the lower one and places the median too high. The correct median is the mean of both middle values.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In a set of 11 ordered values, which value is the median?

**Back:** The 6th value.

**Choices:**

- The 6th value. ✔
  - _why:_ Five ordered values lie below the 6th and five lie above it, so the 6th value is exactly in the middle of the 11 values.
- The 5th value.
  - _why:_ The 5th value has four values below it and six above it, so it is not central. The 6th value is the median of 11 values.
- The 7th value.
  - _why:_ The 7th value has six values below it and four above it, so it is not central. The 6th value is the median of 11 values.
- The 11th value.
  - _why:_ The 11th value is the largest value in an ordered set, not the middle one. With 11 values, the median is the 6th value.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find $Q_1$ from 11 ordered values, you find the middle of the [ ___ ] values below the median.

**Back:** To find $Q_1$ from 11 ordered values, you find the middle of the 5 values below the median.

**Choices:**

- 5 ✔
  - _why:_ The 6th value is the median, leaving five values below it. The middle of those five lower values gives the lower quartile, $Q_1$.
- 6
  - _why:_ Counting six values incorrectly includes the median in the lower half. The median is excluded, leaving five values below it for finding $Q_1$.
- 10
  - _why:_ Ten is the number of values remaining after excluding the median from the entire set, not the lower half. Only five values lie below the median.
- 11
  - _why:_ Using all 11 values would locate the overall median again rather than the lower quartile. For $Q_1$, use only the five values below the median.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** When comparing two groups, what two properties should you comment on?

**Back:** The typical score (usually using the median).The consistency/spread (usually using the IQR).

**Choices:**

- The typical score (usually using the median).The consistency/spread (usually using the IQR). ✔
  - _why:_ A useful comparison describes both where each group is centred and how variable it is. The median compares typical scores, while the IQR compares consistency or spread.
- The highest score (usually using the maximum).The lowest score (usually using the minimum).
  - _why:_ The maximum and minimum only describe the extremes and may be affected by unusual values. Groups should be compared using a typical score and a measure of consistency or spread.
- The total score (usually using the sum).The group size (usually using the frequency).
  - _why:_ Totals depend heavily on the number of observations and do not directly compare distributions. Comment instead on the typical score using the median and spread using the IQR.
- The lowest quartile (usually using $Q_1$).The highest quartile (usually using $Q_3$).
  - _why:_ Quoting the quartiles separately misses the intended comparison of centre and spread. Use the median for the typical score and $Q_3-Q_1$ for consistency or spread.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you interpret a higher median when comparing two classes?

**Back:** The group with the higher median has a higher typical score.

**Choices:**

- The group with the higher median has a higher typical score. ✔
  - _why:_ The median is the middle value when scores are ordered, so a higher median indicates that the centre, or typical score, is higher for that group.
- The group with the higher median has a higher maximum score.
  - _why:_ The median describes the middle of the ordered scores, not the greatest score. The correct interpretation is that the group has a higher typical score.
- The group with the higher median has a larger spread of scores.
  - _why:_ Spread is measured using quantities such as the range or IQR, not the median. A higher median means the group has a higher typical score.
- The group with the higher median has a more consistent set of scores.
  - _why:_ Consistency concerns how closely scores are grouped and is assessed using spread. A higher median instead indicates that the group has a higher typical score.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the total of a data set is: $\text{total} = \text{[...]} \times \text{number of values}$.

**Back:** The formula for the total of a data set is: $\text{total} = \boxed{\text{mean}} \times \text{number of values}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\text{mean}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The mean equals the total divided by the number of values. Rearranging this relationship gives total equals mean multiplied by the number of values.
- $$\text{median}$
  - _why:_ The median identifies the middle ordered value and cannot generally be used to calculate the total. The total is found by multiplying the mean by the number of values.
- $$\text{range}$
  - _why:_ The range measures spread by subtracting the lowest value from the highest. The total is instead calculated by multiplying the mean by the number of values.
- $$\text{mode}$
  - _why:_ The mode is the most frequent value and does not determine the total of a data set. The required quantity in this formula is the mean.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the total of a data set is: $\text{total} = \text{mean} \times \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The formula for the total of a data set is: $\text{total} = \text{mean} \times \boxed{\text{number of values}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\text{number of values}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying the mean by the number of values reverses the calculation used to find the mean, producing the total of all values in the data set.
- $$\text{range of values}$
  - _why:_ The range measures the distance between the highest and lowest values, so multiplying by it does not recover the total. The mean must be multiplied by the number of values.
- $$\text{number of groups}$
  - _why:_ Counting groups rather than individual data values ignores how many observations each group contains. The total equals the mean multiplied by the number of values.
- $$\text{sum of values}$
  - _why:_ The sum of the values is already the total, so multiplying the mean by it is circular. The required multiplier is the number of values.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can you not simply average two class means to find the combined mean?

**Back:** Classes may have different numbers of students (different weights).

**Choices:**

- Classes may have different numbers of students (different weights). ✔
  - _why:_ A larger class contributes more scores to the combined data, so its mean must receive greater weight. Simply averaging the two means only works when class sizes are equal.
- Classes may have different ranges of scores (different spreads).
  - _why:_ Different ranges do not prevent class means from being combined directly when class sizes are equal. The real problem is that unequal student numbers give the means different weights.
- Classes may have different middle scores (different medians).
  - _why:_ The medians are irrelevant when calculating a combined mean. Simple averaging can fail because the classes may contain different numbers of students and therefore require different weights.
- Classes may have different highest scores (different maxima).
  - _why:_ Different maximum scores do not determine how class means should be weighted. The key issue is that unequal class sizes contribute different numbers of values to the combined total.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the steps to find a missing group's mean when given the combined mean and the other group's mean.

**Back:** Find the overall total (mean &times; total n).Subtract the known group's total.Divide the remaining total by the missing group's n.

**Choices:**

- Find the overall total (mean &times; total n).Subtract the known group's total.Divide the remaining total by the missing group's n. ✔
  - _why:_ Converting both means into totals allows the known group’s contribution to be removed. Dividing the remaining total by the missing group’s size then gives its mean.
- Find the overall total (mean &times; total n).Subtract the known group's mean.Divide the remaining total by the missing group's n.
  - _why:_ This subtracts a mean from a total, mixing incompatible quantities. First calculate the known group’s total, subtract that from the overall total, then divide by the missing group’s size.
- Find the overall total (mean &times; total n).Subtract the known group's total.Divide the remaining total by the total n.
  - _why:_ After subtraction, the remaining total belongs only to the missing group. Dividing by the combined number of students understates its mean; divide by the missing group’s n instead.
- Find the overall total (mean &times; number of groups).Subtract the known group's total.Divide the remaining total by the missing group's n.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the number of groups does not recover the overall total. Multiply the combined mean by the total number of students before subtracting the known group’s total.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** The highest value in a data set can be found using: $\text{highest value} = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The highest value in a data set can be found using: $\text{highest value} = \boxed{\text{lowest value} + \text{range}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\text{lowest value} + \text{range}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The range equals the highest value minus the lowest value. Rearranging this equation shows that the highest value equals the lowest value plus the range.
- $$\text{lowest value} - \text{range}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the range moves below the lowest value rather than up to the highest. The highest value is found by adding the range to the lowest value.
- $$\text{range} - \text{lowest value}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly reverses the terms from the range formula and does not isolate the highest value. The correct rearrangement is lowest value plus range.
- $$\text{lowest value} + \text{IQR}$
  - _why:_ The IQR covers only the middle 50% of the data, so it cannot locate the maximum. Add the full range to the lowest value instead.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the range for two groups combined?

**Back:** Subtract the overall lowest value from the overall highest value across both groups.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the overall lowest value from the overall highest value across both groups. ✔
  - _why:_ Once the groups are combined, the range uses the smallest and largest values in the entire combined data set, regardless of which group contains them.
- Subtract the lowest group range from the highest group range across both groups.
  - _why:_ Comparing the two separate ranges does not give the combined range. Identify the overall highest and overall lowest individual values, then subtract the latter from the former.
- Add the first group's range to the second group's range across both groups.
  - _why:_ Adding separate ranges double-counts or ignores gaps and overlaps between the groups. The combined range is the overall highest value minus the overall lowest value.
- Subtract the overall highest value from the overall lowest value across both groups.
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and usually produces a negative result. A range is non-negative, so subtract the overall lowest value from the overall highest value.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Does a larger IQR mean a group performed better? Explain.

**Back:** No; it only means the scores were more spread out (less consistent).

**Choices:**

- No; it only means the scores were more spread out (less consistent). ✔
  - _why:_ The IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of scores, not their level. A larger IQR therefore indicates greater variation and less consistency.
- Yes; it means the scores had a higher typical value (better performance).
  - _why:_ A higher typical value would be shown by a higher median, not a larger IQR. The IQR measures spread, so a larger value means less consistent scores.
- Yes; it means the scores had a higher maximum value (best performance).
  - _why:_ The IQR does not identify the maximum because it uses the middle 50% of the data. A larger IQR only shows that these scores are more spread out.
- No; it only means the scores were more closely grouped (more consistent).
  - _why:_ This reverses the interpretation of spread. A smaller IQR indicates more closely grouped, consistent scores, while a larger IQR indicates that the middle scores are more spread out.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Back:** In comparison questions, the word typical should be used for medians and the word consistent for IQR.

**Choices:**

- typical ✔
  - _why:_ The median is a measure of central tendency, so it describes a typical value for each data set rather than how spread out the values are.

  - _why:_ Consistency concerns spread and is judged using the IQR, not the median. The correct word for comparing medians is typical because the median represents a central value.
- variable
  - _why:_ Variability describes how spread out data are, which is assessed using the IQR. The correct word is typical because a median indicates the centre of an ordered data set.
- reliable
  - _why:_ Reliability is not the standard statistical interpretation of a median. The correct word is typical because medians compare the central or representative values of data sets.

**Choices:**

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Define the Range.

**Back:** The highest value minus the lowest value.

**Choices:**

- The highest value minus the lowest value. ✔
  - _why:_ The range measures the full spread of a data set by calculating the difference between its maximum and minimum values.
- The highest value plus the lowest value.
  - _why:_ Adding the extreme values does not measure the distance between them. The range is calculated by subtracting the lowest value from the highest value.
- The lowest value minus the highest value.
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction usually produces a negative answer, but the range is a non-negative measure of spread. Subtract the lowest value from the highest value.
- The second-highest value minus the second-lowest value.
  - _why:_ Ignoring the two extreme observations does not give the range. The range uses the highest and lowest values, subtracting the lowest from the highest.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Back:** When calculating combined means, groups with more people have a greater effect on the final result.

**Choices:**

- more people ✔
  - _why:_ A combined mean is a weighted mean, so a group containing more people contributes more individual values and therefore has greater influence on the final result.
- higher means
  - _why:_ A high group mean may change the result's direction, but it does not determine the group's weighting. The correct fill is more people because weighting depends on group size.
- larger ranges
  - _why:_ The range measures spread and does not determine a group's weight in a combined mean. Groups with more people have greater influence because they contribute more values.
- more extreme values
  - _why:_ Extreme values can affect a group's own mean, but the group's weight in the combined calculation depends on its size. The correct fill is more people.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** If Group A has an IQR of 15 and Group B has an IQR of 8, which group is more consistent?

**Back:** Group B (smaller IQR).

**Choices:**

- Group B (smaller IQR). ✔
  - _why:_ A smaller IQR means the middle 50% of the data is less spread out. Since $8<15$, Group B is more consistent.
- Group A (larger IQR).
  - _why:_ A larger IQR indicates greater spread, not greater consistency. Since Group B has the smaller IQR of 8, its middle 50% is more closely grouped.
- Both groups (similar IQRs).
  - _why:_ The IQRs are not equal, so the groups do not have the same consistency by this measure. Group B is more consistent because 8 is smaller than 15.
- Neither group (means are needed).
  - _why:_ Means compare typical values rather than consistency. IQR directly measures spread, so Group B is more consistent because its IQR of 8 is smaller.
