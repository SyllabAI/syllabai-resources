## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the correct result of multiplying $x$ by $x$?

**Back:** $$x^2$

**Choices:**

- $$x^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying $x$ by itself gives $x \times x = x^2$. The exponent 2 shows that $x$ appears as a factor twice.
- $$2x$
  - _why:_ This confuses multiplication with addition: $x + x = 2x$, but $x \times x = x^2$ because the two powers of $x$ combine.
- $$x$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly leaves the variable unchanged. Since two factors of $x$ are being multiplied, their exponents add, giving $x^1 \times x^1 = x^2$.
- $$x^3$
  - _why:_ This counts one factor too many. The product contains two factors of $x$, not three, so $x \times x = x^2$.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is the number multiplying a variable, such as the 7 in $7x$.

**Back:** A coefficient is the number multiplying a variable, such as the 7 in $7x$.

**Choices:**

- coefficient ✔
  - _why:_ A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable. In $7x$, the variable is $x$ and its coefficient is 7.
- constant
  - _why:_ A constant is a term without a variable, so 7 would be a constant in $7x + 7$. In $7x$, 7 is the coefficient.
- variable
  - _why:_ A variable is the letter representing an unknown or changeable value. In $7x$, $x$ is the variable, while 7 is the coefficient.
- exponent
  - _why:_ An exponent indicates a power, such as the 2 in $x^2$. The number multiplying a variable, such as 7 in $7x$, is its coefficient.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify the term $-3x \cdot 5x$.

**Back:** $$-15x^2$

**Choices:**

- $$-15x^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficients multiply to $-3 \times 5 = -15$, and $x \times x = x^2$. Therefore, the simplified product is $-15x^2$.
- $$-15x$
  - _why:_ The coefficients have been multiplied correctly, but one factor of $x$ has been lost. Since $x \times x = x^2$, the correct result is $-15x^2$.
- $$15x^2$
  - _why:_ This loses the negative sign. A negative coefficient multiplied by a positive coefficient gives a negative product, so the correct result is $-15x^2$.
- $$-8x^2$
  - _why:_ This adds the coefficients instead of multiplying them. The calculation requires $-3 \times 5 = -15$ and $x \times x = x^2$, giving $-15x^2$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What does it mean to expand an expression?

**Back:** To remove brackets by multiplying out every term.

**Choices:**

- To remove brackets by multiplying out every term. ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding removes brackets by multiplying each relevant term across the bracket or brackets. The resulting products are then collected and simplified where possible.
- To remove brackets by adding together every term.
  - _why:_ Brackets cannot generally be removed merely by adding terms. Expanding requires multiplying every relevant term correctly, then collecting any like terms in the result.
- To insert brackets by factorising every term.
  - _why:_ Factorising is the reverse process: it rewrites an expression as a product involving brackets. Expanding instead removes brackets by multiplying out every term.
- To remove powers by dividing every term.
  - _why:_ Expanding does not mean removing powers or dividing terms. It means removing brackets by multiplying out all required products and then simplifying the expression.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** When expanding two brackets, you should produce [ ___ ] initial products before simplifying.

**Back:** When expanding two brackets, you should produce four initial products before simplifying.

**Choices:**

- four ✔
  - _why:_ Each of the two terms in the first bracket multiplies each of the two terms in the second bracket, producing $2 \times 2 = 4$ initial products.
- two
  - _why:_ This counts only one product for each term in a bracket. Each of two terms must multiply both terms in the other bracket, producing four initial products.
- three
  - _why:_ This usually results from missing one cross-product. Two terms multiplied by two terms produce four initial products before any like terms are collected.
- six
  - _why:_ This overcounts the required pairings. With two terms in each bracket, there are $2 \times 2 = 4$ initial products, not six.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(x + 5)(x - 2)$.

**Back:** $$x^2 + 3x - 10$

**Choices:**

- $$x^2 + 3x - 10$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying gives $x^2 - 2x + 5x - 10$. Collecting the middle terms, $-2x + 5x = 3x$, produces $x^2 + 3x - 10$.
- $$x^2 + 7x - 10$
  - _why:_ This treats the middle coefficients as 5 and 2 without preserving the negative sign. The middle terms are $-2x$ and $5x$, which combine to $3x$.
- $$x^2 + 3x + 10$
  - _why:_ The middle term is correct, but the constant sign is not. Multiplying $5$ by $-2$ gives $-10$, so the result is $x^2 + 3x - 10$.
- $$x^2 - 3x - 10$
  - _why:_ The middle terms have been combined in the wrong order. Since $-2x + 5x = 3x$, the correct expansion is $x^2 + 3x - 10$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(3p - 4)(p - 6)$.

**Back:** $$3p^2 - 22p + 24$

**Choices:**

- $$3p^2 - 22p + 24$ ✔
  - _why:_ The four products are $3p^2$, $-18p$, $-4p$ and $24$. Combining the middle terms gives $-22p$, so the expansion is $3p^2 - 22p + 24$.
- $$3p^2 - 14p + 24$
  - _why:_ This treats $-4p$ as positive when combining the middle terms. Both cross-products are negative, so $-18p - 4p = -22p$.
- $$3p^2 - 22p - 24$
  - _why:_ The constant sign is incorrect. Multiplying the two negative constants gives $(-4)(-6) = 24$, so the correct expansion ends with $+24$.
- $$p^2 - 22p + 24$
  - _why:_ This drops the coefficient 3 from the leading product. Multiplying $3p$ by $p$ gives $3p^2$, so the correct result is $3p^2 - 22p + 24$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** The expression $(a - 5)^2$ is equivalent to the expanded form $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The expression $(a - 5)^2$ is equivalent to the expanded form $\boxed{(a - 5)(a - 5)}$.

**Choices:**

- $$(a - 5)(a - 5)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Squaring an expression means multiplying the entire expression by itself. Therefore, $(a - 5)^2$ is exactly equivalent to $(a - 5)(a - 5)$.
- $$(a - 5)(a + 5)$
  - _why:_ This changes one factor and creates a difference of two squares, $a^2 - 25$. Squaring requires two identical factors, so the correct form is $(a - 5)(a - 5)$.
- $$(a - 5)(a - 2)$
  - _why:_ The exponent 2 does not replace the 5 in one factor. It means the entire bracket is repeated as a factor, giving $(a - 5)(a - 5)$.
- $$(a + 5)(a + 5)$
  - _why:_ This changes both subtraction signs to addition signs, representing $(a + 5)^2$. The original bracket must be repeated unchanged as $(a - 5)(a - 5)$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(y - 8)^2$.

**Back:** $$y^2 - 16y + 64$

**Choices:**

- $$y^2 - 16y + 64$ ✔
  - _why:_ Squaring means multiplying $(y-8)$ by itself. Expanding gives $y^2-8y-8y+64$, and combining the two middle terms produces $y^2-16y+64$.
- $$y^2 + 64$
  - _why:_ The middle term has been omitted. Multiplying both brackets gives two products of $-8y$, so the correct expansion is $y^2-16y+64$.
- $$y^2 - 8y + 64$
  - _why:_ This uses only one of the two $-8y$ products. Squaring the bracket produces $-8y-8y=-16y$, so the correct expansion is $y^2-16y+64$.
- $$y^2 + 16y + 64$
  - _why:_ The sign of the middle term has been reversed. Both cross-products are $-8y$, so they combine to $-16y$ and give $y^2-16y+64$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $(y - 8)^2$ not simply $y^2 + 64$?

**Back:** The expansion produces a middle term ($-16y$) because every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second.

**Choices:**

- The expansion produces a middle term ($-16y$) because every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. ✔
  - _why:_ Squaring a bracket means multiplying it by itself. The two cross-products are both $-8y$, so they combine to form the required middle term $-16y$.
- The expansion contains only squared terms because each term in the first bracket is squared separately from the second.
  - _why:_ Squaring each term separately ignores the cross-products. In $(y-8)(y-8)$, each $y$ also multiplies a $-8$, producing the middle term $-16y$.
- The expansion produces cancelling terms ($-8y$ and $+8y$) because the brackets contain terms with opposite signs.
  - _why:_ The cross-products do not cancel because they are both negative: $y(-8)=-8y$ and $(-8)y=-8y$. Their sum is $-16y$, not zero.
- The expansion produces a constant term ($64$) because only the two numerical terms need to be multiplied together.
  - _why:_ The constant term is indeed $64$, but that does not account for the cross-products. Multiplying every term gives the additional middle term $-16y$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** When factorising $x^2 + bx + c$, what two requirements must the numbers in the brackets meet?

**Back:** Multiply to give $c$Add to give $b$

**Choices:**

- Multiply to give $c$Add to give $b$ ✔
  - _why:_ For $(x+m)(x+n)$, expansion gives $x^2+(m+n)x+mn$. Therefore, the two selected numbers must have product $c$ and sum $b$.
- Multiply to give $b$Add to give $c$
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the coefficients. The constants multiply to form $c$, while their sum forms the coefficient $b$ of the middle term.
- Add to give $c$Subtract to give $b$
  - _why:_ The constant term comes from multiplying the bracket constants, not adding them. Their sum must equal $b$, while their product must equal $c$.
- Square to give $c$Multiply to give $b$
  - _why:_ Squaring the selected numbers is not part of the expansion rule. The bracket constants must multiply to $c$ and add to $b$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 + 11x + 24$.

**Back:** $$(x + 3)(x + 8)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x + 3)(x + 8)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numbers $3$ and $8$ multiply to $24$ and add to $11$. Expanding the brackets therefore reproduces $x^2+11x+24$.
- $$(x + 4)(x + 6)$
  - _why:_ Although $4+6=10$ is close to the middle coefficient, it is not $11$. The required pair is $3$ and $8$, giving $(x+3)(x+8)$.
- $$(x + 2)(x + 12)$
  - _why:_ The constants multiply to $24$, but their sum is $14$, so these brackets produce $x^2+14x+24$. The correct factors use $3$ and $8$.
- $$(x - 3)(x - 8)$
  - _why:_ Using two negative signs makes the middle coefficient negative. These brackets expand to $x^2-11x+24$, whereas the correct factorisation is $(x+3)(x+8)$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic has a positive final number and a negative middle term, the signs in the brackets are [ ___ ].

**Back:** If a quadratic has a positive final number and a negative middle term, the signs in the brackets are both negative.

**Choices:**

- both negative ✔
  - _why:_ A positive constant requires matching signs, while a negative middle coefficient requires their sum to be negative. Therefore, both bracket constants must be negative.
- both positive
  - _why:_ Two positive constants would produce a positive middle coefficient when added. A positive final number with a negative middle term requires both signs to be negative.
- opposite signs
  - _why:_ Opposite signs produce a negative constant because their product is negative. Since the final number is positive and the middle term negative, both signs must be negative.
- both zero
  - _why:_ A zero bracket constant would make the final number zero, not positive. Matching negative signs give a positive product and a negative sum, so both signs are negative.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic has a [ ___ ] final number, the signs in the brackets must be different (one positive, one negative).

**Back:** If a quadratic has a negative final number, the signs in the brackets must be different (one positive, one negative).

**Choices:**

- negative ✔
  - _why:_ The final number is the product of the two bracket constants. A negative product requires opposite signs, so one constant is positive and the other is negative.
- positive
  - _why:_ A positive product normally comes from two constants with matching signs, not different signs. Opposite signs are required when the final number is negative.
- zero
  - _why:_ A zero final number means at least one bracket constant is zero, so it does not require one positive and one negative sign. The required description is negative.
- non-negative
  - _why:_ A non-negative final number may be positive or zero, neither of which forces opposite signs. It is specifically a negative final number that requires different signs.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 - 9x + 20$.

**Back:** $$(x - 4)(x - 5)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x - 4)(x - 5)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numbers $-4$ and $-5$ multiply to $20$ and add to $-9$. Expanding the brackets therefore gives $x^2-9x+20$.
- $$(x - 2)(x - 10)$
  - _why:_ The numbers $-2$ and $-10$ multiply to $20$, but add to $-12$, producing $x^2-12x+20$. The required constants are $-4$ and $-5$.
- $$(x + 4)(x + 5)$
  - _why:_ Two positive constants give a positive middle coefficient, so these brackets expand to $x^2+9x+20$. Both constants must instead be negative.
- $$(x + 4)(x - 5)$
  - _why:_ Opposite signs produce a negative constant, so these brackets expand to $x^2-x-20$. The positive constant and negative middle term require $-4$ and $-5$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 + 2x - 35$.

**Back:** $$(x + 7)(x - 5)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x + 7)(x - 5)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The constants $7$ and $-5$ multiply to $-35$ and add to $2$. Thus, expanding the brackets gives exactly $x^2+2x-35$.
- $$(x - 7)(x + 5)$
  - _why:_ These constants multiply to $-35$, but they add to $-2$, producing $x^2-2x-35$. Reversing the signs gives the required middle term.
- $$(x + 7)(x + 5)$
  - _why:_ Although the constants add to $12$, their product is $35$, so these brackets produce $x^2+12x+35$. The correct pair is $7$ and $-5$.
- $$(x + 8)(x - 3)$
  - _why:_ The constants add to $2$, but their product is $-35$ only when they are $7$ and $-5$. This pair instead produces a constant of $-24$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** State the general factorised form for the difference of two squares, $x^2 - a^2$.

**Back:** $$(x + a)(x - a)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x + a)(x - a)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding gives $x^2-ax+ax-a^2=x^2-a^2$. The two middle terms cancel, producing the difference of two squares.
- $$(x + a)(x + a)$
  - _why:_ Repeating the positive bracket produces $x^2+2ax+a^2$, a perfect square trinomial. Opposite signs are needed, so the correct factorisation is $(x+a)(x-a)$.
- $$(x - a)(x - a)$
  - _why:_ Repeating the negative bracket produces $x^2-2ax+a^2$, not a difference of squares. The correct factors have opposite signs: $(x+a)(x-a)$.
- $$(a + x)(a - x)$
  - _why:_ Multiplying these brackets gives $a^2-x^2$, the negative of the required expression. Reversing the subtraction correctly gives $(x+a)(x-a)$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 - 64$.

**Back:** $$(x + 8)(x - 8)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x + 8)(x - 8)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Since $64=8^2$, this is a difference of two squares. The opposite-sign factors expand to $x^2-64$ because the middle terms cancel.
- $$(x + 8)(x + 8)$
  - _why:_ Using two positive brackets gives $x^2+16x+64$, introducing a middle term and a positive constant. The correct opposite-sign factors are $(x+8)(x-8)$.
- $$(x - 8)(x - 8)$
  - _why:_ Using two negative brackets gives $x^2-16x+64$, not $x^2-64$. The difference of squares requires opposite signs: $(x+8)(x-8)$.
- $$(x + 4)(x - 16)$
  - _why:_ Although $4\times16=64$, these unequal numbers create a middle term, giving $x^2-12x-64$. The correct factors are $(x+8)(x-8)$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** The "difference of two squares" rule [ ___ ] apply to expressions with an addition sign, such as $x^2 + 64$.

**Back:** The "difference of two squares" rule does not apply to expressions with an addition sign, such as $x^2 + 64$.

**Choices:**

- does not ✔
  - _why:_ The rule applies only to subtraction of two square terms, in the form $a^2-b^2$. A sum such as $x^2+64$ cannot be factorised this way.
- does
  - _why:_ Applying the rule to a sum is a common sign error: $(x+8)(x-8)$ expands to $x^2-64$. Therefore, the rule does not apply to $x^2+64$.
- can
  - _why:_ Over the real numbers, a sum of two squares cannot use opposite-sign conjugate factors. The sentence requires does not because the rule is restricted to a difference.
- will
  - _why:_ The addition sign prevents the middle terms from cancelling into the required positive constant using real linear factors. The rule does not apply to $x^2+64$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 + 14x + 49$.

**Back:** $$(x + 7)^2$ or $(x + 7)(x + 7)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x + 7)^2$ or $(x + 7)(x + 7)$ ✔
  - _why:_ This is a perfect square trinomial: $49=7^2$ and the middle term is $2\times7\times x=14x$. Expanding either form returns $x^2+14x+49$.
- $$(x - 7)^2$ or $(x - 7)(x - 7)$
  - _why:_ Choosing negative sevens produces a negative middle term, since this expands to $x^2-14x+49$. Positive sevens are required: $(x+7)^2$.
- $$(x + 14)^2$ or $(x + 14)(x + 14)$
  - _why:_ Using the middle-term coefficient directly gives $x^2+28x+196$. Half of $14$ is $7$, so the correct repeated factor is $(x+7)^2$.
- $$(x + 1)(x + 49)$ or $(x + 49)(x + 1)$
  - _why:_ Although $1\times49=49$, these numbers add to $50$, producing $x^2+50x+49$. The required numbers both equal $7$, giving $(x+7)^2$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you verify that a factorisation is correct?

**Back:** Expand the brackets to see if you return to the original expression.

**Choices:**

- Expand the brackets to see if you return to the original expression. ✔
  - _why:_ Expansion reverses factorisation. If multiplying out and collecting like terms reproduces every term of the original expression, the factorisation has been verified.
- Add the terms inside the brackets to see if they match the original expression.
  - _why:_ Factors must be multiplied, not added, so adding their terms does not reverse factorisation. Expand the brackets fully and check that the original expression is recovered.
- Substitute $x = 0$ into both forms to see if the constant terms match.
  - _why:_ Checking only $x=0$ compares the constant terms but can miss incorrect coefficients elsewhere. Expand the brackets to verify that the entire original expression is recovered.
- Differentiate both forms to see if their gradients are equal.
  - _why:_ Equal derivatives can still belong to expressions differing by a constant, so this does not fully verify equality. Expanding the factors should reproduce the complete original expression.
