## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** The bottom number of a fraction is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The bottom number of a fraction is the denominator.

**Choices:**

- denominator ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator is the bottom number of a fraction. It shows how many equal parts the whole has been divided into.
- numerator
  - _why:_ The numerator is the top number, not the bottom number, of a fraction. The bottom number is called the denominator and describes the number of equal parts.
- quotient
  - _why:_ A quotient is the result of a division calculation, not a part of a fraction’s notation. The bottom number of a fraction is the denominator.
- remainder
  - _why:_ A remainder is the amount left over after a division that does not divide exactly. The bottom number of a fraction is called the denominator.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The top number of a fraction is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The top number of a fraction is the numerator.

**Choices:**

- numerator ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator is the top number of a fraction. It indicates how many of the equal parts are being considered or selected.
- denominator
  - _why:_ The denominator is the bottom number, not the top number, of a fraction. The top number is the numerator and counts the selected parts.
- quotient
  - _why:_ A quotient is the answer obtained from division, rather than the top number in a fraction. The top number is called the numerator.
- remainder
  - _why:_ A remainder is left over when one number does not divide exactly into another. The top number of a fraction is called the numerator.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The denominator tells you how many [ ___ ] the whole is split into.

**Back:** The denominator tells you how many equal pieces the whole is split into.

**Choices:**

- equal pieces ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator gives the total number of equal pieces into which the whole is divided. Fractional parts must be equal for the fraction to be meaningful.
- unequal pieces
  - _why:_ A denominator does not describe unequal divisions. A fraction divides a whole into equal pieces, and the denominator states how many equal pieces there are.
- selected pieces
  - _why:_ The selected pieces are counted by the numerator, not the denominator. The denominator tells you the total number of equal pieces in the whole.
- remaining pieces
  - _why:_ The denominator does not count pieces that remain after some are removed. It states how many equal pieces make up the complete whole.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** The numerator tells you how many pieces you [ ___ ].

**Back:** The numerator tells you how many pieces you have.

**Choices:**

- have ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator counts how many equal pieces you have or are considering. For example, in $\frac{3}{5}$, you have three of five equal pieces.
- discard
  - _why:_ The numerator does not count pieces that have been discarded. It tells you how many equal pieces you have or are considering.
- divide
  - _why:_ The denominator determines how many equal pieces the whole is divided into. The numerator instead tells you how many of those pieces you have.
- ignore
  - _why:_ The numerator does not count pieces that are ignored. It counts the equal pieces you have, use or select from the total number of pieces.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** When finding a fraction of an amount, which number do you divide by first?

**Back:** The denominator.

**Choices:**

- The denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ Divide the amount by the denominator first because the denominator gives the number of equal parts. This calculates the value of one equal part.
- The numerator.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the numerator confuses the number of selected parts with the total number of equal parts. Divide by the denominator first, then multiply by the numerator.
- The original amount.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the original amount does not find the value of one fractional part. The amount should first be divided by the fraction’s denominator.
- The final answer.
  - _why:_ The final answer is not available at the start of the calculation. Divide the original amount by the denominator first to find one equal part.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** When finding a fraction of an amount, which number do you multiply by after dividing?

**Back:** The numerator.

**Choices:**

- The numerator. ✔
  - _why:_ After dividing by the denominator to find one equal part, multiply by the numerator to find the required number of those parts.
- The denominator.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the denominator after already dividing by it would return to the original amount. Multiply by the numerator to select the required number of parts.
- The original amount.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the original amount introduces an unnecessary extra factor and gives an incorrect result. After dividing, multiply the quotient by the numerator.
- The fraction bar.
  - _why:_ The fraction bar represents division and is not a numerical factor to multiply by. After dividing by the denominator, multiply by the numerator.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** If it makes mental maths easier, what alternative order can find a fraction of an amount?

**Back:** Multiply by the numerator.Divide by the denominator.

**Choices:**

- Multiply by the numerator.Divide by the denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplication and division can be performed in this alternative order: multiply by the numerator, then divide by the denominator. This produces the same fraction of the amount.
- Multiply by the denominator.Divide by the numerator.
  - _why:_ This uses the reciprocal of the required fraction, calculating denominator divided by numerator. The correct alternative is to multiply by the numerator, then divide by the denominator.
- Divide by the numerator.Multiply by the denominator.
  - _why:_ This also applies the reciprocal of the required fraction, making the amount larger when it should often become smaller. Multiply by the numerator, then divide by the denominator.
- Divide by the denominator.Divide by the numerator.
  - _why:_ Dividing twice calculates the amount divided by the product of both fraction numbers. The valid alternative is to multiply by the numerator and divide by the denominator.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is dividing before multiplying usually easier?

**Back:** It keeps the numbers smaller.

**Choices:**

- It keeps the numbers smaller. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing first often reduces the original amount before multiplication. Working with a smaller quotient makes the later multiplication easier to perform mentally.
- It keeps the denominator smaller.
  - _why:_ The denominator itself does not change during the calculation. Dividing first is usually easier because it reduces the amount, keeping the numbers used in later multiplication smaller.
- It makes the fraction larger.
  - _why:_ Changing the order does not change the fraction or the final result. Dividing first is preferred because it usually keeps the intermediate numbers smaller.
- It always gives a whole number.
  - _why:_ Dividing first does not guarantee a whole-number result, since the amount may not divide exactly by the denominator. Its usual advantage is keeping intermediate numbers smaller.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Calculate $\frac{1}{3}$ of 240.

**Back:** 80

**Choices:**

- 80 ✔
  - _why:_ To find one third, divide the amount by 3. Since $240 \div 3 = 80$, one third of 240 is 80.
- 60
  - _why:_ Dividing 240 by 4 finds one quarter rather than one third. The required calculation is $240 \div 3 = 80$.
- 120
  - _why:_ Dividing 240 by 2 finds one half, not one third. One third is found using $240 \div 3$, which gives 80.
- 720
  - _why:_ Multiplying 240 by 3 makes the amount three times larger instead of finding a fraction of it. Divide by 3 to obtain 80.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Calculate $\frac{2}{3}$ of 240.

**Back:** 160

**Choices:**

- 160 ✔
  - _why:_ First find one third: $240 \div 3 = 80$. Then multiply by the numerator 2, giving $80 \times 2 = 160$.
- 80
  - _why:_ This finds only one third of 240 and omits multiplication by the numerator 2. Doubling 80 gives the correct answer, 160.
- 120
  - _why:_ This treats two thirds as one half. Instead, divide 240 by 3 and multiply by 2: $80 \times 2 = 160$.
- 360
  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying by 3 and then dividing by 2, reversing the fraction. Multiplying 240 by $\frac{2}{3}$ gives 160.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** When working backwards from a fraction value, divide by the [ ___ ] to find one part, then multiply by the [ ___ ] to find the whole.

**Back:** When working backwards from a fraction value, divide by the numerator to find one part, then multiply by the denominator to find the whole.

**Choices:**

- When working backwards from a fraction value, divide by the numerator to find one part, then multiply by the denominator to find the whole. ✔
  - _why:_ The known fraction value represents the number of parts given by the numerator. Dividing by the numerator finds one part, and multiplying by the denominator reconstructs the whole.
- When working backwards from a fraction value, divide by the denominator to find one part, then multiply by the numerator to find the whole.
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the fraction’s two numbers. Divide by the numerator to find one part, then multiply by the denominator because that is the total number of equal parts.
- When working backwards from a fraction value, divide by the numerator to find one part, then multiply by the numerator to find the whole.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the numerator correctly finds one part, but multiplying by it simply rebuilds the known fraction value. Multiply by the denominator to reconstruct the whole.
- When working backwards from a fraction value, divide by the denominator to find one part, then multiply by the denominator to find the whole.
  - _why:_ Dividing and multiplying by the denominator cancel each other, returning the known value rather than the whole. First divide by the numerator, then multiply by the denominator.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** If $\frac{3}{4}$ of a number is 39, what is the number?

**Back:** 52

**Choices:**

- 52 ✔
  - _why:_ The value 39 represents three equal parts, so one part is $39 \div 3 = 13$. Four parts give the whole: $13 \times 4 = 52$.
- 13
  - _why:_ Dividing $39$ by $3$ finds the value of one quarter, not the complete number. Multiplying that result by $4$ gives the whole, which is $52$.
- 156
  - _why:_ Multiplying $39$ by $4$ ignores that 39 represents three parts rather than one. Divide by $3$ first, then multiply by $4$ to obtain $52$.
- 29.25
  - _why:_ This finds three quarters of 39, treating 39 as the whole rather than the given fraction value. Working backwards requires $39 \div 3 \times 4 = 52$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If $\frac{2}{5}$ of a number is 14, what is the original number?

**Back:** 35

**Choices:**

- 35 ✔
  - _why:_ The value 14 represents two equal parts, so one part is $14 \div 2 = 7$. Five parts make the original number: $7 \times 5 = 35$.
- 7
  - _why:_ Dividing $14$ by $2$ finds only one fifth of the original number. Multiply this one-part value by $5$ to get the whole, which is $35$.
- 70
  - _why:_ Multiplying $14$ by $5$ incorrectly treats 14 as one part, although it represents two parts. Divide by $2$ first, then multiply by $5$ to get $35$.
- 5.6
  - _why:_ This calculates two fifths of 14 instead of reversing the fraction operation. Since 14 is already two fifths, use $14 \div 2 \times 5 = 35$.
