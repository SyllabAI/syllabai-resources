## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** To change hours to minutes, [ ___ ] by 60.

**Back:** To change hours to minutes, multiply by 60.

**Choices:**

- multiply ✔
  - _why:_ Each hour contains 60 minutes, so multiplying the number of hours by 60 gives the equivalent number of minutes.
- divide
  - _why:_ Dividing is the inverse conversion and is used when changing minutes to hours. To change hours to minutes, multiply by 60 because each hour contains 60 minutes.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding 60 does not apply the conversion factor correctly. To change hours to minutes, multiply by 60 because every hour contributes 60 minutes.
- subtract
  - _why:_ Subtracting 60 reduces the value rather than converting its units. Hours are changed to minutes by multiplying by 60, since there are 60 minutes per hour.

**Choices:**

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** How many minutes are in $\frac{1}{4}$ hour and $\frac{3}{4}$ hour?

**Back:** $\frac{1}{4}$ hour = 15 minutes$\frac{3}{4}$ hour = 45 minutes

**Choices:**

- $\frac{1}{4}$ hour = 15 minutes$\frac{3}{4}$ hour = 45 minutes ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying each fraction by 60 gives $60 \div 4 = 15$ and $3 \times 15 = 45$, so these are the correct minute equivalents.
- $\frac{1}{4}$ hour = 20 minutes$\frac{3}{4}$ hour = 40 minutes
  - _why:_ This incorrectly divides the hour into three 20-minute parts. Quarters divide 60 by 4, so one quarter is 15 minutes and three quarters is 45 minutes.
- $\frac{1}{4}$ hour = 10 minutes$\frac{3}{4}$ hour = 30 minutes
  - _why:_ This treats an hour as if it contained only 40 minutes. Using 60 minutes per hour gives $\frac{1}{4}$ hour as 15 minutes and $\frac{3}{4}$ hour as 45 minutes.
- $\frac{1}{4}$ hour = 25 minutes$\frac{3}{4}$ hour = 35 minutes
  - _why:_ These values split 60 into unequal parts rather than quarters. Four equal quarters are 15 minutes each, giving 15 minutes and 45 minutes respectively.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you convert a mixed time (e.g. 2 hours 25 minutes) into total minutes?

**Back:** Convert hours to minutes ($2 \times 60 = 120$)Add the remaining minutes ($120 + 25 = 145$)

**Choices:**

- Convert hours to minutes ($2 \times 60 = 120$)Add the remaining minutes ($120 + 25 = 145$) ✔
  - _why:_ The hours must first be expressed in minutes, then the additional minutes are added. This produces a single total of 145 minutes.
- Convert hours to minutes ($2 \div 60 = \frac{1}{30}$)Add the remaining minutes ($\frac{1}{30} + 25 = 25\frac{1}{30}$)
  - _why:_ This reverses the conversion by dividing the hours by 60. Hours must be multiplied by 60, then the remaining minutes added, giving 145 minutes.
- Convert hours to minutes ($2 \times 60 = 120$)Subtract the remaining minutes ($120 - 25 = 95$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting treats the additional 25 minutes as time to remove. They are part of the duration, so they must be added to 120, giving 145 minutes.
- Convert minutes to hours ($25 \div 60 = \frac{5}{12}$)Add the hours ($2 + \frac{5}{12} = 2\frac{5}{12}$)
  - _why:_ This produces total hours rather than the requested total minutes. Convert the 2 hours to 120 minutes and add 25 to obtain 145 minutes.

**Back:** 90 minutes

**Choices:**

- 90 minutes ✔
  - _why:_ One hour is 60 minutes and half an hour is 30 minutes. Adding them gives $60 + 30 = 90$ minutes.
- 75 minutes
  - _why:_ This treats half an hour as 15 minutes, confusing it with a quarter-hour. Half of 60 is 30, so the total is 90 minutes.

  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats half an hour as 20 minutes. Since $\frac{1}{2} \times 60 = 30$, the complete duration is 90 minutes.
- 120 minutes
  - _why:_ This rounds the mixed number up to 2 hours. The extra half-hour is only 30 minutes, so $60 + 30 = 90$ minutes.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate the difference between two durations?

**Back:** Convert both into minutesSubtract the smaller from the larger

**Choices:**

- Convert both into minutesSubtract the smaller from the larger ✔
  - _why:_ Using the same unit makes the durations directly comparable. Subtracting the smaller duration from the larger then gives a positive difference in minutes.
- Convert both into minutesAdd the smaller to the larger
  - _why:_ Adding finds the combined duration, not the difference. After converting both durations to minutes, subtract the smaller value from the larger value.
- Convert one into minutesSubtract the smaller from the larger
  - _why:_ Converting only one duration leaves the values in different units, so they cannot be compared reliably. Convert both to minutes before subtracting the smaller from the larger.
- Convert both into minutesSubtract the larger from the smaller
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction produces a negative result rather than the positive difference between the durations. Subtract the smaller number of minutes from the larger.

## Card 8 — `basic`

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the safest way to find a finish time when adding a duration that crosses an hour?

**Back:** Add the minutes in stages, using the next o’clock time as a checkpoint.

**Choices:**

- Add the minutes in stages, using the next o’clock time as a checkpoint. ✔
  - _why:_ Working to the next whole hour first makes the change of hour explicit. The remaining minutes can then be added without treating clock notation like decimal notation.
- Add all the minutes directly to the minutes part as if the time were a decimal.
  - _why:_ Treating a clock time as a decimal ignores that an hour contains 60 minutes, not 100. The safe method is to add in stages using the next o’clock time.
- Round the starting time to the nearest o’clock time before adding the duration.
  - _why:_ Rounding changes the starting time and therefore gives an inaccurate finish time. Instead, count exactly to the next o’clock checkpoint, then add the remaining duration.
- Subtract the minutes from the previous o’clock time before adding the duration.
  - _why:_ Working backwards to the previous hour introduces an unnecessary subtraction and can shift the answer. Add forwards in stages, using the next o’clock time as the checkpoint.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** A journey starts at 11.35 am and consists of 18 min, 12 min, and 25 min stages. What is the arrival time?

**Back:** 12.30 pm

**Choices:**

- 12.30 pm ✔
  - _why:_ The stages total $18+12+25=55$ minutes. Adding 25 minutes reaches 12.00 pm, and the remaining 30 minutes gives an arrival time of 12.30 pm.
- 12.18 pm
  - _why:_ This results from omitting the 12-minute stage, leaving only $18+25=43$ minutes. All three stages total 55 minutes, giving an arrival time of 12.30 pm.
- 12.05 pm
  - _why:_ This is the time after only the first two stages: $18+12=30$ minutes. The final 25-minute stage must also be added, producing 12.30 pm.
- 12.42 pm
  - _why:_ This can result from counting the 12-minute stage twice, making the duration 67 minutes. The actual total is 55 minutes, so the arrival time is 12.30 pm.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** Midday (noon) is written as [ ___ ].

**Back:** Midday (noon) is written as 12.00 pm.

**Choices:**

- 12.00 pm ✔
  - _why:_ Noon is the instant when the morning ends and the afternoon begins, so it is written as 12.00 pm in the 12-hour clock.
- 12.00 am
  - _why:_ The labels for noon and midnight are commonly confused. However, 12.00 am denotes midnight, while midday or noon is written as 12.00 pm.
- 00.00 pm
  - _why:_ Zero hours is used in 24-hour notation for midnight and is not combined with pm. In 12-hour notation, midday is written as 12.00 pm.
- 12.00 noon
  - _why:_ Although “12 noon” is used informally, it does not fill a statement asking for the am/pm clock notation. The required form is 12.00 pm.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it incorrect to say 7.50 plus 35 minutes equals 7.85?

**Back:** There are only 60 minutes in an hour, so 7.85 is not a valid clock time.

**Choices:**

- There are only 60 minutes in an hour, so 7.85 is not a valid clock time. ✔
  - _why:_ Clock minutes run from 00 to 59 because each hour contains 60 minutes. Therefore, 7.50 plus 35 minutes passes 8.00 and becomes 8.25.
- There are only 100 minutes in an hour, so 7.85 must be rounded to 8.00.
  - _why:_ This treats time as a base-100 decimal measurement. An hour contains 60 minutes, so 35 minutes after 7.50 is 8.25, not a rounded version of 7.85.
- There are only 60 seconds in a minute, so 7.85 should be written as 7.35.
  - _why:_ The relevant conversion is 60 minutes per hour, not 60 seconds per minute. Adding 35 minutes to 7.50 crosses the hour and gives 8.25.
- There are only 10 minute digits in an hour, so 7.85 should be written as 8.05.
  - _why:_ This confuses the ten possible digits with the number of minutes in an hour. Since an hour has 60 minutes, 7.50 plus 35 minutes is 8.25.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** How many digits are always used to write a time in the 24-hour clock?

**Back:** Four digits (e.g. 0715).

**Choices:**

- Four digits (e.g. 0715). ✔
  - _why:_ A standard 24-hour time uses two digits for the hour and two for the minutes. A leading zero is included when necessary, as in 0715.
- Three digits (e.g. 715).
  - _why:_ Omitting the leading zero produces only three digits, but standard 24-hour notation always uses two hour digits and two minute digits. The correct form is 0715.
- Two digits (e.g. 15).
  - _why:_ Two digits can show only an hour or a number of minutes, not a complete time. A 24-hour clock time always uses four digits, such as 0715.
- Six digits (e.g. 071500).
  - _why:_ Six digits include seconds as well as hours and minutes, which are not part of the usual written 24-hour time. The standard format uses four digits.

  - _why:_ Subtracting 12 from 16 correctly gives 4, but 16:32 is after midday, not before it. The correct 12-hour time is therefore 4.32 pm.
- 6.32 pm
  - _why:_ This subtracts 10 from the hour instead of 12. Converting an afternoon 24-hour time requires $16-12=4$, so the correct time is 4.32 pm.
- 16.32 pm
  - _why:_ This mixes 24-hour notation with the pm label. In 12-hour notation, subtract 12 from 16 and include pm, giving 4.32 pm.

  - _why:_ A journey or vehicle is normally represented by a vertical column, not a horizontal row. The horizontal row identifies a particular place or stop.
- departure time
  - _why:_ A departure time usually appears in an individual timetable cell where a row and column meet. The whole horizontal row represents a place or stop.
- ticket type
  - _why:_ Ticket categories are not the usual organising feature of a travel timetable’s rows. A horizontal row normally represents a place or stop on the route.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Ben must be at work by 9.00 am. It is a 10-minute walk from the bus stop. What is the latest bus arrival he can use?

**Back:** 08:50 (or the latest bus arriving before 08:50).

**Choices:**

- 08:50 (or the latest bus arriving before 08:50). ✔
  - _why:_ Ben needs 10 minutes to walk from the bus stop to work, so subtracting 10 minutes from 09:00 gives a latest arrival of 08:50.
- 09:10 (or the latest bus arriving before 09:10).
  - _why:_ This adds the walking time to the deadline instead of subtracting it. Ben must arrive at the stop by 08:50 to reach work by 09:00.
- 09:00 (or the latest bus arriving before 09:00).
  - _why:_ This ignores the 10-minute walk from the bus stop. A bus arriving at 09:00 would make Ben reach work at 09:10, so he needs 08:50.
- 08:40 (or the latest bus arriving before 08:40).
  - _why:_ This subtracts the 10-minute walk twice, making Ben arrive unnecessarily early. Subtracting it once from 09:00 gives the latest bus arrival of 08:50.
