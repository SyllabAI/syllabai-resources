## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In BIDMAS, B stands for [ ___ ] and I stands for [ ___ ].

**Back:** In BIDMAS, B stands for brackets and I stands for indices.

**Choices:**

- In BIDMAS, B stands for brackets and I stands for indices. ✔
  - _why:_ Brackets have the highest priority in BIDMAS, followed by indices such as powers and roots. These are the operations represented by B and I respectively.
- In BIDMAS, B stands for brackets and I stands for integers.
  - _why:_ Integers are types of numbers, not a stage in the order of operations. B stands for brackets and I stands for indices, meaning powers and roots.
- In BIDMAS, B stands for bases and I stands for indices.
  - _why:_ A base is part of an expression involving an index, but B does not represent bases. B stands for brackets, while I stands for indices.
- In BIDMAS, B stands for brackets and I stands for inequalities.
  - _why:_ Inequalities compare quantities and are not an operation-priority level. In BIDMAS, B represents brackets and I represents indices such as squares and cubes.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Which two operations share the D/M BIDMAS level?

**Back:** Division and multiplication.

**Choices:**

- Division and multiplication. ✔
  - _why:_ Division and multiplication have equal priority in BIDMAS. When both appear in an expression, they are carried out from left to right.
- Division and addition.
  - _why:_ This mixes two different priority levels. Division shares its BIDMAS level with multiplication, while addition belongs to the lower A/S level.
- Multiplication and subtraction.
  - _why:_ Subtraction has lower priority than multiplication, so they do not share a level. The D/M level contains division and multiplication.
- Indices and multiplication.
  - _why:_ Indices are evaluated before multiplication and therefore form a separate, higher-priority level. Division and multiplication are the two operations at the D/M level.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Which two operations share the A/S BIDMAS level?

**Back:** Addition and subtraction.

**Choices:**

- Addition and subtraction. ✔
  - _why:_ Addition and subtraction have equal priority in BIDMAS. If both occur without any higher-priority operations remaining, they are performed from left to right.
- Addition and multiplication.
  - _why:_ Multiplication has higher priority than addition, so these operations do not share a BIDMAS level. The A/S level contains addition and subtraction.
- Division and subtraction.
  - _why:_ Division belongs to the higher D/M level rather than the A/S level. Addition and subtraction share the final priority level in BIDMAS.
- Multiplication and division.
  - _why:_ Multiplication and division do share a priority level, but it is the D/M level. The A/S level consists of addition and subtraction.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** In BIDMAS, do the [ ___ ] part first, not simply [ ___ ].

**Back:** In BIDMAS, do the highest-priority part first, not simply left to right.

**Choices:**

- In BIDMAS, do the highest-priority part first, not simply left to right. ✔
  - _why:_ BIDMAS identifies the operation with the highest priority, such as brackets or indices, before lower-priority operations. Left-to-right working applies only between operations sharing a level.
- In BIDMAS, do the leftmost part first, not simply highest priority.
  - _why:_ Starting with the leftmost operation ignores the order of operations. The highest-priority part is completed first; left-to-right working is used only for operations of equal priority.
- In BIDMAS, do the lowest-priority part first, not simply left to right.
  - _why:_ This reverses the BIDMAS hierarchy. The highest-priority operation is performed first, while lower-priority addition and subtraction are usually completed later.
- In BIDMAS, do the highest-priority part first, not simply right to left.
  - _why:_ BIDMAS warns against automatically working left to right, not right to left. First complete the highest-priority part, then work left to right within equal-priority levels.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What should be done first in $(9 - 3) \times 4$?

**Back:** The bracket: $9 - 3$.

**Choices:**

- The bracket: $9 - 3$. ✔
  - _why:_ Brackets have priority over multiplication in BIDMAS, so calculate $9 - 3$ first. The resulting value can then be multiplied by $4$.
- The multiplication: $3 \times 4$.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly ignores the bracket and treats adjacent terms as a multiplication. Calculate the bracket $9 - 3$ first, then multiply its result by $4$.
- The subtraction: $3 - 4$.
  - _why:_ The subtraction inside the expression is $9 - 3$, not $3 - 4$. Because it is enclosed in brackets, $9 - 3$ must be calculated first.
- The multiplication: $9 \times 4$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying $9$ by $4$ first separates the terms incorrectly and ignores the bracket. BIDMAS requires the bracket $9 - 3$ to be evaluated first.

  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying $4 \times 5$ before subtracting, which ignores the bracket. Calculate $10 - 4 = 6$ first, then $6 \times 5 = 30$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** An index tells how many times the [ ___ ] is used as a [ ___ ].

**Back:** An index tells how many times the base number is used as a factor.

**Choices:**

- An index tells how many times the base number is used as a factor. ✔
  - _why:_ In a power, the index gives the number of repeated factors. For example, in $3^4$, the base number $3$ appears as a factor four times.
- An index tells how many times the index number is used as a factor.
  - _why:_ The index counts repeated uses of the base, rather than being repeatedly multiplied itself. In $3^4$, the base $3$ is the factor used four times.
- An index tells how many times the base number is used as a divisor.
  - _why:_ Indices describe repeated multiplication, not repeated division. The base number is used as a factor as many times as the index specifies.
- An index tells how many times the base number is used as a multiple.
  - _why:_ A multiple is a result of multiplication, not a number being multiplied within a product. An index tells how many times the base is used as a factor.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In $6 + 4^2$, which part is worked out before the addition?

**Back:** The index: $4^2$.

**Choices:**

- The index: $4^2$. ✔
  - _why:_ Indices have higher priority than addition in BIDMAS, so $4^2$ is evaluated first. The result is then added to $6$.
- The addition: $6 + 4$.
  - _why:_ Adding $6$ and $4$ first ignores the index and changes the expression. BIDMAS requires $4^2$ to be evaluated before the addition.
- The multiplication: $4 \times 2$.
  - _why:_ An index does not mean multiplying the base by the index. $4^2$ means $4 \times 4$, and this power must be evaluated before adding $6$.
- The addition: $6 + 4^2$.
  - _why:_ The whole expression cannot be added until the power has been evaluated. BIDMAS requires the index $4^2$ to be worked out before the addition.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** In $(3 + 4)^2$, what is squared?

**Back:** The whole bracket, $3 + 4$.

**Choices:**

- The whole bracket, $3 + 4$. ✔
  - _why:_ The exponent is written outside the closing bracket, so it applies to everything inside: $(3+4)^2=7^2$.
- The number 4, not $3 + 4$.
  - _why:_ The exponent would apply only to $4$ if the expression were $3+4^2$. Here it follows the bracket, so the whole of $3+4$ is squared.
- The number 3, not $3 + 4$.
  - _why:_ The exponent is not attached to $3$. Because it appears after the closing bracket, the whole bracket $3+4$ is squared.
- The two terms separately, $3^2 + 4^2$.
  - _why:_ Squaring a sum does not mean squaring each term separately. The bracket is evaluated first, giving $(3+4)^2=7^2$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a bracket-inserting target is bigger than normal, try brackets around [ ___ ] before [ ___ ].

**Back:** If a bracket-inserting target is bigger than normal, try brackets around an addition before a multiplication.

**Choices:**

- If a bracket-inserting target is bigger than normal, try brackets around an addition before a multiplication. ✔
  - _why:_ Bracketing an addition makes it happen before a following multiplication, allowing the larger sum to be multiplied. This often increases the expression compared with normal BIDMAS.
- If a bracket-inserting target is bigger than normal, try brackets around a subtraction before a multiplication.
  - _why:_ Prioritising subtraction usually reduces the quantity that is multiplied, so it is not the standard strategy for a larger target. Bracket an addition before a multiplication instead.
- If a bracket-inserting target is bigger than normal, try brackets around an addition before a division.
  - _why:_ Making a larger sum undergo division does not generally create the intended increase. The useful strategy is to bracket an addition before a multiplication, multiplying the entire sum.
- If a bracket-inserting target is bigger than normal, try brackets around a multiplication before an addition.
  - _why:_ BIDMAS already performs multiplication before addition, so these brackets normally make no change. To increase the result, bracket an addition so it occurs before a multiplication.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Add brackets to make $5 \times 4 + 2 = 30$ true.

**Back:** $5 \times (4 + 2) = 30$

**Choices:**

- $5 \times (4 + 2) = 30$ ✔
  - _why:_ The brackets make $4+2$ happen first, giving $6$. Multiplying by $5$ then produces $5\times 6=30$, so the equation is true.
- $(5 \times 4) + 2 = 30$
  - _why:_ These brackets preserve the normal order and give $20+2=22$, not $30$. The addition must be bracketed: $5\times(4+2)=30$.
- $(5 \times 4 + 2) = 30$
  - _why:_ Brackets around the entire left-hand expression do not alter its internal order, so it remains $20+2=22$. Bracket $4+2$ to obtain $30$.
- $5 \times (4) + 2 = 30$
  - _why:_ Bracketing only $4$ changes nothing, so the result is still $5\times4+2=22$. The correct brackets enclose $4+2$, giving $30$.

  - _why:_ These brackets give $4+2=6$, followed by $5\times6=30$ and then $3$, making $33$. The correct brackets enclose $3+5$.

  - _why:_ Bracketing both additions gives $8\times6=48$, which is too large. Only $3+5$ should be bracketed, leaving the final $2$ to be added.
