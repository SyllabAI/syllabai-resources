## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In an equation, the equals sign means both sides have the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In an equation, the equals sign means both sides have the same value.

**Choices:**

- same value ✔
  - _why:_ An equals sign states that the expressions on its two sides have equal values, even when the expressions are written in different forms.
- same operation
  - _why:_ The two sides do not need to contain the same operation. They must have the same value, which is what the equals sign communicates.
- same variable
  - _why:_ An equation can use different variables or have a variable on only one side. The equals sign means that both sides have the same value.
- same expression
  - _why:_ The expressions on either side may look different, such as $2 + 3$ and $5$. They must have the same value, not the same expression.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** If you change one side of an equation, what must you do to the other side?

**Back:** Make the matching change.

**Choices:**

- Make the matching change. ✔
  - _why:_ An equation remains balanced only when the same operation, using the same amount, is applied to both sides.
- Make the opposite change.
  - _why:_ Using opposite operations on the two sides destroys the balance of the equation. You must make the matching change to both sides.
- Make no change.
  - _why:_ Changing only one side usually makes the two sides unequal. You must make the matching change to the other side to preserve equality.
- Make a different change.
  - _why:_ Different changes do not generally preserve equality. The equation stays balanced when the matching change is made to the other side.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In $x + 5 = 16$, what kind of number is $x$?

**Back:** The unknown number.

**Choices:**

- The unknown number. ✔
  - _why:_ The letter $x$ represents the number that has not yet been found. Solving the equation reveals that unknown number.
- The known number.
  - _why:_ The value of $x$ is not initially given, so it is not a known number. It represents the unknown number to be found.
- The coefficient.
  - _why:_ A coefficient is a number multiplying a variable, such as $3$ in $3x$. Here, $x$ itself represents the unknown number.
- The constant term.
  - _why:_ A constant term is a fixed number such as $5$ or $16$. The variable $x$ represents the unknown number instead.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** A one-step equation can be solved by undoing [ ___ ].

**Back:** A one-step equation can be solved by undoing one operation.

**Choices:**

- one operation ✔
  - _why:_ A one-step equation has one operation acting on the unknown, so solving it requires one inverse operation to isolate the unknown.
- two operations
  - _why:_ Undoing two operations is normally required for a two-step equation. A one-step equation is solved by undoing one operation.
- three operations
  - _why:_ Three operations would require several solving steps rather than one. A one-step equation is solved by undoing one operation.
- no operations
  - _why:_ If no operation needed undoing, the unknown would already be isolated. A one-step equation is solved by undoing one operation.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** To undo adding 9, [ ___ ].

**Back:** To undo adding 9, subtract 9.

**Choices:**

- subtract 9 ✔
  - _why:_ Subtraction is the inverse of addition, so subtracting 9 cancels an added 9 and isolates the unknown.
- add 9
  - _why:_ Adding another 9 increases the original addition rather than cancelling it. To undo adding 9, subtract 9.
- subtract 18
  - _why:_ This subtracts twice the required amount, so the added 9 is not simply cancelled. The inverse change is to subtract 9.
- divide by 9
  - _why:_ Division undoes multiplication, not addition. Since the operation being reversed is adding 9, the correct inverse is to subtract 9.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $x + 5 = 16$.

**Back:** $x = 11$.

**Choices:**

- $x = 11$. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting 5 from both sides gives $x = 16 - 5 = 11$, and checking gives $11 + 5 = 16$.
- $x = 21$.
  - _why:_ This comes from adding 5 to 16 instead of using the inverse operation. Subtracting 5 from both sides gives $x = 11$.
- $x = 10$.
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one subtraction error, since $10 + 5 = 15$, not 16. Correctly calculating $16 - 5$ gives $x = 11$.
- $x = 3.2$.
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing 16 by 5, but division does not undo addition. Subtracting 5 from both sides gives $x = 11$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $m - 6 = 13$.

**Back:** $m = 19$.

**Choices:**

- $m = 19$. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding 6 to both sides cancels the subtraction, giving $m = 13 + 6 = 19$. Substitution confirms that $19 - 6 = 13$.
- $m = 7$.
  - _why:_ This comes from subtracting 6 from 13 rather than undoing the subtraction. Adding 6 to both sides gives $m = 19$.
- $m = 18$.
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one addition error, since $18 - 6 = 12$. Calculating $13 + 6$ correctly gives $m = 19$.
- $m = 78$.
  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying 13 by 6, but multiplication does not undo subtraction. Adding 6 to both sides gives $m = 19$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In $20 - p = 12$, what role does $p$ have?

**Back:** The amount being taken away.

**Choices:**

- The amount being taken away. ✔
  - _why:_ In $20 - p = 12$, $20$ is the starting amount and $p$ is subtracted from it, so $p$ is the amount being taken away.
- The amount that is left over.
  - _why:_ The amount left over is the result, $12$, rather than $p$. The variable $p$ represents the amount being taken away from 20.
- The amount at the beginning.
  - _why:_ The starting amount is $20$, which appears before the subtraction sign. The variable $p$ is the amount being taken away.
- The answer after subtracting.
  - _why:_ The answer after subtracting is $12$, the expression’s resulting difference. The variable $p$ instead represents the amount being taken away.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $20 - p = 12$.

**Back:** $p = 8$.

**Choices:**

- $p = 8$. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting 20 from both sides gives $-p=-8$, and multiplying both sides by $-1$ gives $p=8$. Substitution confirms that $20-8=12$.
- $p = -8$.
  - _why:_ This sign error comes from treating $-p$ as $p$. Subtracting 20 gives $-p=-8$, so multiplying by $-1$ produces the correct answer, $p=8$.
- $p = 32$.
  - _why:_ Adding 12 and 20 ignores that $p$ is being subtracted. Rearranging gives $-p=12-20=-8$, so the correct solution is $p=8$.
- $p = -32$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 12 from 20 finds the correct difference, but assigning it a negative sign reverses the result. Since $20-8=12$, the solution is $p=8$.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a number is written next to a letter, it means [ ___ ].

**Back:** When a number is written next to a letter, it means multiplication.

**Choices:**

- multiplication ✔
  - _why:_ Writing a number directly beside a letter indicates multiplication. For example, $6a$ means $6\times a$, with the multiplication sign omitted by convention.
- addition
  - _why:_ A number beside a letter does not indicate addition; addition requires a plus sign. For example, $6a$ means $6\times a$, so the missing operation is multiplication.
- subtraction
  - _why:_ Subtraction must be shown with a minus sign and is not implied by adjacent symbols. A number written beside a letter represents multiplication.
- division
  - _why:_ Division is usually shown with a fraction line or division symbol, not by placing symbols together. Adjacency such as $6a$ indicates multiplication.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** In $6a$, what is the coefficient of $a$?

**Back:** 6.

**Choices:**

- 6. ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying the variable. In $6a$, the variable $a$ is multiplied by 6, so its coefficient is 6.
- -6.
  - _why:_ A negative coefficient would require an explicit minus sign, as in $-6a$. Since $6a$ is positive, the coefficient of $a$ is 6.
- 1.
  - _why:_ A coefficient of 1 is implied only when no number is written, as in $a$. In $6a$, the written numerical coefficient is 6.
- 0.
  - _why:_ A coefficient of 0 would make the whole term equal to zero. The term $6a$ has 6 multiplying $a$, so the coefficient is 6.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** To solve a multiplication equation, divide by the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To solve a multiplication equation, divide by the coefficient.

**Choices:**

- coefficient ✔
  - _why:_ In an equation such as $6a=24$, the coefficient 6 multiplies the variable. Dividing both sides by that coefficient isolates the variable.
- variable
  - _why:_ Dividing by the variable does not isolate it and may make the equation more complicated. Divide both sides by the numerical coefficient multiplying the variable.
- constant
  - _why:_ A constant is a term without a variable, not necessarily the number multiplying the unknown. To undo multiplication, divide by the variable’s coefficient.
- product
  - _why:_ The product is the result of multiplication, rather than the numerical factor attached to the variable. Divide both sides by the coefficient to isolate the variable.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $8g = 48$.

**Back:** $g = 6$.

**Choices:**

- $g = 6$. ✔
  - _why:_ The variable is multiplied by 8, so dividing both sides by 8 gives $g=48/8=6$. Checking gives $8\times6=48$.
- $g = 40$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 8 from 48 does not undo multiplication by 8. Divide both sides by 8 instead, giving the correct solution $g=6$.
- $g = 384$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 48 by 8 applies another multiplication instead of reversing the original one. Dividing $48$ by the coefficient 8 gives $g=6$.
- $g = 1/6$.
  - _why:_ Dividing 8 by 48 reverses the order of the required calculation. Since $8g=48$, calculate $48/8$ to obtain $g=6$.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** In $d/2 = 6.5$, the fraction line means [ ___ ].

**Back:** In $d/2 = 6.5$, the fraction line means division.

**Choices:**

- division ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction line represents the numerator divided by the denominator. Therefore, $d/2$ means that $d$ is divided by 2.
- multiplication
  - _why:_ Multiplication would be written as $2d$ or $2\times d$, not with a fraction line. The expression $d/2$ represents division by 2.
- subtraction
  - _why:_ Subtraction requires a minus sign, such as $d-2$. A fraction line is a division symbol, so $d/2$ means $d$ divided by 2.
- addition
  - _why:_ Addition requires a plus sign, such as $d+2$, and is not represented by a fraction line. The fraction line means division.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $f/3 = 7$.

**Back:** $f = 21$.

**Choices:**

- $f = 21$. ✔
  - _why:_ Since $f$ is divided by 3, multiply both sides by 3 to reverse the division. This gives $f=7\times3=21$.
- $f = 10$.
  - _why:_ Adding 3 to 7 does not reverse division by 3. Multiplying both sides by 3 gives the correct solution, $f=21$.
- $f = 7/3$.
  - _why:_ Dividing 7 by 3 repeats the division instead of undoing it. Multiply both sides of $f/3=7$ by 3 to get $f=21$.
- $f = 4$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 3 from 7 confuses division with subtraction. Division by 3 is reversed by multiplication by 3, giving $f=21$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Rewrite $h + h + h$ using multiplication.

**Back:** $3h$.

**Choices:**

- $3h$. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding h three times is equivalent to multiplying h by 3, so the expression simplifies to 3h.
- $h^3$.
  - _why:_ A power means repeated multiplication, not repeated addition. Three h terms add to 3h, whereas h³ means h × h × h.
- $h + 3$.
  - _why:_ The coefficient must multiply h rather than being added separately. Since h appears three times, h + h + h becomes 3h.
- $rac{h}{3}$.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 3 would represent sharing h into three equal parts. Here, three copies of h are being added, giving 3h.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $n + n + n = 45$.

**Back:** $n = 15$.

**Choices:**

- $n = 15$. ✔
  - _why:_ The three identical terms add to 3n, so 3n = 45. Dividing both sides by 3 gives n = 15.
- $n = 42$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 3 from 45 confuses the repeated addition with a separate constant. The equation is 3n = 45, so dividing by 3 gives n = 15.
- $n = 135$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 45 by 3 reverses the required operation. Because three n terms total 45, divide 45 by 3 to get n = 15.
- $n = 12$.
  - _why:_ This can result from subtracting the number of terms incorrectly. The repeated terms combine to 3n, and 45 ÷ 3 gives the correct value n = 15.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** A divisor cannot be [ ___ ].

**Back:** A divisor cannot be zero.

**Choices:**

- zero ✔
  - _why:_ Division by zero is undefined, so zero cannot be used as a divisor. Dividing by any non-zero number is mathematically valid.
- one
  - _why:_ One is a valid divisor because dividing by 1 leaves a number unchanged. The value that cannot be a divisor is zero, since division by zero is undefined.
- negative
  - _why:_ Negative numbers can be divisors; they simply produce a result with the appropriate sign. Zero is the only value excluded because division by zero is undefined.
- an integer
  - _why:_ Integers, including many non-zero integers, can be divisors, and divisors can also be fractions. Zero is not allowed because division by zero has no defined result.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $48/y = 8$.

**Back:** $y = 6$.

**Choices:**

- $y = 6$. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply both sides of 48/y = 8 by y, then divide by 8: 48 = 8y, so y = 6.
- $y = 40$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 8 from 48 treats the division as subtraction. Since 48 divided by y equals 8, rearrange using y = 48 ÷ 8, giving y = 6.
- $y = 384$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 48 by 8 reverses the division incorrectly. Solving 48/y = 8 requires dividing 48 by 8, so the correct value is y = 6.
- $y = rac{1}{6}$.
  - _why:_ This takes the reciprocal of the correct value, reversing the variable's position without adjusting the equation properly. Substitution shows 48 ÷ 6 = 8, so y = 6.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you check a value found for an unknown?

**Back:** Substitute it into the original equation.

**Choices:**

- Substitute it into the original equation. ✔
  - _why:_ Substituting the proposed value into the original equation checks whether both sides are equal, confirming that the value satisfies the equation.
- Divide it into the original equation.
  - _why:_ Dividing the value into the equation is not the standard verification method and may change the equation. Substitute the value into the original equation and compare both sides.
- Round it into the original equation.
  - _why:_ Rounding can change an exact solution and may make a correct value appear not to work. Substitute the unrounded value into the original equation to check equality.
- Replace it with zero in the original equation.
  - _why:_ Replacing the unknown with zero ignores the value being tested and only checks one arbitrary case. Substitute the proposed value into the original equation instead.
