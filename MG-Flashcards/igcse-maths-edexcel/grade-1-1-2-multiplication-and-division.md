## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In $6 \times 7 = 42$, what are 6 and 7 called?

**Back:** The factors.

**Choices:**

- The factors. ✔
  - _why:_ The numbers being multiplied are called factors. Here, 6 and 7 are the factors whose multiplication gives the product, 42.
- The dividends.
  - _why:_ Dividends occur in division and are the quantities being divided. In this multiplication, 6 and 7 are called the factors.
- The products.
  - _why:_ Products are the results of multiplication, not the numbers being multiplied. Here, 42 is the product, while 6 and 7 are the factors.
- The quotients.
  - _why:_ Quotients are the results of division, so this confuses division vocabulary with multiplication vocabulary. The numbers 6 and 7 are the factors.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In $6 \times 7 = 42$, what is 42 called?

**Back:** The product.

**Choices:**

- The product. ✔
  - _why:_ The result obtained by multiplying two or more factors is called the product. Multiplying 6 by 7 therefore gives the product 42.
- The factor.
  - _why:_ A factor is one of the numbers being multiplied, so 6 and 7 are factors. Their result, 42, is called the product.
- The quotient.
  - _why:_ A quotient is the result of a division calculation, not multiplication. Since 42 results from multiplying 6 and 7, it is the product.
- The dividend.
  - _why:_ A dividend is the number being divided in a division calculation. In the given multiplication, 42 is instead the product of 6 and 7.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In $42 \div 6 = 7$, what is 42 called?

**Back:** The dividend.

**Choices:**

- The dividend. ✔
  - _why:_ The dividend is the quantity being divided. In this calculation, 42 is divided by 6 to produce the quotient 7.
- The divisor.
  - _why:_ The divisor is the number by which the dividend is divided, so 6 is the divisor here. The number 42 is the dividend.
- The quotient.
  - _why:_ The quotient is the result produced by division, which is 7 in this calculation. The starting quantity 42 is called the dividend.
- The product.
  - _why:_ A product is the result of multiplication, which confuses the inverse operation with the one shown. In this division calculation, 42 is the dividend.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ The quotient is the result of dividing one number by another. Dividing the dividend 42 by the divisor 6 gives the quotient 7.

- The divisor.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** Multiplication and division are [ ___ ], meaning they undo each other.

**Back:** Multiplication and division are inverse operations, meaning they undo each other.

**Choices:**

- inverse operations ✔
  - _why:_ Inverse operations reverse one another’s effects. Multiplication can be undone by division, and division can be checked or reversed using multiplication.
- commutative operations
  - _why:_ Commutative operations allow numbers to be reordered, which does not describe operations undoing each other; division is not commutative. Multiplication and division are inverse operations.
- associative operations
  - _why:_ Associative operations allow numbers to be regrouped without changing the result, rather than undoing another operation. Multiplication and division are inverse operations.
- equivalent operations
  - _why:_ Equivalent operations would produce the same effect or value, whereas multiplication and division reverse each other’s effects. They are therefore inverse operations.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Which inverse calculation checks $8 \times 9 = 72$ by dividing by 8?

**Back:** $72 \div 8 = 9$.

**Choices:**

- $72 \div 8 = 9$. ✔
  - _why:_ An inverse division starts with the product, 72, and divides by one factor, 8. This recovers the other factor, 9, confirming the multiplication.
- $72 \div 9 = 8$.
  - _why:_ Dividing the product by 9 checks the multiplication using the other factor, not by dividing by 8 as requested. The required calculation is $72 \div 8 = 9$.
- $8 \div 72 = 9$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the dividend and divisor, so it does not recover the other factor. Starting with the product gives the correct check: $72 \div 8 = 9$.
- $9 \div 8 = 72$.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly divides one original factor by the other instead of dividing the product by a factor. The correct inverse check is $72 \div 8 = 9$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** In 347, the digit 4 is in the [ ___ ] column.

**Back:** In 347, the digit 4 is in the tens column.

**Choices:**

- tens ✔
  - _why:_ Place values increase from right to left: units, tens, then hundreds. In 347, the 4 represents 40, so it occupies the tens column.
- hundreds
  - _why:_ The hundreds digit in 347 is 3, representing 300. The digit 4 represents 40, so it belongs in the tens column.
- units
  - _why:_ The units digit is the rightmost digit, which is 7 in 347. The 4 is one place to its left, in the tens column.
- thousands
  - _why:_ A thousands digit would appear one place to the left of the hundreds digit, but 347 has only three digits. The 4 is in the tens column.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a multiplication column total is 10 or more, you [ ___ ] into the next place-value column.

**Back:** If a multiplication column total is 10 or more, you carry into the next place-value column.

**Choices:**

- carry ✔
  - _why:_ A total of 10 or more means the column contains at least one complete group of ten, so the tens value is carried into the next place-value column.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding does not describe moving a complete group of ten to the next column. The correct term is carry, because the extra tens are transferred to the next place value.
- multiply
  - _why:_ Multiplying changes a quantity by repeated groups, but it does not describe this column method step. The correct term is carry, meaning transfer the tens to the next column.
- round
  - _why:_ Rounding changes a number to an approximate value and is not used for column totals. The correct term is carry, because tens are moved into the next place-value column.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** In short division, where is a remainder carried?

**Back:** Into the next place-value column.

**Choices:**

- Into the next place-value column. ✔
  - _why:_ A remainder represents part left over at one place value. In short division, it is carried into the next place-value column so it can be combined with the next digit.
- Into the next digit.
  - _why:_ A remainder is not simply attached to the next digit; it is carried according to place value. The correct answer is Into the next place-value column.
- Into the previous place-value column.
  - _why:_ Division proceeds from left to right, so a remainder moves to the following column, not backwards. The correct answer is Into the next place-value column.
- Into the units column.
  - _why:_ A remainder may need to move through several columns, so it is not always placed in the units column. It goes into the next place-value column.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** You can divide [ ___ ], but you cannot divide [ ___ ].

**Back:** You can divide zero by a number, but you cannot divide by zero.

**Choices:**

- You can divide zero by a number, but you cannot divide by zero. ✔
  - _why:_ Zero divided by any non-zero number equals zero. Division by zero is undefined because no number multiplied by zero can produce a non-zero dividend.
- You can divide a number by zero, but you cannot divide zero by a number.
  - _why:_ This reverses the rule: division by zero is undefined, while zero divided by a non-zero number is valid and equals zero. The correct sentence has these cases the other way around.
- You can divide zero by zero, but you cannot divide a number by zero.
  - _why:_ Zero divided by zero is also undefined, not a valid exception. The correct statement is that you can divide zero by a non-zero number, but cannot divide by zero.
- You can divide by zero, but you cannot divide zero by a number.
  - _why:_ The first phrase incorrectly treats division by zero as valid, while zero divided by a non-zero number is valid. The correct sentence states the reverse.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Choices:**

- To multiply by a two-digit number, multiply by the ones digit and by the tens digit, then add. ✔
  - _why:_ A two-digit number is made from its ones and tens parts. Multiplying by each part separately and adding the partial products gives the complete product.
- To multiply by a two-digit number, multiply by the tens digit and by the ones digit, then add.
  - _why:_ Although both digits are mentioned, the method is conventionally stated as multiplying by the ones digit first and then the tens digit. The correct sentence uses that order.
- To multiply by a two-digit number, multiply by the hundreds digit and by the tens digit, then add.
  - _why:_ A two-digit number has ones and tens digits, not a hundreds digit. The correct method multiplies by the ones digit and tens digit.
- To multiply by a two-digit number, multiply by the ones digit and by the hundreds digit, then add.
  - _why:_ The hundreds digit belongs to a three-digit number and is not part of a two-digit multiplier. The correct sentence uses the ones digit and tens digit.

- 30 is a tens number, not just 3. ✔
  - _why:_ Thirty means 3 tens, whereas 3 means 3 ones. Multiplying by 30 therefore includes a factor of ten, represented by the extra zero in place-value notation.

  - _why:_ Thirty is not 30 ones in the place-value explanation; it is 3 tens. The correct answer identifies 30 as a tens number, which requires the extra zero.

**Back:** Directly in line with the original decimal point.

**Choices:**

- Directly in line with the original decimal point. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying $48.26$ by 3 does not change the number of decimal places in the factor. Place the decimal point in the product directly below the original point's position.
- At the end of the answer.
  - _why:_ Putting the point at the end would remove the two decimal places from the product. It must be placed directly in line with the original decimal point.
- One place to the right of the original decimal point.
  - _why:_ The decimal point is not shifted one place merely because the multiplier is 3. It goes directly in line with the original decimal point.

  - _why:_ Moving the point before its original position changes the place value incorrectly. The correct placement is directly in line with the original decimal point.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can $18.2$ be written as $18.20$ during division?

**Back:** Adding a zero after the decimal does not change the value.

**Choices:**

- Adding a zero after the decimal does not change the value. ✔
  - _why:_ A trailing zero after the final decimal digit adds zero hundredths, so $18.2$ and $18.20$ represent exactly the same value.
- Adding a zero after the decimal multiplies the value by 10.
  - _why:_ Multiplication by 10 requires moving the decimal point, not appending a trailing zero. The correct value remains unchanged because $18.20=18.2$.
- Adding a zero after the decimal divides the value by 10.
  - _why:_ Division by 10 would move the decimal point left to give $1.82$. A trailing zero changes only the written form, not the value.
- Adding a zero after the decimal changes the tenths into hundredths.
  - _why:_ The digit 2 remains in the tenths column; the added zero occupies the hundredths column. Therefore, the number's value does not change.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** What effect can dividing by a decimal less than 1 have on the answer?

**Back:** It can make the answer bigger.

**Choices:**

- It can make the answer bigger. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing by a positive decimal less than 1 is equivalent to multiplying by a number greater than 1. For example, dividing by $0.5$ doubles a number.
- It can make the answer smaller.
  - _why:_ For positive numbers, this reverses the usual effect: dividing by a decimal between 0 and 1 makes the quotient bigger, not smaller.
- It can leave the answer unchanged.
  - _why:_ A non-zero positive number stays unchanged only when divided by 1. Dividing by a positive decimal less than 1 makes it bigger.
- It can make the answer negative.
  - _why:_ Dividing a positive number by a positive decimal does not change its sign. When the divisor is less than 1, the positive quotient becomes bigger.
