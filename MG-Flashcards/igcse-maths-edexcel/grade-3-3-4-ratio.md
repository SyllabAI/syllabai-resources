# Ratio Flashcards

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A ratio compares how much of one thing there is compared to [ ___ ].

**Back:** A ratio compares how much of one thing there is compared to another thing.

**Choices:**

- another thing ✔
  - _why:_ A ratio compares two quantities, showing how much of one thing there is relative to another thing.
- the same thing
  - _why:_ Comparing a quantity with itself does not provide a useful ratio. A ratio compares one quantity with another thing.
- nothing else
  - _why:_ A ratio requires two quantities to be compared, so it cannot involve nothing else. The correct term is another thing.
- the total amount
  - _why:_ A ratio does not necessarily compare a quantity with the total. It compares one thing with another thing.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Ratio notation uses a [ ___ ] between the numbers.

**Back:** Ratio notation uses a colon between the numbers.

**Choices:**

- colon ✔
  - _why:_ A colon separates the parts of a ratio. For example, three parts to two parts is written as $3:2$.
- semicolon
  - _why:_ A semicolon is a punctuation mark but is not used in standard ratio notation. Ratios use a colon, as in $3:2$.
- comma
  - _why:_ A comma can separate items in a list, but it does not represent a ratio. Ratio notation uses a colon between the numbers.
- division sign
  - _why:_ Ratios are related to division, but standard ratio notation does not use a division sign. It uses a colon, such as $3:2$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the ratio 3 cups of flour to 1 cup of sugar written?

**Back:** $3:1$

**Choices:**

- $3:1$ ✔
  - _why:_ The flour quantity is written first and the sugar quantity second, so 3 cups of flour to 1 cup of sugar is $3:1$.
- $1:3$
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the quantities. Flour is stated first, so the correct ratio of flour to sugar is $3:1$.
- $3:2$
  - _why:_ The sugar quantity is 1 cup, not 2 cups. Keeping the stated order gives the flour-to-sugar ratio $3:1$.
- $4:1$
  - _why:_ Adding the quantities gives 4 total cups, but totals are not used as the first ratio part. The correct ratio is $3:1$.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** Simplify a ratio by dividing all parts by their [ ___ ].

**Back:** Simplify a ratio by dividing all parts by their highest common factor.

**Choices:**

- highest common factor ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing every part by the highest common factor produces equivalent whole-number parts with no remaining common factor.
- lowest common multiple
  - _why:_ A lowest common multiple is useful for finding common denominators, not simplifying ratios. Divide all parts by their highest common factor.
- highest common multiple
  - _why:_ Common multiples continue indefinitely, so there is no highest common multiple. Ratios are simplified by dividing by the highest common factor.
- lowest common factor
  - _why:_ The lowest common factor is normally 1, so dividing by it would not simplify the ratio. Use the highest common factor instead.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $10:5$.

**Back:** $2:1$

**Choices:**

- $2:1$ ✔
  - _why:_ The highest common factor of 10 and 5 is 5. Dividing both parts by 5 gives $10:5=2:1$.
- $5:1$
  - _why:_ This divides the second part by 5 but only divides the first part by 2. Both parts must be divided by 5, giving $2:1$.
- $2:5$
  - _why:_ This divides only the first part by 5 and leaves the second unchanged. Dividing both parts by 5 gives $2:1$.
- $1:2$
  - _why:_ This reverses the order after simplifying. Dividing both original parts by 5 gives $2:1$, not $1:2$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In the ratio $2:3$, how many total parts are there?

**Back:** $5$

**Choices:**

- $5$ ✔
  - _why:_ The total number of parts is found by adding the ratio numbers. Therefore, $2+3=5$ total parts.
- $6$
  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying $2$ and $3$, but total ratio parts are found by addition. The correct calculation is $2+3=5$.
- $3$
  - _why:_ This uses only the larger part of the ratio and ignores the other part. Adding both parts gives $2+3=5$.
- $1$
  - _why:_ This is the difference between the ratio numbers, not their total. Total parts are found by adding: $2+3=5$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** When sharing a total in a ratio, first [ ___ ] to find the [ ___ ].

**Back:** When sharing a total in a ratio, first add the parts to find the total number of parts.

**Choices:**

- When sharing a total in a ratio, first add the parts to find the total number of parts. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio parts gives the total number of equal parts. The amount being shared can then be divided by this total.
- When sharing a total in a ratio, first multiply the parts to find the total number of parts.
  - _why:_ Multiplying the ratio numbers does not give the number of equal parts. Add the parts to find the total number of parts.
- When sharing a total in a ratio, first add the parts to find the difference between the parts.
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio parts gives their total, not their difference. The correct result is the total number of parts used in the sharing calculation.
- When sharing a total in a ratio, first subtract the parts to find the total number of parts.
  - _why:_ Subtracting finds the difference between the ratio numbers, not the total. Add the parts to find the total number of parts.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** £40 is shared in the ratio $1:3$. What is 1 part worth?

**Back:** £10

**Choices:**

- £10 ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio has $1+3=4$ total parts. Dividing £40 by 4 shows that each part is worth £10.
- £30
  - _why:_ £30 is the value of the 3-part share, not one part. Since £40 is divided into 4 parts, one part is worth £10.
- £40
  - _why:_ This treats the whole amount as one part and ignores the other three parts. There are 4 total parts, so one part is £10.
- £13.33
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing £40 by 3 and overlooks the additional 1 part. There are $1+3=4$ parts, making one part worth £10.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** £60 is shared in the ratio $2:3$. What is 1 part worth?

**Back:** £12

**Choices:**

- £12 ✔
  - _why:_ There are $2+3=5$ parts altogether. Dividing £60 by 5 gives £12, so each single part is worth £12.
- £20
  - _why:_ This divides £60 by 3, using only one ratio number. The total number of parts is $2+3=5$, so one part is worth $£60\div5=£12$.
- £30
  - _why:_ This divides £60 by 2 rather than by the total number of parts. Since $2+3=5$, one part is worth $£60\div5=£12$.
- £10
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the ratio numbers as six parts. The ratio $2:3$ has $2+3=5$ parts, so one part is worth $£60\div5=£12$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** £60 is shared in the ratio $2:3$. How much is the 2-part share?

**Back:** £24

**Choices:**

- £24 ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio contains $2+3=5$ parts, so one part is worth $£60\div5=£12$. The 2-part share is therefore $2\times£12=£24$.
- £36
  - _why:_ This is the 3-part share, not the 2-part share. One part is £12, so the required share is $2\times£12=£24$.
- £20
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing £60 by 3 and does not calculate the requested share correctly. One part is £12, making the 2-part share £24.
- £30
  - _why:_ This simply halves the total, ignoring that the ratio is not $1:1$. The five total parts are worth £12 each, so two parts are worth £24.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** After sharing a total in a ratio, what should the shares add back to?

**Back:** The original total

**Choices:**

- The original total ✔
  - _why:_ Ratio sharing divides the whole amount into proportional shares without changing its size. Adding every share together must therefore reproduce the original total.
- The total number of parts
  - _why:_ The total number of parts is used to find one part, but it is not the amount recovered by adding the shares. The shares must add to the original total.
- The largest share
  - _why:_ Adding all shares includes every recipient’s amount, not only the largest allocation. Their combined value must equal the original total that was shared.
- The value of one part
  - _why:_ The value of one part is found before calculating each share. Adding the completed shares together should instead give the original total amount.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** Only divide by the total parts if the number given is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Only divide by the total parts if the number given is the total amount.

**Choices:**

- total amount ✔
  - _why:_ The sum of the ratio parts represents the whole quantity. Therefore, dividing by the total number of parts is appropriate only when the given number is the total amount.
- largest share
  - _why:_ A largest share corresponds only to its own ratio number, so it should be divided by that person’s parts. Only a total amount is divided by the total parts.
- smallest share
  - _why:_ A smallest share represents one person’s ratio parts rather than the whole ratio. Divide it by that person’s parts; divide by total parts only for a total amount.
- difference between shares
  - _why:_ A difference between shares corresponds to the difference between the ratio numbers, not their sum. Dividing by total parts is valid only when given the total amount.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If a number belongs to one person in a ratio, divide by which parts?

**Back:** Their parts

**Choices:**

- Their parts ✔
  - _why:_ A person’s amount corresponds directly to their number of ratio parts. Dividing that amount by their parts finds the value of one part.
- The total parts
  - _why:_ Total parts are used when the given number is the whole amount. If the number belongs to one person, divide by their own parts instead.
- The other person’s parts
  - _why:_ The given amount matches the parts assigned to its owner, not the other person. Divide by that person’s own parts to find one part.
- The difference in parts
  - _why:_ The difference in parts is relevant when a difference between amounts is given. For one person’s amount, divide by the number of parts belonging to them.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Charlie:Dave is $4:7$. Dave gets 35 sweets. What is 1 part worth?

**Back:** 5 sweets

**Choices:**

- 5 sweets ✔
  - _why:_ Dave’s 35 sweets represent 7 parts. Dividing $35\div7$ gives 5, so the value of one part is 5 sweets.
- 8 sweets
  - _why:_ This approximately divides 35 by Charlie’s 4 parts instead of Dave’s 7 parts. Dave’s amount represents 7 parts, so $35\div7=5$ sweets per part.
- 3 sweets
  - _why:_ This incorrectly divides 35 by all $4+7=11$ parts. Since 35 is Dave’s share of 7 parts, one part is $35\div7=5$ sweets.
- 7 sweets
  - _why:_ This confuses Dave’s number of parts with the value of each part. His 7 parts total 35 sweets, so each part is worth $35\div7=5$ sweets.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Charlie:Dave is $4:7$. Dave gets 35 sweets. How many does Charlie get?

**Back:** 20 sweets

**Choices:**

- 20 sweets ✔
  - _why:_ Dave’s 7 parts equal 35 sweets, so one part is worth 5 sweets. Charlie has 4 parts and therefore receives $4\times5=20$ sweets.
- 28 sweets
  - _why:_ This multiplies Charlie’s 4 parts by Dave’s 7 parts, confusing a ratio number with the value of one part. One part is 5 sweets, so Charlie gets 20.
- 15 sweets
  - _why:_ This uses the difference of 3 parts instead of Charlie’s 4 parts. One part is 5 sweets, so Charlie’s share is $4\times5=20$ sweets.
- 44 sweets
  - _why:_ This treats 35 as a total and distributes it using all 11 parts, leading to an unsuitable calculation. Dave’s 35 sweets represent 7 parts, so Charlie gets 20.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Emma:Fred is $5:2$. What is the difference in parts?

**Back:** 3 parts

**Choices:**

- 3 parts ✔
  - _why:_ The difference between the ratio shares is found by subtraction. Fred has 2 parts and Emma has 5, so the difference is $5-2=3$ parts.
- 7 parts
  - _why:_ This adds the ratio numbers, giving the total number of parts rather than their difference. The required difference is $5-2=3$ parts.
- 2 parts
  - _why:_ This selects Fred’s number of parts instead of comparing the two shares. Subtracting the smaller ratio number from the larger gives $5-2=3$ parts.
- 5 parts
  - _why:_ This selects Emma’s number of parts rather than finding the gap between the shares. Their difference is calculated as $5-2=3$ parts.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** If given a difference, divide the amount difference by the [ ___ ] to find [ ___ ].

**Back:** If given a difference, divide the amount difference by the difference in parts to find 1 part.

**Choices:**

- If given a difference, divide the amount difference by the difference in parts to find 1 part. ✔
  - _why:_ The difference between the amounts corresponds to the difference between the ratio parts. Dividing the amount difference by the difference in parts therefore gives the value of 1 part.
- If given a difference, divide the amount difference by the sum of parts to find 1 part.
  - _why:_ Using the sum of parts is appropriate when you are given a total amount, not a difference. For a difference, divide by the difference in parts to find 1 part.
- If given a difference, divide the amount difference by the difference in parts to find the total amount.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the difference in parts finds the value of 1 part, not the total amount. The total would require multiplying 1 part by all the ratio parts.
- If given a difference, divide the amount difference by the total number of parts to find 1 part.
  - _why:_ The total number of parts is used with a total amount, whereas a difference must be matched with the difference in parts. This mistake gives an incorrect value for 1 part.

**Back:** £12

  - _why:_ This treats Fred’s share as 5 parts or uses the ratio incorrectly. The £18 difference represents 3 parts, making each part £6 and Fred’s 2 parts £12.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** BC is three times AB. What is the ratio AB:BC?

**Back:** $1:3$

**Choices:**

- $1:3$ ✔
  - _why:_ If BC is three times AB, then for every 1 part of AB there are 3 parts of BC. Therefore, the ratio AB:BC is $1:3$.
- $3:1$
  - _why:_ This reverses the requested order and gives BC:AB rather than AB:BC. Since BC is three times AB, the ratio in the stated order is $1:3$.
- $1:2$
  - _why:_ The multiplier is three, not two. Taking AB as 1 part means BC is 3 parts, so AB:BC is $1:3$.
- $2:3$
  - _why:_ This does not represent BC being three times AB, because the second quantity is only 1.5 times the first. Setting AB to 1 gives the correct ratio $1:3$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** AB:BC is $1:3$ and AC is 80 m. What is AB?

**Back:** 20 m

**Choices:**

- 20 m ✔
  - _why:_ AB:BC has 4 parts altogether, and AC is 80 m. Each part is $80 \div 4=20$ m, so AB, which is 1 part, is 20 m.
- 40 m
  - _why:_ This halves 80 m rather than dividing it into the 4 total ratio parts. Each part is 20 m, and AB is one part, so the correct answer is 20 m.
- 60 m
  - _why:_ This calculates the length of BC, which contains 3 of the 4 parts, not AB. One part is 20 m, so AB is 20 m.
- 80 m
  - _why:_ This uses the whole length AC as AB and ignores the ratio. Since AB is only 1 of 4 equal parts of 80 m, its length is 20 m.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Red:blue counters are $3:4$. What fraction are red?

**Back:** $\frac{3}{7}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{7}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are 3 red parts and 4 blue parts, giving 7 parts altogether. The fraction that are red is therefore $\frac{3}{7}$.
- $\frac{4}{7}$
  - _why:_ $$\frac{4}{7}$ is the fraction of counters that are blue, because blue represents 4 of the 7 total parts. Red represents 3 of the 7 parts: $\frac{3}{7}$.
- $\frac{3}{4}$
  - _why:_ This uses the red-to-blue ratio as a fraction instead of comparing red with all counters. The denominator must be $3+4=7$, giving $\frac{3}{7}$.
- $\frac{3}{8}$
  - _why:_ The denominator 8 does not equal the total number of parts; the ratio contains 3 red and 4 blue parts, totalling 7. Therefore the red fraction is $\frac{3}{7}$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Red:blue counters are $3:4$. What fraction are blue?

**Back:** $\frac{4}{7}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{4}{7}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are 3 red parts and 4 blue parts, so there are 7 parts altogether. Blue accounts for 4 of those 7 parts, giving $\frac{4}{7}$.
- $\frac{3}{7}$
  - _why:_ $$\frac{3}{7}$ represents the fraction of counters that are red, not blue. With 7 total parts and 4 blue parts, the blue fraction is $\frac{4}{7}$.
- $\frac{4}{3}$
  - _why:_ This compares blue with red but does not give blue as a fraction of all counters. The denominator must include both colours, so the correct fraction is $\frac{4}{7}$.
- $\frac{4}{8}$
  - _why:_ The denominator should be the total number of ratio parts, $3+4=7$, not 8. Therefore 4 blue parts represent $\frac{4}{7}$ of the counters.
