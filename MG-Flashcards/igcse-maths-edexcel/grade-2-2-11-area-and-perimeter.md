## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between area and perimeter?

**Back:** Area: the amount of flat space inside a shape.Perimeter: the total distance around the outside edge.

**Choices:**

- Area: the amount of flat space inside a shape.Perimeter: the total distance around the outside edge. ✔
  - _why:_ Area describes the surface enclosed by a shape, while perimeter measures the complete distance around its boundary. They use different units: square units for area and length units for perimeter.
- Area: the total distance around the outside edge.Perimeter: the amount of flat space inside a shape.
  - _why:_ The two definitions have been reversed, a common confusion between what is inside a shape and what goes around its boundary. Area is the inside space, while perimeter is the outside distance.
- Area: the longest distance across a shape.Perimeter: the amount of flat space inside a shape.
  - _why:_ The longest distance across a shape is a measurement such as a diagonal, not area. Area measures inside space, while perimeter measures the total distance around the outside edge.
- Area: the total distance around the outside edge.Perimeter: the longest distance across a shape.
  - _why:_ This confuses both concepts with other measurements. Area is the flat space inside a shape, and perimeter is found by adding all the lengths around its outside edge.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Area is measured in [ ___ ] (e.g. cm²), while perimeter is measured in length units (e.g. cm).

**Back:** Area is measured in square units (e.g. cm²), while perimeter is measured in length units (e.g. cm).

**Choices:**

- square units ✔
  - _why:_ Area measures the amount of two-dimensional space covered by a shape, so its units have two dimensions, such as cm². These are called square units.
- length units
  - _why:_ Length units such as centimetres measure one-dimensional distance and are used for perimeter. Area requires square units, such as cm², because it covers a surface.
- cubic units
  - _why:_ Cubic units measure volume in three dimensions, such as cm³. Area is two-dimensional, so it is measured in square units such as cm².
- angle units
  - _why:_ Angle units such as degrees measure rotation or the size of an angle, not surface space. Area is measured in square units, such as cm².

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the area of a rectangle?

**Back:** $$A = l \times w$ (length multiplied by width).

**Choices:**

- $$A = l \times w$ (length multiplied by width). ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle’s area is found by multiplying its length by its width, so the formula is $A = l \times w$. Both measurements must use compatible length units.
- $$A = 2l + 2w$ (twice the length plus twice the width).
  - _why:_ This formula adds all four sides, so it finds the rectangle’s perimeter rather than its area. Area requires multiplication: $A = l \times w$.
- $$A = l + w$ (length plus width).
  - _why:_ Adding the length and width does not cover the rectangle’s surface and is not an area formula. The correct calculation multiplies them: $A = l \times w$.
- $$A = l \div w$ (length divided by width).
  - _why:_ Dividing the dimensions does not calculate the rectangular surface and can also produce unsuitable units. Multiply length by width to find area: $A = l \times w$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the perimeter of a rectangle?

**Back:** $$P = 2l + 2w$ (or $P = 2(l + w)$).

**Choices:**

- $$P = 2l + 2w$ (or $P = 2(l + w)$). ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle has two sides of length $l$ and two sides of width $w$, so its perimeter is $2l + 2w$, equivalently $2(l + w)$.
- $$P = l + w$ (length plus width).
  - _why:_ This adds only one length and one width, omitting the opposite sides. A rectangle’s perimeter includes two of each, so $P = 2l + 2w$.
- $$P = l \times w$ (length multiplied by width).
  - _why:_ Multiplying length by width calculates the rectangle’s area, not the distance around it. The perimeter formula adds both pairs of equal sides: $P = 2l + 2w$.
- $$P = 2l + w$ (twice the length plus the width).
  - _why:_ This includes two lengths but only one width, so one side is missing from the boundary total. The correct formula includes two widths as well: $P = 2l + 2w$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the side length of a square if you know its area?

**Back:** Calculate the square root of the area ($\sqrt{A}$).

**Choices:**

- Calculate the square root of the area ($\sqrt{A}$). ✔
  - _why:_ For a square, area equals side length squared, $A=s^2$. Taking the square root reverses the squaring operation, giving the side length as $s=\sqrt{A}$.
- Calculate the square of the area ($A^2$).
  - _why:_ Squaring the area moves further away from the side length and produces incorrect units. Since $A=s^2$, take the square root to obtain $s=\sqrt{A}$.
- Calculate half of the area ($A \div 2$).
  - _why:_ Halving the area has no general connection with a square’s side length. The side is the number which squares to give the area, so use $\sqrt{A}$.
- Calculate the cube root of the area ($\sqrt[3]{A}$).
  - _why:_ A cube root reverses cubing and is associated with three-dimensional volume, not the area of a square. Square area requires the square root, $\sqrt{A}$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** A square has area 81 cm². What is its perimeter?

**Back:** Find side length: $\sqrt{81} = 9$ cm.Multiply by 4: $9 \times 4 = 36$ cm.

**Choices:**

- Find side length: $\sqrt{81} = 9$ cm.Multiply by 4: $9 \times 4 = 36$ cm. ✔
  - _why:_ The side length of a square is the square root of its area, so $\sqrt{81}=9$ cm. A square has four equal sides, giving a perimeter of $4 \times 9=36$ cm.
- Find side length: $81 \div 4 = 20.25$ cm.Multiply by 4: $20.25 \times 4 = 81$ cm.
  - _why:_ Dividing the area by four treats the area as though it were a perimeter. The side length comes from $\sqrt{81}=9$ cm, so the perimeter is $4 \times 9=36$ cm.
- Find side length: $\sqrt{81} = 9$ cm.Multiply by 2: $9 \times 2 = 18$ cm.
  - _why:_ The square root correctly finds the side, but multiplying by two counts only a pair of sides. A square has four equal sides, so the perimeter is $9 \times 4=36$ cm.
- Find side length: $81$ cm.Multiply by 4: $81 \times 4 = 324$ cm.
  - _why:_ The area value is not itself the side length; confusing these gives an excessively large perimeter. The side is $\sqrt{81}=9$ cm, making the perimeter $36$ cm.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** A square has perimeter 32 cm. What is its area?

**Back:** Find side length: $32 \div 4 = 8$ cm.Square the side: $8^2 = 64$ cm².

**Choices:**

- Find side length: $32 \div 4 = 8$ cm.Square the side: $8^2 = 64$ cm². ✔
  - _why:_ A square’s four equal sides give a side length of $32 \div 4=8$ cm. Squaring this side gives the area: $8^2=64$ cm².
- Find side length: $32 \div 4 = 8$ cm.Multiply the side by 4: $8 \times 4 = 32$ cm².
  - _why:_ The side length is found correctly, but multiplying by four returns the perimeter rather than the area. Area requires squaring the side, so $8^2=64$ cm².
- Find side length: $32 \div 2 = 16$ cm.Square the side: $16^2 = 256$ cm².
  - _why:_ Dividing the perimeter by two counts two sides instead of all four, making the side twice as large. Use $32 \div 4=8$ cm, then square to get $64$ cm².
- Find side length: $\sqrt{32}$ cm.Square the side: $(\sqrt{32})^2 = 32$ cm².
  - _why:_ Taking a square root is used when area is known and the side is required. Here the perimeter gives the side as $32 \div 4=8$ cm, producing an area of $64$ cm².

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** If the length of a rectangle is twice its width ($w$), what is the expression for its perimeter?

**Back:** $$6w$ (from $w + w + 2w + 2w$).

**Choices:**

- $$6w$ (from $w + w + 2w + 2w$). ✔
  - _why:_ The two widths total $2w$ and the two lengths total $4w$, so the perimeter is $2w + 4w = 6w$.
- $$4w$ (from $w + w + w + w$).
  - _why:_ This treats all four sides as width $w$, ignoring that each length is $2w$. Adding $w + w + 2w + 2w$ gives the correct perimeter, $6w$.
- $$3w$ (from $w + 2w$).
  - _why:_ This adds only one width and one length, giving half the perimeter. A rectangle has two of each side, so the correct perimeter is $2(w + 2w) = 6w$.
- $$2w^2$ (from $2w \times w$).
  - _why:_ Multiplying length by width calculates the area, not the perimeter. Perimeter requires adding all four sides, which gives $w + w + 2w + 2w = 6w$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** If the length of a rectangle is three times its width ($w$), what is the expression for its area?

**Back:** $$3w^2$ (from $3w \times w$).

**Choices:**

- $$3w^2$ (from $3w \times w$). ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle’s area is length multiplied by width. With length $3w$ and width $w$, the area is $3w \times w = 3w^2$.
- $$8w$ (from $w + w + 3w + 3w$).
  - _why:_ Adding all four sides calculates the perimeter rather than the area. Multiplying the length $3w$ by the width $w$ gives the correct area, $3w^2$.
- $$4w^2$ (from $(3w + w) \times w$).
  - _why:_ This incorrectly adds the length and width before multiplying. Rectangle area is simply length multiplied by width, so $3w \times w = 3w^2$.
- $$9w^2$ (from $3w \times 3w$).
  - _why:_ This uses $3w$ for both dimensions, incorrectly treating the rectangle as a square. Its width is $w$, so the area is $3w \times w = 3w^2$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the area of a triangle?

**Back:** $$A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ (half $\times$ base $\times$ perpendicular height).

**Choices:**

- $$A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ (half $\times$ base $\times$ perpendicular height). ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle occupies half the area of a parallelogram with the same base and perpendicular height. Therefore its area is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $$A = bh$ (base $\times$ perpendicular height).
  - _why:_ This is the area formula for a parallelogram and misses the factor of one-half. A triangle’s area is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, using its perpendicular height.
- $$A = \frac{1}{2}(b + h)$ (half $\times$ base plus height).
  - _why:_ Adding the base and height cannot produce an area because it does not create squared units. The dimensions must be multiplied, giving $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $$A = \frac{1}{2}b^2$ (half $\times$ base $\times$ base).
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses the base twice and ignores the perpendicular height. Triangle area depends on both measurements, so the correct formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** For triangles and parallelograms, the height used in area formulas must be [ ___ ] to the base, never the slanted side.

**Back:** For triangles and parallelograms, the height used in area formulas must be perpendicular to the base, never the slanted side.

**Choices:**

- perpendicular ✔
  - _why:_ The height is the shortest distance from the base to the opposite vertex or side, measured at a right angle. Therefore it must be perpendicular to the base.
- parallel
  - _why:_ A parallel line never meets the base, so it cannot represent the height between the base and the opposite vertex or side. The required height is perpendicular to the base.
- diagonal
  - _why:_ A diagonal joins non-adjacent vertices and is not necessarily at a right angle to the base. Area formulas require the perpendicular distance from the base.
- adjacent
  - _why:_ An adjacent side may be slanted and therefore may not measure the shortest distance from the base. The height used for area must be perpendicular to the base.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** For triangles and parallelograms, the height used in area formulas must be perpendicular to the base, never the [ ___ ] side.

**Back:** For triangles and parallelograms, the height used in area formulas must be perpendicular to the base, never the slanted side.

**Choices:**

- slanted ✔
  - _why:_ A slanted side is not generally perpendicular to the base, so using it would give an incorrect area. The height must be the right-angled distance from the base.
- parallel
  - _why:_ The warning concerns a visible side that learners often mistake for the height, namely the slanted side. A parallel side is not a candidate for the perpendicular height.
- horizontal
  - _why:_ Whether a side is horizontal depends only on how the shape is drawn, not on its role in the formula. The side to avoid using as height is the slanted side.
- opposite
  - _why:_ In a parallelogram, the opposite side is parallel to the chosen base and helps define the shape. The common error is using the slanted side instead of perpendicular height.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the area of a parallelogram?

**Back:** $$A = bh$ (base $\times$ perpendicular height).

**Choices:**

- $$A = bh$ (base $\times$ perpendicular height). ✔
  - _why:_ A parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. Its area is therefore $A = bh$.
- $$A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ (half $\times$ base $\times$ perpendicular height).
  - _why:_ The factor of one-half belongs to the triangle area formula. A parallelogram uses the full product of its base and perpendicular height, so $A = bh$.
- $$A = bs$ (base $\times$ slanted side).
  - _why:_ The slanted side does not measure the perpendicular distance between the parallel bases. The area must use perpendicular height, giving $A = bh$.
- $$A = 2(b + h)$ (twice base plus height).
  - _why:_ This resembles a perimeter calculation because it adds lengths rather than multiplying dimensions. Parallelogram area is base multiplied by perpendicular height, so $A = bh$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A triangle has area 25 cm² and the base is twice the height ($2h$). Find the height.

**Back:** Substitute: $25 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2h \times h$.Simplify: $25 = h^2$.Solve: $h = 5$ cm.

**Choices:**

- Substitute: $25 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2h \times h$.Simplify: $25 = h^2$.Solve: $h = 5$ cm. ✔
  - _why:_ Substituting base $2h$ into $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ gives $25 = h^2$. Taking the positive square root gives $h = 5$ cm, since height cannot be negative.
- Substitute: $25 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2h \times h$.Simplify: $25 = 2h^2$.Solve: $h = 3.54$ cm.
  - _why:_ This fails to cancel $\frac{1}{2}$ with the factor $2$ in the base. The equation simplifies to $25 = h^2$, so the height is $5$ cm.
- Substitute: $25 = 2h \times h$.Simplify: $25 = 2h^2$.Solve: $h = 3.54$ cm.
  - _why:_ This uses the rectangle or parallelogram formula and omits the triangle’s factor of $\frac{1}{2}$. Correct substitution gives $25 = \frac{1}{2}(2h)(h) = h^2$, hence $h = 5$ cm.
- Substitute: $25 = \frac{1}{2} \times 2h \times h$.Simplify: $25 = h^2$.Solve: $h = 12.5$ cm.
  - _why:_ This divides $25$ by $2$ instead of taking the square root when solving $h^2 = 25$. The positive square root gives the correct height, $h = 5$ cm.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the geometric properties of a parallelogram?

**Back:** A four-sided shape with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

**Choices:**

- A four-sided shape with two pairs of parallel opposite sides. ✔
  - _why:_ A parallelogram is a quadrilateral whose two pairs of opposite sides are parallel. This defining property also leads to equal opposite sides and equal opposite angles.
- A four-sided shape with one pair of parallel opposite sides.
  - _why:_ Having only one pair of parallel opposite sides describes a trapezium in UK terminology. A parallelogram must have two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

  - _why:_ Opposite sides cannot be perpendicular because they face each other rather than meeting. In a parallelogram, both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

  - _why:_ A quadrilateral has only two pairs of opposite sides, so four such pairs are impossible. A parallelogram is defined by two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the area of a trapezium?

**Back:** $$A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$

**Choices:**

- $$A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ ✔
  - _why:_ The parallel side lengths are added, multiplied by the perpendicular height, and halved. This is equivalent to multiplying the average length of the parallel sides by the height.
- $$A = (a+b)h$
  - _why:_ Omitting the factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ doubles the calculated area. The correct formula uses the average of the parallel sides: $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$A = \frac{1}{2}(a-b)h$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the parallel sides does not find their average and can even produce a negative result. They must be added, giving $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$A = \frac{1}{2}abh$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the two parallel side lengths together incorrectly treats the trapezium like a three-dimensional calculation. The correct formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** In the trapezium formula $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, what do $a$ and $b$ represent?

**Back:** The lengths of the parallel sides.

**Choices:**

- The lengths of the parallel sides. ✔
  - _why:_ The variables $a$ and $b$ are the two parallel side lengths. Their mean is multiplied by the perpendicular height $h$ to calculate the trapezium’s area.
- The lengths of the non-parallel sides.
  - _why:_ The sloping or non-parallel sides are not added in the area formula. The variables $a$ and $b$ represent the lengths of the parallel sides.
- The length and the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ The perpendicular height is represented separately by $h$, not by $a$ or $b$. Both $a$ and $b$ are lengths of the parallel sides.
- The lengths of the two diagonals.
  - _why:_ A trapezium’s diagonals are not used in this area formula. The variables $a$ and $b$ represent the lengths of its two parallel sides.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** What three steps are used to calculate trapezium area?

**Back:** Add the parallel sides.Halve the result.Multiply by the perpendicular height.

**Choices:**

- Add the parallel sides.Halve the result.Multiply by the perpendicular height. ✔
  - _why:_ These steps apply $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$: add the parallel side lengths, divide their sum by two, then multiply by the perpendicular height.
- Subtract the parallel sides.Halve the result.Multiply by the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the parallel sides finds their difference rather than their average. They must be added, the sum halved, and the result multiplied by the perpendicular height.
- Add the parallel sides.Double the result.Multiply by the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ Doubling instead of halving makes the answer four times the correct area. Add the parallel sides, halve their sum, then multiply by the perpendicular height.
- Add the non-parallel sides.Halve the result.Multiply by the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ The area formula uses the parallel sides, not the sloping or non-parallel sides. Add the parallel sides, halve the sum, then multiply by the perpendicular height.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** When calculating the perimeter of a compound shape, which edges are ignored?

**Back:** Any joined edges inside the shape; only the outside boundary is counted.

**Choices:**

- Any joined edges inside the shape; only the outside boundary is counted. ✔
  - _why:_ Perimeter is the total distance around a shape’s exterior. Edges where component shapes join are internal, so they do not form part of the outside boundary.
- Any curved edges inside the shape; only the straight boundary is counted.
  - _why:_ Curved exterior edges still contribute to perimeter; being curved does not exclude them. Only joined edges inside the compound shape are ignored, while the entire outside boundary is counted.
- Any short edges inside the shape; only the longest boundary is counted.
  - _why:_ Edge length does not determine whether an edge contributes to perimeter. Every outside edge is counted, while joined edges inside the compound shape are ignored.
- Any outside edges around the shape; only the joined boundary is counted.
  - _why:_ This reverses the perimeter rule by counting internal joins instead of the exterior. Joined edges inside are ignored, and only the outside boundary is included.

## Card 22 — `cloze`

**Front:** A square with side $s$ has area $\text{[...]}$ and perimeter $4s$.

**Back:** A square with side $s$ has area $\boxed{s^2}$ and perimeter $4s$.

**Choices:**

- $$s^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ A square’s area is length multiplied by width. Both dimensions equal $s$, so its area is $s \times s = s^2$.
- $$s$
  - _why:_ Using $s$ alone gives a length rather than an area and misses the second dimension. Multiplying the two equal side lengths gives the correct area, $s^2$.
- $$2s$
  - _why:_ Adding two side lengths gives neither the square’s area nor its full perimeter. Area requires multiplication of the dimensions, so it is $s \times s = s^2$.
- $$4s^2$
  - _why:_ Multiplying $s^2$ by four incorrectly treats the square as four separate regions of area $s^2$. The whole square’s area is simply $s^2$.

## Card 23 — `cloze`

**Front:** A square with side $s$ has area $s^2$ and perimeter $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** A square with side $s$ has area $s^2$ and perimeter $\boxed{4s}$.

**Choices:**

- $$4s$ ✔
  - _why:_ A square has four equal sides, each of length $s$. Adding them gives $s+s+s+s=4s$, so the perimeter is $4s$.
- $$s^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring the side length calculates the square’s area, not the distance around it. Adding its four equal sides gives the correct perimeter, $4s$.
- $$2s$
  - _why:_ Multiplying by two counts only two of the square’s four equal sides. The perimeter includes every side, so it is $4s$.
- $$8s$
  - _why:_ Multiplying by eight double-counts each of the square’s four sides. Each side is included once, giving the correct perimeter of $4s$.
