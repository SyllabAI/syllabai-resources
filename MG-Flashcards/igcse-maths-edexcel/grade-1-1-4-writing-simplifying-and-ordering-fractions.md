## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a fraction, the numerator is the [ ___ ] number.

**Back:** In a fraction, the numerator is the top number.

**Choices:**

- top ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator is written above the fraction bar, so it is the top number in a fraction.
- bottom
  - _why:_ The bottom number is the denominator, not the numerator. The numerator is the top number, showing how many parts are being considered.
- left
  - _why:_ Fractions are described using positions above and below the fraction bar, not left and right. The numerator is the top number.
- larger
  - _why:_ The numerator is not defined by its size and may be larger or smaller than the denominator. It is the top number in the fraction.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a fraction, the denominator is the [ ___ ] number.

**Back:** In a fraction, the denominator is the bottom number.

**Choices:**

- bottom ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator is written below the fraction bar, so it is the bottom number in a fraction.
- top
  - _why:_ The top number is the numerator, not the denominator. The denominator is the bottom number and usually shows the total number of equal parts.
- right
  - _why:_ Fractions use positions above and below the fraction bar, not right and left. The denominator is the bottom number.
- smaller
  - _why:_ The denominator is not defined by being smaller and can have any value appropriate to the fraction. It is identified as the bottom number.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** If 3 out of 8 equal parts are shaded, what fraction is shaded?

**Back:** $\frac{3}{8}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{8}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator records the 3 shaded parts, while the denominator records all 8 equal parts, giving $\frac{3}{8}$.
- $\frac{8}{3}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the numerator and denominator. The 3 shaded parts belong in the numerator and the 8 total parts belong in the denominator, giving $\frac{3}{8}$.
- $\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses the 5 unshaded parts as the denominator. The denominator must count all 8 equal parts, so the shaded fraction is $\frac{3}{8}$.
- $\frac{5}{8}$
  - _why:_ This gives the fraction that is unshaded because $8-3=5$. The question asks for the 3 shaded parts out of 8, so the answer is $\frac{3}{8}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** For a fraction of a group, what goes in the numerator?

**Back:** The number of chosen objects.

**Choices:**

- The number of chosen objects. ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator counts the objects with the required property, so it contains the number of chosen objects in the group.
- The total number of objects.
  - _why:_ The total number of objects belongs in the denominator. The numerator counts only the chosen objects that meet the condition in the question.
- The number of unchosen objects.
  - _why:_ Counting unchosen objects gives the complementary fraction rather than the requested fraction. The numerator must be the number of chosen objects.
- The number of different objects.
  - _why:_ The numerator does not count how many types of object there are. It counts the individual objects selected by the condition in the question.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** For a fraction of a group, what goes in the denominator?

**Back:** The total number of objects.

**Choices:**

- The total number of objects. ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator represents the whole group, so it contains the total number of objects being considered.
- The number of chosen objects.
  - _why:_ The chosen objects are counted in the numerator. The denominator must represent the whole group, so it is the total number of objects.
- The number of unchosen objects.
  - _why:_ The unchosen objects form only part of the group, not the whole. The denominator is the total number of objects, including chosen and unchosen ones.
- The number of different objects.
  - _why:_ The denominator counts every individual object, not the number of object types. It must contain the total number of objects in the group.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In a fraction question, what does “the rest” mean you should do?

**Back:** Subtract from the total.

**Choices:**

- Subtract from the total. ✔
  - _why:_ “The rest” means the amount remaining after the known part is removed, so subtract the known amount from the total.
- Add to the total.
  - _why:_ Adding would make the amount exceed the original total. To find “the rest”, subtract the known part from the total.
- Divide by the total.
  - _why:_ Division may be used later to form a fraction, but it does not find the remaining amount. First subtract the known part from the total.
- Multiply by the total.
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not find what remains and usually increases the quantity. “The rest” is found by subtracting the known amount from the total.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** There are 13 pencils; 8 are black and the rest are orange. What fraction are orange?

**Back:** $\frac{5}{13}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{5}{13}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are $13-8=5$ orange pencils. The orange pencils are therefore 5 of the 13 pencils, giving $\frac{5}{13}$.
- $\frac{8}{13}$
  - _why:_ This is the fraction of pencils that are black. There are $13-8=5$ orange pencils, so the orange fraction is $\frac{5}{13}$.
- $\frac{5}{8}$
  - _why:_ Although there are 5 orange pencils, the denominator must be the total number of pencils, not the number of black pencils. The answer is $\frac{5}{13}$.
- $\frac{13}{5}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the part and the total. The 5 orange pencils go in the numerator and all 13 pencils go in the denominator, giving $\frac{5}{13}$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Equivalent fractions have the same [ ___ ], even if they look different.

**Back:** Equivalent fractions have the same value, even if they look different.

**Choices:**

- value ✔
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions represent the same proportion or number, so they have the same value even when their numerators and denominators differ.
- numerator
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions can have different numerators; for example, $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{2}{4}$. What remains the same is their value.
- denominator
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions can have different denominators because both parts may be multiplied or divided by the same number. Their value, not their denominator, stays the same.
- appearance
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions often look different because their numerators and denominators are different. They are equivalent because they represent the same value.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you make an equivalent fraction?

**Back:** Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Choices:**

- Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number. ✔
  - _why:_ Applying the same non-zero multiplier or divisor to both parts preserves the fraction’s value because the numerator-to-denominator ratio remains unchanged.
- Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by different numbers.
  - _why:_ Using different numbers changes the numerator-to-denominator ratio. An equivalent fraction is made by multiplying or dividing both parts by the same non-zero number.
- Add or subtract the same number from the numerator and denominator.
  - _why:_ Adding or subtracting equal amounts generally changes a fraction’s value. Equivalence is maintained by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.
- Multiply or divide the numerator but leave the denominator unchanged.
  - _why:_ Changing only the numerator changes the fraction’s value. Both the numerator and denominator must be multiplied or divided by the same non-zero number.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Which is not equivalent to $\frac{3}{5}$: $\frac{6}{10}$, $\frac{9}{15}$, $\frac{12}{20}$, $\frac{15}{30}$, $\frac{18}{30}$?

**Back:** $\frac{15}{30}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{15}{30}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator and denominator by 15 gives $\frac{1}{2}$, not $\frac{3}{5}$, so this is the fraction that is not equivalent.
- $\frac{6}{10}$
  - _why:_ This is formed by multiplying both parts of $\frac{3}{5}$ by 2, so it is equivalent. The non-equivalent fraction is $\frac{15}{30}=\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\frac{9}{15}$
  - _why:_ This is formed by multiplying both parts of $\frac{3}{5}$ by 3, so it is equivalent. The non-equivalent fraction is $\frac{15}{30}=\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\frac{18}{30}$
  - _why:_ Dividing both parts by 6 gives $\frac{3}{5}$, so this fraction is equivalent. The non-equivalent fraction is $\frac{15}{30}=\frac{1}{2}$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Choices:**

- same number ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator and denominator by the same common factor preserves the fraction’s value while producing an equivalent fraction with smaller terms.
- larger number
  - _why:_ Dividing both terms by whichever number is larger will not generally produce whole numbers. They must be divided by the same number that is a common factor.
- smaller number
  - _why:_ The smaller term is not necessarily a factor of the larger term. Simplification requires dividing the numerator and denominator by the same common factor.
- next prime number
  - _why:_ The next prime number may not divide either term exactly. The numerator and denominator must instead be divided by the same common factor.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor apart from [ ___ ].

**Back:** A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor apart from 1.

**Choices:**

- 1 ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction is fully simplified when the greatest common factor of its numerator and denominator is 1, so no further common division is possible.
- 0
  - _why:_ Zero is not a factor used to test simplest form, and division by zero is undefined. The numerator and denominator must have no common factor apart from 1.
- 2
  - _why:_ If both terms share a factor of 2, they can still be divided by 2, so the fraction is not simplest. Their only common factor must be 1.
- 10
  - _why:_ A common factor of 10 would allow both terms to be divided again. In simplest form, the numerator and denominator have no common factor apart from 1.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{36}{48}$.

**Back:** $\frac{3}{4}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{4}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The greatest common factor of 36 and 48 is 12. Dividing both terms by 12 gives $\frac{36\div12}{48\div12}=\frac{3}{4}$.
- $\frac{6}{8}$
  - _why:_ Dividing both terms by 6 gives an equivalent fraction, but it is not fully simplified because both terms still share 2. Dividing again gives $\frac{3}{4}$.
- $\frac{4}{6}$
  - _why:_ This results from dividing the numerator and denominator inconsistently, so it changes the fraction’s value. Dividing both original terms by 12 gives $\frac{3}{4}$.
- $\frac{4}{5}$
  - _why:_ Dividing 36 by 9 and 48 by a different amount changes the fraction’s value. Both terms should be divided by 12, giving $\frac{3}{4}$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** For an increase as a fraction of last year’s cost, what is the denominator?

**Back:** The original amount.

**Choices:**

- The original amount. ✔
  - _why:_ An increase as a fraction of last year’s cost compares the change with the starting value, so the original amount is used as the denominator.
- The new amount.
  - _why:_ Using the new amount measures the increase relative to the final cost rather than last year’s cost. The required denominator is the original amount.
- The increase amount.
  - _why:_ The increase belongs in the numerator because it is the quantity being compared. The denominator is the original amount, representing last year’s cost.
- The average amount.
  - _why:_ Averaging the old and new costs gives neither the required reference value nor the stated comparison. The denominator must be the original amount.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A bus pass rises from £30 to £42. What is the increase as a simplified fraction of last year’s cost?

**Back:** $\frac{2}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The increase is $£42-£30=£12$. Comparing this with the original £30 gives $\frac{12}{30}$, which simplifies to $\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{2}{7}$
  - _why:_ This comes from comparing the £12 increase with the new £42 cost. The denominator should be the original £30, giving $\frac{12}{30}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{7}{5}$
  - _why:_ This compares the new cost, £42, with the old cost, £30, rather than finding the increase. The required fraction is $\frac{12}{30}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This does not use the £12 increase produced by subtracting £30 from £42. The correct comparison is $\frac{12}{30}$, which simplifies to $\frac{2}{5}$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** To order fractions, first change them to a [ ___ ].

**Back:** To order fractions, first change them to a common denominator.

**Choices:**

- common denominator ✔
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions with a common denominator can be ordered by comparing their numerators directly, since each fraction is then divided into equal-sized parts.
- mixed number
  - _why:_ Converting fractions to mixed numbers does not create equally sized fractional parts and is impossible for proper fractions without writing zero wholes. Use a common denominator instead.
- unit fraction
  - _why:_ Rewriting every fraction as a unit fraction would usually change its value because unit fractions have numerator 1. Equivalent fractions should instead be given a common denominator.
- whole number
  - _why:_ Most fractions cannot be changed into whole numbers without rounding, which loses their exact values. They should be converted to equivalent fractions with a common denominator.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Once fractions have the same denominator, which part decides which is smaller?

**Back:** The numerator; smaller numerator means smaller fraction.

**Choices:**

- The numerator; smaller numerator means smaller fraction. ✔
  - _why:_ When fractions have the same denominator, each part is the same size, so the fraction with fewer parts has the smaller numerator and is smaller.
- The denominator; smaller denominator means smaller fraction.
  - _why:_ This reverses the role of the denominator. With equal denominators, compare the numerators because they count how many equal parts are present; the smaller numerator gives the smaller fraction.
- The numerator; larger numerator means smaller fraction.
  - _why:_ The comparison direction is reversed. Equal denominators mean a larger numerator represents more equal parts, so the larger numerator gives the larger fraction, not the smaller one.
- The denominator; larger denominator means smaller fraction.
  - _why:_ A larger denominator does usually make unit fractions smaller, but that is not the rule when denominators are already equal. In that case, compare the numerators.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Put in ascending order: $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{1}{5}$.

**Back:** $\frac{1}{5}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{1}{2}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{1}{5}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{1}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Their decimal values are $0.2$, $0.25$, $0.3$, $0.4$, and $0.5$, respectively, so this list increases from the smallest fraction to the largest.
- $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{1}{5}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{1}{2}$
  - _why:_ This puts $\frac{1}{4}$ before $\frac{1}{5}$, but $0.25$ is greater than $0.2$. The correct ascending order starts $\frac{1}{5}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, then $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\frac{1}{5}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{1}{2}$
  - _why:_ The middle pair is reversed: $\frac{2}{5}=0.4$ is greater than $\frac{3}{10}=0.3$. The correct order places $\frac{3}{10}$ before $\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{1}{5}$
  - _why:_ This is descending order rather than ascending order, beginning with the largest fraction. Ascending order must begin with $\frac{1}{5}$ and end with $\frac{1}{2}$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** To decide which fraction is closer to a target, what should you compare?

**Back:** Compare the gaps from the target.

**Choices:**

- Compare the gaps from the target. ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction is closer when its distance from the target is smaller. Comparing the gaps shows which fraction has the least difference from the target value.
- Compare the numerators.
  - _why:_ Numerators alone cannot show closeness when denominators differ, because the fraction's value depends on both parts. Compare each fraction's gap from the target instead.
- Compare the denominators.
  - _why:_ A denominator only describes the number of equal parts and does not directly measure distance from the target. The correct comparison is the size of each gap from the target.
- Compare the fractions’ products with the target.
  - _why:_ Multiplying fractions by the target does not measure how far apart they are and can give misleading results. Subtract each fraction from the target and compare the gaps.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Which is closer to $\frac{1}{2}$: $\frac{2}{5}$ or $\frac{5}{8}$?

**Back:** $\frac{2}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The gap from $\frac{1}{2}$ is $\frac{1}{10}$ for $\frac{2}{5}$, while the gap is $\frac{1}{8}$ for $\frac{5}{8}$. Since $\frac{1}{10}<\frac{1}{8}$, $\frac{2}{5}$ is closer.
- $\frac{5}{8}$
  - _why:_ This may seem closer because $\frac{5}{8}$ is only slightly above $\frac{1}{2}$, but its gap is $\frac{1}{8}$. The gap for $\frac{2}{5}$ is $\frac{1}{10}$, so $\frac{2}{5}$ is closer.
- $\frac{1}{2}$
  - _why:_ The target itself would have zero gap, but it is not one of the two fractions being compared. Between the given choices, $\frac{2}{5}$ is closer because its gap is $\frac{1}{10}$.
- $\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This is a tempting estimate because it is close to $\frac{1}{2}$, but it is not one of the options being compared. The given fraction with the smaller gap is $\frac{2}{5}$.
