## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the term $7x$, what is the coefficient?

**Back:** 7

**Choices:**

- 7 ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying the variable. Since $7x$ means $7 \times x$, its coefficient is 7.
- -7
  - _why:_ A negative coefficient would make the term $-7x$, not $7x$. The numerical factor multiplying $x$ here is positive, so the coefficient is 7.
- 1
  - _why:_ This confuses the implicit coefficient of $x$ alone with the coefficient in $7x$. The written numerical factor multiplying $x$ is 7.
- 49
  - _why:_ This incorrectly squares the numerical factor rather than identifying it. The coefficient is simply the number multiplying $x$, which is 7.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are $x$ and $x^2$ not like terms?

**Back:** They do not have the exact same variable part ($x^2$ is $x$ multiplied by itself).

**Choices:**

- They do not have the exact same variable part ($x^2$ is $x$ multiplied by itself). ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms must contain identical variables raised to identical powers. Here, $x$ has exponent 1 while $x^2$ has exponent 2, so they cannot be collected.
- They do not have the exact same coefficient ($x^2$ has a coefficient of $2$).
  - _why:_ Both terms have an implicit coefficient of 1, so their coefficients are not different. They are unlike because their variable parts, $x$ and $x^2$, have different powers.
- They do not contain the exact same variable ($x^2$ uses a different variable).
  - _why:_ Both terms use the same variable, $x$. They are not like terms because $x$ and $x^2$ contain different powers of that variable.
- They do not have a numerical coefficient ($x$ and $x^2$ contain no numbers).
  - _why:_ Both terms do have numerical coefficients: each coefficient is implicitly 1. The reason they are unlike is that their variable parts have different exponents.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $7x + 5 + 4x - 2 - 3x$ by collecting like terms.

**Back:** $$8x + 3$

**Choices:**

- $$8x + 3$ ✔
  - _why:_ The variable terms combine to $(7+4-3)x=8x$, while the constants combine to $5-2=3$. Therefore the simplified expression is $8x+3$.
- $$11x + 3$
  - _why:_ This omits the subtraction of $3x$ when collecting variable terms. Including every like term gives $(7+4-3)x=8x$, and the constants give 3.
- $$8x + 7$
  - _why:_ This adds 5 and 2 instead of respecting the negative sign before 2. The constants total $5-2=3$, while the variable terms total $8x$.
- $$8x - 3$
  - _why:_ This reverses the sign of the constant total. Since $5-2=3$, not $-3$, and the variable terms total $8x$, the result is $8x+3$.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** The distributive law for expanding brackets is $k(a + b) = $ $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The distributive law for expanding brackets is $k(a + b) = $ $\boxed{ka + kb}$.

**Choices:**

- $$ka + kb$ ✔
  - _why:_ The factor $k$ multiplies each term inside the bracket. Multiplying it by both $a$ and $b$ gives $ka+kb$.
- $$kab$
  - _why:_ Multiplying $a$ and $b$ together changes addition into multiplication. Distribution requires multiplying $k$ by each separate term, giving $ka+kb$.
- $$ka + b$
  - _why:_ This distributes $k$ to $a$ but leaves $b$ unchanged. The outside factor must multiply both terms, so the expansion is $ka+kb$.
- $$k + a + b$
  - _why:_ This treats multiplication by $k$ as adding $k$ to the bracket’s terms. Distribution instead multiplies both terms by $k$, producing $ka+kb$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the result of expanding $8(4s - 3)$?

**Back:** $$32s - 24$

**Choices:**

- $$32s - 24$ ✔
  - _why:_ Distribute 8 across both terms: $8\times4s=32s$ and $8\times(-3)=-24$. Combining these products gives $32s-24$.
- $$32s - 3$
  - _why:_ This multiplies only the first term by 8 and leaves $-3$ unchanged. The factor 8 must multiply both terms, making the constant $-24$.
- $$12s - 24$
  - _why:_ This adds 8 and 4 instead of multiplying them. The variable product is $8\times4s=32s$, and distributing to $-3$ gives $-24$.
- $$32s + 24$
  - _why:_ This loses the negative sign when multiplying the second term. A positive 8 multiplied by negative 3 gives $-24$, so the expansion is $32s-24$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand $5x(3x - 2)$.

**Back:** $$15x^2 - 10x$

**Choices:**

- $$15x^2 - 10x$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply $5x$ by each bracketed term: $5x\times3x=15x^2$ and $5x\times(-2)=-10x$. This gives $15x^2-10x$.
- $$15x^2 - 10$
  - _why:_ This drops the factor $x$ when multiplying $5x$ by $-2$. That product is $-10x$, so the complete expansion is $15x^2-10x$.
- $$15x^2 - 2x$
  - _why:_ This fails to multiply the second term by the numerical factor 5. Since $5x\times(-2)=-10x$, the correct expansion is $15x^2-10x$.
- $$15x^2 + 10x$
  - _why:_ This loses the negative sign from $-2$. Multiplying positive $5x$ by negative 2 gives $-10x$, so the second term must remain negative.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a common mistake when expanding a bracket like $8(4s - 3)$?

**Back:** Only multiplying the first term (e.g. writing $32s - 3$).

**Choices:**

- Only multiplying the first term (e.g. writing $32s - 3$). ✔
  - _why:_ The factor outside a bracket must multiply every term inside it. Multiplying only $4s$ leaves the constant undistributed; 8 must also multiply $-3$ to give $-24$.
- Multiplying both the first and second terms (e.g. writing $32s - 24$).
  - _why:_ Multiplying both bracketed terms is the required use of the distributive law, not a mistake. It correctly produces $8\times4s=32s$ and $8\times(-3)=-24$.
- Preserving the negative sign (e.g. writing $32s - 24$).
  - _why:_ Preserving the negative sign is correct because a positive factor times a negative term remains negative. The actual common mistake is failing to multiply the second term by 8.
- Calculating both products correctly (e.g. writing $32s - 24$).
  - _why:_ Calculating both products accurately is the correct expansion process. The common error is calculating only $8\times4s$ and leaving the bracket’s second term unchanged.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $6(g + 4) + 2(g - 7)$.

**Back:** $$8g + 10$

**Choices:**

- $$8g + 10$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding gives $6g+24+2g-14$. Collecting like terms then produces $8g$, while the constants give $24-14=10$, so the result is $8g+10$.
- $$8g + 38$
  - _why:_ This treats the negative term in $2(g-7)$ as positive, producing $+14$ instead of $-14$. The constants should total $24-14=10$.
- $$8g + 17$
  - _why:_ This fails to multiply $-7$ by the outside factor 2, using $-7$ instead of $-14$. Correct distribution makes the constant total $24-14=10$.
- $$8g - 10$
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction when combining the constants. They total $24-14=10$, not $-10$, while the variable terms correctly combine to $8g$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** How does a minus sign before a bracket (e.g. $-2(5y + 1)$) affect the expansion?

**Back:** It changes the signs of every term inside the bracket when multiplied.

**Choices:**

- It changes the signs of every term inside the bracket when multiplied. ✔
  - _why:_ The negative multiplier applies to every term in the bracket. For example, $-2(5y + 1) = -10y - 2$, so both terms change sign.
- It changes the sign of only the first term inside the bracket when multiplied.
  - _why:_ Applying the minus sign only to the first term ignores the distributive law. The multiplier acts on every term, so $-2(5y + 1) = -10y - 2$.
- It keeps the signs of every term inside the bracket when multiplied.
  - _why:_ Ignoring the negative sign treats the multiplier as positive. Every term must be multiplied by $-2$, giving $-10y - 2$ rather than positive terms.
- It changes the operation between the terms inside the bracket when multiplied.
  - _why:_ The operation inside the bracket is not changed before multiplication. Instead, the negative multiplier is distributed to both terms, changing each resulting term’s sign.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $4(3y - 5) - 2(5y + 1)$.

**Back:** $$2y - 22$

**Choices:**

- $$2y - 22$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding gives $12y - 20 - 10y - 2$. Collecting like terms then gives $(12y - 10y) + (-20 - 2) = 2y - 22$.
- $$2y - 18$
  - _why:_ This comes from treating $-2 \times 1$ as $+2$. The negative multiplier gives $-2$, so the constants total $-20 - 2 = -22$.
- $$22y - 22$
  - _why:_ This adds $12y$ and $10y$, overlooking that the second bracket is multiplied by $-2$. The variable terms are $12y - 10y = 2y$.
- $$2y - 12$
  - _why:_ The variable terms have been combined correctly, but the constants have not. Expanding produces $-20$ and $-2$, which combine to $-22$, giving $2y - 22$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** Factorising is the [ ___ ] of expanding.

**Back:** Factorising is the opposite of expanding.

**Choices:**

- opposite ✔
  - _why:_ Factorising rewrites an expression as a product of factors, while expanding multiplies out those factors. The two processes therefore undo one another.
- result
  - _why:_ An expanded expression can be the result of expanding, but factorising itself is not that result. Factorising is the opposite process because it reconstructs a product of factors.
- purpose
  - _why:_ Factorising is not the purpose of expanding. It is the opposite operation: expanding removes brackets, whereas factorising rewrites an expression using brackets and common factors.
- continuation
  - _why:_ Factorising does not continue the process of expanding; it reverses it. Expanding multiplies out brackets, while factorising places an expression back into a product form.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the two requirements to factorise fully?

**Back:** Take out the highest common factor (HCF) of the numbersTake out the HCF of the variables

**Choices:**

- Take out the highest common factor (HCF) of the numbersTake out the HCF of the variables ✔
  - _why:_ A fully factorised expression has the greatest possible numerical factor and every common variable factor outside the bracket. No further common factor should remain inside.
- Take out the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the numbersTake out the HCF of the variables
  - _why:_ The LCM is used to find common multiples, not common factors. Full factorisation requires the HCF of the numerical coefficients and the HCF of the variables.
- Take out the highest common factor (HCF) of the numbersTake out the highest power of each variable
  - _why:_ Taking the highest variable power can introduce factors that are not present in every term. Use only the greatest power common to all terms, alongside the numerical HCF.
- Take out the smallest coefficient from the numbersTake out every variable in the expression
  - _why:_ The smallest coefficient is not necessarily a factor of every coefficient, and not every variable is necessarily common. Full factorisation uses only the numerical and variable HCFs.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $20y + 35$.

**Back:** $$5(4y + 7)$

**Choices:**

- $$5(4y + 7)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The HCF of $20$ and $35$ is $5$. Dividing both terms by $5$ gives $4y$ and $7$, so $20y + 35 = 5(4y + 7)$.
- $$5(4y + 35)$
  - _why:_ Only the first term has been divided by the outside factor. Both terms must be divided by $5$, so $35 \div 5 = 7$ and the answer is $5(4y + 7)$.
- $$10(2y + 7)$
  - _why:_ Although $20y \div 10 = 2y$, $35$ is not divisible by $10$. Expanding this gives $20y + 70$, whereas the correct factorisation is $5(4y + 7)$.
- $$5(4y + 6)$
  - _why:_ This uses an incorrect division for the constant term: $35 \div 5 = 7$, not $6$. Expanding the correct answer $5(4y + 7)$ returns $20y + 35$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the highest common factor of $12m^2n$ and $18mn^2$?

**Back:** $$6mn$

**Choices:**

- $$6mn$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numerical HCF of $12$ and $18$ is $6$. The lowest powers common to both terms are $m^1$ and $n^1$, giving the HCF $6mn$.
- $$6m^2n$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly takes the higher power of $m$. Since $18mn^2$ contains only one factor of $m$, the common variable factor is $m$, making the HCF $6mn$.
- $$6mn^2$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly takes the higher power of $n$. Since $12m^2n$ contains only one factor of $n$, the greatest variable factor common to both terms is $mn$.
- $$3mn$
  - _why:_ This is a common factor, but it is not the highest one because both coefficients are divisible by $6$. Including the common variables gives the HCF $6mn$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise fully $12m^2n + 18mn^2$.

**Back:** $$6mn(2m + 3n)$

**Choices:**

- $$6mn(2m + 3n)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The HCF is $6mn$. Dividing $12m^2n$ by it gives $2m$, and dividing $18mn^2$ by it gives $3n$, producing $6mn(2m + 3n)$.
- $$3mn(4m + 6n)$
  - _why:_ This expands to the original expression, but it is not fully factorised because $2$ remains common inside the bracket. Taking it out gives $6mn(2m + 3n)$.
- $$6m(2mn + 3n^2)$
  - _why:_ This is equivalent to the original expression, but it leaves a common factor of $n$ inside the bracket. Taking out the full HCF gives $6mn(2m + 3n)$.
- $$6mn(2m + 2n)$
  - _why:_ The second term has been divided incorrectly: $18mn^2 \div 6mn = 3n$, not $2n$. The fully factorised expression is therefore $6mn(2m + 3n)$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you check if your factorisation is correct?

**Back:** Expand the brackets; it should return to the original expression.

**Choices:**

- Expand the brackets; it should return to the original expression. ✔
  - _why:_ Factorising and expanding are opposite processes. Multiplying the factors back out should reproduce every term and sign in the original expression exactly.
- Substitute one value; matching results prove the expressions are identical.
  - _why:_ Two different expressions can produce the same result for one chosen value, so this does not prove equivalence. Expanding the brackets must reproduce the original expression term by term.
- Factorise the brackets again; they should contain no numerical terms.
  - _why:_ Factorising again does not directly verify the multiplication, and correctly factorised brackets may contain numbers. The reliable check is to expand and compare with the original expression.
- Count the terms; there should be the same number in both expressions.
  - _why:_ Factorisation usually changes the visible number of terms, so term counting cannot establish equivalence. Expanding the factors should instead return exactly to the original expression.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the most efficient first step to solve $5(b + 4) = 45$?

**Back:** Divide both sides by 5.

**Choices:**

- Divide both sides by 5. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing by 5 immediately removes the outer multiplication, giving $b + 4 = 9$. Subtracting 4 then gives $b = 5$.
- Subtract 4 from both sides.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 4 does not undo the addition inside the brackets because the entire bracket is multiplied by 5. Divide both sides by 5 first.
- Divide both sides by 45.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 45 does not cancel the coefficient outside the brackets. The efficient first step is to divide both sides by 5, producing $b + 4 = 9$.
- Multiply both sides by 5.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 5 increases the coefficient rather than cancelling it. Division by 5 is the inverse operation needed to isolate the bracket efficiently.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $5(b + 4) = 45$.

**Back:** $$b = 5$

**Choices:**

- $$b = 5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by 5 gives $b + 4 = 9$. Subtracting 4 from both sides then gives the solution $b = 5$.
- $$b = 13$
  - _why:_ This results from adding 4 after obtaining $b + 4 = 9$. The correct inverse operation is subtraction, so $b = 9 - 4 = 5$.
- $$b = 8.2$
  - _why:_ This treats $5(b + 4)$ as $5b + 4$, failing to multiply 4 by 5. Dividing first gives $b + 4 = 9$, so $b = 5$.
- $$b = 41$
  - _why:_ This subtracts 4 from 45 but ignores that the whole bracket is multiplied by 5. First divide by 5, then subtract 4 to obtain $b = 5$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $4(c - 3) = 18$.

**Back:** $$c = 7.5$

**Choices:**

- $$c = 7.5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by $4$ gives $c - 3 = 4.5$. Adding $3$ to both sides then gives $c = 7.5$.
- $$c = 1.5$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $3$ after dividing reverses the required operation. From $c - 3 = 4.5$, add $3$, rather than subtracting it, to obtain $c = 7.5$.
- $$c = 5.25$
  - _why:_ This results from treating $4(c - 3)$ as $4c - 3$. Correct expansion gives $4c - 12 = 18$, so $4c = 30$ and $c = 7.5$.
- $$c = 18$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the bracket as though $4(c - 3)$ meant $(4 - 3)c$. Dividing by $4$ first and then adding $3$ gives $c = 7.5$.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebra, a [ ___ ] is a letter that represents a number, while a term is a part of an expression separated by plus or minus signs.

**Back:** In algebra, a variable is a letter that represents a number, while a term is a part of an expression separated by plus or minus signs.

**Choices:**

- variable ✔
  - _why:_ A variable is a letter or symbol used to represent a number whose value may be unknown or may change within an algebraic expression.
- constant
  - _why:_ A constant is a fixed numerical value, not a letter representing a number that may vary. The defined algebraic object is a variable.
- coefficient
  - _why:_ A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable, such as 5 in $5x$. A letter representing a number is called a variable.
- expression
  - _why:_ An expression is a mathematical combination of terms and operations, rather than a single letter representing a number. Such a letter is called a variable.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebra, a variable is a letter that represents a number, while a [ ___ ] is a part of an expression separated by plus or minus signs.

**Back:** In algebra, a variable is a letter that represents a number, while a term is a part of an expression separated by plus or minus signs.

**Choices:**

- term ✔
  - _why:_ A term is one part of an algebraic expression. Terms are separated from one another by addition or subtraction signs, including their associated signs.
- expression
  - _why:_ An expression consists of one or more terms linked by operations, so it is not an individual part separated by plus or minus signs. That part is a term.
- equation
  - _why:_ An equation states that two expressions are equal and therefore contains an equals sign. A part of an expression separated by plus or minus signs is a term.
- factor
  - _why:_ A factor is a quantity multiplied by another quantity, not a part identified by addition or subtraction boundaries. Such a part of an expression is a term.
