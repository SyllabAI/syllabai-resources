# Angles in Parallel Lines Flashcards

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** Parallel lines stay [ ___ ] and [ ___ ].

**Back:** Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet.

**Choices:**

- Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet. ✔
  - _why:_ Parallel lines remain a constant perpendicular distance apart, so they never converge or intersect, even when extended indefinitely in both directions.
- Parallel lines stay different distances apart and never meet.
  - _why:_ Lines whose separation changes are not parallel, even if they never meet in the part of the diagram shown. Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet.
- Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and eventually meet.
  - _why:_ Meeting lines cannot be parallel because parallel lines do not intersect, however far they are extended. Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet.
- Parallel lines stay closer and closer together and eventually meet.
  - _why:_ Getting closer and eventually meeting describes converging lines, not parallel lines. Parallel lines maintain the same separation and never meet when extended.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A transversal is a line that [ ___ ].

**Back:** A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines.

**Choices:**

- crosses two or more other lines ✔
  - _why:_ A transversal intersects at least two other lines at distinct points, creating the angle relationships used when those lines are parallel.
- crosses exactly one other line
  - _why:_ Crossing only one line does not create the two intersections needed for transversal angle relationships. A transversal crosses two or more other lines.
- runs parallel to two other lines
  - _why:_ Running parallel means the line does not intersect the other lines, whereas a transversal must cross two or more other lines.
- meets two other lines at one point
  - _why:_ Passing through a shared intersection is not the usual transversal arrangement because it does not create separate crossings. A transversal crosses two or more other lines.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** An angle next to 68° on a straight line is what?

**Back:** 112°.

**Choices:**

- 112°. ✔
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles on a straight line sum to 180°, so the missing angle is $180° - 68° = 112°$.
- 68°.
  - _why:_ This repeats the given angle instead of using the straight-line total. Adjacent angles on a straight line sum to 180°, giving $180° - 68° = 112°$.
- 122°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts 68° from 190° rather than the straight-line total of 180°. The required angle is $180° - 68° = 112°$.
- 292°.
  - _why:_ This uses the reflex-angle total of 360° instead of the 180° total for a straight line. The adjacent angle is $180° - 68° = 112°$.

**Back:** 68°.

  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles are equal, so the angle directly opposite the given 68° angle also measures 68°.
- 112°.
  - _why:_ 112° is the supplementary angle adjacent to 68°, found using a straight-line total. The vertically opposite angle is equal to 68°.
- 136°.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly doubles the given angle. Vertically opposite angles are equal rather than added together, so the required angle is 68°.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles in a triangle add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles in a triangle add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ The three interior angles of every triangle have a total of 180°, regardless of the triangle’s side lengths or shape.
- 360°
  - _why:_ 360° is the interior-angle total for a quadrilateral, not a triangle. The three interior angles of a triangle add to 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ 90° is the size of one right angle, not the total for every triangle. A triangle’s three interior angles add to 180°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ 270° can result from counting three right angles, but a triangle cannot contain three right angles. Its interior angles always total 180°.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** When parallel lines are cut by a transversal, what is true of corresponding angles?

**Back:** Corresponding angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- Corresponding angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ For parallel lines, corresponding angles occupy the same relative position at the two intersections and therefore have equal measures.
- Corresponding angles add to 180°.
  - _why:_ Adding to 180° is the relationship for co-interior angles, not corresponding angles. When the lines are parallel, corresponding angles are equal.
- Corresponding angles are vertically opposite.
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles occur at the same intersection, whereas corresponding angles occur at different intersections. Corresponding angles are equal when the lines are parallel.
- Corresponding angles are unequal.
  - _why:_ Corresponding angles may look separated in the diagram, but parallel lines ensure that angles in matching positions are equal, not unequal.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Corresponding angles are in the [ ___ ] at each crossing.

**Back:** Corresponding angles are in the same relative position at each crossing.

**Choices:**

- same relative position ✔
  - _why:_ Corresponding angles occupy matching locations around their two intersections, such as top-right at each crossing, so they are in the same relative position.
- opposite relative position
  - _why:_ An opposite relative position identifies a different angle relationship rather than corresponding angles. Corresponding angles occupy the same relative position at each crossing.
- alternate relative position
  - _why:_ Alternate positions are associated with alternate angles, which lie on opposite sides of the transversal. Corresponding angles occupy the same relative position at each crossing.
- vertically opposite position
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles are across from each other at one intersection, not at matching positions across two intersections. Corresponding angles use the same relative position.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** Alternate angles lie [ ___ ] and on [ ___ ].

**Back:** Alternate angles lie between the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal.

**Choices:**

- Alternate angles lie between the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal. ✔
  - _why:_ Alternate angles are the interior pair formed between the two parallel lines, with one angle lying on each side of the transversal.
- Alternate angles lie between the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal.
  - _why:_ Angles between the parallel lines on the same side of the transversal are co-interior angles. Alternate angles are between the lines on opposite sides of the transversal.
- Alternate angles lie in corresponding positions and on the same side of the transversal.
  - _why:_ Angles in matching positions on the same side are corresponding angles, not alternate angles. Alternate angles lie between the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal.
- Alternate angles lie outside the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal.
  - _why:_ This describes same-side exterior angles rather than alternate angles. Alternate angles lie between the parallel lines and on opposite sides of the transversal.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** When parallel lines are cut by a transversal, what is true of alternate angles?

**Back:** Alternate angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- Alternate angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ For two parallel lines cut by a transversal, alternate angles form a matching pair on opposite sides of the transversal, so they have equal measures.
- Alternate angles are supplementary.
  - _why:_ Supplementary angles add to 180°, which is the rule for co-interior angles. Alternate angles formed by a transversal across parallel lines are equal.
- Alternate angles differ by 90°.
  - _why:_ A 90° difference is not a parallel-line angle rule. When a transversal crosses parallel lines, each pair of alternate angles has equal measures.
- Alternate angles add to 360°.
  - _why:_ Angles around a point add to 360°, but this does not describe a pair of alternate angles. Alternate angles between parallel lines are equal.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** Co-interior angles are inside the parallel lines and on the [ ___ ] of the transversal.

**Back:** Co-interior angles are inside the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal.

**Choices:**

- same side ✔
  - _why:_ Co-interior angles lie between the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal. Their measures consequently add to 180°.

  - _why:_ Angles on opposite sides of the transversal are associated with the alternate-angle pattern. Co-interior angles instead lie on the same side of the transversal.
- left side
  - _why:_ The definition does not depend on a fixed left-hand position, since a diagram can be rotated. Co-interior angles are on the same side of the transversal.

  - _why:_ The right-hand position is not part of the definition and changes if the diagram is rotated. Co-interior angles lie on the same side of the transversal.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What is true of co-interior angles on parallel lines?

**Back:** Co-interior angles add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- Co-interior angles add to 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ Co-interior angles lie on the same side of a transversal between two parallel lines. They are supplementary, meaning their measures have a total of 180°.
- Co-interior angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Equality is the rule for alternate and corresponding angles, not generally for co-interior angles. Co-interior angles are supplementary and therefore add to 180°.
- Co-interior angles add to 90°.
  - _why:_ A total of 90° describes complementary angles, not co-interior angles. Co-interior angles on parallel lines are supplementary, so they add to 180°.
- Co-interior angles add to 360°.
  - _why:_ A total of 360° applies to angles around a point, not a co-interior pair. Co-interior angles between parallel lines add to 180°.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** If co-interior angles are 113° and $x$, what equation should you write?

**Back:** $x + 113^\circ = 180^\circ$.

**Choices:**

- $x + 113^\circ = 180^\circ$. ✔
  - _why:_ Co-interior angles on parallel lines are supplementary, so their measures must total 180°. Adding the unknown angle to 113° gives the required equation.
- $x = 113^\circ$.
  - _why:_ Setting the angles equal confuses co-interior angles with alternate or corresponding angles. Co-interior angles add to 180°, so the equation is $x + 113^\circ = 180^\circ$.
- $x + 113^\circ = 90^\circ$.
  - _why:_ Using 90° incorrectly treats the two angles as complementary. Co-interior angles are supplementary, so the correct equation is $x + 113^\circ = 180^\circ$.
- $x - 113^\circ = 180^\circ$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the two angle measures does not express their supplementary relationship. Their sum is 180°, giving the equation $x + 113^\circ = 180^\circ$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What property of a parallelogram lets you use parallel-line angle facts on its sides?

**Back:** Opposite sides are parallel.

**Choices:**

- Opposite sides are parallel. ✔
  - _why:_ A parallelogram has two pairs of opposite sides that are parallel. These parallel sides allow corresponding, alternate and co-interior angle facts to be applied.
- Adjacent sides are equal.
  - _why:_ Adjacent sides are not generally equal in a parallelogram; that is a property of a rhombus. Parallel-line angle facts apply because opposite sides are parallel.
- Diagonals are perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular diagonals are a property of some special parallelograms, such as rhombuses, but not all parallelograms. The relevant property is that opposite sides are parallel.
- All angles are equal.
  - _why:_ All angles are equal only in rectangular parallelograms, not in every parallelogram. Parallel-line angle rules can be used because opposite sides are parallel.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite [ ___ ] are [ ___ ].

**Back:** In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite equal sides are equal.

**Choices:**

- In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite equal sides are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ An isosceles triangle has two equal sides. The angles opposite those equal sides are the two equal base angles.
- In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite unequal sides are equal.
  - _why:_ Unequal sides have unequal opposite angles, not equal ones. In an isosceles triangle, it is the angles opposite the equal sides that are equal.
- In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite equal sides are supplementary.
  - _why:_ The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal rather than supplementary. They are the angles opposite the triangle’s equal sides.
- In an isosceles triangle, angles opposite unequal sides are supplementary.
  - _why:_ This combines two incorrect claims: unequal sides do not have equal opposite angles, and those angles are not necessarily supplementary. Angles opposite equal sides are equal.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** If EG = FG in triangle EFG, which base angles are equal?

**Back:** Angles at E and F.

**Choices:**

- Angles at E and F. ✔
  - _why:_ Equal sides have equal opposite angles. Side EG is opposite the angle at F, while side FG is opposite the angle at E, so those angles are equal.
- Angles at E and G.
  - _why:_ The angle at G lies between the two equal sides and is the vertex angle, not a base angle. The equal base angles are at E and F.
- Angles at F and G.
  - _why:_ The angle at G is the vertex angle between EG and FG, so it is not one of the equal base angles. The equal angles are at E and F.
- Both angles at G.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly repeats the vertex angle rather than identifying the two base angles. Because EG = FG, the opposite angles at F and E are equal.
