## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** Area is measured in [ ___ ], such as cm², m² or mm².

**Back:** Area is measured in square units, such as cm², m² or mm².

**Choices:**

- square units ✔
  - _why:_ Area describes the amount of two-dimensional space inside a shape, so it is measured in square units such as cm², m² or mm².
- linear units
  - _why:_ Linear units measure one-dimensional quantities such as length or perimeter. Area measures two-dimensional space, so the correct term is square units.
- cubic units
  - _why:_ Cubic units measure three-dimensional volume rather than area. Because area covers a two-dimensional surface, it is measured in square units.
- standard units
  - _why:_ Standard units is too general and does not identify the required dimension. Area is specifically measured in square units, as shown by cm² and m².

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the area formula for a rectangle with base $b$ and height $h$?

**Back:** $A=bh$

**Choices:**

- $A=bh$ ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle contains $b$ rows of height $h$, or equivalently has area equal to its base multiplied by its perpendicular height.
- $A=b+h$
  - _why:_ Adding the base and height does not calculate the space inside the rectangle. Its area is found by multiplying these dimensions, giving $A=bh$.
- $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$
  - _why:_ The factor of one-half belongs to the triangle formula, since a triangle is half a corresponding rectangle. A rectangle’s area is $A=bh$.
- $A=b^2h^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring both dimensions incorrectly multiplies the rectangle’s true area by another factor of $bh$. The correct formula is simply $A=bh$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the area formula for a triangle with base $b$ and height $h$?

**Back:** $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$

**Choices:**

- $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$ ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle occupies half the area of a rectangle or parallelogram with the same base and perpendicular height, so its area is $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $A=bh$
  - _why:_ Multiplying base by height gives the area of the corresponding rectangle or parallelogram. A triangle occupies half that area, so $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $A=\frac{1}{2}(b+h)$
  - _why:_ Adding base and height does not measure the triangle’s two-dimensional area. Multiply the base by the perpendicular height and halve the result: $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $A=\frac{1}{4}bh$
  - _why:_ Halving both dimensions introduces a factor of one-quarter instead of one-half. Only the product $bh$ is halved, giving $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the area formula for a trapezium with parallel sides $a$, $b$ and height $h$?

**Back:** $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$

**Choices:**

- $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ ✔
  - _why:_ The mean length of the two parallel sides is $\frac{a+b}{2}$. Multiplying this by the perpendicular height gives $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $A=\frac{1}{2}(a-b)h$
  - _why:_ This uses the difference between the parallel sides rather than their mean total. A trapezium’s area is $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $A=(a+b)h$
  - _why:_ Omitting the factor of one-half doubles the area by using the sum rather than the mean of the parallel sides. The correct formula is $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $A=\frac{1}{2}abh$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the parallel sides together treats them as perpendicular dimensions and gives incorrect units after also multiplying by height. Use $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** For triangles and trapezia, the height must be [ ___ ] to the [ ___ ].

**Back:** For triangles and trapezia, the height must be perpendicular to the base.

**Choices:**

- For triangles and trapezia, the height must be perpendicular to the base. ✔
  - _why:_ Area formulas require the shortest separation from the base to the opposite vertex or parallel side. This distance meets the chosen base at a right angle.
- For triangles and trapezia, the height must be parallel to the base.
  - _why:_ A line parallel to the base does not measure the separation from it. The height must be perpendicular to the base, meeting it at a right angle.
- For triangles and trapezia, the height must be perpendicular to the sloping side.
  - _why:_ The height is not generally measured perpendicular to a sloping side. It must be perpendicular to whichever side is being used as the base.
- For triangles and trapezia, the height must be parallel to the sloping side.
  - _why:_ Neither condition identifies the perpendicular distance required by the area formulas. The height must be perpendicular to the base, not parallel to a sloping side.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** When can a sloping side be used as the height of a triangle or trapezium?

**Back:** Only if it is perpendicular to the base.

**Choices:**

- Only if it is perpendicular to the base. ✔
  - _why:_ A height is the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex or parallel side. Therefore, a sloping side counts only when it meets the base at $90°$.
- Only if it is parallel to the base.
  - _why:_ Being parallel to the base prevents a side from measuring the required separation from it. The sloping side is a height only if it is perpendicular to the base.
- Only if it is the longest side.
  - _why:_ A side’s length does not determine whether it is a height. A sloping side can be used only when it is perpendicular to the chosen base.
- Only if it is labelled on the diagram.
  - _why:_ A side being labelled does not make it a perpendicular height. It can be used in the area formula only if it is perpendicular to the base.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Parallel lines stay [ ___ ] and [ ___ ].

**Back:** Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet.

**Choices:**

- Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet. ✔
  - _why:_ Parallel lines have the same direction and constant perpendicular separation. Consequently, they remain the same distance apart and do not intersect, however far they are extended.
- Parallel lines stay further and further apart and never meet.
  - _why:_ Lines whose separation continually increases are diverging rather than parallel. Parallel lines remain the same distance apart and never meet when extended.
- Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and eventually meet.
  - _why:_ Lines that eventually meet are not parallel, even if they appear nearly parallel in a short diagram. Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet.
- Parallel lines stay closer and closer together and eventually meet.
  - _why:_ Lines that approach and meet are converging, not parallel. True parallel lines maintain a constant distance between them and never intersect.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** If a diagram is labelled not accurately drawn, what should you use to find lengths?

**Back:** Use the labelled lengths only.

**Choices:**

- Use the labelled lengths only. ✔
  - _why:_ A diagram marked not accurately drawn may have misleading proportions or angles. Calculations must therefore use the stated measurements rather than lengths estimated from the picture.
- Use the visual proportions only.
  - _why:_ The picture’s proportions may be deliberately inaccurate, so visual estimates are unreliable. Use the labelled lengths only when calculating missing lengths or areas.
- Use the measured lengths only.
  - _why:_ Measuring the printed diagram assumes it has been drawn to scale, which the warning explicitly denies. Calculations should use the labelled lengths only.
- Use the grid square counts only.
  - _why:_ Counting grid squares is valid only when an accurate grid and scale are provided. For a diagram not accurately drawn, use the labelled lengths only.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** For a compound shape made of joined pieces, what is the area strategy?

**Back:** Split into simpler shapes.Find each area separately.Add the areas.

**Choices:**

- Split into simpler shapes.Find each area separately.Add the areas. ✔
  - _why:_ Splitting a compound shape into familiar shapes allows each area to be calculated using a standard formula. Because the pieces are joined without overlapping, their areas are then added.
- Split into simpler shapes.Find each perimeter separately.Add the perimeters.
  - _why:_ This confuses area with perimeter. The correct strategy is to split the compound shape, find the area of each simpler shape separately, and then add those areas.
- Split into simpler shapes.Find each area separately.Multiply the areas.
  - _why:_ Multiplying the separate areas does not give the area of the combined shape. After splitting and finding each piece’s area, the areas must be added together.
- Measure the outside edges.Find their total length.Square the result.
  - _why:_ Adding outside edges calculates perimeter, and squaring that result does not calculate area. Instead, split the shape into simpler pieces, calculate their areas, and add them.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** For right-angled compound shapes, splitting along a [ ___ ] or [ ___ ] line usually creates rectangles.

**Back:** For right-angled compound shapes, splitting along a horizontal or vertical line usually creates rectangles.

**Choices:**

- For right-angled compound shapes, splitting along a horizontal or vertical line usually creates rectangles. ✔
  - _why:_ Horizontal and vertical splitting lines follow the directions of the perpendicular sides. Extending these lines across a right-angled compound shape usually divides it into rectangles.
- For right-angled compound shapes, splitting along a diagonal or vertical line usually creates rectangles.
  - _why:_ A diagonal split generally creates triangles or other non-rectangular pieces. Horizontal or vertical lines align with the right-angled sides and usually create rectangles.
- For right-angled compound shapes, splitting along a horizontal or diagonal line usually creates rectangles.
  - _why:_ The diagonal line is the misconception here because it cuts across the perpendicular grid of the shape. Horizontal and vertical splits are the pair that usually create rectangles.
- For right-angled compound shapes, splitting along a diagonal or curved line usually creates rectangles.
  - _why:_ Diagonal and curved lines do not normally produce rectangles, whose sides must meet at right angles. Horizontal and vertical splitting lines follow the shape’s perpendicular edges.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you not multiply total width by total height for an L-shape?

**Back:** It includes space not in the shape.

**Choices:**

- It includes space not in the shape. ✔
  - _why:_ Total width multiplied by total height gives the area of the complete surrounding rectangle. An L-shape has a missing section, so this calculation includes space outside the shape.
- It excludes space inside the shape.
  - _why:_ The bounding rectangle does not exclude part of the L-shape; it includes too much. Total width times total height counts the missing corner, which is not in the shape.
- It measures the perimeter of the shape.
  - _why:_ Multiplying width by height calculates rectangular area, not perimeter. The problem is that the resulting rectangle includes the L-shape’s missing section, so its area is too large.
- It counts the missing space twice.
  - _why:_ The missing section is included once, not counted twice, when total width is multiplied by total height. The correct approach splits the L-shape or subtracts the missing area.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** In the L-shape example, the bottom length is 13 cm and the top length is 6 cm. What is the missing right width?

**Back:** $13-6=7$ cm

**Choices:**

- $13-6=7$ cm ✔
  - _why:_ The full bottom length consists of the 6 cm top section and the missing right width. Subtracting gives $13-6=7$, so the missing width is 7 cm.
- $13+6=19$ cm
  - _why:_ Adding treats the two given lengths as separate adjoining sections, but 6 cm is already part of the total 13 cm. Subtraction gives $13-6=7$ cm.
- $13-6=8$ cm
  - _why:_ The correct operation has been chosen, but the subtraction contains an arithmetic error. Since $13-6=7$, the missing right width is 7 cm, not 8 cm.
- $13-6=6$ cm
  - _why:_ This repeats the known top length instead of calculating the difference. The missing section completes 6 cm to 13 cm, so its width is $13-6=7$ cm.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** An L-shape is split into rectangles $6\text{ cm}\times8\text{ cm}$ and $7\text{ cm}\times5\text{ cm}$. What is its total area?

**Back:** 83 cm²

**Choices:**

- 83 cm² ✔
  - _why:_ The rectangle areas are $6\times8=48$ cm² and $7\times5=35$ cm². Adding the non-overlapping pieces gives $48+35=83$ cm².
- 65 cm²
  - _why:_ This can result from using 5 cm as the height of both rectangles: $6\times5+7\times5=65$. The correct areas are 48 cm² and 35 cm², totalling 83 cm².
- 90 cm²
  - _why:_ This can result from incorrectly using 6 cm as the second rectangle’s height. Its height is 5 cm, so the total is $6\times8+7\times5=83$ cm².
- 104 cm²
  - _why:_ This is the area of a $13\text{ cm}\times8\text{ cm}$ bounding rectangle, which includes the missing part of the L-shape. Adding the two given rectangles gives 83 cm².

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** In a right-angled triangle, which two sides are the base and height?

**Back:** The two sides that meet at the right angle.

**Choices:**

- The two sides that meet at the right angle. ✔
  - _why:_ The base and perpendicular height must meet at 90°. In a right-angled triangle, the two sides forming the right angle therefore provide the base and height.
- The two sides that are equal in length.
  - _why:_ Equal sides identify an isosceles triangle but do not necessarily give a perpendicular base and height. In a right-angled triangle, use the two sides meeting at the right angle.
- The two sides that meet at an acute angle.
  - _why:_ Sides meeting at an acute angle are not perpendicular, so they cannot directly serve as base and height. Use the two sides that meet at the 90° angle.
- The two sides that include the hypotenuse.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse does not meet either adjacent side at 90°, so a perpendicular height would need to be constructed. The simpler base and height meet at the right angle.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A compound shape has a $3\text{ cm}\times10\text{ cm}$ rectangle and a triangle with base 6 cm and height 4 cm. What is the total area?

**Back:** 42 cm²

**Choices:**

- 42 cm² ✔
  - _why:_ The rectangle has area $3\times10=30$ cm². The triangle has area $\frac{1}{2}\times6\times4=12$ cm², giving a total of $30+12=42$ cm².
- 54 cm²
  - _why:_ This uses $6\times4=24$ cm² for the triangle and forgets to halve it. The triangle is 12 cm², so the total area is $30+12=42$ cm².
- 36 cm²
  - _why:_ This can result from dividing the triangle’s base-height product by 4 rather than 2. Its area is 12 cm², which added to 30 cm² gives 42 cm².
- 12 cm²
  - _why:_ This is only the triangle’s area, so the rectangle has been omitted. Adding the rectangle’s 30 cm² to the triangle’s 12 cm² gives 42 cm².

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** For a shaded region with a hole, what area calculation is used?

**Back:** Whole area - unshaded area

**Choices:**

- Whole area - unshaded area ✔
  - _why:_ The whole area includes both the shaded region and the hole. Subtracting the unshaded hole from the whole area leaves exactly the required shaded area.
- Whole area + unshaded area
  - _why:_ Adding the unshaded area counts the hole twice rather than removing it. The shaded region is found by subtracting the unshaded hole from the whole area.
- Whole area × unshaded area
  - _why:_ Multiplying two areas produces inappropriate squared-area units and does not remove the hole. The required calculation is whole area minus unshaded area.
- Whole area ÷ unshaded area
  - _why:_ Dividing the areas gives a ratio rather than the shaded area. Because the hole lies inside the whole shape, its unshaded area must be subtracted.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** If there are several identical holes, subtract [ ___ ] of them, not just [ ___ ].

**Back:** If there are several identical holes, subtract all of them, not just one.

**Choices:**

- If there are several identical holes, subtract all of them, not just one. ✔
  - _why:_ Every hole removes area from the compound shape. For identical holes, calculate one hole’s area, multiply by the number of holes, and subtract the resulting total.
- If there are several identical holes, subtract one of them, not just all.
  - _why:_ This reverses the required instruction. Every hole reduces the total area, so the area of all the holes must be subtracted, rather than the area of only one.
- If there are several identical holes, subtract half of them, not just one.
  - _why:_ Subtracting only half the holes leaves some removed regions included in the answer. The correct method is to subtract the combined area of all the holes.
- If there are several identical holes, subtract none of them, not just one.
  - _why:_ Ignoring the holes gives the area of the complete outer shape rather than the remaining material. The areas of all the holes must be subtracted.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A wall is $5\text{ m}\times3\text{ m}$ and a door is $1\text{ m}\times2\text{ m}$. What is the painted area?

**Back:** 13 m²

**Choices:**

- 13 m² ✔
  - _why:_ The wall has area 5 × 3 = 15 m² and the door has area 1 × 2 = 2 m². Therefore, the painted area is 15 - 2 = 13 m².
- 17 m²
  - _why:_ This comes from adding the door’s 2 m² area to the wall’s 15 m² area. The door is unpainted, so its area must be subtracted, giving 13 m².
- 14 m²
  - _why:_ This subtracts only 1 m², using one door dimension instead of its area. The door covers 1 × 2 = 2 m², so the painted area is 13 m².
- 12 m²
  - _why:_ This treats the door’s dimensions as an area by adding 1 + 2 and subtracting 3 m². Area requires multiplication, so subtracting 2 m² gives 13 m².

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a price is given per square metre, the area must be in [ ___ ] before multiplying by the price.

**Back:** If a price is given per square metre, the area must be in m² before multiplying by the price.

**Choices:**

- m² ✔
  - _why:_ A price per square metre applies to an area measured in square metres. Converting the area to m² before multiplying ensures that the units match the given rate.
- cm²
  - _why:_ Square centimetres do not match a price quoted per square metre. The area must first be converted to m², otherwise the calculated cost will be incorrectly scaled.
- m
  - _why:_ Metres measure length rather than area, so they cannot be multiplied directly by a price per square metre. The required area unit is m².
- m³
  - _why:_ Cubic metres measure volume, not area. A rate per square metre must be multiplied by an area expressed in m² to produce the correct price.

  - _why:_ This uses only the rectangle’s 6,300 m² area and omits the triangular section. Including the triangle gives 8,900 m², which sells for £35,600.

  - _why:_ This calculates the triangular area as 80 × 65, forgetting to divide by 2. Using 2,600 m² for the triangle gives a total sale price of £35,600.

  - _why:_ This is the field’s total area in square metres, not the amount received. Multiplying 8,900 m² by the price of £4 per m² gives £35,600.
