## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A percentage is parts out of [ ___ ]; 100% represents the [ ___ ].

**Back:** A percentage is parts out of 100; 100% represents the whole amount.

**Choices:**

- A percentage is parts out of 100; 100% represents the whole amount. ✔
  - _why:_ Percent means “per hundred”, so percentages describe parts out of 100. Therefore, 100 parts out of 100 represent the whole amount.
- A percentage is parts out of 10; 100% represents the whole amount.
  - _why:_ Confusing percentages with tenths gives a denominator of 10. Percent means parts out of 100, and 100% therefore represents the whole amount.
- A percentage is parts out of 100; 100% represents the double amount.
  - _why:_ Treating 100% as an increase of 100% leads to double the amount. On its own, 100% is exactly the whole amount; 200% is double.
- A percentage is parts out of 1000; 100% represents the half amount.
  - _why:_ Parts out of 1000 are measured in per mille, not percent, and half is 50%. A percentage is out of 100, with 100% representing the whole amount.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Convert 25% to a decimal.

**Back:** 0.25

**Choices:**

- 0.25 ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing 25 by 100 converts the percentage to a decimal: $25 \div 100 = 0.25$. Therefore, 25% is equal to 0.25.
- 2.5
  - _why:_ Dividing by 10 instead of 100 leaves the decimal point one place too far right. Percent means out of 100, so 25% is $25 \div 100 = 0.25$.
- 0.025
  - _why:_ Dividing by 1000 moves the decimal point one place too far left. Converting a percentage requires division by 100, so 25% equals 0.25.
- 0.75
  - _why:_ This is the decimal for 75%, the complement of 25%, rather than 25% itself. Dividing 25 by 100 gives the correct decimal, 0.25.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** 50% means [ ___ ] of an amount.

**Back:** 50% means half of an amount.

**Choices:**

- half ✔
  - _why:_ Since $50\% = \frac{50}{100} = \frac{1}{2}$, finding 50% of an amount is the same as finding half of it.
- double
  - _why:_ Doubling an amount gives 200%, not 50%. Since $50\% = \frac{1}{2}$, 50% of an amount means half of that amount.
- a quarter
  - _why:_ A quarter corresponds to $\frac{1}{4} = 25\%$, not 50%. Since $50\% = \frac{1}{2}$, the correct term is half.
- three quarters
  - _why:_ Three quarters corresponds to $\frac{3}{4} = 75\%$, not 50%. Dividing 50 by 100 gives $\frac{1}{2}$, so the correct term is half.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** In percentage questions, “of” means [ ___ ].

**Back:** In percentage questions, “of” means multiply.

**Choices:**

- multiply ✔
  - _why:_ In a calculation such as 20% of 50, “of” indicates multiplication: $0.20 \times 50$. Therefore, percentage multipliers are multiplied by the amount.
- divide
  - _why:_ Division may be used when first converting or simplifying a percentage, but “of” connects the percentage to the amount by multiplication. For example, 20% of 50 is $0.20 \times 50$.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding is used when increasing an amount after calculating the percentage, but “of” itself means multiply. For example, 20% of 50 is found using $0.20 \times 50$.
- subtract
  - _why:_ Subtracting may be required after finding a percentage decrease, but it is not the meaning of “of”. The percentage is multiplied by the amount first.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** For a percentage increase, what do you do with the percentage amount?

**Back:** Add it to the original amount.

**Choices:**

- Add it to the original amount. ✔
  - _why:_ An increase makes the original amount larger, so the calculated percentage amount must be added to the original amount to obtain the new value.
- Subtract it from the original amount.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the percentage amount produces a percentage decrease, not an increase. For an increase, add the percentage amount to the original amount.
- Replace the original amount with it.
  - _why:_ The percentage amount represents only the increase, not the new total. It must be added to the original amount to find the increased value.
- Divide it by the original amount.
  - _why:_ Dividing the percentage amount by the original finds a proportion rather than a new value. A percentage increase requires adding the percentage amount to the original.

## Card 24 — `basic`

**Front:** For simple interest, which amount is used each year?

**Back:** The original amount, or principal.

**Choices:**

- The original amount, or principal. ✔
  - _why:_ Simple interest is calculated each year using the initial sum invested or borrowed, called the principal. Previously earned interest is not included in later calculations.
- The current balance, including previous interest.
  - _why:_ Using the current balance means earning interest on previous interest, which is compound interest. Simple interest always uses the original amount, or principal.
- The interest earned in the previous year.
  - _why:_ Simple interest is not calculated from earlier interest payments. It is calculated each year from the original amount, also known as the principal.
- The final amount, after all interest.
  - _why:_ The final amount is only known after the interest has been calculated, so it cannot be the yearly base. Simple interest uses the original principal.
