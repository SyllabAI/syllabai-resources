## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In $4,372$, what is the digit 7 worth?

**Back:** 7 tens or 70.

**Choices:**

- 7 tens or 70. ✔
  - _why:_ The 7 is in the tens column, so it represents seven groups of ten. Therefore, its place value is 7 tens, which equals 70.
- 7 hundreds or 700.
  - _why:_ This treats the 7 as though it were in the hundreds column. It is in the tens column, so its value is 7 tens, or 70.
- 7 ones or 7.
  - _why:_ This ignores the position of the digit and uses its face value. The 7 occupies the tens column, so it is worth 70.
- 7 thousands or 7,000.
  - _why:_ This confuses the 7 with the leftmost digit in the thousands column. The 7 is actually in the tens column and is worth 70.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In $58.26$, the 5 is worth [ ___ ] and the 2 is worth [ ___ ].

**Back:** In $58.26$, the 5 is worth 50 and the 2 is worth two tenths.

**Choices:**

- In $58.26$, the 5 is worth 50 and the 2 is worth two tenths. ✔
  - _why:_ The 5 is in the tens column, making its value 50. The 2 is immediately after the decimal point, so it represents two tenths.
- In $58.26$, the 5 is worth 5 and the 2 is worth two tenths.
  - _why:_ This uses the face value of 5 instead of its place value. The 5 is in the tens column, so it is worth 50; the 2 is two tenths.
- In $58.26$, the 5 is worth 50 and the 2 is worth two hundredths.
  - _why:_ This places the 2 one decimal position too far right. As the first digit after the decimal point, it represents two tenths, while the 5 represents 50.
- In $58.26$, the 5 is worth 500 and the 2 is worth two hundredths.
  - _why:_ Both digits have been shifted one place to the left or right incorrectly. The 5 is worth 50, and the first decimal digit represents two tenths.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In a column method, what is the main alignment rule?

**Back:** Line up digits with the same place value.

**Choices:**

- Line up digits with the same place value. ✔
  - _why:_ Digits must be aligned by units, tens, hundreds and corresponding decimal places. This ensures that each column combines quantities with the same place value.
- Line up digits with the same digit.
  - _why:_ Matching identical digits does not preserve their values, because the same digit can occur in different columns. Digits must instead be aligned by place value.
- Line up digits by their left-hand edge.
  - _why:_ Aligning the left-hand edges can put units beneath tens when numbers have different lengths. Column methods require digits with the same place value to line up.
- Line up digits by their size.
  - _why:_ The visual or numerical size of individual digits does not determine their column. Digits must be aligned according to place value, such as units beneath units.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** If a column addition totals 10 or more, what should you do?

**Back:** Carry into the next column.

**Choices:**

- Carry into the next column. ✔
  - _why:_ A total of 10 or more contains an extra group of ten. Write the units digit in the current column and carry the remaining amount into the next column.
- Exchange from the next column.
  - _why:_ Exchanging from the next column is used in subtraction when the top digit is too small. In addition, a total of 10 or more must be carried.
- Write the total in the same column.
  - _why:_ Writing a two-digit total in one column destroys the place-value alignment. Write its units digit there and carry the tens digit into the next column.
- Ignore the tens digit.
  - _why:_ Ignoring the tens digit loses part of the total and makes the answer too small. The tens digit must be carried into the next place-value column.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Carrying changes [ ___ ] of one place value into [ ___ ] of the next place value.

**Back:** Carrying changes 10 of one place value into 1 of the next place value.

**Choices:**

- Carrying changes 10 of one place value into 1 of the next place value. ✔
  - _why:_ The base-ten system groups ten units of any place value into one unit of the next place value, such as 10 tens becoming 1 hundred.
- Carrying changes 100 of one place value into 1 of the next place value.
  - _why:_ This skips a place-value column by grouping one hundred at once. Carrying uses the base-ten relationship: 10 of one place value becomes 1 of the next.
- Carrying changes 10 of one place value into 10 of the next place value.
  - _why:_ This fails to convert between adjacent place values and would multiply the amount by ten. Instead, 10 units of one place value become 1 of the next.
- Carrying changes 1 of one place value into 10 of the next place value.
  - _why:_ This reverses the carrying relationship and describes moving towards a smaller place value. Carrying converts 10 of one place value into 1 of the next.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $368 + 74$.

**Back:** $442$

**Choices:**

- $442$ ✔
  - _why:_ Adding the units gives $8+4=12$, so write 2 and carry 1. The tens then total 14, producing $368+74=442$.
- $432$
  - _why:_ This omits the carried 1 from the units calculation. Since $8+4=12$, that extra ten must be included, giving the correct total of $442$.
- $438$
  - _why:_ This adds only the 70 from 74 and leaves out its 4 units. Including both parts gives $368+70+4=442$.
- $452$
  - _why:_ This effectively counts an extra ten while carrying. After writing 2 from $8+4=12$, the tens total is 14, so the correct result is $442$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In column subtraction, what should you do if the top digit is too small?

**Back:** Exchange from the next column.

**Choices:**

- Exchange from the next column. ✔
  - _why:_ Exchange one unit from the next place-value column for ten units in the current column. This makes the top digit large enough for the subtraction.
- Carry into the next column.
  - _why:_ Carrying into the next column is associated with addition totals of 10 or more. In subtraction, exchange from the next column when the top digit is too small.
- Subtract the top digit from the bottom digit.
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction in one column changes the calculation and can hide the need to exchange. Instead, exchange from the next column and keep the original order.
- Add 10 without changing the next column.
  - _why:_ Adding 10 without reducing the next column changes the number’s total value. Exchange correctly by adding 10 to the current column and subtracting 1 from the next.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Back:** Exchanging 1 ten gives 10 ones.

**Choices:**

- Exchanging 1 ten gives 10 ones. ✔
  - _why:_ A ten has the same value as ten ones, so it can be exchanged during subtraction without changing the total value.
- Exchanging 1 ten gives 100 ones.
  - _why:_ This confuses tens with hundreds. One ten is worth 10 ones; 100 ones would have the value of one hundred.
- Exchanging 1 one gives 10 tens.
  - _why:_ This reverses the place-value relationship and increases the value. The valid exchange is 1 ten for 10 ones, which preserves the total.
- Exchanging 10 tens gives 1 one.
  - _why:_ This reduces a value of one hundred to one, so it is not an equivalent exchange. One ten exchanges for 10 ones.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** For decimals, the first digit after the decimal point is [ ___ ] and the second is [ ___ ].

**Back:** For decimals, the first digit after the decimal point is tenths and the second is hundredths.

**Choices:**

- For decimals, the first digit after the decimal point is tenths and the second is hundredths. ✔
  - _why:_ Moving right from the decimal point, place values divide by 10 each time. The first position is tenths and the second is hundredths.
- For decimals, the first digit after the decimal point is units and the second is tenths.
  - _why:_ Units are immediately to the left of the decimal point, not after it. The first two positions to the right are tenths and hundredths.
- For decimals, the first digit after the decimal point is hundredths and the second is thousandths.
  - _why:_ This shifts both place values one position too far to the right. The first decimal digit represents tenths and the second represents hundredths.
- For decimals, the first digit after the decimal point is tenths and the second is thousandths.
  - _why:_ This skips the hundredths position. Decimal places progress from tenths to hundredths and then thousandths as they move right.

**Back:** The decimal points.

**Choices:**

- The decimal points. ✔
  - _why:_ Aligning decimal points places digits of equal place value in the same columns, allowing units, tenths and hundredths to be combined correctly.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can placeholder zeros be added after a decimal point?

**Back:** They do not change the number’s value.

**Choices:**

- They do not change the number’s value. ✔
  - _why:_ Zeros written at the end of a decimal do not add any extra tenths, hundredths or thousandths, so the represented value remains unchanged.
- They always increase the number’s value.
  - _why:_ This confuses appending decimal zeros with multiplying by 10. A trailing zero changes the notation and place-value columns, but not the number’s value.
- They always decrease the number’s value.
  - _why:_ Adding trailing zeros does not divide the number or shift its decimal point. They leave the value unchanged while making decimal columns easier to align.
- They double the number’s value.
  - _why:_ A trailing placeholder zero does not perform multiplication. It simply shows an unused decimal place, so the number’s value stays the same.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** For a calculation containing only addition and subtraction, what is a safe method?

**Back:** Work from left to right.

**Choices:**

- Work from left to right. ✔
  - _why:_ Addition and subtraction have equal priority, so a calculation containing only these operations is evaluated in order from left to right.
- Work from right to left.
  - _why:_ Working right to left reverses the required order and can change the result because subtraction is not commutative. Addition and subtraction should be performed from left to right.
- Do all addition first.
  - _why:_ Addition does not take priority over subtraction; the two operations have equal precedence. Without brackets, they should be carried out from left to right.
- Do all subtraction first.
  - _why:_ Subtraction does not take priority over addition; both operations have equal precedence. A calculation containing only addition and subtraction should be worked from left to right.

## Card 22 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a longer calculation, the sign before a number [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a longer calculation, the sign before a number belongs with that number.

**Choices:**

- belongs with that number ✔
  - _why:_ The sign determines whether that particular number is added or subtracted. Keeping each sign attached to its number helps prevent sign errors when rearranging or calculating.
- can be ignored during working
  - _why:_ Ignoring the sign changes whether the number is added or subtracted, often changing the answer substantially. The sign belongs with the number immediately following it.
- belongs with the previous number
  - _why:_ A sign applies to the number that follows it, not the preceding number. Treating it as part of the previous term commonly causes errors in longer calculations.
- always means subtract that number
  - _why:_ A plus sign means the following number is added, while a minus sign means it is subtracted. Therefore, the sign belongs with that number rather than always indicating subtraction.
