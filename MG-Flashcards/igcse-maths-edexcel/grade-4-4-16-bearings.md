## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A full turn is [ ___ ]; a straight line is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A full turn is 360°; a straight line is 180°.

**Choices:**

- A full turn is 360°; a straight line is 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ One complete rotation returns to the starting direction and measures 360°, while the angle formed by a straight line is half a turn, measuring 180°.
- A full turn is 180°; a straight line is 360°.
  - _why:_ This reverses the two standard angle facts. A full turn measures 360°, whereas a straight line represents half a turn and measures 180°.
- A full turn is 360°; a straight line is 90°.
  - _why:_ A 90° angle is a quarter turn, not a straight line. A full turn measures 360°, and a straight line measures 180°.
- A full turn is 180°; a straight line is 90°.
  - _why:_ This confuses a half turn with a full turn and a quarter turn with a straight line. The correct measures are 360° and 180°, respectively.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What do angles in a triangle add up to?

**Back:** 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ The three interior angles of any triangle have a total of 180°, regardless of the triangle’s side lengths or individual angle sizes.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ A total of 360° applies to angles around a point or the interior angles of a quadrilateral. The angles inside a triangle add to 180°.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ A right angle measures 90°, but that is not the total for every triangle. The three interior angles of a triangle add to 180°.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ A total of 270° may result from confusing three right angles with a triangle’s angle sum. Every triangle’s interior angles instead total 180°.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** A bearing is measured from which reference direction?

**Back:** North.

**Choices:**

- North. ✔
  - _why:_ Bearings always begin from the North line at the relevant starting point. The angle is then measured clockwise from North to the required direction.
- East.
  - _why:_ East corresponds to a bearing of 090°, but it is not the reference direction. Bearings are measured clockwise starting from North, which is 000°.
- South.
  - _why:_ South corresponds to a bearing of 180°, so it is a possible destination direction rather than the reference direction. Bearings start from North.
- West.
  - _why:_ West corresponds to a bearing of 270°, but bearings do not start there. Their reference direction is always North at the starting point.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** A bearing is measured clockwise or anticlockwise?

**Back:** Clockwise.

**Choices:**

- Clockwise. ✔
  - _why:_ By convention, a bearing is the angle turned clockwise from North to the required direction. This ensures that each direction has one standard three-figure bearing.
- Anticlockwise.
  - _why:_ Measuring anticlockwise would generally produce a different angle and is not the bearings convention. Bearings are measured clockwise from the North line.
- Either direction.
  - _why:_ A bearing uses one specified direction of rotation, rather than whichever direction gives the smaller angle. It must be measured clockwise from North.
- The shortest direction.
  - _why:_ Using the shortest direction can lead to an anticlockwise angle, which is not a standard bearing. The angle must always be measured clockwise from North.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How many digits must a three-figure bearing have before °?

**Back:** Three digits.

**Choices:**

- Three digits. ✔
  - _why:_ A three-figure bearing contains exactly three digits before the degree symbol. Leading zeros are added when necessary, such as writing 8° as 008°.
- Two digits.
  - _why:_ Using two digits omits a required leading zero for bearings below 100°. A three-figure bearing must contain exactly three digits before the degree symbol.
- One digit.
  - _why:_ A single digit gives an ordinary angle notation rather than a three-figure bearing. Leading zeros must be included to make exactly three digits.
- Four digits.
  - _why:_ Four digits would add an unnecessary leading zero and would no longer be a three-figure bearing. Exactly three digits must appear before the degree symbol.

- In the bearing of B from A, stand at B and look towards B.
  - _why:_ Standing at B and looking towards B gives no direction between the two locations. The wording “B from A” requires standing at A and looking towards B.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Back:** The North line at A.

**Choices:**

- The North line at A. ✔
  - _why:_ The phrase “from A” makes A the starting point of the bearing. Therefore, the clockwise angle must be measured from the North line drawn at A.

## Card 8 — `basic`

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** As three-figure bearings, East is [ ___ ] and South is [ ___ ].

**Back:** As three-figure bearings, East is 090° and South is 180°.

**Choices:**

- As three-figure bearings, East is 090° and South is 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ Bearings are measured clockwise from North. East is a quarter-turn clockwise, giving 090°, while South is a half-turn clockwise, giving 180°.
- As three-figure bearings, East is 180° and South is 090°.
  - _why:_ This swaps the bearings for East and South. From North, East is 90° clockwise and South is 180° clockwise, so their bearings are 090° and 180° respectively.
- As three-figure bearings, East is 270° and South is 180°.
  - _why:_ The bearing 270° points West, not East. East is reached by turning 90° clockwise from North, so its three-figure bearing is 090°; South is 180°.
- As three-figure bearings, East is 090° and South is 270°.
  - _why:_ The bearing 270° points West, not South. South is a half-turn clockwise from North, so its bearing is 180°, while East is correctly represented by 090°.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Back:** As three-figure bearings, North is 000° and West is 270°.

**Choices:**

- As three-figure bearings, North is 090° and West is 270°.
  - _why:_ The bearing 090° points East, not North. North is the zero direction and must be written as 000° in three-figure form; West is 270°.
- As three-figure bearings, North is 000° and West is 090°.
  - _why:_ The bearing 090° points East rather than West. Travelling clockwise from North to West requires three quarter-turns, so West has a bearing of 270°.
- As three-figure bearings, North is 180° and West is 090°.
  - _why:_ This confuses North with South and West with East. North is the starting direction at 000°, while West is 270° clockwise from North.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the three-figure bearing of South-West?

**Back:** 225°.

**Choices:**

- 225°. ✔
  - _why:_ South-West lies halfway between South at 180° and West at 270°. Adding 45° to 180° gives the three-figure bearing 225°.
- 135°.
  - _why:_ The bearing 135° points South-East, showing that East and West have been confused. South-West lies between 180° and 270°, exactly halfway at 225°.
- 215°.
  - _why:_ This uses an incorrect 35° offset from South. The eight principal compass directions are separated by 45°, so South-West is 180° + 45° = 225°.
- 315°.
  - _why:_ The bearing 315° points North-West, not South-West. South-West is halfway between South at 180° and West at 270°, giving a bearing of 225°.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A direction is 34° anticlockwise from North. What clockwise bearing is it?

**Back:** 326°.

**Choices:**

- 326°. ✔
  - _why:_ A 34° anticlockwise turn from North is equivalent to a clockwise turn of the remainder of a full circle. Therefore, the bearing is 360° - 34° = 326°.
- 034°.
  - _why:_ This treats an anticlockwise angle as though it were clockwise. Subtracting 34° from a full turn converts it correctly: 360° - 34° = 326°.
- 304°.
  - _why:_ This results from subtracting 56° rather than the stated 34°. The equivalent clockwise bearing is found using 360° - 34°, which equals 326°.
- 336°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts 24° from 360°, losing ten degrees from the given angle. The correct conversion is 360° - 34° = 326°.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** When drawing a bearing, where should the protractor centre be placed?

**Back:** On the starting point.

**Choices:**

- On the starting point. ✔
  - _why:_ A bearing describes the turn made at the starting point, so the protractor’s centre must be placed there. This allows the angle to be measured from North accurately.
- On the destination point.
  - _why:_ Placing the centre at the destination measures the reverse direction instead. The bearing begins at the starting point, so the protractor’s centre must be positioned there.
- On the North arrow.
  - _why:_ The North arrow shows the reference direction but is not necessarily the angle’s vertex. The protractor’s centre belongs on the starting point, where the bearing begins.
- On the line midpoint.
  - _why:_ The midpoint is not the vertex from which the direction is measured. Bearings are measured at the starting point, so the protractor’s centre must be placed there.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** If protractor numbers get smaller as you move clockwise from North, what is wrong?

**Back:** You are using the wrong scale.

**Choices:**

- You are using the wrong scale. ✔
  - _why:_ Bearing angles must increase as you move clockwise from North. If the numbers decrease, you are reading the protractor’s opposite scale and must switch scales.
- You have misplaced the protractor centre.
  - _why:_ A misplaced centre affects the angle’s vertex but does not determine whether the numbers increase clockwise. Decreasing numbers show that the wrong protractor scale is being read.
- You have aligned with the East line.
  - _why:_ Aligning with East would create a 90° offset, but it does not explain the decreasing numbers. That pattern means you are reading the wrong protractor scale.
- You have marked the destination point.
  - _why:_ Marking the destination does not control the order of the protractor numbers. If they decrease clockwise from North, the wrong scale is being used.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ A bearing is defined as the clockwise angle measured from the North line at the starting point to the line showing the direction of travel.

  - _why:_ This confuses both the rotation and the endpoint of the angle. A bearing is measured clockwise from North to the line of travel, with its vertex at the starting point.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** Reverse bearings differ by [ ___ ] because the opposite direction is a [ ___ ].

**Back:** Reverse bearings differ by 180° because the opposite direction is a half-turn.

**Choices:**

- Reverse bearings differ by 180° because the opposite direction is a half-turn. ✔
  - _why:_ A reverse bearing points in exactly the opposite direction. Opposite directions are separated by 180°, which is one half-turn around a point.
- Reverse bearings differ by 90° because the opposite direction is a quarter-turn.
  - _why:_ A 90° quarter-turn gives a perpendicular direction, not the opposite direction. Reverse bearings differ by 180° because changing direction is a half-turn.
- Reverse bearings differ by 360° because the opposite direction is a full-turn.
  - _why:_ A 360° full-turn returns to the original direction rather than reversing it. Reverse bearings differ by 180°, corresponding to a half-turn.
- Reverse bearings differ by 180° because the opposite direction is a full-turn.
  - _why:_ The angular difference of 180° is correct, but 180° is a half-turn, not a full-turn. A full-turn is 360° and returns to the original direction.
