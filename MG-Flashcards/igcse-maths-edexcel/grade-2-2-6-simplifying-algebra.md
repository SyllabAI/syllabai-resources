## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a variable in an algebraic expression?

**Back:** A letter that stands for a number.

**Choices:**

- A letter that stands for a number. ✔
  - _why:_ A variable is represented by a letter whose numerical value may be unknown or may change. For example, $x$ is the variable in $3x + 2$.
- A number that multiplies a letter.
  - _why:_ This describes a coefficient, which is the numerical multiplier of a variable. A variable is a letter that stands for a number.
- A term that has no letter.
  - _why:_ This describes a constant term, such as 5 or -3. A variable is a letter used to stand for a number.
- A symbol that means an operation.
  - _why:_ Symbols such as + and − indicate operations, rather than variables. A variable is a letter that represents a number.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In the term $6m$, what is the coefficient?

**Back:** 6

**Choices:**

- 6 ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying the variable. Since $6m$ means $6 \times m$, its coefficient is 6.
- 0
  - _why:_ Choosing 0 confuses the coefficient with an absent constant term. In $6m$, the number multiplying $m$ is 6, so the coefficient is 6.
- 1
  - _why:_ A coefficient of 1 is implied only when a variable appears alone, as in $m$. In $6m$, the written multiplier and coefficient is 6.
- 12
  - _why:_ This unnecessarily doubles the numerical multiplier. The term already means $6 \times m$, so its coefficient is 6, not 12.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is a term with no variable, such as 5 or -3.

**Back:** A constant is a term with no variable, such as 5 or -3.

**Choices:**

- constant ✔
  - _why:_ A constant is a term with a fixed numerical value and no variable. Examples include 5, -3 and 12.
- coefficient
  - _why:_ A coefficient is a number that multiplies a variable, such as 6 in $6m$. A term containing no variable is called a constant.
- variable
  - _why:_ A variable is a letter representing a number, so it is not a term with no variable. Such a term is called a constant.
- expression
  - _why:_ An expression can contain variables, constants and operations, so it is not specifically a term without a variable. The required term is constant.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $x + x + x$.

**Back:** $$3x$

**Choices:**

- $$3x$ ✔
  - _why:_ The three terms are like terms, each with an implied coefficient of 1. Adding their coefficients gives $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$, producing $3x$.
- $$x^3$
  - _why:_ This confuses repeated addition with repeated multiplication: $x^3$ means $x \times x \times x$. Adding three identical $x$ terms gives $3x$.
- $$3 + x$
  - _why:_ Counting the terms as 3 but retaining only one $x$ does not combine their coefficients. Adding three coefficients of 1 gives $3x$.
- $$2x$
  - _why:_ This misses one of the three $x$ terms. Their coefficients add as $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$, so the simplified result is $3x$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebra, a single $x$ means $\text{[...]}$, not zero $x$.

**Back:** In algebra, a single $x$ means $\boxed{1x}$, not zero $x$.

**Choices:**

- $$1x$ ✔
  - _why:_ When no coefficient is written before a variable, the coefficient is understood to be 1. Therefore, $x$ means exactly the same as $1x$.
- $$0x$
  - _why:_ A coefficient of 0 would make the whole term equal to zero. A variable written alone has an implied coefficient of 1, so $x$ means $1x$.
- $$x^2$
  - _why:_ This confuses an omitted coefficient with an omitted index. A single $x$ has coefficient 1 and means $1x$, whereas $x^2$ means $x \times x$.
- $$1 + x$
  - _why:_ An implied coefficient represents multiplication, not addition. A variable written alone means $1 \times x$, written as $1x$, rather than $1 + x$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** Like terms must have exactly the same [ ___ ].

**Back:** Like terms must have exactly the same variable part.

**Choices:**

- variable part ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms have identical variables raised to identical powers. Their coefficients may differ, but the complete variable part must be exactly the same.
- coefficient
  - _why:_ Coefficients do not need to match: for example, $2x$ and $5x$ are like terms. It is their variable part that must be identical.
- numerical value
  - _why:_ Like terms can have different values because their coefficients may differ. They are classified by having exactly the same variable part, including any powers.
- number of terms
  - _why:_ The number of terms in an expression does not determine whether individual terms are alike. Like terms must instead have exactly the same variable part.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $k + k + 9$.

**Back:** $$2k + 9$

**Choices:**

- $$2k + 9$ ✔
  - _why:_ The two $k$ terms are like terms, so their implied coefficients add to 2. The constant 9 is unlike them and remains unchanged.
- $$k + 9$
  - _why:_ This loses one of the two $k$ terms by failing to add their coefficients. Since $k + k = 2k$, the result is $2k + 9$.
- $$2k + 18$
  - _why:_ Only the two like $k$ terms should be combined. The 9 appears once and must not be doubled, so the result is $2k + 9$.
- $$11k$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly combines the constant 9 with the variable terms as though all three were alike. Only $k + k$ combines, giving $2k + 9$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can $3a$ and $3b$ not be collected together?

**Back:** They are unlike terms (they have different variables).

**Choices:**

- They are unlike terms (they have different variables). ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms must have exactly the same variable part. Although both coefficients are 3, one term contains $a$ and the other contains $b$, so they cannot be collected.
- They have different coefficients (one coefficient is 3 and the other is 1).
  - _why:_ Both terms have coefficient 3, so this misidentifies their numerical multipliers. They cannot be collected because their variables, $a$ and $b$, are different.
- They are constant terms (their values do not change).
  - _why:_ Both terms contain variables, so neither is a constant term. They cannot be collected because $a$ and $b$ give them different variable parts.
- They are like terms (both contain one variable).
  - _why:_ Having one variable each is not enough to make terms alike; the variables must be identical. Since one has $a$ and the other has $b$, they are unlike terms.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Are $x$ and $x^2$ like terms? Why?

**Back:** No; they have different powers.

**Choices:**

- No; they have different powers. ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms must contain exactly the same variables raised to the same powers. Here, $x$ has power $1$, while $x^2$ has power $2$.
- Yes; they have the same variable.
  - _why:_ Sharing the variable $x$ is not sufficient for terms to be alike. Their powers must also match, so $x$ and $x^2$ are not like terms.
- Yes; they have different powers.
  - _why:_ Different powers make terms unlike, rather than like. Since $x=x^1$ and $x^2$ have different exponents, they cannot be collected together.
- No; they have different variables.
  - _why:_ Both terms contain the same variable, $x$, so the variables are not different. They are unlike because the powers of $x$ are different.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the four terms in $8c - 5d + 2c - d$.

**Back:** $8c$$-5d$$2c$$-d$

**Choices:**

- $8c$$-5d$$2c$$-d$ ✔
  - _why:_ Terms are separated by addition or subtraction signs, and each preceding sign belongs to its term. Therefore the four terms include the negative signs on both $d$-terms.
- $8c$$5d$$2c$$d$
  - _why:_ This drops both subtraction signs, incorrectly making the $d$-terms positive. A sign belongs to the term after it, so the terms are $8c$, $-5d$, $2c$, and $-d$.
- $8c$$-5d$$-2c$$-d$
  - _why:_ The plus sign before $2c$ makes that term positive, not negative. The four terms are $8c$, $-5d$, $2c$, and $-d$.
- $-8c$$-5d$$2c$$-d$
  - _why:_ The first term has no written sign, so it is positive rather than negative. The terms are $8c$, $-5d$, $2c$, and $-d$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** In an expression, the sign before a term [ ___ ].

**Back:** In an expression, the sign before a term belongs to that term.

**Choices:**

- belongs to that term ✔
  - _why:_ A plus or minus sign immediately before a term determines whether that entire term is positive or negative, so it must remain attached when identifying or rearranging terms.
- belongs to the previous term
  - _why:_ This reverses how signs are attached in an expression. A sign belongs to the term immediately after it, which is why $-5d$ is one complete term.
- can always be ignored
  - _why:_ Ignoring signs changes the values of negative terms and often produces an incorrect simplification. The sign before a term belongs to that term and must move with it.
- applies only to the variable
  - _why:_ The sign affects the entire term, including its coefficient and variable part. For example, the minus sign in $-5d$ applies to the whole term.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $10a - 3b + 4a - 5b$.

**Back:** $$14a - 8b$

**Choices:**

- $$14a - 8b$ ✔
  - _why:_ Collecting like terms gives $10a+4a=14a$ and $-3b-5b=-8b$. Terms containing different variables cannot be combined with each other.
- $$14a + 2b$
  - _why:_ This treats $-3b-5b$ as though the second negative were positive. Adding two negative coefficients gives $-8$, so the simplified expression is $14a-8b$.
- $$6a - 8b$
  - _why:_ This subtracts the coefficient $4$ from $10$ despite the plus sign before $4a$. The $a$-terms total $14a$, giving $14a-8b$.
- $$14a - 2b$
  - _why:_ This finds the difference between $3$ and $5$ instead of adding two negative coefficients. Since $-3-5=-8$, the result is $14a-8b$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** The simplified way to write $a \times b$ is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The simplified way to write $a \times b$ is $\boxed{ab}$.

**Choices:**

- $$ab$ ✔
  - _why:_ In algebra, adjacent letters represent multiplication, so the multiplication sign between $a$ and $b$ can be omitted and the product written as $ab$.
- $$a+b$
  - _why:_ Replacing multiplication with addition changes the operation and therefore the expression’s value. The product of $a$ and $b$ is written in simplified form as $ab$.
- $$a^b$
  - _why:_ Writing $a^b$ makes $b$ an exponent, meaning a power rather than a product. Multiplication of the two variables is written simply as $ab$.
- $$2ab$
  - _why:_ The coefficient $2$ would double the original product, but no factor of $2$ appears in $a\times b$. The simplified product is therefore $ab$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $5a \times 2b$.

**Back:** $$10ab$

**Choices:**

- $$10ab$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply the numerical coefficients to get $5\times2=10$, then include both variable factors. This gives the product $10ab$.
- $$7ab$
  - _why:_ This adds the coefficients instead of multiplying them. Since the operation is multiplication, $5\times2=10$, and including both variables gives $10ab$.
- $$10a+b$
  - _why:_ This multiplies the numbers but incorrectly changes multiplication between the variable factors into addition. Both variables remain factors, so the product is $10ab$.
- $$10a^2b$
  - _why:_ The factor $a$ appears only once, so it should not be squared. Multiplying the coefficients and retaining one of each variable gives $10ab$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $3a \times 4b$ not $7ab$?

**Back:** Coefficients must be multiplied ($3 \times 4$), not added.

**Choices:**

- Coefficients must be multiplied ($3 \times 4$), not added. ✔
  - _why:_ Because the terms are being multiplied, their numerical coefficients must also be multiplied. Thus $3\times4=12$, making the product $12ab$ rather than $7ab$.
- Coefficients must be added ($3 + 4$), not multiplied.
  - _why:_ Adding the coefficients is the misconception that produces $7ab$. The multiplication sign requires $3\times4=12$, so the correct product is $12ab$.
- Coefficients must be subtracted ($4 - 3$), not multiplied.
  - _why:_ Subtraction is not justified when the original operation is multiplication. The coefficients must be multiplied, giving $3\times4=12$ and therefore $12ab$.
- Coefficients must be concatenated ($3$ and $4$), not multiplied.
  - _why:_ Placing coefficient digits together would incorrectly produce $34$ and is not an algebraic multiplication rule. The coefficients multiply to $12$, so the result is $12ab$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** The expression $y^3$ means $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The expression $y^3$ means $\boxed{y \times y \times y}$.

**Choices:**

- $$y \times y \times y$ ✔
  - _why:_ An exponent shows how many times the base is used as a factor. The power $y^3$ therefore contains three factors of $y$ multiplied together.
- $$y \times 3$
  - _why:_ This treats the exponent as a multiplier, confusing $y^3$ with $3y$. An exponent of $3$ means multiplying three factors: $y\times y\times y$.
- $$y + y + y$
  - _why:_ Repeated addition of $y$ gives $3y$, not $y^3$. A power represents repeated multiplication, so $y^3$ means $y\times y\times y$.
- $$y \times y$
  - _why:_ This uses only two factors of $y$, which represents $y^2$. The exponent $3$ requires three factors, giving $y\times y\times y$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $r \times r \times r$.

**Back:** $$r^3$

**Choices:**

- $$r^3$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying three identical factors of $r$ means that $r$ is used as a factor three times, which is written in index notation as $r^3$.
- $$r^2$
  - _why:_ An exponent of 2 represents only two factors of $r$. The expression contains three identical factors multiplied together, so index notation gives $r^3$.
- $$3r$
  - _why:_ Writing $3r$ confuses repeated multiplication with repeated addition. Three factors of $r$ are multiplied, so the simplified expression is $r^3$, not a multiple of $r$.
- $$r^4$
  - _why:_ Adding the powers incorrectly gives an exponent one too large. There are exactly three factors of $r$, so the correct index is 3 and the answer is $r^3$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $4x^2 + 2x^2 - x^2$.

**Back:** $$5x^2$

**Choices:**

- $$5x^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ All three terms contain $x^2$, so their coefficients can be combined. Since $4+2-1=5$, the expression simplifies to $5x^2$.
- $$6x^2$
  - _why:_ The coefficients have been added without subtracting the final coefficient: $4+2-1=5$, not 6. Since the terms are like terms, the answer is $5x^2$.
- $$5x^4$
  - _why:_ Combining like terms changes only their coefficients; it does not add the powers of $x$. Calculating $4+2-1$ gives the correct result $5x^2$.
- $$5x$
  - _why:_ The power of $x$ remains 2 when like terms are added or subtracted. Combining the coefficients gives $4+2-1=5$, so the answer is $5x^2$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{12h}{3}$.

**Back:** $$4h$

**Choices:**

- $$4h$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the coefficient 12 by 3 gives 4, while the factor $h$ remains unchanged. Therefore, $rac{12h}{3}=4h$.
- $$4$
  - _why:_ Cancelling $h$ is invalid because there is no factor of $h$ in the denominator. Only $12$ is divided by 3, giving the correct answer $4h$.
- $$9h$
  - _why:_ Subtracting 3 from 12 gives 9, but the fraction requires division rather than subtraction. Since $12\div3=4$, the correct simplification is $4h$.
- $$36h$
  - _why:_ Multiplying 12 by 3 gives 36, but the fraction bar indicates division. Dividing the coefficient by 3 gives $12\div3=4$, so the answer is $4h$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{15d}{d}$.

**Back:** 15

**Choices:**

- 15 ✔
  - _why:_ The common factor $d$ cancels because $d/d=1$ for $d\ne0$. This leaves the numerical coefficient 15, so the simplified result is 15.
- $$15d$
  - _why:_ Cancelling the common factor $d$ removes it from both numerator and denominator. Since $d/d=1$ for $d\ne0$, the expression simplifies to 15, not $15d$.
- 1
  - _why:_ Although $d/d=1$, the coefficient 15 must remain. Cancelling only the common factor $d$ therefore leaves 15 rather than reducing the entire numerator to 1.
- 0
  - _why:_ Division by the same non-zero variable gives 1, not 0. The remaining coefficient is 15, so $rac{15d}{d}$ simplifies to 15.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{8y + 4y}{2}$.

**Back:** Collect: $12y / 2$Answer: $6y$

**Choices:**

- Collect: $12y / 2$Answer: $6y$ ✔
  - _why:_ The like terms in the numerator combine to give $8y+4y=12y$. Dividing this result by 2 gives $12y\div2=6y$.
- Collect: $12y / 2$Answer: $12y$
  - _why:_ The numerator is collected correctly, but it must still be divided by 2. Since $12y\div2=6y$, the final answer is $6y$, not $12y$.
- Collect: $8y / 2$Answer: $4y$
  - _why:_ The like terms in the numerator should be added, so $8y+4y=12y$, not $8y$. Dividing the collected numerator by 2 then gives $6y$.
- Collect: $12y / 4$Answer: $3y$
  - _why:_ The denominator remains 2; the coefficient 4 in the numerator is part of the term $4y$, not an additional divisor. Thus $12y\div2=6y$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $7g + 4h - 2g + 3h$.

**Back:** $$5g + 7h$

**Choices:**

- $$5g + 7h$ ✔
  - _why:_ Collecting like terms separately gives $7g-2g=5g$ and $4h+3h=7h$. Therefore, the whole expression simplifies to $5g+7h$.
- $$9g + 7h$
  - _why:_ The subtraction of $2g$ has been overlooked. Combining the $g$-terms gives $7g-2g=5g$, while the $h$-terms give $4h+3h=7h$.
- $$5g + h$
  - _why:_ The $h$-coefficients should be added, not subtracted: $4+3=7$. The $g$-terms give $7g-2g=5g$, so the result is $5g+7h$.
- $$12g + 2h$
  - _why:_ Unlike terms cannot be combined by adding all their coefficients. The $g$-terms and $h$-terms must be collected separately, producing $5g+7h$.
