## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** An angle less than 90° is [ ___ ]; an angle exactly 90° is [ ___ ].

**Back:** An angle less than 90° is acute; an angle exactly 90° is right.

**Choices:**

- An angle less than 90° is acute; an angle exactly 90° is right. ✔
  - _why:_ Acute angles measure between 0° and 90°, while right angles measure exactly 90°. These are the standard classifications for the two angle ranges.
- An angle less than 90° is right; an angle exactly 90° is acute.
  - _why:_ This reverses the classifications. An angle below 90° is acute, whereas an angle measuring exactly 90° is a right angle.
- An angle less than 90° is obtuse; an angle exactly 90° is obtuse.
  - _why:_ Obtuse angles are greater than 90° and less than 180°, so neither description fits. Below 90° is acute, and exactly 90° is right.
- An angle less than 90° is acute; an angle exactly 90° is reflex.
  - _why:_ A reflex angle is greater than 180°, so it cannot measure exactly 90°. An angle below 90° is acute, and one exactly 90° is right.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** An angle more than 90° but less than 180° is [ ___ ]; an angle more than 180° but less than 360° is [ ___ ].

**Back:** An angle more than 90° but less than 180° is obtuse; an angle more than 180° but less than 360° is reflex.

**Choices:**

- An angle more than 90° but less than 180° is obtuse; an angle more than 180° but less than 360° is reflex. ✔
  - _why:_ An obtuse angle lies between 90° and 180°, while a reflex angle lies between 180° and 360°. The endpoints themselves are excluded.
- An angle more than 90° but less than 180° is reflex; an angle more than 180° but less than 360° is obtuse.
  - _why:_ The two classifications have been reversed. Obtuse angles lie between 90° and 180°, while reflex angles lie between 180° and 360°.
- An angle more than 90° but less than 180° is acute; an angle more than 180° but less than 360° is reflex.
  - _why:_ Acute angles are less than 90°, so the first classification is wrong. The correct terms are obtuse for 90° to 180° and reflex for 180° to 360°.
- An angle more than 90° but less than 180° is obtuse; an angle more than 180° but less than 360° is straight.
  - _why:_ A straight angle is exactly 180°, not any angle above it. The first range is obtuse, and the second range is reflex.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Where must the protractor centre be placed when measuring an angle?

**Back:** On the vertex.

**Choices:**

- On the vertex. ✔
  - _why:_ The vertex is the point where the two arms of an angle meet. Aligning the protractor’s centre with it ensures the scale measures from the correct point.
- On the midpoint of one arm.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of an arm is not the angle’s centre and gives a displaced measurement. The protractor centre must be placed on the vertex where the arms meet.
- On the end of one arm.
  - _why:_ Placing the centre at an arm’s endpoint confuses the line segment with the angle’s origin. It must be positioned on the vertex where both arms meet.
- On the longest arm.
  - _why:_ The protractor’s baseline should align with one arm, but its centre should not sit anywhere along that arm. The centre must be exactly on the vertex.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** When measuring with a protractor, which scale should you read?

**Back:** The scale starting from the arm lined up with 0°.

**Choices:**

- The scale starting from the arm lined up with 0°. ✔
  - _why:_ The aligned arm represents the starting direction, so its reading must be 0°. Following that scale to the other arm gives the angle’s correct size.
- The scale starting from the arm lined up with 180°.
  - _why:_ Starting from 180° uses the opposite protractor scale and usually produces the supplementary reading. Read the scale whose 0° is aligned with the starting arm.
- The scale printed around the outer edge.
  - _why:_ The outer scale is not always the correct one; it depends on the angle’s starting arm. Use whichever scale begins at 0° on the aligned arm.
- The scale printed around the inner edge.
  - _why:_ The inner scale is not automatically correct, because either scale may begin at the aligned arm. Choose the scale showing 0° at that arm.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** An angle is clearly acute, but your protractor reading is 140°. What went wrong?

**Back:** You used the wrong scale.

**Choices:**

- You used the wrong scale. ✔
  - _why:_ A 140° reading is obtuse, contradicting the angle’s visibly acute size. The other protractor scale gives the supplementary reading, 40°, which is acute.
- You misplaced the protractor centre.
  - _why:_ A misplaced centre can distort a reading, but the paired protractor scales specifically produce 140° instead of the acute 40°. The wrong scale was used.
- You turned the protractor upside down.
  - _why:_ Reversing the protractor does not cause an error if the correct 0° scale is selected. A 140° reading for an acute angle comes from using the wrong scale.
- You forgot to use a ruler.
  - _why:_ A ruler is unnecessary for reading an existing angle and does not explain the supplementary value of 140°. The error was reading the wrong protractor scale.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** A drawn 110° angle looks tiny. What error is most likely?

**Back:** You used the wrong scale.

**Choices:**

- You used the wrong scale. ✔
  - _why:_ An angle of 110° is obtuse and should look larger than a right angle. Reading the opposite scale gives 70°, producing a visibly smaller angle.
- You used a blunt pencil.
  - _why:_ A blunt pencil may reduce drawing precision slightly, but it would not make 110° appear systematically acute. The likely mistake is using the opposite protractor scale.
- You misplaced the protractor centre.
  - _why:_ Misplacing the centre can distort the construction, but a tiny angle corresponding to 70° is the classic supplementary-reading error. The wrong scale was used.
- You drew the arms too short.
  - _why:_ A short arm can still form a correctly sized 110° angle because angle size depends on direction, not arm length. The wrong scale was used.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles on a straight line add up to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ A straight line represents a half-turn, which measures 180°. Therefore, all adjacent angles lying along one side of that line total 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ A total of 90° describes a right angle, or quarter-turn, rather than a straight line. Angles on a straight line add to 180°.
- 360°
  - _why:_ A total of 360° applies to angles around a point, representing a full turn. A straight line is only a half-turn, so its angles total 180°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ A total of 270° represents three-quarters of a turn, not a straight line. A straight line forms a half-turn measuring 180°.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** A ray splits a straight line into 128° and $x$. Find $x$.

**Back:** 52°

**Choices:**

- 52° ✔
  - _why:_ Angles on a straight line total 180°, so $x=180°-128°=52°$. The two adjacent angles therefore complete the straight line exactly.
- 232°
  - _why:_ This comes from subtracting 128 from 360, treating the diagram as angles around a point. A straight line totals 180°, giving $x=52°$.
- 38°
  - _why:_ This is the difference between 128° and 90°, which incorrectly uses a right angle total. A straight line totals 180°, so $x=52°$.
- 128°
  - _why:_ This treats the two angles as equal rather than supplementary. They must add to 180°, so subtracting gives $x=180°-128°=52°$.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** Perpendicular lines meet at [ ___ ].

**Back:** Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.

**Choices:**

- 90° ✔
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines cross to form four right angles. A right angle measures 90°, so the angle between any two perpendicular lines is 90°.
- 180°
  - _why:_ 180° is the angle on a straight line, not the angle where perpendicular lines meet. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°, forming right angles.
- 45°
  - _why:_ 45° is half a right angle and commonly appears when a right angle is bisected. Perpendicular lines themselves meet at a full right angle of 90°.
- 360°
  - _why:_ 360° represents one complete turn or the total around a point. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°, producing four equal right angles around their intersection.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles around a point add up to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles around a point add up to 360°.

**Choices:**

- 360° ✔
  - _why:_ Angles making a complete turn around one point have a total of 360°. This is true regardless of how many individual angles divide the turn.
- 180°
  - _why:_ 180° is the total for angles on a straight line, which is only half a complete turn. Angles around a point total 360°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ 90° is one quarter-turn and is the size of a right angle. A complete turn around a point contains four quarter-turns, totalling 360°.
- 540°
  - _why:_ 540° is the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon, not the total around one point. A complete turn around a point is 360°.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Angles around a point are 90°, 105°, 118° and $x$. Find $x$.

**Back:** 47°

**Choices:**

- 47° ✔
  - _why:_ Angles around a point total 360°. The known angles sum to $90° + 105° + 118° = 313°$, leaving $360° - 313° = 47°$.
- 57°
  - _why:_ This comes from adding the known angles as 303° rather than 313°. Their correct sum is 313°, so the missing angle is $360° - 313° = 47°$.
- 137°
  - _why:_ This omits the 90° angle when finding the known total. All three given angles must be included, so $x = 360° - 313° = 47°$.
- 53°
  - _why:_ This can result from misreading 118° as 112°, producing an incorrect known total of 307°. Using 118° gives a total of 313° and $x = 47°$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles in a triangle add up to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ The three interior angles of every triangle have a total of 180°. This angle sum applies to scalene, isosceles, equilateral and right-angled triangles.
- 360°
  - _why:_ 360° is the interior-angle sum of a quadrilateral or the total around a point. A triangle has three interior angles whose total is 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ 90° is the size of a right angle, not the total for every triangle. Even a right-angled triangle has two further angles, making 180° altogether.
- 540°
  - _why:_ 540° is the interior-angle sum of a pentagon, which has five sides. A three-sided polygon is a triangle, and its interior angles total 180°.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** In a triangle, angles A and C are 58° and 41°. Find angle B.

**Back:** 81°

**Choices:**

- 81° ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle’s angles total 180°. The two known angles sum to $58° + 41° = 99°$, so angle B is $180° - 99° = 81°$.
- 99°
  - _why:_ This is only the sum of the two known angles, not the missing angle. Subtract that sum from 180° to obtain $180° - 99° = 81°$.
- 79°
  - _why:_ This uses 178° as the triangle’s angle total instead of 180°. The correct calculation is $180° - (58° + 41°) = 81°$.
- 83°
  - _why:_ This follows from incorrectly adding 58° and 41° as 97°. Their actual sum is 99°, leaving $180° - 99° = 81°$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** In an isosceles triangle, equal sides imply which angles are equal?

**Back:** The opposite angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- The opposite angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Equal sides in a triangle have equal angles directly opposite them. Therefore, identifying the two equal sides also identifies the pair of equal opposite angles.
- The angles opposite unequal sides are equal.
  - _why:_ Angles opposite unequal sides are unequal because the longer side faces the larger angle. In an isosceles triangle, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal.
- The top angle and either base angle are equal.
  - _why:_ The top angle is not generally equal to a base angle; that would require an equilateral triangle. The equal angles are those opposite the two equal sides.
- The three interior angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Three equal interior angles describe an equilateral triangle, not a general isosceles triangle. An isosceles triangle guarantees equality only between the angles opposite its equal sides.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** An isosceles triangle has one base angle 72°. Find the top angle.

**Back:** 36°

**Choices:**

- 36° ✔
  - _why:_ The two base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, so they total $72° + 72° = 144°$. The top angle is $180° - 144° = 36°$.
- 54°
  - _why:_ This subtracts one 72° angle and then halves the remaining 108°, incorrectly treating the other two angles as equal. Both base angles are 72°, leaving 36°.
- 108°
  - _why:_ This subtracts only one 72° base angle from 180° and overlooks the second equal base angle. Subtracting both gives $180° - 144° = 36°$.
- 72°
  - _why:_ This assumes the top angle equals the base angles, which would make all three angles equal. Only the two base angles match, so the top angle is 36°.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What is each angle in an equilateral triangle?

**Back:** 60°

**Choices:**

- 60° ✔
  - _why:_ An equilateral triangle has three equal interior angles. Since angles in a triangle total 180°, each angle is 180° ÷ 3 = 60°.
- 45°
  - _why:_ This comes from halving a right angle, which is unrelated to the defining properties of an equilateral triangle. Its three equal angles are each 60°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ A right angle may appear in some triangles, but not in an equilateral triangle. Dividing the 180° angle total equally among three angles gives 60°.
- 120°
  - _why:_ This confuses each interior angle with the exterior angle of an equilateral triangle. Each interior angle is 60°, while each corresponding exterior angle is 120°.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What is each angle in a square?

**Back:** 90°

**Choices:**

- 90° ✔
  - _why:_ Every corner of a square is a right angle, so each interior angle measures 90°. Its four angles therefore total 360°.
- 45°
  - _why:_ A square’s diagonal can bisect a corner into two 45° angles, but the complete interior angle at each corner is 90°.
- 60°
  - _why:_ This confuses a square with an equilateral triangle, whose angles are 60°. A square has four right angles, so each angle is 90°.
- 360°
  - _why:_ This is the total of all four interior angles in a square, not the size of each one. Dividing 360° by four gives 90°.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** If two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are [ ___ ], so the triangle is [ ___ ].

**Back:** If two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are equal, so the triangle is isosceles.

**Choices:**

- If two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are equal, so the triangle is isosceles. ✔
  - _why:_ Equal angles in a triangle have equal opposite sides. A triangle with two equal sides is isosceles, which is the converse of the isosceles triangle theorem.
- If two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are equal, so the triangle is equilateral.
  - _why:_ Two equal angles guarantee only that the two opposite sides are equal; they do not force the third side to match. The triangle is isosceles, not necessarily equilateral.
- If two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are parallel, so the triangle is isosceles.
  - _why:_ Equal angles face equal sides, not parallel sides. Those equal sides meet at a vertex, and having two equal sides makes the triangle isosceles.
- If two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are unequal, so the triangle is scalene.
  - _why:_ This reverses the equal-angle relationship: equal angles have equal opposite sides, so the triangle cannot be scalene. It must be isosceles.

**Choices:**

- 58° ✔
  - _why:_ The two base angles are equal and together total 180° - 64° = 116°. Dividing by two gives each base angle as 58°.

  - _why:_ This is the combined size of the two equal base angles, not the size of each one. Dividing 116° by two gives 58° per base angle.

  - _why:_ This assumes all three angles are equal, as in an equilateral triangle. Only the base angles are equal, and each is (180° - 64°) ÷ 2 = 58°.

  - _why:_ This results from treating both base angles as 64° and finding the remaining angle. The given 64° is the top angle, so each base angle is 58°.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** A, B and C lie on a straight line. Angle ABD is 58° and angle DCB is 31°. Find angle BDC.

**Back:** 27°

**Choices:**

- 27° ✔
  - _why:_ Angle ABD is exterior to triangle BCD, so angle DBC is 180° - 58° = 122°. Therefore angle BDC is 180° - 122° - 31° = 27°.
- 91°
  - _why:_ This treats the exterior angle ABD as the triangle’s interior angle at B. The interior angle is 122°, so angle BDC is 180° - 122° - 31° = 27°.
- 89°
  - _why:_ Adding the two stated angles does not find angle BDC, especially because 58° is exterior to the triangle. Using the supplementary interior angle gives 27°.
- 149°
  - _why:_ Subtracting only the 31° angle ignores the triangle’s 122° interior angle at B. Subtracting both interior angles from 180° gives 27°.
