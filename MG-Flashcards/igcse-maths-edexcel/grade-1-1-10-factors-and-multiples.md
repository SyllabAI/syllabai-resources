## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A factor divides into a number [ ___ ].

**Back:** A factor divides into a number exactly, with no remainder.

**Choices:**

- exactly, with no remainder ✔
  - _why:_ A factor divides a number to give a whole-number quotient, leaving no remainder. For example, 5 is a factor of 20 because $20 \div 5 = 4$.
- with a remainder of 1
  - _why:_ A remainder of 1 means the division is not exact, so the divisor is not a factor. A factor divides exactly, with no remainder.
- approximately, after rounding
  - _why:_ Rounding can make a non-whole quotient appear whole, but it does not establish a factor. A factor must divide exactly, with no remainder.
- only when the quotient is odd
  - _why:_ The quotient may be odd or even; its parity is irrelevant. A divisor is a factor whenever it divides exactly, with no remainder.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Back:** A multiple is found by multiplying by a whole number.

**Choices:**

- multiplying by a whole number ✔
  - _why:_ Multiples of a number are produced by multiplying it by whole numbers. For example, $6 \times 5 = 30$, so 30 is a multiple of 6.
- adding it to a whole number
  - _why:_ Adding an arbitrary whole number does not necessarily produce a multiple. Multiples are found by multiplying the original number by a whole number.
- dividing by a whole number
  - _why:_ Dividing usually produces a quotient and is used when testing factors. A multiple is found by multiplying the original number by a whole number.
- subtracting a whole number
  - _why:_ Subtracting an arbitrary whole number does not preserve the times-table pattern. Multiples are obtained by multiplying the original number by a whole number.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is 5 a factor of 20?

**Back:** 20 ÷ 5 = 4 exactly.

**Choices:**

- 20 ÷ 5 = 4 exactly. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing 20 by 5 gives the whole number 4 with no remainder. Therefore, 5 divides 20 exactly and is a factor of 20.
- 20 ÷ 5 = 5 exactly.
  - _why:_ This uses an incorrect quotient: $20 \div 5$ is 4, not 5. Since the result is exactly 4, 5 is a factor of 20.
- 20 ÷ 5 = 4 with remainder.
  - _why:_ Although the quotient 4 is correct, there is no remainder because $5 \times 4 = 20$. This exact division makes 5 a factor of 20.
- 20 ÷ 5 = 0.25 exactly.
  - _why:_ The value 0.25 comes from reversing the division and calculating $5 \div 20$. The required calculation is $20 \div 5 = 4$ exactly.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** In exam questions, “between 25 and 40” usually means [ ___ ] and [ ___ ].

**Back:** In exam questions, “between 25 and 40” usually means bigger than 25 and smaller than 40.

**Choices:**

- In exam questions, “between 25 and 40” usually means bigger than 25 and smaller than 40. ✔
  - _why:_ Without wording such as “inclusive”, between normally excludes both endpoints. Values must therefore satisfy $25 < x < 40$.
- In exam questions, “between 25 and 40” usually means bigger than or equal to 25 and smaller than or equal to 40.
  - _why:_ This interpretation includes both endpoints and corresponds to “from 25 to 40 inclusive”. Usually, “between 25 and 40” requires values bigger than 25 and smaller than 40.
- In exam questions, “between 25 and 40” usually means bigger than or equal to 25 and smaller than 40.
  - _why:_ This includes the lower endpoint but excludes the upper one, creating a one-sided interpretation. Usually, both endpoints are excluded: $25 < x < 40$.
- In exam questions, “between 25 and 40” usually means bigger than 25 and smaller than or equal to 40.
  - _why:_ This excludes 25 but incorrectly includes 40. Unless “inclusive” is stated, between normally excludes both endpoints, giving $25 < x < 40$.

  - _why:_ Ending in 0 or 5 is the divisibility test for 5. An even number must end in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Back:** A factor pair is two whole numbers that multiply to make the target number.

**Choices:**

- multiply to make the target number ✔
  - _why:_ A factor pair consists of two whole numbers whose product equals the target number. For example, 3 and 8 are a factor pair of 24 because $3 \times 8 = 24$.

  - _why:_ This describes an addition pair rather than a factor pair. The two numbers in a factor pair must multiply, not add, to make the target number.
- divide to make the target number
  - _why:_ Factor pairs are defined using multiplication, even though division can help find them. The two whole numbers must multiply together to produce the target number.
- subtract to make the target number
  - _why:_ This uses subtraction instead of the operation that defines factors. A factor pair contains two whole numbers that multiply to make the target number.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are 3 and 8 a factor pair of 24?

**Back:** 3 × 8 = 24.

**Choices:**

- 3 × 8 = 24. ✔
  - _why:_ Two numbers form a factor pair when their product equals the target number. Multiplying 3 by 8 gives 24, so both numbers are factors of 24.
- 3 + 8 = 24.
  - _why:_ Factor pairs are established by multiplication, not addition, and $3 + 8$ equals 11. The correct calculation is $3 \times 8 = 24$.
- 3 × 8 = 11.
  - _why:_ This mistakenly gives the sum of 3 and 8 as their product. Multiplication gives $3 \times 8 = 24$, which proves they are a factor pair.
- 24 ÷ 8 = 8.
  - _why:_ This division calculation is incorrect because $24 \div 8 = 3$. Equivalently, $3 \times 8 = 24$, which shows that 3 and 8 form a factor pair.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** List all factors of 28 in order.

**Back:** 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28.

**Choices:**

- 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28. ✔
  - _why:_ These come from the factor pairs $1 \times 28$, $2 \times 14$, and $4 \times 7$. Each number divides 28 exactly, with no remainder.
- 1, 2, 3, 7, 14, 28.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly includes 3 and misses 4. Since $28 \div 3$ is not a whole number but $28 \div 4 = 7$, the factors are 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, and 28.
- 1, 2, 4, 6, 14, 28.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly includes 6 and omits 7. The factor pair $4 \times 7 = 28$ shows that 7 is a factor, while 6 does not divide 28 exactly.
- 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 28.
  - _why:_ This replaces 14 with 8. Since $2 \times 14 = 28$, 14 is a factor, whereas $28 \div 8$ is not a whole number.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** When listing all factors, when can you stop testing factor pairs?

**Back:** When the factor pairs start repeating.

**Choices:**

- When the factor pairs start repeating. ✔
  - _why:_ Factor pairs eventually reappear in reverse order. Once this happens, continuing to test numbers will only reproduce factors already found, so the list is complete.
- When the first non-factor is found.
  - _why:_ A non-factor does not mean later numbers cannot be factors. For example, 3 is not a factor of 28 but 4 is; stop only when factor pairs start repeating.
- When the first five numbers are tested.
  - _why:_ The number of tests needed depends on the target number, so five tests is not a general rule. Testing can stop when the factor pairs begin to repeat.
- When the two factors differ by 1.
  - _why:_ Many numbers have no factor pair whose members differ by 1, so this condition may never occur. The reliable stopping point is when factor pairs start repeating.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Back:** A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two factors.

**Choices:**

- two factors ✔
  - _why:_ A prime number has exactly two positive whole-number factors: 1 and the number itself. Having any additional factor would make the number composite rather than prime.
- one factor
  - _why:_ The number 1 has only one positive factor, but it is not prime. A prime number must have exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
- three factors
  - _why:_ A number with three factors is not prime because it has a factor besides 1 and itself. Prime numbers have exactly two positive whole-number factors.
- four factors
  - _why:_ Having four factors makes a number composite, not prime. By definition, a prime number greater than 1 has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

**Choices:**
