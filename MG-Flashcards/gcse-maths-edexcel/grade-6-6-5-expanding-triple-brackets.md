## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between expanding and simplifying an expression?

**Back:** Expanding: Removing brackets by multiplying terms.Simplifying: Combining like terms to shorten the expression.

**Choices:**

- Expanding: Removing brackets by multiplying terms.Simplifying: Combining like terms to shorten the expression. ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding uses multiplication to remove brackets, whereas simplifying collects terms with identical variable parts. These are separate processes, although both may be needed when manipulating one expression.
- Expanding: Combining like terms to shorten the expression.Simplifying: Removing brackets by multiplying terms.
  - _why:_ This reverses the two definitions. Expanding removes brackets through multiplication, while simplifying shortens an expression by collecting like terms after any necessary expansion.
- Expanding: Adding powers when terms are added.Simplifying: Dividing every term by its coefficient.
  - _why:_ Powers are not added when algebraic terms are added, and simplifying does not mean dividing coefficients. Expanding removes brackets; simplifying combines like terms.
- Expanding: Factorising terms into brackets.Simplifying: Combining terms with different powers.
  - _why:_ Factorising is the reverse of expanding, and terms with different powers cannot be combined. Expanding removes brackets, while simplifying collects only like terms.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Terms like $3x^2$ and $-5x^2$ are called [ ___ ] because they have the same variable part (letter and power).

**Back:** Terms like $3x^2$ and $-5x^2$ are called like terms because they have the same variable part (letter and power).

**Choices:**

- like terms ✔
  - _why:_ Both terms have the identical variable part, $x^2$. Their different numerical coefficients do not matter, so they are classified as like terms.
- unlike terms
  - _why:_ Unlike terms have different variable parts, such as $x$ and $x^2$. Since both terms contain exactly $x^2$, the correct classification is like terms.
- common factors
  - _why:_ A common factor is something that divides multiple terms, not a classification based on matching variable parts. Terms containing the same $x^2$ part are like terms.
- equivalent terms
  - _why:_ Equivalent expressions have equal values, but $3x^2$ and $-5x^2$ are not equal in general. They are like terms because their variable parts match.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the result of expanding $2x(x + 4)$?

**Back:** $$2x^2 + 8x$

**Choices:**

- $$2x^2 + 8x$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying $2x$ by each bracket term gives $2x\times x=2x^2$ and $2x\times4=8x$, producing $2x^2+8x$.
- $$2x^2 + 4x$
  - _why:_ This fails to multiply the constant $4$ by the coefficient $2$. Since $2x\times4=8x$, the correct result is $2x^2+8x$.
- $$2x^2 + 8$
  - _why:_ This drops the $x$ when multiplying $2x$ by $4$. The product is $8x$, not $8$, so the expansion is $2x^2+8x$.
- $$2x^2 + 6x$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly adds the coefficients $2$ and $4$ instead of multiplying them. Distributing $2x$ gives $2x^2+8x$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can $3x^2$ and $4x$ not be added together to make $7x^3$?

**Back:** They are unlike terms; terms must have the same power of $x$ to be combined.

**Choices:**

- They are unlike terms; terms must have the same power of $x$ to be combined. ✔
  - _why:_ The variable parts $x^2$ and $x$ have different powers, making them unlike terms. Addition cannot combine them into one algebraic term.
- They are like terms; the coefficients must be multiplied rather than added.
  - _why:_ The terms are not like because their powers of $x$ differ. They therefore remain separate; multiplying the coefficients would not represent the original addition.
- They are unlike terms; the powers should be added to make $7x^3$.
  - _why:_ Although they are unlike terms, powers are added only when multiplying matching bases, not when adding terms. Thus $3x^2+4x$ cannot become $7x^3$.
- They are like terms; terms must have different powers of $x$ to be combined.
  - _why:_ This reverses the rule for like terms. Terms can be collected only when their variable parts, including the powers of $x$, are identical.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In the context of double brackets, what does the FOIL acronym stand for?

**Back:** FirstOutsideInsideLast

**Choices:**

- FirstOutsideInsideLast ✔
  - _why:_ FOIL names the four products formed when multiplying two binomials: the first terms, outside terms, inside terms and last terms.
- FirstOppositeInsideLast
  - _why:_ The O refers to the outside pair of terms, not opposite terms. FOIL correctly stands for First, Outside, Inside and Last.
- FrontOutsideInsideLeft
  - _why:_ Front and left are not FOIL multiplication categories. The four products are identified as First, Outside, Inside and Last.
- FirstOutsideInverseLast
  - _why:_ The I refers to multiplying the two inside terms, not finding an inverse. FOIL stands for First, Outside, Inside and Last.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the three main stages to expand a triple bracket expression?

**Back:** Expand and simplify two brackets first.Multiply that quadratic result by the third bracket.Collect like terms for the final answer.

**Choices:**

- Expand and simplify two brackets first.Multiply that quadratic result by the third bracket.Collect like terms for the final answer. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying two brackets first produces a manageable quadratic. Multiplying this by the remaining bracket and collecting like terms then gives the simplified cubic result.
- Expand and simplify all three brackets separately.Add the three linear results to the original expression.Collect coefficients for the final answer.
  - _why:_ Individual brackets cannot be expanded without multiplication, and triple brackets are multiplied rather than added. First expand two brackets, multiply by the third, then collect like terms.
- Add and simplify two brackets first.Multiply that linear result by the third bracket.Collect powers for the final answer.
  - _why:_ The first two brackets must be multiplied, not added, and their product is quadratic. After multiplying by the third bracket, collect like terms rather than powers.
- Expand and simplify two brackets first.Divide that quadratic result by the third bracket.Collect unlike terms for the final answer.
  - _why:_ The quadratic must be multiplied by the third bracket, not divided by it. Only like terms can then be collected in the final expression.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step when expanding a squared bracket like $(x - 2)^2$?

**Back:** Rewrite it as two identical brackets: $(x - 2)(x - 2)$.

**Choices:**

- Rewrite it as two identical brackets: $(x - 2)(x - 2)$. ✔
  - _why:_ A squared bracket means the entire bracket is multiplied by itself. Writing two identical brackets makes every term, including the middle term, easier to calculate correctly.
- Rewrite it as two opposite brackets: $(x - 2)(x + 2)$.
  - _why:_ Changing the second sign creates a difference of two squares and removes the middle term. Squaring requires two identical brackets: $(x - 2)(x - 2)$.
- Rewrite it by squaring each term: $x^2 + 4$.
  - _why:_ Squaring terms separately omits the cross-products, producing no middle term. The bracket must first be written as $(x - 2)(x - 2)$ before expanding.
- Rewrite it by doubling each term: $2x - 4$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying the terms by two confuses squaring with doubling. A squared bracket means multiplying the bracket by itself, giving $(x - 2)(x - 2)$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Back:** A common mistake is thinking $(x + 5)^2$ equals $x^2 + 25$; this is wrong because the middle term (from the Outside and Inside parts of FOIL) is missing.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ The Outside and Inside products are both $5x$, so together they produce the middle term $10x$. Therefore, $(x+5)^2=x^2+10x+25$.

  - _why:_ The constant term is already present as $25$, obtained from $5\times5$. The missing part is the middle term $10x$, formed by combining the two cross-products.
- leading term
  - _why:_ The leading term is $x^2$, which is already included. The omitted part is the middle term $10x$, produced by the Outside and Inside multiplications.
- squared term
  - _why:_ The squared terms $x^2$ and $25$ are both shown. The mistake is omitting the middle term $10x$, which comes from adding $5x$ and $5x$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** When multiplying three linear brackets (e.g., $(x+a)(x+b)(x+c)$), what is the highest power of $x$ expected in the answer?

**Back:** $$x^3$ (a cubic term)

**Choices:**

- $$x^3$ (a cubic term) ✔
  - _why:_ Selecting $x$ from each of the three linear brackets gives $x\times x\times x=x^3$. Therefore, the expanded expression has degree three.
- $$x^2$ (a quadratic term)
  - _why:_ A quadratic is the highest power produced by only two linear brackets. Three factors of $x$ multiply to $x^3$, so the expected leading term is cubic.
- $$x^4$ (a quartic term)
  - _why:_ A quartic leading term would require four linear factors containing $x$. With only three brackets, multiplying the three $x$ terms gives $x^3$.
- $$x^6$ (a sextic term)
  - _why:_ Adding or multiplying the powers incorrectly can suggest degree six. Each bracket contributes one factor of $x$, so their product is $x^3$, a cubic term.

**Choices:**

- Multiply 3x by the whole quadratic.Multiply -2 by the whole quadratic.Add the results and collect like terms. ✔

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** In what order should the powers of $x$ be written in a final expanded answer?

**Back:** Descending powers (e.g., $x^3$, then $x^2$, then $x$, then the constant).

**Choices:**

- Descending powers (e.g., $x^3$, then $x^2$, then $x$, then the constant). ✔
  - _why:_ Standard polynomial form lists terms from the highest exponent to the lowest. This makes the degree clear and allows like expressions to be compared easily.
- Ascending powers (e.g., the constant, then $x$, then $x^2$, then $x^3$).
  - _why:_ Although ascending order can represent the same expression, it is not the requested standard ordering. Final expanded answers should be written in descending powers of $x$.
- Alternating powers (e.g., $x^3$, then $x$, then $x^2$, then the constant).
  - _why:_ Alternating exponents does not follow standard polynomial order and makes terms harder to check. Use descending powers: $x^3$, $x^2$, $x$, then the constant.
- Coefficient order (e.g., the largest coefficient first, then the next largest, then the smallest).
  - _why:_ Polynomial terms are ordered by their exponents, not by the sizes of their coefficients. The highest power of $x$ should appear first, followed by lower powers.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** What happens to the $x^2$ term when expanding $(x + 4)(x - 2)(x - 2)$ if the quadratic part is $x^2 - 4x + 4$?

**Back:** You multiply $x$ by $-4x$ and $4$ by $x^2$, then combine them (e.g., $-4x^2 + 4x^2 = 0$).

**Choices:**

- You multiply $x$ by $-4x$ and $4$ by $x^2$, then combine them (e.g., $-4x^2 + 4x^2 = 0$). ✔
  - _why:_ Both products are $x^2$ terms with opposite coefficients. They are like terms, so $-4x^2+4x^2$ cancels to zero and no $x^2$ term remains.
- You multiply $x$ by $-4x$ and $4$ by $x^2$, then add their coefficients (e.g., $-4x^2 + 4x^2 = 8x^2$).
  - _why:_ Adding the magnitudes while ignoring the negative sign causes the error. The coefficients are $-4$ and $4$, whose sum is zero, so the $x^2$ terms cancel.
- You multiply $x$ by $x^2$ and $4$ by $4$, then combine them (e.g., $x^3 + 16 = 17x^3$).
  - _why:_ The terms $x^3$ and $16$ are not like terms and cannot be combined. The $x^2$ coefficient instead comes from $x(-4x)$ and $4(x^2)$, which cancel.
- You multiply $x$ by $-4x$ and $4$ by $x^2$, then multiply them (e.g., $-4x^2 \times 4x^2 = -16x^4$).
  - _why:_ These products arise as separate terms in a sum, so they must be added rather than multiplied together. Their opposite coefficients cancel, leaving no $x^2$ term.
