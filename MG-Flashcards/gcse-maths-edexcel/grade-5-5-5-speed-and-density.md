## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In a rate like miles per hour, what operation does per signal?

**Back:** Divide: miles ÷ hours.

**Choices:**

- Divide: miles ÷ hours. ✔
  - _why:_ “Per” means “for each”, so miles per hour is calculated by dividing the number of miles by the number of hours.
- Multiply: miles × hours.
  - _why:_ Multiplication combines the two quantities rather than forming a rate. “Per” signals division, so miles must be divided by hours.
- Subtract: miles − hours.
  - _why:_ Subtracting unlike quantities does not produce a meaningful rate. “Per” means divide, so miles per hour is miles divided by hours.
- Add: miles + hours.
  - _why:_ Adding distance and time cannot give a rate because they have different units. “Per” signals division, giving miles divided by hours.

**Back:** Average speed = distance ÷ time.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ Average speed measures the distance covered per unit of time, so total distance is divided by the total time taken.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** For $a=\frac{b}{c}$, the top quantity is $b=$ [ ___ ].

**Back:** For $a=\frac{b}{c}$, the top quantity is $b=$ a × c.

**Choices:**

- a × c ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying both sides of $a=\frac{b}{c}$ by $c$ cancels the denominator, giving $a × c=b$. Therefore the top quantity is found using $a × c$.
- a ÷ c
  - _why:_ Dividing $a$ by $c$ does not reverse the original division. Multiplying both sides by $c$ gives $b=a × c$, so the top quantity is $a × c$.
- c ÷ a
  - _why:_ Dividing $c$ by $a$ reverses the quantities without correctly rearranging the equation. Since $ac=b$, the required expression for the top quantity is $a × c$.
- a + c
  - _why:_ Adding the two quantities is not a valid way to undo division by $c$. Multiply both sides by $c$, which gives the correct expression $a × c$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** For $a=\frac{b}{c}$, how do you find the bottom quantity $c$?

**Back:** $c=\frac{b}{a}$.

**Choices:**

- $c=\frac{b}{a}$. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying $a=\frac{b}{c}$ by $c$ gives $ac=b$. Dividing by $a$ then isolates the bottom quantity, so $c=\frac{b}{a}$.
- $c=\frac{a}{b}$.
  - _why:_ Reversing the fraction without following algebraic steps swaps the required numerator and denominator. From $ac=b$, dividing by $a$ gives $c=\frac{b}{a}$.
- $c=a × b$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying $a$ and $b$ does not isolate the denominator in the original fraction. Rearranging $ac=b$ requires division by $a$, giving $c=\frac{b}{a}$.
- $c=b-a$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting $a$ from $b$ treats the fractional relationship as an additive one. The equation rearranges through multiplication and division, giving $c=\frac{b}{a}$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** For speed in mph, time must be in [ ___ ]; for m/s, time must be in [ ___ ].

**Back:** For speed in mph, time must be in hours; for m/s, time must be in seconds.

**Choices:**

- For speed in mph, time must be in hours; for m/s, time must be in seconds. ✔
  - _why:_ Miles per hour uses hours as its time unit, while metres per second uses seconds. Matching the time units prevents a factor-of-60 or factor-of-3600 error.
- For speed in mph, time must be in minutes; for m/s, time must be in seconds.
  - _why:_ Minutes do not match the “per hour” unit in mph, although seconds are correct for m/s. Convert the mph time to hours before calculating speed.
- For speed in mph, time must be in hours; for m/s, time must be in minutes.
  - _why:_ Hours are correct for mph, but minutes do not match the “per second” unit in m/s. Time must be measured in seconds for an m/s calculation.
- For speed in mph, time must be in seconds; for m/s, time must be in hours.
  - _why:_ The time units have been reversed. Mph requires time in hours because it means miles per hour, while m/s requires time in seconds.
