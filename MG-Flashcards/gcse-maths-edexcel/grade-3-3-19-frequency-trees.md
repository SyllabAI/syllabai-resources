## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In a frequency tree, what is a frequency?

**Back:** The count of items or people in a category.

**Choices:**

- The count of items or people in a category. ✔
  - _why:_ Frequency records how many items or people belong to a category. Each number shown at a node is therefore a count, rather than a fraction or percentage.
- The percentage of items or people in a category.
  - _why:_ A percentage describes a proportion out of 100, not the frequency itself. The frequency is the count of items or people belonging to the category.
- The probability of items or people being in a category.
  - _why:_ Probability measures how likely an outcome is, whereas a frequency tree displays actual counts. The frequency is the count of items or people in the category.
- The difference between the numbers in two categories.
  - _why:_ Comparing two category totals may produce a difference, but that is not a frequency. A frequency is the count of items or people within one category.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** How is a missing child node calculated if the parent total and one child are known?

**Back:** Subtract the known child from the parent total.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the known child from the parent total. ✔
  - _why:_ The two child frequencies must add to the parent total. Removing the known child frequency from that total leaves the frequency of the missing child node.
- Add the known child to the parent total.
  - _why:_ Adding the known child counts it again and produces a value larger than the remaining group. Subtract the known child from the parent total to find the missing child.
- Divide the parent total by the known child.
  - _why:_ Division finds a ratio, not the remaining frequency. Since the child nodes combine to make the parent total, subtract the known child from the parent total.
- Multiply the known child by the parent total.
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not separate the parent total into its two child groups and gives an unrealistically large result. The missing child is found using subtraction.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In a frequency tree, what does a branch represent?

**Back:** A category or choice that splits a total into groups.

**Choices:**

- A category or choice that splits a total into groups. ✔
  - _why:_ Each branch labels or represents one possible category or choice. Following the branches shows how the frequency at a parent node is separated into smaller groups.
- A total that combines all previous groups into one.
  - _why:_ A total is written at a node, whereas a branch shows how that total is divided. It represents a category or choice that splits the total into groups.
- A frequency showing the number in the whole sample.
  - _why:_ A frequency is a numerical count displayed at a node, not the connecting branch. A branch represents a category or choice used to split a total.
- A calculation that changes a frequency into a percentage.
  - _why:_ Frequency-tree branches organise counts rather than convert them into percentages. A branch represents a category or choice that divides a total into separate groups.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a frequency tree, the [ ___ ] must equal the sum of its connected child nodes.

**Back:** In a frequency tree, the parent node must equal the sum of its connected child nodes.

**Choices:**

- parent node ✔
  - _why:_ A parent node gives the total before the next split. Its connected child nodes divide that total into categories, so their frequencies must add back to the parent.
- child node
  - _why:_ A child node is only one of the smaller groups and does not normally equal the sum of its siblings. The parent node equals the sum of its connected child nodes.
- branch label
  - _why:_ A branch label names a category and is not a numerical total that can equal a sum. The parent node must equal the sum of its connected child nodes.
- final category
  - _why:_ A final category is one part of the split, not the combined total of the connected groups. The parent node is the value equal to the children’s sum.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a frequency tree, the parent node must equal the sum of its connected [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a frequency tree, the parent node must equal the sum of its connected child nodes.

**Choices:**

- child nodes ✔
  - _why:_ Child nodes show the frequencies after a parent total has been split into categories. Adding all connected child-node frequencies must reproduce the value at the parent node.
- parent nodes
  - _why:_ Parent nodes contain the totals being split, so they are not the smaller connected values being added. A parent node equals the sum of its connected child nodes.
- branch labels
  - _why:_ Branch labels name the categories and are not numerical frequencies that can be added. The values summed to reproduce the parent total are those at the connected child nodes.
- starting totals
  - _why:_ Starting totals occur earlier in the tree and are not the groups directly produced by a split. The parent node equals the sum of its connected child nodes.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** If 62 people are surveyed and 27 are boys, how many are girls?

**Back:** 35 (62 - 27 = 35).

**Choices:**

- 35 (62 - 27 = 35). ✔
  - _why:_ The boys and girls together make the total of 62 people. Subtracting the 27 boys leaves 35 people, so 35 of those surveyed are girls.
- 89 (62 + 27 = 89).
  - _why:_ Adding gives the combined value of 62 and 27 rather than the number remaining within the surveyed total. Subtracting the 27 boys from 62 gives 35 girls.
- 34 (62 - 27 = 34).
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one subtraction error. Correctly subtracting 27 from 62 gives 35, so there are 35 girls in the surveyed group.
- 45 (62 - 27 = 45).
  - _why:_ This results from an incorrect subtraction in the units and tens columns. Borrowing correctly gives 12 - 7 = 5 and 5 - 2 = 3, making 35.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Frequency trees are generally read from [ ___ ] to right.

**Back:** Frequency trees are generally read from left to right.

**Choices:**

- left ✔
  - _why:_ The initial total is generally placed on the left, with branches extending towards later categories on the right. The tree is therefore read from left to right.
- right
  - _why:_ Starting on the right would reverse the usual structure and make the sentence imply no movement across the tree. Frequency trees are generally read from left to right.
- top
  - _why:_ Some diagrams can be arranged vertically, but the stated standard orientation is horizontal. Frequency trees are generally followed from the starting total on the left towards the right.
- bottom
  - _why:_ Bottom-to-right does not describe the usual direction of a frequency tree. The starting total is generally on the left, so the diagram is read from left to right.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Frequency trees are generally read from left to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Frequency trees are generally read from left to right.

**Choices:**

- right ✔
  - _why:_ Branches generally extend from an initial total on the left towards increasingly specific groups on the right. Following this structure means reading the frequency tree from left to right.
- left
  - _why:_ Reading from left to left would not move through the branches towards the later groups. Frequency trees usually begin with the total on the left and continue to the right.
- top
  - _why:_ The top may contain one category, but it is not the general reading direction stated here. Frequency trees are normally followed horizontally from left to right.
- bottom
  - _why:_ The lower branch represents one possible category rather than the overall reading direction. Frequency trees are generally read from the initial total on the left to the right.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Where should an overall total (e.g. "52 students chose drink A") be placed in a tree split by Gender then Drink?

**Back:** It is the sum of both "Drink A" nodes (not a single node).

**Choices:**

- It is the sum of both "Drink A" nodes (not a single node). ✔
  - _why:_ Because the tree first separates students by gender, Drink A appears once under each gender. The overall Drink A total is found by adding those two frequencies.
- It is the sum of both "Drink B" nodes (not a single node).
  - _why:_ This selects the branches for the wrong drink. The overall Drink A total is the sum of the two Drink A nodes, one under each gender.
- It is placed in the first "Drink A" node (not split between nodes).
  - _why:_ This treats one gender branch as though it represents everyone. The total must be split between genders, so the two Drink A nodes must sum to 52.
- It is the difference between both "Drink A" nodes (not their sum).
  - _why:_ Subtracting would compare the Drink A frequencies for the two genders rather than combine them. The overall total is found by adding both Drink A nodes.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** To calculate a frequency from a fraction clue, use the formula: $$part = \text{group total} \times \text{[...]}$$

**Back:** To calculate a frequency from a fraction clue, use the formula: $$part = \text{group total} \times \boxed{fraction}$$

**Choices:**

- fraction ✔
  - _why:_ A fractional part of a group is calculated by multiplying the group total by the given fraction, such as $40 \times \frac{3}{5}$.
- denominator
  - _why:_ Using only the denominator ignores the numerator and therefore the required number of equal parts. Multiply the group total by the complete fraction.
- numerator
  - _why:_ Multiplying by only the numerator omits the division represented by the denominator. The correct multiplier is the complete fraction given in the clue.
- reciprocal
  - _why:_ Using the reciprocal reverses the fraction and usually produces an excessive value. The group total must be multiplied by the original fraction, not its reciprocal.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** When given a clue like "$\frac{2}{5}$ of the girls", which frequency is used as the total for the calculation?

**Back:** The girls total.

**Choices:**

- The girls total. ✔
  - _why:_ The phrase “of the girls” names the whole group to which the fraction applies, so the girls total must be multiplied by $\frac{2}{5}$.
- The boys total.
  - _why:_ This uses the other gender’s frequency rather than the group named in the clue. The words “of the girls” mean the girls total is required.
- The students total.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly applies the fraction to everyone in the tree. Since the clue specifies “of the girls”, the calculation must use only the girls total.
- The selected branch total.
  - _why:_ This confuses the unknown part with the starting total. The named group is the girls, so their total is used before finding the required branch frequency.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** If 40 students play tennis and $\frac{3}{5}$ go on Saturday, how many go on Saturday?

**Back:** 24 students ($$40 \times \frac{3}{5} = 24$$).

**Choices:**

- 24 students ($$40 \times \frac{3}{5} = 24$$). ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing 40 into five equal parts gives 8 students per fifth, and taking three of those parts gives $3 \times 8 = 24$ students.
- 8 students ($$40 \div 5 = 8$$).
  - _why:_ This finds one fifth of the students but forgets to take three fifths. Multiplying 8 by the numerator 3 gives the correct answer of 24 students.
- 16 students ($$40 \times \frac{2}{5} = 16$$).
  - _why:_ This calculates the complementary fraction $\frac{2}{5}$ who do not go on Saturday. The required $\frac{3}{5}$ of 40 is 24 students.
- 120 students ($$40 \times 3 = 120$$).
  - _why:_ This multiplies by the numerator but ignores division by the denominator. The calculation must use the whole fraction: $40 \times \frac{3}{5} = 24$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Back:** In a fraction clue, the words "of the" identify which group total to use.

**Choices:**

- "of the" ✔
  - _why:_ The words “of the” introduce the group that acts as the whole in a fraction calculation, so the total immediately following them is used.
- "out of"
  - _why:_ The words “out of” usually describe a ratio between a favourable number and a total. In a fraction clue, “of the” identifies the group total to multiply.
- "more than"
  - _why:_ The words “more than” describe an increase or comparison, not the whole group for a fractional calculation. The group total is identified by “of the”.
- "fewer than"
  - _why:_ The words “fewer than” indicate a comparison or subtraction rather than a fractional part of a group. The relevant total follows the words “of the”.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you determine the denominator for a probability question from a tree?

**Back:** It is the frequency of the group being chosen from.

**Choices:**

- It is the frequency of the group being chosen from. ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator counts all possible people or items available for selection. Therefore, it is the total frequency of the group from which the choice is made.
- It is the frequency of the outcome being asked for.
  - _why:_ This confuses the numerator with the denominator. The desired outcome supplies the numerator, while the total group being chosen from supplies the denominator.
- It is the frequency of the largest branch in the tree.
  - _why:_ Branch size does not determine a probability denominator. The denominator must count every possible selection within the group specified in the question.
- It is the frequency of the whole sample in every question.
  - _why:_ Using the whole sample ignores conditional wording that restricts the choice to a subgroup. The denominator is the frequency of the group actually being chosen from.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** If the question says "One of the left-handed people is chosen", what is the denominator?

**Back:** The total frequency of left-handed people.

**Choices:**

- The total frequency of left-handed people. ✔
  - _why:_ The choice is restricted to left-handed people, so every left-handed person is a possible selection. Their total frequency therefore forms the denominator.
- The total frequency of all people.
  - _why:_ This ignores the restriction to left-handed people and makes the sample space too large. The denominator is only the total frequency of left-handed people.
- The total frequency of right-handed people.
  - _why:_ This uses the opposite group from the one named in the question. Since the person is chosen from left-handed people, their total is the denominator.
- The wanted frequency of left-handed people.
  - _why:_ This confuses the favourable outcome with all possible choices. The wanted left-handed subgroup forms the numerator, while all left-handed people form the denominator.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** Probability from frequencies is calculated as: $$\frac{\text{[...]}}{\text{number possible}}$$

**Back:** Probability from frequencies is calculated as: $$\frac{\boxed{\text{number wanted}}}{\text{number possible}}$$

**Choices:**

- $$\text{number wanted}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator counts the outcomes that satisfy the condition being asked about, while the denominator counts all outcomes possible within the stated group.
- $$\text{number possible}$
  - _why:_ Putting the number possible in both positions would incorrectly give a probability of 1. The numerator must be the number of outcomes wanted.
- $$\text{group total}$
  - _why:_ The group total belongs in the denominator because it counts all possible selections. The numerator must count only those selections matching the required outcome.
- $$\text{number unwanted}$
  - _why:_ This calculates the probability of the complementary outcome rather than the event requested. The numerator must be the number wanted, with the number possible below it.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** Probability from frequencies is calculated as: $$\frac{\text{number wanted}}{\text{[...]}}$$

**Back:** Probability from frequencies is calculated as: $$\frac{\text{number wanted}}{\boxed{\text{number possible}}}$$

**Choices:**

- $$\text{number possible}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A probability from frequencies is the number of wanted outcomes divided by the total number of possible outcomes in the group from which the selection is made.
- $$\text{number wanted}$
  - _why:_ Repeating the wanted frequency in the denominator would always produce a probability of 1. The denominator must be the number possible in the relevant group.
- $$\text{number unwanted}$
  - _why:_ The unwanted frequency helps calculate the complementary probability, but it is not the denominator. Divide the number wanted by the total number possible.
- $$\text{number remaining}$
  - _why:_ The number remaining is not necessarily the full sample space and may exclude wanted outcomes. The denominator must include every possible outcome in the relevant group.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A tree shows 12 female left-handers out of 30 left-handers. What is the probability that a left-hander chosen at random is female?

**Back:** $$$\frac{12}{30}$$ (or $\frac{2}{5}$)

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{12}{30}$$ (or $\frac{2}{5}$) ✔
  - _why:_ The selection is restricted to left-handers, so the denominator is all 30 left-handers. Of these, 12 are female, giving $\frac{12}{30}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $$$\frac{12}{70}$$ (or $\frac{6}{35}$)
  - _why:_ Using 70 treats the selection as being made from all people. The person is chosen from the 30 left-handers, so the probability is $\frac{12}{30}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $$$\frac{30}{70}$$ (or $\frac{3}{7}$)
  - _why:_ This calculates the proportion of all people who are left-handed, not the proportion of left-handers who are female. The required probability is $\frac{12}{30}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $$$\frac{18}{30}$$ (or $\frac{3}{5}$)
  - _why:_ This uses the number of male left-handers, giving the complementary probability. Female left-handers account for 12 of the 30 left-handers, so the answer is $\frac{12}{30}$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A tree shows 12 female left-handers out of 70 total people. What is the probability that a person chosen at random is a female left-hander?

**Back:** $$$\frac{12}{70}$$ (or $\frac{6}{35}$)

**Choices:**

- $$$\frac{12}{70}$$ (or $\frac{6}{35}$) ✔
  - _why:_ The person is chosen from all 70 people, so 70 is the denominator. There are 12 female left-handers, giving $\frac{12}{70}=\frac{6}{35}$.
- $$$\frac{12}{30}$$ (or $\frac{2}{5}$)
  - _why:_ Using 30 would be correct only if the person were chosen from the left-handers. Here the selection is from all 70 people, so the answer is $\frac{12}{70}$.
- $$$\frac{30}{70}$$ (or $\frac{3}{7}$)
  - _why:_ This gives the probability that a randomly chosen person is left-handed, including both females and males. Only 12 are female left-handers, so the required probability is $\frac{12}{70}$.
- $$$\frac{58}{70}$$ (or $\frac{29}{35}$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting 12 from 70 counts everyone who is not a female left-hander, producing the complementary probability. The required event has probability $\frac{12}{70}=\frac{6}{35}$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the exam technique for identifying the denominator for a probability question?

**Back:** Circle the group being chosen from on the tree.

**Choices:**

- Circle the group being chosen from on the tree. ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator is the total frequency of the group from which the selection is made. Circling that group helps distinguish it from the smaller group representing the wanted outcome.
- Circle the group matching the wanted outcome on the tree.
  - _why:_ The wanted outcome supplies the numerator rather than the denominator. To identify the denominator, circle the entire group from which the person or item is being chosen.
- Circle the final branch mentioned in the question.
  - _why:_ The final branch may identify the wanted outcome, but it does not necessarily represent every possible selection. Circle the group being chosen from to obtain the denominator.
- Circle the grand total at the start of the tree.
  - _why:_ The grand total is the denominator only when selection is from the entire population. For a conditional question, circle the particular group being chosen from instead.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a person is chosen from a particular group, the probability is called a [ ___ ].

**Back:** When a person is chosen from a particular group, the probability is called a conditional probability.

**Choices:**

- conditional probability ✔
  - _why:_ A conditional probability measures the chance of an event when the possible outcomes have been restricted to a specified group or when another condition is already known.
- complementary probability
  - _why:_ A complementary probability is the chance that an event does not happen. Choosing from a specified group instead restricts the sample space, producing a conditional probability.
- experimental probability
  - _why:_ Experimental probability is estimated using observed results or frequencies. The key feature here is that selection is restricted to a particular group, making it a conditional probability.
- theoretical probability
  - _why:_ Theoretical probability is calculated from equally likely outcomes rather than observed data. Restricting the choice to a particular group describes a conditional probability.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the first node on the far left of a frequency tree typically contain?

**Back:** The grand total.

**Choices:**

- The grand total. ✔
  - _why:_ The far-left node represents the complete population before it is divided into categories. Its frequency is therefore the grand total for the whole tree.
- The first branch total.
  - _why:_ A first branch total appears after the initial split and represents only one category. The far-left node comes before any split and contains the grand total.
- The final category frequency.
  - _why:_ Final category frequencies appear at the ends of the tree after all divisions. The first node instead contains the grand total before categories are separated.
- The first outcome probability.
  - _why:_ A frequency tree records numbers of people or items rather than probabilities at its nodes. The far-left node typically shows the grand total for the population.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you verify a frequency tree is completed correctly?

**Back:** Ensure branches add up to their parent nodesEnsure end nodes sum to the grand total

**Choices:**

- Ensure branches add up to their parent nodesEnsure end nodes sum to the grand total ✔
  - _why:_ Each parent frequency must be divided completely between its branches, so the child nodes add to the parent. All final categories must also account for the grand total.
- Ensure branches multiply to their parent nodesEnsure end nodes sum to the grand total
  - _why:_ Multiplication is associated with some probability-tree calculations, not frequency-tree checks. Child branch frequencies must add to their parent, while all end nodes sum to the grand total.
- Ensure branches add up to their parent nodesEnsure end nodes equal the grand total
  - _why:_ Individual end nodes represent separate final categories and should not each equal the whole population. They must collectively sum to the grand total, while branches add to parents.
- Ensure branches subtract from their parent nodesEnsure end nodes sum to the first branch
  - _why:_ Subtraction can find a missing branch, but the completed child frequencies must add to their parent. All end nodes across the tree must sum to the grand total.
