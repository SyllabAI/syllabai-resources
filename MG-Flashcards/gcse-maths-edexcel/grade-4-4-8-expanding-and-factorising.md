## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** An expression such as $4x+7$ has [ ___ ].

**Back:** An expression such as $4x+7$ has no equals sign.

**Choices:**

- no equals sign ✔
  - _why:_ Expressions contain numbers, variables and operations but do not assert equality, so $4x+7$ has no equals sign.
- an equals sign
  - _why:_ An equals sign would turn the expression into an equation, such as $4x+7=15$. The given expression has no equals sign.
- an inequality sign
  - _why:_ An inequality sign would create an inequality, such as $4x+7>15$. The given algebraic expression contains no equals sign.
- a fixed solution
  - _why:_ A fixed solution belongs to an equation or inequality after a variable is solved. The expression $4x+7$ has no equals sign.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Like terms have the same [ ___ ].

**Back:** Like terms have the same variable part.

**Choices:**

- variable part ✔
  - _why:_ Terms are like terms when their variables and corresponding powers match exactly, so they have the same variable part.
- coefficient
  - _why:_ Like terms may have different coefficients: for example, $3x$ and $8x$ are like terms. They must instead have the same variable part.
- constant term
  - _why:_ A constant term has no variable, whereas many like terms do. Like terms are identified by having the same variable part.
- numerical value
  - _why:_ The numerical values of like terms can differ because their coefficients can differ. Their variable part, including each exponent, must be the same.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you collect like terms?

**Back:** Add or subtract their coefficients.

**Choices:**

- Add or subtract their coefficients. ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms share an identical variable part, so their coefficients can be added or subtracted while that variable part remains unchanged.
- Add or subtract their variables.
  - _why:_ The variables are not added or subtracted separately; they remain unchanged. Collect like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
- Multiply or divide their coefficients.
  - _why:_ Multiplying coefficients finds a product rather than collecting a sum or difference. Like terms are collected by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
- Add or subtract their exponents.
  - _why:_ Exponents remain unchanged when like terms are collected. The correct method is to add or subtract the coefficients of terms with matching variable parts.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $8x+5x-4$.

**Back:** $13x-4$

**Choices:**

- $13x-4$ ✔
  - _why:_ The like terms $8x$ and $5x$ have coefficients that add to $13$, while the constant $-4$ remains unchanged.
- $3x-4$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the coefficients instead of adding them gives $3x$. Since both variable terms are positive, $8x+5x=13x$, giving $13x-4$.
- $9x$
  - _why:_ The constant $-4$ is not a like term with the $x$-terms, so it cannot be added to their coefficient. The result is $13x-4$.
- $13x+4$
  - _why:_ Treating $-4$ as positive changes its sign. Collecting only $8x$ and $5x$ gives $13x$, while the constant remains $-4$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** To expand one bracket, multiply the outside factor by [ ___ ].

**Back:** To expand one bracket, multiply the outside factor by every term inside.

**Choices:**

- every term inside ✔
  - _why:_ The distributive law requires the factor outside a bracket to multiply every term inside, including terms separated by addition or subtraction.
- only the first term
  - _why:_ Multiplying only the first term leaves the bracket only partly expanded. The outside factor must multiply every term inside the bracket.
- only the final term
  - _why:_ Multiplying only the final term omits the other products required by the distributive law. The outside factor must multiply every term inside.
- every term outside
  - _why:_ Terms outside the bracket are not the contents being distributed over. To expand one bracket, the outside factor multiplies every term inside.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand $6(3x+4)$.

**Back:** $18x+24$

**Choices:**

- $18x+24$ ✔
  - _why:_ Distributing $6$ across both terms gives $6\times3x=18x$ and $6\times4=24$, so the expanded expression is $18x+24$.
- $18x+4$
  - _why:_ The constant $4$ has been left unchanged instead of being multiplied by $6$. Distributing to both terms gives $18x+24$.
- $9x+10$
  - _why:_ Adding $6$ to each term is not how brackets are expanded. Multiplying both terms by $6$ gives $18x+24$.
- $18x+10$
  - _why:_ Multiplying $3x$ by $6$ correctly gives $18x$, but $6\times4$ is $24$, not $10$. The correct expansion is $18x+24$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What multiplication is missed in the incorrect expansion $6(3x+4)=18x+4$?

**Back:** Multiplying $6$ by $4$.

**Choices:**

- Multiplying $6$ by $4$. ✔
  - _why:_ The $6$ was distributed to $3x$ but not to the constant $4$. The missing product is $6\times4=24$.
- Multiplying $6$ by $3x$.
  - _why:_ The product $6\times3x$ was included correctly as $18x$. The omitted multiplication is $6$ by $4$, which should produce $24$.
- Multiplying $3x$ by $4$.
  - _why:_ The two terms inside the bracket are added, not multiplied together. Expansion requires multiplying the outside $6$ by $4$ as well as by $3x$.
- Multiplying $18x$ by $4$.
  - _why:_ The term $18x$ is already the result of multiplying $6$ by $3x$ and should not then multiply $4$. The missing product is $6\times4$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand $3p(p-5)$.

**Back:** $3p^2-15p$

**Choices:**

- $3p^2-15p$ ✔
  - _why:_ Distributing $3p$ gives $3p\times p=3p^2$ and $3p\times(-5)=-15p$, producing $3p^2-15p$.
- $3p^2-15$
  - _why:_ The outside factor is $3p$, so multiplying it by $-5$ gives $-15p$, not $-15$. The correct expansion is $3p^2-15p$.
- $3p-15p$
  - _why:_ Multiplying $3p$ by $p$ gives $3p^2$, not $3p$. The second product is $-15p$, so the correct result is $3p^2-15p$.
- $3p^2+15p$
  - _why:_ The negative sign in $p-5$ must be retained when multiplying by $3p$. The second product is $-15p$, giving $3p^2-15p$.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** Factorising is the reverse of [ ___ ].

**Back:** Factorising is the reverse of expanding.

**Choices:**

- expanding ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding removes brackets by multiplying terms, while factorising reverses this process by rewriting an expression as a product containing brackets.
- simplifying
  - _why:_ Simplifying combines or reduces parts of an expression but is not the reverse process. Factorising is the reverse of expanding because it introduces factors and brackets.
- solving
  - _why:_ Solving finds values that satisfy an equation, rather than rewriting an expression as a product. Factorising is specifically the reverse of expanding.
- substituting
  - _why:_ Substituting replaces variables with known values and does not reverse bracket multiplication. Factorising reverses expanding by placing an expression into a product form.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What HCF should be taken out first when factorising $8r+20$?

**Back:** $4$

**Choices:**

- $4$ ✔
  - _why:_ The highest number dividing both $8$ and $20$ is $4$. There are no variables common to both terms, so the HCF is $4$.
- $2$
  - _why:_ Although $2$ divides both coefficients, it is not their highest common factor. The greatest number dividing both $8$ and $20$ is $4$.
- $8$
  - _why:_ Using the first coefficient alone overlooks whether it divides the other coefficient. Since $8$ does not divide $20$, the HCF is $4$.
- $20$
  - _why:_ Using the constant term alone overlooks whether it divides the other coefficient. Since $20$ does not divide $8$, the HCF is $4$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $8r+20$.

**Back:** $4(2r+5)$

**Choices:**

- $4(2r+5)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both terms by their HCF, $4$, gives $8r ÷ 4=2r$ and $20 ÷ 4=5$, producing the stated factorisation.
- $4(2r+4)$
  - _why:_ The constant was incorrectly divided, since expanding this expression gives $8r+16$. Dividing $20$ by $4$ gives $5$, so the correct result is $4(2r+5)$.
- $4(4r+5)$
  - _why:_ The coefficient of $r$ was not divided by the outside factor, so expansion gives $16r+20$. The correct factorisation is $4(2r+5)$.
- $8(r+5)$
  - _why:_ The constant inside the bracket was not divided by $8$, so expansion gives $8r+40$. Taking out the HCF $4$ gives $4(2r+5)$.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** To check a factorised answer, [ ___ ] it.

**Back:** To check a factorised answer, expand it.

**Choices:**

- expand ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying the outside factor by every term inside the brackets reproduces the original expression, so expanding is the appropriate way to check a factorisation.
- factorise
  - _why:_ Factorising again does not directly verify that the brackets reproduce the original expression. The answer should be expanded and compared with the expression given.
- simplify
  - _why:_ Simplifying may tidy an expression, but it does not specifically check the multiplication represented by a factorised form. Expand the brackets to recover the original expression.
- solve
  - _why:_ Solving applies to an equation with an unknown value, not to checking an equivalent expression. Expand the factorised form and compare it with the original.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What full common factor should be taken out of $14a^2b+21ab^2$?

**Back:** $7ab$

**Choices:**

- $7ab$ ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient HCF is $7$, while both terms contain at least one $a$ and one $b$. Therefore, the full common factor is $7ab$.
- $7a$
  - _why:_ This includes the coefficient HCF and the common $a$, but misses the common factor $b$. The full common factor is therefore $7ab$.
- $7b$
  - _why:_ This includes the coefficient HCF and the common $b$, but misses the common factor $a$. Both variables are shared, so the full common factor is $7ab$.
- $14ab$
  - _why:_ The variable part is common, but $14$ is not a factor of $21$. The coefficient HCF is $7$, making the full common factor $7ab$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Fully factorise $14a^2b+21ab^2$.

**Back:** $7ab(2a+3b)$

**Choices:**

- $7ab(2a+3b)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing each term by $7ab$ gives $2a$ and $3b$. Expanding the result reproduces $14a^2b+21ab^2$, confirming the full factorisation.
- $7ab(2a+3ab)$
  - _why:_ An extra $a$ has been left in the second bracket term, so expansion gives $21a^2b^2$ instead of $21ab^2$. The correct result is $7ab(2a+3b)$.
- $7ab(2a^2+3b)$
  - _why:_ The first term retains one too many factors of $a$, producing $14a^3b$ when expanded. Dividing $14a^2b$ by $7ab$ gives $2a$.
- $7ab(2a+3b^2)$
  - _why:_ The second term retains one too many factors of $b$, producing $21ab^3$ when expanded. Dividing $21ab^2$ by $7ab$ gives $3b$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $7(2a^2b+3ab^2)$ not fully factorised?

**Back:** Both $a$ and $b$ can still be taken out.

**Choices:**

- Both $a$ and $b$ can still be taken out. ✔
  - _why:_ Each term inside the brackets contains both $a$ and $b$, so these variables remain common factors. Taking out $7ab$ gives the fully factorised form.
- Both $2$ and $3$ can still be taken out.
  - _why:_ The coefficients $2$ and $3$ have an HCF of $1$, so neither can be taken from both terms. The remaining common factors are $a$ and $b$.
- Both $a^2$ and $b^2$ can still be taken out.
  - _why:_ Each term contains only one guaranteed common factor of each variable, not their squares. Both $a$ and $b$ can still be taken out once.
- Both $14$ and $21$ can still be taken out.
  - _why:_ Neither original coefficient divides both terms: the numerical HCF is only $7$. The incomplete factorisation instead leaves both $a$ and $b$ common inside the brackets.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** After expanding brackets, simplify by collecting [ ___ ].

**Back:** After expanding brackets, simplify by collecting like terms.

**Choices:**

- like terms ✔
  - _why:_ Terms with identical variable parts and powers can be combined by adding or subtracting their coefficients, so like terms should be collected after expansion.
- unlike terms
  - _why:_ Terms with different variable parts or powers cannot be combined by addition or subtraction. After expanding, only like terms should be collected.
- common factors
  - _why:_ Common factors are taken out during factorising rather than collected after expansion. The simplification step combines like terms that have matching variables and powers.
- numerical terms
  - _why:_ Limiting collection to constants would leave matching algebraic terms unsimplified. All like terms, including terms with identical variable parts, should be collected.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $5(s+3)+2(s-4)$.

**Back:** $7s+7$

**Choices:**

- $7s+7$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding gives $5s+15+2s-8$. Collecting like terms produces $7s$, while the constants combine to give $15-8=7$.
- $7s+23$
  - _why:_ Adding $8$ instead of subtracting it ignores the negative term from $2(s-4)$. The constants are $15-8=7$, so the correct result is $7s+7$.
- $7s-7$
  - _why:_ The constant terms have been combined in the wrong order. Expanding gives constants of $15$ and $-8$, which total $7$, so the correct result is $7s+7$.
- $10s+7$
  - _why:_ The coefficients should be added after expansion, not multiplied. Since $5s+2s=7s$ and $15-8=7$, the correct simplified expression is $7s+7$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand $-3(y+5)$.

**Back:** $-3y-15$

**Choices:**

- $-3y-15$ ✔
  - _why:_ The multiplier $-3$ applies to both terms inside the bracket. Therefore $-3\times y=-3y$ and $-3\times5=-15$, giving $-3y-15$.
- $-3y+15$
  - _why:_ A negative multiplied by a positive remains negative, so the constant term cannot be $+15$. Distributing $-3$ correctly gives $-3y-15$.
- $3y-15$
  - _why:_ The negative multiplier must also make the $y$ term negative. Multiplying both terms in the bracket by $-3$ gives the correct expansion $-3y-15$.
- $-3y-5$
  - _why:_ The $5$ has not been multiplied by $-3$. Every term inside the bracket must be multiplied, so the constant becomes $-15$ and the answer is $-3y-15$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $4(2y-3)-3(y+5)$.

**Back:** $5y-27$

**Choices:**

- $5y-27$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding gives $8y-12-3y-15$. Collecting the variable terms and constants separately gives $8y-3y=5y$ and $-12-15=-27$.
- $5y+3$
  - _why:_ Treating $-3\times5$ as positive loses the negative multiplier outside the second bracket. It produces $-15$, so the correct simplified result is $5y-27$.
- $11y-27$
  - _why:_ The term $-3y$ must be subtracted from $8y$, not added to it. Thus the coefficient is $8-3=5$, giving $5y-27$.
- $5y-17$
  - _why:_ The constant $5$ has effectively been subtracted without being multiplied by $3$. The second bracket contributes $-15$, so the constants total $-27$ and the answer is $5y-27$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If a whole bracket is multiplied by a number in an equation, what is usually quicker to do first?

**Back:** Divide both sides by the outside number.

**Choices:**

- Divide both sides by the outside number. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by the outside multiplier preserves the equation and removes that multiplier immediately, leaving the bracketed expression ready to solve.
- Subtract the outside number from both sides.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the multiplier does not undo multiplication. The inverse operation is division, so both sides should be divided by the outside number first.
- Divide one side by the outside number.
  - _why:_ Changing only one side breaks the equality of the equation. To cancel the multiplier correctly, divide both sides by the outside number.
- Multiply both sides by the outside number.
  - _why:_ Multiplying again increases the coefficient instead of cancelling it. Division is the inverse of multiplication, so divide both sides by the outside number.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $6(d+2)=48$.

**Back:** $d=6$

**Choices:**

- $d=6$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by $6$ gives $d+2=8$. Subtracting $2$ from both sides then gives the solution $d=6$.
- $d=8$
  - _why:_ This stops after dividing $48$ by $6$, leaving $d+2=8$ rather than isolating $d$. Subtracting $2$ gives the correct solution $d=6$.
- $d=10$
  - _why:_ Adding $2$ reverses the required final operation. After division gives $d+2=8$, subtracting $2$, rather than adding it, produces $d=6$.
- $d=46$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $2$ directly from $48$ ignores that the whole bracket is multiplied by $6$. Divide by $6$ first, then subtract $2$ to obtain $d=6$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $2(v-6)=15$.

**Back:** $v=13.5$

**Choices:**

- $v=13.5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by $2$ gives $v-6=7.5$. Adding $6$ to both sides then produces the solution $v=13.5$.
- $v=1.5$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $6$ after dividing uses the wrong inverse operation. Since the equation becomes $v-6=7.5$, adding $6$ gives the correct answer $v=13.5$.
- $v=10.5$
  - _why:_ This comes from expanding as $2v-6$ instead of $2v-12$. The multiplier applies to both bracket terms, and solving correctly gives $v=13.5$.
- $v=21$
  - _why:_ Adding $6$ to $15$ but failing to divide by the outside multiplier does not isolate the bracket. Divide by $2$ first, then add $6$ to get $v=13.5$.
