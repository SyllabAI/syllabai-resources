## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In algebra, what is a variable?

**Back:** A letter that stands for an unknown or changing number.

**Choices:**

- A letter that stands for an unknown or changing number. ✔
  - _why:_ A variable represents a value that is unknown or can change. Letters such as $x$, $n$, and $p$ are commonly used as variables.
- A number that has the same value in every calculation.
  - _why:_ A value that stays fixed is a constant, not a variable. A variable is a letter that represents an unknown or changing number.
- A letter that tells you which operation to perform.
  - _why:_ Operation symbols such as $+$ and $-$ indicate what calculation to perform. A variable is a letter standing for an unknown or changing number.
- A number written directly in front of a letter.
  - _why:_ A number multiplying a variable is called a coefficient. The variable itself is the letter representing an unknown or changing number.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A mathematical phrase made from numbers, variables, and operations, but with no equals sign, is called an [ ___ ].

**Back:** A mathematical phrase made from numbers, variables, and operations, but with no equals sign, is called an expression.

**Choices:**

- expression ✔
  - _why:_ An expression combines numbers, variables, and operations without stating that two quantities are equal. For example, $3x + 5$ is an expression.
- equation
  - _why:_ An equation includes an equals sign connecting two equal expressions. A mathematical phrase with operations and variables but no equals sign is an expression.
- inequality
  - _why:_ An inequality compares quantities using a sign such as $$. With no comparison or equals sign, the mathematical phrase is an expression.
- coefficient
  - _why:_ A coefficient is a number multiplying a variable, such as $4$ in $4x$. The whole mathematical phrase without an equals sign is an expression.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a question mean when it asks for an answer in terms of $t$?

**Back:** The final answer must use the letter $t$.

**Choices:**

- The final answer must use the letter $t$. ✔
  - _why:_ An answer in terms of $t$ must express the required quantity using $t$. The letter remains in the final algebraic expression rather than being evaluated.
- The final answer must replace the letter $t$.
  - _why:_ Replacing $t$ would require a numerical value, which has not necessarily been supplied. In terms of $t$ means the final answer must still use $t$.
- The final answer must equal the letter $t$.
  - _why:_ The required quantity does not have to equal $t$; it may be an expression such as $2t+3$. The final answer simply needs to use $t$.
- The final answer must define the letter $t$.
  - _why:_ The instruction is not asking for a definition of the variable. It requires the answer to be written as an expression that uses $t$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the standard algebraic notation for half of price $p$?

**Back:** $$\frac{p}{2}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{p}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Finding half means dividing by $2$. Therefore, half of a price represented by $p$ is written as $\frac{p}{2}$.
- $$\frac{p}{4}$
  - _why:_ Dividing by $4$ finds one quarter, not one half. Half of the price is found by dividing $p$ by $2$, giving $\frac{p}{2}$.
- $$2p$
  - _why:_ Multiplying by $2$ doubles the price rather than halves it. Half of $p$ is found by dividing by $2$, so it is $\frac{p}{2}$.
- $$p - 2$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $2$ reduces the price by a fixed amount rather than finding half. Half of any value $p$ is $\frac{p}{2}$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Words like more than or longer than usually indicate the operation of [ ___ ].

**Back:** Words like more than or longer than usually indicate the operation of addition.

**Choices:**

- addition ✔
  - _why:_ Phrases such as “more than” and “longer than” describe an increase from an original amount, so they usually translate into addition.
- subtraction
  - _why:_ Subtraction is usually indicated by phrases such as “less than” or “shorter than”. “More than” and “longer than” indicate an increase, so addition is used.
- multiplication
  - _why:_ Multiplication is indicated by wording such as “times as many” or “double”. “More than” and “longer than” normally mean that an amount is added.
- division
  - _why:_ Division is indicated by phrases such as “shared equally” or “half of”. “More than” and “longer than” describe an increase and therefore indicate addition.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** Words like fewer than or gives away usually indicate the operation of [ ___ ].

**Back:** Words like fewer than or gives away usually indicate the operation of subtraction.

**Choices:**

- subtraction ✔
  - _why:_ “Fewer than” describes a smaller amount, while “gives away” means an amount is removed. Both phrases therefore usually translate into subtraction.
- addition
  - _why:_ Addition would increase the original amount, which conflicts with having fewer items or giving some away. These phrases indicate subtraction because an amount is removed.
- multiplication
  - _why:_ Multiplication applies to repeated groups or phrases such as “times as many”. Having fewer items or giving items away means subtracting from the original amount.
- division
  - _why:_ Division involves sharing or splitting an amount into equal parts. “Fewer than” and “gives away” instead describe removing an amount, so they indicate subtraction.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Write an expression for "13 years younger than $n$".

**Back:** $$n - 13$

**Choices:**

- $$n - 13$ ✔
  - _why:_ “13 years younger than $n$” means starting with the age $n$ and reducing it by $13$. This gives the expression $n-13$.
- $$n + 13$
  - _why:_ Adding $13$ gives an age that is older than $n$, not younger. A person $13$ years younger than $n$ has age $n-13$.
- $$13 - n$
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the subtraction and can produce a negative age. Start with the original age $n$ and subtract $13$, giving $n-13$.
- $$13n$
  - _why:_ Writing two quantities together means multiplication, so this represents thirteen times the age. “13 years younger” requires subtracting $13$ from $n$, giving $n-13$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is "13 years younger than $n$" written as $n - 13$ rather than $13 - n$?

**Back:** You must start with the original age and then take the 13 years away.

**Choices:**

- You must start with the original age and then take the 13 years away. ✔
  - _why:_ The age $n$ is the starting quantity, and “13 years younger” means subtracting $13$ from it. Subtraction order matters, so the expression is $n-13$.
- You must start with the difference in ages and then take the original age away.
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction by treating $13$ as the starting quantity. The original age is $n$, so taking $13$ years away gives $n-13$.
- You must start with the original age and then add the 13 years on.
  - _why:_ Adding $13$ would represent someone older than $n$. The word “younger” means the age is reduced, so $13$ must be subtracted from $n$.
- You must start with the original age and then multiply it by 13.
  - _why:_ Multiplication would mean thirteen times the original age, not an age difference of thirteen years. “13 years younger” requires $13$ to be subtracted from $n$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the neat algebraic way to write $16 \times w$?

**Back:** $$16w$

**Choices:**

- $$16w$ ✔
  - _why:_ A number placed directly beside a variable represents multiplication, so $16 \times w$ is written neatly as $16w$.
- $$16 + w$
  - _why:_ This changes multiplication into addition. Algebra omits the multiplication sign between a number and a variable, so $16 \times w$ is written as $16w$.
- $$\frac{16}{w}$
  - _why:_ This represents 16 divided by $w$, not 16 multiplied by $w$. The required algebraic expression is $16w$.
- $$\frac{w}{16}$
  - _why:_ This represents $w$ divided by 16, reversing the required operation. Multiplication of 16 and $w$ is written as $16w$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Write an expression for the age of a cousin who is twice as old as Noah (age $a$).

**Back:** $$2a$

**Choices:**

- $$2a$ ✔
  - _why:_ Twice Noah’s age means multiplying his age $a$ by 2, giving the cousin’s age as $2a$.
- $$a + 2$
  - _why:_ Adding 2 would make the cousin two years older than Noah, not twice as old. Twice the age $a$ is $2a$.
- $$\frac{a}{2}$
  - _why:_ Dividing $a$ by 2 gives half Noah’s age, which reverses the relationship. A cousin twice as old has age $2a$.
- $$a^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring $a$ means multiplying the age by itself, not doubling it. Twice Noah’s age is found by calculating $2 \times a = 2a$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebra, the word per (e.g. pounds per hour) usually indicates the operation of [ ___ ].

**Back:** In algebra, the word per (e.g. pounds per hour) usually indicates the operation of multiplication.

**Choices:**

- multiplication ✔
  - _why:_ When a per-unit amount is used to calculate a total, the rate is multiplied by the number of units, so the intended operation here is multiplication.
- addition
  - _why:_ Addition combines separate quantities rather than applying a per-unit rate. To calculate a total from a rate and a number of units, the intended operation is multiplication.
- subtraction
  - _why:_ Subtraction finds a difference and does not apply a rate to a quantity. The intended operation for using a per-unit amount to find a total is multiplication.
- exponentiation
  - _why:_ Exponentiation repeatedly multiplies a quantity by itself and is not used for an ordinary per-unit calculation. The intended operation here is multiplication.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** If total money taken is $R$ and each ticket costs £5, how is the number of tickets calculated?

**Back:** Divide the total money by the cost of one ticket: $\frac{R}{5}$.

**Choices:**

- Divide the total money by the cost of one ticket: $\frac{R}{5}$. ✔
  - _why:_ The number sold equals total revenue divided by the price of each ticket. Since each ticket costs £5, the number is $R \div 5 = \frac{R}{5}$.
- Multiply the total money by the cost of one ticket: $5R$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying revenue by £5 does not recover the number of tickets. The number sold is total revenue divided by the price per ticket, giving $\frac{R}{5}$.
- Add the cost of one ticket to the total money: $R + 5$.
  - _why:_ Adding £5 changes the revenue rather than finding how many £5 tickets produced it. Divide the total $R$ by £5 to obtain $\frac{R}{5}$.
- Subtract the cost of one ticket from the total money: $R - 5$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting one ticket price only removes £5 from the revenue. Finding the number of equal £5 groups requires division, so the answer is $\frac{R}{5}$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a term in an expression?

**Back:** A single part of the expression separated by plus or minus signs.

**Choices:**

- A single part of the expression separated by plus or minus signs. ✔
  - _why:_ Terms are the individual parts joined by addition or subtraction. For example, $3x$ and $2y$ are the two terms in $3x + 2y$.
- A single letter of the expression separated by multiplication or division signs.
  - _why:_ A term can contain numbers, letters and multiplication, so it is not necessarily a single letter. Terms are separated by plus or minus signs.
- A single number of the expression separated by equals or inequality signs.
  - _why:_ Terms can include variables as well as numbers, and equals signs separate sides of equations rather than terms. Terms are separated by plus or minus signs.
- A complete expression made from numbers or letters only.
  - _why:_ A term is one part of an expression, not necessarily the entire expression. Its boundary is identified by the plus or minus signs separating it from other terms.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the terms in the expression $3x + 2y$.

**Back:** $3x$$2y$

**Choices:**

- $3x$$2y$ ✔
  - _why:_ Terms are separated by plus or minus signs. The plus sign divides this expression into the two complete terms $3x$ and $2y$.
- $3$$2$
  - _why:_ These are only the numerical coefficients, not the complete terms. Each coefficient remains attached to its variable, so the terms are $3x$ and $2y$.
- $x$$y$
  - _why:_ These are only the variables, with their coefficients omitted. The full parts separated by the plus sign are $3x$ and $2y$.
- $3x + 2$$y$
  - _why:_ A plus sign separates terms, so it cannot be included inside the first term here. The expression divides into $3x$ and $2y$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A juice costs $j$ and a chocolate costs $h$. Write an expression for the total cost of 4 juices and 3 chocolates.

**Back:** $$4j + 3h$

**Choices:**

- $$4j + 3h$ ✔
  - _why:_ Four juices cost $4j$ and three chocolates cost $3h$. Adding these two costs gives the total expression $4j + 3h$.
- $$3j + 4h$
  - _why:_ This swaps the quantities of juices and chocolates. Four items at cost $j$ and three at cost $h$ give $4j + 3h$.
- $$4j \times 3h$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the two group costs does not produce the total shopping cost. The costs must be added, giving $4j + 3h$.
- $$j + h + 7$
  - _why:_ The quantities 4 and 3 must multiply the respective item prices, not be added as seven pounds. The correct total is $4j + 3h$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** A shop has $s$ small packs (4 pens each) and $b$ big packs (10 pens each). Write an expression for the total number of pens.

**Back:** $$4s + 10b$

**Choices:**

- $$4s + 10b$ ✔
  - _why:_ The small packs contain $4s$ pens altogether and the big packs contain $10b$ pens. Adding both groups gives $4s + 10b$.
- $$10s + 4b$
  - _why:_ This assigns the pack sizes to the wrong variables. Small packs contain 4 pens and big packs contain 10, so the total is $4s + 10b$.
- $$4s \times 10b$
  - _why:_ The numbers of pens from the two pack types should be added, not multiplied together. Their combined total is $4s + 10b$.
- $$14sb$
  - _why:_ Adding 4 and 10 and multiplying both variables incorrectly combines unlike terms. Count each pack type separately, then add them to obtain $4s + 10b$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Write a formula for total cost $C$ if muffins are 80p and cookies are 50p, using variables $m$ and $k$.

**Back:** $$C = 80m + 50k$

**Choices:**

- $$C = 80m + 50k$ ✔
  - _why:_ Each of the $m$ muffins costs 80p, giving $80m$, and each of the $k$ cookies costs 50p, giving $50k$. Their sum is the total cost.
- $$C = 50m + 80k$
  - _why:_ This swaps the two prices, charging 50p per muffin and 80p per cookie. Matching each price to its item gives $C = 80m + 50k$.
- $$C = 130m + 130k$
  - _why:_ This adds the prices first and then incorrectly charges 130p for every item. The separate item costs must remain separate, giving $C = 80m + 50k$.
- $$C = 80 + m + 50 + k$
  - _why:_ This adds each price to the number bought instead of multiplying them. Cost equals price times quantity, so the formula is $C = 80m + 50k$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** Terms that have the same variable part (e.g. $n$ and $3n$) are known as [ ___ ].

**Back:** Terms that have the same variable part (e.g. $n$ and $3n$) are known as like terms.

**Choices:**

- like terms ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms have exactly the same variable part, including matching powers. Their numerical coefficients may differ, so $n$ and $3n$ are like terms.
- unlike terms
  - _why:_ Unlike terms have different variable parts, such as $n$ and $n^2$. Since $n$ and $3n$ share the same variable part, they are like terms.
- equivalent terms
  - _why:_ Equivalent expressions have the same value, but $n$ and $3n$ are not generally equal. Terms sharing the same variable part are called like terms.
- constant terms
  - _why:_ Constant terms contain no variables, whereas $n$ and $3n$ both contain $n$. Because their variable parts match, they are called like terms.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Ben is $n$. Cara is 9 years older ($n+9$). Dan is 4 years younger ($n-4$). Write a simplified expression for their total age.

**Back:** $$3n + 5$

**Choices:**

- $$3n + 5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Adding the ages gives $n+(n+9)+(n-4)$. The three $n$ terms make $3n$, while the constants give $9-4=5$, so the total is $3n+5$.
- $$3n + 13$
  - _why:_ This treats Dan’s 4-year difference as positive instead of negative. The constants combine as $9-4=5$, so the simplified total is $3n+5$.
- $$n + 5$
  - _why:_ This combines the constants correctly but fails to add all three $n$ terms. Since each person’s age contains one $n$, the total is $3n+5$.
- $$3n - 5$
  - _why:_ This reverses the sign of the constant total. Cara adds 9 years and Dan subtracts 4 years, so the net change is $+5$ and the answer is $3n+5$.
