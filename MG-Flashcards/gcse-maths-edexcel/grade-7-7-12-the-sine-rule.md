# The Sine Rule Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 7 · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In triangle ABC, which side is opposite angle C?

**Back:** Side AB

**Choices:**

- Side AB ✔
  - _why:_ Angle C is formed at vertex C, so the side that does not meet that vertex is AB. Therefore, AB is opposite angle C.
- Side AC
  - _why:_ AC meets angle C at vertex C, so it is adjacent to that angle. The side opposite angle C is AB because it does not touch vertex C.
- Side BC
  - _why:_ BC meets angle C at vertex C, making it adjacent rather than opposite. AB is the only side that does not meet vertex C.
- Side C
  - _why:_ C labels a vertex and angle here, not one of the triangle’s named sides. The side lying opposite vertex C is AB.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the minimum information required to use the sine rule?

**Back:** One complete opposite pair (side and angle)One other side or angle

**Choices:**

- One complete opposite pair (side and angle)One other side or angle ✔
  - _why:_ The sine rule requires one known side and its opposite angle to form a complete ratio, plus another side or angle whose corresponding value can be found.
- Two known side lengthsNo known angles
  - _why:_ Two sides alone do not provide a known sine-rule ratio because neither opposite angle is known. One complete opposite side-angle pair and one additional value are required.
- Two known anglesNo known sides
  - _why:_ Two angles alone determine the triangle’s shape but not its scale, so no side length can be calculated. A complete opposite pair and another value are needed.
- One adjacent pair (side and angle)One other side or angle
  - _why:_ An adjacent side-angle pair cannot form a matching sine-rule ratio. The known side and angle must be opposite each other, with one additional side or angle.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What calculation should be performed first if a triangle has two known angles and one side?

**Back:** Find the third angle using the $180^\text{o}$ sum rule.

**Choices:**

- Find the third angle using the $180^\text{o}$ sum rule. ✔
  - _why:_ The interior angles of a triangle total $180^\text{o}$. Subtracting the two known angles gives the third angle, which may complete the opposite pair needed for the sine rule.
- Find the missing side using the sine rule immediately.
  - _why:_ The sine rule should not be applied before identifying the required opposite side-angle pairs. First find the third angle by subtracting the two known angles from $180^\text{o}$.
- Find the angle total by adding the two known angles.
  - _why:_ Adding the known angles does not directly give the missing angle. The third angle equals $180^\text{o}$ minus the sum of the two known angles.
- Find the third angle using the $90^\text{o}$ sum rule.
  - _why:_ Using $90^\text{o}$ incorrectly assumes that the triangle is right-angled. For any triangle, subtract the two known angles from the full $180^\text{o}$ angle sum.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** When using the sine rule, ensure your calculator is in [ ___ ] mode.

**Back:** When using the sine rule, ensure your calculator is in degrees mode.

**Choices:**

- degrees ✔
  - _why:_ GCSE triangle angles are measured in degrees unless stated otherwise, so degree mode ensures that sine values and inverse-sine results use the correct angular unit.
- radians
  - _why:_ Radians are a different unit of angular measure and would produce unsuitable sine values for angles given in degrees. The calculator must be set to degrees mode.
- gradians
  - _why:_ Gradians divide a full turn into 400 units rather than 360, so they do not match GCSE angles stated in degrees. Use degrees mode instead.
- standard form
  - _why:_ Standard form controls how numbers are displayed, not how trigonometric angles are interpreted. The calculator’s angular setting must be degrees mode.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** When calculating a missing side, how should the sine rule formula be arranged?

**Back:** Side lengths on top: $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$

**Choices:**

- Side lengths on top: $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Placing each side above the sine of its opposite angle makes it straightforward to rearrange for an unknown side while preserving the correct opposite pairings.
- Side lengths on top: $\frac{a}{\sin B} = \frac{b}{\sin A}$
  - _why:_ This crosses the opposite pairs: side $a$ belongs with angle $A$, while side $b$ belongs with angle $B$. The correct ratios are $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}$.
- Angles on top: $\frac{A}{a} = \frac{B}{b}$
  - _why:_ The sine rule uses the sine of each angle, not the raw angle itself. For a missing side, use $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}$.
- Side lengths on top: $\frac{a}{\cos A} = \frac{b}{\cos B}$
  - _why:_ Using cosine confuses the sine rule with cosine-based methods. Each side must be divided by the sine of its opposite angle: $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** When calculating a missing angle, how should the sine rule formula be arranged?

**Back:** Sines on top: $\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b}$

**Choices:**

- Sines on top: $\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Placing each angle’s sine above its opposite side allows the equation to be rearranged to isolate the sine of the missing angle before using inverse sine.
- Sines on top: $\frac{\sin A}{b} = \frac{\sin B}{a}$
  - _why:_ The denominators have been paired with the wrong angles. Angle $A$ is opposite side $a$ and angle $B$ is opposite side $b$, giving $\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}$.
- Cosines on top: $\frac{\cos A}{a} = \frac{\cos B}{b}$
  - _why:_ The sine rule requires sine rather than cosine, so this applies the wrong trigonometric function. The correct arrangement is $\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}$.
- Angles on top: $\frac{A}{a} = \frac{B}{b}$
  - _why:_ Using raw angle values instead of their sines is not the sine rule. The required ratios are $\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a missing side, the formula is: $\frac{\text{[...]}}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$

**Back:** To find a missing side, the formula is: $\frac{\boxed{a}}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$

**Choices:**

- $$a$ ✔
  - _why:_ The sine rule pairs each side with the sine of its opposite angle. Side $a$ is opposite angle $A$, so the numerator above $\sin A$ must be $a$.
- $$b$
  - _why:_ Using $b$ above $\sin A$ incorrectly pairs side $b$ with angle $A$. Side $b$ is opposite $B$, while the side paired with $\sin A$ is $a$.
- $$c$
  - _why:_ Using $c$ confuses the three corresponding side-angle pairs. Side $c$ belongs with $\sin C$; because the denominator is $\sin A$, the numerator must be $a$.
- $$d$
  - _why:_ A triangle conventionally labels its three opposite side-angle pairs as $a,A$, $b,B$, and $c,C$. There is no corresponding $d$ here, so $\sin A$ must pair with $a$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a missing side, the formula is: $\frac{a}{\text{[...]}} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$

**Back:** To find a missing side, the formula is: $\frac{a}{\boxed{\sin A}} = \frac{b}{\sin B}$

**Choices:**

- $$\sin A$ ✔
  - _why:_ Side $a$ is opposite angle $A$, so its denominator must be the sine of that angle. This creates the corresponding ratio $\frac{a}{\sin A}$.
- $$A$
  - _why:_ The sine rule uses the sine of an angle, not the angle value itself. The denominator paired with side $a$ must therefore be $\sin A$.
- $$\cos A$
  - _why:_ Using cosine confuses the sine rule with a different trigonometric relationship. Side $a$ must be divided by the sine of its opposite angle, $\sin A$.
- $$\sin B$
  - _why:_ Angle $B$ is opposite side $b$, not side $a$. Keeping opposite pairs together means the denominator beneath $a$ must be $\sin A$.

**Choices:**

- $$\sin A$ ✔

  - _why:_ This confuses the sine rule with a cosine relationship. The numerator must be $\sin A$ because each side is paired with the sine of its opposite angle.
- $$\sin B$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly pairs side $a$ with angle $B$. The sine rule matches each side with its opposite angle, so the numerator must be $\sin A$.

  - _why:_ This uses both the wrong trigonometric function and the wrong angle. Side $a$ must be paired with $\sin A$ in the sine rule.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a missing angle, the formula is: $\frac{\sin A}{\text{[...]}} = \frac{\sin B}{b}$

**Back:** To find a missing angle, the formula is: $\frac{\sin A}{\boxed{a}} = \frac{\sin B}{b}$

**Choices:**

- $$a$ ✔
  - _why:_ Angle $A$ is opposite side $a$, so the sine rule forms the corresponding ratio $\frac{\sin A}{a}$.
- $$b$
  - _why:_ This pairs angle $A$ with the side opposite angle $B$. Each sine must be paired with its corresponding opposite side, so the denominator is $a$.
- $$2a$
  - _why:_ This introduces an unnecessary factor of two, perhaps confused with the triangle area formula. The side opposite angle $A$ is simply $a$.
- $$c$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly pairs angle $A$ with the side opposite angle $C$. In standard triangle notation, angle $A$ is opposite side $a$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the purpose of the inverse sine ($\sin^{-1}$) function in this topic?

**Back:** To find an angle from its sine value.

**Choices:**

- To find an angle from its sine value. ✔
  - _why:_ After rearranging the sine rule to obtain $\sin A$, applying $\sin^{-1}$ gives the corresponding angle $A$.
- To find a side length from an angle.
  - _why:_ Finding a side length requires rearranging the sine rule, not applying inverse sine alone. The inverse sine function converts a known sine value into an angle.
- To find a sine value from an angle.
  - _why:_ This describes the ordinary sine function rather than inverse sine. The inverse function $\sin^{-1}$ takes a sine value and returns the corresponding angle.
- To find a cosine value from an angle.
  - _why:_ Cosine values are calculated using the cosine function, not inverse sine. In this topic, $\sin^{-1}$ is used to recover an angle from its sine value.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should side lengths be kept as full calculator values until the final step?

**Back:** To prevent rounding errors in the final perimeter or area.

**Choices:**

- To prevent rounding errors in the final perimeter or area. ✔
  - _why:_ Rounding intermediate side lengths can carry inaccuracies into later addition or area calculations. Keeping full calculator values preserves accuracy until the final answer is rounded.
- To prevent unit errors in the final perimeter or area.
  - _why:_ Units must be handled consistently, but retaining full calculator values does not prevent unit errors. It prevents intermediate rounding from reducing the accuracy of the final result.
- To prevent angle errors in the final perimeter or area.
  - _why:_ Angle errors arise from using the wrong angle or calculator mode, not from storing full side values. Full values are retained to minimise accumulated rounding errors.
- To prevent formula errors in the final perimeter or area.
  - _why:_ Keeping extra decimal places cannot correct a wrongly selected formula. Its purpose is to avoid accumulating rounding errors before the final perimeter or area is calculated.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the two steps to find the perimeter of a triangle using the sine rule?

**Back:** Calculate all missing side lengthsAdd all three sides together

**Choices:**

- Calculate all missing side lengthsAdd all three sides together ✔
  - _why:_ Perimeter is the total distance around a shape. After the sine rule supplies any unknown side lengths, all three sides of the triangle must be added.
- Calculate all missing angle sizesAdd all three angles together
  - _why:_ Adding the angles confirms their total is $180^\circ$, but it does not give perimeter. The missing side lengths must be calculated and all three sides added.
- Calculate all missing side lengthsMultiply all three sides together
  - _why:_ Multiplying the side lengths does not measure the boundary of a triangle. Once missing sides are found, perimeter is calculated by adding all three side lengths.
- Calculate all missing side lengthsAdd only two sides together
  - _why:_ This omits one edge of the triangle, so it cannot give the complete distance around it. Perimeter requires the sum of all three side lengths.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the area of a non-right-angled triangle?

**Back:** $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$

**Choices:**

- $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ ✔
  - _why:_ Two side lengths and their included angle determine the area. Multiplying $ab$ by $\sin C$ gives the corresponding perpendicular component, then halving gives the triangle’s area.
- $$\text{Area} = ab\sin C$
  - _why:_ This misses the factor of $\frac{1}{2}$, doubling the triangle’s area. The correct formula is $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$.
- $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\cos C$
  - _why:_ This substitutes cosine for sine, confusing the area formula with relationships from the cosine rule. The area uses $\sin C$ with the two enclosing sides.
- $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)\sin C$
  - _why:_ The side lengths must be multiplied, not added, because area is based on base times perpendicular height. The correct formula is $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** In the area formula $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, what does C represent?

**Back:** The included angle (the angle between sides $a$ and $b$).

**Choices:**

- The included angle (the angle between sides $a$ and $b$). ✔
  - _why:_ In $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$, angle $C$ must lie between the two side lengths being multiplied, $a$ and $b$.
- The included angle (the angle between sides $a$ and $c$).
  - _why:_ The angle between sides $a$ and $c$ is angle $B$ in standard notation. The formula shown uses angle $C$, which lies between sides $a$ and $b$.
- The included angle (the angle between sides $b$ and $c$).
  - _why:_ The angle between sides $b$ and $c$ is angle $A$, not angle $C$. Here, $C$ is the included angle between sides $a$ and $b$.
- The exterior angle (the angle outside sides $a$ and $b$).
  - _why:_ The standard area formula uses the interior included angle between the two known sides. Therefore, $C$ is the interior angle between $a$ and $b$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the ambiguous case, the identity used to find obtuse angles is $\sin \theta = \sin(\text{[...]})$.

**Back:** In the ambiguous case, the identity used to find obtuse angles is $\sin \theta = \sin(\boxed{180^\circ - \theta})$.

**Choices:**

- $$180^\circ - \theta$ ✔
  - _why:_ Sine is positive in the first and second quadrants, so an acute angle $\theta$ and its supplementary obtuse angle $180^\circ - \theta$ have equal sine values.
- $$360^\circ - \theta$
  - _why:_ This gives a reflex angle with the opposite sine sign for angles between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. The supplementary angle is found using $180^\circ - \theta$.
- $$90^\circ - \theta$
  - _why:_ This is the complementary angle and its sine generally equals $\cos\theta$, not $\sin\theta$. The second possible triangle angle is $180^\circ - \theta$.
- $$180^\circ + \theta$
  - _why:_ Adding $180^\circ$ changes the sign of the sine, so it does not produce the same positive sine value. The obtuse alternative is $180^\circ - \theta$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If $\sin^{-1}$ gives an acute angle of $52.6^\circ$ but the question states the angle is obtuse, what is the value?

**Back:** $$180^\circ - 52.6^\circ = 127.4^\circ$

**Choices:**

- $$180^\circ - 52.6^\circ = 127.4^\circ$ ✔
  - _why:_ The sine of an acute angle equals the sine of its supplementary obtuse angle. Subtracting $52.6^\circ$ from $180^\circ$ therefore gives the required obtuse angle, $127.4^\circ$.
- $$180^\circ + 52.6^\circ = 232.6^\circ$
  - _why:_ Adding the acute angle produces a reflex angle rather than an obtuse one. The supplementary angle is found by subtraction: $180^\circ - 52.6^\circ = 127.4^\circ$.
- $$90^\circ + 52.6^\circ = 142.6^\circ$
  - _why:_ Adding the angle to $90^\circ$ does not produce the supplementary angle with the same sine. Subtract from $180^\circ$ instead, giving $127.4^\circ$.
- $$180^\circ - 52.6^\circ = 137.4^\circ$
  - _why:_ The correct method is shown, but the subtraction contains an arithmetic error. Calculating it accurately gives $180^\circ - 52.6^\circ = 127.4^\circ$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you identify an opposite side for the sine rule?

**Back:** It is the side across from the angle, not touching the angle's vertex.

**Choices:**

- It is the side across from the angle, not touching the angle's vertex. ✔
  - _why:_ An opposite side lies directly across the triangle from its corresponding angle. It does not meet that angle's vertex, so the side and angle form a sine-rule pair.
- It is the side next to the angle, touching the angle's vertex.
  - _why:_ A side touching the angle's vertex is adjacent, not opposite. The opposite side lies across from the angle and does not touch the angle's vertex.
- It is the side longest in the triangle, regardless of the angle's vertex.
  - _why:_ The longest side is opposite the largest angle, but it is not automatically opposite every chosen angle. Identify the side directly across from the specific angle.
- It is the side shortest in the triangle, regardless of the angle's vertex.
  - _why:_ The shortest side is opposite the smallest angle, but side length alone does not identify an opposite pair. Use the side directly across from the specified angle.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** When given side $c$, angle $C$, and side $a$, how do you find angle A?

**Back:** $$\sin A = \frac{a \sin C}{c}$ followed by $\sin^{-1}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\sin A = \frac{a \sin C}{c}$ followed by $\sin^{-1}$. ✔
  - _why:_ From the sine rule, $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$. Rearranging gives $\sin A=\frac{a\sin C}{c}$, then inverse sine gives the angle.
- $$\sin A = \frac{c \sin C}{a}$ followed by $\sin^{-1}$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the side ratio when rearranging the sine rule. Matching each side with its opposite angle gives $\sin A=\frac{a\sin C}{c}$ before applying inverse sine.
- $$\sin A = \frac{a \cos C}{c}$ followed by $\sin^{-1}$.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly replaces the sine of the known angle with cosine. The sine rule uses sine for both angles, so $\sin A=\frac{a\sin C}{c}$.
- $$\sin A = \frac{c \cos C}{a}$ followed by $\sin^{-1}$.
  - _why:_ This both reverses the required side ratio and uses cosine instead of sine. Correct rearrangement of the sine rule gives $\sin A=\frac{a\sin C}{c}$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What specific condition triggers the 'ambiguous case' (obtuse angle twist)?

**Back:** When a calculated angle could be acute or obtuse, and the problem specifies which one to use.

**Choices:**

- When a calculated angle could be acute or obtuse, and the problem specifies which one to use. ✔
  - _why:_ Sine has the same positive value for supplementary acute and obtuse angles. If the question specifies the angle type, this determines whether to use the inverse-sine result or its supplement.
- When a calculated angle could be acute or right-angled, and the problem specifies which one to use.
  - _why:_ A positive sine value generally pairs an acute angle with its supplementary obtuse angle, not a right angle. The ambiguity is resolved by choosing acute or obtuse as specified.
- When a calculated angle could be obtuse or reflex, and the problem specifies which one to use.
  - _why:_ Interior angles of a triangle cannot be reflex, so this is not the sine-rule ambiguity. The possible supplementary solutions within the relevant range are acute and obtuse.
- When a calculated angle could be equal or complementary, and the problem specifies which one to use.
  - _why:_ Complementary angles add to $90^\circ$ and do not generally have equal sines. The ambiguous case concerns supplementary acute and obtuse angles, which add to $180^\circ$.
