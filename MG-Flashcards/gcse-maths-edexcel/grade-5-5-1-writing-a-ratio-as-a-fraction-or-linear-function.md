## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** If Sam and Tia share money in the ratio 3:2, what fraction of the total does Tia receive?

**Back:** $$\frac{2}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{2}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Tia has 2 of the 5 total ratio parts, because $3+2=5$. Therefore, her fraction of the total is $\frac{2}{5}$.
- $$\frac{2}{3}$
  - _why:_ This compares Tia’s 2 parts only with Sam’s 3 parts, rather than with the total. There are 5 parts altogether, so Tia receives $\frac{2}{5}$.
- $$\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This is Sam’s fraction, because Sam has 3 of the 5 total parts. Tia has 2 parts, so she receives $\frac{2}{5}$.
- $$\frac{1}{5}$
  - _why:_ The difference between their shares is 1 part, but Tia’s share is 2 parts. Out of 5 total parts, she receives $\frac{2}{5}$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Given $p:q=7:3$, write $p$ in terms of $q$.

**Back:** $$p = \frac{7}{3}q$

**Choices:**

- $$p = \frac{7}{3}q$ ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio gives $\frac{p}{q}=\frac{7}{3}$. Multiplying both sides by $q$ produces the linear relationship $p=\frac{7}{3}q$.
- $$p = \frac{3}{7}q$
  - _why:_ This reverses the ratio and would make $p$ smaller than $q$. Since $p:q=7:3$, the correct relationship is $p=\frac{7}{3}q$.
- $$p = \frac{10}{3}q$
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio parts gives 10, but the total is not used to compare $p$ directly with $q$. Their multiplier is $\frac{7}{3}$, so $p=\frac{7}{3}q$.
- $$p = \frac{7}{10}q$
  - _why:_ This treats $q$ as the total of 10 ratio parts. Directly comparing the 7 parts of $p$ with the 3 parts of $q$ gives $p=\frac{7}{3}q$.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $A:B=5:2$, it is a common mistake to write $A = \frac{2}{5}B$; the correct linear relationship is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If $A:B=5:2$, it is a common mistake to write $A = \frac{2}{5}B$; the correct linear relationship is $\boxed{A = \frac{5}{2}B}$.

**Choices:**

- $$A = \frac{5}{2}B$ ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio means $\frac{A}{B}=\frac{5}{2}$. Multiplying by $B$ gives $A=\frac{5}{2}B$, so $A$ is larger than $B$.
- $$A = \frac{2}{5}B$
  - _why:_ This reverses the ratio and makes $A$ smaller than $B$, contradicting the ratio $5:2$. The correct relationship is $A=\frac{5}{2}B$.
- $$A = \frac{5}{7}B$
  - _why:_ Using 7 as the denominator incorrectly divides by the total number of parts. Direct comparison uses 5 parts to 2 parts, giving $A=\frac{5}{2}B$.
- $$A = \frac{7}{2}B$
  - _why:_ Adding the two ratio parts to make 7 does not give the multiplier from $B$ to $A$. That multiplier is $5\div2$, so $A=\frac{5}{2}B$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** When combining $a:b=2:5$ and $b:c=3:4$, what is the first step?

**Back:** Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the shared quantity $b$.

**Choices:**

- Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the shared quantity $b$. ✔
  - _why:_ The two ratios assign different part values to $b$. Finding the LCM of 5 and 3 allows both ratios to be scaled until their $b$ values match.
- Find the highest common factor (HCF) of the shared quantity $b$.
  - _why:_ The HCF would not produce equal values for the shared term in both ratios. The first step is finding the LCM of 5 and 3 so both $b$ values can match.
- Find the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the outer quantities $a$ and $c$.
  - _why:_ Only $b$ appears in both ratios, so it is the quantity that must be matched. Find the LCM of its values 5 and 3, not of $a$ and $c$.
- Find the difference between the parts of the shared quantity $b$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 3 from 5 does not create equivalent ratios with matching $b$ values. Find the LCM, 15, then scale each ratio so $b$ has 15 parts.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** If $a:b=2:5$ and $b:c=3:4$, what value should the $b$ parts be scaled to?

**Back:** 15 (the LCM of 5 and 3).

**Choices:**

- 15 (the LCM of 5 and 3). ✔
  - _why:_ The shared quantity $b$ has 5 parts in one ratio and 3 in the other. Their lowest common multiple is 15, so both are scaled to 15.
- 8 (the sum of 5 and 3).
  - _why:_ Adding the two values does not make equivalent ratios with equal $b$ parts. The smallest value divisible by both 5 and 3 is 15.
- 2 (the difference of 5 and 3).
  - _why:_ Taking the difference does not provide a common multiple for the two $b$ values. Both ratios must instead be scaled so $b$ equals 15 parts.
- 5 (the greater of 5 and 3).
  - _why:_ Choosing the larger value fails because 5 is not a multiple of 3. The smallest number divisible by both values is 15, so $b$ must be scaled to 15.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** The combined ratio of $a:b=2:5$ and $b:c=3:4$ is $a:b:c = $ [ ___ ].

**Back:** The combined ratio of $a:b=2:5$ and $b:c=3:4$ is $a:b:c = $ 6:15:20.

**Choices:**

- 6:15:20 ✔
  - _why:_ Scale $2:5$ by 3 to get $6:15$, and scale $3:4$ by 5 to get $15:20$. Matching $b$ gives the combined ratio $6:15:20$.
- 2:15:4
  - _why:_ Only changing the shared $b$ value breaks the equivalence of both original ratios. All terms must be scaled consistently, producing $6:15$ and $15:20$, hence $6:15:20$.
- 6:15:12
  - _why:_ The second ratio has been scaled inconsistently: multiplying $b=3$ by 5 requires multiplying $c=4$ by 5 as well. Therefore, $c=20$ and the ratio is $6:15:20$.
- 10:15:20
  - _why:_ The first ratio has been scaled incorrectly: changing $b$ from 5 to 15 multiplies it by 3, so $a=2$ must become 6. The combined ratio is $6:15:20$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a common multiplier ($k$) used for in ratio problems?

**Back:** To convert ratio parts into real/actual amounts.

**Choices:**

- To convert ratio parts into real/actual amounts. ✔
  - _why:_ A common multiplier gives the value of one ratio part. Multiplying every ratio number by $k$ converts the simplified parts into the corresponding actual quantities.
- To convert ratio parts into fractions of the total.
  - _why:_ Fractions of the total are found by dividing each part by the sum of all parts, not by using $k$. The common multiplier converts parts into actual amounts.
- To convert ratio parts into their simplest whole numbers.
  - _why:_ Simplifying a ratio requires division by a common factor, whereas $k$ scales every part to an actual quantity. Its purpose is to convert ratio parts into real amounts.
- To convert ratio parts into a common shared quantity.
  - _why:_ Matching a shared quantity is used when combining linked ratios, usually through an LCM. A common multiplier $k$ instead converts ratio parts into real or actual amounts.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** If $D:E:F = 15:6:20$ and Finn ($F$) has 14 more counters than Dylan ($D$), how many parts represent 14 counters?

**Back:** 5 parts (since $20 - 15 = 5$).

**Choices:**

- 5 parts (since $20 - 15 = 5$). ✔
  - _why:_ Finn has 20 ratio parts and Dylan has 15, so the difference is 5 parts. Those 5 parts correspond to the stated difference of 14 counters.
- 35 parts (since $20 + 15 = 35$).
  - _why:_ Adding the parts gives Finn and Dylan’s combined share, not the difference between them. Their difference is $20-15=5$ parts, which represents 14 counters.
- 14 parts (since Finn has 14 more counters).
  - _why:_ This confuses the number of counters with the number of ratio parts. The ratio difference is $20-15=5$ parts, and those 5 parts are worth 14 counters.
- 4 parts (since $20 \div 5 = 4$).
  - _why:_ Dividing Finn’s parts by the ratio difference does not measure how many parts separate the shares. Subtracting Dylan’s 15 parts from Finn’s 20 gives 5 parts.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a question involves items like people or sweets, the final answer must be a [ ___ ].

**Back:** When a question involves items like people or sweets, the final answer must be a whole number.

**Choices:**

- whole number ✔
  - _why:_ People and individual sweets cannot normally be divided into fractional items, so the result must be rounded or interpreted as a whole number.
- decimal
  - _why:_ A decimal could represent part of an item, which is unsuitable when counting indivisible people or sweets. The final answer must be a whole number.
- fraction
  - _why:_ A fraction may describe a proportion, but it cannot be the final count of indivisible items. The required final answer is a whole number.
- percentage
  - _why:_ A percentage expresses a proportion rather than a count of individual items. For people or sweets, the final answer must be a whole number.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** In the ratio "red to not red = 3:7", how many parts are in the total?

**Back:** 10 parts ($3 + 7 = 10$).

**Choices:**

- 10 parts ($3 + 7 = 10$). ✔
  - _why:_ The two categories cover the entire group, so their ratio parts are added. Therefore, $3 + 7 = 10$ parts altogether.
- 4 parts ($7 - 3 = 4$).
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio values gives their difference, not the total number of parts. The parts must be added, giving $3 + 7 = 10$.
- 21 parts ($3 \times 7 = 21$).
  - _why:_ Multiplying the two ratio values does not calculate the total number of parts. Adding them gives the correct total of $10$ parts.
- 7 parts (the not red part only).
  - _why:_ Using only the larger ratio value ignores the three red parts. Both categories must be included, so the total is $3 + 7 = 10$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** If won to not won is 5:3, what fraction of the games were won?

**Back:** $$\frac{5}{8}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{5}{8}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are $5 + 3 = 8$ equal parts altogether, and won games occupy five of them. The fraction won is therefore $\frac{5}{8}$.
- $$\frac{5}{3}$
  - _why:_ Writing one side directly over the other confuses a ratio with a fraction of the total. There are eight total parts, so the answer is $\frac{5}{8}$.
- $$\frac{3}{8}$
  - _why:_ This uses the three not-won parts as the numerator, so it gives the fraction not won. The fraction won is $\frac{5}{8}$.
- $$\frac{8}{5}$
  - _why:_ Reversing the numerator and denominator produces an improper fraction greater than one, which cannot represent the proportion won. The correct fraction is $\frac{5}{8}$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Red:not red is 3:7 and Green:not green is 2:3. What total number of parts works for both to compare them?

**Back:** 10 parts (the LCM of 10 and 5).

**Choices:**

- 10 parts (the LCM of 10 and 5). ✔
  - _why:_ The ratios contain $3 + 7 = 10$ parts and $2 + 3 = 5$ parts. Their lowest common multiple is $10$, allowing direct comparison.
- 5 parts (the total for Green only).
  - _why:_ Five is the total for the Green ratio but cannot represent the ten parts in the Red ratio using whole scaling. The required LCM is $10$.
- 15 parts (the sum of 10 and 5).
  - _why:_ Adding the two totals does not necessarily produce a common multiple. Fifteen is not divisible by $10$, whereas the lowest common multiple is $10$.
- 25 parts (the square of 5).
  - _why:_ Squaring the smaller total is unrelated to finding a common scale, and $25$ is not divisible by $10$. The required LCM is $10$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** If Red is $\frac{3}{10}$ of a total and Green is $\frac{4}{10}$ of a total, the remaining Blue parts make up $\text{[...]}$ of the total.

**Back:** If Red is $\frac{3}{10}$ of a total and Green is $\frac{4}{10}$ of a total, the remaining Blue parts make up $\boxed{\frac{3}{10}}$ of the total.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{3}{10}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Red and Green occupy $\frac{3}{10} + \frac{4}{10} = \frac{7}{10}$. Subtracting from the whole leaves Blue with $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{7}{10}$
  - _why:_ This is the combined Red and Green fraction, not the remainder. Subtracting $\frac{7}{10}$ from $1$ leaves Blue with $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{4}{10}$
  - _why:_ This assumes Blue has the same share as Green without calculating the remainder. Red and Green use $\frac{7}{10}$, leaving Blue with $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{1}{10}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the two given fractions finds their difference rather than the unused portion. The complement of $\frac{7}{10}$ is $\frac{3}{10}$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Points $A, B, C, D$ lie in order on a line. Write segment $BD$ as the sum of two smaller segments.

**Back:** $$BC + CD$

**Choices:**

- $$BC + CD$ ✔
  - _why:_ Because $C$ lies between $B$ and $D$, travelling from $B$ to $D$ consists of segment $BC$ followed by segment $CD$.
- $$AB + BC$
  - _why:_ These adjacent segments run from $A$ to $C$, so their sum is $AC$, not $BD$. Segment $BD$ is correctly written as $BC + CD$.
- $$AB + CD$
  - _why:_ These segments are separated by $BC$, so their sum does not represent one continuous journey from $B$ to $D$. The correct expression is $BC + CD$.
- $$AC + CD$
  - _why:_ These adjacent segments run from $A$ to $D$, producing $AD$ rather than $BD$. Starting at $B$ requires the sum $BC + CD$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Points $A, B, C, D$ lie in order. Write segment $AC$ as the sum of two smaller segments.

**Back:** $$AB + BC$

**Choices:**

- $$AB + BC$ ✔
  - _why:_ Because $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$, the distance from $A$ to $C$ is the sum of the adjacent segments $AB$ and $BC$.
- $$BC + CD$
  - _why:_ These adjacent segments run from $B$ to $D$, so their sum is $BD$, not $AC$. Segment $AC$ is correctly written as $AB + BC$.
- $$AB + CD$
  - _why:_ This omits the middle segment $BC$, so it does not form the continuous distance from $A$ to $C$. The correct sum is $AB + BC$.
- $$AC + CD$
  - _why:_ This combines the entire segment $AC$ with the additional segment $CD$, giving $AD$. To express $AC$ using smaller segments, use $AB + BC$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $AB:BD = 1:4$ and we let $AB = x$, then the sum of the segments $BC + CD$ must equal $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If $AB:BD = 1:4$ and we let $AB = x$, then the sum of the segments $BC + CD$ must equal $\boxed{4x}$.

**Choices:**

- $$4x$ ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio $AB:BD = 1:4$ means $BD$ is four times $AB$. Since $BD = BC + CD$ and $AB=x$, the sum is $4x$.
- $$\frac{x}{4}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the ratio and divides by four, even though $BD$ corresponds to four parts while $AB$ corresponds to one. The required sum is $4x$.
- $$3x$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio parts gives their difference, not the length represented by $BD$. Four ratio parts correspond to $BD = BC + CD = 4x$.
- $$5x$
  - _why:_ Adding both ratio values gives the total length $AD$, not the segment $BD$. Since $BD$ alone represents four parts, $BC + CD = 4x$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If $(x+y):z = 3:5$, how can this be written as an equation?

**Back:** $$5(x+y) = 3z$

**Choices:**

- $$5(x+y) = 3z$ ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio gives $\frac{x+y}{z}=\frac{3}{5}$. Cross-multiplying places $5$ with $(x+y)$ and $3$ with $z$, producing the stated equation.
- $$3(x+y) = 5z$
  - _why:_ This pairs each quantity with its own ratio number rather than cross-multiplying. Since $\frac{x+y}{z}=\frac{3}{5}$, the correct equation is $5(x+y)=3z$.
- $$5(x+y) = 8z$
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio parts to obtain $8$ is not part of converting a ratio into an equation. Cross-multiplying $\frac{x+y}{z}=\frac{3}{5}$ gives $5(x+y)=3z$.
- $$3(x+y) = 2z$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio parts to obtain $2$ does not preserve the original ratio. Writing it as $\frac{x+y}{z}=\frac{3}{5}$ and cross-multiplying gives $5(x+y)=3z$.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the ratio $A:B = 4:7$, $B$ in terms of $A$ is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** In the ratio $A:B = 4:7$, $B$ in terms of $A$ is $\boxed{B = \frac{7}{4}A}$.

**Choices:**

- $$B = \frac{7}{4}A$ ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio means $\frac{A}{B}=\frac{4}{7}$. Rearranging gives $B=\frac{7}{4}A$, so $B$ is larger than $A$, consistently with seven parts compared with four.
- $$B = \frac{4}{7}A$
  - _why:_ This uses the ratio in the wrong direction and would make $B$ smaller than $A$. Since $B$ has seven parts for every four of $A$, $B=\frac{7}{4}A$.
- $$B = \frac{3}{4}A$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio numbers to obtain $3$ does not give the scale factor between the quantities. Seven parts compared with four means $B=\frac{7}{4}A$.
- $$B = \frac{11}{4}A$
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio parts to obtain $11$ does not determine the multiplier from $A$ to $B$. Dividing seven parts by four parts gives $B=\frac{7}{4}A$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** What two things should be checked before using a given 'difference' in a multi-part ratio problem?

**Back:** The shared quantity is matched.The combined ratio is established.

**Choices:**

- The shared quantity is matched.The combined ratio is established. ✔
  - _why:_ Matching the common quantity puts both ratios on a consistent scale. A combined ratio can then be formed, allowing the stated difference to be linked to the correct numbers of parts.
- The largest quantity is identified.The ratio parts are added.
  - _why:_ The largest quantity and total number of parts do not show how two linked ratios correspond. The shared quantity must be matched first, then the combined ratio established.
- The shared quantity is matched.The original ratios are kept separate.
  - _why:_ Matching the shared quantity is necessary, but leaving the ratios separate prevents the given difference from being assigned within one consistent relationship. The combined ratio must also be established.
- The different quantities are matched.The combined total is established.
  - _why:_ Matching different quantities confuses their roles, while finding a total is not the same as combining ratios. The common quantity must be matched and the combined ratio established.
