# Exchange Rates Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 3 · 22 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** An exchange rate tells you how much one currency is worth in [ ___ ].

**Back:** An exchange rate tells you how much one currency is worth in another currency.

**Choices:**

- another currency ✔
  - _why:_ An exchange rate compares the values of two currencies, expressing how much of another currency can be obtained for one unit of the first currency.
- the same currency
  - _why:_ An exchange rate compares two different currencies, not amounts within the same currency. It tells you how much one currency is worth in another currency.
- a percentage
  - _why:_ A percentage describes a proportion rather than a currency value. An exchange rate states how much one currency is worth in another currency.
- a bank account
  - _why:_ A bank account may hold currency, but it is not the comparison used by an exchange rate. The value is expressed in another currency.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the exchange rate £1 = €1.16 mean?

**Back:** £1 is worth €1.16.

**Choices:**

- £1 is worth €1.16. ✔
  - _why:_ The rate places £1 on the left and its equivalent euro value on the right, so each pound can be exchanged for €1.16.
- €1 is worth £1.16.
  - _why:_ This swaps the currencies while keeping the same number, which does not preserve equivalent value. The given rate means £1 is worth €1.16.
- £1.16 is worth €1.
  - _why:_ This reverses the amounts and currencies shown in the rate. The original statement directly says that £1 has the same value as €1.16.
- £1 is worth €0.86.
  - _why:_ The value €0.86 is approximately the reciprocal rate for converting one euro into pounds. The stated rate means £1 is worth €1.16.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a rate starts with £1 and you change pounds into the other currency, [ ___ ] by the rate.

**Back:** When a rate starts with £1 and you change pounds into the other currency, multiply by the rate.

**Choices:**

- multiply ✔
  - _why:_ Each pound is worth the stated number of units of the other currency, so multiplying the number of pounds by the rate gives the converted amount.
- divide
  - _why:_ Dividing would be appropriate when converting the other currency back into pounds. Starting with pounds at a £1-based rate requires multiplying by the rate.
- subtract
  - _why:_ An exchange rate is a scale factor, not an amount to remove. To change pounds into the other currency, multiply the pound amount by the rate.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding the rate treats it as a fixed currency amount rather than a value per pound. The correct operation is to multiply by the rate.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** If £1 = €1.18, changing euros into pounds means [ ___ ] by 1.18.

**Back:** If £1 = €1.18, changing euros into pounds means dividing by 1.18.

**Choices:**

- dividing ✔
  - _why:_ The rate gives euros for each pound, so converting euros back into pounds requires the inverse operation: divide the euro amount by 1.18.
- multiplying
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 1.18 converts pounds into euros, not euros into pounds. For the reverse conversion, divide the euro amount by 1.18.
- adding
  - _why:_ Adding 1.18 ignores that an exchange rate is a multiplier relating two currencies. To find pounds from euros, divide by 1.18.
- subtracting
  - _why:_ Subtracting the rate treats 1.18 as a fixed amount rather than euros per pound. The correct reverse conversion is division by 1.18.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How should £67.796... be rounded to the nearest penny?

**Back:** £67.80.

**Choices:**

- £67.80. ✔
  - _why:_ Rounding to the nearest penny means keeping two decimal places. The third decimal digit is 6, so the hundredths digit rounds up from 9, carrying to give £67.80.
- £67.79.
  - _why:_ This truncates the value instead of rounding it. Because the third decimal digit is 6, the second decimal place rounds up, giving £67.80.
- £67.796.
  - _why:_ This keeps three decimal places, but pounds and pence require two decimal places. Rounding using the third decimal digit gives £67.80.
- £68.00.
  - _why:_ This rounds to the nearest pound rather than the nearest penny. Keeping two decimal places and rounding correctly gives £67.80.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Calculate £265 in euros if £1 = €1.18.

**Back:** 265 × 1.18 = €312.70.

**Choices:**

- 265 × 1.18 = €312.70. ✔
  - _why:_ The rate gives €1.18 for each pound, so £265 is multiplied by 1.18. This calculation gives €312.70.
- 265 ÷ 1.18 = €224.58.
  - _why:_ Division reverses the conversion and would be used when changing euros into pounds. Converting £265 into euros requires multiplication, giving €312.70.
- 265 × 1.18 = €312.07.
  - _why:_ The conversion operation is correct, but the decimal digits have been misplaced in the product. Calculating 265 × 1.18 accurately gives €312.70.
- 265 + 1.18 = €266.18.
  - _why:_ Adding treats the exchange rate as a fixed amount rather than euros per pound. Multiply £265 by 1.18 to obtain €312.70.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Calculate €80 in pounds if £1 = €1.18, to the nearest penny.

**Back:** 80 ÷ 1.18 = £67.80.

**Choices:**

- 80 ÷ 1.18 = £67.80. ✔
  - _why:_ The rate gives euros per pound, so euros are converted back into pounds by division. The result is £67.796..., which rounds to £67.80.
- 80 × 1.18 = £94.40.
  - _why:_ Multiplication converts pounds into euros, so it uses the rate in the wrong direction here. Dividing €80 by 1.18 gives £67.80 to the nearest penny.
- 80 ÷ 1.18 = £67.79.
  - _why:_ The division is correct, but the result has been truncated rather than rounded. Since £67.796... has a third decimal digit of 6, it rounds to £67.80.
- 80 × 0.18 = £14.40.
  - _why:_ Using only the decimal part of the rate mistakes 1.18 for an 18% conversion. The full reverse calculation is 80 ÷ 1.18, giving £67.80.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** If €1 = £0.87, what operation changes euros into pounds?

**Back:** Multiply by 0.87.

**Choices:**

- Multiply by 0.87. ✔
  - _why:_ The rate directly gives £0.87 for every euro, so multiplying the number of euros by 0.87 produces the equivalent amount in pounds.
- Divide by 0.87.
  - _why:_ Division would reverse the stated conversion and change pounds into euros. Since each euro equals £0.87, euros are converted into pounds by multiplying by 0.87.
- Add 0.87.
  - _why:_ Adding assumes the rate is a single fixed amount rather than an amount for every euro. The euro amount must be multiplied by 0.87.
- Multiply by 1.87.
  - _why:_ Adding 1 to the rate incorrectly treats £0.87 as an extra amount on top of every euro. The stated conversion factor is exactly 0.87.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** A meal costs €32. If €1 = £0.87, what is the cost in pounds?

**Back:** 32 × 0.87 = £27.84.

**Choices:**

- 32 × 0.87 = £27.84. ✔
  - _why:_ The rate gives £0.87 for each euro, so multiply the euro price by 0.87. This gives 32 × 0.87 = £27.84.
- 32 ÷ 0.87 = £36.78.
  - _why:_ Dividing reverses the required conversion because the rate states the pound value of one euro. Multiply instead: 32 × 0.87 = £27.84.
- 32 × 87 = £2784.
  - _why:_ This calculation treats 87 pence as £87 by omitting the decimal point. Using £0.87 per euro gives 32 × 0.87 = £27.84.
- 32 × 0.87 = £27.13.
  - _why:_ The conversion operation is correct, but the multiplication has been evaluated inaccurately. Multiplying 32 by 0.87 gives £27.84, not £27.13.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a cost such as £40 per night, per means [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a cost such as £40 per night, per means for each.

**Choices:**

- for each ✔
  - _why:_ The word “per” associates a stated price or quantity with each single unit. Therefore, £40 per night means £40 for each night.
- for all
  - _why:_ “Per” describes the cost for one unit, not the combined cost for all units. The correct completion is “for each”, as in £40 for each night.
- in total
  - _why:_ “Per” gives a unit rate rather than an overall total. The correct term is “for each”, because the £40 charge applies to each separate night.
- less than
  - _why:_ “Per” does not express a comparison between two quantities. It means “for each”, so £40 per night is a charge of £40 for every individual night.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** In a multi-step exchange-rate question with a cost per night, what should you find before converting?

**Back:** Find the total starting-currency cost first.

**Choices:**

- Find the total starting-currency cost first. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply the cost per night by the number of nights to obtain the complete cost in the original currency. Convert that total using the exchange rate.
- Find the average starting-currency cost first.
  - _why:_ An average is not needed when the cost per night and number of nights are known. Find the total starting-currency cost by multiplying them before converting.
- Find the total destination-currency cost first.
  - _why:_ The destination-currency total cannot be found before carrying out the conversion. First calculate the total starting-currency cost, then apply the exchange rate.
- Find the cheapest single-night cost first.
  - _why:_ Choosing a single-night cost ignores the full length of the stay. Multiply the nightly cost by the number of nights to find the total starting-currency cost first.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A hotel costs ¥9000 per night for 5 nights. What is the total cost in yen?

**Back:** 9000 × 5 = ¥45000.

**Choices:**

- 9000 × 5 = ¥45000. ✔
  - _why:_ The ¥9000 charge applies to each of the 5 nights, so multiplication gives the total: 9000 × 5 = ¥45000.
- 9000 + 5 = ¥9005.
  - _why:_ Adding 5 treats the number of nights as an extra yen amount. The hotel charges ¥9000 on each of 5 nights, so 9000 × 5 = ¥45000.
- 9000 × 4 = ¥36000.
  - _why:_ This off-by-one error charges for only 4 nights rather than all 5. The correct calculation is 9000 × 5 = ¥45000.
- 9000 ÷ 5 = ¥1800.
  - _why:_ Dividing finds one fifth of the nightly charge rather than the cost of 5 nights. Multiply instead: 9000 × 5 = ¥45000.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Convert ¥45000 to pounds if £1 = ¥150.

**Back:** 45000 ÷ 150 = £300.

**Choices:**

- 45000 ÷ 150 = £300. ✔
  - _why:_ Since £1 corresponds to ¥150, divide the number of yen by 150 to find the number of pounds. Therefore, 45000 ÷ 150 = £300.
- 45000 × 150 = £6750000.
  - _why:_ Multiplying uses the exchange rate in the wrong direction and makes the converted value far too large. Divide by 150 instead to obtain £300.
- 45000 ÷ 15 = £3000.
  - _why:_ This drops a zero from the exchange rate and divides by 15 rather than 150. Using the stated rate gives 45000 ÷ 150 = £300.
- 45000 ÷ 100 = £450.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 100 incorrectly treats ¥100 as equal to £1. The stated rate is ¥150 per pound, so 45000 ÷ 150 = £300.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** To compare two prices, they must be in the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To compare two prices, they must be in the same currency.

**Choices:**

- same currency ✔
  - _why:_ Currency amounts can be compared directly only when they use the same unit of money. Convert one or both prices so both are in the same currency.
- different currencies
  - _why:_ Prices in different currencies are not directly comparable because their numerical values use different monetary units. They must first be converted into the same currency.
- same country
  - _why:_ Being offered in the same country does not guarantee that prices use the same monetary unit. Direct comparison requires both prices to be in the same currency.
- same amount
  - _why:_ Prices do not need to have the same numerical amount to be compared. They need to be expressed in the same currency so their values are equivalent units.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** The difference between two prices is found by subtracting the [ ___ ] price from the [ ___ ] price.

**Back:** The difference between two prices is found by subtracting the smaller price from the larger price.

**Choices:**

- The difference between two prices is found by subtracting the smaller price from the larger price. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting the smaller value from the larger value gives a positive measure of the gap between them. This is the difference between the two prices.
- The difference between two prices is found by subtracting the larger price from the larger price.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the larger price from itself always gives zero, not the gap between two distinct prices. Subtract the smaller price from the larger price instead.
- The difference between two prices is found by subtracting the smaller price from the smaller price.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the smaller price from itself always produces zero. To find the actual difference, subtract the smaller of the two prices from the larger one.
- The difference between two prices is found by subtracting the larger price from the smaller price.
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and produces a negative result rather than the usual positive difference. The smaller price should be subtracted from the larger price.

  - _why:_ Each pound is worth 80 rupees, so divide the rupee amount by 80 to find the pounds. Thus, 3200 ÷ 80 = £40.

  - _why:_ This reverses the division by dividing the rate by the amount being converted. Since there are 80 rupees per pound, calculate 3200 ÷ 80 = £40.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Trainers cost £40 in India and £45 in the UK. Which is cheaper?

**Back:** India is cheaper.

**Choices:**

- India is cheaper. ✔
  - _why:_ The trainers cost £40 in India and £45 in the UK. Since £40 is £5 less than £45, buying them in India is cheaper.
- The UK is cheaper.
  - _why:_ This reverses the comparison: the UK price is £45, whereas the Indian price is only £40. Therefore, India is cheaper by £5.
- Both countries cost the same.
  - _why:_ The prices are not equal because £40 and £45 differ by £5. The lower price is £40, so India is the cheaper country.
- India is more expensive.
  - _why:_ This confuses the lower price with the higher price. India’s price is £40 rather than £45, so India is cheaper, not more expensive.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between £45 and £40?

**Back:** 45 - 40 = £5.

**Choices:**

- 45 - 40 = £5. ✔
  - _why:_ The difference is found by subtracting the smaller amount from the larger amount. Subtracting £40 from £45 gives a difference of £5.
- 45 + 40 = £85.
  - _why:_ Adding finds the total of the two prices, not the difference between them. The difference is found using 45 - 40, which equals £5.

  - _why:_ This is a subtraction error, often caused by subtracting the tens and units incorrectly. Counting from £40 to £45 shows that the difference is £5.

  - _why:_ This incorrectly compares only part of the place value or treats the numbers as consecutive. Subtracting £40 from £45 gives £5.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** When choosing the better rate for changing pounds into euros, what should you compare?

**Back:** Compare the euros received for £1.

**Choices:**

- Compare the euros received for £1. ✔
  - _why:_ Expressing every exchange rate as euros received for £1 creates a like-for-like comparison. The rate giving the most euros for £1 is better.
- Compare the pounds needed for €1.
  - _why:_ This uses the reverse direction of exchange. For pounds into euros, compare euros received for £1; the greatest number of euros gives the better rate.
- Compare the euros received for £100.
  - _why:_ Although equivalent rates can be scaled to £100, this is not the standard direct comparison requested. Convert both rates to euros received for £1.
- Compare the pounds received for €1.
  - _why:_ This describes changing euros into pounds, which is the opposite transaction. For pounds into euros, compare how many euros each exchange gives for £1.

  - _why:_ This comes from calculating the reciprocal of 0.88 inaccurately. The correct value is €1.136... per £1, which is greater than City A’s €1.12.

  - _why:_ This assumes the reciprocal of 0.88 is exactly 1.12. It is actually 1.136..., so City B gives slightly more euros than City A.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** For the greatest number of notes, round [ ___ ], not to the nearest whole number.

**Back:** For the greatest number of notes, round down, not to the nearest whole number.

**Choices:**

- down ✔
  - _why:_ Only complete notes can be received, so any fractional result must be rounded down. Rounding up would require more dong than the traveller actually has.
- up
  - _why:_ Rounding up would count a note that cannot be fully paid for. To find the greatest possible number of complete notes, the result must be rounded down.
- upwards
  - _why:_ Rounding upwards overestimates the number of complete notes available and may exceed the traveller’s money. The quotient must instead be rounded down.
- away from zero
  - _why:_ For a positive quotient, rounding away from zero means rounding up, which could include an unaffordable extra note. The number of complete notes is rounded down.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** A traveller changes £600 at £1 = 25000 dong and wants 200000 dong notes. What is the greatest number of notes?

**Back:** 600 × 25000 = 15000000 dong.15000000 ÷ 200000 = 75 notes.

**Choices:**

- 600 × 25000 = 15000000 dong.15000000 ÷ 200000 = 75 notes. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying £600 by 25000 gives 15000000 dong. Dividing this total by 200000 dong per note gives exactly 75 complete notes.
- 600 × 2500 = 1500000 dong.1500000 ÷ 200000 = 7 notes.
  - _why:_ The exchange rate has lost a zero, making the dong total ten times too small. Using 25000 dong per pound gives 15000000 dong and 75 notes.
- 600 × 25000 = 15000000 dong.15000000 ÷ 20000 = 750 notes.
  - _why:_ The note value has lost a zero, so the number of notes becomes ten times too large. Dividing by 200000 dong gives 75 notes.
- 600 ÷ 25000 = 0.024 dong.0.024 ÷ 200000 = 0 notes.
  - _why:_ This divides by the exchange rate instead of multiplying, reversing the conversion. £600 gives 15000000 dong, which can be exchanged for 75 notes.
