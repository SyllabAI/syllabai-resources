## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Name the line segment that goes from the centre of a circle to its edge.

**Back:** Radius

**Choices:**

- Radius ✔
  - _why:_ A radius is a line segment joining the centre of a circle to any point on its circumference.
- Diameter
  - _why:_ A diameter extends from one edge to the opposite edge through the centre. The segment from the centre to one edge is the radius.
- Chord
  - _why:_ A chord joins two points on the circumference and does not have to pass through the centre. A radius joins the centre to the circle’s edge.
- Tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent is a straight line touching the circle at exactly one point. A radius is the segment running from the centre to the edge.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Name the straight line that passes through the centre from edge to edge.

**Back:** Diameter

**Choices:**

- Diameter ✔
  - _why:_ A diameter is a straight line segment joining two points on the circumference and passing through the centre of the circle.
- Radius
  - _why:_ A radius runs only from the centre to one edge, so it is half this length. The full edge-to-edge line through the centre is the diameter.
- Tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent touches the circumference at only one point and does not cross the circle. The edge-to-edge line through the centre is the diameter.
- Arc
  - _why:_ An arc is a curved section of the circumference, not a straight line through the circle. The required straight line is the diameter.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the name for a straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point?

**Back:** Tangent

**Choices:**

- Tangent ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent is a straight line that meets a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact.
- Chord
  - _why:_ A chord has both endpoints on the circumference, so it meets the circle at two points. A line touching at exactly one point is a tangent.
- Secant
  - _why:_ A secant passes through a circle and intersects its circumference at two points. A straight line meeting it at only one point is a tangent.
- Radius
  - _why:_ A radius is a segment from the centre to the circumference, rather than a line touching the circle externally. The required line is a tangent.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** The diameter is twice the length of the [ ___ ] ($d = 2r$).

**Back:** The diameter is twice the length of the radius ($d = 2r$).

**Choices:**

- radius ✔
  - _why:_ The diameter crosses the full width of the circle through its centre, while the radius covers half that distance, so $d = 2r$.
- diameter
  - _why:_ This incorrectly says the diameter is twice itself. The diameter is twice the radius because two radii placed end to end form a diameter.
- circumference
  - _why:_ The circumference is the distance around the circle, not half its diameter. The diameter is twice the radius, giving the relationship $d = 2r$.
- chord
  - _why:_ A chord can have many different lengths depending on its position, so the diameter is not always twice a chord. It is specifically twice the radius.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** State the formula for circumference using the diameter ($d$).

**Back:** $$C = \pi d$

**Choices:**

- $$C = \pi d$ ✔
  - _why:_ A circle’s circumference is found by multiplying its diameter by $\pi$, so the formula using diameter is $C = \pi d$.
- $$C = 2\pi d$
  - _why:_ The factor of $2$ is used when the formula contains the radius, because $d = 2r$. Using diameter directly gives $C = \pi d$.
- $$C = \frac{\pi d}{2}$
  - _why:_ Dividing by $2$ confuses the diameter with the radius and produces only half the circumference. Using the diameter directly, the correct formula is $C = \pi d$.
- $$C = \pi d^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring a length is associated with area, not circumference, and this expression also lacks the area formula’s factor of one quarter. Circumference is $C = \pi d$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** State the formula for circumference using the radius ($r$).

**Back:** $$C = 2\pi r$

**Choices:**

- $$C = 2\pi r$ ✔
  - _why:_ Since the diameter equals $2r$ and $C = \pi d$, substituting $d = 2r$ gives the radius form $C = 2\pi r$.
- $$C = \pi r$
  - _why:_ This misses the factor of $2$ needed to convert the radius into the diameter, so it gives half the circumference. The correct formula is $C = 2\pi r$.
- $$C = 2\pi r^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring the radius changes the quantity from a length to an area, so this cannot represent circumference. The correct linear formula is $C = 2\pi r$.
- $$C = \pi r^2$
  - _why:_ This is the formula for the area enclosed by a circle, not the distance around its edge. The circumference using radius is $C = 2\pi r$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** State the formula for the area of a circle.

**Back:** $$A = \pi r^2$

**Choices:**

- $$A = \pi r^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ A circle’s area is found by squaring its radius and multiplying by pi, so the correct formula is $A = \pi r^2$.
- $$A = 2\pi r$
  - _why:_ This is the formula for circumference, not area. Area measures the space inside the circle and is calculated using $A = \pi r^2$.
- $$A = \pi r$
  - _why:_ This omits the essential step of squaring the radius. The area of a circle is calculated using $A = \pi r^2$.
- $$A = \pi d^2$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly squares the diameter as though it were the radius. The correct formula uses the radius: $A = \pi r^2$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What must be done to the diameter before it can be used in the area formula $A = \pi r^2$?

**Back:** Halve it to find the radius ($r = \frac{d}{2}$).

**Choices:**

- Halve it to find the radius ($r = \frac{d}{2}$). ✔
  - _why:_ The radius is half the diameter, so the diameter must be divided by 2 before substituting into the area formula $A = \pi r^2$.
- Double it to find the radius ($r = 2d$).
  - _why:_ This reverses the relationship between radius and diameter. The diameter is twice the radius, so it must be halved: $r = \frac{d}{2}$.
- Square it to find the radius ($r = d^2$).
  - _why:_ Squaring is applied to the radius within the area formula, not when converting diameter to radius. First use $r = \frac{d}{2}$.
- Use it directly as the radius ($r = d$).
  - _why:_ This confuses the diameter with the radius and makes the calculated area four times too large. The diameter must first be halved using $r = \frac{d}{2}$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the circumference of a circle with diameter 16 mm in terms of $\pi$.

**Back:** $$16\pi$ mm

**Choices:**

- $$16\pi$ mm ✔
  - _why:_ Circumference can be calculated using $C = \pi d$. Substituting the diameter of 16 mm gives $C = \pi \times 16 = 16\pi$ mm.
- $$8\pi$ mm
  - _why:_ This unnecessarily halves the diameter before using $C = \pi d$. Since the given diameter is 16 mm, the circumference is $16\pi$ mm.
- $$32\pi$ mm
  - _why:_ This doubles the diameter, confusing $C = \pi d$ with the equivalent radius formula $C = 2\pi r$. The correct circumference is $16\pi$ mm.
- $$256\pi$ mm
  - _why:_ This squares the diameter, incorrectly applying an area-style calculation to circumference. Using $C = \pi d$ gives a circumference of $16\pi$ mm.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the area of a circle with radius 9 cm in terms of $\pi$.

**Back:** $$81\pi$ $cm^2$

**Choices:**

- $$81\pi$ $cm^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Using $A = \pi r^2$ with $r = 9$ gives $A = \pi \times 9^2 = 81\pi$ $cm^2$.
- $$18\pi$ $cm^2$
  - _why:_ This uses $2\pi r$, which calculates circumference rather than area. Squaring the radius in $A = \pi r^2$ gives $81\pi$ $cm^2$.
- $$9\pi$ $cm^2$
  - _why:_ This multiplies the radius by pi without squaring it. Since $9^2 = 81$, the correct area is $81\pi$ $cm^2$.
- $$162\pi$ $cm^2$
  - _why:_ This doubles the correct area, often from adding an unnecessary factor of 2. The formula is $A = \pi r^2$, giving $81\pi$ $cm^2$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Explain the strategy for finding the shaded area between a large circle and a smaller circle inside it.

**Back:** Subtract the area of the smaller circle from the area of the larger circle.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the area of the smaller circle from the area of the larger circle. ✔
  - _why:_ The shaded region is the part of the larger circle not occupied by the smaller circle, so its area is found using larger area minus smaller area.
- Add the area of the smaller circle to the area of the larger circle.
  - _why:_ Adding counts the inner circle as extra area rather than removing it. The shaded area is the larger circle’s area minus the smaller circle’s area.
- Subtract the area of the larger circle from the area of the smaller circle.
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction produces a negative value because the larger circle has greater area. Subtract the smaller circle’s area from the larger circle’s area.
- Divide the area of the larger circle by the area of the smaller circle.
  - _why:_ Division gives a ratio between the two areas, not the area of the shaded region. Find each area, then subtract the smaller from the larger.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** When calculating the number of boxes or bags needed to cover an area, always [ ___ ] to the nearest whole number.

**Back:** When calculating the number of boxes or bags needed to cover an area, always round up to the nearest whole number.

**Choices:**

- round up ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction of a box or bag cannot usually be purchased, so any non-whole result must be increased to the next whole number to ensure enough coverage.
- round down
  - _why:_ Rounding down would leave part of the area uncovered whenever the calculation is not a whole number. The required number must therefore be rounded up.
- round normally
  - _why:_ Ordinary rounding can reduce values with decimal parts below $0.5$, leaving insufficient materials. Boxes or bags must always be rounded up to the next whole number.
- truncate
  - _why:_ Truncating simply removes the decimal part, which gives too few boxes or bags for complete coverage. The result must instead be rounded up.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the standard units for the area of a circle?

**Back:** Square units (e.g., $cm^2$ or $m^2$)

**Choices:**

- Square units (e.g., $cm^2$ or $m^2$) ✔
  - _why:_ Area measures a two-dimensional surface, so its units are squared. Circle areas are therefore expressed in square units such as $cm^2$ or $m^2$.
- Linear units (e.g., $cm$ or $m$)
  - _why:_ Linear units measure one-dimensional quantities such as radius or circumference. Because area is two-dimensional, it must use square units such as $cm^2$ or $m^2$.
- Cubic units (e.g., $cm^3$ or $m^3$)
  - _why:_ Cubic units measure three-dimensional volume, not the surface of a circle. Circle area is measured in square units such as $cm^2$ or $m^2$.
- Angular units (e.g., degrees or radians)
  - _why:_ Degrees and radians measure angles rather than area. The area of a circle is a two-dimensional quantity measured in square units such as $cm^2$ or $m^2$.
