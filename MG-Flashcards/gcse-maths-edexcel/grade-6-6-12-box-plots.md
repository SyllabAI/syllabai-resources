## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Name the five values required for the "five-number summary" of a box plot.

**Back:** MinimumLower Quartile ($Q_1$)Median ($Q_2$)Upper Quartile ($Q_3$)Maximum

**Choices:**

- MinimumLower Quartile ($Q_1$)Median ($Q_2$)Upper Quartile ($Q_3$)Maximum ✔
  - _why:_ These five values divide the ordered data into four sections and identify both extremes, providing all the positions needed to construct a box plot.
- MinimumLower Quartile ($Q_1$)MeanUpper Quartile ($Q_3$)Maximum
  - _why:_ The mean is not part of a box plot’s five-number summary. The middle value is the median, alongside the minimum, lower quartile, upper quartile and maximum.
- RangeLower Quartile ($Q_1$)Median ($Q_2$)Upper Quartile ($Q_3$)IQR
  - _why:_ The range and IQR are calculated measures rather than positions in the five-number summary. The minimum and maximum must be used with the three quartiles.
- MinimumLower Quartile ($Q_1$)ModeUpper Quartile ($Q_3$)Maximum
  - _why:_ The mode identifies the most frequent value but is not shown by a standard box plot. The median is the central value in the five-number summary.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Which features of a box plot represent the minimum and maximum values?

**Back:** The whiskers (the ends of the lines extending from the box).

**Choices:**

- The whiskers (the ends of the lines extending from the box). ✔
  - _why:_ The whiskers extend from the box to the two extreme values, with one end showing the minimum and the other showing the maximum.
- The edges (the sides forming the boundary of the box).
  - _why:_ The edges of the box represent the lower and upper quartiles, not the extremes. The minimum and maximum are represented by the ends of the whiskers.
- The quartiles (the points dividing the data into four groups).
  - _why:_ Quartiles divide ordered data into four sections but do not both represent the extreme values. The minimum and maximum appear at the whiskers’ ends.
- The median line (the line drawn inside the box).
  - _why:_ The line inside the box represents only the median, not the two extreme values. The whiskers show the minimum and maximum at their endpoints.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the range calculated from a box plot?

**Back:** $$$\text{Maximum} - \text{Minimum}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\text{Maximum} - \text{Minimum}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The range measures the full spread from the smallest value to the largest, so it is calculated by subtracting the minimum from the maximum.
- $$$\text{Minimum} - \text{Maximum}$$
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction usually produces a negative result, but spread cannot be negative. The range is the maximum value minus the minimum value.
- $$$\text{Maximum} + \text{Minimum}$$
  - _why:_ Adding the extreme values does not measure the distance between them. The range is found by subtracting the minimum from the maximum.
- $$$\text{Upper Quartile} - \text{Lower Quartile}$$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the lower quartile from the upper quartile gives the IQR, which measures the middle 50%. The range is maximum minus minimum.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the Interquartile Range (IQR) calculated from a box plot?

**Back:** $$$Q_3 - Q_1$$ (Upper Quartile - Lower Quartile)

**Choices:**

- $$$Q_3 - Q_1$$ (Upper Quartile - Lower Quartile) ✔
  - _why:_ The IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data, extending from the lower quartile $Q_1$ to the upper quartile $Q_3$.
- $$$Q_1 - Q_3$$ (Lower Quartile - Upper Quartile)
  - _why:_ The subtraction has been reversed, which would produce a negative result. The IQR is the upper quartile minus the lower quartile, $Q_3-Q_1$.
- $$$Q_3 + Q_1$$ (Upper Quartile + Lower Quartile)
  - _why:_ Adding the quartiles does not measure the distance between them. The IQR is found by subtracting the lower quartile from the upper quartile.
- $$$Q_2 - Q_1$$ (Median - Lower Quartile)
  - _why:_ This calculates only the spread across one quarter of the data. The full IQR spans the middle 50%, from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is the IQR often considered a more useful measure of spread than the range?

**Back:** It ignores the most extreme values (outliers).

**Choices:**

- It ignores the most extreme values (outliers). ✔
  - _why:_ The IQR uses only the middle 50% of the data, so unusually high or low values have little effect on this measure of spread.
- It includes the most extreme values (outliers).
  - _why:_ Including extreme values is a feature of the range and makes it sensitive to outliers. The IQR is often preferable because it ignores those extremes.
- It identifies the most frequent value (the mode).
  - _why:_ The mode measures frequency rather than spread and cannot be identified reliably from a box plot. The IQR is useful because it ignores extreme values.
- It calculates the average of all values (the mean).
  - _why:_ The mean measures central tendency and is not used to calculate the IQR. The IQR measures spread while reducing the influence of extreme values.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find $Q_1$ if given the IQR and $Q_3$, use the formula $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** To find $Q_1$ if given the IQR and $Q_3$, use the formula $\boxed{Q_1 = Q_3 - \text{IQR}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$Q_1 = Q_3 - \text{IQR}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The IQR is calculated using $\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1$. Rearranging this formula by adding $Q_1$ and subtracting the IQR gives $Q_1=Q_3-\text{IQR}$.
- $$Q_1 = Q_3 + \text{IQR}$
  - _why:_ Adding the IQR would place $Q_1$ above $Q_3$, reversing the correct quartile order. Since $\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1$, the correct rearrangement is $Q_1=Q_3-\text{IQR}$.
- $$Q_1 = \text{IQR} - Q_3$
  - _why:_ This subtraction is reversed and will often produce a negative value. Rearranging $\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1$ correctly gives $Q_1=Q_3-\text{IQR}$.
- $$Q_1 = Q_3 \times \text{IQR}$
  - _why:_ The IQR is the difference between the quartiles, not a scale factor to multiply by. Subtracting the IQR from $Q_3$ gives the correct formula $Q_1=Q_3-\text{IQR}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** When finding quartiles from an odd number of raw data values, where is the median placed?

**Back:** The median is excluded from both the lower and upper halves of the data.

**Choices:**

- The median is excluded from both the lower and upper halves of the data. ✔
  - _why:_ With an odd number of ordered values, the median is the single central value. It is left out when forming the lower and upper halves used to find the quartiles.
- The median is included in both the lower and upper halves of the data.
  - _why:_ Including the central value in both halves is a common quartile-position error. For an odd number of values, the median is excluded from both the lower and upper halves.
- The median is included in the lower half but excluded from the upper half of the data.
  - _why:_ Putting the median into only the lower half makes the two halves unequal and shifts the lower quartile. The median must be excluded from both halves.
- The median is included in the upper half but excluded from the lower half of the data.
  - _why:_ Putting the median into only the upper half makes the two halves unequal and shifts the upper quartile. The median must be excluded from both halves.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What two features must be mentioned when comparing two box plots?

**Back:** The medians (average/typical value)The spread (usually the IQR or range)

**Choices:**

- The medians (average/typical value)The spread (usually the IQR or range) ✔
  - _why:_ The medians compare the typical values of the datasets, while the IQRs or ranges compare their spread. A complete box-plot comparison must discuss both centre and spread.
- The minimums (smallest value)The maximums (largest value)
  - _why:_ Minimums and maximums identify the endpoints but do not compare typical values directly. A complete comparison should discuss the medians and a measure of spread, usually the IQR or range.
- The quartiles (middle 50% values)The sample sizes (number of values)
  - _why:_ Quartiles and sample sizes do not directly provide the required comparison of centre and spread. The two features that must be discussed are the medians and the IQRs or ranges.
- The ranges (overall spread)The outliers (unusual values)
  - _why:_ Range describes spread, but outliers do not replace a comparison of typical values. A complete answer must compare the medians as well as the spread, using the IQR or range.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** When comparing test scores, what does a higher median indicate?

**Back:** That group generally scored higher/performed better.

**Choices:**

- That group generally scored higher/performed better. ✔
  - _why:_ The median represents the middle test score after the results are ordered. A higher median therefore shows that the group’s typical score was higher, indicating generally better performance.
- That group generally scored lower/performed worse.
  - _why:_ This reverses the interpretation of the median. In a test where larger scores are better, a higher median means the group typically scored higher and therefore performed better.
- That group generally varied less/performed more consistently.
  - _why:_ Consistency is measured using spread, such as the IQR or range, rather than the median. A higher median indicates generally higher test scores, not less variation.

  - _why:_ A higher median describes the typical score, not how widely the scores vary. Variation is compared using the IQR or range; the higher median indicates generally better performance.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** If $Q_3 = 41$ and $Q_1 = 26$, calculate the IQR.

**Back:** $$$41 - 26 = 15$$

**Choices:**

- $$$41 - 26 = 15$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The interquartile range is calculated using $Q_3-Q_1$. Subtracting the lower quartile, $26$, from the upper quartile, $41$, gives an IQR of $15$.
- $$$41 + 26 = 67$$
  - _why:_ Adding the quartiles does not measure the spread of the middle half of the data. The IQR is found by subtracting: $Q_3-Q_1=41-26=15$.
- $$$26 - 41 = -15$$
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and produces a negative spread, which an IQR cannot have. Subtract the lower quartile from the upper quartile: $41-26=15$.
- $$$41 - 26 = 14$$
  - _why:_ This is an arithmetic slip, often caused by miscounting the difference inclusively. Direct subtraction gives $41-26=15$, so the interquartile range is $15$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If the maximum is 183 and the range is 96, calculate the minimum.

**Back:** $$$183 - 96 = 87$$

**Choices:**

- $$$183 - 96 = 87$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The range equals maximum minus minimum, so rearranging gives minimum equals maximum minus range. Therefore, subtracting $96$ from $183$ gives a minimum of $87$.
- $$$183 + 96 = 279$$
  - _why:_ Adding the range would produce a value above the stated maximum. Since range equals maximum minus minimum, the minimum is found using $183-96=87$.
- $$$96 - 183 = -87$$
  - _why:_ This reverses the required subtraction and gives an unsuitable negative minimum. Rearranging maximum minus minimum equals range gives minimum equals maximum minus range, so $183-96=87$.
- $$$183 - 96 = 89$$
  - _why:_ The correct method has been chosen, but the subtraction contains an arithmetic error. Borrowing carefully gives $183-96=87$, so the minimum is $87$.
