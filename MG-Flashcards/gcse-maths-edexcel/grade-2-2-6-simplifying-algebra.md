## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the term $5x$, what is the coefficient?

**Back:** 5

**Choices:**

- 5 ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying the variable. In $5x$, the number multiplying $x$ is 5, so 5 is the coefficient.
- $$x$
  - _why:_ This confuses the variable with the coefficient. The letter $x$ is the variable, while the number multiplying it is 5, making 5 the coefficient.
- $$5x$
  - _why:_ This gives the entire algebraic term rather than its coefficient. A coefficient is only the numerical factor, so the coefficient of $5x$ is 5.
- 1
  - _why:_ A coefficient of 1 is implied when a variable appears alone, as in $x$. Here, $x$ is explicitly multiplied by 5, so the coefficient is 5.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a constant in an algebraic expression?

**Back:** A number on its own with no letter attached (e.g. 4 or -7).

**Choices:**

- A number on its own with no letter attached (e.g. 4 or -7). ✔
  - _why:_ A constant has a fixed numerical value and contains no variable. Therefore, standalone numbers such as 4 and -7 are constants in an algebraic expression.
- A letter on its own with no number attached (e.g. $x$ or $y$).
  - _why:_ A letter represents a variable because its value can change. A constant is instead a number with no letter attached, such as 4 or -7.
- A number multiplying a letter in a term (e.g. 4 in $4x$).
  - _why:_ A number multiplying a variable is a coefficient, not a constant term. A constant appears on its own with no letter attached, such as 4 or -7.
- A term containing the same letter and power (e.g. $4x$ or $-7x$).
  - _why:_ Terms with matching letters and powers are like terms, not constants. A constant is a standalone number with no letter attached, such as 4 or -7.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Which part of a term must be identical for two terms to be considered 'like terms'?

**Back:** The letter part (including the power).

**Choices:**

- The letter part (including the power). ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms must contain exactly the same variables raised to the same powers. Their numerical coefficients may differ, so $3x^2$ and $-5x^2$ are like terms.
- The coefficient (including the sign).
  - _why:_ Like terms may have different coefficients and signs; for example, $3x$ and $-7x$ are like terms. Their letter parts, including powers, must be identical.
- The total value (including the coefficient).
  - _why:_ Terms do not need equal values to be like terms. They must instead have identical letter parts and powers, as with $2a^3$ and $9a^3$.
- The number part (including the sign).
  - _why:_ Matching number parts do not make terms alike; $4x$ and $4y$ are not like terms. The letter part, including every power, must be identical.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** Simplify $r + r + r + r = \text{[...]}.

**Back:** Simplify $r + r + r + r = \boxed{4r}.

**Choices:**

- $$4r$ ✔
  - _why:_ The four terms are identical, so their coefficients add: $1+1+1+1=4$. Therefore, four lots of $r$ simplify to $4r$.
- $$r^4$
  - _why:_ This confuses repeated addition with repeated multiplication. Adding four identical $r$ terms gives $4r$; $r^4$ would result from multiplying $r$ by itself four times.
- $$5r$
  - _why:_ This off-by-one error counts five copies of $r$, but the expression contains only four. Adding their coefficients gives $1+1+1+1=4$, so the result is $4r$.
- $$4+r$
  - _why:_ The number of identical terms becomes a coefficient rather than a separate constant. Four copies of $r$ combine as $4r$, not as an addition of 4 and $r$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $6t + 3t - 4t$.

**Back:** $$5t$

**Choices:**

- $$5t$ ✔
  - _why:_ All three terms are like terms, so their coefficients combine: $6+3-4=5$. Keeping the common variable $t$ gives the simplified expression $5t$.
- $$13t$
  - _why:_ This treats the negative coefficient as positive and adds all three magnitudes. The coefficients must be calculated as $6+3-4=5$, giving $5t$.
- $$7t$
  - _why:_ This likely subtracts 3 instead of 4 or mishandles the signs. Combining the coefficients correctly gives $6+3-4=5$, so the result is $5t$.
- $$5$
  - _why:_ This correctly combines the coefficients but drops the variable. Since every term contains $t$, the simplified result must retain that common letter, giving $5t$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In the expression $9a - 2a$, which term does the minus sign belong to?

**Back:** The term following it ($-2a$).

**Choices:**

- The term following it ($-2a$). ✔
  - _why:_ A subtraction sign forms part of the coefficient of the term immediately following it. Therefore, the second term is negative and should be treated as $-2a$.
- The term before it ($9a$).
  - _why:_ A minus sign applies to the term that follows it, not the term before it. Thus, $9a$ is positive and the following term is $-2a$.
- Both terms together ($9a-2a$).
  - _why:_ The minus sign does not make the whole expression negative. It belongs to the following term, giving a positive first term $9a$ and a negative second term $-2a$.
- The letter only ($-a$).
  - _why:_ The sign belongs to the entire following term, including its numerical coefficient. The negative term is $-2a$, rather than the minus sign applying only to the letter.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Choices:**

- $$5g$ ✔
  - _why:_ The $g$ terms are $8g$ and $-3g$. Subtracting their coefficients gives $8-3=5$, so they combine to make $5g$.
- $$11g$
  - _why:_ This ignores the minus sign and adds 3 to 8. The second $g$ term is $-3g$, so the correct calculation is $8-3=5$, giving $5g$.
- $$5$
  - _why:_ This combines the coefficients correctly but drops the variable. Because both collected terms contain $g$, the result must also contain $g$, giving $5g$.
- $$-5g$
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and calculates $3-8$ rather than $8-3$. Following the original order gives $8g-3g=5g$, which is positive.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $5a + 6 - 2a + 3$.

**Back:** $$3a + 9$

**Choices:**

- $$3a + 9$ ✔
  - _why:_ The like terms $5a$ and $-2a$ combine to give $3a$, while the constants $6$ and $3$ combine to give $9$.
- $$7a + 9$
  - _why:_ This treats $-2a$ as positive and adds the coefficients. Keeping its negative sign gives $5a-2a=3a$, so the correct simplification is $3a+9$.
- $$3a + 3$
  - _why:_ This subtracts the constants instead of adding them. Both constants are positive, so $6+3=9$ and the expression simplifies to $3a+9$.
- $$12a$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly combines constants with terms containing $a$. Only like terms can be collected, giving $5a-2a=3a$ and $6+3=9$, so the answer is $3a+9$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can the expression $3a + 4b$ not be simplified?

**Back:** They are unlike terms (different letter parts).

**Choices:**

- They are unlike terms (different letter parts). ✔
  - _why:_ Terms can be added or subtracted only when their letter parts match. Since one term contains $a$ and the other contains $b$, they cannot be collected.
- They have different coefficients (3 and 4).
  - _why:_ Different coefficients do not prevent simplification; for example, $3a+4a=7a$. The expression cannot be simplified because $a$ and $b$ make the terms unlike.
- They contain two variables (letters cannot be added).
  - _why:_ Expressions containing two variables can sometimes be simplified when like terms occur. Here, $3a$ and $4b$ cannot combine specifically because their letter parts are different.
- They are already factorised (nothing can be combined).
  - _why:_ The expression is not written as a product, so it is not factorised. It cannot be collected because $3a$ and $4b$ are unlike terms with different letter parts.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebra, $a \times b$ is written as $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** In algebra, $a \times b$ is written as $\boxed{ab}$.

**Choices:**

- $$ab$ ✔
  - _why:_ In algebra, the multiplication sign between letters is conventionally omitted. Writing the letters next to each other gives $ab$, which means $a$ multiplied by $b$.
- $$a+b$
  - _why:_ A plus sign represents addition rather than multiplication. Algebraic multiplication is written by placing the letters together, so $a\times b$ is written as $ab$.
- $$a-b$
  - _why:_ A minus sign represents subtraction, not multiplication. The standard algebraic notation omits the multiplication sign and writes the two factors together as $ab$.
- $$\frac{a}{b}$
  - _why:_ Writing $a$ over $b$ represents division of $a$ by $b$. For multiplication, the letters are placed next to each other, giving $ab$.

**Back:** $$20x$

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ Multiply the numerical factors to get $5\times4=20$, then retain the variable $x$. Therefore, $5\times4x$ simplifies to $20x$.
- $$9x$
  - _why:_ This comes from adding $5$ and $4$ instead of multiplying them. The operation is multiplication, so $5\times4=20$ and the correct result is $20x$.

- $$20x^2$
  - _why:_ This introduces an extra factor of $x$. Only one $x$ appears in the original expression, so its power remains $1$ and the result is $20x$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the common mistake when simplifying $2a \times 5b$.

**Back:** Adding the coefficients instead of multiplying them (result should be $10ab$, not $7ab$).

**Choices:**

- Adding the coefficients instead of multiplying them (result should be $10ab$, not $7ab$). ✔
  - _why:_ When multiplying algebraic terms, their coefficients must also be multiplied. Since $2\times5=10$ and $a\times b=ab$, the correct product is $10ab$, not $7ab$.
- Subtracting the coefficients instead of multiplying them (result should be $10ab$, not $3ab$).
  - _why:_ Although subtraction would also be an invalid operation, the stated common error producing $7ab$ comes from adding $2$ and $5$. The coefficients should be multiplied to give $10ab$.
- Multiplying the coefficients but adding the letters (result should be $10ab$, not $10a+10b$).
  - _why:_ Adding the letters is an error, but it does not explain the specific incorrect result $7ab$. That result comes from adding the coefficients instead of multiplying them; the answer is $10ab$.
- Adding the coefficients and the letters separately (result should be $10ab$, not $7a+7b$).
  - _why:_ This describes a different incorrect method and result. The identified mistake is adding only the coefficients to obtain $7ab$; multiplying $2$ and $5$ correctly gives $10ab$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a power (or index) indicate in algebra?

**Back:** How many times a letter is multiplied by itself.

**Choices:**

- How many times a letter is multiplied by itself. ✔
  - _why:_ A power records repeated multiplication of the same base. For example, $a^3$ means $a\times a\times a$, so there are three factors of $a$.
- How many times a letter is added to itself.
  - _why:_ Repeated addition produces a multiple, not a power; for example, $a+a+a=3a$. A power indicates repeated multiplication, so $a^3=a\times a\times a$.
- The number that is multiplied by the letter.
  - _why:_ The number multiplying a letter is its coefficient, as $3$ is in $3a$. A power instead shows how many times the letter is multiplied by itself.
- How many different letters are multiplied together.
  - _why:_ A power counts repeated factors of the same base, not the number of different variables. For example, $a^3$ contains three factors of $a$.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ There are three identical factors of $z$, so index notation uses an exponent of $3$. Thus, $z\times z\times z$ simplifies to $z^3$.
- $$3z$
  - _why:_ This confuses repeated multiplication with repeated addition. While $z+z+z=3z$, the product of three factors of $z$ is $z^3$.
- $$z^2$
  - _why:_ An exponent of $2$ represents only two factors, $z\times z$. Since the expression contains three factors of $z$, the correct result is $z^3$.
- $$3z^3$
  - _why:_ The exponent already records the three factors of $z$, so no coefficient of $3$ is introduced. The product is $z^3$, with an implied coefficient of $1$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Are $x^2$ and $x$ like terms?

**Back:** No (they have different powers).

**Choices:**

- No (they have different powers). ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms must contain exactly the same variables raised to the same powers. Since $x^2$ has power 2 and $x$ has power 1, they are not like terms.
- Yes (they have the same variable).
  - _why:_ Sharing the variable $x$ is not sufficient for terms to be like terms. Their powers must also match, so $x^2$ and $x$ are not like terms.
- Yes (they have consecutive powers).
  - _why:_ Powers being consecutive does not make terms alike. Like terms require identical variable parts, so the different powers in $x^2$ and $x$ mean they cannot be collected.
- No (they have different coefficients).
  - _why:_ Coefficients may differ without preventing terms from being like terms. The real reason is that $x^2$ and $x$ have different powers, so their variable parts do not match.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{6d}{d}$.

**Back:** 6

**Choices:**

- 6 ✔
  - _why:_ For $d \ne 0$, the factor $d$ appears in both numerator and denominator and cancels because $\frac{d}{d} = 1$. This leaves 6.
- $$6d$
  - _why:_ This leaves the common factor $d$ uncancelled. For $d \ne 0$, $\frac{d}{d} = 1$, so $\frac{6d}{d} = 6$.
- 1
  - _why:_ Cancelling $d$ does not also cancel the coefficient 6. Since $\frac{d}{d} = 1$, the expression becomes $6 \times 1 = 6$.
- $$6d^2$
  - _why:_ Division by $d$ reduces the number of factors of $d$ rather than increasing its power. The numerator’s $d$ cancels with the denominator’s $d$, leaving 6.
