## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A percentage means "out of [ ___ ]".

**Back:** A percentage means "out of 100".

**Choices:**

- 100 ✔
  - _why:_ Percent means “per hundred”, so a percentage expresses a number of parts out of a total of 100 equal parts.
- 10
  - _why:_ Confusing percentages with tenths makes 10 seem plausible. A percentage means “out of 100”, because the word percent means “per hundred”.
- 1
  - _why:_ This confuses a percentage with a single part of a whole. Percentages are measured out of 100 equal parts, so the correct denominator is 100.
- 1000
  - _why:_ This adds an unnecessary zero and would describe parts per thousand. A percentage is measured per hundred, so it means “out of 100”.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** 100% represents the [ ___ ].

**Back:** 100% represents the whole amount.

**Choices:**

- whole amount ✔
  - _why:_ All 100 out of 100 equal parts make the complete quantity, so 100% represents the whole amount.
- half amount
  - _why:_ Half of an amount is 50%, not 100%. All 100 percentage parts together represent the whole amount.
- double amount
  - _why:_ Double the original amount is 200%, which is twice the whole. The original whole amount is represented by 100%.
- quarter amount
  - _why:_ A quarter of an amount is 25%, since one whole contains four quarters. The whole amount itself is represented by 100%.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find 10% of an amount, divide by [ ___ ].

**Back:** To find 10% of an amount, divide by 10.

**Choices:**

- 10 ✔
  - _why:_ Ten equal groups of 10% make 100%, so dividing the whole amount by 10 gives one group worth 10%.
- 100
  - _why:_ Dividing by 100 finds 1%, not 10%. Since 10% is one tenth of the whole, the amount must be divided by 10.
- 5
  - _why:_ Dividing by 5 finds one fifth of the amount, which is 20%. To calculate 10%, divide the amount by 10.
- 20
  - _why:_ Dividing by 20 finds one twentieth of the amount, which is 5%. To find one tenth, or 10%, divide by 10.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** If a question asks for 50% of an amount, what operation finds it?

**Back:** Halve the amount.

**Choices:**

- Halve the amount. ✔
  - _why:_ Fifty percent is one half of the whole because $50/100 = 1/2$. Therefore, halving the amount finds 50%.
- Double the amount.
  - _why:_ Doubling an amount gives 200%, not 50%. Since 50% is one half of the whole, the amount should be halved.
- Quarter the amount.
  - _why:_ Quartering finds 25%, because a quarter is $25/100$. Fifty percent is one half, so the amount should instead be halved.
- Divide by 50 the amount.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the percentage number does not generally find that percentage and here gives 2%. Since 50% equals one half, halve the amount.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** If a question asks for 25% of an amount, what operation finds it quickly?

**Back:** Divide by 4.

**Choices:**

- Divide by 4. ✔
  - _why:_ Twenty-five percent equals $25/100 = 1/4$, so dividing the amount by 4 quickly finds 25%.
- Multiply by 4.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 4 gives 400% of the original amount, reversing the required operation. Since 25% is one quarter, divide by 4.
- Divide by 2.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 finds one half, which is 50%. Twenty-five percent is one quarter, so divide the amount by 4.
- Divide by 25.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the percentage number is a common misconception and gives 4% here. Since 25% equals one quarter, divide by 4.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** If you know 10% of an amount, how can you find 5%?

**Back:** Halve the 10% amount.

**Choices:**

- Halve the 10% amount. ✔
  - _why:_ Five percent is half of 10%, so halving the known 10% amount gives the value of 5%.
- Double the 10% amount.
  - _why:_ Doubling the 10% amount produces 20%, not 5%. Since 5% is half of 10%, the known amount should be halved.
- Divide by 5 the 10% amount.
  - _why:_ Dividing the 10% amount by 5 produces 2%, because $10\% \div 5 = 2\%$. To find 5%, halve the 10% amount.
- Multiply by 5 the 10% amount.
  - _why:_ Multiplying the 10% amount by 5 produces 50%, not 5%. To reduce 10% to 5%, halve the known amount.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Find 34% of 2500.

**Back:** 850.

**Choices:**

- 850. ✔
  - _why:_ Thirty percent of 2500 is 750 and 4% is 100. Adding these parts gives $750 + 100 = 850$.
- 85.
  - _why:_ This answer is ten times too small, typically from mishandling the decimal percentage. Using $0.34 \times 2500$ gives the correct value of 850.
- 340.
  - _why:_ This treats the calculation as though the base amount were 1000. For 2500, $0.34 \times 2500 = 850$.
- 8500.
  - _why:_ This answer is ten times too large, usually because 34% was converted incorrectly. Since 34% is $0.34$, multiplying by 2500 gives 850.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** If a percentage is greater than 100%, how does the answer compare with the original?

**Back:** It is bigger than the original.

**Choices:**

- It is bigger than the original. ✔
  - _why:_ A percentage greater than 100% represents more than the whole original amount. For example, 120% is 1.2 times the original, so it is bigger.
- It is smaller than the original.
  - _why:_ Confusing percentages above 100% with a percentage decrease reverses the comparison. More than 100% represents more than one whole, so the answer is bigger than the original.
- It is equal to the original.
  - _why:_ Only 100% is equal to the original amount. A percentage greater than 100% represents more than one whole, so the answer is bigger than the original.
- It is half of the original.
  - _why:_ Half of the original is 50%, not a percentage greater than 100%. More than 100% represents more than one whole, making the answer bigger than the original.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Find 215% of 80.

**Back:** 172.

**Choices:**

- 172. ✔
  - _why:_ Convert 215% to 2.15, then calculate $2.15 \times 80 = 172$. Since 215% exceeds 100%, the result is correctly greater than 80.
- 17.2.
  - _why:_ Dividing by an extra factor of 10 gives 21.5% rather than 215%. The correct calculation is $2.15 \times 80 = 172$.
- 92.
  - _why:_ Adding 15% of 80 to the original finds 115%, not 215%. Since 215% is $2.15 \times 80$, the correct answer is 172.
- 215.
  - _why:_ Treating the percentage itself as the answer ignores the original amount of 80. Convert 215% to 2.15 and multiply by 80 to obtain 172.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you not compare percentage amounts using the percentages alone?

**Back:** The wholes may be different.

**Choices:**

- The wholes may be different. ✔
  - _why:_ A percentage describes a proportion of a whole, so equal percentages can represent different amounts when their wholes differ. The whole must therefore be considered when comparing percentage amounts.
- The percentages may be different.
  - _why:_ The percentages are already known and can be compared directly, but they do not reveal the actual amounts. The wholes may differ, causing a smaller percentage to represent a larger amount.
- The units may be different.
  - _why:_ Different units can prevent a direct comparison, but that is not the key percentage issue here. Percentage amounts cannot be compared alone because their wholes may be different.
- The symbols may be different.
  - _why:_ The symbols used do not determine the size of a percentage amount. The important issue is that percentages are proportions of wholes, and those wholes may be different.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** To find the difference between two amounts, what do you subtract?

**Back:** Subtract the smaller from the larger.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the smaller from the larger. ✔
  - _why:_ Difference means the positive distance between two amounts. Subtracting the smaller amount from the larger amount produces that distance without giving a negative answer.
- Subtract the larger from the smaller.
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction gives a negative result rather than the usual positive difference. To find the difference, subtract the smaller amount from the larger amount.
- Subtract the smaller from the smaller.
  - _why:_ Subtracting an amount from itself always gives zero, which does not measure the gap between two different amounts. Subtract the smaller amount from the larger amount instead.
- Subtract the larger from the total.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the larger amount from their total simply returns the smaller amount, not the gap between them. The difference is found by subtracting the smaller from the larger.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If 40% of people are children, what percentage are adults?

**Back:** 60%.

**Choices:**

- 60%. ✔
  - _why:_ Children and adults make up the full 100%. Subtracting the children's percentage gives $100\% - 40\% = 60\%$, so 60% are adults.
- 40%.
  - _why:_ This repeats the percentage of children rather than finding the remaining percentage. Adults account for $100\% - 40\% = 60\%$ of the people.
- 140%.
  - _why:_ Adding 40% to 100% incorrectly creates more than the entire group. Children and adults together total 100%, so adults make up $100\% - 40\% = 60\%$.
- 50%.
  - _why:_ Assuming the group is divided equally ignores the given 40% of children. The adult percentage is the remainder, calculated as $100\% - 40\% = 60\%$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** If 60% of a total is 54, what is 100%?

**Back:** 90.

**Choices:**

- 90. ✔
  - _why:_ If 60% is 54, then 10% is $54 \div 6 = 9$. Multiplying by 10 gives 100% as $9 \times 10 = 90$.
- 32.4.
  - _why:_ Calculating 60% of 54 treats 54 as the whole, although it is already the 60% amount. Scaling $54 \div 60 \times 100$ gives 90.
- 60.
  - _why:_ Using the percentage number as the total ignores the value represented by 60%. Since 10% is $54 \div 6 = 9$, the whole is 90.
- 84.
  - _why:_ Adding 30 to 54 does not scale 60% up to 100% proportionally. Dividing 54 by 0.6 gives the correct total of 90.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** For a percentage increase, what happens to the percentage amount?

**Back:** It is added to the original.

**Choices:**

- It is added to the original. ✔
  - _why:_ A percentage increase first finds a percentage of the original amount, then adds that amount to the original. This produces a new value greater than the starting value.
- It is subtracted from the original.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the percentage amount describes a percentage decrease, not an increase. For an increase, the calculated percentage amount must be added to the original.
- It is divided by the original.
  - _why:_ Division can help compare two quantities, but it does not apply a percentage increase. The percentage amount is calculated and then added to the original value.
- It is replaced by the original.
  - _why:_ Replacing the percentage amount with the original does not change the value or represent an increase. The percentage amount must be added to the original.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** A coat costs £45 and is reduced by 20%. What is the sale price?

**Back:** £36.

**Choices:**

- £36. ✔
  - _why:_ The discount is $20\% \times £45 = £9$. Subtracting it from the original price gives the sale price: $£45 - £9 = £36$.
- £9.
  - _why:_ This is only the value of the 20% discount, not the sale price. Subtract the £9 discount from £45 to obtain a sale price of £36.
- £54.
  - _why:_ Adding £9 applies a 20% increase rather than a reduction. The £9 discount must be subtracted from £45, giving the correct sale price of £36.
- £25.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 20 directly from £45 confuses 20% with £20. The discount is 20% of £45, which is £9, so the sale price is £36.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** After paying a deposit, how do you find the amount left to pay?

**Back:** Subtract the deposit from the total cost.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the deposit from the total cost. ✔
  - _why:_ The deposit has already been paid towards the purchase, so deducting it from the total cost gives the remaining amount that still needs to be paid.
- Add the deposit to the total cost.
  - _why:_ Adding the deposit counts that payment on top of the full price. The amount left is found by subtracting the deposit from the total cost.
- Subtract the total cost from the deposit.
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and would usually produce a negative value. Subtract the deposit from the total cost to find the outstanding amount.
- Divide the total cost by the deposit.
  - _why:_ Division would compare the total cost with the deposit rather than find a remaining balance. Subtract the deposit from the total cost instead.

**Front:** For 6 cans in a "buy 2 get 1 free" offer, how many cans are paid for?

**Back:** 4 cans.

**Choices:**

- 4 cans. ✔
  - _why:_ Each group of three cans contains two paid cans and one free can. Six cans form two groups, so four cans are paid for.

  - _why:_ This treats every second can as free, like a buy one get one free offer. Here, two of every three cans are paid for, giving four.

  - _why:_ This counts only one free can across the whole purchase. Six cans make two groups of three, so two are free and four are paid for.

  - _why:_ This ignores the offer and charges for every can. Two groups qualify for one free can each, leaving four cans to be paid for.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** Simple interest is worked out from the [ ___ ] each year.

**Back:** Simple interest is worked out from the original amount each year.

**Choices:**

- original amount ✔
  - _why:_ Simple interest is calculated each year using the initial principal only. Previous interest is not added to the amount used in later interest calculations.
- current balance
  - _why:_ Using the current balance means interest is calculated on previously earned interest, which describes compound interest. Simple interest always uses the original amount.
- interest earned
  - _why:_ Interest is not calculated from the interest already earned. For simple interest, the same percentage of the original amount is added each year.
- final amount
  - _why:_ The final amount is only known after the interest has been calculated and added. Simple interest is worked out from the original amount each year.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** £600 is invested for 4 years at 1.5% simple interest per year. What is the total interest?

**Back:** £36.

**Choices:**

- £36. ✔
  - _why:_ One year’s interest is $600\times0.015=£9$. Simple interest adds the same £9 each year, so over four years the total is $4\times£9=£36$.
- £9.
  - _why:_ This is only one year’s interest, found from $600\times0.015$. Multiplying the annual £9 by four years gives the correct total interest of £36.
- £24.
  - _why:_ This effectively uses 1% per year instead of the stated 1.5%. At 1.5%, the annual interest is £9, giving £36 over four years.
- £360.
  - _why:_ This treats 1.5% as 15%, making the interest ten times too large. Using $1.5\%=0.015$ gives £9 per year and £36 altogether.
