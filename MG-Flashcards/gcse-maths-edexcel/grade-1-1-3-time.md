# Time Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 1 · 22 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** How many minutes are in 1 hour?

**Back:** 60 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 60 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ An hour is divided into 60 equal minutes, so 1 hour contains exactly 60 minutes.
- 100 minutes.
  - _why:_ This treats time as a base-10 measurement. Time uses 60 minutes per hour, so 1 hour is 60 minutes.
- 30 minutes.
  - _why:_ Thirty minutes is half an hour, not a whole hour. A complete hour contains 60 minutes.
- 24 minutes.
  - _why:_ This confuses the 24 hours in a day with the number of minutes in an hour. There are 60 minutes in 1 hour.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** To convert hours to minutes, [ ___ ]; to convert minutes to hours, [ ___ ].

**Back:** To convert hours to minutes, multiply by 60; to convert minutes to hours, divide by 60.

**Choices:**

- To convert hours to minutes, multiply by 60; to convert minutes to hours, divide by 60. ✔
  - _why:_ Each hour contains 60 minutes, so multiplying converts hours into minutes. Dividing by 60 reverses the conversion and gives the number of hours.
- To convert hours to minutes, divide by 60; to convert minutes to hours, multiply by 60.
  - _why:_ The operations have been reversed. Hours must be multiplied by 60 to find minutes, while minutes must be divided by 60 to find hours.
- To convert hours to minutes, multiply by 100; to convert minutes to hours, divide by 100.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats time as a decimal measurement. There are 60 minutes in an hour, so the conversion factor must be 60, not 100.
- To convert hours to minutes, multiply by 24; to convert minutes to hours, divide by 24.
  - _why:_ Twenty-four is the number of hours in a day, not minutes in an hour. Hour-minute conversions use multiplication or division by 60.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Change 240 minutes to hours.

**Back:** 4 hours.

**Choices:**

- 4 hours. ✔
  - _why:_ Converting minutes to hours requires division by 60. Since $240 \div 60 = 4$, 240 minutes equals 4 hours.
- 6 hours.
  - _why:_ This does not divide the minutes into groups of 60 correctly. Since $240 \div 60 = 4$, the time is 4 hours.
- 3 hours.
  - _why:_ Three hours contains only $3 \times 60 = 180$ minutes. Dividing 240 minutes by 60 gives the correct total of 4 hours.
- 2 hours.
  - _why:_ Two hours contains only $2 \times 60 = 120$ minutes, which is half the given time. The correct calculation is $240 \div 60 = 4$ hours.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How many minutes is half an hour?

**Back:** 30 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 30 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ Half an hour means half of 60 minutes. Calculating $60 \div 2$ gives 30 minutes.
- 15 minutes.
  - _why:_ Fifteen minutes is a quarter of an hour, not half. Half of the 60 minutes in an hour is $60 \div 2 = 30$ minutes.
- 50 minutes.
  - _why:_ This treats half an hour as 0.5 of 100 minutes. Time is based on 60 minutes per hour, so half is 30 minutes.
- 20 minutes.
  - _why:_ Twenty minutes is one third of an hour, not one half. Dividing 60 minutes by 2 gives the correct answer of 30 minutes.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How many minutes is a quarter of an hour?

**Back:** 15 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 15 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ A quarter of an hour is one of four equal parts of 60 minutes. Therefore, $60 \div 4 = 15$ minutes.
- 20 minutes.
  - _why:_ Twenty minutes is one third of an hour. A quarter requires dividing the 60 minutes in an hour by 4, which gives 15 minutes.
- 30 minutes.
  - _why:_ Thirty minutes is half an hour, not a quarter. Dividing 60 minutes into four equal parts gives 15 minutes per quarter.
- 25 minutes.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats an hour as 100 minutes and finds one quarter of 100. An hour has 60 minutes, and $60 \div 4 = 15$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** How many minutes is three quarters of an hour?

**Back:** 45 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 45 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ One quarter of 60 minutes is 15 minutes. Multiplying by 3 gives $15 \times 3 = 45$ minutes for three quarters of an hour.
- 75 minutes.
  - _why:_ This treats three quarters as 0.75 of 100 minutes. An hour has 60 minutes, so $\frac{3}{4} \times 60 = 45$ minutes.
- 40 minutes.
  - _why:_ Forty minutes is two thirds of an hour, not three quarters. Three quarters of 60 is found by calculating $60 \div 4 \times 3 = 45$.
- 50 minutes.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly divides an hour into decimal-style portions. Three quarters means three lots of 15 minutes, giving a total of 45 minutes.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Change $1\frac{1}{4}$ hours to minutes.

**Back:** 75 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 75 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ One hour is 60 minutes and one quarter of an hour is 15 minutes. Adding them gives $60 + 15 = 75$ minutes.
- 90 minutes.
  - _why:_ Ninety minutes equals one and a half hours, so the fractional part has been treated as a half. A quarter-hour is 15 minutes, giving 75 minutes.
- 125 minutes.
  - _why:_ This reads $1\frac{1}{4}$ hours as 1.25 of 100 minutes. Time uses 60 minutes per hour, so $1.25 \times 60 = 75$ minutes.
- 70 minutes.
  - _why:_ This treats a quarter of an hour as 10 minutes. Since $60 \div 4 = 15$, the total is $60 + 15 = 75$ minutes.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** Before comparing time durations, put both durations in the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Before comparing time durations, put both durations in the same unit.

**Choices:**

- same unit ✔
  - _why:_ Using the same unit makes the numerical values directly comparable. For example, converting both durations to minutes allows an accurate comparison.
- different units
  - _why:_ Comparing values in different units can give a misleading result. Both durations must first be expressed in the same unit, such as minutes, before their sizes are compared.
- nearest hour
  - _why:_ Rounding each duration to the nearest hour loses information and may change which is longer. Convert both durations to the same unit without rounding.
- original form
  - _why:_ Leaving durations in their original forms may mean comparing hours with minutes. They should be converted into the same unit so the comparison is valid.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the difference between 40 minutes and $1\frac{1}{4}$ hours.

**Back:** 35 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 35 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ One hour and a quarter equals $60+15=75$ minutes. The difference between 75 minutes and 40 minutes is therefore $75-40=35$ minutes.
- 45 minutes.
  - _why:_ This comes from treating a quarter of an hour as 25 minutes. A quarter-hour is 15 minutes, so $1\frac{1}{4}$ hours is 75 minutes and the difference is 35 minutes.
- 25 minutes.
  - _why:_ This results from converting $1\frac{1}{4}$ hours incorrectly. It equals 75 minutes, and subtracting 40 minutes gives a difference of 35 minutes.
- 20 minutes.
  - _why:_ This compares 40 minutes with only the one-hour part and ignores the extra quarter-hour. Converting the full duration gives 75 minutes, so the difference is 35 minutes.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** When adding minutes to a clock time, it helps to reach the next [ ___ ] time first.

**Back:** When adding minutes to a clock time, it helps to reach the next o’clock time first.

**Choices:**

- o’clock ✔
  - _why:_ Reaching the next o’clock time separates the calculation into a short step to the hour and a simpler step using the remaining minutes.
- half-past
  - _why:_ A half-past time is not always the next useful boundary and may already have passed. Reaching the next o’clock time first makes the remaining minutes easier to add.
- quarter-past
  - _why:_ Quarter-past is not the standard hour boundary used to simplify addition. Count to the next o’clock time, then add the minutes that remain.
- midnight
  - _why:_ Midnight is only one particular time and may be many hours away. The useful method is to count to the next o’clock time before adding the remainder.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Nina leaves at 9:50 am and her journey lasts 39 minutes. What time does she arrive?

**Back:** 10:29 am.

**Choices:**

- 10:29 am. ✔
  - _why:_ From 9:50 am, the first 10 minutes reaches 10:00 am. Adding the remaining 29 minutes gives an arrival time of 10:29 am.
- 10:19 am.
  - _why:_ This adds only 29 minutes rather than the full 39 minutes. Ten minutes reaches 10:00 am, and the remaining 29 minutes gives 10:29 am.
- 11:29 am.
  - _why:_ This carries forward an extra hour. From 9:50 am, 10 minutes reaches 10:00 am and the remaining 29 minutes gives 10:29 am.
- 10:39 am.
  - _why:_ This effectively adds 49 minutes because it fails to account for the 10 minutes used to reach 10:00 am. Only 29 minutes then remain, giving 10:29 am.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** How should 85 minutes be split when adding it to a clock time?

**Back:** 60 minutes + 25 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 60 minutes + 25 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ Sixty minutes is one complete hour, leaving $85-60=25$ minutes. This split makes adding the duration to a clock time straightforward.
- 60 minutes + 15 minutes.
  - _why:_ This split totals only 75 minutes, so 10 minutes would be missed. Since 85 minutes is one hour and 25 minutes, the correct split is 60 minutes + 25 minutes.
- 60 minutes + 35 minutes.
  - _why:_ This split totals 95 minutes, adding 10 minutes too many. Subtracting one 60-minute hour from 85 minutes leaves 25 minutes, not 35 minutes.
- 50 minutes + 25 minutes.
  - _why:_ This split totals only 75 minutes and does not separate out a complete hour. The useful correct split is 60 minutes + 25 minutes.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** A film starts at 6:50 pm and lasts 85 minutes. What time does it finish?

**Back:** 8:15 pm.

**Choices:**

- 8:15 pm. ✔
  - _why:_ Eighty-five minutes is one hour and 25 minutes. Adding one hour to 6:50 pm gives 7:50 pm, then adding 25 minutes gives 8:15 pm.
- 8:05 pm.
  - _why:_ This is the result of adding only 75 minutes, so the finish time is 10 minutes too early. Adding the full 85 minutes to 6:50 pm gives 8:15 pm.
- 7:15 pm.
  - _why:_ This adds only the 25 minutes left after separating 85 minutes into one hour and 25 minutes. Including the full extra hour gives 8:15 pm.
- 8:25 pm.
  - _why:_ This adds 95 minutes rather than 85 minutes, making the result 10 minutes too late. One hour and 25 minutes after 6:50 pm is 8:15 pm.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** Clock minutes go up to [ ___ ], then the next [ ___ ] begins.

**Back:** Clock minutes go up to 59, then the next hour begins.

**Choices:**

- Clock minutes go up to 59, then the next hour begins. ✔
  - _why:_ Clock minutes run from 00 to 59. One minute after a display ending in :59, the minutes return to 00 and the next hour begins.
- Clock minutes go up to 60, then the next hour begins.
  - _why:_ A clock does not display 60 as its minute value. After 59 minutes, the minute count returns to 00 and the next hour begins.
- Clock minutes go up to 59, then the next minute begins.
  - _why:_ The count does reach 59, but the boundary after it starts a new hour, not merely another minute within the same hour.
- Clock minutes go up to 60, then the next minute begins.
  - _why:_ This confuses elapsed minutes with the numbers displayed on a clock. Displayed minutes stop at 59, after which the next hour begins at 00 minutes.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the 24-hour clock, 0745 means [ ___ ] and 1540 means [ ___ ].

**Back:** In the 24-hour clock, 0745 means 7:45 am and 1540 means 3:40 pm.

**Choices:**

- In the 24-hour clock, 0745 means 7:45 am and 1540 means 3:40 pm. ✔
  - _why:_ Times before 1200 are am, so 0745 is 7:45 am. For 1540, subtracting 12 from the hour gives 3:40 pm.
- In the 24-hour clock, 0745 means 7:45 pm and 1540 means 3:40 pm.
  - _why:_ A time beginning with 07 is before midday, so 0745 is 7:45 am, not pm. Subtracting 12 from 15 correctly converts 1540 to 3:40 pm.
- In the 24-hour clock, 0745 means 7:45 am and 1540 means 5:40 pm.
  - _why:_ This converts the afternoon hour incorrectly. For a 24-hour time after 1259, subtract 12 from the hour: $15-12=3$, so 1540 is 3:40 pm.
- In the 24-hour clock, 0745 means 7:45 am and 1540 means 15:40 pm.
  - _why:_ This mixes 24-hour notation with the am/pm system. When pm is included, 15 must be converted by subtracting 12, making 1540 equal to 3:40 pm.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you read down the same column in a timetable?

**Back:** One column is the same bus or train.

**Choices:**

- One column is the same bus or train. ✔
  - _why:_ Each column follows one particular service through its route, so reading down that column gives the times for the same vehicle at successive stops.
- One column is the same station or stop.
  - _why:_ Rows usually represent places or stops, not individual services. The correct rule is that one column represents the same bus or train throughout its journey.
- One column is the same time at each stop.
  - _why:_ Times in a column normally change as the service travels. One column represents the same bus or train, allowing its route and times to be followed.
- One column is the next bus or train.
  - _why:_ Different columns usually show different services rather than different stages of one journey. Reading down one column follows the same bus or train.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** If a timetable has a dash for a place, can you use that bus or train for that place?

**Back:** No - it does not stop there.

**Choices:**

- No - it does not stop there. ✔
  - _why:_ A dash means that the service does not call at that place, so it cannot be used to arrive at or depart from that stop.
- Yes - its time is not shown.
  - _why:_ A dash is not a missing arrival time; it indicates that the service does not stop there. Therefore, that bus or train cannot be used for that place.
- Yes - it stops on request.
  - _why:_ Request stops are normally identified separately, not by a dash. A dash means the bus or train does not stop at that place at all.
- Yes - it stops without a time.
  - _why:_ A dash does not mean the service stops without a scheduled time. It means the service does not stop there, so another service is needed.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A bus leaves Green Road at 0735 and reaches Station at 0805. How long is the journey?

**Back:** 30 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 30 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ From 07:35 to 08:00 is 25 minutes, followed by another 5 minutes to 08:05, giving a total journey time of 30 minutes.
- 70 minutes.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the minute figures as though both times were within the same hour gives 70 minutes incorrectly. Counting across 08:00 gives the correct duration of 30 minutes.
- 25 minutes.
  - _why:_ This counts only from 07:35 to 08:00 and omits the final 5 minutes. Including the journey to 08:05 gives 30 minutes.
- 40 minutes.
  - _why:_ Adding the displayed minute parts, 35 and 5, ignores the change of hour. Counting elapsed time properly from 07:35 to 08:05 gives 30 minutes.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** For latest-time questions, work [ ___ ] from the deadline.

**Back:** For latest-time questions, work backwards from the deadline.

**Choices:**

- backwards ✔
  - _why:_ A latest-time problem starts with the required arrival deadline and subtracts each journey stage, so the calculation must proceed backwards through the schedule.
- forwards
  - _why:_ Working forwards is useful when the departure time is known, but a latest-time question starts from the deadline and requires working backwards by subtracting durations.
- sideways
  - _why:_ Timetable calculations follow chronological steps rather than moving between unrelated columns. For a latest-time question, start at the deadline and work backwards.
- onwards
  - _why:_ Continuing onwards would calculate a later time after the deadline. To find the latest possible starting time, subtract each stage by working backwards.
