# Angles in Polygons Flashcards

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What do angles on a straight line add to?

**Back:** 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ A straight line forms a half-turn, which is 180°. Therefore, adjacent angles lying on the same straight line must have a total of 180°.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ This confuses a straight line with a full turn around a point. A straight line is a half-turn, so its angles add to 180°.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ This mistakes a straight angle for a right angle. A right angle is 90°, but angles on a straight line add to 180°.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ This represents three-quarters of a full turn rather than a straight line. A straight line forms a half-turn, so its angles total 180°.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles around a point add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles around a point add to 360°.

**Choices:**

- 360° ✔
  - _why:_ Angles around a single point make one complete turn. A complete turn measures 360°, so all the angles around the point must total 360°.
- 180°
  - _why:_ This confuses angles around a point with angles on a straight line. Around a point is a complete turn, so the angles add to 360°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ This gives the size of one right angle, only a quarter-turn. Angles all the way around a point form a full turn of 360°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ This accounts for only three-quarters of a turn. Angles around a point complete the entire turn, so their total is 360°.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What do angles in a triangle add to?

**Back:** 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ The three interior angles of every triangle have a total of 180°, regardless of the triangle’s side lengths, angle sizes or orientation.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ This confuses the triangle’s interior angle sum with a full turn. The three interior angles of any triangle always add to 180°.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ A right triangle contains one 90° angle, but that is not the total. All three interior angles of a triangle add to 180°.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ This is three right angles, not the interior angle sum of a triangle. Every triangle’s three interior angles have a total of 180°.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a regular polygon, all sides are [ ___ ] and all interior angles are [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a regular polygon, all sides are equal and all interior angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- In a regular polygon, all sides are equal and all interior angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ A polygon is regular only when it is both equilateral and equiangular. Therefore, every side has equal length and every interior angle has equal size.
- In a regular polygon, all sides are parallel and all interior angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Regular polygons have equal side lengths, not all parallel sides; adjacent sides must meet. Their interior angles are also equal, so both equality conditions are required.
- In a regular polygon, all sides are equal and all interior angles are different.
  - _why:_ Equal sides alone do not give the full definition of a regular polygon. A regular polygon must also have equal interior angles, rather than different ones.
- In a regular polygon, all sides are different and all interior angles are different.
  - _why:_ This reverses both defining properties of a regular polygon. For a polygon to be regular, all its sides and all its interior angles must be equal.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How many triangles can an n-sided polygon be split into from one vertex?

**Back:** n - 2 triangles.

**Choices:**

- n - 2 triangles. ✔
  - _why:_ Drawing diagonals from one vertex divides an n-sided polygon into n - 2 non-overlapping triangles. This result is used to derive the polygon interior angle sum.
- n - 1 triangles.
  - _why:_ This common off-by-one error counts one extra region. Splitting from one vertex creates n - 2 triangles because the chosen vertex belongs to the two boundary sides.
- n triangles.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly assumes one triangle for every side. Diagonals drawn from one vertex actually divide an n-sided polygon into n - 2 triangles.
- n - 3 triangles.
  - _why:_ This is the number of diagonals drawn from one vertex, not the number of triangles formed. Those diagonals create n - 2 triangles.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** For an n-sided polygon, the interior angle sum is [ ___ ].

**Back:** For an n-sided polygon, the interior angle sum is (n - 2) × 180°.

**Choices:**

- (n - 2) × 180° ✔
  - _why:_ An n-sided polygon can be divided from one vertex into n - 2 triangles. Each triangle contributes 180°, giving an interior angle sum of (n - 2) × 180°.
- (n - 1) × 180°
  - _why:_ This counts one too many triangles in the polygon. The correct split produces n - 2 triangles, so the interior angle sum is (n - 2) × 180°.
- n × 180°
  - _why:_ This incorrectly assigns a whole triangle to every side. An n-sided polygon splits into n - 2 triangles, making the sum (n - 2) × 180°.
- (n - 2) × 360°
  - _why:_ This uses 360° for each triangle, confusing a triangle’s angle sum with a full turn. Each triangle contributes 180°, so the formula is (n - 2) × 180°.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the interior angle sum of a pentagon?

**Back:** 540°.

**Choices:**

- 540°. ✔
  - _why:_ A pentagon has five sides, so its interior angle sum is (5 - 2) × 180° = 3 × 180° = 540°.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ This effectively uses only two triangles instead of three. A pentagon splits into 5 - 2 = 3 triangles, so its interior angles total 540°.
- 720°.
  - _why:_ This is the interior angle sum of a hexagon, not a pentagon. For a pentagon, (5 - 2) × 180° gives 540°.
- 900°.
  - _why:_ This multiplies the five sides directly by 180° instead of subtracting two first. The correct calculation is (5 - 2) × 180° = 540°.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** A pentagon has angles 98°, 121°, 116°, 85° and x. Find x.

**Back:** 120°.

**Choices:**

- 120°. ✔
  - _why:_ A pentagon’s interior angles total 540°. The four known angles total 420°, so the missing angle is $540° - 420° = 120°$.
- 110°.
  - _why:_ This results from using 530° as the pentagon’s angle sum. The correct sum is $(5 - 2) \times 180° = 540°$, giving $540° - 420° = 120°$.
- 130°.
  - _why:_ This comes from incorrectly using 550° for the total interior angle sum. A pentagon’s angles total 540°, so the missing angle is $540° - 420° = 120°$.
- 140°.
  - _why:_ This commonly follows from adding the four given angles as 400° rather than 420°. Their correct total is 420°, leaving $540° - 420° = 120°$.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The exterior angles of a polygon, taking one at each vertex, add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** The exterior angles of a polygon, taking one at each vertex, add to 360°.

**Choices:**

- 360° ✔
  - _why:_ One exterior angle at each vertex represents the complete turn made when travelling around the polygon, so the exterior angles always add to 360°.
- 180°
  - _why:_ 180° is a half-turn and is also the sum of the angles on a straight line. A complete turn around any polygon is 360°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ 90° is a quarter-turn and may be one exterior angle of a square, but all the exterior angles of any polygon add to 360°.
- 540°
  - _why:_ 540° is the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon, not the universal exterior-angle sum. One exterior angle per vertex always totals 360°.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For a regular polygon with n sides, what is each exterior angle?

**Back:** 360° ÷ n.

**Choices:**

- 360° ÷ n. ✔
  - _why:_ The exterior angles of every polygon total 360°. In a regular polygon they are equal, so dividing 360° by the number of sides gives each exterior angle.
- 180° ÷ n.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses a half-turn as the exterior-angle total. The exterior angles sum to 360°, so each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $360° \div n$.
- 360° × n.
  - _why:_ Multiplying would make each exterior angle increase as the number of sides increases. The fixed total of 360° must instead be divided equally among $n$ vertices.
- 360° ÷ (n - 2).
  - _why:_ The expression $n - 2$ belongs to the formula for the interior-angle sum. Exterior angles total 360°, so each one is $360° \div n$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the exterior angle of a regular nonagon?

**Back:** 40°.

**Choices:**

- 40°. ✔
  - _why:_ A nonagon has nine sides. Its equal exterior angles divide the full 360° turn into nine parts, so each measures $360° \div 9 = 40°$.
- 20°.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly divides 180° by nine, using a half-turn rather than the full exterior-angle total. The correct calculation is $360° \div 9 = 40°$.
- 45°.
  - _why:_ 45° is the exterior angle of a regular octagon, which has eight sides. A nonagon has nine sides, so its exterior angle is $360° \div 9 = 40°$.
- 50°.
  - _why:_ This does not divide the complete 360° turn equally among nine vertices. For a regular nonagon, each exterior angle is $360° \div 9 = 40°$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** At each corner, the interior angle and adjacent exterior angle add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** At each corner, the interior angle and adjacent exterior angle add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ An interior angle and its adjacent exterior angle lie on a straight line. Angles on a straight line are supplementary, so they add to 180°.
- 360°
  - _why:_ 360° is the total of one exterior angle at every vertex, not the pair at one corner. An adjacent interior and exterior angle form a straight line totalling 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ 90° is a right angle, but the adjacent interior and exterior angles form a straight line rather than a right angle. Their sum is therefore 180°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ 270° is a three-quarter turn and does not describe this linear pair. Adjacent interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line, so their sum is 180°.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** A regular 12-sided polygon has exterior angle 30°. What is its interior angle?

**Back:** 150°.

**Choices:**

- 150°. ✔
  - _why:_ The interior angle and adjacent exterior angle add to 180°. Therefore, the interior angle is $180° - 30° = 150°$.
- 30°.
  - _why:_ This repeats the exterior angle instead of finding its supplement. The adjacent interior and exterior angles total 180°, so the interior angle is $180° - 30° = 150°$.
- 120°.
  - _why:_ This may come from subtracting 30° twice from 180°. Only one adjacent exterior angle is subtracted, giving an interior angle of $180° - 30° = 150°$.
- 330°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts the exterior angle from a full turn, but the interior angle forms a straight line with it. The correct calculation is $180° - 30° = 150°$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the number of sides from a regular polygon's exterior angle?

**Back:** n = 360° ÷ exterior angle.

**Choices:**

- n = 360° ÷ exterior angle. ✔
  - _why:_ The equal exterior angles total 360°. Dividing that total by the size of one exterior angle gives how many equal angles, and therefore how many sides, the polygon has.
- n = 180° ÷ exterior angle.
  - _why:_ This uses 180°, the angle on a straight line, instead of the complete exterior-angle total. The number of sides is $360° \div$ the exterior angle.
- n = 360° × exterior angle.
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not determine how many equal exterior angles fit into 360°. The correct operation is division: $n = 360° \div \text{exterior angle}$.
- n = exterior angle ÷ 360°.
  - _why:_ This reverses the division and would give a value below one for ordinary exterior angles. Divide the full 360° turn by one exterior angle to find $n$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** A regular polygon has exterior angle 30°. How many sides does it have?

**Back:** 12 sides.

**Choices:**

- 12 sides. ✔
  - _why:_ The exterior angles total 360°, and each is 30°. Therefore, the number of equal angles and sides is $360° \div 30° = 12$.
- 6 sides.
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing 180° by 30°, using a half-turn instead of the full exterior-angle total. The correct calculation is $360° \div 30° = 12$ sides.
- 10 sides.
  - _why:_ Ten sides would give each exterior angle as $360° \div 10 = 36°$, not 30°. An exterior angle of 30° gives $360° \div 30° = 12$ sides.
- 15 sides.
  - _why:_ Fifteen sides would produce an exterior angle of $360° \div 15 = 24°$. For an exterior angle of 30°, the polygon has $360° \div 30° = 12$ sides.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** If given a regular polygon's interior angle, first find the exterior angle using [ ___ ].

**Back:** If given a regular polygon's interior angle, first find the exterior angle using 180° - interior angle.

**Choices:**

- 180° - interior angle ✔
  - _why:_ An interior angle and its corresponding exterior angle lie on a straight line, so they total 180°. Therefore, subtract the interior angle from 180°.
- 360° - interior angle
  - _why:_ Using 360° confuses angles around a point with supplementary interior and exterior angles. The correct calculation is 180° - interior angle because the two form a straight line.
- 180° + interior angle
  - _why:_ Adding the angles ignores that their total must be 180°. The exterior angle is found using 180° - interior angle because adjacent interior and exterior angles are supplementary.
- interior angle - 180°
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction produces a negative value for an ordinary convex polygon. The correct exterior angle is 180° - interior angle, giving a positive angle.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A regular polygon has interior angle 156°. How many sides does it have?

**Back:** 15 sides.

**Choices:**

- 15 sides. ✔
  - _why:_ The exterior angle is 180° - 156° = 24°. Since regular exterior angles total 360°, the polygon has 360° ÷ 24° = 15 sides.
- 24 sides.
  - _why:_ This mistakes the 24° exterior angle for the number of sides. Dividing 360° by 24° gives the correct number, which is 15 sides.
- 14 sides.
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one error. The exterior angle is 24°, and 360° ÷ 24° equals exactly 15, so the polygon has 15 sides.
- 13 sides.
  - _why:_ This does not follow from the exterior-angle calculation. The exterior angle is 180° - 156° = 24°, so 360° ÷ 24° gives 15 sides.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Why must you not divide the interior angle sum by the number of sides for an irregular polygon?

**Back:** Its angles are not all equal.

**Choices:**

- Its angles are not all equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the angle sum by the number of sides gives only the mean angle. In an irregular polygon, the individual interior angles are not necessarily equal to that mean.
- Its angles are all equal.
  - _why:_ Equal interior angles would make division useful for finding each angle, as in a regular polygon. An irregular polygon’s angles are not all equal, so division gives only an average.
- Its sides are not all equal.
  - _why:_ Unequal side lengths do not directly prevent calculating individual angles from the sum. The relevant issue is that an irregular polygon’s interior angles are not all equal.
- Its angle sum is not fixed.
  - _why:_ The interior angle sum is fixed by the number of sides, even for an irregular polygon. Division fails because its individual angles are not all equal.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Two missing angles are y and 2y and add to 210°. Find y.

**Back:** 70°.

**Choices:**

- 70°. ✔
  - _why:_ The two angles give y + 2y = 210°, so 3y = 210°. Dividing both sides by 3 gives y = 70°.
- 105°.
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing 210° by 2, but the coefficients total 3 because y + 2y = 3y. Dividing by 3 gives y = 70°.
- 140°.
  - _why:_ This treats 2y as the required value rather than y. Solving y + 2y = 210° gives y = 70°, while 2y equals 140°.
- 210°.
  - _why:_ This uses the total of both missing angles as the value of y. Since y + 2y = 3y, the total must be divided by 3, giving 70°.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** When polygons meet at a point, which angles usually make the 360° total?

**Back:** Interior angles touching the point.

**Choices:**

- Interior angles touching the point. ✔
  - _why:_ At a shared vertex, the polygons’ interior angles fit together around the point. Angles around a point total 360°, so these are the angles normally added.
- Exterior angles touching the point.
  - _why:_ The angles filling the space around the shared point are normally the polygons’ interior angles. Exterior angles lie outside each polygon and are not the angles being combined here.
- Central angles touching the point.
  - _why:_ Central angles are measured at a polygon’s centre, not at a vertex where polygons meet. The interior angles at the shared vertex make the 360° total.
- Reflex angles touching the point.
  - _why:_ Reflex angles are greater than 180° and are not normally the polygon angles fitted around a shared vertex. The relevant angles are the interior angles touching the point.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** A square, regular hexagon and another regular polygon meet at a point. How many sides does the other polygon have?

**Back:** 12 sides.

**Choices:**

- 12 sides. ✔
  - _why:_ The square and hexagon contribute 90° and 120°, leaving 150°. Its exterior angle is 30°, so the regular polygon has 360° ÷ 30° = 12 sides.
- 6 sides.
  - _why:_ A regular hexagon has interior angle 120°, not the required 150°. The missing angle is 360° - 90° - 120° = 150°, corresponding to 12 sides.
- 8 sides.
  - _why:_ A regular octagon has interior angle 135°, so the three angles would total only 345°. The required interior angle is 150°, which belongs to a 12-sided polygon.
- 10 sides.
  - _why:_ A regular decagon has interior angle 144°, making the total 354° rather than 360°. The missing 150° angle gives exterior angle 30° and therefore 12 sides.
