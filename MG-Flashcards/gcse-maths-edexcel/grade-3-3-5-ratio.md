# Ratio Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 3 · 25 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In girls : boys = $3:2$, what does the 3 belong to?

**Back:** The girls.

**Choices:**

- The girls. ✔
  - _why:_ Ratio entries correspond in order to the named groups. Since girls are listed first, the first ratio number, 3, represents the girls’ equal parts.
- The boys.
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the groups. Boys are listed second, so they correspond to 2; the 3 belongs to the girls.
- The total.
  - _why:_ The total number of ratio parts is found by adding 3 and 2 to get 5. The 3 itself belongs to the girls.
- The difference.
  - _why:_ The difference between the ratio parts is 1, found by subtracting 2 from 3. The 3 represents the girls’ share of the ratio.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A ratio $3:2$ means [ ___ ] equal parts of the first amount for every [ ___ ] equal parts of the second amount.

**Back:** A ratio $3:2$ means 3 equal parts of the first amount for every 2 equal parts of the second amount.

**Choices:**

- A ratio $3:2$ means 3 equal parts of the first amount for every 2 equal parts of the second amount. ✔
  - _why:_ Ratio numbers correspond in order to the amounts being compared. The first amount has 3 equal parts for every 2 equal parts of the second amount.
- A ratio $3:2$ means 2 equal parts of the first amount for every 3 equal parts of the second amount.
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the ratio. The first amount corresponds to 3 parts, while the second amount corresponds to 2 parts.
- A ratio $3:2$ means 3 equal parts of the first amount for every 5 equal parts of the second amount.
  - _why:_ Adding 3 and 2 gives 5 total parts, but 5 is not the second amount’s share. The second amount corresponds to 2 parts.
- A ratio $3:2$ means 5 equal parts of the first amount for every 2 equal parts of the second amount.
  - _why:_ This mistakes the total number of parts, 5, for the first amount’s share. The first amount corresponds to 3 parts and the second to 2.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What should you find first in most ratio questions?

**Back:** The value of one part.

**Choices:**

- The value of one part. ✔
  - _why:_ Finding one part creates a unit value that can be multiplied by each ratio number, allowing every required amount or share to be calculated accurately.
- The value of all parts.
  - _why:_ The value of all parts is usually the total already provided. Most ratio calculations begin by dividing to find the value of one part.
- The value of the largest share.
  - _why:_ Calculating the largest share first skips the unitary method and can cause errors. Find one part first, then multiply by the relevant ratio number.
- The value of the ratio difference.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio numbers only finds their difference, which is not generally useful for sharing a total. First find the value of one part.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** If $QR$ is 3 times $PQ$, what is $PQ:QR$?

**Back:** $$1:3$

**Choices:**

- $$1:3$ ✔
  - _why:_ If $PQ$ is one unit, then $QR$ is three such units because it is three times as long. Therefore, $PQ:QR=1:3$.
- $$3:1$
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the lengths. Since $QR$ is three times $PQ$, $PQ$ is the smaller first term, giving $PQ:QR=1:3$.
- $$1:2$
  - _why:_ This treats “3 times” as if the second length were only two units compared with one. Three times $PQ$ gives $PQ:QR=1:3$.
- $$2:3$
  - _why:_ This does not represent one length being three times the other. Taking $PQ$ as one equal unit makes $QR$ three units, so the ratio is $1:3$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** For $PQ:QR=1:3$ and $PR=96$ m, what is $QR$?

**Back:** $$72$ m

**Choices:**

- $$72$ m ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio has $1+3=4$ parts, so each part is $96\div4=24$ m. As $QR$ has 3 parts, it is $3\times24=72$ m.
- $$24$ m
  - _why:_ This finds the value of one part but does not multiply it by $QR$’s three parts. Since one part is 24 m, $QR=72$ m.
- $$32$ m
  - _why:_ This divides the total by $QR$’s ratio number, 3, instead of by all 4 parts. One part is 24 m, making $QR=72$ m.
- $$288$ m
  - _why:_ This multiplies the entire 96 m length by 3, even though 96 m already represents all four parts. Three of those parts total 72 m.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** To share a total in a ratio, first add the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To share a total in a ratio, first add the ratio parts.

**Choices:**

- ratio parts ✔
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio parts gives the total number of equal parts into which the amount must be divided before each person’s or group’s share is calculated.
- shared total
  - _why:_ The shared total is divided, not added, at this stage. First add the ratio parts to determine how many equal parts the total represents.
- final shares
  - _why:_ The final shares cannot be added before they have been calculated. First add the ratio parts, then divide the total and work out each share.
- people involved
  - _why:_ The number of people or groups is not necessarily the number of equal parts. Add the ratio parts themselves before dividing the total.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** A total is shared in the ratio $5:3$. What do you divide by first?

**Back:** $$8$

**Choices:**

- $$8$ ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio contains $5+3=8$ equal parts altogether. Dividing the total by 8 therefore gives the value of one part.
- $$5$
  - _why:_ This uses only the first share’s ratio number rather than the total number of parts. Add $5+3=8$, so the total must first be divided by 8.
- $$3$
  - _why:_ This uses only the second share’s ratio number. Both shares make up the total, so add $5+3=8$ and divide by 8 first.
- $$2$
  - _why:_ This subtracts the ratio numbers, giving $5-3=2$, rather than finding the total parts. Sharing requires $5+3=8$, so divide by 8 first.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a ratio like $4:3:2$, there are [ ___ ] parts, not [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a ratio like $4:3:2$, there are 9 parts, not 3.

**Choices:**

- In a ratio like $4:3:2$, there are 9 parts, not 3. ✔
  - _why:_ The total number of equal parts is the sum of the ratio numbers: $4+3+2=9$. The three entries count groups, not total parts.
- In a ratio like $4:3:2$, there are 7 parts, not 3.
  - _why:_ This adds only 4 and 3, omitting the final 2. All three ratio numbers must be included, so there are $4+3+2=9$ parts.
- In a ratio like $4:3:2$, there are 9 parts, not 2.
  - _why:_ Although 9 is the correct total, the common mistake being contrasted is counting the three ratio entries as three parts. Therefore, the sentence should say “not 3”.
- In a ratio like $4:3:2$, there are 3 parts, not 9.
  - _why:_ This confuses the number of groups with the number of equal parts and reverses the comparison. Adding $4+3+2$ shows there are 9 parts, not 3.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Share 63 sweets in the ratio $4:3:2$. What is one part?

**Back:** 7 sweets

**Choices:**

- 7 sweets ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio contains $4+3+2=9$ parts altogether. Dividing 63 sweets by 9 gives 7 sweets for each part.
- 9 sweets
  - _why:_ This gives the total number of ratio parts rather than their value. There are 9 parts, so one part is $63\div9=7$ sweets.
- 14 sweets
  - _why:_ This results from dividing 63 by an incorrect number of parts. The ratio totals $4+3+2=9$ parts, making each part worth 7 sweets.
- 21 sweets
  - _why:_ This is the value of 3 parts, not one part. The 9 total parts share 63 sweets, so one part is $63\div9=7$ sweets.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Beth gets the 3 parts in a $4:3:2$ share of 63 sweets. How many sweets?

**Back:** 21 sweets

**Choices:**

- 21 sweets ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio has 9 parts in total, so one part is $63\div9=7$ sweets. Beth receives 3 parts, giving $3\times7=21$ sweets.
- 28 sweets
  - _why:_ This is the amount represented by 4 parts, not Beth’s 3 parts. One part is 7 sweets, so Beth receives $3\times7=21$ sweets.
- 14 sweets
  - _why:_ This is the amount represented by 2 parts rather than Beth’s 3 parts. Since one part is 7 sweets, Beth receives 21 sweets.
- 7 sweets
  - _why:_ This is only the value of one ratio part. Beth receives 3 parts, so her share is $3\times7=21$ sweets.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** If one person's amount is given, match that amount to the [ ___ ].

**Back:** If one person's amount is given, match that amount to the correct ratio part.

**Choices:**

- correct ratio part ✔
  - _why:_ The known amount must be paired with the ratio part belonging to that person. This allows the value of one part, and then other shares, to be calculated.
- total ratio amount
  - _why:_ The given amount belongs to one person, so it should not automatically be matched to the ratio total. It must be matched to that person’s correct ratio part.
- difference between parts
  - _why:_ A difference is used when the question gives how much more or less one person has. A person’s known amount must instead match their correct ratio part.
- smallest ratio part
  - _why:_ Choosing the smallest part regardless of the named person assigns the amount incorrectly. The amount must be matched to the correct ratio part for that person.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Ellen : Faisal = $2:5$. Faisal gets £85. Which part value matches £85?

**Back:** 5 parts

**Choices:**

- 5 parts ✔
  - _why:_ Faisal is the second person in Ellen : Faisal, so his amount corresponds to the second ratio number. In $2:5$, £85 therefore represents 5 parts.
- 2 parts
  - _why:_ Two parts belong to Ellen, the first person named in the ratio. Faisal is the second person, so his £85 corresponds to 5 parts.
- 7 parts
  - _why:_ Seven is the total number of parts because $2+5=7$, not Faisal’s individual share. Faisal’s £85 matches the 5 parts assigned to him.
- 3 parts
  - _why:_ Three is the difference between the ratio parts, but the question asks which part value represents Faisal’s amount. Faisal’s £85 corresponds to 5 parts.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Ellen : Faisal = $2:5$. Faisal gets £85. How much does Ellen get?

**Back:** £34

**Choices:**

- £34 ✔
  - _why:_ Faisal’s 5 parts equal £85, so one part is $85\div5=17$. Ellen receives 2 parts, giving $2\times17=£34$.
- £17
  - _why:_ This is the value of one part, not Ellen’s full share. Ellen receives 2 parts, so her amount is $2\times£17=£34$.
- £42.50
  - _why:_ Dividing £85 by 2 uses Ellen’s ratio part as the divisor instead of Faisal’s 5 parts. One part is £17, so Ellen gets £34.
- £212.50
  - _why:_ This reverses the ratio scaling and makes Ellen’s smaller share larger than Faisal’s. Ellen gets $\frac{2}{5}$ of £85, which is £34.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** In ratio questions, how do you find the difference in parts?

**Back:** Subtract the ratio parts.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the ratio parts. ✔
  - _why:_ A difference compares how far apart two shares are, so subtracting the smaller ratio part from the larger gives the number of parts represented by that difference.
- Add the ratio parts.
  - _why:_ Adding finds the total number of parts, not the difference between the shares. To find the part difference, subtract the smaller ratio part from the larger.
- Multiply the ratio parts.
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not measure the gap between two ratio shares. The difference in parts is found by subtracting the smaller ratio part from the larger.
- Divide the ratio parts.
  - _why:_ Division compares how many times larger one ratio part is, rather than finding their difference. Subtract the smaller part from the larger to find the gap.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** In a $2:7$ ratio, what part difference matches “35 more”?

**Back:** 5 parts

**Choices:**

- 5 parts ✔
  - _why:_ The difference between the two shares is found by subtraction. In the ratio $2:7$, the difference is $7-2=5$ parts, so 35 matches 5 parts.
- 9 parts
  - _why:_ Nine is the total number of parts from $2+7$, not the difference between the shares. The phrase “35 more” corresponds to $7-2=5$ parts.
- 7 parts
  - _why:_ Seven parts represent the larger person’s complete share, not how much more they receive. The difference is $7-2=5$ parts, so 35 matches 5 parts.
- 2 parts
  - _why:_ Two parts represent the smaller person’s complete share, not the gap between the shares. The difference is $7-2=5$ parts, so 35 matches 5 parts.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Harper : Imani = $3:8$. Imani has 30 more badges. What is one part?

**Back:** 6 badges

**Choices:**

- 6 badges ✔
  - _why:_ Imani has $8-3=5$ more ratio parts than Harper. Those 5 parts equal 30 badges, so one part is $30\div5=6$ badges.
- 10 badges
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing 30 by Harper’s 3 parts instead of by the part difference. The difference is $8-3=5$ parts, so one part is 6 badges.
- 3.75 badges
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing 30 by Imani’s 8 parts, treating the difference as her entire share. The 30-badge difference represents 5 parts, making one part 6 badges.
- 30 badges
  - _why:_ This treats the whole difference as a single ratio part. Since Imani’s lead represents $8-3=5$ parts, each part is $30\div5=6$ badges.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Harper : Imani = $3:8$. Imani has 30 more badges. How many badges does Imani have?

**Back:** 48 badges

**Choices:**

- 48 badges ✔
  - _why:_ The difference of 5 ratio parts represents 30 badges, so one part is 6 badges. Imani has 8 parts, giving 8 × 6 = 48 badges.
- 18 badges
  - _why:_ This finds Harper’s amount by multiplying 3 parts by 6. The question asks for Imani’s 8 parts, so she has 8 × 6 = 48 badges.
- 80 badges
  - _why:_ This treats the 30-badge difference as if it represented Harper’s 3 parts. It represents the 5-part difference, so one part is 6 and Imani has 48 badges.
- 66 badges
  - _why:_ This adds Harper’s and Imani’s amounts to find the total number of badges. Imani alone has 8 × 6 = 48 badges.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** For “30 more” in a ratio question, divide 30 by the [ ___ ], not the larger part.

**Back:** For “30 more” in a ratio question, divide 30 by the difference between the ratio parts, not the larger part.

**Choices:**

- difference between the ratio parts ✔
  - _why:_ The stated difference in quantities corresponds to the difference between their ratio parts. Dividing by that difference gives the value represented by one ratio part.
- larger of the ratio parts
  - _why:_ Dividing by the larger part wrongly treats the difference in quantities as the larger person’s whole share. Divide by the difference between the ratio parts instead.
- sum of the ratio parts
  - _why:_ The sum of the parts is used when a total quantity is given. For “30 more”, divide by the difference between the ratio parts.
- smaller of the ratio parts
  - _why:_ Dividing by the smaller part wrongly treats the stated difference as the smaller share. The difference in quantities matches the difference between the ratio parts.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Red : yellow : white = $5:4:1$. How many parts are in one full batch?

**Back:** 10 parts

**Choices:**

- 10 parts ✔
  - _why:_ One full batch contains every part in the three-part ratio. Adding the ratio numbers gives 5 + 4 + 1 = 10 parts.
- 9 parts
  - _why:_ This adds only the red and yellow parts, leaving out the white part. All three numbers must be included, giving 5 + 4 + 1 = 10 parts.
- 20 parts
  - _why:_ This doubles the sum of the ratio numbers without justification. One full batch has 5 + 4 + 1 = 10 parts.
- 4 parts
  - _why:_ This selects the yellow ratio number rather than finding the size of the full batch. Adding all three ratio parts gives 10 parts.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** For greatest amount questions, the [ ___ ] shows which ingredient runs out first.

**Back:** For greatest amount questions, the smaller one-part value shows which ingredient runs out first.

**Choices:**

- smaller one-part value ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing each available amount by its required ratio part gives possible one-part values. The smaller value limits the number of complete batches because that ingredient runs out first.
- larger one-part value
  - _why:_ The larger one-part value belongs to the ingredient that can support more batches, so it does not limit production. The smaller one-part value identifies what runs out first.
- larger ratio part
  - _why:_ A larger ratio part does not necessarily identify the limiting ingredient because the available quantities may differ. Compare one-part values and choose the smaller one.
- total amount available
  - _why:_ The total available amount ignores how much of each ingredient the ratio requires. The smaller one-part value shows which ingredient limits the number of complete batches.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** If a question says the remaining counters are in a ratio, do you split the whole total or what is left?

**Back:** Split only what is left.

**Choices:**

- Split only what is left. ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio describes the counters remaining after earlier amounts have been removed. Subtract those amounts first, then divide only the remainder according to the ratio.
- Split the whole total.
  - _why:_ Using the whole total incorrectly includes counters already assigned or removed. First calculate the remainder, then split only what is left in the stated ratio.
- Split only what was used.
  - _why:_ The used counters are no longer part of the remaining group described by the ratio. Subtract them and split only what is left.
- Split the original amount twice.
  - _why:_ Applying the ratio to the original amount twice double-counts counters and ignores the stated remainder. The ratio must be applied only to what is left.

## Card 23 — `cloze`

**Front:** [ ___ ] means what is left after taking away the amounts already used.

**Back:** Remaining means what is left after taking away the amounts already used.

**Choices:**

- Remaining ✔
  - _why:_ “Remaining” describes the amount left after known amounts have been removed from an original total. It signals that subtraction is needed before any later calculation.
- Total
  - _why:_ “Total” means the complete amount before anything is taken away, not the amount left afterwards. The correct term for what is left is “remaining”.
- Used
  - _why:_ “Used” describes the amount already taken or allocated, rather than what is still left. The amount left after subtraction is called “remaining”.
- Initial
  - _why:_ “Initial” refers to the starting amount before any changes are made. The amount left after taking away what was used is called “remaining”.

## Card 24 — `basic`

**Front:** There are 160 counters. 25% are red and $\frac{1}{4}$ are blue. How many remain?

**Back:** 80 counters

**Choices:**

- 80 counters ✔
  - _why:_ 25% of 160 is 40, and one quarter of 160 is also 40. Subtracting both groups gives 160 - 40 - 40 = 80 counters.
- 120 counters
  - _why:_ This subtracts only one group of 40 counters and overlooks that both red and blue counters must be removed. Subtracting 80 altogether leaves 80 counters.
- 40 counters
  - _why:_ This gives the size of either the red group or the blue group, not the remainder. Removing both 40-counter groups from 160 leaves 80 counters.
- 95 counters
  - _why:_ This treats one quarter as 25 counters rather than 25% of 160. Both fractions equal 40 counters, so removing 80 leaves 80 counters.

## Card 25 — `basic`

**Front:** 80 remaining counters are split yellow : green = $3:1$. How many are yellow?

**Back:** 60 counters

**Choices:**

- 60 counters ✔
  - _why:_ The ratio has 4 equal parts altogether, and yellow represents 3 of them. One part is 80 ÷ 4 = 20, so yellow counters total 3 × 20 = 60.
- 20 counters
  - _why:_ This is the value of one ratio part, found by dividing 80 by 4. Yellow represents three parts, so the correct answer is 60 counters, not one part.
- 40 counters
  - _why:_ This treats yellow as half of the counters, rather than 3 out of 4 equal parts. Since 3/4 of 80 is 60, the correct answer is 60 counters.
- 80 counters
  - _why:_ This uses the total number of counters without applying the 3:1 split. Yellow takes three of the four equal parts, so the correct answer is 60 counters.
