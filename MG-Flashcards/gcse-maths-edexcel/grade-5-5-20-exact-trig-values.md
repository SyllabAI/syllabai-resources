## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What defines an exact value in trigonometry?

**Back:** An answer written without rounding, typically as a fraction or a surd.

**Choices:**

- An answer written without rounding, typically as a fraction or a surd. ✔
  - _why:_ An exact value preserves the complete mathematical value without approximation. Fractions and surds, such as $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, commonly express exact trigonometric values.
- An answer written with rounding, typically as a decimal or a percentage.
  - _why:_ Rounding produces an approximation rather than an exact value. The right answer is written without rounding, usually as a fraction or surd that preserves the complete value.
- An answer found using a calculator, typically as a decimal to a stated accuracy.
  - _why:_ A calculator decimal given to a stated accuracy is normally approximate. An exact value is written without rounding, typically using a fraction or a surd.
- An answer written as a whole number, typically after rounding a fraction or a surd.
  - _why:_ Rounding a fraction or surd to a whole number loses information, so the result is not exact. Exact values retain the original fraction or surd without rounding.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Which side of a right-angled triangle is the hypotenuse?

**Back:** The longest side, which is always opposite the right angle.

**Choices:**

- The longest side, which is always opposite the right angle. ✔
  - _why:_ In every right-angled triangle, the side facing the $90^\circ$ angle is the hypotenuse. Pythagoras’ theorem also confirms that it must be the longest side.
- The shortest side, which is always opposite the smallest angle.
  - _why:_ Although the shortest side is opposite the smallest angle, it is not the hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is the longest side and lies opposite the right angle.
- The longest side, which is always next to the right angle.
  - _why:_ This correctly identifies the hypotenuse as longest but places it incorrectly. The two legs form the right angle, while the hypotenuse lies opposite the right angle.
- The opposite side, which is always opposite the chosen acute angle.
  - _why:_ The side opposite a chosen acute angle is one of the triangle’s legs, not the hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is specifically opposite the right angle and is longest.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The side adjacent to an angle $\theta$ is the side next to it that is not the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The side adjacent to an angle $\theta$ is the side next to it that is not the hypotenuse.

**Choices:**

- hypotenuse ✔
  - _why:_ For a chosen acute angle, two sides touch the angle: one leg and the hypotenuse. The adjacent side means the touching leg, so the hypotenuse is excluded.
- opposite
  - _why:_ The opposite side lies across from $\theta$ and does not touch it, so this does not distinguish the adjacent side. The excluded touching side is the hypotenuse.
- adjacent
  - _why:_ Excluding the adjacent side would make the definition circular and leave no named leg. The adjacent side is the leg beside $\theta$ that is not the hypotenuse.
- shortest side
  - _why:_ The adjacent leg is not necessarily the shortest side; its length depends on the chosen angle and triangle. It is identified by excluding the hypotenuse.

**Back:** SOHCAHTOA defines the sine ratio as $\sin\theta = \frac{\boxed{\text{opposite}}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\text{hypotenuse}$
  - _why:_ Putting the hypotenuse in both numerator and denominator would always give $1$, regardless of the angle. Sine instead uses opposite over hypotenuse.
- $$\text{shortest side}$
  - _why:_ The numerator is determined by its position relative to $\theta$, not by its length. SOH specifies the opposite side, which is not necessarily the shortest side.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $\tan(90^\circ)$ not defined?

**Back:** The adjacent side is zero, and dividing by zero is impossible.

**Choices:**

- The adjacent side is zero, and dividing by zero is impossible. ✔
  - _why:_ Tangent is opposite divided by adjacent. At $90^\circ$ the adjacent side is zero, so calculating tangent would require division by zero, which is undefined.
- The opposite side is zero, and dividing by zero is impossible.
  - _why:_ This swaps the numerator and denominator in the tangent ratio. At $90^\circ$ the adjacent side, not the opposite side, is zero, making division by zero unavoidable.
- The hypotenuse is zero, and dividing by zero is impossible.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse cannot be zero in a non-degenerate right-angled triangle. Tangent is opposite divided by adjacent, and at $90^\circ$ it is the adjacent side that is zero.
- The adjacent side is zero, and zero divided by a number is impossible.
  - _why:_ Zero divided by a non-zero number is defined and equals zero. The problem is that tangent requires the opposite side to be divided by the zero adjacent side.
