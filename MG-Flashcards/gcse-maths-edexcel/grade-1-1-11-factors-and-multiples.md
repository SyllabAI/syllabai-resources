## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A multiple of a number is an answer in that number’s [ ___ ].

**Back:** A multiple of a number is an answer in that number’s times table.

**Choices:**

- times table ✔
  - _why:_ A number’s multiples are produced by multiplying it by whole numbers, which is exactly the sequence shown in its times table.
- factor list
  - _why:_ A factor list contains numbers that divide the original number exactly, not its multiples. The correct term is “times table”, which lists successive multiples.
- number line
  - _why:_ A number line can display many different values, so it is not specifically a list of multiples. Multiples appear in the number’s times table.
- place value chart
  - _why:_ A place value chart shows the value of digits in different positions, not multiples. The answers obtained by repeated multiplication form a times table.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** To find multiples of 6, what equal jumps do you count in?

**Back:** Count in 6s.

**Choices:**

- Count in 6s. ✔
  - _why:_ Successive multiples of 6 are found by repeatedly adding 6, giving 6, 12, 18, 24 and so on.
- Count in 3s.
  - _why:_ Counting in 3s includes numbers such as 3 and 9, which are not multiples of 6. Count in 6s to generate only multiples of 6.
- Count in 12s.
  - _why:_ Counting in 12s produces only every second multiple of 6, missing values such as 6 and 18. Count in 6s instead.
- Count in 1s.
  - _why:_ Counting in 1s lists every whole number rather than specifically generating multiples of 6. Equal jumps of 6 are required.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** Multiples of a number keep going [ ___ ].

**Back:** Multiples of a number keep going forever.

**Choices:**

- forever ✔
  - _why:_ Any whole number can be multiplied by larger and larger whole numbers, so there is no greatest multiple and the sequence continues forever.
- until 100
  - _why:_ One hundred is not a stopping point for multiples; multiplication can continue using larger whole numbers. Multiples therefore keep going forever.
- until they repeat
  - _why:_ Multiples do not cycle back to the beginning because their values continue increasing. There is no final multiple, so they continue forever.
- only to 1,000
  - _why:_ One thousand is merely one possible value, not an upper limit. Multiples can be generated beyond any chosen number and therefore continue forever.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Which multiple of 6 is between 25 and 35?

**Back:** 30.

**Choices:**

- 30. ✔
  - _why:_ The multiples around this interval are 24, 30 and 36. Only 30 is greater than 25 and less than 35.
- 24.
  - _why:_ Although 24 is a multiple of 6, it lies below 25 and is therefore outside the stated interval. The required multiple is 30.
- 32.
  - _why:_ Although 32 lies between 25 and 35, it is not divisible by 6. The multiple of 6 in the interval is 30.
- 36.
  - _why:_ Although 36 is a multiple of 6, it is greater than 35 and therefore outside the stated interval. The required multiple is 30.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you tell whether a whole number is even or odd?

**Back:** Check the last digit.

**Choices:**

- Check the last digit. ✔
  - _why:_ A whole number is even when its final digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8; otherwise, its final digit makes it odd.
- Check the first digit.
  - _why:_ The first digit does not determine parity; for example, 12 is even while 13 is odd. The last digit must be checked.
- Check the digit sum.
  - _why:_ Adding the digits is useful for divisibility by 3 or 9, not for deciding parity. Evenness depends only on the last digit.
- Check the number of digits.
  - _why:_ Numbers with the same number of digits can have different parity, such as 24 and 25. The last digit determines whether a number is even or odd.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** A factor divides into a number exactly, with [ ___ ].

**Back:** A factor divides into a number exactly, with nothing left over.

**Choices:**

- nothing left over ✔
  - _why:_ A factor divides a number to give a whole-number quotient and a remainder of zero, meaning that nothing is left over.
- one left over
  - _why:_ A remainder of 1 means the division is not exact, so the divisor is not a factor. A factor leaves nothing left over.
- some left over
  - _why:_ Any amount left over is a non-zero remainder, showing that the division is not exact. A factor must leave nothing left over.
- a decimal left over
  - _why:_ A decimal result indicates that the division has not produced a whole-number quotient. A factor divides exactly, with nothing left over.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is 3 a factor of 18?

**Back:** $$18 \div 3 = 6$ exactly.

**Choices:**

- $$18 \div 3 = 6$ exactly. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing 18 by 3 gives the whole number 6 with no remainder. This exact division proves that 3 is a factor of 18.
- $$18 \div 3 = 5$ exactly.
  - _why:_ This is an arithmetic error because 18 divided by 3 equals 6, not 5. The exact quotient of 6 proves that 3 is a factor.
- $$18 \div 3 = 6$ with remainder 1.
  - _why:_ The quotient is 6 with no remainder, so adding a remainder of 1 is incorrect. Exact division is what establishes 3 as a factor.
- $$18 \times 3 = 54$ exactly.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 18 by 3 gives a true product but does not test whether 3 divides into 18. The relevant calculation is 18 divided by 3.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Which factor of 12 pairs with 2?

**Back:** 6, because 2 × 6 = 12.

**Choices:**

- 6, because 2 × 6 = 12. ✔
  - _why:_ Factors pair together to make the original number when multiplied. Since 2 × 6 = 12, the factor paired with 2 is 6.
- 4, because 2 × 4 = 12.
  - _why:_ This confuses the factor paired with 3 with the factor paired with 2. Since 2 × 6 = 12, the required factor is 6, not 4.
- 3, because 2 × 3 = 12.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 2 by 3 gives 6 rather than 12. The matching factor is 6 because 2 × 6 = 12.
- 12, because 2 × 12 = 12.
  - _why:_ This treats 12 itself as the factor paired with 2, but 2 × 12 = 24. The correct factor is 6 because 2 × 6 = 12.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** For 12, 3 is a [ ___ ], but 24 is a [ ___ ].

**Back:** For 12, 3 is a factor, but 24 is a multiple.

**Choices:**

- For 12, 3 is a factor, but 24 is a multiple. ✔
  - _why:_ Three is a factor because it divides 12 exactly, while 24 is a multiple because it equals 12 multiplied by the whole number 2.
- For 12, 3 is a multiple, but 24 is a factor.
  - _why:_ This reverses factors and multiples. Three divides 12 exactly, so it is a factor; 24 equals 12 × 2, so it is a multiple.
- For 12, 3 is a factor, but 24 is a factor.
  - _why:_ Although 3 is a factor of 12, 24 cannot divide 12 to give a whole number. Instead, 24 is a multiple because 12 × 2 = 24.
- For 12, 3 is a multiple, but 24 is a multiple.
  - _why:_ Although 24 is a multiple of 12, 3 is not. Three is a factor because 12 ÷ 3 = 4, which is a whole number.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** A prime number is bigger than 1 and has exactly [ ___ ]: [ ___ ].

**Back:** A prime number is bigger than 1 and has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

**Choices:**

- A prime number is bigger than 1 and has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. ✔
  - _why:_ A prime number has precisely two positive factors: 1 and the number itself. Having no other factors distinguishes prime numbers from composite numbers.
- A prime number is bigger than 1 and has exactly one factor: itself only.
  - _why:_ This overlooks that every positive whole number is divisible by 1. A prime has exactly two factors, and these are 1 and the number itself.
- A prime number is bigger than 1 and has exactly two factors: 2 and itself.
  - _why:_ This wrongly assumes 2 is a factor of every prime; in fact, all primes except 2 are odd. The two factors are 1 and the number itself.
- A prime number is bigger than 1 and has exactly three factors: 1, 2 and itself.
  - _why:_ This gives an extra factor and would exclude odd primes such as 3 and 5. A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is 1 not prime?

**Back:** It has only one factor.

**Choices:**

- It has only one factor. ✔
  - _why:_ The only positive factor of 1 is 1 itself. Prime numbers must have exactly two distinct factors, 1 and themselves, so 1 is not prime.
- It has exactly two factors.
  - _why:_ This gives the defining number of factors for a prime, but 1 has only one factor: 1. Therefore, it does not meet the definition of a prime number.
- It has no factors.
  - _why:_ One does have a factor because 1 divides it exactly. Its only factor is 1, whereas a prime number must have exactly two distinct factors.
- It is an even number.
  - _why:_ One is odd, not even, and parity is not the reason it is non-prime. It is not prime because it has only one factor.

**Front:** What is the only even prime number?

**Back:** 2.

**Choices:**

- 2. ✔
  - _why:_ Two has exactly two factors, 1 and 2, so it is prime. Every other even number is divisible by 2 and therefore has additional factors.
- 3.
  - _why:_ Three is prime but odd, so it does not satisfy both parts of the question. The only even prime is 2, whose factors are 1 and 2.
- 5.
  - _why:_ Five has exactly two factors and is therefore prime, but it is odd. The only number that is both even and prime is 2.
- 7.
  - _why:_ Seven is a prime number because its only factors are 1 and 7, but it is odd. The only even prime number is 2.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Which prime numbers are between 30 and 40?

**Back:** 31 and 37.

**Choices:**

- 31 and 37. ✔
  - _why:_ Neither 31 nor 37 has any factors other than 1 and itself. All the other whole numbers strictly between 30 and 40 are composite.
- 31 and 39.
  - _why:_ Thirty-nine may look prime, but it is divisible by 3 because 3 × 13 = 39. The prime numbers in this interval are 31 and 37.
- 33 and 37.
  - _why:_ Thirty-three is composite because 3 × 11 = 33. Thirty-seven is prime, but the other prime number between 30 and 40 is 31.
- 31 and 35.
  - _why:_ Thirty-five is composite because 5 × 7 = 35. Thirty-one is prime, and the other prime number between 30 and 40 is 37.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** A square number is made by multiplying a number by [ ___ ].

**Back:** A square number is made by multiplying a number by itself.

**Choices:**

- itself ✔
  - _why:_ A square number is the product of a whole number multiplied by itself. For example, 5 × 5 = 25, so 25 is a square number.
- the next number
  - _why:_ Multiplying consecutive numbers produces an oblong or pronic number, not generally a square. A square is formed by multiplying a number by itself, such as 4 × 4.
- the previous number
  - _why:_ Multiplying a number by the previous number uses two different factors and does not generally make a square. A square is made by multiplying a number by itself.
- a different number
  - _why:_ A product of two different numbers is not the defining form of a square number. A square is specifically formed by multiplying a number by itself.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** From 18, 20, 22, 25, 28 and 30, which number is square?

**Back:** 25.

**Choices:**

- 25. ✔
  - _why:_ A square number is an integer multiplied by itself. Since $5 \times 5 = 25$, 25 is the only square number in the given list.
- 20.
  - _why:_ Being even does not make a number square. The correct answer is 25 because $5 \times 5 = 25$, whereas no integer multiplied by itself equals 20.
- 28.
  - _why:_ This may be mistaken for a square because it is close to 25, but $28$ is not an integer squared. The correct answer is $25 = 5^2$.
- 30.
  - _why:_ A multiple of a familiar number is not necessarily square. Although $30$ is divisible by 5, the correct answer is $25$ because $25 = 5^2$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** From 4, 11, 12, 15, 18, 25 and 29, choose a factor of 20.

**Back:** 4.

**Choices:**

- 4. ✔
  - _why:_ A factor divides a number exactly with no remainder. Since $20 \div 4 = 5$, 4 is a factor of 20.
- 11.
  - _why:_ Confusing a nearby number with an exact divisor leads to this choice. The correct answer is 4 because $20 \div 4 = 5$, while 11 leaves a remainder.
- 12.
  - _why:_ Being a multiple of 4 does not make a number a factor of 20. The correct answer is 4, which divides 20 exactly; 12 does not.
- 25.
  - _why:_ This confuses factors with numbers related through a shared factor of 5. The correct answer is 4 because it divides 20 exactly, whereas 25 is greater than 20.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A number is odd, a factor of 30, and a multiple of 5. What are the two possible numbers?

**Back:** 5 and 15.

**Choices:**

- 5 and 15. ✔
  - _why:_ The factors of 30 that are odd and divisible by 5 are 5 and 15. Both divide 30 exactly and are multiples of 5.
- 2 and 10.
  - _why:_ Although both numbers are factors of 30, neither is odd, so they fail one condition. The two numbers satisfying all three conditions are 5 and 15.
- 1 and 3.
  - _why:_ Both numbers are odd factors of 30, but neither is a multiple of 5. The correct pair is 5 and 15, which satisfies every condition.
- 10 and 30.
  - _why:_ Both numbers are multiples of 5, but 10 and 30 are even rather than odd. The required odd factors and multiples of 5 are 5 and 15.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** For an odd total made from two prime numbers, which prime should you try first?

**Back:** 2.

**Choices:**

- 2. ✔
  - _why:_ Every prime except 2 is odd, and two odd numbers have an even sum. Therefore an odd total from two primes must include the only even prime, 2.
- 3.
  - _why:_ Although 3 is prime, it is odd; adding it to another odd prime gives an even total. Try 2 first because it is the only even prime.
- 5.
  - _why:_ Choosing 5 confuses a familiar prime with the prime needed for parity. Since 5 and every other odd prime sum to an even number, the correct choice is 2.
- 7.
  - _why:_ Although 7 is prime, it cannot combine with another odd prime to produce an odd total. The correct prime to try is 2, the only even prime.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Find two prime numbers with a sum of 21.

**Back:** 2 and 19.

**Choices:**

- 2 and 19. ✔
  - _why:_ Both 2 and 19 are prime, and their sum is $2 + 19 = 21$. Because 21 is odd, one of the two primes must be 2.
- 3 and 17.
  - _why:_ Both numbers are prime, but their sum is $3 + 17 = 20$, not 21. The required pair is 2 and 19, whose sum is 21.
- 5 and 17.
  - _why:_ Both numbers are prime, but their sum is $5 + 17 = 22$, which is one too large. The correct pair is 2 and 19.
- 7 and 13.
  - _why:_ Both numbers are prime, but their sum is $7 + 13 = 20$, not 21. The correct pair is 2 and 19 because it totals 21.
