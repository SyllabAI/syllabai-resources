## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** The [ ___ ] is the middle value after the data are in [ ___ ].

**Back:** The median is the middle value after the data are in order.

**Choices:**

- The median is the middle value after the data are in order. ✔
  - _why:_ The median is found by arranging the data in order and identifying the middle value, or the two middle values when the data set has even size.
- The mean is the middle value after the data are in order.
  - _why:_ This confuses the median with the mean. The median is the middle value after the data have been placed in order.
- The median is the middle value after the data are in groups.
  - _why:_ Grouping the data does not reliably reveal its middle value. The median is found after the values have been arranged in order.
- The mode is the middle value after the data are in frequency order.
  - _why:_ This mixes up the mode and median. The mode is based on frequency, while the median is the middle value after ordering the data.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is the middle of the original list unsafe for the median?

**Back:** The list may not be in order.

**Choices:**

- The list may not be in order. ✔
  - _why:_ Position alone is meaningful only after sorting. In an unordered original list, the central written entry may not be the median value.
- The list may contain repeated values.
  - _why:_ Repeated values do not make the median unsafe. The problem is that the original list may not be in order, so its written middle can be misleading.
- The list may have an odd length.
  - _why:_ An odd number of values actually gives one middle position after sorting. The original list is unsafe because it may not be in order.
- The list may include negative values.
  - _why:_ Negative values can be ordered and included normally when finding a median. The danger is using the middle position before putting the list in order.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The [ ___ ] is the value that appears [ ___ ].

**Back:** The mode is the value that appears most often.

**Choices:**

- The mode is the value that appears most often. ✔
  - _why:_ The mode is determined by frequency: it is the value occurring most often in the data set, not a measure based on position or calculation.
- The median is the value that appears most often.
  - _why:_ This confuses the median with the mode. The median concerns the middle position, whereas the mode is the value that appears most often.
- The mode is the value that appears least often.
  - _why:_ The least frequent value is not the mode. The mode is the value with the greatest frequency, meaning it appears most often.
- The mean is the value that appears least often.
  - _why:_ The mean is calculated by dividing the total by the number of values, not by counting the least frequent value. The mode appears most often.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** If no value repeats, what is the mode?

**Back:** There is no mode.

**Choices:**

- There is no mode. ✔
  - _why:_ A mode must have a greater frequency than the other values. If every value occurs only once, no value is most frequent, so there is no mode.
- There is a mode.
  - _why:_ Equal frequencies do not make every value a mode in this GCSE context. If no value repeats, none occurs most often, so there is no mode.
- The first value is the mode.
  - _why:_ The first value's position does not make it the mode. The mode depends on frequency, and with no repeated values there is no mode.
- The largest value is the mode.
  - _why:_ The largest value is the maximum, not automatically the mode. Since no value repeats, none has the greatest frequency and there is no mode.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** The range is [ ___ ] minus [ ___ ].

**Back:** The range is largest value minus smallest value.

**Choices:**

- The range is largest value minus smallest value. ✔
  - _why:_ The range measures the total spread of the data, so it is calculated by subtracting the smallest value from the largest value.
- The range is smallest value minus largest value.
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction usually produces a negative result. The range is non-negative and is found using largest value minus smallest value.
- The range is largest value minus second smallest value.
  - _why:_ Using the second smallest value ignores part of the data's spread. The range must use both extremes: largest value minus smallest value.
- The range is highest frequency minus smallest value.
  - _why:_ Frequency determines the mode, not the range. To measure the spread, subtract the smallest value in the data set from the largest value.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the range of 152 cm, 168 cm, 160 cm, 174 cm, 152 cm, 181 cm?

**Back:** 29 cm

**Choices:**

- 29 cm ✔
  - _why:_ The largest measurement is 181 cm and the smallest is 152 cm. Subtracting them gives a range of $181-152=29$ cm.
- 28 cm
  - _why:_ This off-by-one result can come from counting whole centimetres between the endpoints. The range uses direct subtraction: $181-152=29$ cm.
- 21 cm
  - _why:_ This uses 160 cm as the minimum and overlooks the two 152 cm values. The correct extremes give $181-152=29$ cm.
- 333 cm
  - _why:_ This adds the largest and smallest measurements instead of subtracting them. The range is the difference, so $181-152=29$ cm.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** With an [ ___ ] number of values, the median uses the [ ___ ].

**Back:** With an even number of values, the median uses the two middle values.

**Choices:**

- With an even number of values, the median uses the two middle values. ✔
  - _why:_ An even-sized ordered data set has no single central position. Its median is therefore found from the two middle values, usually by calculating their mean.
- With an odd number of values, the median uses the two middle values.
  - _why:_ An odd number of values has one middle value after ordering, not two. The median uses two middle values when the number of values is even.
- With an even number of values, the median uses the middle value.
  - _why:_ An even-sized data set has two central positions, so choosing only one would be arbitrary. The median must use the two middle values.
- With an odd number of values, the median uses the middle value.
  - _why:_ This states the rule for an odd-sized data set. When the number of values is even, the median uses the two middle values instead.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the median of 118 g, 124 g, 126 g, 132 g, 140 g, 150 g?

**Back:** 129 g

**Choices:**

- 129 g ✔
  - _why:_ With six ordered values, the median is the mean of the two middle values. The middle values are 126 g and 132 g, giving $(126+132)\div2=129$ g.
- 126 g
  - _why:_ This selects the lower middle value instead of averaging both middle values. For six values, average 126 g and 132 g to obtain the median of 129 g.
- 132 g
  - _why:_ This selects the upper middle value instead of averaging both middle values. Because there are six values, the median is $(126+132)\div2=129$ g.
- 131 g
  - _why:_ This results from averaging the wrong pair of values. The two middle values are 126 g and 132 g, whose mean is 129 g.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** For the mean, divide the [ ___ ] by the [ ___ ].

**Back:** For the mean, divide the total of values by the number of values.

**Choices:**

- For the mean, divide the total of values by the number of values. ✔
  - _why:_ The mean is calculated by adding all the values to find their total, then dividing that total by how many values there are.
- For the mean, divide the number of values by the total of values.
  - _why:_ This reverses the mean calculation. The total of the values must be divided by the number of values, not the number divided by the total.
- For the mean, divide the total of values by the range of values.
  - _why:_ The range measures spread and is not the divisor when finding a mean. Divide the total of the values by the number of values.
- For the mean, divide the highest value by the number of values.
  - _why:_ Using only the highest value ignores the other data values. The mean requires the total of all values to be divided by the number of values.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Back:** 11

**Choices:**

- 11 ✔
  - _why:_ The six values total $14+6+10+6+12+18=66$. Dividing this total by 6 gives a mean of $66\div6=11$.
- 10
  - _why:_ This is the median, found from the middle pair after ordering, rather than the mean. The total is 66, so the mean is $66\div6=11$.
- 12
  - _why:_ This can result from an addition or division slip. The values total 66, and dividing by all six values gives $66\div6=11$, not 12.
- 66
  - _why:_ This is the total of the values rather than their mean. The total of 66 must be divided by the six values, giving a mean of 11.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** To work backwards from a mean: [ ___ ] = [ ___ ].

**Back:** To work backwards from a mean: total = mean × number of values.

**Choices:**

- To work backwards from a mean: total = mean × number of values. ✔
  - _why:_ Since mean equals total divided by the number of values, reversing the division gives total equals mean multiplied by the number of values.
- To work backwards from a mean: mean = total × number of values.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly multiplies the total by the number of values. To reconstruct the total, multiply the mean by the number of values.
- To work backwards from a mean: total = mean ÷ number of values.
  - _why:_ Dividing the mean by the number of values makes the result too small. The total is found by multiplying the mean by the number of values.
- To work backwards from a mean: number of values = total × mean.
  - _why:_ This uses multiplication while making the number of values the subject incorrectly. The required relationship is total equals mean multiplied by number of values.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** When adding a new value, what should you do to the old mean first?

**Back:** Turn it back into a total.

**Choices:**

- Turn it back into a total. ✔
  - _why:_ A mean does not preserve the full sum by itself. Multiply the old mean by the old number of values to recover the total before adding the new value.
- Turn it back into a median.
  - _why:_ The median is the middle ordered value and cannot be recovered from the mean alone. Instead, multiply the old mean by the number of values to obtain the total.
- Turn it back into a range.
  - _why:_ The range is the difference between the greatest and least values, not a step in updating a mean. First convert the old mean into a total.
- Turn it back into a frequency.
  - _why:_ A frequency records how often a value occurs, so it cannot be obtained by converting the mean. Multiply the old mean by the number of values to recover the total.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Mean of 6 numbers is 20. Mean of 2 is 14. What is the mean of the other 4?

**Back:** 23

**Choices:**

- 23 ✔
  - _why:_ The total of all six numbers is $6\times20=120$, while the two numbers total $2\times14=28$. The remaining mean is $(120-28)\div4=23$.
- 26
  - _why:_ This treats the two subgroup means as equally weighted and solves $(14+x)\div2=20$. The groups have different sizes; using their totals gives $(120-28)\div4=23$.
- 26.5
  - _why:_ This subtracts the subgroup mean 14 only once from the total, giving $(120-14)\div4=26.5$. Its two values actually total 28, so the answer is 23.
- 22
  - _why:_ This results from the subtraction error $120-28=88$. The correct remaining total is 92, and dividing it by the four remaining numbers gives 23.
