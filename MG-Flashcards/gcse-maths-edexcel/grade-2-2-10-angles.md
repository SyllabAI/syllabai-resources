# Angles Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 2 · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** An angle is measured in [ ___ ]; the point where its arms meet is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** An angle is measured in degrees; the point where its arms meet is the vertex.

**Choices:**

- An angle is measured in degrees; the point where its arms meet is the vertex. ✔
  - _why:_ Degrees quantify the amount of turning between the two arms of an angle. The vertex is their shared endpoint, where the two arms meet.
- An angle is measured in degrees; the point where its arms meet is the centre.
  - _why:_ Calling this point the centre confuses angle terminology with circles. Angles are measured in degrees, and the shared endpoint of their arms is called the vertex.
- An angle is measured in centimetres; the point where its arms meet is the vertex.
  - _why:_ Centimetres measure length rather than turning. An angle is measured in degrees, while the point at which its two arms meet is correctly called the vertex.
- An angle is measured in centimetres; the point where its arms meet is the endpoint.
  - _why:_ This mixes length measurement with imprecise line terminology. Angles are measured in degrees, and the particular shared endpoint where both arms meet is the vertex.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A right angle is [ ___ ]; a straight line angle is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A right angle is 90°; a straight line angle is 180°.

**Choices:**

- A right angle is 90°; a straight line angle is 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ A quarter turn measures 90°, so it forms a right angle. A half turn measures 180°, producing an angle along a straight line.
- A right angle is 180°; a straight line angle is 90°.
  - _why:_ These values have been reversed. A right angle is a quarter turn, or 90°, whereas an angle on a straight line is a half turn, or 180°.
- A right angle is 45°; a straight line angle is 180°.
  - _why:_ The straight line value is correct, but 45° is half a right angle. A complete right angle is a quarter turn and therefore measures 90°.
- A right angle is 90°; a straight line angle is 360°.
  - _why:_ The right-angle value is correct, but 360° represents a full turn. A straight line makes only a half turn, so its angle is 180°.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Classify an angle of 40°.

**Back:** Acute.

**Choices:**

- Acute. ✔
  - _why:_ An acute angle is greater than 0° but less than 90°. Since 40° lies within that interval, it is classified as acute.
- Right.
  - _why:_ A right angle must measure exactly 90°, not merely less than 90°. Since 40° is between 0° and 90°, the correct classification is acute.
- Obtuse.
  - _why:_ An obtuse angle is greater than 90° but less than 180°. Since 40° is below 90°, it is an acute angle instead.
- Reflex.
  - _why:_ A reflex angle is greater than 180° but less than 360°. Since 40° is much smaller than 180°, it is classified as acute.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Classify an angle of 130°.

**Back:** Obtuse.

**Choices:**

- Obtuse. ✔
  - _why:_ An obtuse angle is greater than 90° but less than 180°. Since 130° lies within this interval, its correct classification is obtuse.
- Acute.
  - _why:_ Acute angles are smaller than 90°. Since 130° is greater than 90° but less than 180°, the correct classification is obtuse.
- Reflex.
  - _why:_ Reflex angles are greater than 180°. Since 130° lies between 90° and 180°, it is obtuse rather than reflex.
- Right.
  - _why:_ A right angle measures exactly 90°. Since 130° exceeds 90° without reaching 180°, it must be classified as an obtuse angle.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Classify an angle of 245°.

**Back:** Reflex.

**Choices:**

- Reflex. ✔
  - _why:_ A reflex angle is greater than 180° but less than 360°. Since 245° lies in that range, it is correctly classified as reflex.
- Obtuse.
  - _why:_ Obtuse angles are between 90° and 180°. Since 245° is greater than 180° but less than 360°, it is a reflex angle.
- Acute.
  - _why:_ Acute angles are smaller than 90°, so this classification ignores the angle’s much larger turn. At 245°, the angle is correctly classified as reflex.
- Straight.
  - _why:_ A straight angle measures exactly 180°. Since 245° extends 65° beyond a straight angle, it falls in the reflex-angle range.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What tool is used to measure or draw angles?

**Back:** A protractor.

**Choices:**

- A protractor. ✔
  - _why:_ A protractor has a degree scale designed for measuring existing angles and marking given angles accurately when drawing them.
- A compass.
  - _why:_ A compass draws circles and arcs but does not provide a degree scale for measuring angles. The appropriate measuring and drawing tool is a protractor.
- A ruler.
  - _why:_ A ruler measures lengths and draws straight lines, but it has no degree scale. Angles are measured or marked out using a protractor.
- A set square.
  - _why:_ A set square provides only certain fixed angles, commonly 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°. A protractor measures or draws angles of varied sizes.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** When measuring an angle, where should the protractor's centre go?

**Back:** On the vertex.

**Choices:**

- On the vertex. ✔
  - _why:_ The protractor’s centre must coincide with the angle’s vertex so that its baseline and degree scale measure the turn accurately from the correct point.
- On the 0° mark.
  - _why:_ The 0° mark is used to choose the correct scale, not to position the centre. The protractor’s centre should be placed directly on the angle’s vertex.
- On the middle of one arm.
  - _why:_ Placing the centre partway along an arm shifts the measuring point and produces an incorrect reading. The centre must be aligned with the vertex.
- On the end of one arm.
  - _why:_ The end of an arm is not the point from which the angle opens. The protractor’s centre must be placed on the vertex where both arms meet.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a protractor, read from the [ ___ ] that lies on [ ___ ].

**Back:** On a protractor, read from the 0° line that lies on one arm of the angle.

**Choices:**

- On a protractor, read from the 0° line that lies on one arm of the angle. ✔
  - _why:_ The correct scale begins at 0° on the arm aligned with the protractor’s baseline. Following that scale to the other arm gives the angle’s measurement.
- On a protractor, read from the 180° line that lies on one arm of the angle.
  - _why:_ Starting at 180° selects the scale in the wrong direction and commonly gives the supplementary reading. Begin with the 0° line aligned to one arm.
- On a protractor, read from the 0° line that lies on the curved edge of the protractor.
  - _why:_ The curved edge contains the numbered scales but is not aligned with the angle. The relevant 0° line must lie along one arm of the angle.
- On a protractor, read from the 90° line that lies on the other arm of the angle.
  - _why:_ The 90° line is a fixed quarter-turn reference, not the starting line for general measurements. Start from the 0° line aligned with one arm.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** After marking 110° with a protractor, how do you complete the angle?

**Back:** Draw a straight line from the vertex through the mark.

**Choices:**

- Draw a straight line from the vertex through the mark. ✔
  - _why:_ The second arm must begin at the same vertex as the first arm and pass through the 110° mark to form the measured angle.
- Draw a straight line from the mark to the end of the first arm.
  - _why:_ Joining the mark to the first arm’s endpoint creates a line that does not start at the vertex. The second arm must run from the vertex through the mark.
- Draw a curved line from the vertex through the mark.
  - _why:_ An angle is formed by two straight arms sharing a vertex, not by a curved arm. Draw a straight line from the vertex through the mark.
- Draw a straight line from the mark to the protractor’s centre.
  - _why:_ The protractor’s centre should already be positioned over the vertex, so drawing towards it does not create the second arm. Draw from the vertex through the 110° mark.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles on a straight line add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles on a straight line add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ A straight line represents half of a complete 360° turn, so all adjacent angles lying along it have a total of 180°.
- 360°
  - _why:_ A full turn totals 360°, which applies to angles around a point. Angles forming a straight line make half a turn, so they add to 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ The value 90° is a quarter turn and describes a right angle. A straight line is a half turn, so its angles total 180°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ The value 270° represents three-quarters of a turn rather than a straight line. A straight line is half a turn and therefore measures 180°.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** A straight line is split into 127° and $x$. Find $x$.

**Back:** 53°.

**Choices:**

- 53°. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles on a straight line add to 180°. Therefore, subtracting the known angle gives $x=180°-127°=53°$.
- 43°.
  - _why:_ This results from subtracting 127° from 170° rather than 180°. Angles on a straight line total 180°, so $x=180°-127°=53°$.
- 63°.
  - _why:_ This is caused by an arithmetic slip in the subtraction. Using the straight-line total correctly gives $x=180°-127°=53°$, not 63°.
- 307°.
  - _why:_ Adding 127° and 180° confuses the required operation. The two adjacent angles must total 180°, so the missing angle is $180°-127°=53°$.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** Perpendicular lines meet at a [ ___ ], which is [ ___ ].

**Back:** Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, which is 90°.

**Choices:**

- Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, which is 90°. ✔
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines intersect to form four equal right angles. Each right angle is one-quarter of a full 360° turn, so it measures 90°.
- Perpendicular lines meet at an obtuse angle, which is 90°.
  - _why:_ Although 90° is the correct measurement, an angle of exactly 90° is a right angle, not an obtuse angle. Perpendicular lines meet at right angles.
- Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, which is 180°.
  - _why:_ The angle type is correct, but 180° is a straight angle rather than a right angle. Perpendicular lines intersect at 90°.
- Perpendicular lines meet at an acute angle, which is 180°.
  - _why:_ An acute angle is less than 90°, while 180° is a straight angle. Perpendicular lines instead meet at a right angle measuring 90°.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** A right angle is split into 34° and $x$. Find $x$.

**Back:** 56°.

**Choices:**

- 56°. ✔
  - _why:_ A right angle measures 90°, so its two parts must total 90°. Therefore, $x=90°-34°=56°$.
- 66°.
  - _why:_ This comes from subtracting 34° incorrectly from 100° rather than using the right-angle total. The correct calculation is $90°-34°=56°$.
- 124°.
  - _why:_ Adding 34° to 90° gives an angle larger than the whole right angle. The missing part must be found by $90°-34°=56°$.
- 46°.
  - _why:_ This reflects a subtraction error, often treating 90 as if its units digit were larger than 4. Correct borrowing gives $90°-34°=56°$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Angles around a point add to what?

**Back:** 360°.

**Choices:**

- 360°. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles around a point form one complete turn. Since a complete turn measures 360°, all the angles around the point add to 360°.
- 180°.
  - _why:_ The value 180° is only half a turn and is the total for angles on a straight line. Around a point, the angles complete a full 360° turn.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ The value 90° is one-quarter of a complete turn and represents a right angle. Angles around a point make a full turn totalling 360°.
- 540°.
  - _why:_ The value 540° combines one and a half turns, so it exceeds the total around a single point. One complete turn contains exactly 360°.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Angles around a point are 90°, 105°, 118° and $x$. Find $x$.

**Back:** 47°.

**Choices:**

- 47°. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles around a point total 360°. The known angles sum to $90°+105°+118°=313°$, leaving $x=360°-313°=47°$.
- 57°.
  - _why:_ This follows from misadding the known angles as 303° instead of 313°. Their correct total is 313°, so $x=360°-313°=47°$.
- 137°.
  - _why:_ This omits the 90° angle and subtracts only 105° and 118° from 360°. All three known angles must be included, giving $x=47°$.
- 313°.
  - _why:_ This is the total of the three known angles, not the missing angle. Subtract that subtotal from 360° to obtain $x=47°$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** The three angles inside any triangle add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** The three angles inside any triangle add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ The interior angles of every triangle have a fixed total of 180°, regardless of the triangle’s side lengths or individual angle sizes.
- 360°
  - _why:_ The value 360° is the total around a point, not inside a triangle. The three interior angles of every triangle add to 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ The value 90° is the measure of one right angle and can be a single triangle angle. It is not the total, which is always 180°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ The value 270° is three-quarters of a complete turn and is too large for a triangle’s interior-angle total. The correct total is 180°.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** A triangle has angles 66°, 41° and $x$. Find $x$.

**Back:** 73°.

**Choices:**

- 73°. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles in a triangle total 180°. Subtracting 66° and 41° gives $x = 180° - 66° - 41° = 73°$.
- 107°.
  - _why:_ Adding 66° and 41° gives 107°, but that is the sum of the known angles, not the missing angle. Subtracting 107° from 180° gives 73°.
- 74°.
  - _why:_ This results from an arithmetic slip when subtracting the known angles. Since triangle angles total 180°, the calculation is $180° - 66° - 41° = 73°$.
- 139°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts only the 41° angle from 180° and ignores the 66° angle. Both known angles must be subtracted, giving $x = 73°$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** In an isosceles triangle, equal sides imply what about the base angles?

**Back:** The base angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- The base angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ In an isosceles triangle, the two equal sides are opposite two equal angles. These opposite angles are the base angles, so they have the same size.
- The base angles are supplementary.
  - _why:_ Supplementary angles total 180°, but the base angles of an isosceles triangle do not generally do this. Equal sides imply that the opposite base angles are equal.
- The base angles are perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines meet at 90°, which is unrelated to the equal-side property of an isosceles triangle. The angles opposite the equal sides are equal.
- The base angles are unequal.
  - _why:_ This reverses the defining angle property of an isosceles triangle. Because the two sides are equal, the angles opposite those sides - the base angles - are equal.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** An isosceles triangle has base angles 68° and 68°. Find the top angle.

**Back:** 44°.

**Choices:**

- 44°. ✔
  - _why:_ The angles in a triangle sum to 180°. The two base angles total 136°, so the top angle is $180° - 136° = 44°$.
- 136°.
  - _why:_ This is the sum of the two base angles, not the remaining angle. Subtracting their total, 136°, from 180° gives the top angle of 44°.
- 112°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts only one 68° base angle from 180°. Both base angles must be included, so the top angle is $180° - 68° - 68° = 44°$.
- 68°.
  - _why:_ This assumes all three angles are equal because the triangle is isosceles. Only the base angles are equal here, and the angle sum gives a top angle of 44°.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** A square corner and an equilateral-triangle corner meet at a shared point. Find the combined angle.

**Back:** 150°.

**Choices:**

- 150°. ✔
  - _why:_ A square corner is 90° and an equilateral-triangle corner is 60°. Their combined angle is therefore $90° + 60° = 150°$.
- 120°.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the equilateral-triangle corner as 30°. Every interior angle of an equilateral triangle is 60°, so the combined angle is $90° + 60° = 150°$.
- 180°.
  - _why:_ This assumes the two corners form a straight angle. Their actual sizes are 90° and 60°, so adding them gives a combined angle of 150°, not 180°.
- 210°.
  - _why:_ This uses the reflex angle around the shared point rather than the combined interior angle. The required angle is $90° + 60° = 150°$; the remaining reflex portion is 210°.
