## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a number line, numbers get [ ___ ] moving right and [ ___ ] moving left.

**Back:** On a number line, numbers get bigger moving right and smaller moving left.

**Choices:**

- On a number line, numbers get bigger moving right and smaller moving left. ✔
  - _why:_ Values increase as you move right along a number line and decrease as you move left, so numbers become bigger to the right and smaller to the left.
- On a number line, numbers get smaller moving right and bigger moving left.
  - _why:_ This reverses the direction of increasing values. Numbers become bigger when moving right along a number line and smaller when moving left.
- On a number line, numbers get bigger moving right and bigger moving left.
  - _why:_ Although values increase when moving right, they do not also increase when moving left. Numbers become smaller as you move left along a number line.
- On a number line, numbers get smaller moving right and smaller moving left.
  - _why:_ Although values decrease when moving left, they do not also decrease when moving right. Numbers become bigger as you move right along a number line.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Which is smaller: $-6$ or $-2$?

**Back:** $-6$

**Choices:**

- $-6$ ✔
  - _why:_ Negative numbers farther to the left on a number line are smaller. Since $-6$ lies to the left of $-2$, it is the smaller value.
- $-2$
  - _why:_ This treats the number closer to zero as smaller. Among negative numbers, the value farther from zero is smaller, so $-6$ is smaller than $-2$.
- $2$
  - _why:_ Dropping the negative sign changes the value to a positive number. Both given values are negative, and $-6$ is smaller because it lies farther left.
- $6$
  - _why:_ Using the magnitude alone ignores the negative signs. On the number line, $-6$ lies to the left of $-2$, making $-6$ the smaller number.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** Adding a positive number moves [ ___ ]; subtracting a positive number moves [ ___ ].

**Back:** Adding a positive number moves right; subtracting a positive number moves left.

**Choices:**

- Adding a positive number moves right; subtracting a positive number moves left. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding a positive value increases a number, which means moving right on the number line. Subtracting a positive value decreases it, which means moving left.
- Adding a positive number moves left; subtracting a positive number moves right.
  - _why:_ This reverses both operations. Adding a positive number increases the value and moves right, while subtracting a positive number decreases the value and moves left.
- Adding a positive number moves right; subtracting a positive number moves right.
  - _why:_ Subtracting a positive number does not increase the value. Addition moves right, but subtraction of a positive number decreases the value and therefore moves left.
- Adding a positive number moves left; subtracting a positive number moves left.
  - _why:_ Adding a positive number does not decrease the value. It moves right on the number line, whereas subtracting a positive number moves left.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Adding a negative number moves [ ___ ]; subtracting a negative number moves [ ___ ].

**Back:** Adding a negative number moves left; subtracting a negative number moves right.

**Choices:**

- Adding a negative number moves left; subtracting a negative number moves right. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding a negative decreases the value, so it moves left. Subtracting a negative is equivalent to adding a positive, so it moves right.
- Adding a negative number moves right; subtracting a negative number moves left.
  - _why:_ This ignores how the negative sign changes each operation. Adding a negative moves left, while subtracting a negative becomes addition and moves right.
- Adding a negative number moves left; subtracting a negative number moves left.
  - _why:_ Adding a negative does move left, but subtracting a negative has the opposite effect. It is equivalent to adding a positive, so it moves right.
- Adding a negative number moves right; subtracting a negative number moves right.
  - _why:_ Subtracting a negative moves right, but adding a negative does not. Adding a value below zero decreases the result, so it moves left.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $6 - 11$.

**Back:** $-5$

**Choices:**

- $-5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Starting at $6$ and subtracting $11$ means moving eleven places left on the number line. This passes zero and finishes at $-5$.
- $5$
  - _why:_ This finds the difference between $11$ and $6$ but loses the sign. Since more is subtracted than the starting value, the result is $-5$.
- $-17$
  - _why:_ This effectively makes both numbers negative and adds their magnitudes. Starting from positive $6$ and moving $11$ left gives $-5$, not $-17$.
- $17$
  - _why:_ This adds $6$ and $11$ instead of subtracting. Subtracting $11$ from $6$ moves past zero by five places, giving $-5$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $-4 + (-6)$.

**Back:** $-10$

**Choices:**

- $-10$ ✔
  - _why:_ Adding two negative numbers means adding their magnitudes and keeping the negative sign. Since $4+6=10$, the result is $-10$.
- $-2$
  - _why:_ This subtracts the magnitudes instead of adding them. Both terms are negative, so their magnitudes add: $4+6=10$, giving $-10$.
- $10$
  - _why:_ The magnitudes have been added correctly, but the negative sign has been lost. Adding two negative numbers produces a negative result, so the answer is $-10$.
- $2$
  - _why:_ This both subtracts the magnitudes and gives the result the wrong sign. Adding two negatives combines their magnitudes, so $-4+(-6)=-10$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Subtracting a negative is the same as [ ___ ] the positive version.

**Back:** Subtracting a negative is the same as adding the positive version.

**Choices:**

- adding ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting a negative reverses the subtraction operation, making it equivalent to adding the corresponding positive number. For example, $5-(-2)=5+2$.
- subtracting
  - _why:_ Keeping subtraction unchanged overlooks the double negative. Subtracting a negative changes to adding the positive version, as in $5-(-2)=5+2$.
- multiplying
  - _why:_ The rule that two negative signs produce a positive does not turn subtraction into multiplication. Subtracting a negative is equivalent to adding its positive version.
- dividing
  - _why:_ Negative signs can affect division signs, but no division occurs here. Subtracting a negative changes the operation to adding the corresponding positive number.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $9 - (-4)$.

**Back:** $13$

**Choices:**

- $13$ ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting a negative is equivalent to adding the corresponding positive, so $9 - (-4) = 9 + 4 = 13$.
- $5$
  - _why:_ This treats the two minus signs as a single subtraction sign. Subtracting a negative becomes addition, so $9 - (-4) = 9 + 4 = 13$.
- $-13$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly gives the result a negative sign. The two minus signs combine to make addition, giving $9 + 4 = 13$.
- $-5$
  - _why:_ This subtracts $4$ from $9$ and then incorrectly makes the result negative. Subtracting $-4$ means adding $4$, so the answer is $13$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Choose two numbers from $-8$, $-3$, $3$, $8$ that add to $-11$.

**Back:** $-8$ and $-3$

**Choices:**

- $-8$ and $-3$ ✔
  - _why:_ Adding the two negative numbers combines their magnitudes: $-8 + (-3) = -(8 + 3) = -11$.
- $-8$ and $3$
  - _why:_ These numbers have different signs, so their magnitudes are subtracted: $-8 + 3 = -5$. The pair that totals $-11$ is $-8$ and $-3$.
- $-3$ and $8$
  - _why:_ These numbers total $5$ because $-3 + 8 = 5$. To obtain $-11$, both selected numbers must be negative: $-8$ and $-3$.
- $3$ and $8$
  - _why:_ Adding two positive numbers gives $3 + 8 = 11$, not $-11$. Choosing their negative counterparts, $-8$ and $-3$, gives the required total.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** If two numbers add to a negative answer, the [ ___ ] number or numbers have the bigger effect.

**Back:** If two numbers add to a negative answer, the negative number or numbers have the bigger effect.

**Choices:**

- negative ✔
  - _why:_ A negative total means the negative values outweigh the positive values. Therefore, the negative number or numbers have the greater overall effect on the sum.
- positive
  - _why:_ Positive numbers cannot have the bigger effect if the resulting sum is negative. The negative number or numbers must outweigh the positives.
- zero
  - _why:_ Zero has no effect when added, so it cannot determine that a sum is negative. The negative number or numbers must have the bigger effect.
- even
  - _why:_ Even numbers are not inherently more influential in addition; their signs and magnitudes matter. A negative total shows that the negative numbers have the bigger effect.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** For multiplication or division, what sign do same signs give?

**Back:** Positive

**Choices:**

- Positive ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying or dividing two numbers with matching signs always gives a positive result, whether both original numbers are positive or both are negative.
- Negative
  - _why:_ A negative result comes from different signs, not matching signs. Two positive signs or two negative signs give a positive result in multiplication and division.
- Zero
  - _why:_ Matching signs do not make a product or quotient zero unless one number itself is zero. The sign rule gives a positive result.
- The sign of the larger number
  - _why:_ The sizes of the numbers affect the magnitude, not the sign of the result. For multiplication or division, matching signs always give a positive sign.

**Back:** $-35$

**Choices:**

- $-35$ ✔
  - _why:_ The signs are different, so the product is negative. Since $7 \times 5 = 35$, it follows that $-7 \times 5 = -35$.

  - _why:_ This has the correct magnitude but ignores the different-sign rule. A negative number multiplied by a positive number gives a negative result, so the answer is $-35$.

  - _why:_ This comes from adding $-7$ and $5$ instead of multiplying them. The required calculation is $7 \times 5 = 35$, with a negative sign, giving $-35$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Back:** $-3$

- $-3$ ✔
  - _why:_ Divide both sides of the equation by $6$. Since $-18 \div 6=-3$, multiplying the resulting number by $6$ gives the required $-18$.
- $3$
  - _why:_ Dropping the negative sign is the tempting error here. A positive number multiplied by $6$ remains positive, while $-18 \div 6=-3$.
- $-12$
  - _why:_ Adding $6$ to $-18$ does not reverse multiplication by $6$. The inverse operation is division, and $-18 \div 6=-3$.
- $-108$
  - _why:_ Multiplying $-18$ by $6$ repeats the original operation instead of undoing it. Dividing $-18$ by $6$ gives the correct number, $-3$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between $-5^\circ\text{C}$ and $6^\circ\text{C}$?

**Back:** $11^\circ\text{C}$

**Choices:**

- $11^\circ\text{C}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The difference is the distance between the temperatures. It takes $5^\circ\text{C}$ to move from $-5^\circ\text{C}$ to zero and another $6^\circ\text{C}$ to reach $6^\circ\text{C}$, totalling $11^\circ\text{C}$.
- $1^\circ\text{C}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the magnitudes, $6-5$, ignores that the temperatures lie on opposite sides of zero. Their distance is $5+6=11^\circ\text{C}$.
- $-11^\circ\text{C}$
  - _why:_ Calculating $-5-6$ gives a directed change rather than the positive distance between the temperatures. The difference is the magnitude of the gap, $11^\circ\text{C}$.
- $5^\circ\text{C}$
  - _why:_ This counts only the distance from $-5^\circ\text{C}$ to zero. The additional distance from zero to $6^\circ\text{C}$ makes the total difference $11^\circ\text{C}$.
