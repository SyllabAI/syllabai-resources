## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the expression $3x^2 - 5x + 7$, which term is the constant term?

**Back:** 7

**Choices:**

- 7 ✔
  - _why:_ The constant term is the term containing no variable. In $3x^2 - 5x + 7$, only 7 has no factor of $x$.
- 3
  - _why:_ The number 3 is the coefficient of $x^2$, not the constant term. The constant term is 7 because it does not contain $x$.
- -5
  - _why:_ The number -5 is the coefficient of $x$, not the constant term. The correct answer is 7 because that term contains no variable.
- 0
  - _why:_ A constant term is present, so it is not implicitly zero. The correct answer is 7 because 7 is the term without a variable.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is the number multiplying a variable, such as 3 in $3x$.

**Back:** A coefficient is the number multiplying a variable, such as 3 in $3x$.

**Choices:**

- coefficient ✔
  - _why:_ A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable. For example, the coefficient of $x$ in $3x$ is 3.
- constant
  - _why:_ A constant is a term without a variable, rather than a number multiplying one. The correct term is coefficient, as 3 is the coefficient in $3x$.
- exponent
  - _why:_ An exponent shows the power to which a variable is raised, such as 2 in $x^2$. The number multiplying a variable is its coefficient.
- variable
  - _why:_ A variable is the letter representing an unknown or changing value. The number multiplying that variable is called its coefficient, such as 3 in $3x$.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** To [ ___ ] means to put an expression back into brackets, while to expand means to remove them.

**Back:** To factorise means to put an expression back into brackets, while to expand means to remove them.

**Choices:**

- factorise ✔
  - _why:_ Factorising rewrites an expression as a product of factors, usually shown using brackets. It is the reverse process of expanding brackets.
- simplify
  - _why:_ Simplifying makes an expression more concise but does not necessarily put it into brackets. Factorise is the correct term for reversing expansion and forming a product.
- expand
  - _why:_ Expanding removes brackets by multiplication, so it is the opposite process. Factorise is the correct term for putting an expression back into brackets.
- rearrange
  - _why:_ Rearranging changes the order or position of terms, often within an equation. Factorise specifically means rewriting an expression as factors inside brackets.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** To factorise means to put an expression back into brackets, while to [ ___ ] means to remove them.

**Back:** To factorise means to put an expression back into brackets, while to expand means to remove them.

**Choices:**

- expand ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding removes brackets by multiplying every relevant term and then collecting like terms. It is the reverse process of factorising an expression.
- simplify
  - _why:_ Simplifying may involve collecting like terms or reducing an expression, but it does not specifically mean removing brackets. The required term is expand.
- factorise
  - _why:_ Factorising puts an expression into brackets rather than removing them. The opposite process, which multiplies out the brackets, is called expanding.
- solve
  - _why:_ Solving finds the value of an unknown in an equation; it does not mean removing brackets. The correct term is expand, which means multiplying out brackets.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify the expression $x^2 + 7x - 3x - 21$ by collecting like terms.

**Back:** $$x^2 + 4x - 21$

**Choices:**

- $$x^2 + 4x - 21$ ✔
  - _why:_ The like terms are $7x$ and $-3x$, which combine to give $4x$. The $x^2$ and constant terms remain unchanged.
- $$x^2 + 10x - 21$
  - _why:_ This treats $-3x$ as positive and adds 7 and 3. Keeping the negative sign gives $7x-3x=4x$, so the result is $x^2+4x-21$.
- $$x^2 + 4x + 21$
  - _why:_ The variable terms have been collected correctly, but the constant’s sign has changed. The original constant is $-21$, so the answer is $x^2+4x-21$.
- $$5x^2 - 21$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly combines $x^2$ and $x$ terms, which are not like terms. Only $7x$ and $-3x$ combine, giving $x^2+4x-21$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** When expanding two brackets, how many multiplications must you perform to avoid missing terms?

**Back:** 4

**Choices:**

- 4 ✔
  - _why:_ Each of the two terms in the first bracket must multiply each of the two terms in the second bracket, giving $2\times2=4$ multiplications.
- 2
  - _why:_ This counts only one multiplication for each term in the first bracket. Each must multiply both terms in the second bracket, so 4 multiplications are required.
- 3
  - _why:_ Three multiplications usually means one cross-term has been missed. Two terms multiplied by two terms produce four separate multiplications before like terms are collected.
- 6
  - _why:_ This overcounts the required products. With two terms in each bracket, every first-bracket term pairs with two second-bracket terms, giving exactly 4 multiplications.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] expression has a squared variable as its highest power, such as $x^2 + 4x - 12$.

**Back:** A quadratic expression has a squared variable as its highest power, such as $x^2 + 4x - 12$.

**Choices:**

- quadratic ✔
  - _why:_ A quadratic expression has highest variable power 2. Since the leading power in $x^2+4x-12$ is $x^2$, the expression is quadratic.
- linear
  - _why:_ A linear expression has highest variable power 1, such as $3x+2$. An expression whose highest power is 2 is called quadratic.
- cubic
  - _why:_ A cubic expression has highest variable power 3, such as $x^3+2x$. A highest power of 2 makes the expression quadratic.
- exponential
  - _why:_ In an exponential expression, the variable appears in the exponent, such as $2^x$. A polynomial with highest power $x^2$ is quadratic.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(x + 5)(x - 4)$.

**Back:** $$x^2 + x - 20$

**Choices:**

- $$x^2 + x - 20$ ✔
  - _why:_ The four products are $x^2$, $-4x$, $5x$, and $-20$. Combining the middle terms gives $-4x+5x=x$, producing $x^2+x-20$.
- $$x^2 + 9x - 20$
  - _why:_ This treats the cross-term $-4x$ as positive, giving $5x+4x$. Keeping its negative sign gives $5x-4x=x$, so the result is $x^2+x-20$.
- $$x^2 - x - 20$
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction when combining the cross-terms. Since $5x-4x=x$, not $-x$, the correct expansion is $x^2+x-20$.
- $$x^2 + x + 20$
  - _why:_ The constant product has the wrong sign. Multiplying $5$ by $-4$ gives $-20$, so the correct expansion is $x^2+x-20$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the result of multiplying $3x$ by $2x$?

**Back:** $$6x^2$

**Choices:**

- $$6x^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply the coefficients to get $3 \times 2 = 6$, then multiply $x \times x = x^2$, giving $6x^2$.
- $$6x$
  - _why:_ This omits the index produced when the variables are multiplied. Since $x \times x = x^2$, the correct product is $6x^2$.
- $$5x^2$
  - _why:_ This adds the coefficients instead of multiplying them. The coefficients give $3 \times 2 = 6$, while $x \times x = x^2$, so the answer is $6x^2$.
- $$6x^3$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly adds an extra power of $x$. Multiplying $x^1$ by $x^1$ gives $x^{1+1}=x^2$, so the correct result is $6x^2$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(4x - 1)(2x + 3)$.

**Back:** $$8x^2 + 10x - 3$

**Choices:**

- $$8x^2 + 10x - 3$ ✔
  - _why:_ The four products are $8x^2$, $12x$, $-2x$, and $-3$. Combining the middle terms gives $12x-2x=10x$, producing $8x^2+10x-3$.
- $$8x^2 + 14x - 3$
  - _why:_ This treats the product $(-1)(2x)$ as $+2x$. It is $-2x$, so the middle terms combine to $12x-2x=10x$ and the answer is $8x^2+10x-3$.
- $$8x^2 + 10x + 3$
  - _why:_ The constant sign is wrong: multiplying $-1$ by $+3$ gives $-3$, not $+3$. The correctly expanded expression is $8x^2+10x-3$.
- $$8x^2 + 12x - 3$
  - _why:_ This misses the inner product $(-1)(2x)=-2x$. All four products must be included, so the middle coefficient is $12-2=10$ and the result is $8x^2+10x-3$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** When expanding brackets, a negative multiplied by a negative results in a [ ___ ] value.

**Back:** When expanding brackets, a negative multiplied by a negative results in a positive value.

**Choices:**

- positive ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying two numbers with the same sign gives a positive result. Therefore, a negative multiplied by another negative produces a positive value.
- negative
  - _why:_ A negative result comes from multiplying numbers with different signs. Two negative factors have the same sign, so their product is positive.
- zero
  - _why:_ A product is zero only when at least one factor is zero. Two negative factors instead multiply to produce a positive value.
- unchanged
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not leave the sign unchanged automatically. The sign rule states that two negative factors produce a positive value.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step to expanding a squared bracket like $(b - 6)^2$?

**Back:** Rewrite it as two brackets: $(b - 6)(b - 6)$.

**Choices:**

- Rewrite it as two brackets: $(b - 6)(b - 6)$. ✔
  - _why:_ A squared bracket means the entire bracket is multiplied by itself. Therefore, $(b-6)^2$ must first be written as $(b-6)(b-6)$.
- Rewrite it as two brackets: $(b - 6)(b + 6)$.
  - _why:_ Changing the second sign creates a difference of two squares, not a squared bracket. Both factors must be identical, so the correct rewrite is $(b-6)(b-6)$.
- Rewrite it as two terms: $b^2 - 6^2$.
  - _why:_ Squaring a bracket does not mean squaring each term separately; this misses the middle term. The correct first step is to write $(b-6)(b-6)$.
- Rewrite it as two brackets: $(b - 6)(b - 12)$.
  - _why:_ Doubling the constant inside one bracket misinterprets the square. A squared bracket is multiplied by an identical copy, so it becomes $(b-6)(b-6)$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(b - 6)^2$.

**Back:** $$b^2 - 12b + 36$

**Choices:**

- $$b^2 - 12b + 36$ ✔
  - _why:_ Writing $(b-6)^2$ as $(b-6)(b-6)$ gives $b^2-6b-6b+36$. Combining the middle terms produces $b^2-12b+36$.
- $$b^2 - 36$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the expression as a difference of two squares and omits the middle term. Expanding two identical brackets gives $b^2-12b+36$.
- $$b^2 - 12b - 36$
  - _why:_ The constant sign is wrong because $(-6)(-6)=+36$. Including the two middle products gives the correct expansion $b^2-12b+36$.
- $$b^2 - 6b + 36$
  - _why:_ This includes only one of the two middle products. Both are $-6b$, so they combine to $-12b$, giving $b^2-12b+36$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** When factorising $x^2 + bx + c$, what two properties must the chosen number pair satisfy?

**Back:** Multiply to give $c$ (the product)Add to give $b$ (the sum)

**Choices:**

- Multiply to give $c$ (the product)Add to give $b$ (the sum) ✔
  - _why:_ In $(x+m)(x+n)$, the constant is $mn$ and the middle coefficient is $m+n$. Therefore, the numbers must multiply to $c$ and add to $b$.
- Add to give $c$ (the sum)Multiply to give $b$ (the product)
  - _why:_ This reverses the product and sum conditions. Expanding $(x+m)(x+n)$ shows that $mn=c$ and $m+n=b$, so the pair multiplies to $c$ and adds to $b$.
- Multiply to give $b$ (the product)Add to give $c$ (the sum)
  - _why:_ This assigns the coefficients to the wrong operations. The bracket constants create $c$ when multiplied and create $b$ when added, so the pair multiplies to $c$ and adds to $b$.
- Multiply to give $c$ (the product)Subtract to give $b$ (the difference)
  - _why:_ The middle coefficient comes from adding the two cross terms, not subtracting the bracket constants. The chosen numbers must multiply to $c$ and add to $b$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 + 9x + 20$.

**Back:** $$(x + 4)(x + 5)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x + 4)(x + 5)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numbers $4$ and $5$ multiply to $20$ and add to $9$. Therefore, $x^2+9x+20$ factorises as $(x+4)(x+5)$.
- $$(x + 2)(x + 10)$
  - _why:_ Although $2$ and $10$ multiply to $20$, they add to $12$, not $9$. The required pair is $4$ and $5$, giving $(x+4)(x+5)$.
- $$(x - 4)(x - 5)$
  - _why:_ The constants multiply to $20$, but their sum is $-9$, producing a negative middle term. A positive $9x$ requires $(x+4)(x+5)$.
- $$(x + 1)(x + 20)$
  - _why:_ Although $1$ and $20$ multiply to $20$, they add to $21$, not $9$. The numbers $4$ and $5$ satisfy both conditions, giving $(x+4)(x+5)$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** If a quadratic has a positive constant and a negative middle term, what are the signs in the brackets?

**Back:** Both are negative.

**Choices:**

- Both are negative. ✔
  - _why:_ Two negative constants multiply to give a positive constant and add to give a negative middle coefficient. Therefore, both signs in the brackets must be negative.
- Both are positive.
  - _why:_ Two positive constants give a positive constant but also a positive middle coefficient. To produce a negative middle term while keeping the constant positive, both signs must be negative.
- The first is positive and the second is negative.
  - _why:_ Opposite signs make the bracket constants multiply to a negative value, contradicting the positive constant. Both signs must be negative to give a positive product and negative sum.
- The first is negative and the second is positive.
  - _why:_ Using one negative and one positive sign makes the constant term negative. A positive constant with a negative middle term requires two negative bracket constants.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the bracket signs if the constant term of a quadratic is negative?

**Back:** One positive and one negative.

**Choices:**

- One positive and one negative. ✔
  - _why:_ A negative constant comes from multiplying numbers with opposite signs. Therefore, the two brackets must contain one positive term and one negative term.
- One positive and one positive.
  - _why:_ Two positive terms multiply to give a positive constant. A negative constant requires opposite signs, so there must be one positive sign and one negative sign.
- One negative and one negative.
  - _why:_ Two negative terms also multiply to give a positive constant. To produce a negative constant, one bracket must be positive and the other negative.
- The signs depend only on the linear term.
  - _why:_ The linear term helps determine which signed numbers are used, but the constant determines their sign relationship. A negative product requires one positive and one negative sign.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $n^2 - 4n - 21$.

**Back:** $$(n - 7)(n + 3)$

**Choices:**

- $$(n - 7)(n + 3)$ ✔
  - _why:_ The numbers $-7$ and $3$ multiply to $-21$ and add to $-4$. Expanding the brackets therefore gives $n^2-4n-21$.
- $$(n - 3)(n + 7)$
  - _why:_ The numbers $-3$ and $7$ multiply to $-21$ but add to $4$, producing $n^2+4n-21$. The required pair is $-7$ and $3$.
- $$(n - 7)(n - 3)$
  - _why:_ Using two negative signs makes the constant positive because $(-7)(-3)=21$. The required factors have opposite signs: $(n-7)(n+3)$.
- $$(n + 7)(n - 3)$
  - _why:_ These constants multiply to $-21$, but they add to $4$, so the middle term would be $+4n$. They must instead be $-7$ and $3$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** The difference of two squares formula is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The difference of two squares formula is $\boxed{a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)}$.

**Choices:**

- $$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding $(a-b)(a+b)$ gives $a^2+ab-ab-b^2$. The middle terms cancel, leaving precisely the difference $a^2-b^2$.
- $$a^2 + b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$
  - _why:_ The product $(a-b)(a+b)$ equals $a^2-b^2$, not a sum of squares. Over the real numbers, $a^2+b^2$ does not factorise using this identity.
- $$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring $(a-b)$ produces $a^2-2ab+b^2$, including a middle term and a positive $b^2$. Difference of squares uses conjugate brackets instead.
- $$a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring $(a+b)$ produces $a^2+2ab+b^2$, so neither the middle term nor the final sign matches. The correct factors have opposite signs.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise $x^2 - 64$.

**Back:** $$(x - 8)(x + 8)$

**Choices:**

- $$(x - 8)(x + 8)$ ✔
  - _why:_ Since $64=8^2$, the expression is $x^2-8^2$. Applying the difference of two squares identity gives $(x-8)(x+8)$.
- $$(x - 8)(x - 8)$
  - _why:_ Using two negative signs gives a positive constant and a middle term: $(x-8)^2=x^2-16x+64$. Difference of squares requires opposite bracket signs.
- $$(x + 8)(x + 8)$
  - _why:_ Using two positive signs gives $(x+8)^2=x^2+16x+64$, not $x^2-64$. The factors must have opposite signs so the middle terms cancel.
- $$(x - 4)(x + 4)$
  - _why:_ This confuses $64$ with $4^2$ rather than $8^2$. Expanding gives $x^2-16$, whereas the required factorisation is $(x-8)(x+8)$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $x^2 + 64$ not a difference of two squares?

**Back:** It contains a plus sign instead of a subtraction sign.

**Choices:**

- It contains a plus sign instead of a subtraction sign. ✔
  - _why:_ The difference of two squares identity requires subtraction: $a^2-b^2$. Although both $x^2$ and $64$ are squares, they are joined by addition.
- It contains a subtraction sign instead of a plus sign.
  - _why:_ The expression actually contains addition, not subtraction. That plus sign is precisely why the difference of two squares identity cannot be applied.
- It contains terms that are not both perfect squares.
  - _why:_ Both terms are perfect squares because $x^2=(x)^2$ and $64=8^2$. The obstacle is the plus sign rather than either term’s form.
- It has a coefficient of one instead of two.
  - _why:_ A coefficient of $1$ makes $x^2$ a perfect square and does not prevent factorisation. The identity fails here because the squares are added rather than subtracted.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Factorise the perfect square $x^2 - 16x + 64$.

**Back:** $$(x - 8)^2$

**Choices:**

- $$(x - 8)^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Squaring $(x-8)$ gives $x^2-16x+64$: the middle term is $2(x)(-8)=-16x$, and the constant is $(-8)^2=64$.
- $$(x + 8)^2$
  - _why:_ The positive sign produces a positive middle term, since $(x+8)^2=x^2+16x+64$. The negative middle term requires $(x-8)^2$.
- $$(x - 4)^2$
  - _why:_ Halving $16$ correctly gives $8$, not $4$. Squaring $(x-4)$ produces $x^2-8x+16$, so both the middle and constant terms are wrong.
- $$(x - 8)(x + 8)$
  - _why:_ Opposite signs make the middle terms cancel, giving $x^2-64$. A perfect-square trinomial needs two identical negative factors, so the answer is $(x-8)^2$.
