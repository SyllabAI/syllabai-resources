## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** For a circle with radius $r$, what is the area formula?

**Back:** $\pi r^2$

**Choices:**

- $\pi r^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ The area of a circle is found by multiplying $\pi$ by the square of its radius, giving $A = \pi r^2$.
- $2\pi r$
  - _why:_ This is the circumference formula, which measures the distance around the circle. The area formula squares the radius, so the correct answer is $\pi r^2$.
- $\pi r$
  - _why:_ This omits the required square on the radius, so it does not calculate a two-dimensional area. The correct area formula is $\pi r^2$.
- $\pi r^3$
  - _why:_ Cubing the radius would produce cubic units, which are used for volume rather than area. A circle’s area is calculated using $\pi r^2$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** For a circle with radius $r$, what is the circumference formula?

**Back:** $2\pi r$

**Choices:**

- $2\pi r$ ✔
  - _why:_ The circumference is the distance around a circle. Since the diameter is $2r$ and circumference is $\pi$ times the diameter, the formula is $2\pi r$.
- $\pi r^2$
  - _why:_ This calculates the circle’s area rather than the distance around it. Circumference uses the radius without squaring it, giving the correct formula $2\pi r$.
- $\pi r$
  - _why:_ This misses the factor of $2$ caused by the diameter being twice the radius. The circumference formula is therefore $2\pi r$.
- $2\pi r^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring the radius incorrectly introduces square units, which measure area rather than length. The circumference is a length and is calculated using $2\pi r$.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The diameter of a circle is [ ___ ].

**Back:** The diameter of a circle is twice the radius.

**Choices:**

- twice the radius ✔
  - _why:_ The radius runs from the centre to the circumference, while the diameter crosses the full circle through the centre. It therefore consists of two radii.
- half the radius
  - _why:_ This reverses the relationship between radius and diameter. The radius is half the diameter, so the diameter is correctly described as twice the radius.
- equal to the radius
  - _why:_ This overlooks that a diameter extends from one side of the circle to the other through the centre. Its length is twice the radius.
- four times the radius
  - _why:_ A diameter contains two radii placed end to end, not four. Therefore, the diameter of a circle is twice the radius.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** An arc is part of the [ ___ ] of a circle.

**Back:** An arc is part of the circumference of a circle.

**Choices:**

- circumference ✔
  - _why:_ An arc is a curved section of the boundary of a circle. Since that complete boundary is the circumference, an arc forms part of the circumference.
- radius
  - _why:_ A radius is a straight line from the centre to the circumference, whereas an arc is curved. An arc is part of the circle’s circumference.
- diameter
  - _why:_ A diameter is a straight line across the circle through its centre, not part of its curved edge. An arc is part of the circumference.
- area
  - _why:_ Area describes the two-dimensional space inside a circle, while an arc is a length along its boundary. It is therefore part of the circumference.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What fraction of a full circle is a sector with angle $\theta$?

**Back:** $\frac{\theta}{360}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{\theta}{360}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A full circle contains $360^\circ$, so a sector with angle $\theta$ occupies the fraction $\theta$ divided by $360$ of the complete circle.
- $\frac{\theta}{180}$
  - _why:_ Dividing by $180$ compares the sector with a semicircle rather than a full circle. Since a full turn is $360^\circ$, the correct fraction is $\frac{\theta}{360}$.
- $\frac{360}{\theta}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the required fraction and can produce a value greater than $1$ for a minor sector. The sector’s fraction is $\frac{\theta}{360}$.
- $\frac{\theta}{2\pi}$
  - _why:_ The denominator $2\pi$ applies when the angle is measured in radians. Here $\theta$ is compared with $360^\circ$, so the fraction is $\frac{\theta}{360}$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What fraction of a full circle is a $120^\circ$ sector?

**Back:** $\frac{1}{3}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{1}{3}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A full circle is $360^\circ$, and $120 \div 360 = \frac{1}{3}$. Therefore, a $120^\circ$ sector occupies one third of the circle.
- $\frac{2}{3}$
  - _why:_ This is the fraction occupied by the remaining $240^\circ$ major sector. The $120^\circ$ sector itself is $\frac{120}{360} = \frac{1}{3}$.
- $\frac{1}{6}$
  - _why:_ This results from incorrectly comparing $120^\circ$ with $720^\circ$ or halving the correct fraction. Dividing by $360^\circ$ gives $\frac{120}{360} = \frac{1}{3}$.
- $\frac{1}{4}$
  - _why:_ One quarter of a circle corresponds to $90^\circ$, not $120^\circ$. Since $120 \div 360 = \frac{1}{3}$, the sector is one third.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** A sector is made from two [ ___ ] and the [ ___ ] between them.

**Back:** A sector is made from two radii and the arc between them.

**Choices:**

- A sector is made from two radii and the arc between them. ✔
  - _why:_ A sector is the region enclosed by two straight radii extending from the centre and the curved arc of the circumference joining their endpoints.
- A sector is made from two diameters and the arc between them.
  - _why:_ Diameters cross the entire circle and do not form the two sides of a general sector. A sector is bounded by two radii and the arc between them.
- A sector is made from two radii and the chord between them.
  - _why:_ A chord is a straight line joining two points on the circumference and helps bound a segment, not a sector. A sector uses the curved arc.
- A sector is made from two chords and the circumference between them.
  - _why:_ The straight boundaries of a sector must meet at the centre, so they are radii rather than chords. Its curved boundary is the arc between them.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** A minor sector is [ ___ ]; a major sector is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A minor sector is less than half a circle; a major sector is more than half a circle.

**Choices:**

- A minor sector is less than half a circle; a major sector is more than half a circle. ✔
  - _why:_ A minor sector has a central angle below $180^\circ$, so it occupies less than half the circle. A major sector exceeds $180^\circ$ and occupies more than half.
- A minor sector is more than half a circle; a major sector is less than half a circle.
  - _why:_ This swaps the definitions of minor and major sectors. A minor sector is less than half a circle, while a major sector is more than half.
- A minor sector is exactly half a circle; a major sector is more than half a circle.
  - _why:_ Exactly half a circle is a semicircular sector with a $180^\circ$ angle, not a minor sector. A minor sector must be less than half a circle.
- A minor sector is less than half a circle; a major sector is exactly half a circle.
  - _why:_ A sector occupying exactly half the circle has a $180^\circ$ angle and is semicircular, not major. A major sector must occupy more than half the circle.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What formula finds the area of a sector with radius $r$ and angle $\theta$?

**Back:** $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ A sector occupies the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of a full circle, whose area is $\pi r^2$, so these quantities are multiplied.
- $\frac{\theta}{180}\times \pi r^2$
  - _why:_ Using 180 as the denominator incorrectly treats a semicircle as the whole shape. A complete turn is $360^\circ$, so the area is $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2$.
- $\frac{\theta}{360}\times 2\pi r$
  - _why:_ The factor $2\pi r$ is the circumference, so this formula calculates arc length rather than area. Sector area uses $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2$.
- $\frac{360}{\theta}\times \pi r^2$
  - _why:_ The fraction has been inverted, making smaller angles produce larger areas. The sector’s fraction of the circle is $\frac{\theta}{360}$, giving $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the area of a sector with radius 8 cm and angle $135^\circ$?

**Back:** $24\pi\text{ cm}^2$

**Choices:**

- $24\pi\text{ cm}^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ The sector is $\frac{135}{360}=\frac{3}{8}$ of a circle. Multiplying $\frac{3}{8}$ by $\pi(8^2)=64\pi$ gives $24\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
- $48\pi\text{ cm}^2$
  - _why:_ This result is twice the correct area, typically from using 180 instead of 360 as the full-turn denominator. Using $\frac{135}{360}\times\pi(8^2)$ gives $24\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
- $19.2\pi\text{ cm}^2$
  - _why:_ This uses $135/360=3/8$ incorrectly as $3/10$. The correct calculation is $\frac{3}{8}\times64\pi=24\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
- $32\pi\text{ cm}^2$
  - _why:_ This comes from treating $135^\circ$ as half a circle. It is actually three-eighths of a full turn, so the area is $\frac{3}{8}\times64\pi=24\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is $24\pi$ to 3 significant figures?

**Back:** $75.4$

**Choices:**

- $75.4$ ✔
  - _why:_ Calculating $24\times\pi$ gives approximately $75.3982$. The first three significant digits are 7, 5 and 3, and the following 9 rounds the result to $75.4$.
- $75.3$
  - _why:_ This truncates the decimal instead of rounding it. Since $24\pi\approx75.3982$, the fourth significant digit is 9, so the third digit rounds up to give $75.4$.
- $7.54$
  - _why:_ The decimal point has been shifted one place left, effectively dividing the value by 10. Since $24\pi\approx75.3982$, the correct three-significant-figure answer is $75.4$.
- $754$
  - _why:_ The decimal point has been shifted one place right, making the value ten times too large. As $24\pi\approx75.3982$, it rounds to $75.4$ to three significant figures.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** If a sector angle is $220^\circ$, what fraction should be used for its area?

**Back:** $\frac{220}{360}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{220}{360}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A full circle contains $360^\circ$, so a $220^\circ$ sector represents $220$ out of $360$ equal angular parts. Its required fraction is therefore $\frac{220}{360}$.
- $\frac{220}{180}$
  - _why:_ Using 180 as the denominator compares the sector with a semicircle rather than a full circle. Sector area must use the fraction $\frac{220}{360}$.
- $\frac{140}{360}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $220^\circ$ from $360^\circ$ gives the complementary $140^\circ$ sector, not the stated sector. The required area fraction is $\frac{220}{360}$.
- $\frac{360}{220}$
  - _why:_ The fraction has been inverted, producing a value greater than 1 even though the sector is smaller than a circle. The correct fraction is $\frac{220}{360}$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** The arc length is the length of the [ ___ ] of a sector.

**Back:** The arc length is the length of the curved edge of a sector.

**Choices:**

- curved edge ✔
  - _why:_ An arc is the curved part of a circle’s circumference between the two radii. Therefore, arc length measures the sector’s curved edge rather than either straight side.
- straight edge
  - _why:_ A sector has two straight edges, and each is a radius rather than an arc. Arc length specifically measures the curved edge forming part of the circumference.
- sector radius
  - _why:_ The radius measures the distance from the centre to the circumference, not distance around the circle. Arc length is the length of the sector’s curved edge.
- whole perimeter
  - _why:_ The perimeter also includes both straight radii, so it is not the same as arc length. Arc length measures only the curved edge of the sector.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What formula finds arc length for radius $r$ and angle $\theta$?

**Back:** $\frac{\theta}{360}\times 2\pi r$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{\theta}{360}\times 2\pi r$ ✔
  - _why:_ The circumference of a full circle is $2\pi r$. A sector with angle $\theta$ contains the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of that circumference.
- $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r^2$
  - _why:_ This is the formula for sector area because it uses the circle’s area, $\pi r^2$. Arc length instead uses the circumference: $\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r$.
- $\frac{\theta}{360}\times \pi r$
  - _why:_ The circumference is $2\pi r$, not $\pi r$, so omitting the factor of 2 halves every arc length. The correct formula is $\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r$.
- $\frac{360}{\theta}\times 2\pi r$
  - _why:_ The angular fraction has been inverted, making shorter sectors produce larger lengths. A sector occupies $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of the circumference, giving $\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r$.

**Back:** $6\pi\text{ cm}$

**Choices:**

- $6\pi\text{ cm}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The sector is $\frac{108}{360}=\frac{3}{10}$ of a circle. Its arc is therefore $\frac{3}{10}\times2\pi(10)=6\pi\text{ cm}$.
- $3\pi\text{ cm}$
  - _why:_ This result omits the factor of 2 from the circumference formula, using $\pi r$ instead of $2\pi r$. The correct arc length is $6\pi\text{ cm}$.
- $12\pi\text{ cm}$
  - _why:_ This doubles the circumference factor, effectively using $4\pi r$ instead of $2\pi r$. Applying $\frac{108}{360}$ to the correct circumference gives $6\pi\text{ cm}$.
- $10\pi\text{ cm}$
  - _why:_ This treats $108^\circ$ as half of a full turn. It is actually $\frac{3}{10}$ of $360^\circ$, so the arc length is $6\pi\text{ cm}$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** If an answer is required “in terms of $\pi$”, what should you do?

**Back:** Leave $\pi$ in the answer.

**Choices:**

- Leave $\pi$ in the answer. ✔
  - _why:_ An exact answer in terms of $\pi$ retains the symbol rather than replacing it with a decimal approximation. Numerical factors may be simplified, but $\pi$ remains present.
- Use $3.14$ in the answer.
  - _why:_ Replacing $\pi$ with 3.14 gives an approximation rather than an exact answer in terms of $\pi$. The symbol $\pi$ should remain in the final expression.
- Round $\pi$ in the answer.
  - _why:_ Rounding $\pi$ produces an approximate value and removes the requested exact form. An answer in terms of $\pi$ must leave $\pi$ unchanged in the result.
- Replace $\pi$ with a decimal.
  - _why:_ Converting to a decimal removes $\pi$ and usually introduces rounding error. When the question requests an answer in terms of $\pi$, retain the $\pi$ symbol.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What formula finds the perimeter of a sector from its radius and arc length?

**Back:** $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+2r$

**Choices:**

- $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+2r$ ✔
  - _why:_ A sector’s boundary consists of its curved arc and two straight radii, so its perimeter is the arc length plus $2r$.
- $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+r$
  - _why:_ This counts only one of the sector’s two straight radii. The complete boundary includes both radii, so the correct formula is $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+2r$.
- $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+2\pi r$
  - _why:_ The term $2\pi r$ is the circumference of a complete circle, not the sector’s straight edges. Those edges total $2r$, giving $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+2r$.
- $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+r^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring the radius produces an area quantity, so it cannot be added to an arc length. The two straight sides total $2r$, giving $\text{perimeter}=\text{arc length}+2r$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A sector has radius 4.8 cm and arc length $2\pi$ cm. What is its perimeter?

**Back:** $2\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$

**Choices:**

- $2\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The sector perimeter is its arc length plus two radii. Since $2r=2\times4.8=9.6$ cm, the perimeter is $2\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$.
- $2\pi+4.8\text{ cm}$
  - _why:_ This adds only one radius to the arc length, missing the sector’s second straight side. Adding $2\times4.8=9.6$ cm gives $2\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$.
- $4\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly doubles the given arc length from $2\pi$ to $4\pi$. Keeping the stated arc and adding both radii gives $2\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$.
- $2\pi+19.2\text{ cm}$
  - _why:_ This adds four radii rather than the sector’s two radii. The straight edges total only $2\times4.8=9.6$ cm, so the perimeter is $2\pi+9.6\text{ cm}$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** For shaded regions, shaded area = [ ___ ].

**Back:** For shaded regions, shaded area = total area - unshaded area.

**Choices:**

- total area - unshaded area ✔
  - _why:_ The total region is divided into shaded and unshaded parts. Subtracting the unshaded area from the total therefore leaves exactly the shaded area.
- total area + unshaded area
  - _why:_ Adding the unshaded area counts that region twice because it is already included in the total. The shaded area is found using total area - unshaded area.
- unshaded area - total area
  - _why:_ This reverses the subtraction and would normally produce a negative result. The larger total area must come first: total area - unshaded area.
- total area ÷ unshaded area
  - _why:_ Dividing two areas gives a ratio rather than the area of the shaded region. The required area is found using total area - unshaded area.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** What shortcut formula finds sector area when radius $r$ and arc length $L$ are given?

**Back:** $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$

**Choices:**

- $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$ ✔
  - _why:_ A sector’s area can be found directly from its radius and arc length by taking half their product, so $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$.
- $A=rL$
  - _why:_ This omits the factor of one-half and therefore gives twice the sector’s actual area. The correct shortcut is $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$.
- $A=\frac{1}{2}r^2L$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly squares the radius and produces dimensions of cubic length rather than area. Using radius and arc length requires $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$.
- $A=2rL$
  - _why:_ This multiplies the radius-arc product by 2 instead of halving it, making the result four times too large. The correct formula is $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the sector area when $r=8$ cm and $L=5\pi$ cm.

**Back:** $20\pi\text{ cm}^2$

**Choices:**

- $20\pi\text{ cm}^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Using $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$ gives $A=\frac{1}{2}\times8\times5\pi=20\pi$. As this is an area, the units are $\text{cm}^2$.
- $40\pi\text{ cm}^2$
  - _why:_ This calculates $rL$ but omits the factor of one-half, doubling the sector area. Halving $8\times5\pi=40\pi$ gives $20\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
- $10\pi\text{ cm}^2$
  - _why:_ This effectively halves the product twice, making the result half the required area. Applying $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$ once gives $20\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
- $160\pi\text{ cm}^2$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly squares the radius when using the radius-arc shortcut. The formula is $A=\frac{1}{2}rL$, which gives $\frac{1}{2}\times8\times5\pi=20\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
