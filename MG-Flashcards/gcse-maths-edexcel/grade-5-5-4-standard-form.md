## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the two requirements for a number to be in standard form ($a \times 10^n$)?

**Back:** $1 \le a $n$ is an integer

**Choices:**

- $1 \le a $n$ is an integer ✔
  - _why:_ Standard form requires a coefficient from 1 inclusive to 10 exclusive. The power of 10 must have an integer exponent, which may be positive, negative, or zero.
- $0 $n$ is an integer
  - _why:_ This incorrectly allows coefficients below 1 and includes 10. Standard form requires $1 \le a < 10$, while $n$ must be an integer.
- $1 \le a $n$ is a positive integer
  - _why:_ Restricting $n$ to positive integers wrongly excludes small numbers and numbers between 1 and 10. The exponent may be any integer, including zero or a negative integer.
- $1 $n$ is an integer
  - _why:_ The boundary signs are reversed: a coefficient of 1 is permitted, but a coefficient of 10 is not. The required range is $1 \le a < 10$.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In standard form, the coefficient $a$ must be [ ___ ] and less than 10.

**Back:** In standard form, the coefficient $a$ must be at least 1 and less than 10.

**Choices:**

- at least 1 ✔
  - _why:_ The coefficient in standard form must satisfy $1 \le a < 10$, so it may equal 1 but cannot be any smaller.
- greater than 1
  - _why:_ Using a strict inequality wrongly excludes 1. A coefficient of exactly 1 is valid, so the correct lower condition is at least 1.
- at most 1
  - _why:_ This reverses the lower-bound condition and would exclude valid coefficients such as 2.5. The coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10.
- greater than 0
  - _why:_ This incorrectly permits coefficients such as 0.4. Standard form requires the coefficient to be at least 1 so there is exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** How does the decimal point move when multiplying a number by $10^n$ if $n$ is positive?

**Back:** Moves to the right.

**Choices:**

- Moves to the right. ✔
  - _why:_ A positive power of 10 makes the number larger. Multiplying by $10^n$ therefore moves the decimal point $n$ places to the right.
- Moves to the left.
  - _why:_ Moving left applies when the exponent is negative. A positive exponent makes the number larger, so the decimal point moves $n$ places to the right.
- Does not move.
  - _why:_ The decimal point stays fixed only when $n=0$, since $10^0=1$. For a positive exponent, it moves $n$ places to the right.
- Moves left if $n$ is odd.
  - _why:_ The direction does not depend on whether the exponent is odd or even. Every positive exponent moves the decimal point to the right.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How does the decimal point move when multiplying a number by $10^n$ if $n$ is negative?

**Back:** Moves to the left.

**Choices:**

- Moves to the left. ✔
  - _why:_ A negative power of 10 has a value below 1, so multiplication makes the number smaller and moves the decimal point to the left.
- Moves to the right.
  - _why:_ Moving right applies to positive powers of 10. A negative exponent represents division by a power of 10, so the decimal point moves left.
- Does not move.
  - _why:_ No movement occurs only for an exponent of zero because $10^0=1$. A negative exponent moves the decimal point to the left.
- Moves right if $n$ is odd.
  - _why:_ Odd and even exponents follow the same direction rule. Any negative exponent makes the number smaller, so the decimal point moves to the left.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $12.3 \times 10^4$ not in standard form?

**Back:** The coefficient (12.3) is not less than 10.

**Choices:**

- The coefficient (12.3) is not less than 10. ✔
  - _why:_ A standard-form coefficient must satisfy $1 \le a < 10$. Since 12.3 exceeds the upper limit, the expression must be rewritten with a smaller coefficient.
- The coefficient (12.3) is not an integer.
  - _why:_ The coefficient does not need to be an integer; decimal coefficients such as 1.23 are valid. The problem is that 12.3 is not less than 10.
- The coefficient (12.3) has too many decimal places.
  - _why:_ Standard form places no limit on the number of decimal places in the coefficient. The expression fails because its coefficient is 12.3, which is not less than 10.
- The exponent (4) is not negative.
  - _why:_ The exponent may be positive, negative, or zero as long as it is an integer. The actual issue is that the coefficient 12.3 is not less than 10.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Back:** $$4.8 \times 10^4$

**Choices:**

- $$4.8 \times 10^4$ ✔
  - _why:_ Changing 0.48 to 4.8 multiplies the coefficient by 10, so the power must decrease from 5 to 4 to keep the overall value unchanged.
- $$4.8 \times 10^5$
  - _why:_ This multiplies the coefficient by 10 without compensating in the exponent, making the value ten times too large. The exponent must decrease to 4.
- $$4.8 \times 10^3$
  - _why:_ This decreases the exponent by two rather than one, making the result ten times too small. Moving from 0.48 to 4.8 requires an exponent of 4.
- $$4.8 \times 10^6$
  - _why:_ Increasing both the coefficient and exponent makes the result one hundred times too large. Multiplying the coefficient by 10 requires decreasing the exponent to 4.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** When converting an ordinary number to standard form, use a [ ___ ] exponent for large numbers and a negative exponent for small decimals between 0 and 1.

**Back:** When converting an ordinary number to standard form, use a positive exponent for large numbers and a negative exponent for small decimals between 0 and 1.

**Choices:**

- positive ✔
  - _why:_ Large ordinary numbers require multiplication by a positive power of 10 because the decimal point has been moved left to form a coefficient between 1 and 10.
- negative
  - _why:_ Negative exponents represent values smaller than 1, not large numbers. Large numbers use a positive exponent because a positive power of 10 increases the coefficient's place value.
- zero
  - _why:_ An exponent of zero gives $10^0=1$, so it does not scale the coefficient into a large number. The correct exponent type is positive.
- fractional
  - _why:_ GCSE standard form uses integer powers of 10, not fractional exponents. Large ordinary numbers require a positive exponent to represent their place value.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the two steps for multiplying numbers in standard form.

**Back:** Multiply the coefficientsAdd the exponents

**Choices:**

- Multiply the coefficientsAdd the exponents ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplication combines the numerical coefficients by multiplying them. Powers of 10 with the same base are multiplied by adding their exponents.
- Multiply the coefficientsMultiply the exponents
  - _why:_ The coefficients are multiplied, but the exponents must be added because $10^a \times 10^b=10^{a+b}$. Multiplying the exponents applies the wrong index law.
- Add the coefficientsAdd the exponents
  - _why:_ Adding exponents is correct, but the coefficients must be multiplied rather than added. Multiplying two standard-form numbers requires multiplication of coefficients and addition of exponents.
- Divide the coefficientsAdd the exponents
  - _why:_ Dividing the coefficients confuses multiplication with the division rule. For multiplication, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of the powers of 10.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Back:** Divide the coefficientsSubtract the exponents

**Choices:**

- Divide the coefficientsSubtract the exponents ✔
  - _why:_ Division combines the numerical coefficients by dividing them. Powers of 10 with the same base are divided by subtracting the denominator's exponent from the numerator's.
- Divide the coefficientsAdd the exponents
  - _why:_ The coefficients are divided, but adding exponents is the multiplication rule. For division, subtract the denominator's exponent from the numerator's exponent.
- Multiply the coefficientsSubtract the exponents
  - _why:_ Subtracting exponents is correct, but multiplying the coefficients confuses division with multiplication. The coefficients must be divided while the exponents are subtracted.
- Subtract the coefficientsSubtract the exponents
  - _why:_ Coefficients are not subtracted when standard-form numbers are divided. The correct process is to divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents.

  - _why:_ Multiplying coefficients and adding powers is the rule for multiplying standard form numbers, not adding or subtracting them. The safest method is converting to ordinary numbers first.

  - _why:_ Powers are combined by addition or subtraction for multiplication and division, not for addition or subtraction. Convert the numbers to ordinary numbers first.

  - _why:_ Adding the powers is a multiplication rule, not an addition rule. With equal powers, add the coefficients and keep $10^5$, giving $5 \times 10^5$.
- $$6 \times 10^5$
  - _why:_ The coefficient should be added, $2 + 3 = 5$, rather than multiplied to get $6$. Therefore the correct result is $5 \times 10^5$.
- $$5 \times 10^4$
  - _why:_ The common power is $10^5$ and does not decrease when adding terms with the same power. Adding the coefficients gives the correct answer, $5 \times 10^5$.
