## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the expression $3^4$, what are the names for the number 3 and the number 4?

**Back:** 3 is the base4 is the index (or power)

**Choices:**

- 3 is the base4 is the index (or power) ✔
  - _why:_ The base is the number being repeatedly multiplied, while the index tells you how many copies of the base are multiplied together.
- 3 is the index4 is the base (or power)
  - _why:_ This reverses the two terms. In $3^4$, 3 is the base being multiplied and 4 is the index, which specifies four copies of 3.
- 3 is the factor4 is the multiplier (or total)
  - _why:_ Factor and multiplier describe multiplication more generally, not the positions in power notation. In $3^4$, 3 is the base and 4 is the index.
- 3 is the exponent4 is the coefficient (or factor)
  - _why:_ This confuses power notation with algebraic coefficients. In $3^4$, 3 is the base, while 4 is the index or exponent.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the expression $3^4$, the index 4 tells you to multiply [ ___ ] copies of the base together.

**Back:** In the expression $3^4$, the index 4 tells you to multiply four copies of the base together.

**Choices:**

- four ✔
  - _why:_ An index states how many copies of the base are multiplied together. Therefore, $3^4$ means $3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3$.
- three
  - _why:_ This mistakes the base for the number of copies. The index is 4, so four copies of the base 3 are multiplied together.
- two
  - _why:_ Two copies would represent $3^2$, not $3^4$. The index 4 means that four copies of 3 must be multiplied together.
- five
  - _why:_ This incorrectly counts one more copy than the index states. Since the index is 4, exactly four copies of the base are multiplied.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Back:** 27

**Choices:**

- 27 ✔
  - _why:_ The index 3 means multiply three copies of the base: $3^3 = 3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$.
- 9
  - _why:_ This evaluates $3^2$ by multiplying only two copies of 3. For $3^3$, three copies are required, giving $3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$.
- 6
  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying the base by the index, $3 \times 3$. An index represents repeated multiplication, so $3^3 = 27$.
- 81
  - _why:_ This uses four copies of 3 and therefore evaluates $3^4$. The index in $3^3$ requires only three copies, giving 27.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $5^2$ not equal to 10?

**Back:** $$5^2$ means 5 multiplied by itself ($5 \times 5$), not multiplied by 2.

**Choices:**

- $$5^2$ means 5 multiplied by itself ($5 \times 5$), not multiplied by 2. ✔
  - _why:_ The index 2 tells you to use two copies of the base 5. Therefore, $5^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25$, rather than 10.
- $$5^2$ means 5 multiplied by 2 ($5 \times 2$), so it is equal to 10.
  - _why:_ This treats the index as an ordinary multiplier. The index gives the number of copies of the base, so $5^2 = 5 \times 5 = 25$.
- $$5^2$ means 5 added to itself ($5 + 5$), not multiplied by itself.
  - _why:_ This confuses repeated multiplication with repeated addition. Squaring 5 requires multiplying two copies of 5, so $5^2 = 25$, not 10.
- $$5^2$ means 2 multiplied by itself ($2 \times 2$), with 5 as the index.
  - _why:_ This reverses the base and index. In $5^2$, 5 is the base and 2 is the index, so the calculation is $5 \times 5 = 25$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Write the repeated multiplication $6 \times 6 \times 6 \times 6$ in power notation.

**Back:** $6^4$

**Choices:**

- $6^4$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are four identical factors of 6. Power notation writes the repeated factor as the base and the number of copies as the index, giving $6^4$.
- $4^6$
  - _why:_ This reverses the base and index. The repeated factor is 6 and it appears four times, so the correct power notation is $6^4$.
- $6^3$
  - _why:_ This misses one of the four factors and represents only $6 \times 6 \times 6$. Four copies of 6 must be written as $6^4$.
- $24^1$
  - _why:_ This multiplies 6 by the number of factors instead of representing repeated multiplication. Four factors of 6 are written as $6^4$, not as $24^1$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is the answer you get when a number is multiplied by itself.

**Back:** A square number is the answer you get when a number is multiplied by itself.

**Choices:**

- square number ✔
  - _why:_ A square number is produced by multiplying an integer by itself. For example, $7 \times 7 = 49$, so 49 is a square number.
- cube number
  - _why:_ A cube number comes from multiplying three copies of a number, not two. Multiplying a number by itself once produces a square number.
- square root
  - _why:_ A square root is a number that produces a given value when squared. The answer produced by multiplying a number by itself is a square number.
- prime number
  - _why:_ A prime number has exactly two positive factors, which is unrelated to this definition. Multiplying a number by itself produces a square number.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the first five square numbers?

**Back:** 1, 4, 9, 16, 25

**Choices:**

- 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 ✔
  - _why:_ These are obtained by squaring the first five positive integers: $1^2 = 1$, $2^2 = 4$, $3^2 = 9$, $4^2 = 16$ and $5^2 = 25$.
- 1, 2, 4, 8, 16
  - _why:_ These are powers of 2 rather than consecutive square numbers. Squaring the first five positive integers gives 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25.
- 1, 3, 9, 27, 81
  - _why:_ These are powers of 3, not the results of squaring consecutive positive integers. The first five square numbers are 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25.
- 2, 4, 6, 8, 10
  - _why:_ These are the first five positive even numbers, not square numbers. Squaring 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 gives 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Is the square of an even number (like $6^2$) even or odd?

**Back:** Even (e.g. 36)

**Choices:**

- Even (e.g. 36) ✔
  - _why:_ An even number contains a factor of 2, so multiplying it by itself still gives a multiple of 2. For example, $6^2 = 36$.
- Odd (e.g. 49)
  - _why:_ The example 49 is the square of the odd number 7, not an even number. Squaring any even number produces an even result, such as $6^2 = 36$.
- It can be either (e.g. 36 or 49)
  - _why:_ This confuses squares of even numbers with squares in general. While 49 is an odd square, the square of an even number is always even.
- Neither (e.g. 36)
  - _why:_ Every integer is either even or odd, and 36 is even because it is divisible by 2. The square of an even number is always even.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What question should you ask yourself to find $\sqrt{100}$?

**Back:** What number multiplied by itself gives 100?

**Choices:**

- What number multiplied by itself gives 100? ✔
  - _why:_ A square root identifies the number that produces the original value when multiplied by itself. Since $10 \times 10 = 100$, asking this question gives $\sqrt{100}=10$.
- What number multiplied by 2 gives 100?
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 2 finds half of 100, which is 50, rather than its square root. Ask what number multiplied by itself gives 100; that number is 10.
- What number divided by itself gives 100?
  - _why:_ Any non-zero number divided by itself equals 1, not 100. To find a square root, ask what number multiplied by itself gives 100; the answer is 10.
- What number added to itself gives 100?
  - _why:_ Adding a number to itself finds half of 100, which is 50. Square roots involve multiplication, so ask what number multiplied by itself gives 100; it is 10.

**Back:** 8

**Choices:**

- 8 ✔
  - _why:_ The square root of 64 is the positive number that gives 64 when squared. Since $8 \times 8 = 64$, $\sqrt{64}=8$.

- 16

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** Squaring and square rooting are [ ___ ] operations that undo each other.

**Back:** Squaring and square rooting are opposite operations that undo each other.

**Choices:**

- opposite ✔
  - _why:_ Squaring a positive number and then taking its square root returns the original number. The two operations therefore undo each other and are described here as opposite operations.
- equivalent
  - _why:_ Equivalent operations produce the same result, but squaring and square rooting generally do not. They are opposite operations because each reverses the effect of the other.
- identical
  - _why:_ Identical operations would perform the same mathematical process. Squaring multiplies a number by itself, while square rooting reverses that process, so they are opposite operations.
- commutative
  - _why:_ Commutative describes operations whose inputs can change order without changing the result, such as addition. Squaring and square rooting instead undo each other, so they are opposite operations.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a cube number?

**Back:** The answer when a number is multiplied by itself three times.

**Choices:**

- The answer when a number is multiplied by itself three times. ✔
  - _why:_ Cubing uses three equal factors: $n^3=n \times n \times n$. The result of multiplying a number by itself three times in this sense is therefore a cube number.
- The answer when a number is multiplied by itself two times.
  - _why:_ Using two equal factors gives a square number, such as $5^2=5 \times 5=25$. A cube number uses three equal factors: $n \times n \times n$.
- The answer when a number is added to itself three times.
  - _why:_ Repeated addition produces a multiple, not a cube number. A cube is formed using multiplication with three equal factors, written $n^3=n \times n \times n$.
- The answer when a number is multiplied by three once.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 3 produces a multiple of 3 and does not usually produce a cube. A cube number is formed from three equal factors: $n \times n \times n$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Evaluate $4^3$.

**Back:** 64

**Choices:**

- 64 ✔
  - _why:_ The exponent 3 means that three factors of 4 are multiplied together. Therefore, $4^3=4 \times 4 \times 4=16 \times 4=64$.
- 12
  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying the base by the exponent, $4 \times 3$, rather than using repeated multiplication. Cubing gives $4 \times 4 \times 4=64$.
- 16
  - _why:_ This is $4^2$, so it uses only two factors of 4. The exponent 3 requires three factors, giving $4 \times 4 \times 4=64$.
- 81
  - _why:_ This is $3^4$, produced by swapping the base and exponent. The expression $4^3$ uses three factors of 4, so its value is 64.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** The mathematical symbol for the cube root of 125 is [ ___ ].

**Back:** The mathematical symbol for the cube root of 125 is $\sqrt[3]{125}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\sqrt[3]{125}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The small 3 is the root index and indicates a cube root. Therefore, the cube root of 125 is written $\sqrt[3]{125}$.
- $$\sqrt{125}$
  - _why:_ A radical sign without an index represents a square root, not a cube root. The required index is 3, so the correct symbol is $\sqrt[3]{125}$.
- $$\sqrt[125]{3}$
  - _why:_ This swaps the root index and the number being rooted, so it means the 125th root of 3. The cube root of 125 is $\sqrt[3]{125}$.
- $$3\sqrt{125}$
  - _why:_ Placing 3 before the radical means three times the square root of 125. For a cube root, 3 must be the index: $\sqrt[3]{125}$.

**Back:** 3

**Choices:**

- 3 ✔
  - _why:_ A cube root is the number that gives the original value when cubed. Since $3 \times 3 \times 3=27$, $\sqrt[3]{27}=3$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is the cube root of 27 not equal to 9?

**Back:** Cube root is not dividing by 3; it is the inverse of cubing ($3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$).

**Choices:**

- Cube root is not dividing by 3; it is the inverse of cubing ($3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$). ✔
  - _why:_ A cube root reverses cubing, so it asks which number appears as three equal factors. Because $3^3=27$, the cube root is 3 rather than $27 \div 3=9$.
- Cube root is not dividing by 3; it is the inverse of squaring ($\sqrt{27} \times \sqrt{27} = 27$).
  - _why:_ The inverse of squaring is square rooting, not cube rooting. Cube rooting reverses cubing, and $3 \times 3 \times 3=27$, so the cube root is 3.
- Cube root is not dividing by 3; it is the inverse of multiplying by 3 ($9 \times 3 = 27$).
  - _why:_ The inverse of multiplying by 3 is dividing by 3, which gives 9 but does not find a cube root. Cube rooting reverses cubing, so $\sqrt[3]{27}=3$.
- Cube root is not dividing by 3; it means finding three equal addends ($9 + 9 + 9 = 27$).
  - _why:_ Finding three equal addends uses repeated addition and gives one third of 27. A cube root requires three equal factors, and $3 \times 3 \times 3=27$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you determine the value of $10^4$ by looking at the index?

**Back:** The index 4 tells you there are four zeros after the 1 (10,000).

**Choices:**

- The index 4 tells you there are four zeros after the 1 (10,000). ✔
  - _why:_ A positive integer power of 10 gives 1 followed by as many zeros as the index. Therefore, $10^4$ is 1 followed by four zeros.
- The index 4 tells you there are three zeros after the 1 (1,000).
  - _why:_ This counts the initial factor of 10 as one of the four multiplications. Since $10^4=10\times10\times10\times10$, the result has four zeros: 10,000.
- The index 4 tells you to multiply 10 by 4 (40).
  - _why:_ An index indicates repeated multiplication, not multiplication by the index. Multiplying four factors of 10 gives $10^4=10,000$, which has four zeros after the 1.
- The index 4 tells you there are four zeros before the 1 (0.0001).
  - _why:_ This confuses a positive index with a negative index: $0.0001$ is $10^{-4}$. A positive index of 4 gives 10,000, with four zeros after the 1.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** List the powers of 2 from $2^1$ to $2^6$.

**Back:** 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64

**Choices:**

- 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 ✔
  - _why:_ Each new power of 2 is found by multiplying the previous value by 2. Starting with $2^1=2$ gives the sequence ending with $2^6=64$.
- 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12
  - _why:_ These are the first six positive multiples of 2, not powers of 2. Powers are formed by repeated multiplication, giving 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.
- 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32
  - _why:_ This sequence begins with $2^0=1$, creating an off-by-one error. Starting at $2^1$ gives 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.
- 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 128
  - _why:_ This skips $2^6=64$ and finishes with $2^7=128$. Doubling each term in order gives 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** From the list [4, 8, 9, 16, 27, 64], which number is both a square number and a cube number?

**Back:** 64 ($8^2$ and $4^3$)

**Choices:**

- 64 ($8^2$ and $4^3$) ✔
  - _why:_ The number 64 is both a perfect square and a perfect cube because $8^2=64$ and $4^3=64$. It is the only such number in the list.
- 16 ($4^2$ and $2^4$)
  - _why:_ Although 16 is a square, writing it as $2^4$ makes it a fourth power, not a cube. The number 64 satisfies both conditions as $8^2$ and $4^3$.
- 8 ($4^2$ and $2^3$)
  - _why:_ The number 8 is a cube because $2^3=8$, but it is not the square of an integer; $4^2$ is 16. The required number is 64.
- 27 ($3^2$ and $3^3$)
  - _why:_ The number 27 is a cube because $3^3=27$, but $3^2=9$, so it is not also a square. The number 64 is both $8^2$ and $4^3$.
