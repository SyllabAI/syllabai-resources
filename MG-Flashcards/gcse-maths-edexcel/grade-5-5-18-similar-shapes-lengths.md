## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What two conditions must be met for two shapes to be mathematically similar?

**Back:** All matching angles are equalAll matching lengths are multiplied by the same scale factor

**Choices:**

- All matching angles are equalAll matching lengths are multiplied by the same scale factor ✔
  - _why:_ Similar shapes have identical corresponding angles and proportional corresponding lengths. A single linear scale factor must convert every matching length on one shape to the other.
- All matching angles are multiplied by the same scale factorAll matching lengths are equal
  - _why:_ Angles are not multiplied by the scale factor, and matching lengths need not be equal. Similar shapes have equal matching angles and matching lengths related by one scale factor.
- All matching angles are equalAll matching areas are equal
  - _why:_ Similar shapes can have different areas, so equal areas are not required. Their matching angles are equal, while every matching length is multiplied by the same scale factor.
- All matching angles have the same totalAll matching lengths differ by the same amount
  - _why:_ Similarity requires individual matching angles to be equal, not merely equal angle totals. Matching lengths have a constant ratio rather than a constant difference.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** An enlargement means every length has been multiplied by one fixed number called the [ ___ ].

**Back:** An enlargement means every length has been multiplied by one fixed number called the scale factor.

**Choices:**

- scale factor ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor is the fixed multiplier applied to every length in an enlargement. Using one multiplier preserves the shape’s proportions and matching angles.
- area scale factor
  - _why:_ The area scale factor applies to areas and equals the square of the length scale factor. The fixed number multiplying every length is the scale factor.
- volume scale factor
  - _why:_ The volume scale factor describes how volumes change and equals the cube of the length scale factor. The multiplier applied directly to lengths is the scale factor.
- translation vector
  - _why:_ A translation vector specifies horizontal and vertical movement without changing size. The fixed multiplier used to enlarge every length is called the scale factor.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you identify corresponding sides in similar shapes?

**Back:** Match the vertices (corners) or find the sides between matching angle labels.

**Choices:**

- Match the vertices (corners) or find the sides between matching angle labels. ✔
  - _why:_ Corresponding vertices occupy equivalent positions in the similar shapes. Therefore, sides joining matching vertices, or lying between matching angle labels, are corresponding sides.
- Match the side lengths or find the sides in the same page position.
  - _why:_ Corresponding sides can have different lengths and positions because a similar shape may be enlarged, rotated or reflected. Match vertices or use the sides between matching angle labels.
- Match the parallel sides or find the sides with matching orientations.
  - _why:_ Rotation can change a side’s orientation, and corresponding sides need not appear parallel on the page. Match corresponding vertices or sides between matching angle labels instead.
- Match the longest sides or find the sides beside matching length labels.
  - _why:_ Matching longest sides can help, but it is not a complete method and length labels may differ after enlargement. Corresponding sides are identified using matching vertices or angles.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** If triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $PQR$ and $AB$ matches $PQ$, the scale factor $k$ is calculated as $k = \frac{\text{[...]}}{AB}$.

**Back:** If triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $PQR$ and $AB$ matches $PQ$, the scale factor $k$ is calculated as $k = \frac{\boxed{PQ}}{AB}$.

**Choices:**

- $$PQ$ ✔
  - _why:_ Since $AB$ corresponds to $PQ$, the scale factor from triangle $ABC$ to triangle $PQR$ is the new matching length $PQ$ divided by the original length $AB$.
- $$AC$
  - _why:_ This mixes two sides from triangle $ABC$ rather than comparing corresponding sides across the triangles. The numerator must be $PQ$, which corresponds directly to $AB$.
- $$QR$
  - _why:_ Although $QR$ belongs to triangle $PQR$, it does not correspond to $AB$. The numerator must be the matching side $PQ$ so the ratio compares equivalent lengths.
- $$PR$
  - _why:_ Using $PR$ pairs non-corresponding sides and therefore gives an invalid scale factor. Since $AB$ matches $PQ$, the required numerator is $PQ$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** If triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $PQR$ and $AB$ matches $PQ$, the scale factor $k$ is calculated as $k = \frac{PQ}{\text{[...]}}$.

**Back:** If triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $PQR$ and $AB$ matches $PQ$, the scale factor $k$ is calculated as $k = \frac{PQ}{\boxed{AB}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$AB$ ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator is the original matching side $AB$. Dividing its corresponding new length $PQ$ by $AB$ gives the scale factor from triangle $ABC$ to triangle $PQR$.
- $$AC$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly pairs $PQ$ with a non-corresponding side. Since the question states that $AB$ matches $PQ$, the denominator must be $AB$.
- $$BC$
  - _why:_ Using $BC$ compares sides in different corresponding positions, so the ratio is not the required scale factor. The denominator must be $AB$, which matches $PQ$.
- $$QR$
  - _why:_ This compares two sides within triangle $PQR$ instead of corresponding sides from the two triangles. The correct denominator is $AB$, the side matching $PQ$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** To find a missing length on a larger similar shape, what do you do with the scale factor?

**Back:** Multiply the corresponding length on the smaller shape by the scale factor.

**Choices:**

- Multiply the corresponding length on the smaller shape by the scale factor. ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor from the smaller shape to the larger shape is greater than one. Multiplying a corresponding smaller length by it gives the larger length.
- Divide the corresponding length on the smaller shape by the scale factor.
  - _why:_ Dividing would make the smaller length even shorter, which reverses the required enlargement. Multiply the corresponding smaller length by the scale factor to obtain the larger length.
- Add the corresponding length on the smaller shape to the scale factor.
  - _why:_ A scale factor is a multiplier, not a fixed amount added to each side. Multiply the corresponding smaller length by the scale factor to preserve proportionality.
- Multiply the corresponding length on the smaller shape by the square of the scale factor.
  - _why:_ Squaring the scale factor is used when converting areas, not lengths. For a missing length, multiply the corresponding smaller length by the scale factor itself.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** To find a missing length on a smaller similar shape, what do you do with the scale factor?

**Back:** Divide the corresponding length on the larger shape by the scale factor.

**Choices:**

- Divide the corresponding length on the larger shape by the scale factor. ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor multiplies smaller lengths to produce larger ones, so the inverse operation is required. Divide the corresponding larger length by the scale factor.
- Multiply the corresponding length on the larger shape by the scale factor.
  - _why:_ Multiplying would produce an even larger length rather than the required smaller one. Reverse the enlargement by dividing the corresponding larger length by the scale factor.
- Subtract the scale factor from the corresponding length on the larger shape.
  - _why:_ A scale factor describes a multiplicative relationship, not a fixed difference. Divide the corresponding larger length by the scale factor to find the smaller length.
- Divide the corresponding length on the larger shape by the square of the scale factor.
  - _why:_ Using the square confuses length scale factors with area scale factors and makes the result too small. Divide the larger length by the scale factor itself.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the relationship between a large triangle and a smaller triangle created by a line drawn parallel to one of its sides?

**Back:** The triangles are mathematically similar.

**Choices:**

- The triangles are mathematically similar. ✔
  - _why:_ The parallel line creates equal corresponding angles, while the triangles also share an angle. Equal matching angles establish that the two triangles are mathematically similar.
- The triangles are mathematically congruent.
  - _why:_ The triangles have equal corresponding angles but generally have different side lengths, so they are not congruent. The parallel line ensures that they are mathematically similar.
- The triangles have mathematically equal areas.
  - _why:_ The smaller triangle occupies only part of the larger triangle, so their areas are not equal. Parallel lines create equal matching angles, making the triangles mathematically similar.
- The triangles have mathematically equal perimeters.
  - _why:_ Similar triangles usually have perimeters related by the length scale factor rather than equal perimeters. The parallel line establishes equal matching angles, so the triangles are mathematically similar.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a triangle side has been split by a parallel line, the full side length is found by adding the [ ___ ] and the extra part together.

**Back:** When a triangle side has been split by a parallel line, the full side length is found by adding the small part and the extra part together.

**Choices:**

- small part ✔
  - _why:_ The full side is made from the small part plus the extra part, so these two adjacent lengths must be added together.
- whole side
  - _why:_ Adding the whole side to the extra part counts the extra section twice. The full length is found by adding the small part and the extra part.
- corresponding side
  - _why:_ A corresponding side belongs to the similar triangle comparison and is not necessarily a section of this side. Add the small part to the extra part.
- scale factor
  - _why:_ A scale factor is a multiplier, not a section with a length that can be added. The full side equals the small part plus the extra part.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** In a "bow-tie" diagram with parallel sides, how are the corresponding sloping sides identified?

**Back:** They are the sides that lie on the same straight line passing through the crossing point.

**Choices:**

- They are the sides that lie on the same straight line passing through the crossing point. ✔
  - _why:_ Each pair of corresponding sloping sides forms one continuous straight line through the crossing point, allowing the sides in the two similar triangles to be matched.
- They are the sides that are parallel to each other on opposite sides of the crossing point.
  - _why:_ The sloping corresponding sides are not parallel; they continue along the same straight line through the crossing point. The other pair of sides is marked parallel.
- They are the sides that have the same measured length on either side of the crossing point.
  - _why:_ Corresponding sides of similar triangles need not have equal lengths unless the scale factor is 1. They are identified by lying on the same straight line.
- They are the sides that meet the same parallel side without passing through the crossing point.
  - _why:_ Matching sides by which parallel side they meet reverses the correspondence. Corresponding sloping sides lie on one straight line passing through the central crossing point.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the extra part (e.g., $ED$) of a side if you know the whole length ($AD$) and the small part ($AE$)?

**Back:** $$ED = AD - AE$ (Subtract the small part from the whole length).

**Choices:**

- $$ED = AD - AE$ (Subtract the small part from the whole length). ✔
  - _why:_ The whole side satisfies $AD = AE + ED$, so rearranging gives $ED = AD - AE$. The extra section is the whole minus the known small section.
- $$ED = AD + AE$ (Add the small part to the whole length).
  - _why:_ Adding $AE$ to the whole length counts the small section twice and produces a value larger than $AD$. The correct calculation is $ED = AD - AE$.
- $$ED = AE - AD$ (Subtract the whole length from the small part).
  - _why:_ This subtraction is reversed and would normally produce a negative length because $AD$ is the whole side. The correct order is $ED = AD - AE$.
- $$ED = AD \div AE$ (Divide the whole length by the small part).
  - _why:_ Division finds a ratio or scale factor rather than the remaining length. Since $AD = AE + ED$, the extra part is found using $ED = AD - AE$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If the ratio of two corresponding sides $AB:AC$ is $2:5$, what scale factor takes $AB$ to $AC$?

**Back:** $$k = \frac{5}{2}$ (or $2.5$)

**Choices:**

- $$k = \frac{5}{2}$ (or $2.5$) ✔
  - _why:_ To map $AB$ to $AC$, divide the target length by the original length: $k = AC \div AB = 5 \div 2 = 2.5$.
- $$k = \frac{2}{5}$ (or $0.4$)
  - _why:_ This reverses the scale factor and would map $AC$ back to $AB$. Taking $AB$ to $AC$ requires target divided by original, giving $\frac{5}{2}$.
- $$k = \frac{5}{3}$ (or $1.67$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio terms before dividing is not how scale factors are calculated. Divide the target value 5 by the original value 2 to obtain $\frac{5}{2}$.
- $$k = \frac{7}{2}$ (or $3.5$)
  - _why:_ Adding the ratio terms before dividing incorrectly changes the target length from 5 to 7. The required calculation is $5 \div 2$, giving $\frac{5}{2}$.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the ratio of corresponding lengths is $1:3$, the scale factor is [ ___ ].

**Back:** If the ratio of corresponding lengths is $1:3$, the scale factor is 3.

**Choices:**

- 3 ✔
  - _why:_ The second corresponding length is three times the first because $3 \div 1 = 3$. Therefore, the enlargement scale factor from the first shape to the second is 3.
- $$\frac{1}{3}$
  - _why:_ This is the reciprocal scale factor and describes mapping the larger length back to the smaller one. From the first length to the second, the scale factor is 3.
- 2
  - _why:_ Subtracting the ratio values gives the difference in ratio parts, not the scale factor. The multiplier from 1 part to 3 parts is $3 \div 1 = 3$.
- 4
  - _why:_ Adding the two ratio values does not give the enlargement multiplier. A length of 1 becomes 3, so the required scale factor is $3 \div 1 = 3$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are two right-angled triangles similar if they share one other angle?

**Back:** Because they have two equal angles (the shared angle and the 90 degree angle), so the third must also be equal.

**Choices:**

- Because they have two equal angles (the shared angle and the 90 degree angle), so the third must also be equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Two pairs of corresponding angles are equal: the shared angle and the right angles. By angle-angle similarity, the triangles are similar, and their third angles also match.
- Because they have one equal angle (the shared angle), so all the remaining sides and angles must also be equal.
  - _why:_ One equal angle alone is insufficient to prove similarity. The proof works because both triangles also have a 90 degree angle, giving two equal corresponding angles.
- Because they have two equal sides (the sides beside the shared angle), so the third side must also be equal.
  - _why:_ Sharing an angle does not mean the adjacent sides have equal lengths. Similarity follows from two equal angles: the shared angle and the 90 degree angle.
- Because they have one right angle (the 90 degree angle), so every pair of right-angled triangles must be similar.
  - _why:_ A right angle alone does not establish similarity because right-angled triangles can have different acute angles. The additional shared angle supplies the second equal angle needed.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you prove that two rectangles are not mathematically similar?

**Back:** Show that the length scale factor and the width scale factor are different.

**Choices:**

- Show that the length scale factor and the width scale factor are different. ✔
  - _why:_ Similar shapes require one constant scale factor for every pair of corresponding lengths. Different scale factors for the rectangles’ lengths and widths prove that they are not similar.
- Show that the length scale factor and the width scale factor are equal.
  - _why:_ Equal length and width scale factors support similarity rather than disprove it, because all corresponding sides change by the same multiplier. To prove non-similarity, show they differ.
- Show that the areas of the rectangles and the perimeters of the rectangles are different.
  - _why:_ Similar rectangles of different sizes normally have different areas and perimeters, so this does not disprove similarity. Instead, compare the length and width scale factors.
- Show that the corresponding angles and the right angles are equal.
  - _why:_ All rectangles have four equal 90 degree angles, including rectangles that are not similar. Non-similarity is proved when the length and width scale factors are different.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** Even though all rectangles have equal angles (90 degrees), they are only similar if their matching sides have the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Even though all rectangles have equal angles (90 degrees), they are only similar if their matching sides have the same multiplier.

**Choices:**

- same multiplier ✔
  - _why:_ Similar rectangles have one constant scale factor, so every pair of corresponding sides must be related by the same multiplier.
- same length
  - _why:_ Requiring equal side lengths would make the rectangles congruent, not merely similar. Similar rectangles can differ in size, provided corresponding sides have the same multiplier.
- same difference
  - _why:_ Similarity is based on multiplicative scaling, not adding or subtracting a constant difference. Corresponding sides must therefore have the same multiplier.
- same perimeter
  - _why:_ Equal perimeters do not guarantee that rectangles have matching proportions. For similarity, every pair of corresponding sides must have the same multiplier.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** Two shapes are mathematically similar if one is an [ ___ ] of the other.

**Back:** Two shapes are mathematically similar if one is an enlargement of the other.

**Choices:**

- enlargement ✔
  - _why:_ An enlargement scales every length by one constant factor while preserving angles, so the original and resulting shapes are mathematically similar.
- distortion
  - _why:_ A distortion can alter angles or change different lengths by different factors, so it does not preserve similarity. The required transformation is an enlargement.
- shear
  - _why:_ A shear changes angles and therefore usually changes the shape’s proportions. Mathematically similar shapes are related by an enlargement.
- stretch
  - _why:_ A stretch may scale different directions by different amounts, changing the shape’s proportions. Similar shapes require a uniform enlargement with one scale factor.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** If the parallel side on triangle A is 8cm and the matching side on triangle B is 20cm, what is the scale factor from A to B?

**Back:** $$2.5$ (calculated as $20 \div 8$)

**Choices:**

- $$2.5$ (calculated as $20 \div 8$) ✔
  - _why:_ The scale factor from A to B is the matching length in B divided by the matching length in A, giving $20 \div 8 = 2.5$.
- $$0.4$ (calculated as $8 \div 20$)
  - _why:_ This reverses the division and gives the scale factor from B to A. From A to B, calculate $20 \div 8$, which equals $2.5$.
- $$12$ (calculated as $20 - 8$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting finds the difference between the lengths, not the multiplicative scale factor. Divide the B length by the A length: $20 \div 8 = 2.5$.
- $$160$ (calculated as $20 \times 8$)
  - _why:_ Multiplying the two matching lengths does not compare their sizes. The scale factor from A to B is $20 \div 8 = 2.5$.
