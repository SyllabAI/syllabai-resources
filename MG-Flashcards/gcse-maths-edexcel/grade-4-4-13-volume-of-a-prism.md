## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Given cross-section area $A$ and prism length $L$, what is the prism volume?

**Back:** $V = A \times L$

**Choices:**

- $V = A \times L$ ✔
  - _why:_ A prism has the same cross-section throughout its length, so multiplying the cross-sectional area by the prism length gives its volume in cubic units.
- $V = A + L$
  - _why:_ Adding area and length combines incompatible square and linear units. The correct relationship is $V = A \times L$, which produces cubic units.
- $V = A \div L$
  - _why:_ Dividing the cross-sectional area by the length does not calculate the space occupied. A prism’s volume is found using $V = A \times L$.
- $V = L \div A$
  - _why:_ Dividing length by area gives inappropriate units and reverses the required operation. The volume is the cross-sectional area multiplied by the length: $V = A \times L$.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a prism, the [ ___ ] is the repeated 2D shape; the [ ___ ] is how far it is stretched.

**Back:** In a prism, the cross-section is the repeated 2D shape; the length/depth is how far it is stretched.

**Choices:**

- In a prism, the cross-section is the repeated 2D shape; the length/depth is how far it is stretched. ✔
  - _why:_ The cross-section is the constant 2D end shape of a prism, while the length or depth measures the distance through which that shape extends.
- In a prism, the volume is the repeated 2D shape; the length/depth is how far it is stretched.
  - _why:_ Volume describes the total 3D space occupied, not the repeated 2D shape. That shape is the cross-section, while length or depth measures its extension.
- In a prism, the cross-section is the repeated 2D shape; the area is how far it is stretched.
  - _why:_ Area measures the size of the 2D cross-section rather than how far the prism extends. The stretching distance is called its length or depth.
- In a prism, the length is the repeated 2D shape; the cross-section is how far it is stretched.
  - _why:_ Length is a distance, not the repeated 2D shape, and the cross-section is a shape rather than the stretching distance. The correct terms are cross-section and length/depth respectively.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** [ ___ ] uses cubic units such as cm³; [ ___ ] uses square units such as cm².

**Back:** Volume uses cubic units such as cm³; area uses square units such as cm².

**Choices:**

- Volume uses cubic units such as cm³; area uses square units such as cm². ✔
  - _why:_ Volume measures three-dimensional space and therefore uses cubic units. Area measures two-dimensional surface or region size and therefore uses square units.
- Area uses cubic units such as cm³; area uses square units such as cm².
  - _why:_ Area is two-dimensional, so it uses square units rather than cubic units. Volume is three-dimensional and uses cubic units, while area uses square units.
- Volume uses cubic units such as cm³; length uses square units such as cm².
  - _why:_ Length is one-dimensional and uses units such as cm, not square units. Volume uses cubic units, whereas area uses square units such as cm².
- Mass uses cubic units such as cm³; perimeter uses square units such as cm².
  - _why:_ Mass is measured in units such as grams, while perimeter uses linear units. Cubic units measure volume and square units measure area.

**Back:** 240 cm³

**Choices:**

- 240 cm³ ✔
  - _why:_ The cross-sectional area is $6 \times 5 = 30$ cm². Multiplying by the 8 cm length gives $30 \times 8 = 240$ cm³.
- 120 cm³
  - _why:_ This halves the cross-sectional area as though the rectangle were a triangle. Its area is $30$ cm², so the volume is $30 \times 8 = 240$ cm³.
- 480 cm³
  - _why:_ This doubles the correct result, likely by counting the prism length twice. The calculation is $6 \times 5 \times 8 = 240$ cm³.
- 19 cm³
  - _why:_ This comes from adding the three dimensions, $6 + 5 + 8$, instead of multiplying. Volume requires $6 \times 5 \times 8 = 240$ cm³.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Why multiply, not add, cross-section area and prism length?

**Back:** cm² × cm = cm³; adding gives mismatched units.

**Choices:**

- cm² × cm = cm³; adding gives mismatched units. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying square centimetres by centimetres combines two cross-sectional dimensions with the prism’s length, producing the three-dimensional unit cm³ required for volume.
- cm² + cm = cm³; multiplying gives mismatched units.
  - _why:_ Square centimetres and centimetres cannot be added to form cubic centimetres because they measure different dimensions. Multiplication is required: cm² × cm = cm³.
- cm² × cm = cm²; adding gives cm³.
  - _why:_ Multiplying cm² by cm adds the unit powers, giving cm³ rather than cm². Addition cannot produce cm³ because area and length have incompatible units.
- cm² ÷ cm = cm³; multiplying gives mismatched units.
  - _why:_ Dividing cm² by cm gives cm, not cm³, so it removes a dimension. Multiplying area by length correctly gives cm² × cm = cm³.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the area of an L-shaped cross-section after splitting it into rectangles?

**Back:** Find each rectangle area.Add the areas.

**Choices:**

- Find each rectangle area.Add the areas. ✔
  - _why:_ Splitting the L-shape creates non-overlapping rectangles. Calculate each rectangle’s area using length × width, then add those areas to recover the complete cross-sectional area.
- Find each rectangle perimeter.Add the perimeters.
  - _why:_ Perimeter measures boundary length rather than enclosed area, so adding perimeters cannot give the cross-sectional area. Find each rectangle’s area and add the areas.
- Find each rectangle area.Multiply the areas.
  - _why:_ Multiplying the separate rectangle areas produces units to the fourth power and does not combine the regions. Their non-overlapping areas must be added together.
- Find each rectangle area.Subtract the areas.
  - _why:_ Subtracting would remove one of the rectangles even though both form the L-shape. After splitting into non-overlapping rectangles, find and add their areas.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a triangle, the area formula is [ ___ ]; $h$ is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a triangle, the area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$; $h$ is the perpendicular height.

**Choices:**

- For a triangle, the area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$; $h$ is the perpendicular height. ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle occupies half the corresponding rectangle, giving $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$. The height must be measured perpendicular to the chosen base.
- For a triangle, the area formula is $A = bh$; $h$ is the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ This omits the factor of $\frac{1}{2}$ and therefore calculates the area of a corresponding rectangle. A triangle’s area is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, using the perpendicular height.
- For a triangle, the area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$; $h$ is the hypotenuse.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse is not automatically the height because it may not be perpendicular to the chosen base. In $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, $h$ is the perpendicular height.
- For a triangle, the area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$; $h$ is the sloping height.
  - _why:_ This confuses the triangle formula with the trapezium formula and uses a non-perpendicular measurement. A triangle uses $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, where $h$ is the perpendicular height.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** When is the hypotenuse usable in a triangle area calculation?

**Back:** Only when paired with a perpendicular height.

**Choices:**

- Only when paired with a perpendicular height. ✔
  - _why:_ Any side, including the hypotenuse, can serve as the triangle’s base, but the corresponding height must meet that chosen side at a right angle.
- Whenever it is the triangle’s longest side.
  - _why:_ Being the longest side does not make the hypotenuse a valid height or guarantee a suitable pairing. It is usable only as a base paired with a perpendicular height.
- Only when paired with another sloping side.
  - _why:_ Two sloping side lengths do not directly provide base and perpendicular height. The hypotenuse is usable only when it is paired with a height perpendicular to it.
- Whenever the triangle contains a right angle.
  - _why:_ A right angle identifies the hypotenuse but does not make it the perpendicular height. It is usable in the area formula only with its corresponding perpendicular height.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** A trapezium has one pair of [ ___ ]; its area formula is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides; its area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.

**Choices:**

- A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides; its area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$. ✔
  - _why:_ The lengths $a$ and $b$ are the parallel sides. Their mean length is $\frac{1}{2}(a+b)$, which is multiplied by the perpendicular height $h$.
- A trapezium has one pair of equal sides; its area formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
  - _why:_ A trapezium is identified by its pair of parallel sides, not necessarily equal sides. Its area is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, where $a$ and $b$ are parallel.
- A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides; its area formula is $A = (a+b)h$.
  - _why:_ This adds the parallel sides but fails to average them, doubling the area. The correct formula is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- A trapezium has one pair of perpendicular sides; its area formula is $A = ab$.
  - _why:_ This confuses a trapezium with shapes whose area comes from multiplying perpendicular dimensions. A trapezium has parallel sides and uses $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** In $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ for a trapezium, what does $h$ measure?

**Back:** The perpendicular height between the parallel sides.

**Choices:**

- The perpendicular height between the parallel sides. ✔
  - _why:_ The height is the shortest distance between the two parallel sides, measured at a right angle. This perpendicular distance is multiplied by their mean length.
- The sloping distance between the parallel sides.
  - _why:_ A sloping measurement is longer than the true height and would overestimate the area. The formula requires the perpendicular height between the parallel sides.
- The horizontal length of the longer parallel side.
  - _why:_ The parallel side lengths are represented by $a$ and $b$, not $h$. The value $h$ is the perpendicular distance separating those two sides.
- The difference in length between the parallel sides.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the parallel side lengths may describe their horizontal offset, but it is not the height. The value $h$ measures the perpendicular distance between them.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A triangular prism has cross-section base 7 cm, height $x$ cm, length 12 cm and volume 252 cm³. What equation finds $x$?

**Back:** $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x \times 12 = 252$

- $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x \times 12 = 252$ ✔
  - _why:_ The triangular cross-section has area $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x$. Multiplying this by the prism length $12$ and equating to the volume gives the correct equation.
- $7 \times x \times 12 = 252$
  - _why:_ This treats the triangular cross-section as a rectangle by omitting the factor of $\frac{1}{2}$. The correct equation is $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x \times 12 = 252$.
- $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x = 252$
  - _why:_ This calculates only the triangular cross-sectional area and omits the prism length. Multiplying by $12$ gives $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x \times 12 = 252$.
- $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 12 \times 12 = 252$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses the prism length as both the triangle’s height and the prism length. The unknown height must remain $x$, giving $\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x \times 12 = 252$.
