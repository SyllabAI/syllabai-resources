# Congruent Triangles Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 7 · 23 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** After proving two triangles are congruent, what equality fact can you use?

**Back:** Corresponding sides and angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- Corresponding sides and angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Congruent triangles have exactly the same size and shape, so every pair of corresponding sides has equal length and every pair of corresponding angles has equal measure.
- Corresponding sides and angles are proportional.
  - _why:_ Proportional measurements are associated with similar triangles and do not necessarily mean the triangles are the same size. For congruent triangles, corresponding sides and angles are equal.
- Corresponding sides and angles are parallel.
  - _why:_ Corresponding parts occupy matching positions but do not have to be parallel. Congruence allows you to conclude that corresponding sides have equal lengths and corresponding angles have equal measures.
- Corresponding sides and angles are perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines meet at $90^\circ$, which is unrelated to the general consequence of congruence. Corresponding sides and angles in congruent triangles are equal.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, the order shows B matches [ ___ ].

**Back:** In $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, the order shows B matches E.

**Choices:**

- E ✔
  - _why:_ Congruence notation lists corresponding vertices in the same order. Since B is the second vertex of $ABC$, it matches E, the second vertex of $DEF$.
- D
  - _why:_ Matching B with D incorrectly pairs the second vertex of the first triangle with the first vertex of the second. The order shows that B matches E.
- F
  - _why:_ Matching B with F incorrectly pairs the second vertex with the third vertex. Reading both triangle names in order gives $A\leftrightarrow D$, $B\leftrightarrow E$, and $C\leftrightarrow F$.
- A
  - _why:_ A belongs to the first triangle and cannot be B’s corresponding vertex in the second triangle. The notation pairs B, the second vertex, with E.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** If $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XZY$, which angle matches $\angle QPR$?

**Back:** $$\angle QPR$ matches $\angle ZXY$.

**Choices:**

- $$\angle QPR$ matches $\angle ZXY$. ✔
  - _why:_ The order gives $P\leftrightarrow X$, $Q\leftrightarrow Z$, and $R\leftrightarrow Y$. Therefore $Q-P-R$ corresponds to $Z-X-Y$, so the matching angle is $\angle ZXY$.
- $$\angle QPR$ matches $\angle XZY$.
  - _why:_ This places Z at the angle’s vertex, so it corresponds to the angle at Q rather than P. Since $P\leftrightarrow X$, the correct angle is $\angle ZXY$.
- $$\angle QPR$ matches $\angle XYZ$.
  - _why:_ This angle has Y as its vertex, corresponding to R rather than P. Using $Q\leftrightarrow Z$, $P\leftrightarrow X$, and $R\leftrightarrow Y$ gives $\angle ZXY$.
- $$\angle QPR$ matches $\angle YZX$.
  - _why:_ This choice reverses the correspondence and places Z at the vertex. The middle letter P must match X, so the corresponding angle is $\angle ZXY$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** If $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle XZY$, which side matches $QR$?

**Back:** $$QR$ matches $ZY$.

**Choices:**

- $$QR$ matches $ZY$. ✔
  - _why:_ The congruence order pairs Q with Z and R with Y. A side’s endpoints must map to their corresponding endpoints, so $QR$ matches $ZY$.
- $$QR$ matches $XZ$.
  - _why:_ The side $XZ$ joins the vertices corresponding to P and Q, so it matches $PQ$, not $QR$. Since $Q\leftrightarrow Z$ and $R\leftrightarrow Y$, $QR$ matches $ZY$.
- $$QR$ matches $XY$.
  - _why:_ The side $XY$ joins the vertices corresponding to P and R, so it matches $PR$. Mapping Q to Z and R to Y shows that $QR$ matches $ZY$.
- $$QR$ matches $ZX$.
  - _why:_ Reversing the letters of a side does not change its endpoints: $ZX$ is the same side as $XZ$, which corresponds to $PQ$. The match for $QR$ is $ZY$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** The SSS congruence test needs [ ___ ] equal.

**Back:** The SSS congruence test needs three corresponding sides equal.

**Choices:**

- three corresponding sides ✔
  - _why:_ SSS stands for side-side-side. The test proves congruence when all three sides of one triangle are equal to the three corresponding sides of the other triangle.
- two corresponding sides
  - _why:_ Two equal side pairs alone do not fix a triangle’s shape, so different triangles may satisfy this information. SSS requires all three pairs of corresponding sides to be equal.
- three corresponding angles
  - _why:_ Three equal angle pairs establish similarity, not congruence, because the triangles may have different sizes. SSS specifically requires three corresponding sides to be equal.
- two corresponding angles
  - _why:_ Two corresponding angles determine the triangle’s shape but not its scale, so this information alone cannot prove congruence. SSS requires three corresponding sides to be equal.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** For SAS congruence, where must the equal angle be?

**Back:** The included angle between the two equal sides.

**Choices:**

- The included angle between the two equal sides. ✔
  - _why:_ For SAS, the angle must be enclosed by the two sides known to be equal. This fixes the triangle uniquely and allows congruence to be established.
- The non-included angle beside one equal side.
  - _why:_ Using a non-included angle gives SSA information, which can produce more than one possible triangle. SAS requires the included angle between the two equal sides.
- The opposite angle across from both equal sides.
  - _why:_ An angle opposite one of the known sides is not enclosed by both sides and gives ambiguous SSA information. The required angle is the included angle between the equal sides.
- The largest angle inside each of the triangles.
  - _why:_ The size ranking of the angle is irrelevant to SAS. It must occupy the specific position between the two corresponding equal sides, making it the included angle.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** The ASA/AAS test uses two corresponding [ ___ ] and one corresponding side.

**Back:** The ASA/AAS test uses two corresponding angles and one corresponding side.

**Choices:**

- angles ✔
  - _why:_ ASA and AAS each use two pairs of equal corresponding angles and one pair of equal corresponding sides. The side’s position distinguishes ASA from AAS.
- vertices
  - _why:_ Vertices are points and are not measured as equal evidence for congruence. ASA and AAS require two corresponding angles and one corresponding side.
- lengths
  - _why:_ Treating the two measurements as lengths would give three side measurements rather than ASA or AAS. These tests use two corresponding angles and one corresponding side.
- diagonals
  - _why:_ A triangle has no diagonals because each pair of non-adjacent vertices is already joined by a side. ASA and AAS use two corresponding angles and one corresponding side.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** For RHS congruence, what angle fact must be true?

**Back:** Both triangles are right-angled.

**Choices:**

- Both triangles are right-angled. ✔
  - _why:_ RHS stands for right angle-hypotenuse-side, so each triangle must contain a $90^\circ$ angle. Equal hypotenuses and one other equal side can then establish congruence.
- Both triangles are acute-angled.
  - _why:_ Acute triangles contain only angles smaller than $90^\circ$, so the RHS test cannot apply. RHS requires both triangles to be right-angled.
- Both triangles are obtuse-angled.
  - _why:_ An obtuse triangle has an angle greater than $90^\circ$ and cannot satisfy the right-angle condition in RHS. Both triangles must instead be right-angled.
- Both triangles are equiangular.
  - _why:_ Equiangular triangles have three equal angles and are similar, but they are not necessarily congruent or right-angled. RHS specifically requires both triangles to be right-angled.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** For RHS congruence, which side must be equal as the longest side?

**Back:** The hypotenuses are equal.

**Choices:**

- The hypotenuses are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ In right-angled triangles, the hypotenuse is always the longest side. RHS congruence requires the two hypotenuses to have equal lengths.
- The corresponding bases are equal.
  - _why:_ Treating a chosen base as the longest side ignores the defining feature of a right-angled triangle. The hypotenuses must be equal because each is opposite the right angle.
- The right-angle sides are equal.
  - _why:_ The sides forming the right angle are shorter legs, not the longest sides. RHS requires equal hypotenuses, together with one equal pair of corresponding legs.
- The perpendicular sides are equal.
  - _why:_ The perpendicular sides form the right angle and are not hypotenuses. The required longest sides are the hypotenuses, which lie opposite the right angles.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** For RHS congruence, what side fact is needed besides equal hypotenuses?

**Back:** One other corresponding side is equal.

**Choices:**

- One other corresponding side is equal. ✔
  - _why:_ RHS uses a right angle, equal hypotenuses and one other pair of equal corresponding sides. These three facts fix the triangles’ size and shape.
- One other non-corresponding side is equal.
  - _why:_ Comparing sides that do not occupy matching positions cannot establish RHS congruence. One other pair of corresponding sides must be equal alongside the equal hypotenuses.
- One other corresponding side is parallel.
  - _why:_ Parallel sides describe direction rather than length, so this does not meet the RHS condition. One other pair of corresponding sides must have equal lengths.
- One other corresponding side is twice as long.
  - _why:_ A doubled side length would make the triangles different sizes rather than congruent. RHS requires one other corresponding side to be equal in length.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Why do three equal angles not prove congruent triangles?

**Back:** AAA gives the same shape, not necessarily the same size.

**Choices:**

- AAA gives the same shape, not necessarily the same size. ✔
  - _why:_ Equal corresponding angles guarantee similarity, so the triangles have the same shape. Their corresponding sides may be scaled, meaning the triangles need not be congruent.
- AAA gives the same size, but not necessarily the same shape.
  - _why:_ This reverses the similarity result. Three equal corresponding angles fix the shape, but side lengths may differ, so the triangles are not necessarily the same size.
- AAA proves both triangles have equal corresponding side lengths.
  - _why:_ Equal angles do not determine absolute side lengths because one triangle may be an enlargement of the other. AAA establishes the same shape, not necessarily the same size.
- AAA only applies when all three angles are right angles.
  - _why:_ A triangle cannot contain three right angles because its angles total 180°. AAA can show similarity for any three matching angles, but it does not prove congruence.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** A common side is [ ___ ] in both triangles.

**Back:** A common side is equal to itself in both triangles.

**Choices:**

- equal to itself ✔
  - _why:_ A common side is the same line segment in both triangles, so its length is equal to itself. This shared length can be used in a congruence proof.
- different in length
  - _why:_ A shared side cannot have two different lengths because it is the same line segment in both triangles. The correct fact is that it is equal to itself.
- twice as long
  - _why:_ This treats the shared segment as though it changes length between the triangles. A common side is identical in both, so it is equal to itself.
- perpendicular to itself
  - _why:_ A side does not form a right angle with itself. The useful congruence fact is about length: the common side is equal to itself in both triangles.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a parallelogram, opposite sides are [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal.

**Choices:**

- equal ✔
  - _why:_ A defining property of every parallelogram is that each pair of opposite sides has equal length. This fact can provide corresponding equal sides in a congruence proof.
- perpendicular
  - _why:_ Perpendicular sides meet at 90°, which is not generally true of opposite sides in a parallelogram. Opposite sides are equal in length and parallel.
- adjacent
  - _why:_ Opposite sides lie across from one another rather than sharing a vertex, so they are not adjacent. The required length property is that opposite sides are equal.
- unequal
  - _why:_ This contradicts a defining property of parallelograms. Both pairs of opposite sides have equal lengths, which is often used when proving triangles congruent.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What do diagonals of a parallelogram do to each other?

**Back:** They bisect each other - cut each other in half.

**Choices:**

- They bisect each other - cut each other in half. ✔
  - _why:_ The diagonals of every parallelogram meet at their shared midpoint. Therefore, each diagonal divides the other into two segments of equal length.
- They bisect the angles - cut each corner angle in half.
  - _why:_ Confusing parallelograms with rhombuses makes this tempting, but parallelogram diagonals do not generally bisect the corner angles. They bisect each other at their intersection.
- They meet at right angles - cross at 90°.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular diagonals occur in special parallelograms such as rhombuses, but not in every parallelogram. The universal property is that the diagonals bisect each other.
- They are equal in length - have the same measurement.
  - _why:_ Equal diagonals are a property of rectangles, not all parallelograms. In every parallelogram, the diagonals bisect each other, although their total lengths may differ.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** When two straight lines cross, what angle equality can you use?

**Back:** Vertically opposite angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- Vertically opposite angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ When two straight lines intersect, the angles directly opposite one another are vertically opposite angles. Each opposite pair always has equal angle measures.
- Adjacent angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles at an intersection usually form a straight line and sum to 180°, rather than being equal. Vertically opposite angles are the equal pairs.
- Corresponding angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Corresponding angles require a transversal crossing parallel lines, not merely two intersecting lines. At a simple intersection, vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Angles on a straight line are equal.
  - _why:_ Angles on a straight line add to 180° but are not generally equal. The equality created whenever two lines cross is between vertically opposite angles.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** On parallel lines, which angle equality can help prove triangles congruent?

**Back:** Alternate angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- Alternate angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ When a transversal crosses parallel lines, alternate angles lie between the lines on opposite sides of the transversal. These angles are equal and can support congruence proofs.
- Co-interior angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Co-interior angles between parallel lines add to 180° rather than being equal in general. Alternate angles form an equal pair that can be used in congruence proofs.
- Adjacent angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles share a vertex and side but are not automatically equal. With parallel lines, the relevant guaranteed equality here is between alternate angles.
- Angles on a straight line are equal.
  - _why:_ Angles on a straight line sum to 180° and need not have equal measures. Alternate angles formed by a transversal across parallel lines are equal.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** A radius to the point where a tangent touches a circle is [ ___ ] to the tangent.

**Back:** A radius to the point where a tangent touches a circle is perpendicular to the tangent.

**Choices:**

- perpendicular ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent meets the radius at the point of contact at a right angle, so the radius is perpendicular to the tangent.
- parallel
  - _why:_ A tangent cannot be parallel to the radius at their point of contact. The radius and tangent meet at a right angle, so the correct term is perpendicular.
- oblique
  - _why:_ An oblique angle is not a right angle. The circle theorem states that a radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
- inclined at $45^\circ$
  - _why:_ The angle is not specifically $45^\circ$; it is always $90^\circ$. Therefore, the radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** In a circle with centre O, why is $OP=OQ$ for points P and Q on the circle?

**Back:** $$OP$ and $OQ$ are radii of the same circle.

**Choices:**

- $$OP$ and $OQ$ are radii of the same circle. ✔
  - _why:_ Both segments run from the centre O to points on the circumference. All radii of the same circle have equal length, so $OP=OQ$.
- $$OP$ and $OQ$ are equal chords of the same circle.
  - _why:_ Chords have both endpoints on the circumference, whereas these segments start at the centre. They are radii of the same circle, so their lengths are equal.
- $$OP$ and $OQ$ are diameters of the same circle.
  - _why:_ A diameter passes through the centre and has two endpoints on the circumference. Each segment has only one radius length, so both are radii of the same circle.
- $$OP$ and $OQ$ are tangents from the same point.
  - _why:_ A tangent touches a circle at one point and does not run from the centre to the circumference. These segments are equal because they are radii of the same circle.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** In the tangent proof with triangles XPO and XQO, which side is the shared hypotenuse?

**Back:** $XO$.

**Choices:**

- $XO$. ✔
  - _why:_ Both right-angled triangles contain $XO$, and it lies opposite their right angles at P and Q. It is therefore their shared hypotenuse.
- $XP$.
  - _why:_ $$XP$ is a leg of triangle XPO and does not belong to triangle XQO. The side shared by both triangles, and their hypotenuse, is $XO$.
- $XQ$.
  - _why:_ $$XQ$ is a leg of triangle XQO but is not a side of triangle XPO. The common hypotenuse in both triangles is $XO$.
- $OP$.
  - _why:_ $$OP$ is a radius and a perpendicular leg of triangle XPO, not a shared side. Both triangles instead contain the hypotenuse $XO$.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** A midpoint cuts a line segment into [ ___ ].

**Back:** A midpoint cuts a line segment into two equal parts.

**Choices:**

- two equal parts ✔
  - _why:_ By definition, a midpoint lies halfway along a line segment and divides it into exactly two sections of equal length.
- two unequal parts
  - _why:_ A point that creates unequal sections is not the midpoint. A midpoint is halfway along the segment and therefore cuts it into two equal parts.
- three equal parts
  - _why:_ A single midpoint makes exactly two sections, not three. Dividing a segment into three equal parts requires two trisection points rather than one midpoint.
- two parts in a $1:2$ ratio
  - _why:_ A $1:2$ division places the point away from the centre of the segment. A midpoint divides the segment in a $1:1$ ratio, producing two equal parts.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** In the midpoint-parallelogram proof, how do you get $XM=NP$?

**Back:** $XM=MZ$ from midpoint M.$MZ=NP$ in parallelogram ZMNP.

**Choices:**

- $XM=MZ$ from midpoint M.$MZ=NP$ in parallelogram ZMNP. ✔
  - _why:_ M is the midpoint of $XZ$, giving $XM=MZ$. Opposite sides of parallelogram ZMNP are equal, giving $MZ=NP$, so $XM=NP$ by transitivity.
- $XM=MZ$ from midpoint M.$MZ=MN$ in parallelogram ZMNP.
  - _why:_ The midpoint equality is valid, but adjacent sides of a parallelogram are not necessarily equal. The required second equality is $MZ=NP$ because these are opposite sides.
- $XM=XZ$ from midpoint M.$MZ=NP$ in parallelogram ZMNP.
  - _why:_ A midpoint makes each half equal, not one half equal to the entire segment. The correct chain is $XM=MZ$ and $MZ=NP$, hence $XM=NP$.
- $XM=MN$ from midpoint M.$MN=NP$ in parallelogram ZMNP.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of $XZ$ relates $XM$ to $MZ$, not to $MN$, while adjacent parallelogram sides need not be equal. Use $XM=MZ=NP$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** In quadrilateral WXYZ with $WX=WZ$ and $YX=YZ$, which common side completes SSS for triangles WXY and WZY?

**Back:** $WY$ is common to both triangles.

**Choices:**

- $WY$ is common to both triangles. ✔
  - _why:_ Both triangles include the diagonal $WY$, so $WY=WY$. Together with the two given pairs of equal sides, this establishes SSS congruence.
- $WX$ is common to both triangles.
  - _why:_ $$WX$ belongs to triangle WXY but not triangle WZY, so it cannot be the common side. Both triangles contain the diagonal $WY$.
- $WZ$ is common to both triangles.
  - _why:_ $$WZ$ is a side of triangle WZY only, rather than a shared side. The diagonal $WY$ appears in both triangles and completes SSS.
- $XY$ is common to both triangles.
  - _why:_ $$XY$ belongs only to triangle WXY; triangle WZY contains $YZ$ instead. The side present in both triangles is $WY$.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** Once $\triangle WXY \cong \triangle WZY$, why is $\angle WXY=\angle WZY$?

**Back:** They are corresponding angles in congruent triangles.

**Choices:**

- They are corresponding angles in congruent triangles. ✔
  - _why:_ The congruence order pairs vertex X in the first triangle with vertex Z in the second. Therefore, the angles at those corresponding vertices are equal.
- They are vertically opposite angles in congruent triangles.
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles arise where two straight lines intersect, which is not the reason here. The angles are equal because they correspond in congruent triangles.
- They are alternate interior angles in congruent triangles.
  - _why:_ Alternate interior angles require parallel lines and a transversal, neither of which establishes this equality. Congruence makes the angles equal because they are corresponding angles.
- They are base angles in congruent triangles.
  - _why:_ No single isosceles triangle contains these as its equal base angles. They are equal because vertex X corresponds to vertex Z in the congruent triangles.
