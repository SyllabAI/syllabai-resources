## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a word-problem fraction, use $\frac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}$: the denominator is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a word-problem fraction, use $\frac{\text{part}}{\text{total}}$: the denominator is the total number of items.

**Choices:**

- total number of items ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator represents the whole group being considered, so it is the total number of items. The numerator represents the items belonging to the specified part.
- number of selected items
  - _why:_ This confuses the denominator with the numerator. The selected items form the part, while the denominator must be the total number of items in the whole group.
- number of unselected items
  - _why:_ Counting the unselected items gives the complementary part, not the denominator. The denominator is the total number of items, including both selected and unselected items.
- difference between the parts
  - _why:_ Subtracting the sizes of the parts does not give a fraction’s denominator. The denominator records the total number of items in the complete group.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** 20 counters, 6 yellow: what fraction are yellow before simplifying?

**Back:** $\frac{6}{20}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{6}{20}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are 6 yellow counters out of 20 counters altogether, so the part-over-total fraction is $\frac{6}{20}$. No simplification is required by the question.
- $\frac{14}{20}$
  - _why:_ This counts the 14 counters that are not yellow. The yellow counters are the required part, so the fraction before simplifying is $\frac{6}{20}$.
- $\frac{6}{14}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses the number of non-yellow counters as the denominator. The denominator must be all 20 counters, giving $\frac{6}{20}$.
- $\frac{20}{6}$
  - _why:_ This reverses part and total. The 6 yellow counters belong in the numerator and all 20 counters belong in the denominator, giving $\frac{6}{20}$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** 20 counters, 6 yellow: what fraction are yellow in simplest form?

**Back:** $\frac{3}{10}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{10}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The fraction is initially $\frac{6}{20}$. Dividing both the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor, 2, gives the simplest form $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $\frac{7}{10}$
  - _why:_ This is the simplified fraction of counters that are not yellow, since $20-6=14$ and $\frac{14}{20}=\frac{7}{10}$. The yellow fraction is $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $\frac{3}{7}$
  - _why:_ This compares the 6 yellow counters with the 14 non-yellow counters instead of the total. Using 20 as the denominator and simplifying gives $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $\frac{6}{10}$
  - _why:_ This halves only the denominator, which changes the fraction’s value. Dividing both 6 and 20 by 2 gives the correct simplest form, $\frac{3}{10}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** 15 pens, 6 red, rest green: how many pens are green?

**Back:** 9

**Choices:**

- 9 ✔
  - _why:_ The 15 pens consist of 6 red pens and the remaining green pens. Subtracting gives $15-6=9$, so there are 9 green pens.
- 6
  - _why:_ This repeats the number of red pens rather than finding the remainder. Subtracting the 6 red pens from all 15 pens gives 9 green pens.
- 21
  - _why:_ This comes from adding 15 and 6, even though the red pens are already included in the total. The green count is $15-6=9$.
- 11
  - _why:_ This results from an incorrect subtraction of 6 from 15. The correct calculation is $15-6=9$, so 9 of the pens are green.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** 15 pens, 6 red, rest green: what fraction are green in simplest form?

**Back:** $\frac{3}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are $15-6=9$ green pens, so the fraction is $\frac{9}{15}$. Dividing both numbers by 3 gives the simplest form $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{2}{5}$
  - _why:_ This is the fraction of pens that are red, because $\frac{6}{15}=\frac{2}{5}$. The green fraction is $\frac{9}{15}=\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{4}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly compares the 9 green pens with the 12 obtained by adding green and half the red count. The correct part-over-total calculation gives $\frac{3}{5}$.
- $\frac{5}{3}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the simplified fraction, producing an improper fraction greater than 1 even though green pens are only part of the total. The correct fraction is $\frac{3}{5}$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Choices:**

- same number ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero common factor preserves the fraction’s value. Using the highest common factor produces simplest form in one step.
- two different numbers
  - _why:_ Dividing by different numbers changes the fraction’s value rather than simplifying it equivalently. Both the numerator and denominator must be divided by the same common factor.
- numerator only
  - _why:_ Dividing both parts by the numerator alone is generally impossible unless it also divides the denominator exactly. A fraction is simplified using the same common factor.
- denominator only
  - _why:_ Focusing only on the denominator does not preserve the fraction’s value and may not produce whole numbers. Both parts must be divided by the same common factor.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{24}{32}$.

**Back:** $\frac{3}{4}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{4}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The highest common factor of 24 and 32 is 8. Dividing both numbers by 8 gives $\frac{24\div8}{32\div8}=\frac{3}{4}$.
- $\frac{4}{3}$
  - _why:_ This inverts the simplified fraction. Since 24 is less than 32, the answer must remain less than 1; dividing both by 8 gives $\frac{3}{4}$.
- $\frac{3}{8}$
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing the numerator and denominator by different numbers, which changes the fraction’s value. Dividing both by 8 gives $\frac{3}{4}$.
- $\frac{2}{3}$
  - _why:_ This may result from subtracting 8 repeatedly rather than dividing by a common factor. Simplification requires dividing 24 and 32 by 8, giving $\frac{3}{4}$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common factor except [ ___ ].

**Back:** A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1.

**Choices:**

- 1 ✔
  - _why:_ Every whole number has 1 as a factor. A fraction is in simplest form when 1 is the only positive factor shared by its numerator and denominator.
- 0
  - _why:_ Zero is not a factor of positive integers because division by zero is undefined. The only common factor allowed in a fraction’s simplest form is 1.
- 2
  - _why:_ If both numbers have 2 as a factor, they can still be divided by 2, so the fraction is not fully simplified. The only permitted common factor is 1.
- 10
  - _why:_ A shared factor of 10 would mean both numbers could be divided by 10, so further simplification is possible. In simplest form, their only common factor is 1.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** If the numerator and denominator are both even, what divisor should you try first?

**Back:** 2

**Choices:**

- 2 ✔
  - _why:_ Every even integer is divisible by 2, so both parts can always be divided by 2 before checking whether the fraction simplifies further.
- 4
  - _why:_ Being even does not guarantee divisibility by 4 - for example, 6 is even but not divisible by 4. The first guaranteed divisor is 2.
- 6
  - _why:_ Divisibility by 6 requires divisibility by both 2 and 3, while evenness guarantees only divisibility by 2. Therefore, try 2 first.
- 8
  - _why:_ An even number need not be divisible by 8 - for example, 10 is not. Both even parts are always divisible by 2, so start there.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** If the numerator and denominator both end in 0 or 5, what divisor should you try first?

**Back:** 5

**Choices:**

- 5 ✔
  - _why:_ Any whole number ending in 0 or 5 is divisible by 5, so 5 is a guaranteed common divisor to try when both parts have these endings.
- 10
  - _why:_ Numbers ending in 0 are divisible by 10, but numbers ending in 5 are not. Since either ending is allowed, the guaranteed divisor is 5.
- 3
  - _why:_ Ending in 0 or 5 does not establish divisibility by 3; that depends on the sum of the digits. The guaranteed divisor is 5.
- 25
  - _why:_ Only some numbers ending in 0 or 5 are divisible by 25 - for example, 30 is not. All such numbers are divisible by 5.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Last year £30, this year £42: what is the increase?

**Back:** £12

**Choices:**

- £12 ✔
  - _why:_ Subtract the original cost from the new cost: £42 - £30 = £12. Therefore, the cost has increased by £12.
- £10
  - _why:_ This results from comparing only the tens digits and ignores the remaining units. Subtracting the complete amounts gives £42 - £30 = £12.
- £32
  - _why:_ This subtracts £10 from £42 rather than subtracting the original £30. The increase is found using £42 - £30 = £12.
- £72
  - _why:_ This adds the two costs instead of finding their difference. An increase is calculated by subtracting the old value: £42 - £30 = £12.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A cost rises from £30 to £42. As a fraction of last year’s cost, what is the increase in simplest form?

**Back:** $\frac{2}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The increase is £42 - £30 = £12. As a fraction of the original £30, this is $rac{12}{30}$, which simplifies to $rac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{2}{7}$
  - _why:_ This divides the £12 increase by the new cost of £42, giving $rac{12}{42}=rac{2}{7}$. The question requires division by the original £30, giving $rac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{7}{5}$
  - _why:_ This represents the new cost as a fraction of the old cost: $rac{42}{30}=rac{7}{5}$. The increase alone is $rac{12}{30}=rac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This treats £18 as the increase, but the actual difference is £42 - £30 = £12. Thus the required fraction is $rac{12}{30}=rac{2}{5}$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** Equivalent fractions have the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Equivalent fractions have the same value.

**Choices:**

- same value ✔
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions may use different numerators and denominators, but they represent the same proportion or point on the number line, so they have the same value.
- same numerator
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions usually have different numerators because both parts have been scaled by a common factor. Their defining feature is that they have the same value.
- same denominator
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions can have different denominators, such as $rac{1}{2}$ and $rac{2}{4}$. They are equivalent because they have the same value.
- same appearance
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions often look different because their numerators and denominators have been scaled. What makes them equivalent is that they represent the same value.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Which is not equivalent to $\frac{2}{3}$: $\frac{8}{12}$, $\frac{10}{15}$, $\frac{14}{21}$, $\frac{15}{24}$, $\frac{18}{27}$?

**Back:** $\frac{15}{24}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{15}{24}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Simplifying gives $rac{15}{24}=rac{5}{8}$, which is not $rac{2}{3}$. The other listed fractions all simplify to $rac{2}{3}$.
- $\frac{8}{12}$
  - _why:_ Dividing both parts by 4 gives $rac{8}{12}=rac{2}{3}$, so this fraction is equivalent. The non-equivalent fraction is $rac{15}{24}=rac{5}{8}$.
- $\frac{10}{15}$
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator and denominator by 5 gives $rac{10}{15}=rac{2}{3}$. The fraction that does not simplify to $rac{2}{3}$ is $rac{15}{24}$.
- $\frac{14}{21}$
  - _why:_ Dividing both parts by 7 gives $rac{14}{21}=rac{2}{3}$, so it is equivalent. Instead, $rac{15}{24}$ simplifies to $rac{5}{8}$.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** To make an equivalent fraction, multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To make an equivalent fraction, multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same number.

**Choices:**

- same number ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying or dividing both parts by the same non-zero number preserves the numerator-to-denominator ratio, so the resulting fraction has the same value.
- two different numbers
  - _why:_ Using different multipliers or divisors changes the ratio between the numerator and denominator. Both parts must be multiplied or divided by the same number.
- numerator and denominator respectively
  - _why:_ Scaling the numerator by the numerator and the denominator by the denominator generally changes the fraction’s value. Both parts require the same multiplier or divisor.
- two consecutive numbers
  - _why:_ Using consecutive numbers applies unequal scale factors to the numerator and denominator, changing their ratio. An equivalent fraction requires the same number for both parts.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** With the same denominator, which is smaller: $\frac{3}{20}$ or $\frac{7}{20}$?

**Back:** $\frac{3}{20}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{3}{20}$ ✔
  - _why:_ When denominators are equal, the fraction with the smaller numerator is smaller. Since 3 is less than 7, $rac{3}{20}$ is smaller.
- $\frac{7}{20}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the comparison of the numerators. With equal denominators, 7 twentieths is greater than 3 twentieths, so the smaller fraction is $rac{3}{20}$.
- $\frac{3}{7}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly combines the two numerators and is not one of the fractions being compared. With equal denominators, $rac{3}{20}$ is smaller because 3 < 7.
- $\frac{20}{7}$
  - _why:_ This inverts $rac{7}{20}$ and produces a value greater than 1, rather than comparing the given fractions. The smaller given fraction is $rac{3}{20}$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** For denominators 4, 10, 5 and 2, what common denominator is used in the notes?

**Back:** 20

**Choices:**

- 20 ✔
  - _why:_ The lowest common multiple of 4, 10, 5 and 2 is 20, so each fraction can be rewritten with denominator 20.
- 10
  - _why:_ Although 10 is divisible by 10, 5 and 2, it is not divisible by 4. The common denominator used is 20.
- 4
  - _why:_ Using the largest original denominator does not guarantee a common denominator, since 4 does not divide into 10. The notes use 20.
- 40
  - _why:_ Although 40 is a possible common denominator, it is not the denominator used in the notes. The lowest common denominator is 20.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Order from smallest: $\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{3}{10}$, $\frac{2}{5}$, $\frac{1}{2}$.

**Back:** $\frac{1}{4},\ \frac{3}{10},\ \frac{2}{5},\ \frac{1}{2}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{1}{4},\ \frac{3}{10},\ \frac{2}{5},\ \frac{1}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Using denominator 20 gives $\frac{5}{20}$, $\frac{6}{20}$, $\frac{8}{20}$ and $\frac{10}{20}$, so the fractions are already listed from smallest to largest.
- $\frac{1}{2},\ \frac{2}{5},\ \frac{3}{10},\ \frac{1}{4}$
  - _why:_ This places the fractions in decreasing rather than increasing order. With denominator 20, the correct numerators are 5, 6, 8 and 10 from smallest to largest.
- $\frac{3}{10},\ \frac{1}{4},\ \frac{2}{5},\ \frac{1}{2}$
  - _why:_ Comparing only the original numerators wrongly places $\frac{3}{10}$ below $\frac{1}{4}$. Their equivalents are $\frac{6}{20}$ and $\frac{5}{20}$, so $\frac{1}{4}$ is smaller.
- $\frac{1}{4},\ \frac{3}{10},\ \frac{1}{2},\ \frac{2}{5}$
  - _why:_ A larger denominator does not automatically mean a larger fraction. Since $\frac{2}{5}=\frac{8}{20}$ and $\frac{1}{2}=\frac{10}{20}$, $\frac{2}{5}$ must come first.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a “closer to” question, the fraction with the [ ___ ] from the target is closer.

**Back:** For a “closer to” question, the fraction with the smaller distance from the target is closer.

**Choices:**

- smaller distance ✔
  - _why:_ Closeness is measured by the absolute difference between each fraction and the target. The fraction with the smaller distance therefore lies nearer to the target.
- larger distance
  - _why:_ A larger distance means the fraction is farther from the target, not closer. The correct comparison selects the fraction with the smaller distance.
- positive difference
  - _why:_ The sign only shows which side of the target a fraction lies on; it does not determine closeness. The smaller absolute distance identifies the closer fraction.
- negative difference
  - _why:_ A negative difference means the fraction lies below the target, but it may still be far away. Closeness depends on the smaller distance from the target.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Which is closer to 1: $\frac{5}{4}$ or $\frac{4}{5}$?

**Back:** $\frac{4}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{4}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The distances from 1 are $\frac{1}{5}$ for $\frac{4}{5}$ and $\frac{1}{4}$ for $\frac{5}{4}$. Since $\frac{1}{5}<\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{4}{5}$ is closer.
- $\frac{5}{4}$
  - _why:_ Being above 1 does not make a fraction closer. Its distance is $\frac{1}{4}$, while $\frac{4}{5}$ is only $\frac{1}{5}$ from 1.
- They are equally close
  - _why:_ Reciprocal fractions are not necessarily equally distant from 1. Their distances are $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{5}$, so $\frac{4}{5}$ is closer.
- Neither fraction
  - _why:_ Both fractions can be compared by subtracting them from 1 and taking absolute values. The smaller distance is $\frac{1}{5}$, belonging to $\frac{4}{5}$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Which is closer to $\frac{1}{2}$: $\frac{2}{5}$ or $\frac{5}{8}$?

**Back:** $\frac{2}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The distances from $\frac{1}{2}$ are $\frac{1}{10}$ for $\frac{2}{5}$ and $\frac{1}{8}$ for $\frac{5}{8}$. Since $\frac{1}{10}<\frac{1}{8}$, $\frac{2}{5}$ is closer.
- $\frac{5}{8}$
  - _why:_ Comparing decimal values gives distances of $0.1$ for $\frac{2}{5}$ and $0.125$ for $\frac{5}{8}$. Therefore $\frac{2}{5}$, not $\frac{5}{8}$, is closer.
- They are equally close
  - _why:_ The fractions lie on opposite sides of $\frac{1}{2}$, but their distances are unequal. Since $\frac{1}{10}<\frac{1}{8}$, $\frac{2}{5}$ is closer.
- Neither fraction
  - _why:_ Both distances can be calculated exactly, so a closer fraction can be identified. The distance $\frac{1}{10}$ makes $\frac{2}{5}$ closer than $\frac{5}{8}$.
