## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the gradient of a straight line represent?

**Back:** The steepness of the line (how much it goes up or down for every 1 unit across).

**Choices:**

- The steepness of the line (how much it goes up or down for every 1 unit across). ✔
  - _why:_ Gradient measures the line’s vertical change for each unit of horizontal change, so it describes both how steep the line is and its direction.
- The length of the line (how far it travels between its two endpoints).
  - _why:_ Length measures the distance between two points, not the rate at which the line rises or falls. Gradient represents steepness: the vertical change per 1 unit across.
- The intercept of the line (where it crosses the horizontal or vertical axis).
  - _why:_ An intercept gives a point where the line crosses an axis, rather than its steepness. Gradient measures how much the line rises or falls per unit across.
- The position of the line (how far it is from the coordinate axes).
  - _why:_ A line’s position does not determine its gradient because parallel lines can have different positions but equal gradients. Gradient represents vertical change for every 1 unit across.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the rise over run formula for gradient?

**Back:** $$$\text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$\text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ Gradient is rise over run: divide the vertical change, represented by the change in $y$, by the horizontal change, represented by the change in $x$.
- $$$\text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } x}{\text{change in } y}$$
  - _why:_ This reverses rise over run and produces the reciprocal of the gradient. The correct formula divides the change in $y$ by the change in $x$.
- $$$\text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x + \text{change in } y}$$
  - _why:_ Adding the two changes in the denominator does not calculate steepness. Gradient is rise over run, so it is the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$.
- $$$\text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } x + \text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly includes the horizontal change in the numerator. Gradient compares vertical change alone with horizontal change, using change in $y$ divided by change in $x$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Back:** The y-coordinate (the second number).

**Choices:**

- The y-coordinate (the second number). ✔
  - _why:_ In an ordered pair $(x, y)$, the second value is the $y$-coordinate. It gives the point’s vertical position, measured up or down.
- The x-coordinate (the first number).
  - _why:_ The first number is the $x$-coordinate, which determines horizontal position rather than vertical position. The second number, the $y$-coordinate, determines vertical position.
- The origin (the point $(0, 0)$).
  - _why:_ The origin is the fixed point $(0, 0)$ and does not determine another point’s vertical position. That position is given by the second number, the $y$-coordinate.
- The gradient (the rate of change).
  - _why:_ Gradient describes a line’s steepness, not the vertical position of an individual point. In $(x, y)$, vertical position is determined by the second value, the $y$-coordinate.

  - _why:_ Run does not mean the total distance travelled between two points. It is specifically the horizontal change, or change in $x$, used in the gradient formula.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formal coordinate formula for gradient $m$ using points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$?

**Back:** $$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

**Choices:**

- $$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$ ✔
  - _why:_ Gradient is vertical change divided by horizontal change. Using the labelled coordinates, that is the difference in $y$-values over the matching difference in $x$-values.
- $$$m = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{y_2 - y_1}$$
  - _why:_ This swaps the numerator and denominator, giving the reciprocal of the gradient. The correct formula puts the change in $y$ on top and the change in $x$ underneath.
- $$$m = \frac{y_2 + y_1}{x_2 + x_1}$$
  - _why:_ This adds coordinates instead of finding their differences. Gradient measures change, so the correct formula is $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$.
- $$$m = \frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_2 - x_1}$$
  - _why:_ This reverses only the $y$-subtraction, which changes the sign of the gradient. If you reverse the order on top, you must also reverse it on the bottom.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Calculate the gradient of the line passing through $(3, 2)$ and $(7, 10)$.

**Back:** $$$m = \frac{10 - 2}{7 - 3} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$

**Choices:**

- $$$m = \frac{10 - 2}{7 - 3} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$ ✔
  - _why:_ The change in $y$ is $10 - 2 = 8$ and the change in $x$ is $7 - 3 = 4$. Dividing gives $m = 2$.
- $$$m = \frac{7 - 3}{10 - 2} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$$
  - _why:_ This swaps the change in $x$ and the change in $y$, giving the reciprocal. Gradient is vertical change over horizontal change, so the answer is $2$.
- $$$m = \frac{10 - 2}{7 + 3} = \frac{8}{10} = \frac{4}{5}$$
  - _why:_ This adds the x-coordinates instead of subtracting them. The horizontal change is $7 - 3 = 4$, so the correct gradient is $\frac{8}{4} = 2$.
- $$$m = \frac{2 - 10}{7 - 3} = \frac{-8}{4} = -2$$
  - _why:_ This reverses the y-values but not the x-values, causing a sign error. The order must match, so $\frac{10 - 2}{7 - 3} = 2$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a common mistake when substituting negative coordinates into the gradient formula?

**Back:** Forgetting that subtracting a negative becomes addition (e.g., $2 - (-4) = 6$).

**Choices:**

- Forgetting that subtracting a negative becomes addition (e.g., $2 - (-4) = 6$). ✔
  - _why:_ When a negative coordinate is subtracted, the two minus signs combine to make addition. This is why $2 - (-4)$ equals $6$, not $-2$.
- Forgetting that adding a negative becomes subtraction (e.g., $2 + (-4) = -2$).
  - _why:_ This statement is a correct rule for adding a negative, not the key substitution mistake here. The common gradient slip is missing that subtracting a negative becomes addition.
- Forgetting that subtracting a positive becomes addition (e.g., $2 - 4 = 6$).
  - _why:_ Subtracting a positive does not become addition; $2 - 4 = -2$. The relevant mistake is with negative coordinates, where $2 - (-4)$ should become $2 + 4$.
- Forgetting that multiplying a negative becomes addition (e.g., $2 \times (-4) = 6$).
  - _why:_ This mixes up multiplication with subtraction in the gradient formula. The common issue is subtracting a negative coordinate, where the operation becomes addition, such as $2 - (-4) = 6$.

  - _why:_ Using opposite subtraction orders changes the sign of only one difference, producing the negative of the correct gradient. The order must be consistent for $x$ and $y$.
