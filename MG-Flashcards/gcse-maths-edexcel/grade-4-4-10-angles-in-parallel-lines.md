# Angles in Parallel Lines Flashcards

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the angle sum on a straight line?

**Back:** 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ A straight line forms a half-turn, so angles meeting at a point along it have a total of 180°.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ 90° is the size of a right angle, not the angle sum on a straight line. A straight line is a half-turn, so the correct sum is 180°.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ 360° is the angle sum around a point, representing a full turn. A straight line represents only a half-turn, so its angle sum is 180°.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ 270° represents three-quarters of a full turn and is not the straight-line angle sum. Angles on a straight line always add to 180°.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Two straight lines cross. What is true about vertically opposite angles?

**Back:** They are equal.

**Choices:**

- They are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles are formed directly across from one another when two straight lines intersect, and each pair always has the same size.
- They are adjacent.
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles lie next to each other and share a side, whereas vertically opposite angles lie across the intersection. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- They are always unequal.
  - _why:_ The angles may look different because of the diagram’s orientation, but vertically opposite angles formed by intersecting straight lines are always equal.
- They are on the same side.
  - _why:_ Angles on the same side of an intersection are adjacent rather than vertically opposite. Vertically opposite angles lie across from each other and are equal.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the angle sum in a triangle?

**Back:** 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ The three interior angles of every triangle add to 180°, regardless of the triangle’s side lengths or whether it is acute, right-angled or obtuse.
- 90°.
  - _why:_ 90° is the size of one right angle, not the total of a triangle’s interior angles. The three angles in a triangle add to 180°.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ 360° is the sum of angles around a point or in a quadrilateral. A triangle has three interior angles whose sum is 180°.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ 270° can result from confusing a triangle’s angle sum with three right angles. The interior angles of any triangle add to 180°.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles on a [ ___ ] add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles on a straight line add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- Angles on a straight line add to 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ A straight line represents a half-turn, which measures 180°. Therefore, all adjacent angles formed along the straight line must total 180°.
- Angles on a straight line add to 90°.
  - _why:_ 90° is a right angle and represents only a quarter-turn. A straight line is a half-turn, so angles on it add to 180°.
- Angles on a right angle add to 180°.
  - _why:_ Angles dividing a right angle total 90°, not 180°. The 180° rule applies to angles on a straight line because it forms a half-turn.
- Angles on a full turn add to 90°.
  - _why:_ A full turn measures 360°, while 90° is only a quarter-turn. The required rule is that angles on a straight line add to 180°.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Two straight lines cross. One angle is 68°. What is the vertically opposite angle?

**Back:** 68°.

**Choices:**

- 68°. ✔
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles are always equal, so the angle directly opposite the given 68° angle also measures 68°.
- 112°.
  - _why:_ 112° is the adjacent angle because it combines with 68° to make 180°. The vertically opposite angle is equal to the original angle, so it is 68°.
- 34°.
  - _why:_ Halving 68° is not part of the vertically opposite angle rule. Vertically opposite angles have equal measures, so the required angle is 68°.
- 292°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 68° from 360° finds the remainder of a full turn, not one vertically opposite angle. The opposite angle is equal to 68°.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** An angle is 68° on a straight line. What is the adjacent angle?

**Back:** 112°.

**Choices:**

- 112°. ✔
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles on a straight line add to 180°. Subtracting gives 180° - 68° = 112°, so the adjacent angle is 112°.
- 68°.
  - _why:_ 68° would be the vertically opposite angle, not the adjacent one. Adjacent angles on a straight line total 180°, giving 180° - 68° = 112°.
- 22°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting from 90° incorrectly treats the pair as complementary angles. Angles on a straight line total 180°, so the adjacent angle is 112°.
- 292°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting from 360° incorrectly uses the full-turn rule. The two adjacent angles form a straight line, so the calculation is 180° - 68° = 112°.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** The notation $AB \parallel CD$ means line [ ___ ].

**Back:** The notation $AB \parallel CD$ means line AB is parallel to CD.

**Choices:**

- AB is parallel to CD ✔
  - _why:_ The symbol $\parallel$ means “is parallel to”, so $AB \parallel CD$ states that lines AB and CD remain the same distance apart and never meet.
- AB is perpendicular to CD
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines meet at 90° and are represented by a different symbol. The symbol $\parallel$ states that AB is parallel to CD.
- AB intersects CD at 45°
  - _why:_ The parallel symbol gives no 45° intersection; parallel lines do not intersect. The notation means that AB is parallel to CD.
- AB is equal in length to CD
  - _why:_ Matching lengths are shown with equality notation or matching tick marks, not the parallel symbol. $AB \parallel CD$ means AB is parallel to CD.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** On a diagram, how are parallel lines often marked?

**Back:** With matching arrows.

**Choices:**

- With matching arrows. ✔
  - _why:_ Matching arrow markings placed on two or more lines show that those lines are parallel. Different arrow styles can distinguish separate sets of parallel lines.
- With matching ticks.
  - _why:_ Matching tick marks usually show that line segments have equal lengths, not that lines are parallel. Parallel lines are identified using matching arrow markings.
- With right-angle squares.
  - _why:_ A small square marks a 90° angle and therefore indicates perpendicular lines. Parallel lines are instead shown using matching arrow markings.
- With matching angle arcs.
  - _why:_ Matching arcs indicate that particular angles are equal, rather than directly marking lines as parallel. Parallel lines are conventionally identified by matching arrows.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Where is the interior region for two parallel lines?

**Back:** The space between the parallel lines.

**Choices:**

- The space between the parallel lines. ✔
  - _why:_ The interior region consists of all the space lying between the two parallel lines, regardless of the angle or position of the transversal.
- The space outside the parallel lines.
  - _why:_ The space outside the two lines is the exterior region. The interior region is the space between the parallel lines.
- The space above both parallel lines.
  - _why:_ The area above both lines is only part of the exterior region. The interior region is the complete space between the parallel lines.
- The space on the transversal line.
  - _why:_ The transversal crosses the parallel lines but does not define the interior region by itself. The interior region is the space between the parallel lines.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What angle rule is shown by an F-shape in parallel lines?

**Back:** Corresponding angles are equal.

**Choices:**

- Corresponding angles are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ An F-shape identifies angles in the same relative position at the two intersections. These are corresponding angles, and they are equal when the lines are parallel.
- Alternate angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Alternate angles are equal, but they are identified by a Z-shape rather than an F-shape. An F-shape shows that corresponding angles are equal.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles occur across from each other at a single intersection. An F-shape spans two intersections and shows that corresponding angles are equal.
- Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  - _why:_ Co-interior angles form a C-shape and sum to 180°. An F-shape instead identifies corresponding angles, which are equal when the lines are parallel.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** Corresponding angles are in the [ ___ ] at each crossing.

**Back:** Corresponding angles are in the same relative position at each crossing.

**Choices:**

- same relative position ✔
  - _why:_ At each intersection, corresponding angles occupy the same position relative to the transversal and the parallel line, such as above-right at both crossings.
- opposite relative position
  - _why:_ Angles in opposite positions at the two intersections are associated with alternate-angle arrangements. Corresponding angles occupy the same relative position at each crossing.
- same interior region
  - _why:_ Merely lying within the interior region does not make two angles corresponding. They must occupy the same relative position at their respective crossings.
- adjacent relative position
  - _why:_ Adjacent positions describe angles next to one another at an intersection, not corresponding angles. Corresponding angles occupy the same relative position at each crossing.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Back:** Alternate angles are on opposite sides of the transversal.

- opposite sides ✔

- parallel sides
  - _why:_ The lines themselves may be parallel, but this does not describe the angles’ positions relative to the transversal. Alternate angles lie on opposite sides of it.
- adjacent sides
  - _why:_ Adjacent angles share a vertex at one intersection, whereas alternate angles occur at different intersections. They lie on opposite sides of the transversal.

  - _why:_ In an isosceles triangle, equal sides have equal opposite angles. Therefore, the two angles opposite the equal sides are equal.

**Back:** $AB$.

**Choices:**

- $AB$. ✔
  - _why:_ In parallelogram $ABCD$, the vertices are named consecutively. This makes $AB$ opposite $CD$, and opposite sides of a parallelogram are parallel.
- $BC$.
  - _why:_ Side $BC$ meets $CD$ at vertex $C$, so the sides are adjacent rather than parallel. The opposite side parallel to $CD$ is $AB$.
- $AD$.
  - _why:_ Side $AD$ meets $CD$ at vertex $D$, so these are adjacent sides. The side opposite and parallel to $CD$ is $AB$.
- $AC$.
  - _why:_ The line $AC$ is a diagonal joining opposite vertices, not a side of the parallelogram. The side parallel to $CD$ is $AB$.

**Front:** Isosceles triangle $EFG$ has $EG=FG$ and $\angle GEF=68°$. What is $\angle EFG$?

**Back:** 68°.

**Choices:**

- 68°. ✔
  - _why:_ Sides $EG$ and $FG$ are equal, so their opposite angles at $F$ and $E$ are equal. Therefore, $angle EFG=angle GEF=68°$.
- 44°.
  - _why:_ This is the remaining angle at $G$, found from $180°-68°-68°=44°$. The requested angle at $F$ equals the given angle at $E$, so it is 68°.
- 112°.
  - _why:_ This is the supplementary angle to 68°, but no straight-line calculation is needed. Equal sides $EG$ and $FG$ give equal opposite angles at $F$ and $E$, both 68°.
- 56°.
  - _why:_ This comes from halving the 112° left after subtracting one 68° angle, overlooking that the equal base angles are already determined. The angle at $F$ is 68°.

## Card 26 — `basic`

**Front:** If $EF \parallel GD$ and $FG$ is a transversal, why can $\angle EFG$ equal $\angle FGD$?

**Back:** They are alternate angles.

**Choices:**

- They are alternate angles. ✔
  - _why:_ When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, alternate interior angles are equal. Here, $FG$ crosses the parallel lines $EF$ and $GD$, so the two angles match.
- They are corresponding angles.
  - _why:_ Corresponding angles occupy matching positions at the two intersections, which these angles do not. They lie between the parallel lines on opposite sides of the transversal, so they are alternate angles.
- They are vertically opposite angles.
  - _why:_ Vertically opposite angles occur across from each other at the same intersection. These angles are at different intersections and are equal because they are alternate angles.
- They are co-interior angles.
  - _why:_ Co-interior angles lie on the same side of a transversal and sum to $180°$ rather than generally being equal. These are alternate angles, so they are equal.

## Card 27 — `basic`

**Front:** In triangle $ABE$, $\angle ABE=54°$ and $\angle AEB=38°$. Find $\angle BAE$.

**Back:** 88°.

**Choices:**

- 88°. ✔
  - _why:_ The interior angles of a triangle sum to $180°$. Therefore, $\angle BAE=180°-54°-38°=88°$.
- 92°.
  - _why:_ This is the sum $54°+38°$, not the remaining angle. Subtracting that sum from the triangle total gives $\angle BAE=180°-92°=88°$.
- 126°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts only the $54°$ angle from $180°$ and overlooks the $38°$ angle. Subtracting both known angles gives $\angle BAE=88°$.
- 142°.
  - _why:_ This subtracts only the $38°$ angle from $180°$ and ignores the $54°$ angle. Both known angles must be subtracted, giving $\angle BAE=88°$.
