## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What line is the starting line for every bearing?

**Back:** The North line.

**Choices:**

- The North line. ✔
  - _why:_ Every bearing starts from the North line at the point where the angle is measured. The angle is then measured clockwise from this line.
- The South line.
  - _why:_ Using South as the starting line confuses bearings with some descriptions of direction. Every bearing begins at the North line and is measured clockwise.
- The East line.
  - _why:_ East corresponds to a bearing of 090°, but it is not the starting line. Bearings always begin from the North line before turning clockwise.
- The West line.
  - _why:_ West corresponds to a bearing of 270°, rather than the starting line. Every bearing is measured clockwise from the North line.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A bearing is measured from [ ___ ] and goes [ ___ ].

**Back:** A bearing is measured from North and goes clockwise.

**Choices:**

- A bearing is measured from North and goes clockwise. ✔
  - _why:_ The standard convention for bearings is to begin at the North line and measure the angle clockwise. This ensures that each direction has one unambiguous bearing.
- A bearing is measured from South and goes clockwise.
  - _why:_ Starting from South uses the wrong reference line. A bearing is measured from North and goes clockwise, regardless of the direction of travel.
- A bearing is measured from North and goes anticlockwise.
  - _why:_ Measuring anticlockwise reverses the standard bearing convention. A bearing begins at North and goes clockwise around the point where the angle is measured.
- A bearing is measured from East and goes anticlockwise.
  - _why:_ This changes both parts of the bearing convention. Bearings are measured from North, not East, and turn clockwise rather than anticlockwise.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In the bearing of B from A, which point is the angle measured at?

**Back:** At A.

**Choices:**

- At A. ✔
  - _why:_ The phrase “B from A” means the journey or direction starts at A. Therefore, the bearing angle is drawn and measured at A.
- At B.
  - _why:_ Choosing B reverses the order of the bearing. In “the bearing of B from A”, A is the starting point, so the angle is measured at A.
- At the midpoint.
  - _why:_ The angle is not measured halfway between the points. The word “from” identifies A as the starting point, so the bearing angle is measured at A.
- At either point.
  - _why:_ Bearings in opposite directions are generally different, so the angle cannot be measured at either point. “B from A” specifically requires measurement at A.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Bearings are written using [ ___ ], so 67° is written as [ ___ ].

**Back:** Bearings are written using three figures, so 67° is written as 067°.

**Choices:**

- Bearings are written using three figures, so 67° is written as 067°. ✔
  - _why:_ Bearings must contain three figures. Since 67 has only two figures, a zero is placed at the front to give the bearing 067°.
- Bearings are written using two figures, so 67° is written as 67°.
  - _why:_ Leaving 67° as two figures ignores the standard notation for bearings. Bearings use three figures, so a leading zero is required and the answer is 067°.
- Bearings are written using three figures, so 67° is written as 670°.
  - _why:_ Appending a zero changes the angle from 67° to 670°, which is not a valid single-turn bearing. Add the zero at the front to obtain 067°.
- Bearings are written using four figures, so 67° is written as 0067°.
  - _why:_ Four-figure notation adds an unnecessary leading zero. Bearings use exactly three figures, so 67° needs only one zero at the front: 067°.

## Card 6 — `basic`

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Back:** If a bearing has fewer than three digits, add leading zeros at the front.

**Choices:**

- If a bearing has fewer than three digits, add leading zeros at the front. ✔
  - _why:_ Bearings are always written using three digits, so zeros are placed before a shorter bearing. For example, a bearing of 45° is written as 045°.
- If a bearing has fewer than three digits, add trailing zeros at the back.
  - _why:_ Adding zeros at the end changes the angle, turning 45° into 450°. The correct convention is to add leading zeros at the front, giving 045°.
- If a bearing has fewer than three digits, add leading zeros at the back.
  - _why:_ Leading zeros belong before the existing digits, not after them. A two-digit bearing such as 45° must therefore be written as 045°.
- If a bearing has fewer than three digits, add trailing zeros at the front.
  - _why:_ Trailing zeros would alter the value of the bearing rather than standardise its notation. Bearings use leading zeros at the front until three digits are shown.

  - _why:_ Since 120 lies between 90 and 180, this bearing points into the quadrant between East and South when measured clockwise from North.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** If a diagram shows a small angle anticlockwise from North, is that angle the bearing?

**Back:** No - bearings go clockwise.

**Choices:**

- No - bearings go clockwise. ✔
  - _why:_ Bearings are measured clockwise from North. An angle marked anticlockwise is therefore not itself the bearing and must be subtracted from 360°.
- Yes - bearings can go anticlockwise.
  - _why:_ The marked angle is measured in the wrong rotational direction. Bearings always go clockwise from North, so an anticlockwise angle must be converted using 360° minus the angle.
- Yes - bearings start from North.
  - _why:_ Bearings do start from North, but they must be measured clockwise rather than anticlockwise. The marked angle is converted by subtracting it from 360°.

  - _why:_ Bearing conventions require clockwise measurement, not whichever rotation is shorter. A small anticlockwise angle is therefore not the bearing; its corresponding bearing is close to 360°.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a marked anticlockwise angle from North, bearing = [ ___ ] - [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a marked anticlockwise angle from North, bearing = 360° - marked angle.

**Choices:**

- For a marked anticlockwise angle from North, bearing = 360° - marked angle. ✔
  - _why:_ A full turn is 360°, so the remaining clockwise angle equals 360° minus the marked anticlockwise angle. This gives the required three-figure bearing.
- For a marked anticlockwise angle from North, bearing = 180° - marked angle.
  - _why:_ Subtracting from 180° treats the diagram as a half-turn and produces the wrong direction. The full angle around a point is 360°, so subtract from 360°.
- For a marked anticlockwise angle from North, bearing = 360° + marked angle.
  - _why:_ Adding the anticlockwise angle to 360° produces an angle greater than one complete turn. The clockwise bearing is the remainder, found using 360° minus the marked angle.
- For a marked anticlockwise angle from North, bearing = marked angle - 360°.
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction gives a negative value for any ordinary marked angle below 360°. The positive clockwise bearing is 360° minus the marked angle.
