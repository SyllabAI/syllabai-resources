## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the term substitution mean in algebra?

**Back:** Replacing variables (letters) with numbers to calculate a value.

**Choices:**

- Replacing variables (letters) with numbers to calculate a value. ✔
  - _why:_ Substitution replaces each algebraic variable with its given numerical value. The resulting expression can then be evaluated using the usual order of operations.
- Replacing numbers with variables (letters) to create an expression.
  - _why:_ This reverses the substitution process by replacing numbers with letters. Substitution means replacing variables with given numbers so that the value of an expression can be calculated.
- Combining variables (letters) with numbers to simplify an expression.
  - _why:_ Combining like terms is a form of algebraic simplification, not substitution. Substitution specifically involves replacing variables with given numerical values and then calculating the result.
- Rearranging variables (letters) and numbers to change a formula.
  - _why:_ Rearranging a formula changes which variable is the subject; it does not substitute values. Substitution replaces variables with given numbers to calculate a value.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What does an expression like $7m$ mean in terms of operation?

**Back:** $$7 \times m$

**Choices:**

- $$7 \times m$ ✔
  - _why:_ A number written directly beside a variable is a coefficient multiplying that variable. Therefore, $7m$ means that $m$ is multiplied by $7$.
- $$7 + m$
  - _why:_ Writing a number beside a variable does not indicate addition. An addition sign would need to be shown explicitly; $7m$ means $7 \times m$.
- $$7 - m$
  - _why:_ Adjacent algebraic terms indicate multiplication, not subtraction. A subtraction sign would have to be written explicitly, so $7m$ means $7 \times m$.
- $$m \div 7$
  - _why:_ Placing $7$ before $m$ makes it the coefficient of $m$, not a divisor. The notation $7m$ represents $7 \times m$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What does it mean when two variables touch, such as $jk$?

**Back:** They are multiplied together ($j \times k$).

**Choices:**

- They are multiplied together ($j \times k$). ✔
  - _why:_ In algebra, variables written next to one another are multiplied. The multiplication sign is normally omitted, so $jk$ is shorthand for $j \times k$.
- They are added together ($j + k$).
  - _why:_ Touching variables indicate multiplication rather than addition. Addition must include a plus sign, while $jk$ is the shortened algebraic notation for $j \times k$.
- They are subtracted together ($j - k$).
  - _why:_ Subtraction requires an explicit minus sign between the variables. When $j$ and $k$ touch, the omitted operation is multiplication, giving $j \times k$.
- They are divided together ($j \div k$).
  - _why:_ Division would normally be shown using a fraction bar or division sign. Variables written directly beside each other are multiplied, so $jk$ means $j \times k$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between an expression and a formula?

**Back:** Expression: a calculation with numbers and variables (e.g., $4x + 5y$).Formula: a rule connecting variables (e.g., $A = lw$).

**Choices:**

- Expression: a calculation with numbers and variables (e.g., $4x + 5y$).Formula: a rule connecting variables (e.g., $A = lw$). ✔
  - _why:_ An expression combines terms and operations without stating an equality. A formula uses an equals sign to show a general relationship or rule connecting variables.
- Expression: a rule connecting variables (e.g., $A = lw$).Formula: a calculation with numbers and variables (e.g., $4x + 5y$).
  - _why:_ This swaps the two definitions. An expression is a calculation involving numbers and variables, while a formula is a general rule showing how variables are connected.
- Expression: a calculation with an equals sign (e.g., $4x = 5y$).Formula: a calculation without an equals sign (e.g., $A + lw$).
  - _why:_ This reverses the role of the equals sign. An expression has no equality, while a formula uses an equals sign to state a rule connecting variables.
- Expression: a statement that two values are equal (e.g., $4x = 20$).Formula: a list of separate algebraic terms (e.g., $A$, $l$, $w$).
  - _why:_ A statement that two values are equal is an equation, and a list of terms is not a formula. An expression is a calculation; a formula connects variables by a rule.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** In the BIDMAS order of operations, what do Brackets and Indices represent?

**Back:** Brackets: groups of calculations to do first.Indices: powers (e.g., $x^2$).

**Choices:**

- Brackets: groups of calculations to do first.Indices: powers (e.g., $x^2$). ✔
  - _why:_ BIDMAS starts by evaluating grouped calculations inside brackets, followed by indices such as squares, cubes and other powers, before division, multiplication, addition and subtraction.
- Brackets: powers (e.g., $x^2$).Indices: groups of calculations to do first.
  - _why:_ This swaps the two BIDMAS terms. Brackets group calculations that are handled first, while indices are powers such as squares and cubes.
- Brackets: groups of calculations to do last.Indices: numbers multiplying variables (e.g., $2x$).
  - _why:_ Brackets are evaluated first, not last, and a number multiplying a variable is a coefficient. Indices are powers, such as the $2$ in $x^2$.
- Brackets: multiplication signs hidden between terms.Indices: numbers added to variables (e.g., $x + 2$).
  - _why:_ Neither definition matches BIDMAS. Brackets group calculations to be completed first, while indices describe powers, such as squaring in $x^2$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** When substituting negative numbers, the safest method is to put the values in [ ___ ].

**Back:** When substituting negative numbers, the safest method is to put the values in brackets.

**Choices:**

- brackets ✔
  - _why:_ Brackets keep the negative sign attached to the substituted value. This prevents sign errors, especially when the value is multiplied, squared or subtracted.
- minus signs
  - _why:_ Adding extra minus signs can change the value rather than protect it. Negative substituted values should be enclosed in brackets so their signs remain attached correctly.
- fractions
  - _why:_ Writing every negative value as a fraction does not prevent sign errors. Enclosing the whole substituted value in brackets clearly preserves its negative sign.
- indices
  - _why:_ Indices represent powers and would change the mathematical operation being performed. Negative substituted values should instead be placed in brackets to preserve their signs.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Back:** A positive number (25).

**Choices:**

- A positive number (25). ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying two negative factors gives a positive product, so $(-5)^2=(-5)\times(-5)=25$. The brackets ensure the negative sign is included in the squaring.
- A negative number (-25).
  - _why:_ This treats the square as applying only to 5 while leaving the negative sign outside. Because the whole number is bracketed, $(-5)^2=25$, a positive number.
- A positive number (10).
  - _why:_ This doubles 5 instead of squaring it. Squaring means multiplying the number by itself, so $(-5)\times(-5)=25$, which is positive.
- A negative number (-10).
  - _why:_ This both doubles the value instead of squaring it and incorrectly retains the negative sign. Squaring $-5$ gives $(-5)\times(-5)=25$.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Back:** The fraction line in a formula means divide.

**Choices:**

- divide ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction line represents division: the expression above the line is divided by the expression below it. For example, $\frac{6}{3}$ means $6\div3$.
- multiply

- subtract
  - _why:_ Subtraction is represented by a minus sign, not a fraction line. The fraction line instructs you to divide the expression above it by the expression below it.
- add
  - _why:_ Addition is represented by a plus sign, whereas a fraction line represents division. The numerator must be divided by the denominator.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate a formula containing a large fraction?

**Back:** Work out the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) separately before dividing.

**Choices:**

- Work out the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) separately before dividing. ✔
  - _why:_ Evaluating the complete numerator and denominator first preserves their intended grouping. Once both values are known, divide the numerator’s value by the denominator’s value.
- Work out the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) separately before multiplying.
  - _why:_ A fraction line represents division rather than multiplication. Calculate the entire numerator and denominator separately, then divide the numerator’s value by the denominator’s value.
- Divide each term in the top (numerator) by each term in the bottom (denominator) separately.
  - _why:_ Dividing terms individually across both parts can change the value and ignores the grouping created by the fraction line. Evaluate each whole part separately before dividing.
- Add the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) together before dividing.
  - _why:_ Adding the numerator and denominator does not evaluate a fraction. Work out each complete expression separately, then divide the numerator’s result by the denominator’s result.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** Subtracting a negative number, such as in $-15 - (-8)$, is the same as [ ___ ].

**Back:** Subtracting a negative number, such as in $-15 - (-8)$, is the same as adding.

**Choices:**

- adding ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting a quantity means adding its additive inverse. The inverse of $-8$ is $+8$, so $-15-(-8)=-15+8=-7$.
- subtracting
  - _why:_ Keeping the operation as subtraction overlooks the two adjacent negative signs. Subtracting $-8$ changes to adding $8$, so the correct operation is adding.
- multiplying
  - _why:_ Two negative signs change subtraction into addition; they do not indicate multiplication here. Thus $-15-(-8)$ becomes $-15+8$, which equals $-7$.
- dividing
  - _why:_ The negative signs do not create a division operation. Subtracting a negative reverses the operation to addition, making the expression $-15+8$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it important to use brackets when substituting a negative value into $b^2$?

**Back:** To ensure the entire negative number is squared, resulting in a positive value.

**Choices:**

- To ensure the entire negative number is squared, resulting in a positive value. ✔
  - _why:_ Writing $(-5)^2$ makes the base of the power the entire number $-5$. Multiplying $(-5)$ by itself then gives the positive result 25.
- To ensure the exponent is applied before substitution, resulting in a negative value.
  - _why:_ The exponent is applied after substitution whether or not the value is positive. Brackets specifically make the negative sign part of the squared base, producing a positive value.
- To ensure the negative sign stays outside the square, resulting in a negative value.
  - _why:_ Leaving the negative sign outside would give $-5^2=-(5^2)=-25$. Brackets instead include the negative sign in the base, so $(-5)^2=25$.
- To ensure the coefficient and negative value are squared, resulting in a positive value.
  - _why:_ There is no coefficient in $b^2$ to be squared. The brackets are needed so the complete substituted value, including its negative sign, is squared.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** In the calculation $2(a + b)$, which part must be calculated first?

**Back:** The addition inside the brackets $(a + b)$.

**Choices:**

- The addition inside the brackets $(a + b)$. ✔
  - _why:_ Brackets take priority in the order of operations. The values of $a$ and $b$ must therefore be added before their sum is multiplied by $2$.
- The multiplication outside the brackets $2(a + b)$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by $2$ first ignores the priority given to brackets. The addition $a+b$ must be evaluated first, and its result is then multiplied by $2$.
- The term $a$ before the term $b$.
  - _why:_ The terms $a$ and $b$ do not have separate priority simply because one appears first. The required first operation is the addition inside the brackets.
- The larger value among $a$ and $b$.
  - _why:_ Operation order does not depend on which substituted value is larger. The brackets determine the priority, so $a+b$ must be calculated before multiplying by $2$.
