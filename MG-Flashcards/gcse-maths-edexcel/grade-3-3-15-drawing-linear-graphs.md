## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the coordinate pair $(x, y)$, what movement does the first number represent?

**Back:** Movement along the horizontal $x$-axis.

**Choices:**

- Movement along the horizontal $x$-axis. ✔
  - _why:_ The first coordinate gives the horizontal position, so it shows how far to move left or right along the $x$-axis.
- Movement up or down on the vertical $y$-axis.
  - _why:_ This confuses the two coordinates. The first number controls horizontal movement along the $x$-axis, while the second controls vertical movement.
- Movement along the horizontal $y$-axis.
  - _why:_ Although the first coordinate represents horizontal movement, the horizontal axis is the $x$-axis, not the $y$-axis.
- Movement up or down on the vertical $x$-axis.
  - _why:_ This combines the wrong direction with the wrong orientation. The first coordinate represents left or right movement along the horizontal $x$-axis.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the name of the point $(0,0)$ where the axes cross?

**Back:** The origin.

**Choices:**

- The origin. ✔
  - _why:_ The origin is the point where the horizontal and vertical axes intersect. Its coordinates are $(0,0)$ because both coordinate values are zero.
- The vertex.
  - _why:_ A vertex is a corner or turning point of a shape or graph. The point $(0,0)$ where the coordinate axes cross is called the origin.
- The midpoint.
  - _why:_ A midpoint lies halfway between two specified points. The fixed point $(0,0)$ where the two coordinate axes cross is called the origin.
- The quadrant.
  - _why:_ A quadrant is one of the four regions formed by the axes, not the point where they meet. That point is the origin.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** In an equation like $y=3x-1$, what does it mean to substitute $x=2$?

**Back:** Replace $x$ with 2 and calculate the resulting $y$ value.

**Choices:**

- Replace $x$ with 2 and calculate the resulting $y$ value. ✔
  - _why:_ Substitution means replacing a variable with its given value. Putting $x=2$ into the equation gives $y=3(2)-1$, which can then be calculated.
- Replace $y$ with 2 and calculate the resulting $x$ value.
  - _why:_ This treats the given value as a replacement for $y$. Since $x=2$ is specified, replace $x$ with 2 and calculate $y$.
- Replace $x$ with -2 and calculate the resulting $y$ value.
  - _why:_ This common sign error substitutes the opposite value. The statement $x=2$ requires replacing $x$ with positive 2 before calculating $y$.
- Replace $x$ with $2x$ and calculate the resulting $y$ value.
  - _why:_ Substitution replaces the variable with the stated number; it does not multiply the variable by that number. Replace $x$ with 2 and then calculate $y$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a linear equation $y=mx+c$, when $x=0$, the $y$-value is always the [ ___ ] (the constant).

**Back:** In a linear equation $y=mx+c$, when $x=0$, the $y$-value is always the number on its own (the constant).

**Choices:**

- number on its own ✔
  - _why:_ When $x=0$, the term $mx$ becomes zero, leaving $y=c$. Therefore, the $y$-value is the number written on its own.
- number multiplying $x$
  - _why:_ The number multiplying $x$ is the gradient $m$, not the constant. Setting $x=0$ removes the entire $mx$ term, leaving the number on its own.
- power of $x$
  - _why:_ The power of $x$ describes the variable’s exponent rather than the constant. When $x=0$, the remaining $y$-value is the number on its own.
- value of $x$
  - _why:_ The value of $x$ has been set to zero, but the resulting $y$-value is $c$. This is represented by the number on its own.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the defining characteristic of a linear graph?

**Back:** It forms a straight line.

**Choices:**

- It forms a straight line. ✔
  - _why:_ A linear relationship has a constant rate of change, so all its plotted points lie on one straight line rather than forming a curve.
- It forms a smooth curve.
  - _why:_ A smooth curve indicates that the rate of change is varying, as in a quadratic graph. A linear graph instead forms a straight line.
- It forms a complete circle.
  - _why:_ A circular graph does not represent a linear relationship and cannot generally be written in the form $y=mx+c$. A linear graph forms a straight line.
- It forms separate points.
  - _why:_ Isolated points do not by themselves form the continuous graph of a linear equation. The plotted points should be joined to form a straight line.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you tell from an equation (e.g. $y=2x+1$) that the graph will be linear?

**Back:** The $x$ variable is not squared or cubed.

**Choices:**

- The $x$ variable is not squared or cubed. ✔
  - _why:_ In a linear equation, $x$ has power 1 rather than higher powers such as $x^2$ or $x^3$. Its graph therefore has a constant gradient.
- The $x$ variable is always squared or cubed.
  - _why:_ Squared or cubed terms produce non-linear graphs, such as parabolas or cubic curves. A linear equation has no squared or cubed $x$ terms.
- The $x$ variable is in the denominator.
  - _why:_ Putting $x$ in a denominator creates a reciprocal-type relationship, whose graph is curved and discontinuous. In a linear equation, $x$ is not squared or cubed.
- The $x$ variable is multiplied by $y$.
  - _why:_ A product such as $xy$ generally produces a non-linear relationship between the variables. A linear equation instead has $x$ only to the first power.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the three main steps to draw a linear graph from an equation?

**Back:** Complete a table of values.Plot the coordinate points.Join the points with a straight line (using a ruler).

**Choices:**

- Complete a table of values.Plot the coordinate points.Join the points with a straight line (using a ruler). ✔
  - _why:_ A table supplies several coordinate pairs, which are plotted accurately. Since the equation is linear, a ruler is then used to join them with one straight line.
- Complete a table of values.Plot the coordinate points.Join the points with a smooth curve (freehand).
  - _why:_ A smooth curve is inappropriate because a linear equation has a constant gradient. After completing the table and plotting the points, join them with a straight line.
- Complete a table of values.Swap the $x$- and $y$-coordinates when plotting.Join the points with a straight line (using a ruler).
  - _why:_ Swapping each coordinate reverses the roles of $x$ and $y$, placing points incorrectly. Plot each pair as $(x,y)$ before joining the points with a straight line.
- Choose just one coordinate.Plot that coordinate point.Draw any straight line through it (using a ruler).
  - _why:_ One point is insufficient because infinitely many straight lines can pass through it. Calculate and plot several coordinate points, then use a ruler to join them.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Back:** If points plotted for a linear equation do not form a straight line, there is a mistake in the table calculation or the plotting.

**Choices:**

- straight line ✔
  - _why:_ A linear equation has a constant rate of change, so all correctly calculated and plotted points lie on one straight line.
- curved line
  - _why:_ A curve suggests a changing rate of change, which is not characteristic of a linear equation. The points should form a straight line if the table and plotting are correct.
- horizontal line
  - _why:_ Only equations with zero gradient produce horizontal lines. A general linear equation can have a non-zero gradient, so correctly plotted points should form a straight line.
- vertical line
  - _why:_ A vertical line does not represent a function of $x$ in the usual form $y=mx+c$. Correctly plotted points from a linear equation form a straight line.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For the equation $y=2-3x$, calculate the value of $y$ when $x=-2$.

**Back:** $$y=8$ (because $-3 \times -2 = 6$, and $2+6=8$).

**Choices:**

- $$y=8$ (because $-3 \times -2 = 6$, and $2+6=8$). ✔
  - _why:_ Substituting $x=-2$ gives $y=2-3(-2)=2+6=8$. The product of two negative numbers is positive, so the final value is 8.
- $$y=-4$ (because $-3 \times -2 = -6$, and $2-6=-4$).
  - _why:_ This treats the product of two negative numbers as negative. Their product is positive, so $y=2+6=8$, not $-4$.
- $$y=4$ (because $3 \times -2 = -6$, and $2-(-6)=4$).
  - _why:_ The arithmetic shown does not follow the equation, and $2-(-6)$ equals 8 rather than 4. Substitution gives $2-3(-2)=2+6=8$.
- $$y=-8$ (because $2-3=-1$, and $-1 \times -2=-8$).
  - _why:_ The constant and coefficient must be used as $2-3x$, not combined before multiplying by $x$. Substituting directly gives $2-3(-2)=8$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a coefficient?

**Back:** The number multiplying a variable (e.g. -3 in $-3x$).

**Choices:**

- The number multiplying a variable (e.g. -3 in $-3x$). ✔
  - _why:_ A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable. In $-3x$, the coefficient of $x$ is $-3$.
- The number being added to a variable (e.g. -3 in $-3x$).
  - _why:_ The number in $-3x$ is multiplying $x$, not being added to it. A coefficient is the number multiplying the variable, so the coefficient is $-3$.
- The number dividing a variable (e.g. -3 in $-3x$).
  - _why:_ Division would be shown with a fraction or division symbol, whereas $-3x$ means $-3$ multiplied by $x$. The coefficient is the number multiplying the variable.
- The variable multiplying a number (e.g. -3 in $-3x$).
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles in the definition and incorrectly calls the variable the coefficient. In $-3x$, the numerical factor $-3$ is the coefficient.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Back:** When plotting fractional $y$-values (e.g. 1.5), the points should be marked halfway between the grid lines.

**Choices:**

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the steps to find the $y$-value for a specific $x$ using a drawn graph.

**Back:** Find the $x$ value on the horizontal axis.Move vertically to the line.Move horizontally to read the value on the $y$-axis.

**Choices:**

- Find the $x$ value on the horizontal axis.Move vertically to the line.Move horizontally to read the value on the $y$-axis. ✔
  - _why:_ The given $x$-value is read on the horizontal axis, then projected vertically to the graph. From the line, move horizontally to the vertical axis to read $y$.
- Find the $x$ value on the vertical axis.Move horizontally to the line.Move vertically to read the value on the $y$-axis.
  - _why:_ The $x$-value belongs on the horizontal axis, and the final horizontal movement reads the $y$-value. Reversing both axes and movements gives the wrong coordinate.
- Find the $y$ value on the horizontal axis.Move vertically to the line.Move horizontally to read the value on the $x$-axis.
  - _why:_ The starting value is $x$, so it must be located on the horizontal axis. The endpoint is $y$, which is read from the vertical axis after moving horizontally from the line.
- Find the $x$ value on the horizontal axis.Move horizontally to the line.Move vertically to read the value on the $y$-axis.
  - _why:_ Starting on the horizontal axis is correct, but reaching the line requires a vertical movement. Moving horizontally first follows a constant $y$-value instead of the chosen $x$-value.

**Choices:**

- Subtract $x$ from both sides to get $y=5-x$. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting $x$ from both sides gives $y=5-x$. This isolates $y$, making it straightforward to calculate values and draw the graph.
- Add $x$ to both sides to get $y=5+x$.
  - _why:_ Adding $x$ does not isolate $y$ correctly from $x+y=5$. Subtracting $x$ from both sides gives $y=5-x$.

  - _why:_ Subtracting 5 produces $x+y-5=0$, not the displayed rearrangement. To make $y$ the subject, subtract $x$ from both sides and obtain $y=5-x$.
- Subtract $y$ from both sides to get $x=5-y$.
  - _why:_ This correctly rearranges the equation for $x$, but the question asks for a form with $y$ as the subject. Subtracting $x$ gives $y=5-x$.
