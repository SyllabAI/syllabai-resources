## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** 1 cm = [ ___ ] and 1 m = [ ___ ].

**Back:** 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 100 cm.

**Choices:**

- 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 100 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ There are 10 millimetres in each centimetre and 100 centimetres in each metre, as both conversions move from a larger metric unit to a smaller one.
- 1 cm = 100 mm and 1 m = 100 cm.
  - _why:_ The centimetre-to-millimetre conversion has an extra factor of 10. Since 1 cm equals 10 mm, the correct conversions are 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 100 cm.
- 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 1000 cm.
  - _why:_ The metre-to-centimetre conversion has an extra zero. A metre contains 100 centimetres, so the correct conversions are 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 100 cm.
- 1 cm = 100 mm and 1 m = 1000 cm.
  - _why:_ Both conversions incorrectly multiply by 10 too much. The metric relationships are 1 cm = 10 mm and 1 m = 100 cm.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** 1 km = [ ___ ] and 1 hour = [ ___ ].

**Back:** 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 3600 seconds.

**Choices:**

- 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 3600 seconds. ✔
  - _why:_ The prefix kilo- means 1000, so 1 km is 1000 m. One hour contains 60 minutes of 60 seconds each, giving 3600 seconds.
- 1 km = 100 m and 1 hour = 3600 seconds.
  - _why:_ The kilometre conversion is missing a factor of 10 because kilo- means 1000. The correct relationships are 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 3600 seconds.
- 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 60 seconds.
  - _why:_ This confuses the seconds in one minute with the seconds in one hour. Multiplying 60 minutes by 60 seconds gives 3600 seconds, while 1 km equals 1000 m.
- 1 km = 10000 m and 1 hour = 600 seconds.
  - _why:_ Both values result from place-value errors. Kilo- represents 1000, and an hour contains 60 times 60 seconds, so the correct values are 1000 m and 3600 seconds.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** When converting to a smaller unit, which operation is usually used?

**Back:** Usually multiply.

**Choices:**

- Usually multiply. ✔
  - _why:_ A smaller unit fits into the same measurement more times, so the numerical value usually increases. For example, metres are converted to centimetres by multiplying by 100.
- Usually divide.
  - _why:_ Dividing is the usual operation when converting to a larger unit. Converting to a smaller unit increases the numerical value, so multiplication is usually required.
- Usually add.
  - _why:_ Unit conversions use scale factors rather than adding a fixed amount. Converting to a smaller unit usually requires multiplication because more of the smaller units represent the same quantity.
- Usually subtract.
  - _why:_ Subtracting a fixed amount does not preserve equivalent measurements across different units. Converting to a smaller unit usually means multiplying by the relevant conversion factor.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Back:** Usually divide.

**Choices:**

- Usually divide. ✔
  - _why:_ A larger unit fits into the same measurement fewer times, so the numerical value usually decreases. For example, centimetres are converted to metres by dividing by 100.
- Usually multiply.
  - _why:_ Multiplication is usually used when converting to a smaller unit, where the numerical value increases. Converting to a larger unit generally requires division by the conversion factor.
- Usually add.
  - _why:_ Adding a fixed amount is not how equivalent units are related. To convert to a larger unit, divide by the appropriate conversion factor so the numerical value decreases.
- Usually subtract.
  - _why:_ Subtracting does not account for the scale relationship between units. Converting to a larger unit usually requires division, such as dividing centimetres by 100 to obtain metres.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Convert 3.7 m to cm.

**Back:** 3.7 m = 370 cm.

**Choices:**

- 3.7 m = 370 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ Each metre contains 100 centimetres, so converting 3.7 m to centimetres requires multiplying by 100. This moves the decimal point two places right to give 370 cm.
- 3.7 m = 37 cm.
  - _why:_ This multiplies by 10 instead of 100, treating metres as though they contained only 10 centimetres. Since 1 m = 100 cm, 3.7 m equals 370 cm.
- 3.7 m = 0.037 cm.
  - _why:_ This divides by 100, reversing the required operation. Centimetres are smaller than metres, so the number must increase: multiplying 3.7 by 100 gives 370 cm.
- 3.7 m = 3700 cm.
  - _why:_ This multiplies by 1000 and introduces an extra zero. As 1 m contains 100 cm, multiplying 3.7 by 100 gives the correct value of 370 cm.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** On a conversion graph, if given an x-axis value, what is the first move to the line?

**Back:** Move vertically up to the line.

**Choices:**

- Move vertically up to the line. ✔
  - _why:_ An x-axis value fixes the horizontal coordinate. From that point, travel vertically upward until reaching the conversion line, then move horizontally to read the corresponding y-axis value.
- Move horizontally across to the line.
  - _why:_ Horizontal movement is used first when starting from a y-axis value. From a given x-axis value, move vertically up to the conversion line.
- Move vertically down to the line.
  - _why:_ On a typical first-quadrant conversion graph, the line lies above the x-axis. Starting at the given x-axis value, move vertically up rather than down to meet it.
- Move diagonally up to the line.
  - _why:_ Diagonal movement changes both coordinates at once and does not preserve the given x-value. Move vertically up from the x-axis so the horizontal coordinate remains fixed.

**Back:** About 21 pints.

  - _why:_ The conversion is directly proportional: $12 \times \frac{35}{20}=21$. Therefore, 12 litres is approximately 21 pints.

  - _why:_ Subtracting 8 pints because the volume falls by 8 litres incorrectly assumes equal numerical units. Using direct proportion gives $12 \times \frac{35}{20}=21$ pints.
- About 14 pints.
  - _why:_ This underestimates the result by using an incorrect conversion rate. Since each litre is approximately $\frac{35}{20}$ pints, 12 litres is about 21 pints.

  - _why:_ This doubles the required result, as though 12 litres were 24 litres. Applying the graph’s conversion ratio gives $12 \times \frac{35}{20}=21$ pints.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** With direct proportion, doubling one measurement [ ___ ] the other.

**Back:** With direct proportion, doubling one measurement doubles the other.

**Choices:**

- doubles ✔
  - _why:_ In direct proportion, both quantities change by the same scale factor. Multiplying one measurement by 2 therefore multiplies the other measurement by 2.
- halves
  - _why:_ Halving the other quantity describes an inverse relationship, not direct proportion. With direct proportion, doubling one measurement doubles the other.
- quadruples
  - _why:_ Quadrupling would incorrectly apply the doubling factor twice. In direct proportion, both quantities use the same scale factor, so doubling one doubles the other.
- triples
  - _why:_ Tripling uses a scale factor of 3 rather than matching the given factor of 2. Direct proportion means doubling one measurement doubles the other.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** If 10 inches ≈ 25.4 cm, estimate 5 inches in cm.

**Back:** 5 inches ≈ 12.7 cm.

**Choices:**

- 5 inches ≈ 12.7 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ Five inches is half of 10 inches, so the corresponding length is half of 25.4 cm. Therefore, $25.4 \div 2=12.7$ cm.
- 5 inches ≈ 25.4 cm.
  - _why:_ This keeps the centimetre measurement unchanged even though the number of inches has halved. Halving 25.4 cm gives the correct estimate of 12.7 cm.
- 5 inches ≈ 50.8 cm.
  - _why:_ This doubles 25.4 cm rather than halving it. Since 5 inches is half of 10 inches, the corresponding measurement is 12.7 cm.
- 5 inches ≈ 127 cm.
  - _why:_ This decimal-place error makes the result ten times too large. Five inches is half of 10 inches, so it equals approximately 12.7 cm.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** For area conversions, [ ___ ] the length conversion factor.

**Back:** For area conversions, square the length conversion factor.

**Choices:**

- square ✔
  - _why:_ Area has two dimensions, so the length conversion factor is applied twice. For example, converting centimetres to millimetres uses $10^2=100$ for square units.
- double
  - _why:_ Doubling the conversion factor confuses multiplication by 2 with two-dimensional scaling. Area conversions require the length conversion factor to be squared.
- cube
  - _why:_ Cubing the conversion factor is required for three-dimensional volume, not two-dimensional area. For area conversions, the length conversion factor must be squared.
- halve
  - _why:_ Halving does not account for the two perpendicular lengths that form an area. The length conversion factor must be squared for area conversions.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If 1 cm = 10 mm, what is 1 cm² in mm²?

**Back:** 1 cm² = 100 mm².

**Choices:**

- 1 cm² = 100 mm². ✔
  - _why:_ Because $1\text{ cm}=10\text{ mm}$, both dimensions are multiplied by 10. Therefore, $1\text{ cm}^2=10^2\text{ mm}^2=100\text{ mm}^2$.
- 1 cm² = 10 mm².
  - _why:_ This applies the linear conversion factor only once. Area is two-dimensional, so the factor must be squared: $10^2=100$, giving 100 mm².
- 1 cm² = 20 mm².
  - _why:_ Multiplying the length factor by 2 does not convert an area. The factor of 10 must be squared, so 1 cm² equals 100 mm².
- 1 cm² = 1000 mm².
  - _why:_ This cubes the factor of 10, which is appropriate for volume rather than area. Squaring the factor gives $10^2=100$ mm².
