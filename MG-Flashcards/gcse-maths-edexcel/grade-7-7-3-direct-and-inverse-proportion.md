## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the symbol $\propto$ represent in a formula?

**Back:** Is proportional to

**Choices:**

- Is proportional to ✔
  - _why:_ The symbol indicates a proportional relationship between two quantities. Their values are connected by a constant multiplier called the constant of proportionality.
- Is equal to
  - _why:_ An equals sign, $=$, represents equality. The symbol $\propto$ instead means that one quantity is proportional to another through a constant multiplier.
- Is approximately equal to
  - _why:_ Approximate equality is represented by $\approx$, not $\propto$. The symbol $\propto$ states that two quantities are related by a constant proportion.
- Is not equal to
  - _why:_ Inequality is represented by symbols such as $\ne$, $$. The symbol $\propto$ means “is proportional to” and describes a scaling relationship.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the name of the constant $k$ in proportion equations?

**Back:** The constant of proportionality

**Choices:**

- The constant of proportionality ✔
  - _why:_ The value $k$ is called the constant of proportionality because it remains fixed and converts the proportional relationship into an equation.
- The coefficient of proportionality
  - _why:_ A coefficient is a broader term for a numerical multiplier, but in a proportion equation $k$ is specifically called the constant of proportionality.
- The gradient of proportionality
  - _why:_ The gradient describes the slope of a graph and only coincides with $k$ in some direct-proportion graphs. The general name for $k$ is the constant of proportionality.
- The variable of proportionality
  - _why:_ A variable can take different values, whereas $k$ remains fixed for a given proportional relationship. It is therefore the constant of proportionality.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Write the formula for $A$ is directly proportional to the square of $B$.

**Back:** $$A = kB^2$

**Choices:**

- $$A = kB^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Direct proportionality to the square of $B$ means $A\propto B^2$. Introducing the constant of proportionality $k$ gives $A=kB^2$.
- $$A = kB$
  - _why:_ This represents direct proportionality to $B$, not to its square. Squaring the stated variable gives the correct formula $A=kB^2$.
- $$A = \frac{k}{B^2}$
  - _why:_ Placing $B^2$ in the denominator creates inverse proportionality to the square of $B$. Direct proportionality requires $A=kB^2$.
- $$A = kB^3$
  - _why:_ Cubing $B$ uses the wrong power and would make $A$ proportional to the cube of $B$. The stated square relationship is $A=kB^2$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Write the formula for $P$ is inversely proportional to the cube of $Q$.

**Back:** $$P = \frac{k}{Q^3}$

**Choices:**

- $$P = \frac{k}{Q^3}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Inverse proportionality to the cube of $Q$ means $P\propto 1/Q^3$. Introducing the constant $k$ gives $P=\frac{k}{Q^3}$.
- $$P = \frac{k}{Q}$
  - _why:_ This omits the cube and represents inverse proportionality to $Q$ itself. Inverse proportionality to the cube requires $P=\frac{k}{Q^3}$.
- $$P = kQ^3$
  - _why:_ Multiplying by $Q^3$ describes direct proportionality to the cube, reversing the stated relationship. For inverse proportionality, the cube belongs in the denominator.
- $$P = \frac{k}{Q^2}$
  - _why:_ This uses a square rather than the stated cube, so the power is incorrect. The required inverse-cube formula is $P=\frac{k}{Q^3}$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $y$ is directly proportional to $x$, as $x$ increases, $y$ [ ___ ] by the same scale factor.

**Back:** If $y$ is directly proportional to $x$, as $x$ increases, $y$ increases by the same scale factor.

**Choices:**

- increases ✔
  - _why:_ In direct proportion, $y=kx$, so multiplying $x$ by a scale factor multiplies $y$ by exactly the same factor. Therefore, as $x$ increases, $y$ increases.
- decreases
  - _why:_ Decreasing $y$ as $x$ increases describes an inverse relationship. In direct proportion, both quantities change in the same direction and by the same scale factor.
- stagnates
  - _why:_ A constant $y$ would mean it does not depend on $x$. In direct proportion, increasing $x$ increases $y$ by the same scale factor.
- fluctuates
  - _why:_ Direct proportion gives a consistent same-direction change, not repeated movement up and down. Since $y=kx$, increasing $x$ makes $y$ increase by the same scale factor.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the three main steps to solve most proportion problems?

**Back:** Write the formula with $k$Substitute values to find $k$Substitute the new value and solve

**Choices:**

- Write the formula with $k$Substitute values to find $k$Substitute the new value and solve ✔
  - _why:_ A proportion problem is solved by forming the equation with $k$, using known paired values to calculate $k$, and then substituting the new value to find the unknown.
- Write the formula without $k$Substitute values to remove $k$Substitute the new value and estimate
  - _why:_ Eliminating $k$ before using known values loses the constant that defines the relationship. First write the formula, then find $k$, and finally substitute and solve.
- Write the formula with $k$Substitute the new value to find $k$Substitute known values and solve
  - _why:_ The new value cannot normally determine $k$ because its paired unknown is not yet known. Use the known pair to find $k$ before substituting the new value.
- Write the formula with $k=1$Substitute values to check $k$Substitute the new value and solve
  - _why:_ Assuming $k=1$ confuses proportionality with equality and ignores the known data. Calculate $k$ from the given pair, then use it with the new value.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** In the inverse proportion formula $y = \frac{k}{x}$, what calculation involving $x$ and $y$ always equals $k$?

**Back:** The product $xy$ (or $x \times y$)

**Choices:**

- The product $xy$ (or $x \times y$) ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying $y=\frac{k}{x}$ by $x$ gives $xy=k$. Therefore, the product of corresponding $x$ and $y$ values remains constant.
- The sum $x+y$ (or $x + y$)
  - _why:_ The sum is not fixed in an inverse relationship and changes as $x$ and $y$ change. Rearranging $y=\frac{k}{x}$ shows that the constant product is $xy=k$.
- The quotient $x/y$ (or $x \div y$)
  - _why:_ The quotient is generally not constant because dividing $x$ by $y$ produces a changing value. Multiplying the equation by $x$ gives the constant product $xy=k$.
- The difference $x-y$ (or $x - y$)
  - _why:_ The difference between inversely proportional quantities does not stay fixed. From $y=\frac{k}{x}$, multiplication by $x$ shows that their product $xy$ equals $k$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$, as $x$ increases, $y$ [ ___ ].

**Back:** If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$, as $x$ increases, $y$ decreases.

**Choices:**

- decreases ✔
  - _why:_ For inverse proportion, $y=\frac{k}{x}$. As a positive value of $x$ increases, the denominator grows, so the corresponding value of $y$ decreases.
- increases
  - _why:_ Increasing alongside $x$ describes direct rather than inverse proportion. In $y=\frac{k}{x}$, a larger positive denominator produces a smaller value of $y$.
- stagnates
  - _why:_ A constant $y$ would not respond to changes in $x$. In inverse proportion, $xy=k$, so increasing positive $x$ forces $y$ to decrease.
- doubles
  - _why:_ Doubling occurs only under particular direct-proportion changes, not whenever $x$ increases. In inverse proportion, increasing positive $x$ makes $y$ decrease.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the two required features of a direct proportion graph ($y \propto x$).

**Back:** Straight linePasses through the origin (0,0)

**Choices:**

- Straight linePasses through the origin (0,0) ✔
  - _why:_ Direct proportion has the form $y = kx$, which produces a straight line. Substituting $x = 0$ gives $y = 0$, so the line passes through the origin.
- Straight lineCrosses the y-axis above (0,0)
  - _why:_ A non-zero y-intercept indicates a relationship of the form $y = kx + c$, not direct proportion. A direct proportion graph must be straight and pass through the origin.
- Curved linePasses through the origin (0,0)
  - _why:_ Passing through the origin alone is insufficient; the curved shape is the misconception here. Since direct proportion has equation $y = kx$, its graph must also be a straight line.
- Straight linePasses through the point (0,1)
  - _why:_ The point (0,1) gives a y-intercept of 1, so the variables are not directly proportional. The required straight line must instead pass through the origin (0,0).

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the graph of $y \propto x^2$.

**Back:** A U-shaped curve (parabola) with its vertex at the origin.

**Choices:**

- A U-shaped curve (parabola) with its vertex at the origin. ✔
  - _why:_ The equation has the form $y = kx^2$. For positive $k$, this is a parabola whose minimum point is the origin and whose two sides rise symmetrically.
- A straight line (linear graph) passing through the origin.
  - _why:_ A straight line through the origin represents $y \propto x$, not $y \propto x^2$. Squaring $x$ produces a U-shaped parabola with its vertex at the origin.
- A reciprocal curve with branches approaching both axes.
  - _why:_ Branches that approach the axes are characteristic of an inverse relationship such as $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$. A square relationship produces a U-shaped parabola at the origin.
- A V-shaped graph with its turning point at the origin.
  - _why:_ A V-shaped graph represents an absolute-value relationship such as $y = k|x|$. Squaring produces a smooth U-shaped parabola rather than two straight sections meeting at the origin.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the graph of $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ look like?

**Back:** Two curved branches (typically in the top-right and bottom-left quadrants).

**Choices:**

- Two curved branches (typically in the top-right and bottom-left quadrants). ✔
  - _why:_ For $y = \frac{k}{x}$ with positive $k$, $x$ and $y$ have the same sign. This creates two curved branches that approach but never reach either axis.
- One U-shaped curve with its vertex at the origin.
  - _why:_ A U-shaped curve is associated with a square relationship such as $y \propto x^2$. A reciprocal relationship instead has two separate curved branches approaching the coordinate axes.
- One straight diagonal line passing through the origin.
  - _why:_ A straight line through the origin represents direct proportion, $y \propto x$. For $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$, the changing reciprocal rate creates two separate curved branches.
- Two curved branches that cross both coordinate axes.
  - _why:_ The branches approach the axes asymptotically but never cross them, because $x = 0$ is undefined and $y$ cannot equal zero. They typically occupy the top-right and bottom-left quadrants.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** How does the graph of $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$ differ from $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ regarding the x-axis?

**Back:** Both branches are above the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis).

**Choices:**

- Both branches are above the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis). ✔
  - _why:_ Because $x^2$ is positive for every non-zero $x$, $\frac{k}{x^2}$ is positive when $k$ is positive. The equal outputs for $x$ and $-x$ give y-axis symmetry.
- Both branches are below the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis).
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the square as making the reciprocal negative. For positive $k$, $x^2$ is always positive, so both branches lie above the x-axis and are symmetric about the y-axis.
- The branches are on opposite sides of the x-axis (symmetric about the origin).
  - _why:_ Origin symmetry applies to $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ because changing the sign of $x$ changes the sign of $y$. Squaring removes that sign change, placing both branches above the x-axis.
- Both branches cross the x-axis (symmetric about the y-axis).
  - _why:_ A reciprocal-square graph approaches $y = 0$ but never reaches it, so neither branch crosses the x-axis. Both branches remain above it and are symmetric about the y-axis.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** A straight line graph that [ ___ ] does not represent direct proportion.

**Back:** A straight line graph that misses the origin does not represent direct proportion.

**Choices:**

- misses the origin ✔
  - _why:_ A direct proportion has equation $y = kx$, so its graph must pass through (0,0). Any straight line that misses the origin has a non-zero intercept and is not direct proportion.
- passes through the origin
  - _why:_ Passing through the origin is essential rather than disqualifying. Together with being a straight line, this shows a relationship of the form $y = kx$, which is direct proportion.
- has a positive gradient
  - _why:_ A positive gradient does not prevent direct proportion; for example, $y = 2x$ is directly proportional. The feature that rules it out is missing the origin, indicating a non-zero intercept.
- slopes downwards
  - _why:_ A downward-sloping line can still represent direct proportion when $k$ is negative, provided it passes through the origin. A straight line fails the test only when it misses the origin.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Write the formula for $y$ being directly proportional to the square root of $x$.

**Back:** $$y = k\sqrt{x}$

**Choices:**

- $$y = k\sqrt{x}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Direct proportionality to the square root means $y \propto \sqrt{x}$. Replacing the proportionality sign with an equals sign requires the constant of proportionality $k$, giving $y = k\sqrt{x}$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{\sqrt{x}}$
  - _why:_ Placing $\sqrt{x}$ in the denominator changes the relationship to inverse proportion with the square root. Direct proportion requires multiplication by $\sqrt{x}$, so the formula is $y = k\sqrt{x}$.
- $$y = kx^2$
  - _why:_ This formula makes $y$ directly proportional to the square of $x$, not its square root. The square-root relationship is written $y = k\sqrt{x}$.
- $$y = \sqrt{kx}$
  - _why:_ Putting $k$ inside the root makes the effective multiplier $\sqrt{k}$ rather than the stated constant $k$. The standard proportionality equation is $y = k\sqrt{x}$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you use a table of values to determine if $y \propto x^2$ or $y \propto x^3$?

**Back:** Test if $\frac{y}{x^2}$ or $\frac{y}{x^3}$ gives the same constant $k$ for every pair.

**Choices:**

- Test if $\frac{y}{x^2}$ or $\frac{y}{x^3}$ gives the same constant $k$ for every pair. ✔
  - _why:_ Rearranging $y = kx^2$ gives $k = \frac{y}{x^2}$, while rearranging $y = kx^3$ gives $k = \frac{y}{x^3}$. The correct model has a constant ratio for every pair.
- Test if $\frac{x}{y^2}$ or $\frac{x}{y^3}$ gives the same constant $k$ for every pair.
  - _why:_ This reverses the variables and squares or cubes the wrong one. Rearranging the proposed relationships requires testing $\frac{y}{x^2}$ and $\frac{y}{x^3}$ for a constant value.
- Test if $\frac{y}{x}$ or $\frac{x}{y}$ gives the same constant $k$ for every pair.
  - _why:_ These ratios test simple direct proportion between $x$ and $y$, not square or cube proportion. The relevant tests are $\frac{y}{x^2}$ and $\frac{y}{x^3}$.
- Test if $y-x^2$ or $y-x^3$ gives the same constant $k$ for every pair.
  - _why:_ Subtracting tests an additive relationship such as $y = x^2 + k$, rather than proportionality. For $y = kx^2$ or $y = kx^3$, divide by the relevant power of $x$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** When solving $\sqrt{s} = 3$, the value of $s$ is [ ___ ].

**Back:** When solving $\sqrt{s} = 3$, the value of $s$ is 9.

**Choices:**

- 9 ✔
  - _why:_ Squaring both sides of $\sqrt{s} = 3$ gives $s = 3^2 = 9$. Checking confirms that the principal square root of 9 is 3.
- 6
  - _why:_ This comes from doubling 3 instead of squaring it. To undo a square root, square both sides: $s = 3^2 = 9$.
- 3
  - _why:_ This leaves the square root operation unresolved. Squaring both sides of $\sqrt{s} = 3$ gives $s = 9$, and indeed $\sqrt{9} = 3$.
- $$\sqrt{3}$
  - _why:_ Taking another square root applies the operation again rather than reversing it. The inverse operation is squaring, so $s = 3^2 = 9$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If $r = k\sqrt{s}$ and $r=10$ when $s=25$, calculate $k$.

**Back:** $$k = 2$ ($10 = k \times 5$)

**Choices:**

- $$k = 2$ ($10 = k \times 5$) ✔
  - _why:_ Since $\sqrt{25}=5$, substitution gives $10=5k$. Dividing both sides by $5$ gives the constant of proportionality $k=2$.
- $$k = 50$ ($10 \times 5 = k$)
  - _why:_ This multiplies by $5$ instead of dividing by it. Since $10=5k$, dividing both sides by $5$ gives the correct value $k=2$.
- $$k = 0.4$ ($10 = 25k$)
  - _why:_ This uses $s=25$ directly and ignores the square root. The equation requires $\sqrt{25}=5$, so $10=5k$ and therefore $k=2$.
- $$k = 5$ ($10 = 2k$)
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the known output and the constant when rearranging. Substitution actually gives $10=5k$, which leads to $k=2$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the common mistake when translating "$p$ is directly proportional to $q$" into an equation?

**Back:** Swapping variables; the first variable mentioned should be on the left ($p = kq$).

**Choices:**

- Swapping variables; the first variable mentioned should be on the left ($p = kq$). ✔
  - _why:_ The statement identifies $p$ as the dependent variable, so it belongs on the left. Direct proportion means multiplying $q$ by a constant: $p=kq$.
- Keeping variables in order; the second variable mentioned should be on the left ($q = kp$).
  - _why:_ Putting the second variable on the left reverses the stated dependency. As $p$ is directly proportional to $q$, the correct translation is $p=kq$.
- Using a constant of proportionality; directly proportional variables should be written without $k$ ($p = q$).
  - _why:_ Directly proportional variables are not necessarily equal, so omitting $k$ incorrectly assumes a constant of $1$. The general equation is $p=kq$.
- Multiplying by the second variable; direct proportion requires division by $q$ ($p = \frac{k}{q}$).
  - _why:_ Dividing a constant by $q$ describes inverse proportion, not direct proportion. The first variable remains on the left and the correct equation is $p=kq$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** In inverse proportion, if the input gets larger, the output should usually get [ ___ ].

**Back:** In inverse proportion, if the input gets larger, the output should usually get smaller.

**Choices:**

- smaller ✔
  - _why:_ In inverse proportion, increasing the input reduces the output so that their product remains constant. Therefore, the output usually gets smaller.
- larger
  - _why:_ An output getting larger with the input describes direct rather than inverse proportion. In inverse proportion, increasing the input makes the output smaller.
- unchanged
  - _why:_ An unchanged output would indicate no dependence on the input. In inverse proportion, the output must get smaller as the input gets larger.
- equal
  - _why:_ Inverse proportion does not mean that the input and output become equal. As the input gets larger, the output usually gets smaller to maintain a constant product.

- $y=kx$: variables scale in the opposite direction$y=\frac{k}{x}$: variables scale in the same direction
  - _why:_ This reverses the two relationships. Direct proportion makes the variables scale together, while inverse proportion makes one decrease by the corresponding factor when the other increases.
- $y=kx$: variables scale in the same direction$y=\frac{k}{x}$: variables scale in the same direction
  - _why:_ The direct-proportion statement is right, but the inverse-proportion statement is not. In $y=\frac{k}{x}$, increasing $x$ causes $y$ to decrease, so they scale oppositely.
- $y=kx$: variables scale in the opposite direction$y=\frac{k}{x}$: variables scale in opposite directions
  - _why:_ The inverse-proportion statement is right, but the direct-proportion statement is not. In $y=kx$, scaling $x$ by a factor scales $y$ by that same factor.
