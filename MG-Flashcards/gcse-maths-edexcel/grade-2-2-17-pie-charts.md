## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A pie chart represents the [ ___ ]; the full circle is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A pie chart represents the whole group; the full circle is 360°.

**Choices:**

- A pie chart represents the whole group; the full circle is 360°. ✔
  - _why:_ Every sector is a portion of the whole group, and the angles around the centre of a complete circle total 360°.
- A pie chart represents the whole group; the full circle is 180°.
  - _why:_ The 180° value is the angle in a semicircle, not a full circle. A pie chart represents the whole group, and its full circle is 360°.
- A pie chart represents the one category; the full circle is 360°.
  - _why:_ One sector represents one category, but the entire pie chart represents the whole group. The complete circle containing all categories is 360°.
- A pie chart represents the half group; the full circle is 90°.
  - _why:_ A 90° sector is only one quarter of a circle, not the complete chart. The pie chart represents the whole group, and the full circle is 360°.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In a pie chart, what does the largest sector show?

**Back:** The largest frequency or amount.

**Choices:**

- The largest frequency or amount. ✔
  - _why:_ Sector angle is proportional to frequency or amount, so the category represented by the largest sector has the largest frequency or amount.
- The smallest frequency or amount.
  - _why:_ The smallest frequency or amount produces the smallest sector, not the largest. Because sector size is proportional to frequency, the largest sector shows the largest frequency or amount.
- The total frequency or amount.
  - _why:_ The total is represented by the complete pie chart rather than one sector. The largest individual sector shows the largest frequency or amount among the categories.
- The average frequency or amount.
  - _why:_ A sector represents one category rather than the average of all categories. The largest sector therefore identifies the largest frequency or amount.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What angle is a right-angle sector in a pie chart?

**Back:** 90°.

**Choices:**

- 90°. ✔
  - _why:_ A right angle is defined as 90°, so a sector bounded by two perpendicular radii has an angle of 90° at the centre.
- 45°.
  - _why:_ A 45° angle is half a right angle. A right-angle sector has an angle of 90° at the centre of the pie chart.
- 180°.
  - _why:_ A 180° angle forms a straight line and represents a semicircle. A right-angle sector is half this size, so its angle is 90°.
- 360°.
  - _why:_ The 360° value is the angle around a complete circle, not a right-angle sector. A right angle measures 90°.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What fraction of a pie chart is a right-angle sector?

**Back:** $\frac{1}{4}$.

**Choices:**

- $\frac{1}{4}$. ✔
  - _why:_ A right angle is 90°, while a full pie chart is 360°. Therefore the fraction is $\frac{90}{360}=\frac{1}{4}$.
- $\frac{1}{2}$.
  - _why:_ One half of a circle is 180°, so this treats a right angle as a straight angle. Since $\frac{90}{360}=\frac{1}{4}$, the correct fraction is one quarter.
- $\frac{1}{3}$.
  - _why:_ One third of a circle is 120°, not 90°. Dividing the sector angle by the full 360° gives $\frac{90}{360}=\frac{1}{4}$.
- $\frac{1}{8}$.
  - _why:_ One eighth of a circle is 45°, which is half a right angle. A 90° sector occupies $\frac{90}{360}=\frac{1}{4}$ of the chart.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a fraction from a sector, compare its [ ___ ] with [ ___ ].

**Back:** To find a fraction from a sector, compare its angle with 360°.

**Choices:**

- To find a fraction from a sector, compare its angle with 360°. ✔
  - _why:_ A sector's fraction of the pie chart is its angle divided by the 360° in a full circle, giving $\frac{\text{sector angle}}{360°}$.
- To find a fraction from a sector, compare its angle with 100.
  - _why:_ Using 100 confuses percentages with angles. For an angle-based fraction, compare the sector angle with the 360° in a complete circle.
- To find a fraction from a sector, compare its frequency with 360°.
  - _why:_ Frequency and degrees are different types of quantity, so this comparison is invalid. Compare the sector's angle with the full-circle angle of 360°.
- To find a fraction from a sector, compare its angle with 180°.
  - _why:_ The 180° denominator represents only a semicircle and would make the fraction twice as large. A full pie chart is 360°, so compare the angle with 360°.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Leah's travel sector is 90° and represents £180. What is her total spending?

**Back:** £720.

**Choices:**

- £720. ✔
  - _why:_ A 90° sector is $\frac{90}{360}=\frac{1}{4}$ of the chart. If one quarter is £180, the total is $£180\times4=£720$.
- £360.
  - _why:_ Doubling £180 treats the 90° sector as half of the pie chart. It is actually one quarter, so multiplying by four gives a total of £720.
- £540.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by three incorrectly treats 90° as one third of the full circle. Since $360\div90=4$, the total spending is $£180\times4=£720$.
- £648.
  - _why:_ This results from comparing 90° with 100 as though the angle were a percentage. Angles must be compared with 360°, so the total is $£180\times\frac{360}{90}=£720$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** For pie-chart angles, use [ ___ ], not [ ___ ], unless working with percentages.

**Back:** For pie-chart angles, use 360°, not 100, unless working with percentages.

**Choices:**

- For pie-chart angles, use 360°, not 100, unless working with percentages. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles in a complete circle total 360°, so pie-chart angle calculations use 360°. The value 100 is the whole only when quantities are expressed as percentages.
- For pie-chart angles, use 180°, not 100, unless working with percentages.
  - _why:_ The 180° value covers only a semicircle, so using it doubles calculated proportions. Pie-chart angles use the complete-circle total of 360°, while percentages use 100.
- For pie-chart angles, use 360°, not 180, unless working with percentages.
  - _why:_ Although 360° is correct, 180 is not the percentage total being contrasted here. Pie-chart angles use 360°, while percentage calculations use 100.
- For pie-chart angles, use 100, not 360°, unless working with percentages.
  - _why:_ This reverses the angle and percentage totals. A complete circle contains 360°, whereas a complete quantity expressed as a percentage is 100%.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the total frequency?

**Back:** Add all frequencies.

**Choices:**

- Add all frequencies. ✔
  - _why:_ The total frequency counts every observation across all categories, so it is found by adding together all of the individual category frequencies.
- Subtract all frequencies.
  - _why:_ Subtracting frequencies does not count all observations and can produce a meaningless result. The total frequency is found by adding every category's frequency together.
- Add all sector angles.
  - _why:_ Adding sector angles gives 360°, the total angle in the chart, rather than the number of observations. To find total frequency, add all frequencies.
- Find the largest frequency.
  - _why:_ The largest frequency counts only the biggest category and leaves out the others. Total frequency is obtained by adding the frequencies of every category.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find a sector angle from a frequency?

**Back:** $\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total frequency}}\times 360^\circ$.

**Choices:**

- $\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total frequency}}\times 360^\circ$. ✔
  - _why:_ The frequency as a fraction of the total gives the fraction of the complete circle occupied by the category. Multiplying this fraction by $360^\circ$ gives its sector angle.
- $\frac{\text{total frequency}}{\text{frequency}}\times 360^\circ$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the required fraction, producing an angle greater than a full circle for most categories. Use $\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total frequency}}\times 360^\circ$ because the category is a fraction of the total.
- $\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total frequency}}\times 180^\circ$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by $180^\circ$ treats the pie chart as a semicircle. A complete pie chart contains $360^\circ$, so the correct multiplier is $360^\circ$.
- $\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total frequency}}\times 100^\circ$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 100 converts the fraction into a percentage, not an angle for a pie chart. Multiply by $360^\circ$, the angle around a complete circle.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find a frequency from a sector angle?

**Back:** $\frac{\text{sector angle}}{360^\circ}\times \text{total frequency}$.

**Choices:**

- $\frac{\text{sector angle}}{360^\circ}\times \text{total frequency}$. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the sector angle by $360^\circ$ finds the fraction of the whole pie represented. Multiplying that fraction by the total frequency gives the category’s frequency.
- $\frac{\text{sector angle}}{\text{total frequency}}\times 360^\circ$.
  - _why:_ This divides the angle by the number of data values, mixing quantities incorrectly. Divide by the full-circle angle, $360^\circ$, then multiply by the total frequency.
- $\frac{360^\circ}{\text{sector angle}}\times \text{total frequency}$.
  - _why:_ This uses the reciprocal of the sector’s fraction and makes smaller sectors produce larger frequencies. The correct fraction is $\frac{\text{sector angle}}{360^\circ}$.
- $\frac{\text{sector angle}}{360^\circ}\div \text{total frequency}$.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the total frequency makes the result smaller instead of scaling the sector fraction to the data set. Multiply the fraction by the total frequency.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** A 90° sector has frequency 15. What is the total frequency?

**Back:** 60.

**Choices:**

- 60. ✔
  - _why:_ A $90^\circ$ sector is one quarter of a $360^\circ$ circle. If one quarter has frequency 15, the total frequency is $15\times 4=60$.
- 30.
  - _why:_ This treats $90^\circ$ as half of the full circle and doubles 15. It is actually one quarter of $360^\circ$, so the total is $15\times4=60$.
- 45.
  - _why:_ This multiplies the frequency by 3 rather than by 4. Four $90^\circ$ sectors make a full $360^\circ$ circle, so the total frequency is 60.
- 4.
  - _why:_ Dividing $360^\circ$ by $90^\circ$ gives the number of equal sectors, not the total frequency. Each of the four sectors represents 15, giving $4\times15=60$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the remaining sector angle after 90°, 120° and 60°?

**Back:** 90°.

**Choices:**

- 90°. ✔
  - _why:_ The given sectors total $90^\circ+120^\circ+60^\circ=270^\circ$. Subtracting this from the full $360^\circ$ circle leaves a remaining angle of $90^\circ$.
- 270°.
  - _why:_ This is the sum of the three given angles, not the remaining sector. Subtract $270^\circ$ from the complete-circle angle of $360^\circ$ to get $90^\circ$.
- 100°.
  - _why:_ This results from an arithmetic error when subtracting the known total from $360^\circ$. The known angles sum to $270^\circ$, leaving exactly $90^\circ$.
- 110°.
  - _why:_ This undercounts the combined given angles before subtracting from the full circle. Their correct sum is $270^\circ$, so the remaining sector is $360^\circ-270^\circ=90^\circ$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What tool is used to draw accurate sector angles?

**Back:** A protractor.

**Choices:**

- A protractor. ✔
  - _why:_ A protractor is marked in degrees and is designed to measure and draw angles accurately, making it the correct tool for constructing pie-chart sectors.
- A compass.
  - _why:_ A compass draws the circle and arcs but does not measure angles in degrees. A protractor is needed to measure and draw each sector angle accurately.
- A ruler.
  - _why:_ A ruler draws straight radii but cannot measure angles in degrees. The sector angles must be measured using a protractor before their boundary lines are drawn.
- A set square.
  - _why:_ A set square provides only fixed angles, such as $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$. A protractor can measure any required sector angle.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** When drawing sectors, measure angles from the [ ___ ] using [ ___ ].

**Back:** When drawing sectors, measure angles from the centre using radii.

**Choices:**

- When drawing sectors, measure angles from the centre using radii. ✔
  - _why:_ Every sector angle has its vertex at the centre of the circle. Its two straight boundaries are radii running from the centre to the circumference.
- When drawing sectors, measure angles from the circumference using radii.
  - _why:_ Measuring from the circumference creates an inscribed angle rather than the required central angle. Pie-chart angles are measured from the centre, with radii forming their boundaries.
- When drawing sectors, measure angles from the centre using chords.
  - _why:_ Chords join two points on the circumference and do not form the standard boundaries of a sector. Sector angles are measured at the centre using radii.
- When drawing sectors, measure angles from the circumference using chords.
  - _why:_ This confuses central sector construction with lines and angles based on the circumference. A pie-chart sector is measured from the centre and bounded by radii.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Plain = 10, Cream = 14, Chocolate = 16. Which category is the mode?

**Back:** Chocolate.

**Choices:**

- Chocolate. ✔
  - _why:_ The mode is the category with the greatest frequency. Chocolate has frequency 16, which is higher than Cream’s 14 and Plain’s 10.
- Plain.
  - _why:_ Choosing Plain confuses the mode with the least frequent category. Plain has frequency 10, while Chocolate has the greatest frequency, 16, so Chocolate is the mode.
- Cream.
  - _why:_ Cream has the middle frequency of 14, not the greatest frequency. Chocolate occurs 16 times, so Chocolate is the modal category.
- No mode.
  - _why:_ There would be no mode if no category had a uniquely greatest frequency. Chocolate has the single highest frequency of 16, so it is the mode.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** The sector angles in a completed pie chart should add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** The sector angles in a completed pie chart should add to 360°.

**Choices:**

- 360° ✔
  - _why:_ A completed pie chart represents one full turn around a circle. One full turn measures $360^\circ$, so all sector angles must total this amount.
- 180°
  - _why:_ This is the angle in a semicircle, so it would represent only half of a pie chart. A completed circular pie chart has sector angles totalling $360^\circ$.
- 90°
  - _why:_ This is a quarter-turn angle and represents only one quarter of a circle. All sectors in a complete pie chart must add to the full $360^\circ$.
- 720°
  - _why:_ This represents two complete turns, which would count the pie chart twice. A single completed circle contains one full turn, equal to $360^\circ$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If a car is chosen at random, what is assumed?

**Back:** Each car has an equal chance.

**Choices:**

- Each car has an equal chance. ✔
  - _why:_ Choosing a car at random means every individual car is equally likely to be selected, so none is favoured over another.
- Each colour has an equal chance.
  - _why:_ This confuses choosing a car with choosing a colour. Individual cars have an equal chance, but colours containing more cars are more likely to be selected.
- Each car has a 50% chance.
  - _why:_ Random selection does not automatically mean a 50% probability. Each car has an equal chance, with its probability determined by the total number of cars.
- Each car has a different chance.
  - _why:_ This reverses the meaning of random selection in this context. Each individual car is assumed to have an equal chance of being chosen.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** With 15 white cars out of 50, the fraction in simplest form is [ ___ ].

**Back:** With 15 white cars out of 50, the fraction in simplest form is $\frac{3}{10}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{3}{10}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both 15 and 50 by their highest common factor, 5, gives 3 and 10, so the fraction simplifies to $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{15}{50}$
  - _why:_ This represents the correct proportion but has not been simplified. Dividing the numerator and denominator by 5 gives the simplest form, $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This results from dividing the numerator and denominator by different amounts, which changes the fraction’s value. Dividing both by 5 gives $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{7}{10}$
  - _why:_ This is the fraction of cars that are not white, since 35 out of 50 are non-white. The white-car fraction is $\frac{3}{10}$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** There are 5 blue cars out of 50. What is the probability of not picking blue?

**Back:** $\frac{9}{10}$.

**Choices:**

- $\frac{9}{10}$. ✔
  - _why:_ There are 45 cars that are not blue, so the probability is $\frac{45}{50}$. Dividing both parts by 5 gives $\frac{9}{10}$.
- $\frac{1}{10}$.
  - _why:_ This is the probability of picking a blue car, since $\frac{5}{50}=\frac{1}{10}$. Its complement, the probability of not picking blue, is $\frac{9}{10}$.
- $\frac{4}{5}$.
  - _why:_ This follows from incorrectly simplifying $\frac{5}{50}$ to $\frac{1}{5}$. The blue probability is $\frac{1}{10}$, so the non-blue probability is $\frac{9}{10}$.
- $\frac{19}{20}$.
  - _why:_ This treats 5 out of 50 as 5% instead of 10%. Since blue cars make up $\frac{1}{10}$, not-blue cars make up $\frac{9}{10}$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can't you compare actual category numbers from two pie charts if totals are missing?

**Back:** Pie charts show proportions; the totals are unknown.

**Choices:**

- Pie charts show proportions; the totals are unknown. ✔
  - _why:_ Slice sizes show each category’s share of its chart, not its actual frequency. Without each chart’s total, the corresponding category numbers cannot be calculated or compared.
- Pie charts show totals; the proportions are unknown.
  - _why:_ This reverses what a pie chart displays. Its slices show proportions, while the total represented by the complete circle remains unknown unless separately provided.
- Pie charts show angles; the frequencies are known.
  - _why:_ Although slice angles can be measured, they reveal only proportions. Actual frequencies are not known without the total number represented by each complete pie chart.
- Pie charts show frequencies; the totals are shown.
  - _why:_ A pie chart does not directly provide frequencies or totals. It shows proportions, so actual category numbers cannot be compared when the totals are missing.
