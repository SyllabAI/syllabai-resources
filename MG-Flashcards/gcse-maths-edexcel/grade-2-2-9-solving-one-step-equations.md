## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the equals sign mean in an equation?

**Back:** Both sides have the same value.

**Choices:**

- Both sides have the same value. ✔
  - _why:_ An equation states that the expressions on its left and right represent equal quantities, even when they are written in different forms.
- Both sides have the same numbers.
  - _why:_ The two sides do not need to contain identical numbers. The equals sign means both complete expressions have the same value.
- Both sides use the same operation.
  - _why:_ Different operations can appear on either side of an equation. The equals sign shows that the resulting values of both sides are the same.
- The right side gives the final answer.
  - _why:_ An equals sign does not simply introduce a final answer; it compares two expressions. Both sides must have the same value.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Back:** A variable is a letter that stands for a number you do not know yet.

**Choices:**

- number you do not know yet ✔
  - _why:_ A variable uses a letter, such as $x$, to represent an unknown number that can be found by solving the equation.
- number that must equal zero
  - _why:_ A variable is not required to equal zero; zero is only one possible value. It represents a number whose value is not known yet.
- number printed after the equals sign
  - _why:_ A variable does not represent where a number is written in an equation. It stands for a number whose value is not known yet.
- number of operations in the equation

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** An inverse operation [ ___ ] another operation.

**Back:** An inverse operation undoes another operation.

**Choices:**

- undoes ✔
  - _why:_ An inverse operation reverses the effect of another operation. For example, subtraction undoes addition, while division undoes multiplication.
- repeats
  - _why:_ Repeating an operation applies it again rather than reversing it. An inverse operation undoes the original operation, such as subtracting after adding.
- replaces
  - _why:_ Replacing an operation does not necessarily reverse its effect. An inverse operation specifically undoes another operation to help isolate the variable.
- ignores
  - _why:_ Ignoring an operation leaves its effect unchanged. An inverse operation undoes that effect, such as dividing to undo multiplication.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the missing number: $6+\Box=17$.

**Back:** $$\Box=11$

**Choices:**

- $$\Box=11$ ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting $6$ from both sides gives the missing value: $17-6=11$. The check $6+11=17$ confirms the result.
- $$\Box=23$
  - _why:_ Adding $6$ and $17$ produces $23$, but the missing number must be added to $6$. Subtracting gives $17-6=11$.
- $$\Box=10$
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one subtraction error. The required difference is $17-6=11$, and checking gives $6+11=17$.
- $$\Box=12$
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one calculation error because $6+12=18$, not $17$. The correct missing number is $17-6=11$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the missing number: $19-\Box=12$.

**Back:** $$\Box=7$

**Choices:**

- $$\Box=7$ ✔
  - _why:_ The missing number is the difference between $19$ and $12$. Since $19-12=7$, substituting $7$ makes the equation true.
- $$\Box=31$
  - _why:_ Adding $19$ and $12$ treats the equation as though the missing number were a total. Instead, $19-12=7$, so the missing number is $7$.
- $$\Box=-7$
  - _why:_ The negative sign comes from reversing the subtraction incorrectly. Since $19-7=12$, the missing number is positive $7$.
- $$\Box=8$
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one subtraction error: $19-8=11$, not $12$. Calculating $19-12$ correctly gives the missing number $7$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** A one-step equation is solved using [ ___ ].

**Back:** A one-step equation is solved using one inverse operation.

**Choices:**

- one inverse operation ✔
  - _why:_ Only one operation must be undone in a one-step equation, so applying its inverse once isolates the variable and solves the equation.
- two inverse operations
  - _why:_ Needing two inverse operations would normally indicate a two-step equation. A one-step equation requires exactly one inverse operation to isolate the variable.
- one identical operation
  - _why:_ Repeating the original operation does not undo it. A one-step equation is solved with one inverse operation, such as subtracting to undo addition.
- no inverse operations
  - _why:_ Without an inverse operation, the operation affecting the variable remains in place. A one-step equation requires one inverse operation to isolate the variable.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** In $x+7=19$, what inverse operation gets $x$ on its own?

**Back:** Subtract 7.

**Choices:**

- Subtract 7. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting $7$ undoes the addition of $7$. Applying it to both sides gives $x=19-7$, leaving $x$ on its own.
- Add 7.
  - _why:_ Adding another $7$ repeats the existing operation rather than undoing it. Subtracting $7$ from both sides isolates $x$.
- Subtract 19.
  - _why:_ The operation applied directly to $x$ is adding $7$, so its inverse is subtracting $7$, not subtracting the right-hand value.
- Divide by 7.
  - _why:_ Division by $7$ is the inverse of multiplication by $7$, not addition. Subtracting $7$ is required to undo $x+7$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $x+7=19$.

**Back:** $$x=12$

**Choices:**

- $$x=12$ ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting $7$ from both sides gives $x=19-7=12$. Checking confirms that $12+7=19$.
- $$x=26$
  - _why:_ Adding $7$ to $19$ applies the wrong operation and gives $26$. Subtracting $7$ from both sides instead gives $x=12$.
- $$x=11$
  - _why:_ This is an off-by-one subtraction error because $11+7=18$, not $19$. Correctly calculating $19-7$ gives $x=12$.
- $$x=133$
  - _why:_ Multiplying $19$ by $7$ confuses addition with multiplication and does not undo $+7$. The inverse operation is subtraction, giving $x=12$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** In $m-6=10$, what inverse operation gets $m$ on its own?

**Back:** Add 6.

**Choices:**

- Add 6. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding 6 to both sides reverses the subtraction of 6. This leaves $m$ alone on the left and gives $m=16$.
- Subtract 6.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 6 again does not undo the original subtraction; it produces $m-12$. The inverse of subtracting 6 is adding 6 to both sides.
- Add 10.
  - _why:_ Using the number on the right as the operation does not cancel the $-6$. Add 6 to both sides because inverse operations undo each other.
- Divide by 6.
  - _why:_ Division would undo multiplication by 6, not subtraction of 6. Add 6 to both sides to cancel the $-6$ and isolate $m$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** For $23-n=14$, what calculation finds the amount taken away?

**Back:** $$23-14=9$

**Choices:**

- $$23-14=9$ ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting the amount left from the starting amount finds what was taken away. Since $23-14=9$, the missing amount is 9.
- $$23+14=37$
  - _why:_ Adding combines the starting and remaining amounts rather than finding what was removed. Subtract the amount left from the start: $23-14=9$.
- $$14-23=-9$
  - _why:_ Reversing the subtraction creates a negative result even though the amount taken away is positive. Calculate starting amount minus remaining amount: $23-14=9$.
- $$23-9=14$
  - _why:_ This checks that removing 9 leaves 14, but it already assumes the missing amount is known. To find it directly, calculate $23-14=9$.

  - _why:_ In $23-n=14$, $n$ is the difference between 23 and 14. Calculating $23-14$ gives $n=9$, and $23-9=14$ confirms it.

  - _why:_ Since $7a$ means $7\times a$, divide both sides by 7. This gives $a=42\div7=6$.

  - _why:_ Subtracting 7 from 42 does not undo multiplication by 7. Divide both sides by 7 instead, giving $a=42\div7=6$.
- $$a=49$
  - _why:_ Adding 7 to 42 treats $7a$ as though it meant $a+7$. It means $7\times a$, so division gives $a=6$.

  - _why:_ Multiplying 42 by 7 applies the same operation rather than its inverse. Divide 42 by 7 to isolate $a$, giving $a=6$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Rewrite $r+r+r$ using multiplication shorthand.

**Back:** $$3r$

**Choices:**

- $$3r$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are three identical $r$ terms being added. Repeated addition is written as multiplication, so $r+r+r=3\times r=3r$.
- $$r^3$
  - _why:_ A power of 3 represents multiplication, $r\times r\times r$, not repeated addition. Three added $r$ terms combine to make $3r$.
- $$2r$
  - _why:_ This counts only two of the three identical terms. Each $r$ contributes one to the coefficient, so $r+r+r=3r$.
- $$3+r$
  - _why:_ The 3 counts how many copies of $r$ are present, so it must be a coefficient rather than a separate term. The shorthand is $3r$.

- $$r=9$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are three identical terms, so $3r=27$. Dividing both sides by $3$ gives $r=9$, and checking gives $9+9+9=27$.

  - _why:_ This treats the total as the value of each term. Since three lots of $r$ equal $27$, divide by $3$ to obtain $r=9$.
- $$r=8$
  - _why:_ This likely comes from an arithmetic or counting slip. Three lots of $8$ make $24$, not $27$; dividing $27$ by $3$ gives $r=9$.

  - _why:_ Subtraction uses a minus sign rather than a fraction bar. The fraction bar means divide, with the quantity above divided by the quantity below.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $\frac{30}{y}=6$.

**Back:** $$y=5$

**Choices:**

- $$y=5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying both sides by $y$ gives $30=6y$. Dividing by $6$ then gives $y=5$, and $30\div5=6$ confirms the answer.
- $$y=180$
  - _why:_ This multiplies $30$ by $6$, overlooking that $y$ is the divisor. From $30=6y$, divide $30$ by $6$ to get $y=5$.
- $$y=0.2$
  - _why:_ This reverses the required division and calculates $6\div30$. The equation asks what divides $30$ to make $6$, so $y=30\div6=5$.

  - _why:_ This subtracts $6$ from $30$, treating the equation like $30-y=6$. Here $y$ is a divisor, and solving $30=6y$ gives $y=5$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you check an answer to an equation?

**Back:** Substitute it into the original equation.

**Choices:**

- Substitute it into the original equation. ✔
  - _why:_ Replacing the unknown with the proposed answer checks whether both sides of the original equation have the same value. If they do, the answer satisfies the equation.
- Subtract it from the original equation.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the answer from an equation does not directly test whether it satisfies the original relationship. Substitute the answer for the unknown and compare both sides instead.
- Multiply it by the original equation.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the answer changes the equation rather than checking the proposed solution. The reliable check is to substitute the answer into the original equation.
- Round it before using the original equation.
  - _why:_ Rounding can alter an exact solution and make a valid answer appear wrong. Substitute the unrounded answer into the original equation and verify that both sides match.
