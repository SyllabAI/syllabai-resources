# Addition and Subtraction Flashcards

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is one symbol used to make a number; [ ___ ] means a digit's value depends on its position.

**Back:** A digit is one symbol used to make a number; place value means a digit's value depends on its position.

**Choices:**

- A digit is one symbol used to make a number; place value means a digit's value depends on its position. ✔
  - _why:_ A digit is a single numerical symbol, such as 5. Place value describes how its position determines its value, so 5 may represent five, fifty or five hundred.
- A digit is one symbol used to make a number; face value means a digit's value depends on its position.
  - _why:_ Face value is the digit's value considered by itself, regardless of position. Place value is the term for a digit's value changing according to its position.
- A number is one symbol used to make a number; place value means a digit's value depends on its position.
  - _why:_ A number can contain several symbols, whereas one symbol used to form it is a digit. Place value correctly describes how a digit's position affects its value.
- A place value is one symbol used to make a number; digit means a digit's value depends on its position.
  - _why:_ The two terms have been reversed. A digit is one symbol used to make a number, while place value explains why the digit's position determines its value.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In column addition or subtraction, what should be directly underneath each other?

**Back:** Matching place values.

**Choices:**

- Matching place values. ✔
  - _why:_ Digits with the same place value must be aligned, so ones are beneath ones, tens beneath tens and hundreds beneath hundreds before calculating each column.
- Matching digits.
  - _why:_ Equal-looking digits do not need to be aligned if they occupy different positions. Matching place values must be directly underneath each other so each column represents the same value.
- The largest digits.
  - _why:_ Digit size does not determine column alignment. Matching place values must be underneath each other, ensuring that ones, tens and hundreds are added or subtracted correctly.
- The final digits.
  - _why:_ Aligning only the final digits can fail when decimals or place-value columns differ. Matching place values must be directly underneath each other before carrying out the calculation.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** [ ___ ] combines amounts to find a total; its answer is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Addition combines amounts to find a total; its answer is the sum.

**Choices:**

- Addition combines amounts to find a total; its answer is the sum. ✔
  - _why:_ Addition combines two or more amounts, and the resulting total is called the sum. For example, the sum of 3 and 4 is 7.
- Addition combines amounts to find a total; its answer is the difference.
  - _why:_ Difference names the result of subtraction, not addition. Addition combines amounts to produce a total, and this resulting total is called the sum.
- Subtraction combines amounts to find a total; its answer is the sum.
  - _why:_ Subtraction removes one amount from another rather than combining amounts. Addition is the operation that combines amounts, and its answer is called the sum.
- Multiplication combines amounts to find a total; its answer is the product.
  - _why:_ Multiplication finds a product using equal groups or repeated scaling. Addition is the operation described here because it combines amounts, and its answer is the sum.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** In column addition, when do you carry?

**Back:** When a column totals 10 or more.

**Choices:**

- When a column totals 10 or more. ✔
  - _why:_ A column total of 10 or more contains at least one complete group of ten. That group is carried into the next place value column.
- When a column totals more than 10.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly excludes a total of exactly 10, which must also be carried. Carry whenever a column totals 10 or more because ten units form one unit in the next column.
- When a column totals 9 or more.
  - _why:_ A total of 9 still fits as a single digit in its current column. Carrying is required only when the column total reaches 10 or more.
- When a column totals 20 or more.
  - _why:_ Waiting until 20 would leave totals from 10 to 19 incorrectly written in one column. Carry as soon as the column total is 10 or more.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Carrying moves extra value into the [ ___ ]: 14 ones = [ ___ ].

**Back:** Carrying moves extra value into the next place value column: 14 ones = 4 ones and 1 ten.

**Choices:**

- Carrying moves extra value into the next place value column: 14 ones = 4 ones and 1 ten. ✔
  - _why:_ Fourteen ones contain one complete group of ten and four remaining ones. The ten is therefore carried into the next place value column.
- Carrying moves extra value into the same place value column: 14 ones = 4 ones and 1 ten.
  - _why:_ Although the decomposition of 14 is correct, the group of ten cannot remain in the ones column. It moves into the next place value column as one ten.
- Carrying moves extra value into the next place value column: 14 ones = 1 one and 4 tens.
  - _why:_ This reverses the roles of the digits and represents 41 rather than 14. Fourteen ones regroup as 4 ones and 1 ten in the next column.
- Carrying moves extra value into the previous place value column: 14 ones = 1 one and 4 tens.
  - _why:_ Carrying moves left into the next higher place value, not into a previous column, and the digits have also been reversed. Fourteen ones equal 4 ones and 1 ten.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $346 + 78$.

**Back:** 424

**Choices:**

- 424 ✔
  - _why:_ Adding the ones gives $6+8=14$, so write 4 and carry 1. The tens total 12, giving 2 tens and one carried hundred, so the result is 424.
- 414
  - _why:_ This results from failing to include the carried 1 when adding the tens. After $6+8=14$, the tens calculation is $4+7+1=12$, producing 424.
- 416
  - _why:_ This is the result of adding only 70 to 346 and overlooking the extra 8 ones. Including all of 78 gives $346+78=424$.
- 434
  - _why:_ This adds an extra ten, usually by mishandling the carry from the ones column. The correct column totals give 4 ones, 2 tens and 4 hundreds, producing 424.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** [ ___ ] takes one amount away; its answer is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Subtraction takes one amount away; its answer is the difference.

**Choices:**

- Subtraction takes one amount away; its answer is the difference. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtraction removes one amount from another, and the result is called the difference. For example, the difference between 9 and 4 is 5.
- Subtraction takes one amount away; its answer is the sum.
  - _why:_ Sum is the result of addition, where amounts are combined. Subtraction takes one amount away from another, and the resulting answer is called the difference.
- Addition takes one amount away; its answer is the difference.
  - _why:_ Addition combines amounts rather than taking one away. Subtraction is the operation described, and the result obtained after subtracting is called the difference.
- Division takes one amount away; its answer is the quotient.
  - _why:_ Division separates an amount into equal groups or calculates how many times one number fits into another. Subtraction takes an amount away, producing the difference.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In subtraction, when do you exchange?

**Back:** When the top digit is too small.

**Choices:**

- When the top digit is too small. ✔
  - _why:_ Exchange is needed when the top digit in a column is smaller than the digit being subtracted. One unit from the next column is regrouped into the current column.
- When the bottom digit is too small.
  - _why:_ A smaller bottom digit can be subtracted directly from the top digit. Exchange is required when the top digit is too small to subtract the bottom digit in that column.
- When the top digit is larger.
  - _why:_ A larger top digit allows the subtraction to be completed directly without exchanging. Exchange is needed only when the top digit is too small for the subtraction.
- When the digits are equal.
  - _why:_ Equal digits subtract to give zero, so no exchange is necessary. Exchange is required when the top digit is too small to subtract the bottom digit.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** Exchanging takes [ ___ ] from the next column and changes it into [ ___ ] in the current column.

**Back:** Exchanging takes 1 from the next column and changes it into 10 in the current column.

**Choices:**

- Exchanging takes 1 from the next column and changes it into 10 in the current column. ✔
  - _why:_ Each place-value column is worth ten times the column to its right, so one unit from the next column becomes ten units in the current column.
- Exchanging takes 10 from the next column and changes it into 1 in the current column.
  - _why:_ This reverses the exchange. Moving one column to the right converts 1 unit of the next column into 10 units of the current column, not the other way around.
- Exchanging takes 1 from the next column and changes it into 1 in the current column.
  - _why:_ This confuses the base-ten exchange with simply transferring a unit unchanged. One unit from the next column has the same value as 10 units in the current column.
- Exchanging takes 1 from the next column and changes it into 100 in the current column.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses one hundred when moving only one place-value column. Each adjacent column differs by a factor of ten, so 1 becomes 10.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $2470 - 638$.

**Back:** 1832

**Choices:**

- 1832 ✔
  - _why:_ Column subtraction with exchanging gives 10 - 8 = 2, 16 - 3 = 3, 13 - 6 = 7 and 1 - 0 = 1, producing 1832.
- 1842
  - _why:_ The tens digit has been calculated without correctly accounting for the exchange from the hundreds column. Careful column subtraction gives 2470 - 638 = 1832.
- 1932
  - _why:_ This is 100 too large, indicating that the exchange involving the hundreds column was mishandled. Performing each exchange carefully gives 2470 - 638 = 1832.
- 1732
  - _why:_ This is 100 too small, typically caused by reducing the hundreds digit twice during exchanging. Correctly tracking each exchanged unit gives 2470 - 638 = 1832.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** How can you check a subtraction answer?

**Back:** Check answer + amount subtracted = starting number.

**Choices:**

- Check answer + amount subtracted = starting number. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtraction and addition are inverse operations. Adding the amount subtracted back to the answer should therefore reconstruct the original starting number.
- Check answer - amount subtracted = starting number.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the amount again continues moving away from the starting number rather than reversing the calculation. The valid check is answer + amount subtracted = starting number.
- Check answer + starting number = amount subtracted.
  - _why:_ Adding the starting number to the answer does not reverse the original subtraction. The inverse check must add the amount subtracted to the answer to recover the starting number.
- Check starting number + answer = amount subtracted.
  - _why:_ The relationship has been reversed incorrectly: the amount subtracted is not generally recovered by adding the answer to the starting number. Instead, answer + amount subtracted = starting number.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** For decimal addition or subtraction, what must be lined up first?

**Back:** The decimal points.

**Choices:**

- The decimal points. ✔
  - _why:_ Aligning the decimal points places digits with equal place values in the same columns, allowing ones, tenths, hundredths and other places to be combined correctly.
- The right-hand digits.
  - _why:_ The final digits can represent different place values when decimals have unequal lengths. Decimal points must be aligned so corresponding place-value columns match.
- The left-hand digits.
  - _why:_ Aligning the first digits can place ones beneath tens or hundreds, particularly when integer parts have different lengths. The decimal points must be lined up first.
- The largest digits.
  - _why:_ The largest digits do not determine place value and may occur in unrelated columns. Lining up the decimal points ensures that digits of matching place value align.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Why does lining up decimal points align the digits correctly?

**Back:** It aligns ones, tenths and hundredths.

**Choices:**

- It aligns ones, tenths and hundredths. ✔
  - _why:_ A decimal point fixes each digit’s place value, so aligning the points places ones under ones, tenths under tenths and hundredths under hundredths.
- It aligns tens, hundreds and thousands.
  - _why:_ Tens, hundreds and thousands lie to the left of the ones column and are not the key sequence around a decimal point. The aligned places are ones, tenths and hundredths.
- It aligns ones, tens and hundreds.
  - _why:_ This sequence incorrectly treats decimal places as becoming larger from left to right. Aligning decimal points matches ones, tenths and hundredths in their correct columns.
- It aligns tenths, hundredths and thousandths.
  - _why:_ This omits the ones column and shifts every stated place one column too far right. Decimal-point alignment correctly matches ones, tenths and hundredths.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** Adding zeros to the end of a decimal [ ___ ]: 7.5 = [ ___ ].

**Back:** Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value: 7.5 = 7.50.

**Choices:**

- Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value: 7.5 = 7.50. ✔
  - _why:_ A zero added after the final decimal digit contributes no additional value. Therefore, 7.5 and 7.50 both represent seven and five tenths.
- Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value: 7.5 = 75.0.
  - _why:_ Appending a zero after the final decimal digit does not move any existing digit to a new place. The correct equality is 7.5 = 7.50, not 75.0.
- Adding zeros to the end of a decimal changes its value: 7.5 = 7.50.
  - _why:_ This mistakes adding a trailing zero for changing place value. A trailing zero contributes no value, so the decimal’s value is unchanged and 7.5 = 7.50.
- Adding zeros to the end of a decimal does not change its value: 7.5 = 0.75.
  - _why:_ Writing 0.75 moves the existing digits one place to the right and divides the value by ten. A trailing zero leaves them fixed, giving 7.5 = 7.50.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** What is wrong with lining up decimal numbers by their right-hand ends?

**Back:** Place values may not match.

**Choices:**

- Place values may not match. ✔
  - _why:_ Decimals can contain different numbers of digits after the point, so matching their right-hand ends may put tenths beneath hundredths instead of matching equal place values.
- The decimal values always increase.
  - _why:_ Right-aligning does not necessarily change the numerical values, but it can place digits of different place values in one column. The problem is that place values may not match.
- Addition changes into multiplication.
  - _why:_ The operation does not automatically become multiplication when numbers are right-aligned. The actual error is that digits representing different place values may be placed in the same column.
- Trailing zeros are always removed.
  - _why:_ Zeros are not necessarily removed by right-aligning decimal numbers. The important issue is that ones, tenths and hundredths may fail to line up with matching place values.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are money answers usually written with two decimal places?

**Back:** Pounds and pence use two decimal places.

**Choices:**

- Pounds and pence use two decimal places. ✔
  - _why:_ There are 100 pence in one pound, so pence represent hundredths of a pound. Two digits after the decimal point show the number of pence.
- Pounds and pence use one decimal place.
  - _why:_ One decimal place represents tenths, but pence are hundredths of a pound because £1 equals 100p. Money answers therefore normally use two decimal places.
- Pounds and pence use three decimal places.
  - _why:_ Three decimal places would represent thousandths of a pound, which are smaller than one penny. Since £1 equals 100p, money normally uses two decimal places.
- Pounds and pence use no decimal places.
  - _why:_ Whole pounds alone cannot show an amount of pence. Because pence are hundredths of a pound, money answers normally need two decimal places.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If a calculation has only + and - signs, what order do you work in?

**Back:** Left to right.

**Choices:**

- Left to right. ✔
  - _why:_ Addition and subtraction have equal priority in the order of operations. When a calculation contains only these operations, they are carried out from left to right.
- Right to left.
  - _why:_ Equal-priority operations are not worked from right to left. When only addition and subtraction appear, calculate from left to right to preserve the intended grouping.
- Addition before subtraction.
  - _why:_ Addition does not have priority over subtraction; they have equal status in the order of operations. A calculation containing only these signs is worked from left to right.
- Subtraction before addition.
  - _why:_ Subtraction does not have priority over addition; both operations have equal status. When no higher-priority operations appear, work through the calculation from left to right.
