## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In 30 cm, what is the unit?

**Back:** cm

**Choices:**

- cm ✔
  - _why:_ The letters after the numerical value identify the unit. Here, cm is the standard abbreviation for centimetres, so the measurement is expressed in centimetres.
- mm
  - _why:_ Millimetres are abbreviated to mm, but that abbreviation does not appear in the measurement. The unit shown after 30 is cm, meaning centimetres.
- m
  - _why:_ Metres are abbreviated to m, without the prefix c. The measurement places cm after the number, so its unit is centimetres.
- km
  - _why:_ Kilometres are abbreviated to km and are much larger units of length. The abbreviation attached to 30 is cm, so the unit is centimetres.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A measurement has two parts: a [ ___ ] and a [ ___ ].

**Back:** A measurement has two parts: a number and a unit.

**Choices:**

- A measurement has two parts: a number and a unit. ✔
  - _why:_ The number gives the size or amount being measured, while the unit states the agreed scale used, such as centimetres, grams or litres.
- A measurement has two parts: a numerator and a denominator.
  - _why:_ Numerators and denominators are parts of a fraction, not the general components of a measurement. A measurement consists of a number and a unit.
- A measurement has two parts: a length and a symbol.
  - _why:_ Length is only one type of quantity, and a unit need not be treated merely as a symbol. Every measurement requires a number and a unit.
- A measurement has two parts: a number and a conversion.
  - _why:_ A conversion changes a measurement from one unit to another, so it is not part of every measurement. The two essential parts are a number and a unit.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What does length measure?

**Back:** Distance or how long something is.

**Choices:**

- Distance or how long something is. ✔
  - _why:_ Length describes the distance from one point to another or the extent of an object, such as its height, width or how long it is.
- Mass or how heavy something is.
  - _why:_ This confuses length with mass, which describes the amount of matter in an object. Length measures distance or how long something is.
- Capacity or how much liquid fits.
  - _why:_ This confuses length with capacity, which describes how much liquid a container can hold. Length instead measures distance or how long something is.
- Time or how long an event lasts.
  - _why:_ The phrase “how long” can refer to duration, making time tempting here. For length, it refers to distance or the physical extent of an object.

  - _why:_ This adds an extra factor of 10, often from confusing metres with a smaller unit. One kilometre equals 1000 metres, not 10,000 metres.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** Changing from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number [ ___ ], so you [ ___ ].

**Back:** Changing from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number bigger, so you multiply.

**Choices:**

- Changing from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number bigger, so you multiply. ✔
  - _why:_ Smaller units are needed in greater numbers to represent the same measurement. Therefore, converting from a bigger unit to a smaller unit requires multiplication.
- Changing from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number smaller, so you multiply.
  - _why:_ Multiplication makes the numerical value bigger, not smaller. Converting from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number bigger, so multiplication is required.
- Changing from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number bigger, so you divide.
  - _why:_ Dividing would make the numerical value smaller and reverses the conversion. Smaller units are more numerous, so the number becomes bigger and you multiply.
- Changing from a bigger unit to a smaller unit makes the number smaller, so you divide.
  - _why:_ This describes conversion in the opposite direction. From a bigger unit to a smaller unit, the numerical value becomes bigger, so you multiply by the conversion factor.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** Decimals use the same conversion rule: still [ ___ ] or [ ___ ] by the conversion number.

**Back:** Decimals use the same conversion rule: still multiply or divide by the conversion number.

**Choices:**

- Decimals use the same conversion rule: still multiply or divide by the conversion number. ✔
  - _why:_ The presence of a decimal does not change the conversion method. Multiply when moving to a smaller unit and divide when moving to a larger unit.
- Decimals use the same conversion rule: still multiply or subtract by the conversion number.
  - _why:_ Subtracting a conversion number does not scale a measurement between metric units. Decimals still require multiplication or division by the appropriate conversion number.
- Decimals use the same conversion rule: still add or divide by the conversion number.
  - _why:_ Adding the conversion number does not preserve the measurement's value in a new unit. The correct operations remain multiplication or division, even with decimals.
- Decimals use the same conversion rule: still add or subtract by the conversion number.
  - _why:_ Metric conversions scale quantities rather than increasing or decreasing them by a fixed amount. Use multiplication or division by the conversion number for decimals.
