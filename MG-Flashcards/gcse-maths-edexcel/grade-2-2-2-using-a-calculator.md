## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does an exam instruction to "write down all the figures on your calculator display" mean?

**Back:** Copy every digit exactly without rounding.

**Choices:**

- Copy every digit exactly without rounding. ✔
  - _why:_ The instruction requires an exact transcription of every digit displayed, including all decimal places shown. No rounding or truncation should be applied.
- Copy every digit shown after rounding.
  - _why:_ Rounding changes the digits displayed and therefore does not follow the instruction. The correct response is to copy every digit exactly without rounding.
- Copy only the first digits without rounding.
  - _why:_ Stopping before the end of the display is truncation, even if the copied digits are unchanged. Every displayed digit must be copied exactly.
- Copy the answer to three decimal places.
  - _why:_ Three decimal places should be used only when specifically requested. Here, every digit visible on the calculator display must be copied without rounding.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** On a calculator, which key converts a fraction answer into a decimal answer?

**Back:** The S-D (or $S \iff D$) key.

**Choices:**

- The S-D (or $S \iff D$) key. ✔
  - _why:_ This key switches the displayed answer between exact forms, such as fractions, and decimal form without requiring the calculation to be entered again.
- The Ans (previous answer) key.
  - _why:_ The Ans key recalls the previous result rather than changing its format. The S-D, or $S \iff D$, key converts a fraction display into a decimal.
- The = (or EXE) key.
  - _why:_ The equals or EXE key completes a calculation but does not normally switch the answer format. The S-D, or $S \iff D$, key performs the conversion.
- The SHIFT (or 2nd) key.
  - _why:_ SHIFT accesses alternative key functions but does not itself convert a fraction answer. The conversion is carried out using the S-D, or $S \iff D$, key.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the mathematical meaning of $1.35^2$?

**Back:** $$1.35 \times 1.35$ (repeated multiplication).

**Choices:**

- $$1.35 \times 1.35$ (repeated multiplication). ✔
  - _why:_ An index of 2 means that the base is used as a factor twice. Therefore, squaring 1.35 means multiplying 1.35 by itself.
- $$1.35 \times 2$ (multiplication by the power).
  - _why:_ An index is not a number by which the base is multiplied. The power 2 means using 1.35 as a factor twice: $1.35 \times 1.35$.
- $$1.35 + 1.35$ (repeated addition).
  - _why:_ Adding the number twice calculates $2 \times 1.35$, not its square. Squaring requires repeated multiplication, so the correct calculation is $1.35 \times 1.35$.
- $$1.35 \times 1.35 \times 1.35$ (three repeated factors).
  - _why:_ Using three factors calculates $1.35^3$, which is the cube. The index 2 requires exactly two factors, giving $1.35 \times 1.35$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** How does the calculator process $3.20 - 0.45^2$ compared to $(3.20 - 0.45)^2$?

**Back:** In $3.20 - 0.45^2$, only 0.45 is squared before subtracting.

**Choices:**

- In $3.20 - 0.45^2$, only 0.45 is squared before subtracting. ✔
  - _why:_ Powers are evaluated before subtraction, so the index applies only to 0.45. Brackets would be needed for the entire difference to be squared.
- In $3.20 - 0.45^2$, the whole expression is squared after subtracting.
  - _why:_ Without brackets around the subtraction, the index cannot apply to the whole expression. Only 0.45 is squared, and that result is then subtracted from 3.20.
- In $3.20 - 0.45^2$, 3.20 and 0.45 are both squared before subtracting.
  - _why:_ An index applies only to the number immediately before it unless brackets indicate otherwise. Therefore, only 0.45 is squared before it is subtracted from 3.20.
- In $3.20 - 0.45^2$, 0.45 is doubled before subtracting.
  - _why:_ The index 2 means square, not multiply by 2. The calculator evaluates $0.45 \times 0.45$ first and subtracts that result from 3.20.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What mathematical operation does the fraction bar represent?

**Back:** Division (top divided by bottom).

**Choices:**

- Division (top divided by bottom). ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction bar means that the numerator is divided by the denominator. For example, $\frac{6}{3}$ represents $6 \div 3$.
- Multiplication (top multiplied by bottom).
  - _why:_ Multiplication may be used with fractions in other calculations, but the fraction bar itself means division. The numerator is divided by the denominator.
- Subtraction (bottom subtracted from top).
  - _why:_ The vertical arrangement can make subtraction seem plausible, but a fraction bar does not mean subtracting the lower value. It represents top divided by bottom.
- Addition (top added to bottom).
  - _why:_ The two numbers in a fraction are not added merely because they appear together. The fraction bar instructs you to divide the top by the bottom.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** How should you enter a long fraction if your calculator does not have a fraction template?

**Back:** Put the whole numerator in brackets before dividing.

**Choices:**

- Put the whole numerator in brackets before dividing. ✔
  - _why:_ Brackets ensure that every term in a multi-part numerator is evaluated before division occurs. Without them, the calculator may divide only the final term.
- Enter the numerator without any brackets before dividing.
  - _why:_ Leaving a multi-part numerator ungrouped allows order of operations to divide only part of it. The whole numerator must be placed in brackets before dividing.
- Put the final numerator term in brackets before dividing.
  - _why:_ Bracketing only the final term does not group the complete numerator, so earlier additions or subtractions may be handled separately. Bracket the whole numerator before dividing.
- Put the denominator before the numerator when dividing.
  - _why:_ Reversing the order calculates the reciprocal of the intended fraction. Enter the whole numerator in brackets first, followed by the division sign and denominator.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Where do you start counting when identifying significant figures?

**Back:** At the first non-zero digit.

**Choices:**

- At the first non-zero digit. ✔
  - _why:_ Leading zeros only locate the decimal point and are not significant. Counting begins at the first non-zero digit and continues through the required number of significant figures.
- At the first digit after the decimal point.
  - _why:_ Decimal places are counted from the decimal point, but significant figures are not. For significant figures, begin at the first non-zero digit wherever it occurs.
- At the first digit including zero.
  - _why:_ Leading zeros are placeholders and do not count as significant figures. Counting must start at the first non-zero digit rather than automatically including an initial zero.
- At the last non-zero digit.
  - _why:_ Starting at the final non-zero digit reverses the required counting direction and misses earlier significant digits. Begin at the first non-zero digit and count from left to right.

**Front:** What is the difference between rounding to 2 decimal places vs 2 significant figures for the number 0.587?

**Back:** 2dp: 0.59 (digits after decimal point)2sf: 0.59 (digits from first non-zero)

**Choices:**

- 2dp: 0.59 (digits after decimal point)2sf: 0.59 (digits from first non-zero) ✔
  - _why:_ Two decimal places keeps two digits after the decimal point, while two significant figures starts at $5$. In both cases, the next digit is $7$, so $0.58$ rounds to $0.59$.
- 2dp: 0.58 (digits after decimal point)2sf: 0.58 (digits from first non-zero)
  - _why:_ This truncates the number rather than rounding it. In each method, the next digit is $7$, so the retained $8$ must round up and both answers are $0.59$.
- 2dp: 0.59 (digits after decimal point)2sf: 0.58 (digits from first non-zero)
  - _why:_ The decimal-place result is rounded correctly, but the significant-figure result is truncated. The third significant digit is $7$, so the second significant digit rounds up to give $0.59$.

  - _why:_ Keeping $0.587$ leaves three decimal places rather than two. The third decimal digit is $7$, so the hundredths digit rounds up and both required results are $0.59$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the rule for rounding up a digit?

**Back:** If the next digit is 5 or more, round up.

**Choices:**

- If the next digit is 5 or more, round up. ✔
  - _why:_ When the digit immediately after the rounding position is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, the retained digit increases by one.
- If the next digit is 4 or more, round up.
  - _why:_ Treating 4 as the threshold rounds too many values upwards. The retained digit increases only when the next digit is 5 or more.
- If the next digit is exactly 5, round up.
  - _why:_ Digits above 5 also require rounding up, so limiting the rule to exactly 5 is incomplete. Round up whenever the next digit is 5 or more.
- If the next digit is 6 or more, round up.
  - _why:_ Using 6 as the threshold incorrectly rounds values followed by 5 downwards. The standard rule is to round up when the next digit is 5 or more.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** When should you perform rounding in a multi-step calculator problem?

**Back:** Only at the very end of the calculation.

**Choices:**

- Only at the very end of the calculation. ✔
  - _why:_ Keeping the full calculator value throughout prevents small rounding errors from accumulating. Round only the final result to the accuracy requested in the question.
- Only at the very start of the calculation.
  - _why:_ Rounding an initial value can introduce an error that is magnified by later operations. Keep full precision and round only at the very end.
- After every step of the calculation.
  - _why:_ Repeated rounding causes errors to accumulate across the calculation, potentially changing the final answer. Retain full calculator values and round only at the very end.
- Whenever the calculator shows a decimal in the calculation.
  - _why:_ A decimal display is not a signal to round, because later steps may need its full precision. Rounding should occur only at the very end.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** In the expression $\sqrt{1.44} = 1.2$, what is the relationship between 1.2 and 1.44?

**Back:** $$1.2 \times 1.2 = 1.44$.

**Choices:**

- $$1.2 \times 1.2 = 1.44$. ✔
  - _why:_ A square root is a number that multiplies by itself to produce the original value. Squaring 1.2 gives 1.44, so its square root is 1.2.
- $$1.2 + 1.2 = 1.44$.
  - _why:_ This confuses squaring with doubling by addition, and the sum is actually 2.4. The square-root relationship is $1.2 \times 1.2 = 1.44$.
- $$1.44 \times 1.44 = 1.2$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the relationship and squares the original number instead of the root. A square root satisfies $1.2 \times 1.2 = 1.44$.
- $$1.2 \times 2 = 1.44$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 2 doubles a number rather than squaring it, and gives 2.4. The correct relationship is $1.2 \times 1.2 = 1.44$.
