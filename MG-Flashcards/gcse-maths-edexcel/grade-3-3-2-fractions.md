## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In $\frac{3}{5}$, what does the numerator 3 tell you?

**Back:** 3 parts are selected.

**Choices:**

- 3 parts are selected. ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator is the top number of a fraction. It shows that 3 of the equal parts making up the whole have been selected.
- 3 equal parts make the whole.
  - _why:_ This confuses the numerator with the denominator, which tells how many equal parts make the whole. The numerator 3 shows that 3 parts are selected.
- 3 parts are unselected.
  - _why:_ The numerator counts the parts that are selected, not those left over. In this fraction, 3 equal parts out of the 5 are selected.
- 3 wholes are selected.
  - _why:_ The numerator counts selected equal parts of one whole, rather than a number of wholes. Here, it means that 3 parts are selected.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In $\frac{3}{5}$, what does the denominator 5 tell you?

**Back:** The whole is split into 5 equal parts.

**Choices:**

- The whole is split into 5 equal parts. ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator is the bottom number and gives the total number of equal parts in the whole. Therefore, the whole is divided into 5 equal parts.
- The whole is split into 3 equal parts.
  - _why:_ This uses the numerator instead of the denominator to count the total parts. The denominator 5 means that the whole is split into 5 equal parts.
- The whole has 5 selected parts.
  - _why:_ This confuses the denominator with the numerator. The denominator shows that there are 5 equal parts altogether, while the numerator shows how many are selected.
- The whole is split into 5 unequal parts.
  - _why:_ Fractions divide a whole into equal parts, not unequal ones. The denominator 5 means that the whole is split into 5 equal parts.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** A fraction is in [ ___ ] when its numerator and denominator cannot both be divided by the same whole number bigger than 1.

**Back:** A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator cannot both be divided by the same whole number bigger than 1.

**Choices:**

- simplest form ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction is in simplest form when no whole number greater than 1 divides both its numerator and denominator, so it cannot be reduced further.
- improper form
  - _why:_ Improper describes a fraction whose numerator is at least as large as its denominator. The stated condition instead defines a fraction in simplest form.
- mixed form
  - _why:_ Mixed form combines a whole number with a proper fraction. A fraction that cannot be reduced by a common factor is in simplest form.
- decimal form
  - _why:_ Decimal form expresses a value using decimal notation rather than a fraction. The condition about having no common factor defines simplest form.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\frac{12}{18}$.

**Back:** $$\frac{2}{3}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{2}{3}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The highest common factor of 12 and 18 is 6. Dividing both numbers by 6 gives $12 \div 6=2$ and $18 \div 6=3$.
- $$\frac{6}{9}$
  - _why:_ Dividing both numbers by 2 gives an equivalent fraction, but it is not fully simplified because 6 and 9 are both divisible by 3. The answer is $\frac{2}{3}$.
- $$\frac{3}{2}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the simplified numerator and denominator, producing the reciprocal. Dividing 12 and 18 by their common factor 6 gives $\frac{2}{3}$.
- $$\frac{4}{9}$
  - _why:_ This results from dividing the numerator and denominator by different numbers, which changes the fraction’s value. Dividing both by 6 gives $\frac{2}{3}$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Equivalent fractions have the [ ___ ] even though they [ ___ ].

**Back:** Equivalent fractions have the same value even though they look different.

**Choices:**

- Equivalent fractions have the same value even though they look different. ✔
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions represent the same proportion or amount, although their numerators and denominators differ. For example, $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{2}{4}$ look different but are equal.
- Equivalent fractions have the same numerator even though they have different denominators.
  - _why:_ Keeping the same numerator while changing the denominator normally changes the fraction’s value. Equivalent fractions must have the same value, even though they look different.
- Equivalent fractions have the different values even though they look different.
  - _why:_ This mistakes equivalent for merely different-looking fractions. Equivalent fractions have the same value because the numerator and denominator have been scaled by the same factor.
- Equivalent fractions have the same denominator even though they represent different amounts.
  - _why:_ Fractions representing different amounts are not equivalent, and equivalent fractions do not need the same denominator. They have the same value even though they look different.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What must you do to the numerator and denominator to make an equivalent fraction?

**Back:** Multiply or divide both by the same number.

**Choices:**

- Multiply or divide both by the same number. ✔
  - _why:_ Applying the same non-zero multiplier or divisor to both the numerator and denominator preserves their ratio, creating a fraction with the same value.
- Add or subtract both by the same number.
  - _why:_ Adding or subtracting the same number does not usually preserve the ratio between numerator and denominator. To make an equivalent fraction, multiply or divide both by the same number.
- Multiply only the numerator by the same number.
  - _why:_ Changing only the numerator changes the fraction’s value rather than creating an equivalent fraction. Both numerator and denominator must be multiplied or divided by the same number.
- Multiply or divide them by different numbers.
  - _why:_ Using different factors changes the ratio between numerator and denominator. Equivalent fractions are made by multiplying or dividing both by the same number.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7}$.

**Back:** $$\frac{5}{7}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{5}{7}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The denominators are already the same, so add the numerators and keep the denominator: $\frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7}=\frac{2+3}{7}=\frac{5}{7}$.
- $$\frac{5}{14}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly adds the denominators as well as the numerators. With equal denominators, keep 7 as the denominator and add only the numerators to get $\frac{5}{7}$.
- $$\frac{6}{7}$
  - _why:_ This multiplies the numerators instead of adding them. Since the denominators match, calculate $2+3=5$ and keep the denominator 7, giving $\frac{5}{7}$.
- $$\frac{1}{7}$
  - _why:_ This subtracts the numerators rather than adding them. The calculation requires $2+3=5$, while the common denominator remains 7, so the answer is $\frac{5}{7}$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Fractions with different denominators must be changed to a [ ___ ] before adding or subtracting.

**Back:** Fractions with different denominators must be changed to a common denominator before adding or subtracting.

**Choices:**

- common denominator ✔
  - _why:_ A common denominator divides each whole into parts of the same size. Once the denominators match, the numerators can be added or subtracted correctly.
- common numerator
  - _why:_ Matching numerators does not make the parts the same size, so it does not prepare fractions for addition or subtraction. The fractions need a common denominator.
- lowest denominator
  - _why:_ The denominator does not simply need to be made as small as possible. Both fractions need the same, or common, denominator before their numerators can be combined.
- mixed number
  - _why:_ Converting fractions to mixed numbers does not ensure that their fractional parts have equal-sized pieces. Fractions with different denominators must first use a common denominator.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{1}{6}+\frac{2}{3}$.

**Back:** $$\frac{5}{6}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{5}{6}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Converting $\frac{2}{3}$ to sixths gives $\frac{4}{6}$. Adding the numerators then gives $\frac{1}{6}+\frac{4}{6}=\frac{5}{6}$.
- $$\frac{3}{6}$
  - _why:_ Adding the numerators while keeping 6 as the denominator ignores that $\frac{2}{3}$ equals $\frac{4}{6}$. The correct sum is $\frac{5}{6}$.
- $$\frac{3}{9}$
  - _why:_ Adding both numerators and denominators is not a valid rule for fraction addition. Using sixths gives $\frac{1}{6}+\frac{4}{6}=\frac{5}{6}$.
- $$\frac{7}{6}$
  - _why:_ An improper result comes from converting or combining the fractions incorrectly. Since $\frac{2}{3}=\frac{4}{6}$, the correct total is $\frac{5}{6}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{5}{8}-\frac{1}{4}$.

**Back:** $$\frac{3}{8}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{3}{8}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Convert $\frac{1}{4}$ to $\frac{2}{8}$, then subtract the numerators: $\frac{5}{8}-\frac{2}{8}=\frac{3}{8}$.
- $$\frac{4}{4}$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the numerator and denominator separately is not valid for fractions. Convert $\frac{1}{4}$ to $\frac{2}{8}$, giving the correct answer $\frac{3}{8}$.
- $$\frac{1}{2}$
  - _why:_ This results from treating $\frac{1}{4}$ as $\frac{1}{8}$. The equivalent fraction is $\frac{2}{8}$, so the correct difference is $\frac{3}{8}$.
- $$\frac{7}{8}$
  - _why:_ Adding $\frac{2}{8}$ instead of subtracting it reverses the required operation. The calculation is $\frac{5}{8}-\frac{2}{8}=\frac{3}{8}$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For denominators 4 and 12, what common denominator shortcut can you use?

**Back:** Use 12, the bigger multiple.

**Choices:**

- Use 12, the bigger multiple. ✔
  - _why:_ Because 12 is already a multiple of 4, it is the lowest common denominator. There is no need to multiply the two denominators together.
- Use 4, the smaller factor.
  - _why:_ Although 4 is the smaller denominator, 12 cannot be rewritten as an integer number of fourths. The suitable common denominator is 12 because both denominators divide into it.
- Use 8, the difference doubled.
  - _why:_ Adding or doubling denominators does not generally produce a common denominator. Here 8 is not divisible by 12, while 12 is divisible by both 4 and 12.
- Use 16, the two denominators added.
  - _why:_ Adding the denominators gives 16, but 16 is not a multiple of 12. The shortcut is to use 12 because it is already a multiple of 4.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** To multiply fractions, multiply the [ ___ ] together and the [ ___ ] together.

**Back:** To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

**Choices:**

- To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. ✔
  - _why:_ Fraction multiplication is performed straight across: multiply the top numbers to obtain the new numerator and the bottom numbers to obtain the new denominator.
- To multiply fractions, multiply the denominators together and the denominators together.
  - _why:_ Using the denominators for both products ignores the original numerators. The top numbers must be multiplied together, followed by the bottom numbers.
- To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and the numerators together.
  - _why:_ Using the numerators for both products ignores the original denominators. Multiply the numerators for the new top and the denominators for the new bottom.
- To multiply fractions, multiply the diagonal terms together and the diagonal terms together.
  - _why:_ Diagonal multiplication is associated with comparing or solving some fraction equations, not ordinary fraction multiplication. Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{3}{4}$ in simplest form.

**Back:** $$\frac{3}{10}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{3}{10}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply straight across to get $\frac{6}{20}$. Dividing both numerator and denominator by 2 simplifies this to $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{5}{9}$
  - _why:_ Adding the numerators and denominators instead of multiplying gives this result. Multiplication gives $\frac{6}{20}$, which simplifies correctly to $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{6}{5}$
  - _why:_ Multiplying only the numerators and retaining a denominator loses the factor of 4. The complete product is $\frac{6}{20}$, which simplifies to $\frac{3}{10}$.
- $$\frac{3}{20}$
  - _why:_ This is the unsimplified product with the numerator calculated incorrectly as 3 rather than $2\times3=6$. The correctly simplified product is $\frac{3}{10}$.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** To divide by a fraction, multiply by its [ ___ ].

**Back:** To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

**Choices:**

- reciprocal ✔
  - _why:_ The reciprocal is formed by swapping a fraction’s numerator and denominator. Multiplying by this reciprocal is equivalent to dividing by the original fraction.
- denominator
  - _why:_ Multiplying by only the denominator does not invert the entire fraction. Division by a fraction is replaced by multiplication by its reciprocal.
- numerator
  - _why:_ Using only the numerator ignores the denominator and does not represent division by the fraction. The whole fraction must be inverted to form its reciprocal.
- negative
  - _why:_ The negative changes a number’s sign and is its additive inverse, not its multiplicative inverse. Fraction division requires multiplication by the reciprocal.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** In keep-change-flip, which fraction is flipped?

**Back:** The second fraction only.

**Choices:**

- The second fraction only. ✔
  - _why:_ Keep the first fraction unchanged, change division to multiplication, and flip only the second fraction to form its reciprocal.
- The first fraction only.
  - _why:_ Flipping the first fraction changes the dividend rather than replacing division by the divisor. Keep the first fraction and flip only the second fraction.
- The two fractions together.
  - _why:_ Taking both reciprocals changes the value of the calculation. In keep-change-flip, the first fraction stays unchanged and only the second fraction is flipped.
- The smaller fraction only.
  - _why:_ The choice is determined by the order of division, not by denominator size. The divisor is the second fraction, so only that fraction is flipped.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $\frac{2}{9}\div\frac{4}{5}$ in simplest form.

**Back:** $$\frac{5}{18}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{5}{18}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Change division to multiplication and flip $\frac{4}{5}$: $\frac{2}{9}\times\frac{5}{4}=\frac{10}{36}$, which simplifies to $\frac{5}{18}$.
- $$\frac{8}{45}$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the fractions without flipping the divisor gives $\frac{8}{45}$. Division requires multiplying $\frac{2}{9}$ by $\frac{5}{4}$, producing $\frac{5}{18}$.
- $$\frac{18}{5}$
  - _why:_ Flipping the first fraction instead of the second reverses the wrong part of the calculation. Keep $\frac{2}{9}$ and flip $\frac{4}{5}$ to obtain $\frac{5}{18}$.
- $$\frac{10}{36}$
  - _why:_ This is equivalent to the correct value but is not in simplest form as requested. Dividing its numerator and denominator by 2 gives $\frac{5}{18}$.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** To calculate with mixed numbers, it is usually easiest to change them into [ ___ ] first.

**Back:** To calculate with mixed numbers, it is usually easiest to change them into improper fractions first.

**Choices:**

- improper fractions ✔
  - _why:_ Improper fractions are easier to calculate with because each mixed number becomes a single fraction, allowing the usual methods for adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing fractions.
- proper fractions
  - _why:_ A mixed number greater than one cannot be represented by a proper fraction of equal value. It should first be converted into an improper fraction.
- equivalent fractions
  - _why:_ Equivalent fractions are useful for finding common denominators, but this conversion specifically changes mixed numbers into improper fractions before carrying out the calculation.
- unit fractions
  - _why:_ A unit fraction has a numerator of one, so it cannot generally represent a mixed number by itself. Mixed numbers should be changed into improper fractions.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Change $2\frac{1}{5}$ into an improper fraction.

**Back:** $$\frac{11}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{11}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator: $2\times5+1=11$. The denominator remains $5$, giving $\frac{11}{5}$.
- $$\frac{10}{5}$
  - _why:_ This includes only $2\times5$ and forgets to add the original numerator. Adding the extra $1$ gives the correct numerator $11$, so the answer is $\frac{11}{5}$.
- $$\frac{7}{5}$
  - _why:_ Adding the whole number directly to the numerator gives $2+1=3$, not $7$. The required calculation is $2\times5+1=11$, giving $\frac{11}{5}$.
- $$\frac{11}{6}$
  - _why:_ The denominator should not be increased when converting a mixed number. The numerator becomes $2\times5+1=11$, while the denominator stays $5$, giving $\frac{11}{5}$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $2\frac{1}{3}+1\frac{1}{6}$.

**Back:** $$3\frac{1}{2}$

**Choices:**

- $$3\frac{1}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Use sixths: $\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{6}$, so the fractional parts total $\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$. The whole-number parts total $3$, giving $3\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$3\frac{2}{9}$
  - _why:_ Adding the numerators and denominators separately incorrectly gives $\frac{2}{9}$. Using a common denominator gives $\frac{2}{6}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$, so the answer is $3\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$3\frac{1}{3}$
  - _why:_ This effectively omits the additional $\frac{1}{6}$. Since $\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{6}$, the fractional total is $\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$, producing $3\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$4\frac{1}{2}$
  - _why:_ The whole-number parts are $2+1=3$, not $4$. The fractional parts add to $\frac{1}{2}$, so the correct total is $3\frac{1}{2}$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Work out $1\frac{1}{2}\times1\frac{2}{3}$. Give a mixed number.

**Back:** $$2\frac{1}{2}$

**Choices:**

- $$2\frac{1}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Convert the mixed numbers to improper fractions: $\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{5}{3}=\frac{15}{6}=\frac{5}{2}$. Converting back gives $2\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$1\frac{1}{3}$
  - _why:_ This comes from multiplying the whole and fractional parts separately, which is not valid for mixed numbers. Converting first gives $\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{5}{3}=\frac{5}{2}=2\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$3\frac{1}{6}$
  - _why:_ This is the result of adding the mixed numbers instead of multiplying them. For multiplication, convert first: $\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{5}{3}=\frac{5}{2}=2\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$2\frac{1}{3}$
  - _why:_ This confuses the fractional part after simplifying the product. The product is $\frac{15}{6}=\frac{5}{2}$, whose remainder is $\frac{1}{2}$, so it equals $2\frac{1}{2}$.
