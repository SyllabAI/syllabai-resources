# Rounding Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 1 · 23 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** If the digit to the right is 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4, what happens?

**Back:** Round down.

**Choices:**

- Round down. ✔
  - _why:_ A following digit from 0 to 4 is below the halfway point, so the rounding digit stays the same and the number rounds down.
- Round up.
  - _why:_ Rounding up for digits below 5 reverses the standard rule. A following digit from 0 to 4 means the number should round down.
- Round to 5.
  - _why:_ Five is the threshold for choosing a direction, not a value to which every number is rounded. Digits from 0 to 4 require rounding down.
- Round to an even digit.
  - _why:_ Rounding to an even digit is associated with a different tie-breaking convention, not ordinary GCSE rounding. A following digit below 5 means round down.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** If the digit to the right is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9, what happens?

**Back:** Round up.

**Choices:**

- Round up. ✔
  - _why:_ A following digit from 5 to 9 reaches or exceeds the halfway point, so the rounding digit increases by one and the number rounds up.
- Round down.
  - _why:_ Rounding down for digits of 5 or more reverses the standard rule. A following digit from 5 to 9 means the number should round up.
- Round to 5.
  - _why:_ Five is the threshold that determines the rounding direction, not the final digit required. Any following digit from 5 to 9 means round up.
- Round to an odd digit.
  - _why:_ Ordinary rounding does not choose an odd final digit. The deciding factor is whether the following digit is at least 5, in which case you round up.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** For the nearest hundred, look at the [ ___ ] digit.

**Back:** For the nearest hundred, look at the tens digit.

**Choices:**

- tens ✔
  - _why:_ The tens digit is immediately to the right of the hundreds digit, so it determines whether the hundreds digit rounds up or down.
- hundreds
  - _why:_ The hundreds digit is the place being rounded, not the deciding digit. For the nearest hundred, inspect the tens digit immediately to its right.
- units
  - _why:_ The units digit is too far to the right to decide rounding to the nearest hundred. The tens digit determines the correct direction.
- thousands
  - _why:_ The thousands digit lies to the left of the hundreds place and is not the deciding digit. For the nearest hundred, look at the tens digit.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** A number rounded to the nearest hundred should end in [ ___ ].

**Back:** A number rounded to the nearest hundred should end in two zeros.

**Choices:**

- two zeros ✔
  - _why:_ Every multiple of one hundred has zeros in both the tens and units places, so a number rounded to the nearest hundred ends in two zeros.
- one zero
  - _why:_ Ending in only one zero identifies a multiple of ten, not necessarily a multiple of one hundred. Nearest-hundred answers must end in two zeros.
- three zeros
  - _why:_ Three zeros indicate a multiple of one thousand, which is more restrictive than required. A number rounded to the nearest hundred ends in two zeros.
- two nines
  - _why:_ Nines do not mark multiples of one hundred. Rounding to the nearest hundred makes both the tens and units digits zero, producing two ending zeros.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** When rounding to the nearest thousand, which digits become zeros?

**Back:** Hundreds, tens and units.

**Choices:**

- Hundreds, tens and units. ✔
  - _why:_ When rounding to the nearest thousand, every place value to the right of the thousands digit is replaced by zero.
- Tens and units.
  - _why:_ This describes the digits replaced when rounding to the nearest hundred. For the nearest thousand, the hundreds, tens and units digits all become zeros.
- Thousands, hundreds and tens.
  - _why:_ The thousands digit is retained or increased, rather than changed to zero. The hundreds, tens and units digits become zeros.
- Hundreds and tens.
  - _why:_ This misses the units digit, which must also become zero. Rounding to the nearest thousand replaces the hundreds, tens and units digits.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** When rounding to decimal places, start counting [ ___ ].

**Back:** When rounding to decimal places, start counting after the decimal point.

**Choices:**

- after the decimal point ✔
  - _why:_ Decimal places are the positions to the right of the decimal point, beginning with tenths, followed by hundredths and thousandths.
- before the decimal point
  - _why:_ Positions before the decimal point are whole-number place values such as units and tens. Decimal places are counted after the decimal point.
- from the leftmost digit
  - _why:_ Starting at the leftmost digit counts significant figures rather than decimal places. For decimal places, counting begins after the decimal point.
- at the decimal point
  - _why:_ The decimal point separates place values but is not itself a decimal place. Counting starts after the decimal point, with the tenths digit.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** Significant figures start from the first [ ___ ] digit.

**Back:** Significant figures start from the first non-zero digit.

**Choices:**

- non-zero ✔
  - _why:_ Leading zeros only locate the decimal point and do not count as significant figures. Therefore, counting begins at the first digit that is not zero.
- zero
  - _why:_ Leading zeros are placeholders rather than significant figures. The correct term is non-zero because significant figures begin at the first digit with a non-zero value.
- decimal
  - _why:_ The decimal point does not determine where significant figures begin. Counting starts from the first non-zero digit, whether it occurs before or after the decimal point.
- final
  - _why:_ Significant figures are counted from the beginning of the meaningful digits, not backwards from the final digit. The correct term is non-zero.
