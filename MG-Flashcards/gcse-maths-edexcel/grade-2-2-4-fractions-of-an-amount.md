**Back:** 100

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ The amount is the original whole being divided into fractional parts. Here, 100 is the amount from which $\frac{3}{5}$ is being found.
- 60
  - _why:_ 60 is the result of calculating $\frac{3}{5}$ of 100, not the original amount. The amount stated in the question is 100.
- 20
  - _why:_ 20 is one fifth of 100, found by dividing by the denominator. The question asks for the original amount, which is 100.
- 5
  - _why:_ 5 is the denominator, which shows the number of equal parts. It is not the amount; the original amount given is 100.

**Back:** The denominator tells you how many equal parts to split the amount into.

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ A denominator shows how many equal parts make up the whole. For example, the denominator 5 means splitting the amount into five equal parts.
- unequal parts
  - _why:_ Fractions divide a whole into equal, not unequal, parts. The denominator specifies the number of equal parts into which the amount is split.
- whole amounts
  - _why:_ The denominator does not count separate whole amounts. It tells you how many equal parts the single whole amount must be divided into.
- decimal places
  - _why:_ Decimal places describe the positions of digits after a decimal point. A fraction’s denominator instead gives the number of equal parts in the whole.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** To find $\frac{a}{b}$ of an amount, what do you do with the denominator?

**Back:** Divide the amount by it first.

**Choices:**

- Divide the amount by it first. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the amount by the denominator finds one equal part. You can then multiply that result by the numerator to take the required number of parts.
- Multiply the amount by it first.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the denominator makes the amount larger rather than splitting it into equal parts. You must divide the amount by the denominator first.
- Divide the numerator by it first.
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator by the denominator only converts the fraction into another form. To find a fraction of an amount, divide the amount by the denominator first.
- Subtract it from the amount first.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the denominator does not split the amount into equal parts. The correct first step is to divide the amount by the denominator.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find a fraction of an amount: [ ___ ] first, then [ ___ ].

**Back:** To find a fraction of an amount: divide by the denominator first, then multiply by the numerator.

**Choices:**

- To find a fraction of an amount: divide by the denominator first, then multiply by the numerator. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing by the denominator finds one equal part, and multiplying by the numerator selects the required number of those parts. This gives the fraction of the amount.
- To find a fraction of an amount: multiply by the denominator first, then divide by the numerator.
  - _why:_ This reverses both operations and generally increases the amount incorrectly. Divide by the denominator first to find one part, then multiply by the numerator.
- To find a fraction of an amount: multiply by the numerator first, then divide by the denominator.
  - _why:_ Although this order can produce the same value mathematically, it does not follow the taught denominator-first method stated here. Divide by the denominator first, then multiply by the numerator.
- To find a fraction of an amount: divide by the numerator first, then multiply by the denominator.
  - _why:_ This confuses the roles of the two fraction numbers and effectively uses the reciprocal. Divide by the denominator first, then multiply by the numerator.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** For a unit fraction of an amount, what operation is needed?

**Back:** Divide by the denominator.

**Choices:**

- Divide by the denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ A unit fraction has a numerator of 1, so only one equal part is needed. Dividing the amount by the denominator finds that single part.
- Multiply by the denominator.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the denominator increases the amount rather than finding one equal part. For a unit fraction, divide the amount by the denominator.
- Divide by the numerator.
  - _why:_ The numerator of a unit fraction is always 1, so dividing by it leaves the amount unchanged. Instead, divide by the denominator.
- Multiply by the numerator.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by the numerator 1 leaves the original amount unchanged. To find a unit fraction of it, divide by the denominator.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Back:** Check your division.

  - _why:_ A positive fraction less than 1 must produce a result smaller than the original positive amount. An answer above 360 suggests that the required division was incorrect.
