# Reverse Percentages Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 5 · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In reverse percentages, the percentage change is based on which amount?

**Back:** The original amount.

**Choices:**

- The original amount. ✔
  - _why:_ Percentage change is calculated as a proportion of the original amount. Reverse percentage calculations therefore reconstruct the original by dividing the final amount by the appropriate multiplier.
- The new amount.
  - _why:_ Using the new amount as the percentage base reverses the relationship incorrectly. The percentage change is based on the original amount, before any increase or decrease occurs.
- The amount of change.
  - _why:_ The amount of change is found from the percentage and the original value; it is not the base itself. The percentage change is based on the original amount.
- The percentage multiplier.
  - _why:_ The multiplier represents the combined original proportion and change, rather than the amount used as the percentage base. Percentage change is always based on the original amount.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** A price increases by 8%. What multiplier is used?

**Back:** 1.08

**Choices:**

- 1.08 ✔
  - _why:_ An 8% increase means the new price is 108% of the original. Converting 108% to a decimal gives the multiplier $1.08$.
- 1.8
  - _why:_ This treats 8% as 80%, producing an 80% increase. An 8% increase leaves 108% of the original, so the correct multiplier is $1.08$.
- 0.92
  - _why:_ Subtracting 8% from 100% gives the multiplier for an 8% decrease. An increase requires adding 8%, giving 108% or $1.08$.
- 1.008
  - _why:_ This converts 8% as though it were 0.8%, placing the decimal one position too far left. Adding $0.08$ to 1 gives $1.08$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** A price decreases by 20%. What multiplier is used?

**Back:** 0.80

**Choices:**

- 0.80 ✔
  - _why:_ A 20% decrease leaves 80% of the original price. Converting 80% to a decimal gives the multiplier $0.80$.
- 1.20
  - _why:_ Adding 20% to 100% gives the multiplier for an increase, not a decrease. A 20% decrease leaves 80%, so the multiplier is $0.80$.
- 0.20
  - _why:_ This uses the proportion removed rather than the proportion remaining. After a 20% decrease, 80% remains, so the required multiplier is $0.80$.
- 0.98
  - _why:_ This treats 20% as 2%, a decimal-place conversion error. Subtracting $0.20$ from 1 gives the correct decrease multiplier of $0.80$.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a percentage increase, the multiplier is [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a percentage increase, the multiplier is bigger than 1.

**Choices:**

- bigger than 1 ✔
  - _why:_ An increase leaves more than 100% of the original amount. Since 100% corresponds to 1, any percentage increase has a multiplier bigger than 1.
- smaller than 1
  - _why:_ A multiplier smaller than 1 leaves less than the original and therefore represents a percentage decrease. An increase requires a multiplier bigger than 1.
- equal to 1
  - _why:_ A multiplier equal to 1 leaves the amount unchanged because it represents exactly 100%. A percentage increase needs a multiplier bigger than 1.
- smaller than 0
  - _why:_ A negative multiplier would generally produce a negative result, not an ordinary percentage increase. An increase produces more than 100% of the original, so its multiplier is bigger than 1.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a percentage decrease, the multiplier is [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a percentage decrease, the multiplier is smaller than 1.

**Choices:**

- smaller than 1 ✔
  - _why:_ A decrease leaves less than 100% of the original amount. Since 100% corresponds to 1, the multiplier for a percentage decrease is smaller than 1.
- bigger than 1
  - _why:_ A multiplier bigger than 1 produces more than the original amount and therefore represents an increase. A decrease requires a multiplier smaller than 1.
- equal to 1
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 1 leaves the amount unchanged, so it represents neither an increase nor a decrease. A percentage decrease uses a multiplier smaller than 1.
- smaller than 0
  - _why:_ A standard percentage decrease leaves a non-negative proportion of the original, not a negative amount. Its multiplier is smaller than 1 but normally at least 0.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** A 25% discount leaves what percentage of the original price?

**Back:** 75%

**Choices:**

- 75% ✔
  - _why:_ A 25% discount removes 25% of the original price. Subtracting this from 100% leaves 75% of the original price.
- 25%
  - _why:_ This is the percentage removed by the discount, not the percentage remaining. Subtracting 25% from 100% shows that 75% of the original price is left.

  - _why:_ This would be the proportion remaining after a 20% discount, confusing 25% with one fifth. A 25% discount leaves $100\%-25\%=75\%$.

  - _why:_ Adding 25% gives the result of a 25% increase rather than a discount. A discount subtracts 25% from 100%, leaving 75%.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** The forward percentage formula is [ ___ ].

**Back:** The forward percentage formula is final = original × multiplier.

**Choices:**

- final = original × multiplier ✔
  - _why:_ A percentage multiplier converts the original amount directly into the final amount, so multiplying the original by the appropriate multiplier gives the value after the percentage change.
- final = original ÷ multiplier
  - _why:_ Dividing reverses a percentage change rather than applying it forwards. The forward formula is final = original × multiplier because the multiplier represents the proportion of the original remaining.
- original = final × multiplier
  - _why:_ This incorrectly multiplies the final amount when trying to identify the original. The forward relationship starts with the original: final = original × multiplier.
- final = multiplier ÷ original
  - _why:_ This reverses the division and does not produce an amount with meaningful units. The correct forward formula is final = original × multiplier.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find the original from a final amount, use [ ___ ].

**Back:** To find the original from a final amount, use original = final ÷ multiplier.

**Choices:**

- original = final ÷ multiplier ✔
  - _why:_ The forward calculation multiplies the original by the multiplier, so its inverse divides the final amount by that same multiplier to recover the original.
- original = final × multiplier
  - _why:_ Multiplying repeats the percentage change instead of reversing it. To recover the starting value, use original = final ÷ multiplier.
- original = multiplier ÷ final
  - _why:_ This reverses the order of the division, producing a small ratio rather than the original amount. The final amount must be divided by the multiplier.
- original = final ÷ percentage
  - _why:_ Dividing by the stated percentage confuses the percentage change with the multiplier. Reverse percentages require original = final ÷ multiplier.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** If a final amount is given after a percentage change, what do you divide by?

**Back:** Divide by the multiplier.

**Choices:**

- Divide by the multiplier. ✔
  - _why:_ The final amount equals the original multiplied by the percentage multiplier, so dividing the final amount by that multiplier reverses the change and finds the original.
- Divide by the percentage change.
  - _why:_ The percentage change alone represents only the increase or decrease, not the final proportion. Divide by the multiplier, such as 1.05 or 0.80, to find the original.
- Divide by the original amount.
  - _why:_ The original amount is the unknown being calculated, so it cannot be the divisor. Divide the given final amount by the multiplier to recover the original.
- Divide by the final amount.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the final amount would produce a ratio rather than the original value. The final amount must instead be divided by the percentage multiplier.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A flat increases by 5% and is now £210,000. What was the original value?

**Back:** £200,000

**Choices:**

- £200,000 ✔
  - _why:_ A 5% increase uses the multiplier 1.05. Dividing £210,000 by 1.05 gives £200,000, which is correctly smaller than the increased final value.
- £199,500
  - _why:_ Subtracting 5% of £210,000 treats the final value as the base, producing £199,500. Reverse the increase by calculating £210,000 ÷ 1.05 = £200,000.
- £220,500
  - _why:_ Adding another 5% applies a second increase and makes the value larger. The original is found by dividing £210,000 by 1.05, giving £200,000.
- £210,000
  - _why:_ This simply repeats the final value and ignores the 5% increase. Dividing £210,000 by the multiplier 1.05 gives the original value of £200,000.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** A game is reduced by 20% to £28. What was the normal price?

**Back:** £35

**Choices:**

- £35 ✔
  - _why:_ A 20% reduction leaves 80% of the normal price, giving a multiplier of 0.8. Therefore, £28 ÷ 0.8 = £35.
- £33.60
  - _why:_ Adding 20% of £28 uses the reduced price as the percentage base. Since £28 represents 80% of the normal price, calculate £28 ÷ 0.8 = £35.
- £22.40
  - _why:_ Taking 20% off £28 applies the discount a second time rather than reversing it. Divide £28 by 0.8 to obtain the normal price of £35.
- £140
  - _why:_ Dividing by 0.2 treats £28 as the 20% discount rather than the remaining 80%. The correct calculation is £28 ÷ 0.8 = £35.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** After an increase, should the original be smaller or bigger than the final amount?

**Back:** Smaller than the final amount.

**Choices:**

- Smaller than the final amount. ✔
  - _why:_ An increase raises the original value to produce the final value. Therefore, the amount before the increase must be smaller than the amount afterwards.
- Bigger than the final amount.
  - _why:_ This reverses the direction of an increase. Because the final amount has grown from the original, the original must be smaller than the final amount.
- Equal to the final amount.
  - _why:_ Equal values would mean no change had occurred. After a genuine percentage increase, the original must be smaller than the final amount.
- Double the final amount.
  - _why:_ An increase does not generally imply that the starting value was double the result. The original must simply be smaller than the increased final amount.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If a bill increased by 4% and the increase was £32, what is £32 as a percentage of the original?

**Back:** 4% of the original.

**Choices:**

- 4% of the original. ✔
  - _why:_ The stated 4% increase equals £32, so £32 represents 4% of the original bill. The original amount would therefore be £32 divided by 0.04.
- 96% of the original.
  - _why:_ This confuses the remaining proportion with the increase. The £32 is the 4% increase itself, so it represents 4% of the original bill, not 96%.
- 104% of the original.
  - _why:_ This treats the increase as the whole new bill. The new bill is 104% of the original, but £32 is only the increase, equal to 4% of the original.
- 0.04% of the original.
  - _why:_ This converts 4% to the decimal 0.04 but incorrectly retains the percent sign. The £32 increase is 4% of the original bill.

**Back:** £60

**Choices:**

- £60 ✔
  - _why:_ The £9 discount represents 15% of the normal price. Dividing £9 by 0.15 gives a normal price of £60.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** It is wrong to add 20% of the sale price after a 20% reduction because the 20% was taken from the [ ___ ].

**Back:** It is wrong to add 20% of the sale price after a 20% reduction because the 20% was taken from the original price.

**Choices:**

- original price ✔
  - _why:_ A percentage reduction is calculated from the price before the reduction. Therefore, the removed 20% is 20% of the original price, not of the lower sale price.
- sale price
  - _why:_ The sale price is the amount remaining after the reduction, so it is only 80% of the starting amount. The 20% reduction was calculated from the original price.
- discount amount
  - _why:_ The discount amount is the value removed, not the base from which the percentage was calculated. The 20% reduction was taken from the original price.
- increased price
  - _why:_ There is no increased price in a reduction calculation; the price falls from its initial value. The 20% was calculated using the original price as the base.
