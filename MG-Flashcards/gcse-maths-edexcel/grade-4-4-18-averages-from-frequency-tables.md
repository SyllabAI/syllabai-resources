## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In a frequency table, what should you add to find the total number of items?

**Back:** Add the frequencies, not the values.

**Choices:**

- Add the frequencies, not the values. ✔
  - _why:_ Each frequency counts how many items have that value. Adding all the frequencies therefore gives the total number of items represented by the table.
- Add the values, not the frequencies.
  - _why:_ Adding the listed values ignores how many times each one occurs. The total number of items is found by adding the frequencies, not the values.
- Multiply the frequencies, not the values.
  - _why:_ Multiplying frequencies does not count the observations and can produce a very large, meaningless result. Add the frequencies to obtain the total number of items.
- Add the value-frequency products, not the values.
  - _why:_ Adding value-frequency products finds the total of all data values, which is used for the mean. The number of items is found by adding the frequencies.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Points frequencies are 3, 5, 4, 2. How many games were played?

**Back:** 14 games.

**Choices:**

- 14 games. ✔
  - _why:_ The frequencies count the games in each points category, so the total is $3+5+4+2=14$. Therefore, 14 games were played.
- 12 games.
  - _why:_ This total misses one of the frequency groups. All four frequencies must be added: $3+5+4+2=14$, so 14 games were played.
- 10 games.
  - _why:_ This comes from adding only the first three frequencies and omitting the final frequency of 2. Including every row gives $3+5+4+2=14$ games.
- 16 games.
  - _why:_ This likely double-counts the final frequency of 2. Each frequency is included once, giving $3+5+4+2=14$ games in total.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** How many times a value, score, or group occurs is its [ ___ ].

**Back:** How many times a value, score, or group occurs is its frequency.

**Choices:**

- frequency ✔
  - _why:_ Frequency is the number of times a particular value, score, or group appears in a data set. It is the count recorded for that category.
- range
  - _why:_ The range measures the spread of data by subtracting the lowest value from the highest. The number of times a value occurs is its frequency.
- median
  - _why:_ The median is the middle value after the data are ordered. The count of how many times a value or group occurs is its frequency.
- interval
  - _why:_ An interval is a span of values used to group data. The number of observations within a value or group is called its frequency.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** For the mean from an exact frequency table, what is multiplied before adding?

**Back:** Multiply each value by its frequency.

**Choices:**

- Multiply each value by its frequency. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying each value by its frequency gives the total contribution from every occurrence of that value. These products are then added before dividing by total frequency.
- Multiply each value by the total frequency.
  - _why:_ Using the total frequency for every value treats each score as if it occurred equally often. Each value must instead be multiplied by its own frequency.
- Multiply each frequency by itself.
  - _why:_ Squaring frequencies does not include the data values and therefore cannot produce the data total. Multiply each value by its corresponding frequency before adding.
- Multiply each value by the next frequency.
  - _why:_ Pairing a value with the next row’s frequency mismatches the data. Each value must be multiplied by the frequency recorded in the same row.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** For exact frequency data, mean = total of [ ___ ] divided by total [ ___ ].

**Back:** For exact frequency data, mean = total of value-frequency products divided by total frequency.

**Choices:**

- For exact frequency data, mean = total of value-frequency products divided by total frequency. ✔
  - _why:_ Value-frequency products give the total of all observations, while total frequency gives the number of observations. Dividing the first total by the second gives the mean.
- For exact frequency data, mean = total of values divided by total frequency.
  - _why:_ Adding each listed value once ignores repeated observations. The numerator must be the total of value-frequency products, divided by the total frequency.
- For exact frequency data, mean = total of value-frequency products divided by total number of rows.
  - _why:_ The number of rows counts distinct values, not observations. The total of value-frequency products must be divided by total frequency to calculate the mean.
- For exact frequency data, mean = total of frequencies divided by total values.
  - _why:_ This reverses and omits the required quantities. The mean is the total of value-frequency products divided by the total frequency.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is adding the different scores and dividing by the number of rows wrong?

**Back:** Frequencies show some scores occurred more often.

**Choices:**

- Frequencies show some scores occurred more often. ✔
  - _why:_ Different frequencies mean that some scores appear more times than others. The mean must account for this by weighting each score using its frequency.
- Values show some scores are larger than others.
  - _why:_ Differences between score sizes do not explain the error. Adding rows once ignores repetition; frequencies show how often each score occurred and must be used as weights.
- Rows each represent the same number of results.
  - _why:_ Rows do not necessarily represent equal numbers of results because their frequencies may differ. The frequencies show that some scores occurred more often than others.
- Products make every score occur exactly once.
  - _why:_ Value-frequency products account for repeated scores rather than making each occur once. Frequencies show how often scores occurred, so simply averaging the listed rows is wrong.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** If the product total is 49 and total frequency is 27, what is the mean to 1 decimal place?

**Back:** 1.8

**Choices:**

- 1.8 ✔
  - _why:_ The mean is $49\div27=1.814\ldots$. The hundredths digit is 1, so rounding to one decimal place gives 1.8.
- 2.0
  - _why:_ This rounds $49\div27=1.814\ldots$ to the nearest whole number before writing a decimal place. Rounding directly to one decimal place gives 1.8.
- 0.6
  - _why:_ This results from reversing the division and calculating $27\div49$. For a frequency-table mean, divide the product total by total frequency: $49\div27=1.8$ to one decimal place.
- 1.9
  - _why:_ The value $49\div27$ is $1.814\ldots$, and the hundredths digit is 1, not 5 or more. It therefore rounds down to 1.8.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** A row with frequency 0 contributes [ ___ ] to the total.

**Back:** A row with frequency 0 contributes nothing to the total.

**Choices:**

- nothing ✔
  - _why:_ A frequency of 0 means that the value occurs no times. Its value-frequency product is therefore 0, so the row adds nothing to the total.
- its value
  - _why:_ The value is not included once when its frequency is 0. Multiplying the value by 0 gives 0, so the row contributes nothing.
- its frequency
  - _why:_ The frequency is used as a multiplier, not added as the row’s contribution. Since the frequency is 0, the value-frequency product contributes nothing.
- one item
  - _why:_ A frequency of 0 means there are no items in that row, not one item. Its value-frequency product is 0, so it contributes nothing.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** If 2 goals were scored in $x$ matches, what is that row’s goal total?

**Back:** $2x$ goals.

**Choices:**

- $2x$ goals. ✔
  - _why:_ Each of the $x$ matches contributes 2 goals, so multiplying frequency by goals per match gives a row total of $2 \times x=2x$ goals.
- $2+x$ goals.
  - _why:_ Adding the number of goals to the number of matches confuses repeated values with a sum. Two goals in each of $x$ matches gives $2 \times x=2x$ goals.
- $\frac{x}{2}$ goals.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 would halve the number of matches rather than total the goals. The row total is frequency multiplied by value, so it is $2x$ goals.
- $x^2$ goals.
  - _why:_ Squaring the number of matches does not use the 2 goals scored per match correctly. Multiplying 2 by the frequency $x$ gives $2x$ goals.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Known goal total is 23, total goals are 39, and the unknown row contributes $2x$. What equation finds $x$?

**Back:** $23+2x=39$

**Choices:**

- $23+2x=39$ ✔
  - _why:_ The known rows contribute 23 goals and the unknown row contributes $2x$ goals. Their sum must equal the overall total of 39 goals.
- $23+x=39$
  - _why:_ This treats $x$ as the row’s goal total, forgetting that each of those matches contributes 2 goals. The unknown contribution is $2x$, giving $23+2x=39$.
- $23+2x=62$
  - _why:_ Adding 23 and 39 to form the right-hand side double-counts the known contribution. The known and unknown contributions together equal 39, so $23+2x=39$.
- $39+2x=23$
  - _why:_ This reverses the total and the known contribution. The smaller known total of 23 is increased by $2x$ to reach 39, so $23+2x=39$.

**Front:** Solve $23+2x=39$.

**Back:** $x=8$

**Choices:**

- $x=8$ ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting 23 from both sides gives $2x=16$. Dividing both sides by 2 then gives $x=8$.
- $x=16$
  - _why:_ This stops after subtracting 23, when the equation is $2x=16$. Dividing by the coefficient 2 is still required, giving $x=8$.
- $x=7$
  - _why:_ This comes from an incorrect subtraction of 23 from 39. The correct difference is 16, so $2x=16$ and therefore $x=8$.
- $x=31$
  - _why:_ This does not isolate the $2x$ term correctly. Subtract 23 from 39 to obtain 16, then divide by 2 to get $x=8$.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** The mode is the value with the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The mode is the value with the highest frequency.

**Choices:**

- highest frequency ✔
  - _why:_ The mode is the data value that occurs most often, so it is identified by the highest frequency in a frequency table.
- lowest frequency
  - _why:_ The lowest frequency identifies the value occurring least often, not the mode. The mode is the value with the highest frequency.
- greatest value
  - _why:_ The largest data value is not necessarily the one occurring most often. The mode depends on frequency, so it is the value with the highest frequency.
- highest cumulative frequency
  - _why:_ Cumulative frequency increases towards the total and does not identify the most common value directly. The mode is found using the highest ordinary frequency.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** For 32 values, which positions locate the median?

**Back:** The 16th and 17th values.

**Choices:**

- The 16th and 17th values. ✔
  - _why:_ An even-sized set has two central positions. For 32 ordered values, these are positions $32\div2=16$ and $16+1=17$.
- The 16th value.
  - _why:_ Using only the 16th value applies the odd-data rule incorrectly. Since 32 is even, the median is located using both the 16th and 17th values.
- The 17th value.
  - _why:_ Using only the 17th value ignores the other central position. For 32 ordered values, the middle lies between the 16th and 17th values.
- The 15th and 16th values.
  - _why:_ These positions are shifted one place too low. Half of 32 is 16, so the two central positions are the 16th and 17th values.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Why use cumulative frequency to find the median?

**Back:** It locates the middle value without writing the list.

**Choices:**

- It locates the middle value without writing the list. ✔
  - _why:_ Cumulative frequency shows which row contains the central position or positions, allowing the median to be found without expanding the frequency table into an ordered list.
- It locates the most frequent value without writing the list.
  - _why:_ The most frequent value is the mode and is found from the largest ordinary frequency. Cumulative frequency is used to locate the middle value for the median.
- It calculates the exact mean without writing the list.
  - _why:_ The mean requires the sum of the values and their frequencies, not cumulative frequency. Cumulative frequency instead locates the middle value needed for the median.
- It identifies the largest value without writing the list.
  - _why:_ The largest value can be read from the final non-zero row and does not require cumulative frequency. Cumulative totals are useful for locating the median’s middle position.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Cumulative frequencies are up to 0 goals = 5 and up to 1 goal = 17. Where are the 16th and 17th values?

**Back:** In the 1-goal row.

**Choices:**

- In the 1-goal row. ✔
  - _why:_ Positions 1 to 5 are in the 0-goal row, while positions 6 to 17 are in the 1-goal row. Therefore, both central values are 1.
- In the 0-goal row.
  - _why:_ The 0-goal row contains only positions 1 to 5 because its cumulative frequency is 5. Positions 16 and 17 fall in the 1-goal row.
- In the 2-goal row.
  - _why:_ The cumulative frequency has already reached 17 by the end of the 1-goal row. Therefore, positions 16 and 17 occur before the 2-goal row.
- Across the 0-goal and 1-goal rows.
  - _why:_ The boundary between these rows is after position 5, not between positions 16 and 17. Both positions lie within the 1-goal row.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** For grouped data, the mean is only an [ ___ ] because exact values are [ ___ ].

**Back:** For grouped data, the mean is only an estimate because exact values are unknown.

**Choices:**

- For grouped data, the mean is only an estimate because exact values are unknown. ✔
  - _why:_ Grouped data gives intervals rather than individual observations, so calculations normally use class midpoints. Because the original exact values are unknown, the resulting mean is an estimate.
- For grouped data, the mean is only an exact value because exact values are unknown.
  - _why:_ Unknown individual values prevent an exact calculation, even though class midpoints can represent each interval. The resulting mean is therefore an estimate, not an exact value.
- For grouped data, the mean is only an estimate because exact values are known.
  - _why:_ If every exact value were known, the mean could be calculated exactly. Grouped tables provide only intervals, so exact values are unknown and the mean is an estimate.
- For grouped data, the mean is only an exact value because exact values are known.
  - _why:_ This describes ungrouped raw data rather than grouped data. In grouped data the individual values are unknown, so midpoints are used and the mean is only an estimate.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** How should you find each midpoint in grouped data?

**Back:** Use $\frac{\text{lower}+\text{upper}}{2}$ for that interval.

**Choices:**

- Use $\frac{\text{lower}+\text{upper}}{2}$ for that interval. ✔
  - _why:_ The midpoint is halfway between the interval boundaries, so add the lower and upper values and divide their sum by 2.
- Use $\frac{\text{upper}-\text{lower}}{2}$ for that interval.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the boundaries and halving gives half the class width, not its midpoint. Add the lower and upper boundaries, then divide by 2.
- Use $\frac{\text{lower}+\text{upper}}{\text{upper}}$ for that interval.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the upper boundary does not locate the centre of an interval. The midpoint is the sum of both boundaries divided by 2.
- Use $\frac{\text{lower}+\text{upper}}{\text{lower}}$ for that interval.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the lower boundary produces a ratio rather than the interval’s central value. Add both boundaries and divide the result by 2.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the midpoint of over 10, up to 15 minutes?

**Back:** 12.5 minutes.

**Choices:**

- 12.5 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ The midpoint is halfway between 10 and 15, so $(10+15)\div2=12.5$ minutes.
- 2.5 minutes.
  - _why:_ This is half the class width, found from $(15-10)\div2$, rather than the midpoint. The midpoint is $(10+15)\div2=12.5$ minutes.
- 10.5 minutes.
  - _why:_ Adding $0.5$ to the lower boundary does not find the centre of a five-minute interval. Averaging 10 and 15 gives 12.5 minutes.
- 15.5 minutes.
  - _why:_ Adding $0.5$ to the upper boundary places the value outside the interval. The average of the two boundaries is 12.5 minutes.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** For grouped data, estimated mean = total of [ ___ ] divided by total [ ___ ].

**Back:** For grouped data, estimated mean = total of midpoint-frequency products divided by total frequency.

**Choices:**

- For grouped data, estimated mean = total of midpoint-frequency products divided by total frequency. ✔
  - _why:_ Each class midpoint represents its observations, so multiplying each midpoint by its frequency estimates the total. Dividing the product total by total frequency gives the estimated mean.
- For grouped data, estimated mean = total of class widths divided by total frequency.
  - _why:_ Class widths describe interval sizes, not the estimated total of the data. Use the total of midpoint-frequency products divided by total frequency.
- For grouped data, estimated mean = total of midpoint-frequency products divided by total class width.
  - _why:_ Dividing by total class width ignores how many observations there are. The midpoint-frequency product total must be divided by total frequency.
- For grouped data, estimated mean = total of midpoints divided by total number of classes.
  - _why:_ Averaging the class midpoints gives every class equal importance, even when frequencies differ. Weight each midpoint by frequency, then divide by total frequency.

**Back:** Over 20, up to 30 minutes.

**Choices:**

- Over 20, up to 30 minutes. ✔
  - _why:_ For 40 people, the median lies around the 20th observation. Cumulative frequency rises from 14 to 25 in this class, so it contains the median.
- Over 10, up to 20 minutes.
  - _why:_ This class contains observations 6 to 14, so it does not contain the 20th observation. The median class is over 20, up to 30 minutes.
- Over 30, up to 40 minutes.
  - _why:_ Choosing a class after cumulative frequency 25 places the median too late in the data. The 20th observation lies over 20, up to 30 minutes.
- Over 0, up to 10 minutes.
  - _why:_ This first class contains only observations 1 to 5, not the middle observation. The 20th observation lies over 20, up to 30 minutes.
