## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the B in BIDMAS stand for?

**Back:** Brackets.

**Choices:**

- Brackets. ✔
  - _why:_ The B in BIDMAS stands for brackets. Any calculation inside brackets is completed before indices, multiplication, division, addition or subtraction.
- Bases.
  - _why:_ A base is the number raised to a power, which may appear when working with indices. The B in BIDMAS stands for brackets.
- Binomials.
  - _why:_ A binomial is an algebraic expression containing two terms, not a stage in the order of operations. The B in BIDMAS stands for brackets.
- Bisectors.
  - _why:_ Bisectors divide angles or lines into equal parts and are unrelated to the order of operations. The B in BIDMAS stands for brackets.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the I in BIDMAS stand for?

**Back:** Indices.

**Choices:**

- Indices. ✔
  - _why:_ The I in BIDMAS stands for indices, meaning powers such as squares and cubes. Indices are evaluated after brackets but before the four basic operations.
- Inverses.
  - _why:_ Inverse operations undo one another, but they are not represented by I in BIDMAS. The I stands for indices, which are powers such as squares and cubes.
- Integers.
  - _why:_ Integers are whole numbers, including positive numbers, negative numbers and zero. They are not an operation in BIDMAS; the I stands for indices.
- Inequalities.
  - _why:_ Inequalities compare quantities using symbols such as $$. They are not part of the BIDMAS order; the I stands for indices.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In BIDMAS, what must be done before everything else?

**Back:** Brackets.

**Choices:**

- Brackets. ✔
  - _why:_ Calculations inside brackets are completed first because brackets can change how the rest of an expression is grouped. Indices and the other operations follow afterwards.
- Indices.
  - _why:_ Indices come before multiplication, division, addition and subtraction, but not before brackets. Calculations inside brackets must be completed first.
- Multiplication.
  - _why:_ Multiplication is completed after brackets and indices. Choosing it first overlooks the opening stages of BIDMAS, so brackets must be handled before everything else.
- Addition.
  - _why:_ Reading an expression from left to right can make addition seem first, but BIDMAS takes priority. Brackets must be completed before all other operations.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** In BIDMAS, what comes after brackets?

**Back:** Indices.

**Choices:**

- Indices. ✔
  - _why:_ Indices are evaluated immediately after brackets in BIDMAS. This includes powers such as squares and cubes, which come before multiplication and division.
- Division.
  - _why:_ Division has equal priority with multiplication, but both come after indices. Once brackets are completed, the next BIDMAS stage is indices.
- Multiplication.
  - _why:_ Multiplication does not follow brackets immediately because indices must be evaluated first. Multiplication and division are handled only after brackets and indices.
- Addition.
  - _why:_ Addition is one of the final BIDMAS operations, alongside subtraction. Indices come directly after brackets and before multiplication, division, addition and subtraction.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** In BIDMAS, multiplication and [ ___ ] have equal priority.

**Back:** In BIDMAS, multiplication and division have equal priority.

**Choices:**

- division ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplication and division have equal priority in BIDMAS. When both occur in the same expression, they are completed from left to right.
- addition
  - _why:_ Addition has lower priority than multiplication and is paired with subtraction instead. Multiplication has equal priority with division, and those operations are handled first.
- subtraction
  - _why:_ Subtraction has equal priority with addition, not multiplication. In BIDMAS, multiplication and division form the equal-priority pair.
- indices
  - _why:_ Indices have higher priority than multiplication and are completed before it. Multiplication instead has equal priority with division.

**Choices:**

- multiplication
  - _why:_ Multiplication has higher priority than addition and is paired with division. Addition has equal priority with subtraction, which is handled from left to right.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** If a calculation has only one operation, how do you work it out?

**Back:** Work it out normally.

**Choices:**

- Work it out normally. ✔
  - _why:_ With only one operation to perform, there is no conflict for BIDMAS to resolve. Apply that operation normally to the numbers given.
- Work it out backwards.
  - _why:_ Reversing the calculation can change the result, especially for subtraction and division. With only one operation, apply that operation normally in the order shown.
- Work it out using the inverse.
  - _why:_ An inverse operation undoes the stated operation rather than carrying it out. When only one operation is present, perform that operation normally.
- Work it out as multiplication.
  - _why:_ Multiplication should not replace whichever operation is actually shown. A calculation containing only one operation is worked out normally using that stated operation.

**Choices:**

- $6 \times 4$. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplication has higher priority than addition in BIDMAS, so $6 \times 4$ is calculated first. The result is then added to $3$.
- $3 + 6$.
  - _why:_ Working strictly from left to right ignores BIDMAS. Multiplication has priority over addition, so $6 \times 4$ must be completed first.

- $3 \times 4$.
  - _why:_ The numbers $3$ and $4$ are not joined by a multiplication sign in the expression. The stated multiplication, $6 \times 4$, is completed first.

**Choices:**

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** An index tells you how many times to multiply a number by [ ___ ].

**Back:** An index tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.

**Choices:**

- itself ✔
  - _why:_ An index represents repeated multiplication using the same base number. For example, $4^2$ contains two factors of $4$, so it means $4 \times 4$.
- the index
  - _why:_ A common misconception is to multiply the base by its index, such as interpreting $4^2$ as $4 \times 2$. The base is instead multiplied by itself.
- the answer
  - _why:_ The answer is produced only after the repeated multiplication has been completed. An index tells how many times the base number is used as a factor with itself.
- its double
  - _why:_ An index does not instruct you to double the base. It shows how many copies of the base are multiplied together, meaning the number is multiplied by itself.

  - _why:_ Multiplication and division have equal priority in BIDMAS. When both occur, perform them in the order they appear, working from left to right.

## Card 26 — `basic`

**Front:** Add brackets to make $5 \times 4 + 2 = 30$ correct.

**Back:** $5 \times (4 + 2) = 30$.

**Choices:**

- $5 \times (4 + 2) = 30$. ✔
  - _why:_ The brackets make the addition happen first, so $4 + 2 = 6$. Multiplying the result by $5$ then gives $5 \times 6 = 30$.
- $(5 \times 4) + 2 = 30$.
  - _why:_ These brackets only group the multiplication already completed first under BIDMAS, producing $20 + 2 = 22$. The correct placement is $5 \times (4 + 2) = 30$.
- $(5 \times 4 + 2) = 30$.
  - _why:_ Brackets around the entire expression do not change its order of operations, so its value remains $22$. Bracketing $4 + 2$ instead makes the value $30$.
- $5 \times (4) + 2 = 30$.
  - _why:_ Brackets around a single number have no effect, leaving $5 \times 4 + 2 = 22$. The addition must be grouped as $5 \times (4 + 2) = 30$.

## Card 27 — `basic`

**Front:** Add brackets to make $3 + 2 \times 4 + 5 = 45$ correct.

**Back:** $(3 + 2) \times (4 + 5) = 45$.

**Choices:**

- $(3 + 2) \times (4 + 5) = 45$. ✔
  - _why:_ The brackets create two sums: $3 + 2 = 5$ and $4 + 5 = 9$. Multiplying these results gives $5 \times 9 = 45$.
- $(3 + 2) \times 4 + 5 = 45$.
  - _why:_ Only the first addition is grouped, so the left-hand side becomes $5 \times 4 + 5 = 25$. Both additions must be bracketed to give $45$.
- $3 + 2 \times (4 + 5) = 45$.
  - _why:_ Grouping only $4 + 5$ gives $3 + 2 \times 9 = 21$, not $45$. Grouping $3 + 2$ as well produces $5 \times 9 = 45$.
- $(3 + 2 \times 4) + 5 = 45$.
  - _why:_ These brackets preserve the original BIDMAS order, giving $3 + 8 + 5 = 16$. Separate brackets around both additions are required to create $5 \times 9 = 45$.
