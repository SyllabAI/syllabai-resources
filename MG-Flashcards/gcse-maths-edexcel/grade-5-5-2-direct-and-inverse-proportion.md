## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a unit rate represent?

**Back:** The amount or value for one single unit of a quantity.

**Choices:**

- The amount or value for one single unit of a quantity. ✔
  - _why:_ A unit rate expresses how much corresponds to exactly one unit, such as cartons made per hour or the cost of one apple.
- The total amount or value for all the units of a quantity.
  - _why:_ This describes a total rather than a unit rate. The unit rate is the amount or value corresponding to one single unit of the quantity.
- The difference in value between two separate units of a quantity.
  - _why:_ A unit rate is not found by comparing two individual units. It gives the amount or value for one single unit of the quantity.
- The amount or value for one hundred units of a quantity.
  - _why:_ This confuses a unit rate with a rate per hundred, such as a percentage. A unit rate gives the amount or value for one single unit.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In direct proportion, multiplying one quantity by a scale factor [ ___ ] the other quantity by the same scale factor.

**Back:** In direct proportion, multiplying one quantity by a scale factor multiplies the other quantity by the same scale factor.

**Choices:**

- multiplies ✔
  - _why:_ Directly proportional quantities change by the same scale factor. If one quantity is multiplied by a number, the other quantity is multiplied by that number too.
- divides
  - _why:_ Dividing the other quantity by the scale factor describes inverse proportion. In direct proportion, the same scale factor multiplies both quantities.
- subtracts
  - _why:_ Subtracting a scale factor confuses multiplicative proportional change with additive change. In direct proportion, multiplying one quantity by a scale factor multiplies the other by the same factor.
- squares
  - _why:_ Squaring the other quantity would create a non-linear relationship. In direct proportion, both quantities are multiplied by exactly the same scale factor.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the general algebraic formula for direct proportion?

**Back:** $$y = kx$

**Choices:**

- $$y = kx$ ✔
  - _why:_ In direct proportion, the ratio $y \div x$ is constant. Calling this constant $k$ gives the general equation $y = kx$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x}$
  - _why:_ This is the general formula for inverse proportion, where $xy$ is constant. Direct proportion has a constant ratio $y \div x$, so its formula is $y = kx$.
- $$y = x + k$
  - _why:_ Adding a constant gives a straight line that does not generally pass through the origin. Direct proportion requires $y \div x$ to be constant, giving $y = kx$.
- $$y = kx^2$
  - _why:_ This represents proportionality to the square of $x$, not direct proportion to $x$. The direct proportion formula is $y = kx$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** A machine makes 800 cartons in 4 hours. How do you calculate the unit rate?

**Back:** $$800 \div 4 = 200$ cartons per hour

**Choices:**

- $$800 \div 4 = 200$ cartons per hour ✔
  - _why:_ The unit rate is the output in one hour, so divide 800 cartons by 4 hours. This gives 200 cartons per hour.
- $$800 \times 4 = 3200$ cartons per hour
  - _why:_ Multiplying finds an output for four times as long instead of one hour. Divide 800 cartons by 4 hours to get 200 cartons per hour.
- $$800 \div 2 = 400$ cartons per hour
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 halves the total but does not account for all 4 hours. Divide 800 by 4 to obtain 200 cartons per hour.
- $$800 - 4 = 796$ cartons per hour
  - _why:_ Subtracting hours from cartons mixes unlike quantities and cannot produce a rate. Divide 800 cartons by 4 hours to get 200 cartons per hour.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** 8 apples cost £1.20. Outline the steps to find the cost of 11 apples.

**Back:** $1.20 \div 8$ to find the cost of 1 appleMultiply that result by 11

**Choices:**

- $1.20 \div 8$ to find the cost of 1 appleMultiply that result by 11 ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing £1.20 by 8 finds the cost of one apple. Multiplying that unit cost by 11 then gives the cost of 11 apples.
- $1.20 \div 11$ to find the cost of 1 appleMultiply that result by 8
  - _why:_ This reverses the known and required numbers. Divide the known cost by 8 apples to find one apple, then multiply that cost by 11.
- $1.20 \times 8$ to find the cost of 1 appleDivide that result by 11
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 8 does not find a unit cost. Divide £1.20 by 8 to find one apple’s cost, then multiply by 11.
- $1.20 \div 8$ to find the cost of 1 appleDivide that result by 11
  - _why:_ The first step correctly finds the cost of one apple, but dividing again makes the value smaller. Multiply the unit cost by 11 to price 11 apples.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** The mass of a wire $m$ is $m=25l$. Find length $l$ when $m=175$.

**Back:** $$l = 7$ ($175 \div 25$)

**Choices:**

- $$l = 7$ ($175 \div 25$) ✔
  - _why:_ Substituting $m=175$ gives $175=25l$. Dividing both sides by 25 isolates $l$, so the wire’s length is 7.
- $$l = 4375$ ($175 \times 25$)
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 25 applies the coefficient twice rather than undoing it. Since $175=25l$, divide 175 by 25 to obtain $l=7$.
- $$l = 150$ ($175 - 25$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting 25 treats the coefficient as an added amount. In $175=25l$, 25 multiplies $l$, so divide 175 by 25 to get $l=7$.
- $$l = 8$ ($175 \div 25$)
  - _why:_ This is an arithmetic slip because $25 \times 7=175$, whereas $25 \times 8=200$. Dividing 175 by 25 therefore gives $l=7$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

  - _why:_ The price of one rice packet lets you calculate four packets. Removing that cost from £5.10 leaves the cost of three soups, which is divided by 3.
- Find cost of 1 rice ($2.25 \div 5$)Find cost of 4 riceAdd 4 rice cost to £5.10Divide result by 3
  - _why:_ Adding the rice cost counts it twice, although it is already included in £5.10. Subtract the four-rice cost, then divide the remainder by 3.
- Find cost of 1 rice ($2.25 \div 5$)Find cost of 3 riceSubtract 3 rice cost from £5.10Divide remainder by 4
  - _why:_ This swaps the numbers of rice packets and soup tins. Remove the cost of 4 rice packets from £5.10, then divide the remainder by 3 soups.
- Find cost of 5 rice (£2.25)Find cost of 4 riceSubtract 4 rice cost from £5.10Multiply remainder by 3
  - _why:_ The remainder is the combined cost of three soup tins, so multiplying by 3 increases it incorrectly. Divide that remainder by 3 to find one tin’s cost.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** In inverse proportion, multiplying one quantity by a scale factor [ ___ ] the other quantity by that same scale factor.

**Back:** In inverse proportion, multiplying one quantity by a scale factor divides the other quantity by that same scale factor.

**Choices:**

- divides ✔
  - _why:_ In inverse proportion, the product of the two quantities remains constant. Multiplying one quantity by a scale factor therefore divides the other by that factor.
- multiplies
  - _why:_ Multiplying both quantities by the same scale factor describes direct proportion. In inverse proportion, multiplying one quantity by a factor divides the other by that factor.
- subtracts
  - _why:_ Subtracting the scale factor confuses multiplicative proportional change with additive change. In inverse proportion, the other quantity is divided by the same scale factor.
- squares
  - _why:_ Squaring the other quantity does not preserve the constant product required by inverse proportion. The other quantity must be divided by the same scale factor.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the general algebraic formula for inverse proportion?

**Back:** $$y = \frac{k}{x}$

**Choices:**

- $$y = \frac{k}{x}$ ✔
  - _why:_ In inverse proportion, the product $xy$ remains constant. Writing this constant as $k$ gives $xy=k$, which rearranges to $y = \frac{k}{x}$.
- $$y = kx$
  - _why:_ This is the formula for direct proportion, where the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ remains constant. In inverse proportion, $xy$ is constant, so $y = \frac{k}{x}$.
- $$y = \frac{x}{k}$
  - _why:_ This makes $y$ increase as $x$ increases, so it describes a direct relationship with constant $\frac{1}{k}$. Inverse proportion requires $y = \frac{k}{x}$.
- $$y = k + x$
  - _why:_ Adding a constant produces a linear relationship with a fixed difference, not inverse proportion. For inverse proportion, the product $xy$ is constant, giving $y = \frac{k}{x}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** 3 painters take 8 days to paint a house. How do you find the total amount of painter-days required for the job?

**Back:** $$3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days

**Choices:**

- $$3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days ✔
  - _why:_ The total work is found by multiplying the number of painters by the number of days. Therefore, the job requires $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.
- $$3 + 8 = 11$ painter-days
  - _why:_ Adding painters and days mixes two different quantities and does not measure total work. Painter-days are calculated by multiplication, so the job requires $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.
- $$8 \div 3 = 2.67$ painter-days
  - _why:_ Dividing the days by the painters gives days per painter rather than the total work completed. Total painter-days equal painters multiplied by days, giving $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.
- $$8 - 3 = 5$ painter-days
  - _why:_ Subtracting the number of painters from the days does not represent total work. Painter-days combine the quantities by multiplication, so the required amount is $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** 3 painters take 8 days to paint a house. How long would 4 painters take?

**Back:** 6 days ($24 \div 4$)

**Choices:**

- 6 days ($24 \div 4$) ✔
  - _why:_ The job requires 24 painter-days in total. Sharing this work equally among 4 painters gives $24 \div 4 = 6$, so they take 6 days.
- 8 days ($24 \div 3$)
  - _why:_ This repeats the calculation for the original 3 painters rather than using the new workforce. With 4 painters, the fixed 24 painter-days give $24 \div 4 = 6$ days.
- 12 days ($24 \div 2$)
  - _why:_ This effectively treats the increase from 3 to 4 painters as though only 2 painters were working. The correct calculation is $24 \div 4 = 6$ days.
- 10 days ($24 - 4 - 10$)
  - _why:_ Subtracting the number of painters does not model inverse proportion or distribute the workload. The fixed total of 24 painter-days must be divided by 4, giving 6 days.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for $y$ being directly proportional to $x$ squared?

**Back:** $$y = kx^2$

**Choices:**

- $$y = kx^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ Direct proportion to $x$ squared means the ratio $\frac{y}{x^2}$ is constant. Calling that constant $k$ gives the formula $y = kx^2$.
- $$y = kx$
  - _why:_ This represents $y$ being directly proportional to $x$, not to its square. Direct proportion to $x^2$ requires the squared term, giving $y = kx^2$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x^2}$
  - _why:_ Placing $x^2$ in the denominator represents inverse proportion to $x^2$. Direct proportion means multiplying the constant by $x^2$, so the correct formula is $y = kx^2$.
- $$y = kx^3$
  - _why:_ This describes direct proportion to the cube of $x$, which changes more rapidly than a squared relationship. Direct proportion to $x^2$ is written $y = kx^2$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x^2$, the formula is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x^2$, the formula is $\boxed{y = \frac{k}{x^2}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Inverse proportion places the relevant power of $x$ in the denominator. Since the relationship is inverse to $x^2$, the formula is $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.
- $$y = kx^2$
  - _why:_ Multiplying by $x^2$ describes direct proportion to $x^2$, so $y$ increases when the magnitude of $x$ increases. Inverse proportion requires $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x}$
  - _why:_ This describes inverse proportion to $x$, but it omits the required square. Being inversely proportional to $x^2$ means the formula is $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.
- $$y = \frac{k}{x^3}$
  - _why:_ This uses a cube and therefore represents inverse proportion to $x^3$. The stated relationship is inverse proportion to the square, so the formula is $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** In the formula $F = \frac{48}{d^2}$, what must you do to $d=4$ before dividing?

**Back:** Square it (to get 16).

**Choices:**

- Square it (to get 16). ✔
  - _why:_ The denominator is $d^2$, so substituting $d=4$ requires calculating $4^2=16$ before dividing. This makes the expression $F=\frac{48}{16}$.
- Double it (to get 8).
  - _why:_ Doubling confuses the exponent 2 with multiplication by 2. The exponent means multiply 4 by itself, so $4^2=16$ before calculating $48 \div 16$.
- Cube it (to get 64).
  - _why:_ Cubing uses the power 3 rather than the power 2 shown in the formula. The denominator requires $4^2=16$, not $4^3=64$.
- Leave it (as 4).
  - _why:_ Using 4 unchanged ignores the exponent in $d^2$. You must square the substituted value, giving $4^2=16$, before dividing 48 by the denominator.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Calculate $F$ in $F = \frac{48}{d^2}$ when $d = 4$.

**Back:** $$F = 3$ ($48 \div 16$)

**Choices:**

- $$F = 3$ ($48 \div 16$) ✔
  - _why:_ Substituting $d=4$ gives $d^2=4^2=16$. Therefore, $F=\frac{48}{16}=3$.
- $$F = 12$ ($48 \div 4$)
  - _why:_ This divides by $d$ and ignores the square in the denominator. Since $d^2=4^2=16$, the correct calculation is $48 \div 16=3$.
- $$F = 6$ ($48 \div 8$)
  - _why:_ This doubles 4 instead of squaring it, confusing an exponent of 2 with multiplication by 2. The denominator is $4^2=16$, so $F=3$.
- $$F = 0.75$ ($48 \div 64$)
  - _why:_ This cubes 4, producing 64, even though the formula contains $d^2$. Squaring gives $4^2=16$, so $F=48 \div 16=3$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the symbol $\propto$ mean?

**Back:** is proportional to

**Choices:**

- is proportional to ✔
  - _why:_ The symbol $\propto$ states that one variable is proportional to another. The precise direct or inverse relationship is then indicated by the accompanying algebraic expression.
- is equal to
  - _why:_ Equality is represented by $=$, whereas $\propto$ indicates a proportional relationship involving an unspecified constant of proportionality. Therefore, the symbol means “is proportional to”.
- is inversely proportional to
  - _why:_ The symbol alone does not specify inverse proportion. An inverse relationship must be shown by a reciprocal expression, such as $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$; $\propto$ means “is proportional to”.
- is approximately equal to
  - _why:_ Approximate equality is represented by $\approx$, not $\propto$. The proportionality symbol indicates that two quantities are related by a constant multiplier and means “is proportional to”.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the graph shape for $y \propto x$.

**Back:** A straight line passing through the origin.

**Choices:**

- A straight line passing through the origin. ✔
  - _why:_ Direct proportion has the form $y = kx$, which is linear and gives $y = 0$ when $x = 0$. Its graph is therefore a straight line through the origin.
- A straight line not passing through the origin.
  - _why:_ A straight line missing the origin has a non-zero intercept, so its equation is $y = kx + c$, not direct proportion. For $y \propto x$, the line passes through the origin.
- A curved line passing through the origin.
  - _why:_ Confusing direct proportion with a power relationship produces a curved graph. Since $y = kx$ has a constant gradient, its graph is a straight line through the origin.
- A straight line passing through the y-intercept.
  - _why:_ Treating the graph as an ordinary linear equation can suggest a separate y-intercept. In direct proportion, the y-intercept is zero, so the straight line passes through the origin.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the graph shape for $y \propto x^2$.

**Back:** A U-shaped parabola through the origin.

**Choices:**

- A U-shaped parabola through the origin. ✔
  - _why:_ The relationship has the form $y = kx^2$. For positive $k$, squaring positive and negative x-values gives the U-shaped symmetry of a parabola with its vertex at the origin.
- An inverted parabola through the origin.
  - _why:_ An inverted parabola would require a negative proportionality constant. The standard graph intended for $y \propto x^2$ uses positive $k$, giving a U-shaped parabola through the origin.
- A V-shaped graph through the origin.
  - _why:_ A V-shaped graph is associated with $y = k|x|$, where each side is straight. Squaring x instead produces a smooth U-shaped parabola through the origin.
- A U-shaped parabola above the origin.
  - _why:_ Shifting the vertex above the origin would add a constant, giving $y = kx^2 + c$. Direct square proportion has no added constant, so the parabola passes through the origin.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** The graph for inverse proportion ($y \propto \frac{1}{x}$) has two curved branches in [ ___ ].

**Back:** The graph for inverse proportion ($y \propto \frac{1}{x}$) has two curved branches in opposite quadrants.

**Choices:**

- opposite quadrants ✔
  - _why:_ For $y = \frac{k}{x}$, x and y have matching signs when $k>0$ and opposite signs when $k<0$. In either case, the branches occupy diagonally opposite quadrants.
- adjacent quadrants
  - _why:_ Adjacent quadrants would place both branches on the same side of one axis. For $y = \frac{k}{x}$, changing the sign of x also changes the sign of y, producing opposite quadrants.
- the upper quadrants
  - _why:_ Placing both branches above the x-axis wrongly assumes y is always positive. In inverse proportion, y changes sign when x changes sign, so the branches lie in opposite quadrants.
- the lower quadrants
  - _why:_ Placing both branches below the x-axis wrongly assumes y is always negative. Since $y = \frac{k}{x}$ changes sign across $x=0$, its branches occupy opposite quadrants.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is the graph for $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$ entirely above the x-axis (for $k > 0$)?

**Back:** Because $x^2$ is always positive.

**Choices:**

- Because $x^2$ is always positive. ✔
  - _why:_ For every allowed value $x \ne 0$, squaring x gives a positive denominator. Since $k>0$, $y = \frac{k}{x^2}$ is positive and lies above the x-axis.
- Because $x$ is always positive.
  - _why:_ The domain includes both positive and negative non-zero x-values, so x itself is not always positive. Squaring makes $x^2$ positive, ensuring $\frac{k}{x^2}>0$ when $k>0$.
- Because $x^2$ is always negative.
  - _why:_ This reverses the sign rule for squaring: the square of any non-zero real number is positive, not negative. Therefore $\frac{k}{x^2}$ is positive when $k>0$.
- Because $k$ is always negative.
  - _why:_ The question explicitly specifies $k>0$, so treating k as negative reverses the stated condition. The graph is above the x-axis because both k and $x^2$ are positive.
