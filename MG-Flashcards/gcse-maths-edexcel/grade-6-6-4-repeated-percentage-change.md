# Repeated Percentage Change Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 6 · 2 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the multiplier for a 10% increase?

**Back:** 1.10

**Choices:**

- 1.10 ✔
  - _why:_ A 10% increase means retaining 100% and adding 10%, giving 110%. Dividing by 100 converts this percentage to the multiplier 1.10.
- 0.10
  - _why:_ This represents only the 10% increase, omitting the original 100% of the quantity. The required multiplier represents 110%, so it is 1.10.
- 1.01
  - _why:_ This multiplier represents a 1% increase because it is equivalent to 101%. A 10% increase gives 110% of the original, requiring 1.10.
- 0.90
  - _why:_ This reverses the direction of the percentage change and produces a 10% decrease. For a 10% increase, add 0.10 to 1 to obtain 1.10.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the multiplier for a 10% decrease?

**Back:** 0.90

**Choices:**

- 0.90 ✔
  - _why:_ A 10% decrease leaves 90% of the original quantity. Dividing 90% by 100 converts it to the decimal multiplier 0.90.
- 0.10
  - _why:_ This represents the 10% removed rather than the 90% remaining. A multiplier must give the new quantity, so the correct multiplier is 0.90.
- 1.10
  - _why:_ This reverses the direction of the change and gives a 10% increase. A 10% decrease leaves 90%, which corresponds to the multiplier 0.90.
- 0.99
  - _why:_ This represents a 1% decrease because it leaves 99% of the original quantity. A 10% decrease leaves 90%, so the multiplier is 0.90.
