## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** Like terms have the same [ ___ ], such as $2x$ and $5x$.

**Back:** Like terms have the same variable part, such as $2x$ and $5x$.

**Choices:**

- variable part ✔
  - _why:_ Like terms have identical variables raised to identical powers, called the variable part. Their numerical coefficients may differ, as they do in $2x$ and $5x$.
- coefficient
  - _why:_ The coefficient is the numerical multiplier, which is $2$ in $2x$ and $5$ in $5x$. The shared feature that makes these like terms is their variable part.
- numerical part
  - _why:_ The numerical parts of $2x$ and $5x$ are different, so this cannot define like terms. Like terms instead have the same variable part.
- constant value
  - _why:_ Neither $2x$ nor $5x$ is a constant because each contains a variable. They are like terms because they share the same variable part, $x$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $(4x-2)+(x+6)+(2x+1)$.

**Back:** $7x+5$

**Choices:**

- $7x+5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Collecting the variable terms gives $4x+x+2x=7x$, while collecting the constants gives $-2+6+1=5$. Therefore the simplified expression is $7x+5$.
- $7x+9$
  - _why:_ The constant total becomes $9$ only if $-2$ is mistakenly treated as positive. Correctly calculating $-2+6+1=5$ gives the answer $7x+5$.
- $6x+5$
  - _why:_ This misses one of the seven lots of $x$, usually by failing to include the standalone $x$. The variable terms total $7x$, giving $7x+5$.
- $7x-5$
  - _why:_ The constants have been given the wrong overall sign. Since $-2+6+1=5$, and the variable terms total $7x$, the correct result is $7x+5$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** In $3x-5$, what number term must be collected?

**Back:** $-5$

**Choices:**

- $-5$ ✔
  - _why:_ The number term includes the sign immediately before it. In $3x-5$, the constant term is therefore negative five, written as $-5$.
- $5$
  - _why:_ Ignoring the subtraction sign changes the value of the constant. The sign belongs to the number term, so the term that must be collected is $-5$.
- $3$
  - _why:_ The $3$ is a coefficient because it multiplies $x$, rather than being a separate number term. The constant number term in the expression is $-5$.
- $-3$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly transfers the negative sign from the constant to the coefficient. The variable term is $3x$, while the number term that must be collected is $-5$.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** To form an equation from a known total, write an expression and set it [ ___ ] to the given value.

**Back:** To form an equation from a known total, write an expression and set it equal to the given value.

**Choices:**

- equal ✔
  - _why:_ An equation states that two quantities have the same value. Therefore, an expression representing the unknown total must be set equal to the given total.
- greater than
  - _why:_ Using an inequality would state that the expression exceeds the known total rather than matches it. To form the required equation, set the expression equal to the given value.
- less than
  - _why:_ A less-than relationship forms an inequality and does not represent an exact known total. The expression must be set equal to the given value to form an equation.
- not equal
  - _why:_ A not-equal sign explicitly says the expression and known total differ, so it cannot model the stated total. The expression should instead be set equal to the value.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Adding and [ ___ ] are inverse operations; multiplying and [ ___ ] are inverse operations.

**Back:** Adding and subtracting are inverse operations; multiplying and dividing are inverse operations.

**Choices:**

- Adding and subtracting are inverse operations; multiplying and dividing are inverse operations. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting reverses the effect of adding, while dividing reverses the effect of multiplying. Each pair can therefore be used to undo the other operation when solving equations.
- Adding and dividing are inverse operations; multiplying and subtracting are inverse operations.
  - _why:_ Dividing does not undo adding, and subtracting does not undo multiplying. The inverse of adding is subtracting, while the inverse of multiplying is dividing.
- Adding and multiplying are inverse operations; multiplying and dividing are inverse operations.
  - _why:_ Multiplication is repeated addition in some contexts, but it does not reverse addition. Subtracting is the inverse of adding, while dividing is the inverse of multiplying.
- Adding and subtracting are inverse operations; multiplying and adding are inverse operations.
  - _why:_ Adding cannot reverse multiplication because it changes a value by a fixed amount rather than a factor. Multiplication is undone by dividing, while adding is undone by subtracting.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Rectangle length $3x+2$, width $x+4$: write the perimeter expression.

**Back:** $2(3x+2)+2(x+4)$

**Choices:**

- $2(3x+2)+2(x+4)$ ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle has two lengths and two widths. Doubling $3x+2$ and doubling $x+4$, then adding them, gives the perimeter expression $2(3x+2)+2(x+4)$.
- $(3x+2)(x+4)$
  - _why:_ Multiplying length by width calculates the rectangle’s area, not its perimeter. Perimeter requires two lengths and two widths, giving $2(3x+2)+2(x+4)$.
- $2(3x+2)+(x+4)$
  - _why:_ This includes two lengths but only one width, so one side is missing. A rectangle has two of each side, giving $2(3x+2)+2(x+4)$.
- $(3x+2)+2(x+4)$
  - _why:_ This includes two widths but only one length, so it does not cover all four sides. The complete perimeter is $2(3x+2)+2(x+4)$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $8x+12=44$.

**Back:** $x=4$

**Choices:**

- $x=4$ ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting $12$ from both sides gives $8x=32$. Dividing both sides by $8$ then gives $x=4$, which satisfies the original equation.
- $x=7$
  - _why:_ This comes from adding $12$ to $44$ before dividing, rather than using the inverse operation. Subtracting $12$ gives $32$, so $x=4$.
- $x=5.5$
  - _why:_ Dividing $44$ by $8$ ignores the additional $12$ on the left-hand side. Subtract $12$ first, then divide $32$ by $8$ to obtain $x=4$.
- $x=32$
  - _why:_ Subtracting $12$ correctly produces $32$, but this stops before undoing the multiplication by $8$. Dividing $32$ by $8$ gives the solution $x=4$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles on a straight line add to [ ___ ]; angles around a point add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Angles on a straight line add to 180°; angles around a point add to 360°.

**Choices:**

- Angles on a straight line add to 180°; angles around a point add to 360°. ✔
  - _why:_ A straight line is a half-turn, so its angles total $180°$. A full turn around a single point is twice as large, so those angles total $360°$.
- Angles on a straight line add to 90°; angles around a point add to 360°.
  - _why:_ A total of $90°$ describes a right angle, not a straight line. Angles on a straight line total $180°$, while angles around a point total $360°$.
- Angles on a straight line add to 180°; angles around a point add to 180°.
  - _why:_ A total of $180°$ represents only a half-turn, so it is insufficient around a point. The correct totals are $180°$ on a straight line and $360°$ around a point.
- Angles on a straight line add to 360°; angles around a point add to 180°.
  - _why:_ These totals reverse a full turn and a half-turn. A straight line is a $180°$ half-turn, whereas angles around a point make a $360°$ full turn.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** A right angle is [ ___ ]; angles in a triangle add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** A right angle is 90°; angles in a triangle add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- A right angle is 90°; angles in a triangle add to 180°. ✔
  - _why:_ A quarter turn measures 90°, while the interior angles of every triangle have a total of 180°. These facts can be used to form equations involving unknown angles.
- A right angle is 180°; angles in a triangle add to 360°.
  - _why:_ This confuses a right angle with a straight angle and a triangle with a full turn. A right angle is 90°, and angles in a triangle total 180°.
- A right angle is 90°; angles in a triangle add to 360°.
  - _why:_ Although a right angle is correctly identified as 90°, 360° is the angle around a point. The interior angles of a triangle add to 180°.
- A right angle is 45°; angles in a triangle add to 180°.
  - _why:_ The triangle angle total is correctly given as 180°, but 45° is half a right angle. A right angle measures 90°.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $4x+(2x+6)=90$.

**Back:** $x=14$

**Choices:**

- $x=14$ ✔
  - _why:_ Expanding and collecting terms gives $6x+6=90$. Subtracting 6 gives $6x=84$, and dividing both sides by 6 gives $x=14$.
- $x=16$
  - _why:_ This can result from adding 6 instead of subtracting it. The equation simplifies to $6x+6=90$, so $6x=84$ and $x=14$.
- $x=21$
  - _why:_ This comes from overlooking the additional $2x$ and dividing 84 by 4. Collecting both variable terms gives $6x=84$, so $x=14$.
- $x=15$
  - _why:_ This results from dividing 90 by 6 before removing the constant 6. First obtain $6x=84$, then divide by 6 to get $x=14$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $(2x+5)+(x+20)+(x-1)=180$.

**Back:** $x=39$

**Choices:**

- $x=39$ ✔
  - _why:_ Collecting like terms gives $4x+24=180$. Subtracting 24 produces $4x=156$, and dividing by 4 gives $x=39$.
- $x=51$
  - _why:_ This comes from adding the constant total of 24 to 180 instead of subtracting it. The correct rearrangement is $4x=156$, giving $x=39$.
- $x=45$
  - _why:_ This comes from dividing 180 by 4 while ignoring the constant terms. The constants total 24, so $4x=156$ and therefore $x=39$.
- $x=41$
  - _why:_ This can result from combining $5+20-1$ incorrectly as 16. The constants actually total 24, giving $4x+24=180$ and hence $x=39$.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a right-angled triangle, the two other angles add to [ ___ ], not 180° by themselves.

**Back:** In a right-angled triangle, the two other angles add to 90°, not 180° by themselves.

**Choices:**

- 90° ✔
  - _why:_ All three angles in a triangle total 180°. Since the right angle already accounts for 90°, the two remaining angles must together account for the other 90°.
- 180°
  - _why:_ This is the total for all three angles, including the right angle. Subtracting the 90° right angle from 180° leaves 90° for the other two angles.
- 360°
  - _why:_ This confuses the angle total of a triangle with the angle around a point. A triangle totals 180°, leaving 90° after its right angle is removed.
- 45°
  - _why:_ Each remaining angle would be 45° only in an isosceles right-angled triangle. In every right-angled triangle, the two remaining angles together add to 90°.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What expression gives the area of a triangle?

**Back:** 1/2 × base × height

**Choices:**

- 1/2 × base × height ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle with a given base and perpendicular height has half the area of the corresponding rectangle or parallelogram, so its area is 1/2 × base × height.
- base × height
  - _why:_ This calculates the area of a rectangle or parallelogram rather than a triangle. A triangle occupies half that area, so the correct expression is 1/2 × base × height.
- 2 × base × height
  - _why:_ This doubles the corresponding rectangle’s area instead of halving it. A triangle’s area is found using 1/2 × base × height.
- 1/2 × base + height
  - _why:_ This incorrectly adds the height rather than multiplying all three factors. The area of a triangle is calculated using 1/2 × base × height.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** How is perpendicular height measured relative to the base?

**Back:** At 90° to the base.

**Choices:**

- At 90° to the base. ✔
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, so the perpendicular height must be measured at 90° to the chosen base, using the shortest distance from the opposite vertex.
- At 45° to the base.
  - _why:_ A 45° line is diagonal rather than perpendicular. The height used in the triangle area formula must meet the base at 90°.
- At 180° to the base.
  - _why:_ An angle of 180° forms a straight line along the base rather than a height. Perpendicular height is measured at 90° to the base.
- At 60° to the base.
  - _why:_ A 60° line may be a sloping side, but it is not perpendicular to the base. The required height must meet the base at 90°.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Right-angled triangle sides $5x$ and $4x$, area 360: what equation is formed?

**Back:** $1/2\times 5x\times 4x=360$

**Choices:**

- $1/2\times 5x\times 4x=360$ ✔
  - _why:_ The two perpendicular sides act as the base and height. Substituting $5x$ and $4x$ into the triangle area formula gives $1/2\times 5x\times 4x=360$.
- $5x\times 4x=360$
  - _why:_ This uses the rectangle area formula and omits the factor of one-half. For a triangle, the equation must be $1/2\times 5x\times 4x=360$.
- $1/2\times (5x+4x)=360$
  - _why:_ This adds the two side lengths instead of multiplying the base and perpendicular height. The triangle area equation is $1/2\times 5x\times 4x=360$.
- $2\times 5x\times 4x=360$
  - _why:_ This doubles the base-height product rather than halving it. Since a triangle is half the corresponding rectangle, the required factor is $1/2$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Why use $x=6$, not $x=-6$, when $x^2=36$ and $x$ is a length?

**Back:** A length cannot be negative.

**Choices:**

- A length cannot be negative. ✔
  - _why:_ Solving $x^2=36$ algebraically gives $x=6$ or $x=-6$, but a geometric length must be non-negative, so only $x=6$ is valid.
- A length must be an even number.
  - _why:_ Lengths do not have to be even; they can be odd, fractional or irrational. The negative solution is rejected because a length cannot be negative.
- A square root is always positive.
  - _why:_ The equation $x^2=36$ has two algebraic solutions, $6$ and $-6$, because both square to 36. Only $6$ is used because a length cannot be negative.
- A negative number cannot be squared.
  - _why:_ Negative numbers can be squared, and $(-6)^2=36$. The value $-6$ is excluded only because $x$ represents a length, which cannot be negative.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Parallelogram opposite sides $4x+1$ and $2x+13$: what equation is formed?

**Back:** $4x+1=2x+13$

**Choices:**

- $4x+1=2x+13$ ✔
  - _why:_ Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal in length, so the expressions representing those sides must be set equal: $4x+1=2x+13$.
- $4x+1+2x+13=0$
  - _why:_ Adding both side expressions and equating the result to zero does not represent equal opposite sides. The required equation is $4x+1=2x+13$.
- $4x+1=2(x+13)$
  - _why:_ The second side is already represented by $2x+13$; the 13 is not multiplied by 2. Equal opposite sides give $4x+1=2x+13$.
- $4x+2=2x+13$
  - _why:_ This changes the constant in the first given expression from 1 to 2. The expressions must be copied accurately, giving $4x+1=2x+13$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What expression gives the area of a parallelogram?

**Back:** base × perpendicular height

**Choices:**

- base × perpendicular height ✔
  - _why:_ A parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height, so its area is base × perpendicular height.
- base × sloping side length
  - _why:_ The sloping side is not generally perpendicular to the base, so using it gives the wrong area. The correct expression is base × perpendicular height.
- base + perpendicular height
  - _why:_ Adding lengths does not calculate area and produces a quantity measured in length units. The area of a parallelogram is base × perpendicular height.
- base × perpendicular height ÷ 2
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 confuses a parallelogram with a triangle. A parallelogram occupies twice the area of the corresponding triangle, so use base × perpendicular height.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Sam has $x$ counters, Tia has twice as many, Ravi has 4 more than Tia. What is Ravi’s expression?

**Back:** $2x+4$

**Choices:**

- $2x+4$ ✔
  - _why:_ Tia has twice Sam’s $x$ counters, giving $2x$. Ravi has 4 more than Tia, so his total is represented by $2x+4$.
- $2(x+4)$
  - _why:_ This doubles both Sam’s counters and the extra 4, incorrectly giving Ravi 8 additional counters. Only Tia’s amount is doubled, so Ravi has $2x+4$.
- $x+6$
  - _why:_ Twice as many counters means multiplying $x$ by 2, not adding 2. Adding Ravi’s extra 4 then gives the correct expression $2x+4$.
- $2x-4$
  - _why:_ Subtracting 4 would mean Ravi has 4 fewer counters than Tia. Ravi has 4 more, so the correct expression is $2x+4$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Alex is $x$, Ben is $3x$, Cara is 5 younger than Ben. What is Cara’s expression?

**Back:** $3x-5$

**Choices:**

- $3x-5$ ✔
  - _why:_ Ben’s age is three times Alex’s, so it is $3x$. Cara is 5 years younger than Ben, giving the expression $3x-5$.
- $3(x-5)$
  - _why:_ This subtracts 5 from Alex’s age before tripling, making Cara 15 years younger than Ben. Subtract 5 from Ben’s age instead, giving $3x-5$.
- $3x+5$
  - _why:_ Adding 5 would make Cara 5 years older than Ben. Since she is 5 younger, her age is represented by $3x-5$.
- $x-5$
  - _why:_ This subtracts 5 from Alex’s age rather than Ben’s. Ben is $3x$, so Cara’s age is 5 less than this: $3x-5$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify the ratio $15:45:40$.

**Back:** $3:9:8$

**Choices:**

- $3:9:8$ ✔
  - _why:_ The greatest common factor of 15, 45 and 40 is 5. Dividing every part by 5 gives the simplified ratio $3:9:8$.
- $1:3:8$
  - _why:_ This divides 15 and 45 by 15 but divides 40 by 5, changing the ratio. Every part must be divided by 5, giving $3:9:8$.
- $3:9:40$
  - _why:_ This divides the first two parts by 5 but leaves the final part unchanged. All three parts must be divided equally, producing $3:9:8$.
- $5:15:8$
  - _why:_ This divides the first two parts by 3 but the final part by 5, so the proportions change. Dividing every part by 5 gives $3:9:8$.
