# Error Intervals Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 3 · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** For ordinary rounding, what inequality form is used for an error interval?

**Back:** $a \le x < b$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is the upper bound excluded in normal rounding?

**Back:** It would round up to the next answer.

**Choices:**

- It would round up to the next answer. ✔
  - _why:_ The upper bound is exactly halfway between consecutive rounded answers. Under ordinary rounding, this value rounds up, so it belongs to the next interval.
- It would round down to the previous answer.
  - _why:_ This confuses the upper and lower boundaries. The upper bound rounds up to the next answer, which is why it is excluded from the interval.
- It would round back to the same answer.
  - _why:_ Treating the upper bound as part of the interval creates overlapping answers. It rounds up to the next answer, so the upper inequality must be strict.
- It would fall below the lower bound.
  - _why:_ The upper bound cannot fall below the lower bound; they are opposite endpoints. It is excluded because it rounds up to the next answer.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** For rounding, find bounds by going [ ___ ] and [ ___ ] from the rounded value.

**Back:** For rounding, find bounds by going half a step down and half a step up from the rounded value.

**Choices:**

- For rounding, find bounds by going half a step down and half a step up from the rounded value. ✔
  - _why:_ Rounding boundaries lie halfway between consecutive possible rounded values. Therefore, subtract half the rounding step for the lower bound and add half for the upper bound.
- For rounding, find bounds by going half a step up and half a step down from the rounded value.
  - _why:_ This swaps the directions of the bounds, placing the lower bound above the value. Go half a step down first and half a step up second.
- For rounding, find bounds by going one full step down and one full step up from the rounded value.
  - _why:_ Using a full step makes the interval twice as wide as it should be. Rounding boundaries are half a step down and half a step up.
- For rounding, find bounds by going half a step down and one full step up from the rounded value.
  - _why:_ The lower adjustment is correct, but the upper adjustment uses a full step. Both boundaries must be exactly half a rounding step from the rounded value.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** Correct to the nearest kg, cm or m has step size [ ___ ] and half-step [ ___ ].

**Back:** Correct to the nearest kg, cm or m has step size 1 whole unit and half-step 0.5 units.

**Choices:**

- Correct to the nearest kg, cm or m has step size 1 whole unit and half-step 0.5 units. ✔
  - _why:_ Consecutive whole-number measurements differ by one unit. The rounding boundaries are halfway between them, so the half-step is $0.5$ units.
- Correct to the nearest kg, cm or m has step size 0.5 units and half-step 0.25 units.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats half a unit as the gap between consecutive whole-number answers. The step is $1$ whole unit and its half-step is $0.5$ units.
- Correct to the nearest kg, cm or m has step size 1 whole unit and half-step 1 whole unit.
  - _why:_ The step size is correct, but the half-step has not been divided by two. Half of $1$ whole unit is $0.5$ units.
- Correct to the nearest kg, cm or m has step size 2 whole units and half-step 1 whole unit.
  - _why:_ This doubles the gap between possible rounded measurements. Nearest-unit answers increase by $1$ whole unit, giving a half-step of $0.5$ units.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** 18 kg correct to the nearest kg: write the error interval for mass $m$.

**Back:** $17.5 \le m < 18.5$.

**Choices:**

- $17.5 \le m < 18.5$. ✔
  - _why:_ Rounding to the nearest kilogram has a half-step of $0.5$ kg. Subtracting and adding $0.5$ gives bounds of $17.5$ and $18.5$.
- $17 \le m < 19$.
  - _why:_ This uses a full kilogram on each side rather than half a kilogram. The correct bounds are $17.5 \le m < 18.5$.
- $17.5 < m \le 18.5$.
  - _why:_ The endpoint inclusion is reversed. A mass of $17.5$ kg rounds to $18$ kg, while $18.5$ kg rounds to $19$ kg, giving $17.5 \le m < 18.5$.
- $18 \le m < 19$.
  - _why:_ This starts at the rounded value instead of half a unit below it. The interval must extend from $17.5$ kg up to, but not including, $18.5$ kg.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** Rounded to 1 decimal place: step size [ ___ ], half-step [ ___ ].

**Back:** Rounded to 1 decimal place: step size 0.1, half-step 0.05.

**Choices:**

- Rounded to 1 decimal place: step size 0.1, half-step 0.05. ✔
  - _why:_ Adjacent numbers to one decimal place differ by $0.1$. Dividing this rounding step by two gives the half-step, $0.05$.
- Rounded to 1 decimal place: step size 0.01, half-step 0.005.
  - _why:_ These values apply to rounding to two decimal places, not one. For one decimal place, the step is $0.1$ and the half-step is $0.05$.
- Rounded to 1 decimal place: step size 0.1, half-step 0.1.
  - _why:_ The step size is correct, but it has not been halved. Half of $0.1$ is $0.05$, so that is the required half-step.
- Rounded to 1 decimal place: step size 1, half-step 0.5.
  - _why:_ These values apply to rounding to the nearest whole number. One-decimal-place values differ by $0.1$, so their half-step is $0.05$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** A number rounds to 9.6 to 1 decimal place. What is the error interval for $y$?

**Back:** $9.55 \le y < 9.65$.

**Choices:**

- $9.55 \le y < 9.65$. ✔
  - _why:_ The step for one decimal place is $0.1$, so the half-step is $0.05$. Subtracting and adding $0.05$ to $9.6$ gives the stated interval.
- $9.5 \le y < 9.7$.
  - _why:_ This uses a full step of $0.1$ on both sides, making the interval twice as wide. The correct half-step is $0.05$, giving $9.55 \le y < 9.65$.
- $9.55 < y \le 9.65$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the endpoint inclusion. The lower bound $9.55$ rounds to $9.6$, while the upper bound $9.65$ rounds to $9.7$.
- $9.595 \le y < 9.605$.
  - _why:_ This uses a half-step of $0.005$, which applies to two decimal places. For one decimal place, use $0.05$, giving $9.55 \le y < 9.65$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Rounded to 2 decimal places: step size [ ___ ], half-step [ ___ ].

**Back:** Rounded to 2 decimal places: step size 0.01, half-step 0.005.

**Choices:**

- Rounded to 2 decimal places: step size 0.01, half-step 0.005. ✔
  - _why:_ Adjacent values to two decimal places differ by one hundredth, or $0.01$. Half of this step is $0.005$.
- Rounded to 2 decimal places: step size 0.1, half-step 0.05.
  - _why:_ These values apply to one decimal place, not two. For two decimal places, the step is $0.01$ and the half-step is $0.005$.
- Rounded to 2 decimal places: step size 0.01, half-step 0.01.
  - _why:_ The step size is right, but it has not been divided by two. Half of $0.01$ is $0.005$, which determines the bounds.
- Rounded to 2 decimal places: step size 0.001, half-step 0.0005.
  - _why:_ These values apply to three decimal places. Two-decimal-place answers increase in steps of $0.01$, so the correct half-step is $0.005$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** A number rounds to 1.34 to 2 decimal places. What is the error interval for $x$?

**Back:** $1.335 \le x < 1.345$.

**Choices:**

- $1.335 \le x < 1.345$. ✔
  - _why:_ Rounding to 2 decimal places uses a half-step of $0.005$. The lower bound is included, while the upper bound is excluded because it rounds to $1.35$.
- $1.33 \le x < 1.35$.
  - _why:_ This uses a full step of $0.01$ on either side instead of a half-step. The correct bounds are $1.335$ and $1.345$, giving $1.335 \le x < 1.345$.
- $1.335 < x \le 1.345$.
  - _why:_ The endpoint signs are reversed. $1.335$ is included because it rounds to $1.34$, but $1.345$ is excluded because it rounds to $1.35$.
- $1.3395 \le x < 1.3405$.
  - _why:_ This uses a half-step of $0.0005$, appropriate for 3 decimal places. For 2 decimal places the half-step is $0.005$, so the bounds are $1.335$ and $1.345$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is $1.345$ not included for numbers rounding to 1.34 to 2 decimal places?

**Back:** $1.345$ rounds to $1.35$, not $1.34$.

**Choices:**

- $1.345$ rounds to $1.35$, not $1.34$. ✔
  - _why:_ The third decimal digit is $5$, so the second decimal digit rounds up. Therefore $1.345$ rounds to $1.35$ and is the excluded upper bound.
- $1.345$ rounds to $1.34$, not $1.35$.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly rounds a final $5$ down. Using the usual GCSE rule, the hundredths digit rounds up, so $1.345$ rounds to $1.35$.
- $1.345$ rounds to $1.36$, not $1.34$.
  - _why:_ Rounding up changes the hundredths digit from $4$ to $5$, not to $6$. Therefore $1.345$ rounds to $1.35$, making it the excluded upper bound.
- $1.345$ rounds to $1.345$, not $1.34$.
  - _why:_ This leaves the number unrounded. To 2 decimal places, the third decimal digit $5$ makes the hundredths digit round up, so the result is $1.35$.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** Significant figures are counted from the first [ ___ ] digit.

**Back:** Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit.

**Choices:**

- non-zero ✔
  - _why:_ Leading zeros only locate the decimal point and are not significant. Counting begins at the first digit that is not zero, so the required term is non-zero.
- zero
  - _why:_ Leading zeros are not significant because they only show place value. Significant figures begin at the first non-zero digit, such as the $4$ in $0.00472$.
- decimal
  - _why:_ The decimal point does not determine where significant figures begin. Counting starts at the first non-zero digit, whether the number is greater than or less than one.
- positive
  - _why:_ Digits themselves are not classified as positive for this rule, and the sign of the number is irrelevant. Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** For significant-figure rounding, what decides the half-step?

**Back:** Half the place value of the last significant figure.

**Choices:**

- Half the place value of the last significant figure. ✔
  - _why:_ Adjacent rounded values differ by the place value of the last significant figure. Each error bound lies halfway between them, so the half-step is half that place value.
- The full place value of the last significant figure.
  - _why:_ Using the full place value makes the interval twice as wide as it should be. Each boundary is half a rounding unit away, so use half the place value.
- Twice the place value of the last significant figure.
  - _why:_ Doubling the place value greatly overestimates the rounding interval. The boundaries are halfway to the adjacent rounded values, so use half the final significant figure’s place value.
- Half the rounded value of the last significant figure.
  - _why:_ The half-step depends on the position of the last significant figure, not half of its numerical value. It is half the place value of that figure.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** 0.472 rounded to 3 significant figures: what half-step is used?

**Back:** 0.0005.

**Choices:**

- 0.0005. ✔
  - _why:_ The final significant figure in $0.472$ is in the thousandths place, with place value $0.001$. Half of $0.001$ is $0.0005$.
- 0.005.
  - _why:_ This is half of $0.01$, treating the final significant figure as hundredths. It is actually in the thousandths place, so the correct half-step is $0.0005$.
- 0.00005.
  - _why:_ This is half of $0.0001$, as though the number had been rounded to 4 significant figures. The final digit is thousandths, so the half-step is $0.0005$.
- 0.001.
  - _why:_ This is the full place value of the final significant figure rather than half of it. The thousandths place has value $0.001$, so the half-step is $0.0005$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** 0.472 rounded to 3 significant figures: write the error interval for $x$.

**Back:** $0.4715 \le x < 0.4725$.

**Choices:**

- $0.4715 \le x < 0.4725$. ✔
  - _why:_ The last significant figure is in the thousandths place, so the half-step is $0.0005$. Subtracting and adding this gives the bounds $0.4715$ and $0.4725$.
- $0.471 \le x < 0.473$.
  - _why:_ This subtracts and adds the full thousandths place value rather than half of it. Using the correct half-step $0.0005$ gives $0.4715 \le x < 0.4725$.
- $0.4715 < x \le 0.4725$.
  - _why:_ The endpoint signs are reversed. $0.4715$ rounds up to $0.472$ and is included, while $0.4725$ rounds up to $0.473$ and is excluded.
- $0.47195 \le x < 0.47205$.
  - _why:_ This uses a half-step of $0.00005$, appropriate when rounding to 4 significant figures. For 3 significant figures the half-step is $0.0005$, giving bounds $0.4715$ and $0.4725$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** 7000 rounded to 1 significant figure: what column is the 7 in?

**Back:** The thousands column.

**Choices:**

- The thousands column. ✔
  - _why:_ In $7000$, the digit $7$ represents seven thousand, so it occupies the thousands column. It is therefore the first and only non-zero significant figure.
- The hundreds column.
  - _why:_ The hundreds column is occupied by the first zero, not the $7$. The digit $7$ represents $7000$, so it is in the thousands column.
- The tens column.
  - _why:_ The tens column is the second zero from the right. The digit $7$ has a value of seven thousand, so it belongs in the thousands column.
- The units column.
  - _why:_ The units column contains the final zero on the right. The digit $7$ represents seven lots of one thousand, placing it in the thousands column.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** 7000 rounded to 1 significant figure: write the error interval for $x$.

**Back:** $6500 \le x < 7500$.

**Choices:**

- $6500 \le x < 7500$. ✔
  - _why:_ The $7$ is in the thousands column, so the rounding unit is $1000$ and the half-step is $500$. This gives $6500 \le x < 7500$.
- $6000 \le x < 8000$.
  - _why:_ This uses the full rounding unit of $1000$ on each side rather than the half-step. Using $500$ gives the correct interval $6500 \le x < 7500$.
- $6500 < x \le 7500$.
  - _why:_ The endpoint signs are reversed. $6500$ rounds up to $7000$ and is included, while $7500$ rounds up to $8000$ and must be excluded.
- $6999.5 \le x < 7000.5$.
  - _why:_ These bounds treat $7000$ as rounded to the nearest whole number. Rounding to 1 significant figure uses a half-step of $500$, giving $6500 \le x < 7500$.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** Truncation means [ ___ ] extra digits without [ ___ ].

**Back:** Truncation means cutting off extra digits without rounding up.

**Choices:**

- Truncation means cutting off extra digits without rounding up. ✔
  - _why:_ Truncation removes all digits beyond the required place value and leaves the final retained digit unchanged, rather than increasing it according to the next digit.
- Truncation means cutting off extra digits without rounding down.
  - _why:_ Truncation is sometimes confused with rounding down, but it simply cuts off extra digits without rounding up or otherwise changing the last retained digit.
- Truncation means rounding off extra digits without rounding up.
  - _why:_ Rounding off requires considering the first discarded digit, whereas truncation means cutting off extra digits without rounding up, regardless of the discarded digits.
- Truncation means cutting off extra digits without rounding to nearest.
  - _why:_ Although truncation does not round to the nearest value, the precise definition is cutting off extra digits without rounding up, leaving the retained digits unchanged.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** For a positive truncated number, where does the error interval start?

**Back:** At the displayed truncated value, included.

**Choices:**

- At the displayed truncated value, included. ✔
  - _why:_ For a positive truncated number, the original value can equal the displayed value, so the interval begins there and includes that lower boundary.
- At the displayed truncated value, not included.
  - _why:_ Excluding the displayed value is an endpoint error. The original number may equal its truncated form, so the interval starts at the displayed truncated value, included.
- At the next possible place value, included.
  - _why:_ The next place-value increment is the upper boundary, not the starting point. For a positive number, the interval starts at the displayed truncated value, included.
- At the halfway rounding value, included.
  - _why:_ Using a halfway value confuses truncation with rounding to the nearest value. A positive truncation interval starts at the displayed truncated value itself, included.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A positive number is truncated to 6.2 to 1 decimal place. What is the upper boundary?

**Back:** 6.3, not included.

**Choices:**

- 6.3, not included. ✔
  - _why:_ Truncating to one decimal place retains values from 6.2 up to, but not including, 6.3; at 6.3 the displayed truncated value would change.
- 6.25, not included.
  - _why:_ The value 6.25 is a rounding boundary, obtained by using half of 0.1. Truncation instead has upper boundary 6.3, which is not included.
- 6.3, included.
  - _why:_ The numerical boundary is right, but including it is an endpoint error. Exactly 6.3 truncates to 6.3, so the upper boundary is 6.3, not included.
- 6.2, included.
  - _why:_ The value 6.2 is the included lower boundary, not the upper boundary. The interval extends to 6.3, which is excluded because it truncates to 6.3.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** A number is truncated to 4.73 to 2 decimal places. What is the error interval for $x$?

**Back:** $4.73 \le x < 4.74$.

**Choices:**

- $4.73 \le x < 4.74$. ✔
  - _why:_ Truncating to two decimal places keeps every value from 4.73 up to 4.74. The lower boundary is included, while 4.74 is excluded.
- $4.725 \le x < 4.735$.
  - _why:_ These are the boundaries for rounding 4.73 to two decimal places. Truncation begins at 4.73 and extends to, but does not include, 4.74.
- $4.73 < x \le 4.74$.
  - _why:_ The endpoint inequalities have been reversed. The value 4.73 can truncate to 4.73, but 4.74 cannot, so $4.73 \le x < 4.74$.
- $4.72 \le x < 4.73$.
  - _why:_ This is the interval for values that truncate to 4.72, an off-by-one place-value increment. Values truncating to 4.73 satisfy $4.73 \le x < 4.74$.
