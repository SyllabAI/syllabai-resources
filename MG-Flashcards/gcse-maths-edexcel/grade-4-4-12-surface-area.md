# Surface Area Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 4 · 23 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** Surface area is the total area of all the [ ___ ] of a 3D shape.

**Back:** Surface area is the total area of all the outside faces of a 3D shape.

**Choices:**

- outside faces ✔
  - _why:_ Surface area is found by adding the areas of every outside face of a 3D shape. Only the surfaces exposed on the exterior are included.
- inside angles
  - _why:_ Angles describe turns between edges and do not have an area. The correct term is outside faces, because surface area totals the exterior surfaces of the shape.
- vertical edges
  - _why:_ Edges are one-dimensional lengths, so adding them would not give an area. Surface area is the total area of all the outside faces.
- corner points
  - _why:_ Corner points, or vertices, have no area and are counted rather than measured. The required term is outside faces, whose areas combine to give surface area.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** Surface area is measured in what type of units?

**Back:** Square units, e.g. cm² or m².

**Choices:**

- Square units, e.g. cm² or m². ✔
  - _why:_ Area measures two-dimensional space, so each length unit is multiplied by another length unit. This produces square units such as cm² or m².
- Linear units, e.g. cm or m.
  - _why:_ Linear units measure one-dimensional quantities such as length or perimeter. Surface area is two-dimensional, so it is measured in square units such as cm² or m².
- Cubic units, e.g. cm³ or m³.
  - _why:_ Cubic units measure three-dimensional volume, not the area covering a shape. Surface area is measured in square units such as cm² or m².
- Angle units, e.g. degrees or radians.
  - _why:_ Degrees and radians measure angles rather than the size of surfaces. Surface area measures two-dimensional regions, so square units such as cm² or m² are required.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What formula is used for the area of a rectangular face?

**Back:** $\text{length}\times\text{width}$

**Choices:**

- $\text{length}\times\text{width}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle’s area is found by multiplying its two perpendicular dimensions. Therefore, the area of a rectangular face is length multiplied by width.
- $2\times\text{length}+2\times\text{width}$
  - _why:_ This adds the four side lengths and therefore calculates the rectangle’s perimeter. Its area is found using length multiplied by width.
- $\frac{1}{2}\times\text{length}\times\text{width}$
  - _why:_ The factor of one-half belongs to the triangle area formula. A rectangular face occupies twice that triangular area, so its formula is length multiplied by width.
- $\text{length}+\text{width}$
  - _why:_ Adding the dimensions does not calculate either the complete perimeter or the enclosed area. The area of a rectangular face is length multiplied by width.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** The perpendicular height is measured [ ___ ], not along a slanted side.

**Back:** The perpendicular height is measured at 90° to the base, not along a slanted side.

**Choices:**

- at 90° to the base ✔
  - _why:_ Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, so the perpendicular height must be measured at 90° to the base rather than along a sloping side.
- parallel to the base
  - _why:_ A line parallel to the base never forms the required height between the base and opposite point. The perpendicular height is measured at 90° to the base.
- along the longest side
  - _why:_ The longest side may be slanted and therefore may not represent the shortest distance from the base. The perpendicular height is measured at 90° to the base.
- at 45° to the base
  - _why:_ A 45° line is diagonal rather than perpendicular, so using it gives an incorrect height. The perpendicular height must be measured at 90° to the base.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Triangle area: [ ___ ], where $h$ is the perpendicular height.

**Back:** Triangle area: $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$, where $h$ is the perpendicular height.

**Choices:**

- $$A=\frac{1}{2}bh$ ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle occupies half the area of a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. Its area is therefore one-half multiplied by base multiplied by height.
- $$A=bh$
  - _why:_ This calculates the area of a rectangle or parallelogram with base $b$ and height $h$. A triangle has half that area, so $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $$A=\frac{1}{2}(b+h)$
  - _why:_ Adding base and height combines lengths and cannot produce square units. Triangle area requires multiplying them and halving the result: $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.
- $$A=2bh$
  - _why:_ This doubles the corresponding rectangle’s area instead of taking half of it. A triangle with base $b$ and perpendicular height $h$ has area $A=\frac{1}{2}bh$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** Trapezium area: [ ___ ], where $a$ and $b$ are parallel sides.

**Back:** Trapezium area: $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, where $a$ and $b$ are parallel sides.

**Choices:**

- $$A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ ✔
  - _why:_ The area of a trapezium equals its average parallel-side length, $\frac{a+b}{2}$, multiplied by its perpendicular height. This gives $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$A=(a+b)h$
  - _why:_ This uses the sum rather than the average of the parallel sides, making the area twice as large. The correct formula is $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$A=\frac{1}{2}abh$
  - _why:_ Multiplying both parallel sides together introduces an extra length factor and gives cubic units. Their lengths must be added, so $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$A=\frac{1}{2}(a-b)h$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the parallel sides finds their difference, not their average length. They must be added before multiplying by half the height: $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** A trapezium has parallel sides 12 cm and 6 cm, height 5 cm. What is its area?

**Back:** 45 cm²

**Choices:**

- 45 cm² ✔
  - _why:_ Using $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ gives $\frac{1}{2}(12+6)\times5=\frac{1}{2}\times18\times5=45$ cm².
- 90 cm²
  - _why:_ This omits the factor of one-half and calculates $(12+6)\times5$. Averaging the parallel sides gives $\frac{1}{2}(12+6)\times5=45$ cm².
- 30 cm²
  - _why:_ This uses only the 12 cm side and halves $12\times5$, ignoring the other parallel side. The trapezium formula gives $45$ cm².
- 15 cm²
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses half the difference between the parallel sides: $\frac{1}{2}(12-6)\times5$. Their lengths must be added, giving $45$ cm².

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why do you double the three different face areas for a cuboid?

**Back:** Opposite faces are the same size.

**Choices:**

- Opposite faces are the same size. ✔
  - _why:_ A cuboid has three pairs of opposite faces. The two faces in each pair have identical dimensions and areas, so each different face area is counted twice.
- Adjacent faces are the same shape.
  - _why:_ Adjacent faces can have different dimensions and areas, even though each is rectangular. Doubling works because opposite faces are the same size and form three matching pairs.
- Opposite edges are the same length.
  - _why:_ Equal opposite edge lengths help define the cuboid, but they do not directly explain doubling face areas. Each area is doubled because opposite faces are the same size.
- All six faces have the same area.
  - _why:_ All six faces have equal area only for a cube, not for every cuboid. A general cuboid has three pairs in which opposite faces are the same size.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** Cuboid total surface area: [ ___ ].

**Back:** Cuboid total surface area: $2lw+2lh+2wh$.

**Choices:**

- $$2lw+2lh+2wh$ ✔
  - _why:_ A cuboid has two faces of each area $lw$, $lh$ and $wh$, so its total surface area is $2lw+2lh+2wh$.
- $$lw+lh+wh$
  - _why:_ This counts only one face of each size. A cuboid has two of every face, so the correct formula is $2lw+2lh+2wh$.
- $$2lw+lh+wh$
  - _why:_ This doubles only the $lw$ face, leaving the other two pairs undercounted. All three face areas must be doubled, giving $2lw+2lh+2wh$.
- $$2l+2w+2h$
  - _why:_ This adds edge lengths rather than face areas, so it cannot calculate surface area. Multiplying pairs of dimensions gives the correct formula $2lw+2lh+2wh$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** A cuboid is 9 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. What is its total surface area?

**Back:** 150 cm²

**Choices:**

- 150 cm² ✔
  - _why:_ The three different face areas are $9\times4=36$, $9\times3=27$ and $4\times3=12$. Doubling their sum gives $2(36+27+12)=150$ cm².
- 75 cm²
  - _why:_ This adds one face of each size but forgets that every face has an opposite matching face. Doubling $36+27+12$ gives the correct total of 150 cm².
- 108 cm²
  - _why:_ This is the volume, found by multiplying all three dimensions, rather than the total area of the faces. The surface area is $2(36+27+12)=150$ cm².
- 138 cm²
  - _why:_ This counts only one $4\times3$ face instead of two. Including both of every face gives $72+54+24=150$ cm².

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** A prism has the same shape all the way through; the repeated end shape is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** A prism has the same shape all the way through; the repeated end shape is the cross-section.

**Choices:**

- cross-section ✔
  - _why:_ The cross-section is the repeated two-dimensional end shape that remains identical along the entire length of a prism.
- side elevation
  - _why:_ A side elevation is a two-dimensional view of an object, not necessarily its repeated end shape. The repeated shape running through a prism is its cross-section.
- surface area
  - _why:_ Surface area is the total area of all the prism’s outer faces, not the repeated end shape. That repeated shape is called the cross-section.
- longitudinal section
  - _why:_ A longitudinal section is a cut made along an object’s length. The identical end shape repeated through a prism is instead called its cross-section.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What formula gives the total surface area of any prism?

**Back:** $2\times\text{cross-section area}+\text{perimeter}\times\text{length}$

**Choices:**

- $2\times\text{cross-section area}+\text{perimeter}\times\text{length}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The two congruent ends contribute twice the cross-section area, while unfolding the side faces produces rectangles with combined area equal to perimeter times length.
- $\text{cross-section area}+\text{perimeter}\times\text{length}$
  - _why:_ This includes only one of the two congruent end faces. Both ends must be counted, so the formula begins with $2\times\text{cross-section area}$.
- $2\times\text{cross-section area}+\text{perimeter}+\text{length}$
  - _why:_ Adding perimeter and length does not produce an area because both are one-dimensional measurements. Their product gives the area of the rectangular side faces.
- $2\times\text{cross-section area}+2\times\text{perimeter}\times\text{length}$
  - _why:_ This doubles the side-face area unnecessarily. The perimeter already includes every edge around the cross-section, so the correct side-face term is just perimeter times length.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** In the prism formula, what does $\text{perimeter}\times\text{length}$ find?

**Back:** The rectangular side faces.

**Choices:**

- The rectangular side faces. ✔
  - _why:_ Each cross-section edge forms a rectangle whose other dimension is the prism’s length. Adding these rectangles gives the cross-section perimeter multiplied by the length.
- The two cross-section faces.
  - _why:_ The two end faces are found using twice the cross-section area. Perimeter multiplied by length instead gives the combined area of the rectangular side faces.
- The volume inside the prism.
  - _why:_ Volume is found by multiplying cross-section area by length. Multiplying the cross-section perimeter by length finds the combined area of the rectangular side faces.
- The perimeter of each end.
  - _why:_ The perimeter is already one factor in the calculation. Multiplying it by the prism’s length converts the boundary strips into the total area of the rectangular side faces.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Triangular prism: cross-section perimeter 36 cm, length 7 cm. What is the side-face area?

**Back:** 252 cm²

**Choices:**

- 252 cm² ✔
  - _why:_ The side-face area of a prism is cross-section perimeter multiplied by length. Therefore, $36\times7=252$, giving a side-face area of 252 cm².
- 43 cm²
  - _why:_ This comes from adding 36 and 7 instead of multiplying them. Side-face area is perimeter times length, so $36\times7=252$ cm².
- 126 cm²
  - _why:_ This incorrectly halves the product, perhaps treating the side faces like a triangle. They are rectangles, so the full calculation is $36\times7=252$ cm².
- 504 cm²
  - _why:_ This doubles the product unnecessarily. The perimeter already includes every side of the triangular cross-section, so $36\times7=252$ cm² is the complete side-face area.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the area of an L-shaped end made by removing a corner from a rectangle?

**Back:** Subtract the missing rectangle’s area.

**Choices:**

- Subtract the missing rectangle’s area. ✔
  - _why:_ The L-shape is the complete outer rectangle with a smaller rectangular corner removed, so subtracting the missing rectangle’s area leaves the required L-shaped area.
- Add the missing rectangle’s area.
  - _why:_ Adding would include the removed corner and make the result larger than the outer rectangle. The missing rectangle’s area must be subtracted from the outer rectangle’s area.
- Subtract the missing rectangle’s perimeter.
  - _why:_ Perimeter measures boundary length and cannot be subtracted from an area. Find both rectangular areas, then subtract the missing rectangle’s area from the outer rectangle’s area.
- Multiply the missing rectangle’s area.
  - _why:_ Multiplying areas does not represent removing a corner and produces the wrong units. Subtract the missing rectangle’s area from the area of the complete outer rectangle.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** An L-shaped end fits inside 8 cm by 7 cm, with a 3 cm by 4 cm corner removed. What is its area?

**Back:** 44 cm²

**Choices:**

- 44 cm² ✔
  - _why:_ The complete outer rectangle has area $8\times7=56$ cm², and the removed corner has area $3\times4=12$ cm². Therefore, $56-12=44$ cm².
- 68 cm²
  - _why:_ This adds the missing corner’s area to the outer rectangle instead of removing it. The correct calculation is $56-12=44$ cm².
- 12 cm²
  - _why:_ This is only the area of the removed $3$ cm by $4$ cm corner. Subtracting it from the outer area gives $56-12=44$ cm².
- 56 cm²
  - _why:_ This is the area of the complete $8$ cm by $7$ cm rectangle and ignores the removed corner. Subtracting $12$ cm² gives 44 cm².

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Why trace around a composite cross-section when finding perimeter?

**Back:** To include the step edges.

**Choices:**

- To include the step edges. ✔
  - _why:_ Tracing the outside boundary ensures that every exposed edge is counted, including the short step edges that are easy to overlook in a composite cross-section.
- To include the internal joins.
  - _why:_ Internal joins are not exposed parts of the perimeter and must not be counted. Tracing is used to include all outside edges, especially the step edges.
- To include the face areas.
  - _why:_ Face areas are needed for surface area, not perimeter. Tracing the boundary helps identify all exposed lengths, including the step edges.
- To include the diagonal lengths.
  - _why:_ Diagonal lengths are not automatically part of a composite cross-section’s boundary. The purpose of tracing is to ensure the step edges are included.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** How many circular faces does a closed cylinder have?

**Back:** Two equal circular faces.

**Choices:**

- Two equal circular faces. ✔
  - _why:_ A closed cylinder has a circular face at each end. These two circles have the same radius, so they are equal in area.
- One circular face.
  - _why:_ One circular face describes an open cylinder with one end missing. A closed cylinder has two equal circular faces, one at each end.
- Three equal circular faces.
  - _why:_ The curved surface is not a circular face. A closed cylinder has exactly two equal circular faces, located at its two ends.
- Two unequal circular faces.
  - _why:_ A cylinder’s two end circles must be equal because they share the same radius. A closed cylinder therefore has two equal circular faces.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** Closed cylinder total surface area: [ ___ ].

**Back:** Closed cylinder total surface area: $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.

**Choices:**

- $$2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$ ✔
  - _why:_ The two circular ends have combined area $2\pi r^2$, while the curved surface has area $2\pi rh$. Adding them gives the total surface area.
- $$\pi r^2+2\pi rh$
  - _why:_ This includes only one circular end, omitting the second area of $\pi r^2$. A closed cylinder requires $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.
- $$2\pi r^2$
  - _why:_ This adds the two circular ends but omits the curved surface. The curved area is $2\pi rh$, so the full formula is $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.
- $$2\pi r^2+\pi rh$
  - _why:_ The curved surface uses the circumference $2\pi r$ multiplied by height, not $\pi r$ multiplied by height. The correct total is $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If a cylinder question gives the diameter, what must you do first?

**Back:** Halve it to get the radius.

**Choices:**

- Halve it to get the radius. ✔
  - _why:_ The radius is the distance from the centre to the circumference, which is half the diameter. Therefore, divide the given diameter by two first.
- Double it to get the radius.
  - _why:_ Doubling confuses the relationship between radius and diameter. The diameter is already twice the radius, so it must be halved to obtain the radius.
- Square it to get the radius.
  - _why:_ Squaring the diameter does not convert it into a radius; squaring is used later in circle-area calculations. First halve the diameter to get the radius.
- Use it as the radius.
  - _why:_ Using the diameter unchanged as the radius doubles the required length and makes area calculations four times too large. The diameter must be halved.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** If area is in cm², how do you convert it to m²?

**Back:** Divide by 10000.

**Choices:**

- Divide by 10000. ✔
  - _why:_ Since $1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}$, squaring the conversion gives $1\text{ m}^2=10000\text{ cm}^2$. Therefore, divide a value in cm² by 10000.
- Divide by 100.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 100 applies the linear length conversion without squaring it. Area conversion requires dividing by $100^2=10000$ to change cm² into m².
- Multiply by 10000.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 10000 converts from m² to cm², the opposite direction. To convert the smaller cm² units into m², divide by 10000.
- Divide by 1000000.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 1000000 incorrectly uses the cubic conversion associated with cm³ and m³. For square units, divide by $100^2=10000$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** In a paint question, what do you do after dividing by coverage per pot?

**Back:** Round up to the next whole pot.

**Choices:**

- Round up to the next whole pot. ✔
  - _why:_ Any positive fraction of an additional pot means another complete pot must be bought. Rounding up ensures there is enough paint for the entire area.
- Round down to the previous whole pot.
  - _why:_ Rounding down would leave too little paint whenever the result is not already a whole number. The number of pots must always be rounded up.
- Round normally to the nearest whole pot.
  - _why:_ Rounding to the nearest whole number can round down and leave insufficient paint. Since only complete pots can be bought, always round up.
- Leave it as a decimal number of pots.
  - _why:_ A decimal result represents the calculated proportion of a pot, but shops sell whole pots. It must be rounded up to the next whole pot.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** Four boxes need 10.24 m² of paint. One pot covers 3 m². How many pots are needed?

**Back:** 4 pots

**Choices:**

- 4 pots ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the required area by the coverage gives $10.24\div3\approx3.41$. Since only whole pots can be bought, this must be rounded up to 4 pots.
- 3 pots
  - _why:_ Three pots cover only $3\times3=9$ m², which is less than 10.24 m². The calculation gives about 3.41, so 4 pots are required.
- 3.41 pots
  - _why:_ This is the unrounded result of $10.24\div3$, but paint is purchased in complete pots. About 3.41 pots must be rounded up to 4 pots.
- 5 pots
  - _why:_ Rounding $10.24\div3\approx3.41$ up gives 4, not 5. Buying five pots would provide more complete pots than the calculation requires.
