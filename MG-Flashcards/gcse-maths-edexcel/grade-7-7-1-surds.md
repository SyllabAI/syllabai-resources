# Surds Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 7 · 11 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a surd?

**Back:** An irrational root left in exact form.

**Choices:**

- An irrational root left in exact form. ✔
  - _why:_ A surd is a root with an irrational value that is kept exactly using a root symbol, such as $\sqrt{2}$, rather than rounded to a decimal.
- A rational root left in exact form.
  - _why:_ A rational root can be evaluated exactly, so it is not a surd. A surd is an irrational root left in exact form, such as $\sqrt{3}$.
- An irrational root written in decimal form.
  - _why:_ Rounding an irrational root gives an approximation and removes its exact form. A surd keeps the irrational root exact, for example $\sqrt{5}$ rather than $2.236$.
- A recurring decimal left in exact form.
  - _why:_ A repeating decimal is rational because it can be expressed as a fraction. A surd is instead an irrational root retained in exact form.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you simplify a square-root surd fully?

**Back:** Find the largest perfect-square factor: $\sqrt{k^2m}=k\sqrt{m}$ for $k\geq0$, leaving no square factor greater than 1 inside the root.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $\sqrt{80}$ fully.

**Back:** $$\sqrt{80}=\sqrt{16\times5}=4\sqrt{5}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Simplify $3\sqrt{48}$ fully.

**Back:** $$3\sqrt{48}=3\sqrt{16\times3}=3\times4\sqrt{3}=12\sqrt{3}$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** When can you add or subtract surds by combining their coefficients?

**Back:** When the simplified root part is the same. For example, $3\sqrt{5}+2\sqrt{5}=5\sqrt{5}$, but $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ cannot be combined into one surd.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the multiplication rule for square roots of non-negative numbers?

**Back:** $$\sqrt{a}\times\sqrt{b}=\sqrt{ab}$ for $a,b\geq0$. For example, $\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{12}=\sqrt{36}=6$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(2+\sqrt{5})(3-\sqrt{5})$.

**Back:** $$6-2\sqrt{5}+3\sqrt{5}-5=1+\sqrt{5}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Expand and simplify $(4-\sqrt{7})^2$.

**Back:** $$16-8\sqrt{7}+7=23-8\sqrt{7}$. Remember the middle term in $(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Rationalise the denominator of $\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}}$.

**Back:** Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$: $\frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3}=2\sqrt{3}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Why does multiplying by the conjugate rationalise a denominator such as $a-\sqrt{b}$?

**Back:** $$(a-\sqrt{b})(a+\sqrt{b})=a^2-b$: the surd terms cancel. Multiply both numerator and denominator by $a+\sqrt{b}$, assuming the original denominator is non-zero.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Rationalise the denominator of $\frac{1}{4-\sqrt{7}}$.

**Back:** $$\frac{1}{4-\sqrt{7}}\times\frac{4+\sqrt{7}}{4+\sqrt{7}}=\frac{4+\sqrt{7}}{16-7}=\frac{4+\sqrt{7}}{9}$.
