## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** Boundary values are found by setting the quadratic expression equal to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Boundary values are found by setting the quadratic expression equal to zero.

**Choices:**

- zero ✔
  - _why:_ Boundary values occur where the quadratic changes sign or touches the axis, so they are found by solving the corresponding quadratic equation with the expression set equal to zero.
- one
  - _why:_ Setting the expression equal to one does not generally identify where its sign may change. The boundary values are found by setting the quadratic expression equal to zero.
- infinity
  - _why:_ Setting the expression equal to infinity cannot produce finite boundary values. Boundaries are the roots of the quadratic, found by setting its expression equal to zero.
- the discriminant
  - _why:_ A quadratic expression is not set equal to its discriminant to locate boundaries. Instead, set the expression equal to zero and solve for its roots.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What do boundary values do to the number line?

**Back:** They split it into regions.

**Choices:**

- They split it into regions. ✔
  - _why:_ Each boundary value divides the number line at a possible change of sign, creating separate regions that can be tested to determine where the inequality holds.
- They mark every solution.
  - _why:_ Boundary values are not automatically all solutions; strict inequalities exclude them. Their role is to split the number line into regions whose signs can be tested.
- They remove the negative regions.
  - _why:_ Boundary values do not remove sections of the number line. They split it into regions, allowing one test value to determine the quadratic’s sign in each region.
- They identify the positive region.
  - _why:_ Boundary values alone do not show which region satisfies the inequality. They split the number line into regions, after which signs or test values identify the solutions.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Find the boundary values for $x^2+7x+12\gt 0$.

**Back:** $x=-4$ and $x=-3$.

**Choices:**

- $x=-4$ and $x=-3$. ✔
  - _why:_ Setting the quadratic equal to zero gives $x^2+7x+12=(x+4)(x+3)=0$, so the two boundary values are $x=-4$ and $x=-3$.
- $x=4$ and $x=3$.
  - _why:_ These signs are reversed: $(x+4)(x+3)=0$ requires each bracket to equal zero. Therefore, the boundary values are $x=-4$ and $x=-3$.
- $x=-4$ and $x=3$.
  - _why:_ This mixes the signs when reading roots from the factors. Since the factorisation is $(x+4)(x+3)$, both roots are negative: $x=-4$ and $x=-3$.
- $x=-6$ and $x=-2$.
  - _why:_ These numbers multiply to $12$ but add to $8$, not $7$. The correct factor pair is $4$ and $3$, giving roots $x=-4$ and $x=-3$.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, the parabola opens [ ___ ].

**Back:** If the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, the parabola opens upwards.

**Choices:**

- upwards ✔
  - _why:_ A positive coefficient of $x^2$ makes the quadratic increase towards positive infinity at both ends, so its graph is a parabola that opens upwards.
- downwards
  - _why:_ A parabola opens downwards only when the coefficient of $x^2$ is negative. A positive coefficient means both arms rise, so it opens upwards.
- sideways
  - _why:_ A vertical parabola does not open sideways; that shape would arise from expressing $x$ in terms of $y^2$. A positive $x^2$ coefficient makes it open upwards.
- inwards
  - _why:_ A parabola cannot open inwards because its two arms separate as they extend. With a positive coefficient of $x^2$, both arms rise and it opens upwards.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** For an upward-opening quadratic, where is it positive relative to its roots?

**Back:** Outside the roots.

**Choices:**

- Outside the roots. ✔
  - _why:_ An upward-opening parabola lies above the $x$-axis to the left of the smaller root and to the right of the larger root, so it is positive outside.
- Between the roots.
  - _why:_ Between two distinct roots, an upward-opening parabola lies below the $x$-axis and is negative. It is positive on the two regions outside the roots.
- At the roots.
  - _why:_ At the roots, the quadratic equals zero rather than being positive. For an upward-opening quadratic, positive values occur in the regions outside the roots.
- To the right of the roots.
  - _why:_ Positivity is not confined to the right of both roots. An upward-opening parabola is positive on both outer regions: left of the smaller root and right of the larger.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** For an upward-opening quadratic, where is it negative relative to its roots?

**Back:** Between the roots.

**Choices:**

- Between the roots. ✔
  - _why:_ An upward-opening parabola falls below the $x$-axis after crossing the smaller root and rises above it after the larger root, so it is negative between them.
- Outside the roots.
  - _why:_ Outside two distinct roots, an upward-opening parabola lies above the $x$-axis and is positive. Its negative values occur in the region between the roots.
- At the roots.
  - _why:_ At either root, the quadratic’s value is zero, not negative. For an upward-opening quadratic with two roots, negative values occur strictly between those roots.
- Near the roots.
  - _why:_ The quadratic is negative across the whole interval between its roots, not only near one of them. An upward-opening parabola lies below the axis between both roots.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** $$\lt$ and $\gt$ are [ ___ ] inequalities, so boundary values are not included.

**Back:** $$\lt$ and $\gt$ are strict inequalities, so boundary values are not included.

**Choices:**

- strict ✔
  - _why:_ The symbols $
lt$ and $
gt$ require values to be genuinely smaller or larger, not equal. They are therefore strict inequalities and exclude boundary values.
- inclusive
  - _why:_ Inclusive inequalities allow equality and use $
le$ or $
ge$. The symbols $
lt$ and $
gt$ do not permit equality, so they are described as strict inequalities.
- non-strict
  - _why:_ Non-strict inequalities include equality and are written with $
le$ or $
ge$. Because $
lt$ and $
gt$ exclude equality, the correct description is strict.
- compound
  - _why:_ A compound inequality combines two inequality conditions; it does not describe whether endpoints are included. The symbols $
lt$ and $
gt$ are strict inequalities.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** $$\le$ and $\ge$ are [ ___ ] inequalities, so boundary values are included.

**Back:** $$\le$ and $\ge$ are inclusive inequalities, so boundary values are included.

**Choices:**

- inclusive ✔
  - _why:_ The symbols $
le$ and $
ge$ allow equality as well as inequality. Boundary values satisfy the equality case, so these are inclusive inequalities and include them.
- strict
  - _why:_ Strict inequalities use $
lt$ or $
gt$ and exclude equality. Since $
le$ and $
ge$ permit equality at boundary values, they are inclusive inequalities.
- exclusive
  - _why:_ Exclusive would mean the boundary values are left out, as with $
lt$ and $
gt$. The symbols $
le$ and $
ge$ include equality, so they are inclusive.
- open
  - _why:_ Open usually describes intervals whose endpoints are excluded. Because $
le$ and $
ge$ include equality and therefore include boundary values, these inequalities are inclusive.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** On a number line, what endpoint is used for $\lt$ or $\gt$?

**Back:** An open circle.

**Choices:**

- An open circle. ✔
  - _why:_ A strict inequality excludes the endpoint itself, so an open circle is used to show that the boundary value is not part of the solution.
- A closed circle.
  - _why:_ A closed circle includes the endpoint and is used for $\le$ or $\ge$. Since $\lt$ and $\gt$ exclude equality, the correct endpoint is an open circle.
- A square endpoint.
  - _why:_ A square endpoint is not the standard notation for inequalities on a number line. Strict inequalities use an open circle because the boundary value is excluded.
- An arrow endpoint.
  - _why:_ An arrow shows that a range continues indefinitely, rather than whether a boundary is included. For $\lt$ or $\gt$, the endpoint is an open circle.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** On a number line, what endpoint is used for $\le$ or $\ge$?

**Back:** A closed circle.

**Choices:**

- A closed circle. ✔
  - _why:_ The symbols $\le$ and $\ge$ include equality, so the endpoint belongs to the solution set and is represented by a closed circle.
- An open circle.
  - _why:_ An open circle excludes the endpoint and is used for strict inequalities. Because $\le$ and $\ge$ include equality, the correct endpoint is a closed circle.
- A square endpoint.
  - _why:_ A square endpoint is not standard number-line notation for an inclusive inequality. A closed circle shows that the boundary value is included in the solution.
- An arrow endpoint.
  - _why:_ An arrow indicates that values continue without bound, not that an endpoint is included. For $\le$ or $\ge$, inclusion is shown by a closed circle.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For a solution outside two roots, should the ranges be joined by and or or?

**Back:** or.

**Choices:**

- or. ✔
  - _why:_ An outside solution consists of two separate ranges, one below the smaller root and one above the larger root. A value can lie in either range, so use “or”.
- and.
  - _why:_ Using “and” would require a number to be below the smaller root and above the larger root simultaneously. Outside ranges are alternatives, so they are joined by “or”.
- both.
  - _why:_ A single value cannot normally lie in both separated outside ranges at once. It may belong to either outside range, so the correct joining word is “or”.
- neither.
  - _why:_ “Neither” would exclude both outside ranges rather than combine them as the solution. Values satisfying an outside inequality lie in one range or the other, so use “or”.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $x^2+5x+6\gt 0$.

**Back:** $x\lt -3$ or $x\gt -2$.

**Choices:**

- $x\lt -3$ or $x\gt -2$. ✔
  - _why:_ Factoring gives $(x+3)(x+2)\gt0$. The product is positive outside the roots $-3$ and $-2$, and strict inequality excludes both endpoints.
- $-3\lt x\lt -2$.
  - _why:_ This selects the interval where $(x+3)$ and $(x+2)$ have opposite signs, making their product negative. The positive solution is $x\lt-3$ or $x\gt-2$.
- $x\le -3$ or $x\ge -2$.
  - _why:_ This correctly identifies the outside regions but mistakenly includes the roots. At $x=-3$ and $x=-2$ the expression equals zero, so strict inequality requires $x\lt-3$ or $x\gt-2$.
- $x\lt -3$ and $x\gt -2$.
  - _why:_ No value can be less than $-3$ and greater than $-2$ simultaneously. The two separate outside ranges must be joined by “or”: $x\lt-3$ or $x\gt-2$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $x^2-8x+15\le 0$.

**Back:** $3\le x\le 5$.

**Choices:**

- $3\le x\le 5$. ✔
  - _why:_ Factoring gives $(x-3)(x-5)\le0$. The product is non-positive between the roots, and equality means the endpoints $3$ and $5$ are included.
- $x\le 3$ or $x\ge 5$.
  - _why:_ This chooses the regions where both factors have the same sign, making the product positive. The non-positive region lies between the roots: $3\le x\le5$.
- $3\lt x\lt 5$.
  - _why:_ This excludes the roots as though the inequality were strict. Because the expression may equal zero, both roots are included, giving $3\le x\le5$.
- $-5\le x\le -3$.
  - _why:_ This comes from reversing the signs of the roots. The factors are $(x-3)(x-5)$, so the roots are $3$ and $5$, giving $3\le x\le5$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $x^2\le 64$.

**Back:** $-8\le x\le 8$.

**Choices:**

- $-8\le x\le 8$. ✔
  - _why:_ Since $x^2\le64$, the magnitude of $x$ is at most $8$. Both positive and negative values are allowed, including the endpoints where $x^2=64$.
- $x\le -8$ or $x\ge 8$.
  - _why:_ This describes values whose squares are at least $64$, reversing the required region. For $x^2\le64$, the solution is the interval $-8\le x\le8$.
- $-8\lt x\lt 8$.
  - _why:_ This treats the inequality as strict and wrongly excludes $-8$ and $8$. Both endpoints square to $64$, so the correct solution is $-8\le x\le8$.
- $-64\le x\le 64$.
  - _why:_ This uses $64$ directly as the boundary instead of taking its square root. Since $\sqrt{64}=8$, the correct interval is $-8\le x\le8$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Rearrange $5x+14\gt x^2$ so one side is zero and the $x^2$ coefficient is positive.

**Back:** $x^2-5x-14\lt 0$.

**Choices:**

- $x^2-5x-14\lt 0$. ✔
  - _why:_ Moving all terms gives $5x+14-x^2\gt0$. Multiplying by $-1$ changes every sign and reverses the inequality, producing $x^2-5x-14\lt0$.
- $x^2-5x-14\gt 0$.
  - _why:_ This keeps the inequality direction unchanged after multiplying every term by $-1$. Multiplication by a negative reverses the sign, so the result is $x^2-5x-14\lt0$.
- $x^2-5x+14\lt 0$.
  - _why:_ The constant sign has not been changed correctly when rearranging. Moving $14$ across, or multiplying by $-1$, makes it $-14$, giving $x^2-5x-14\lt0$.
- $x^2+5x-14\lt 0$.
  - _why:_ The sign of the linear term is wrong. Moving $5x$ to the side containing $x^2$ makes it $-5x$, so the correct inequality is $x^2-5x-14\lt0$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $5x+14\gt x^2$.

**Back:** $-2\lt x\lt 7$.

**Choices:**

- $-2\lt x\lt 7$. ✔
  - _why:_ Rearranging gives $x^2-5x-14\lt0=(x-7)(x+2)\lt0$. An upward-opening quadratic is negative between its roots, so $-2\lt x\lt7$.
- $x\lt -2$ or $x\gt 7$.
  - _why:_ This selects where the upward-opening quadratic is positive, outside its roots. The inequality requires it to be negative, so the solution is between the roots: $-2\lt x\lt7$.
- $-2\le x\le 7$.
  - _why:_ This mistakenly includes the roots, where $5x+14=x^2$ rather than being greater than it. The strict inequality therefore gives $-2\lt x\lt7$.
- $2\lt x\lt 7$.
  - _why:_ This loses the negative sign when solving $x+2=0$. The lower root is $-2$, not $2$, so the correct interval is $-2\lt x\lt7$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What happens to an inequality sign when you multiply or divide by a negative number?

**Back:** It reverses.

**Choices:**

- It reverses. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses their order on the number line, so the direction of the inequality sign must reverse.
- It stays the same.
  - _why:_ Keeping the sign unchanged is valid only when multiplying or dividing by a positive number. With a negative number, the inequality sign reverses.
- It becomes an equals sign.
  - _why:_ Multiplication or division cannot turn an inequality into an equation. When the operation uses a negative number, the inequality sign reverses direction.
- It reverses only when dividing.
  - _why:_ The sign reversal is not limited to division. Multiplying or dividing both sides by any negative number reverses the direction of the inequality.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** A non-monic quadratic has a coefficient of $x^2$ that is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A non-monic quadratic has a coefficient of $x^2$ that is not one.

**Choices:**

- not one ✔
  - _why:_ A quadratic is monic when the coefficient of $x^2$ is exactly one, so a non-monic quadratic has an $x^2$ coefficient that is not one.
- one
  - _why:_ A coefficient of one makes a quadratic monic, not non-monic. For a quadratic to be non-monic, the coefficient of $x^2$ must be not one.
- zero
  - _why:_ If the coefficient of $x^2$ were zero, the expression would no longer be quadratic. A non-monic quadratic instead has an $x^2$ coefficient that is not one.
- negative
  - _why:_ A non-monic quadratic may have a negative coefficient, but it need not do so. Its defining feature is that the coefficient of $x^2$ is not one.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $6x^2+7x-3\lt 0$.

**Back:** $-\frac{3}{2}\lt x\lt \frac{1}{3}$.

**Choices:**

- $-\frac{3}{2}\lt x\lt \frac{1}{3}$. ✔
  - _why:_ Factorising gives $(3x-1)(2x+3)\lt 0$, with roots $-\frac{3}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$. The upward-opening quadratic is negative between these roots.
- $-\frac{1}{3}\lt x\lt \frac{3}{2}$.
  - _why:_ This results from assigning the root signs incorrectly. Solving the factors gives $x=-\frac{3}{2}$ and $x=\frac{1}{3}$, and the required values lie between them.
- $\frac{1}{3}\lt x\lt \frac{3}{2}$.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly makes both boundary values positive. The factor $2x+3$ gives the negative root $-\frac{3}{2}$, so the solution is $-\frac{3}{2}\lt x\lt\frac{1}{3}$.
- $-\frac{3}{2}\lt x\lt -\frac{1}{3}$.
  - _why:_ The second boundary has the wrong sign: $3x-1=0$ gives $x=\frac{1}{3}$, not $-\frac{1}{3}$. The quadratic is negative between the two correct roots.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic does not factorise nicely, use the [ ___ ] to find boundary values.

**Back:** If a quadratic does not factorise nicely, use the quadratic formula to find boundary values.

**Choices:**

- quadratic formula ✔
  - _why:_ The quadratic formula finds the roots of any quadratic equation, including those that do not factorise neatly. These roots provide the boundary values for the inequality.
- linear formula
  - _why:_ Linear equations contain no $x^2$ term, so methods for them do not generally find quadratic roots. The quadratic formula gives the required boundary values.
- gradient formula
  - _why:_ The gradient formula calculates the slope between points and does not solve quadratic equations. The quadratic formula should be used to find the boundary values.
- Pythagoras’ theorem
  - _why:_ Pythagoras’ theorem relates the side lengths of right-angled triangles, rather than finding quadratic roots. The quadratic formula gives the inequality’s boundary values.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** What integer values satisfy $x^2-6x+7\lt 0$?

**Back:** 2, 3 and 4.

**Choices:**

- 2, 3 and 4. ✔
  - _why:_ The roots are $3-\sqrt{2}$ and $3+\sqrt{2}$, approximately $1.59$ and $4.41$. The upward-opening quadratic is negative between them, giving integers 2, 3 and 4.
- 1, 2, 3 and 4.
  - _why:_ Including 1 comes from rounding the lower root down rather than respecting the strict interval. Since $1$ lies below $3-\sqrt{2}$, only 2, 3 and 4 qualify.
- 2, 3, 4 and 5.
  - _why:_ Including 5 comes from rounding the upper root up rather than checking the strict interval. Since $5$ exceeds $3+\sqrt{2}$, only 2, 3 and 4 qualify.
- 3 and 4.
  - _why:_ This omits 2, although substituting it gives $4-12+7=-1$, which is less than zero. The satisfying integer values are 2, 3 and 4.
