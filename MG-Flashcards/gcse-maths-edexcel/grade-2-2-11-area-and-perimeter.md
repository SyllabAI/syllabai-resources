## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between perimeter and area?

**Back:** Perimeter is the total distance around the outside.Area is the amount of space inside.

**Choices:**

- Perimeter is the total distance around the outside.Area is the amount of space inside. ✔
  - _why:_ Perimeter measures the distance around a shape’s boundary, while area measures the amount of surface covered inside it. They also use different units: length units for perimeter and square units for area.
- Perimeter is the amount of space inside.Area is the total distance around the outside.
  - _why:_ These definitions have been swapped. The distance around the boundary is perimeter, while the surface covered inside the shape is area.
- Perimeter is the total distance across the middle.Area is the total distance around the outside.
  - _why:_ Perimeter follows the complete outside boundary, not just a line across the middle. Area describes the inside surface, rather than another distance around the shape.
- Perimeter is the amount of space inside.Area is the total distance across the middle.
  - _why:_ Both quantities have been confused with distances or interior space. Perimeter is the outside boundary distance, whereas area is the amount of space covered inside.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Perimeter uses units of length like [ ___ ], whereas area uses square units like cm² or m².

**Back:** Perimeter uses units of length like cm or m, whereas area uses square units like cm² or m².

**Choices:**

- cm or m ✔
  - _why:_ Perimeter measures a one-dimensional distance, so it uses ordinary length units such as centimetres or metres. Square units are reserved for measuring area.
- cm² or m²
  - _why:_ The squared units belong to area because area measures two dimensions. Perimeter measures one-dimensional distance and therefore uses cm or m.
- cm³ or m³
  - _why:_ Cubic units measure volume in three dimensions, not distance around a shape. Perimeter uses cm or m, while area uses squared units.
- mm² or km²
  - _why:_ Although millimetres and kilometres can measure length, the squared notation makes these area units. The blank requires ordinary length units: cm or m.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the area of a shape calculated on a centimetre grid?

**Back:** Multiply the number of squares across by the number of squares high.

**Choices:**

- Multiply the number of squares across by the number of squares high. ✔
  - _why:_ On a centimetre grid, counting across gives the width in centimetres and counting high gives the height in centimetres. Multiplying them gives the area in square centimetres.
- Add the number of squares across to the number of squares high.
  - _why:_ Adding the two dimensions gives a length sum, not the number of square units covered. The area is found by multiplying the number across by the number high.
- Multiply the number of squares across by 2, then add the number of squares high.
  - _why:_ This mixes an incomplete perimeter-style calculation with the grid dimensions. Area requires both dimensions to be multiplied together: across times high.
- Multiply the number of squares high by the number of squares around.
  - _why:_ Area uses the two perpendicular dimensions, across and high. Counting around is associated with perimeter and does not give the number of square units inside.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** State the formulas for the area ($A$) and perimeter ($P$) of a rectangle with length $l$ and width $w$.

**Back:** $A = l \times w$$P = 2l + 2w$

**Choices:**

- $A = l \times w$$P = 2l + 2w$ ✔
  - _why:_ A rectangle’s area is found by multiplying its length by its width. Its perimeter includes two lengths and two widths, giving $2l + 2w$.
- $A = 2l + 2w$$P = l \times w$
  - _why:_ The two formulas have been exchanged. Multiplication gives the rectangle’s area, while adding two lengths and two widths gives its perimeter.
- $A = l + w$$P = 2lw$
  - _why:_ Adding just one length and width omits half the boundary, while multiplying all dimensions does not represent perimeter. The correct formulas are $A = l \times w$ and $P = 2l + 2w$.
- $A = 2l \times 2w$$P = l + w$
  - _why:_ Doubling both dimensions incorrectly quadruples the area, and adding one length and width gives only half the perimeter. Use $A = l \times w$ and $P = 2l + 2w$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a square with side length $s$, the area formula is $A = \text{[...]}$ and the perimeter formula is $P = 4s$.

**Back:** For a square with side length $s$, the area formula is $A = \boxed{s^2}$ and the perimeter formula is $P = 4s$.

**Choices:**

- $$s^2$ ✔
  - _why:_ A square has equal length and width, both equal to $s$. Using length times width gives $s \times s = s^2$ for its area.
- $$2s$
  - _why:_ Doubling the side gives two sides, not the whole area. The square’s area is found by multiplying the side by itself, giving $s^2$.
- $$4s$
  - _why:_ Multiplying the side by four calculates the square’s perimeter, since it has four equal sides. Its area requires $s \times s$, which is $s^2$.
- $$s + 4$
  - _why:_ Adding four does not combine the square’s two dimensions and has the wrong algebraic structure. Multiplying the equal side lengths gives the area as $s^2$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a square with side length $s$, the area formula is $A = s^2$ and the perimeter formula is $P = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** For a square with side length $s$, the area formula is $A = s^2$ and the perimeter formula is $P = \boxed{4s}$.

**Choices:**

- $$4s$ ✔
  - _why:_ A square has four equal sides, each of length $s$. Adding them gives $s+s+s+s$, which simplifies to $4s$.
- $$s^2$
  - _why:_ Squaring the side calculates the square’s area, not the distance around it. The perimeter is the sum of four equal sides, giving $4s$.
- $$2s$
  - _why:_ This accounts for only two of the square’s four equal sides. Including every side gives $s+s+s+s = 4s$.
- $$s + 4$
  - _why:_ The number of sides is a multiplier, not a quantity to add to the side length. Four sides of length $s$ produce a perimeter of $4s$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** A square has an area of 49 cm². How do you find its perimeter?

**Back:** Find the square root of the area to get the side length ($7$ cm).Multiply the side length by 4 ($28$ cm).

**Choices:**

- Find the square root of the area to get the side length ($7$ cm).Multiply the side length by 4 ($28$ cm). ✔
  - _why:_ For a square, the side length is the square root of its area, so $\sqrt{49}=7$ cm. A square has four equal sides, giving a perimeter of $4 \times 7 = 28$ cm.
- Divide the area by 4 to get the side length ($12.25$ cm).Multiply the side length by 4 ($49$ cm).
  - _why:_ Dividing the area by four does not find a square’s side length; area must be square-rooted. The side is $7$ cm, so the perimeter is $28$ cm.
- Find the square root of the area to get the side length ($7$ cm).Multiply the side length by 2 ($14$ cm).
  - _why:_ The side length is correctly obtained as $7$ cm, but multiplying by two counts only two sides. A square has four equal sides, so its perimeter is $28$ cm.
- Use the area as the side length ($49$ cm).Multiply the side length by 4 ($196$ cm).
  - _why:_ An area value cannot be used directly as a side length because it is measured in square centimetres. Taking $\sqrt{49}$ gives $7$ cm, leading to a perimeter of $28$ cm.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** A rectangle has perimeter 30 cm and its length is twice its width. How many "parts" are used to find the width?

**Back:** 6 parts (width + length + width + length = 1 + 2 + 1 + 2).

**Choices:**

- 6 parts (width + length + width + length = 1 + 2 + 1 + 2). ✔
  - _why:_ The two widths contribute 1 part each and the two lengths contribute 2 parts each. Therefore, the perimeter represents $1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6$ parts.
- 4 parts (width + length + width + length = 1 + 1 + 1 + 1).
  - _why:_ This treats every side as one part and ignores that each length is twice the width. The correct ratio sum is $1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6$ parts.
- 5 parts (width + length + width + length = 1 + 2 + 1 + 1).
  - _why:_ This counts only one length as twice the width. Both lengths represent 2 parts, so the complete perimeter contains $1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6$ parts.
- 8 parts (width + length + width + length = 2 + 2 + 2 + 2).
  - _why:_ This incorrectly counts the widths as 2 parts as well as the lengths. Each width is 1 part, giving $1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6$ parts.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** The area of a triangle with base $b$ and height $h$ is $A = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The area of a triangle with base $b$ and height $h$ is $A = \boxed{\frac{b \times h}{2}}$.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{b \times h}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ A triangle occupies half the area of a parallelogram or rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height, so $b \times h$ must be divided by 2.
- $$b \times h$
  - _why:_ This is the area formula for a parallelogram and misses the halving required for a triangle. The triangle’s area is $\frac{b \times h}{2}$.
- $$\frac{b + h}{2}$
  - _why:_ Adding the base and height does not produce an area because lengths must be multiplied to create square units. The correct formula is $\frac{b \times h}{2}$.
- $$\frac{b \times h}{4}$
  - _why:_ This divides the base-height product by 4 instead of by 2, making the area half its correct value. The triangle’s area is $\frac{b \times h}{2}$.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** The area of a parallelogram with base $b$ and height $h$ is $A = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The area of a parallelogram with base $b$ and height $h$ is $A = \boxed{b \times h}$.

**Choices:**

- $$b \times h$ ✔
  - _why:_ A parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height, so its area is found by multiplying $b$ by $h$.
- $$\frac{b \times h}{2}$
  - _why:_ Dividing by 2 confuses the parallelogram formula with the triangle formula. A parallelogram uses the full base-height product, so its area is $b \times h$.
- $$b + h$
  - _why:_ Adding two lengths does not calculate area or produce square units. The area of a parallelogram is found by multiplying its base by its perpendicular height: $b \times h$.
- $$2b + 2h$
  - _why:_ This resembles a rectangle’s perimeter formula and calculates a boundary length rather than an area. A parallelogram’s area is the base-height product $b \times h$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What specific type of height must be used for triangle and parallelogram area formulas?

**Back:** The perpendicular height (at 90° to the base).

**Choices:**

- The perpendicular height (at 90° to the base). ✔
  - _why:_ Area depends on the shortest distance from the base to the opposite vertex or side. That distance is measured perpendicular to the base, forming a 90° angle.
- The sloping height (along an angled side of the shape).
  - _why:_ A sloping side is not generally the shortest distance from the base and will give an incorrect area. The perpendicular height must meet the base at 90°.
- The vertical height (straight up from the bottom edge).
  - _why:_ The required height is not necessarily vertical because the chosen base may be tilted. It must be perpendicular to that base, meeting it at 90°.
- The longest side (the greatest distance around the shape).
  - _why:_ Side length does not determine the required height unless it happens to be perpendicular to the base. Area formulas require the perpendicular height measured at 90°.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you avoid using the sloping side when calculating the area of a parallelogram?

**Back:** The height must be perpendicular (90°) to the base to correctly calculate the space inside.

**Choices:**

- The height must be perpendicular (90°) to the base to correctly calculate the space inside. ✔
  - _why:_ The perpendicular height measures the shortest distance between the parallel sides. Multiplying this distance by the base gives the parallelogram’s area correctly.
- The height must be parallel (0°) to the base to correctly calculate the space inside.
  - _why:_ A line parallel to the base does not measure the distance between the parallel sides. The required height is perpendicular to the base, forming a 90° angle.
- The height must be equal in length to the base to correctly calculate the space inside.
  - _why:_ The base and height do not need to have equal lengths. The relevant condition is that the height meets the base perpendicularly at 90°.
- The height must be diagonal across the shape to correctly calculate the space inside.
  - _why:_ A diagonal connects opposite vertices and is not generally the required distance between the parallel sides. The area formula uses the perpendicular height at 90° to the base.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate the perimeter of a compound shape?

**Back:** Sum only the outside edges; do not count edges where shapes are joined.

**Choices:**

- Sum only the outside edges; do not count edges where shapes are joined. ✔
  - _why:_ Perimeter measures the total distance around the outside boundary. Edges where component shapes meet are internal, so they must not be included.
- Sum only the inside edges; count edges where shapes are joined.
  - _why:_ Counting inside joined edges adds lengths that do not form part of the boundary. Perimeter is found by adding only the outside edges of the compound shape.
- Multiply the outside edges; do not count edges where shapes are joined.
  - _why:_ Multiplying edge lengths does not calculate perimeter. Perimeter is a total distance, so add every outside edge and exclude edges where shapes meet.
- Sum all the edges; count edges where shapes are joined.
  - _why:_ Joined edges are internal and are not part of the boundary, so including them makes the perimeter too large. Add only the outside edges.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Two 6 cm by 3 cm rectangles are joined along one 3 cm edge. How does this affect the total perimeter compared to two separate rectangles?

**Back:** The perimeter decreases by 6 cm (the joined edge is subtracted twice as it is no longer on the outside).

**Choices:**

- The perimeter decreases by 6 cm (the joined edge is subtracted twice as it is no longer on the outside). ✔
  - _why:_ Each separate rectangle includes the shared 3 cm edge in its perimeter. After joining, both copies become internal, so the total decreases by $3+3=6$ cm.
- The perimeter decreases by 3 cm (the joined edge is subtracted once as it is no longer on the outside).
  - _why:_ The shared 3 cm edge was counted once on each separate rectangle, so joining removes it twice. The total perimeter therefore decreases by 6 cm.
- The perimeter increases by 6 cm (the joined edge is added twice to the outside).
  - _why:_ Joining creates an internal edge rather than two new outside edges. Removing the shared edge from both original perimeters decreases the total by 6 cm.
- The perimeter decreases by 12 cm (both rectangles lose all their joined sides and widths).
  - _why:_ Only the two counted copies of the shared 3 cm edge disappear. Thus the reduction is $3+3=6$ cm, not the full widths of both rectangles.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the three steps to calculate the area of a trapezium.

**Back:** Add the two parallel sides.Halve the sum.Multiply by the perpendicular height.

**Choices:**

- Add the two parallel sides.Halve the sum.Multiply by the perpendicular height. ✔
  - _why:_ The trapezium’s area is the average of its two parallel sides multiplied by the perpendicular height: $rac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- Subtract the two parallel sides.Double the difference.Divide by the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ Area uses the average of the parallel sides multiplied by the perpendicular height. Subtracting and dividing reverses the required operations and does not produce the area.
- Add the two parallel sides.Double the sum.Divide by the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ The sum must be halved, not doubled, and then multiplied by the height. Doubling and dividing gives the wrong relationship between the dimensions and area.
- Multiply the two parallel sides.Halve the product.Add the perpendicular height.
  - _why:_ The parallel sides are averaged by adding them and halving the sum; they are not multiplied together. The resulting average is then multiplied by the height.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the area of a trapezium with parallel sides $a$ and $b$ and height $h$ is $A = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The formula for the area of a trapezium with parallel sides $a$ and $b$ and height $h$ is $A = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h}$.

**Choices:**

- $$rac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ ✔
  - _why:_ The trapezium’s area is half the sum of its parallel sides multiplied by the perpendicular height, giving $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$rac{1}{2}(a-b)h$
  - _why:_ Subtracting the parallel sides finds their difference rather than their average. The area formula requires their sum, so the correct expression is $\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$(a+b)h$
  - _why:_ This omits the halving step, making the area twice as large as it should be. The correct formula averages the parallel sides: $\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- $$rac{1}{2}(a+b+h)$
  - _why:_ Adding the height inside the brackets treats the dimensions as a total rather than multiplying the average parallel side length by the height. The correct formula is $\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** A trapezium has area 48 cm² and parallel sides of 4 cm and 12 cm. How do you find the height ($h$)?

**Back:** Find half the sum of parallel sides: $0.5 \times (4 + 12) = 8$.Divide the area by this result: $48 \div 8 = 6$ cm.

**Choices:**

- Find half the sum of parallel sides: $0.5 \times (4 + 12) = 8$.Divide the area by this result: $48 \div 8 = 6$ cm. ✔
  - _why:_ Using $A=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, the half-sum is $0.5\times16=8$. Rearranging gives $h=48\div8=6$ cm.
- Find half the sum of parallel sides: $0.5 \times (4 + 12) = 8$.Multiply the area by this result: $48 \times 8 = 384$ cm.
  - _why:_ Rearranging the area formula requires dividing the area by the half-sum, not multiplying. Since the half-sum is 8, the height is $48\div8=6$ cm.
- Find the sum of parallel sides: $4 + 12 = 16$.Divide the area by this result: $48 \div 16 = 3$ cm.
  - _why:_ The formula uses half the sum of the parallel sides, which is 8, not the full sum of 16. Therefore the height is $48\div8=6$ cm.
- Find half the difference of parallel sides: $0.5 \times (12 - 4) = 4$.Divide the area by this result: $48 \div 4 = 12$ cm.
  - _why:_ The area formula uses the half-sum, not half the difference. Using the difference gives 12 cm, whereas $0.5\times(4+12)=8$ and the height is 6 cm.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the three essential exam techniques for area and perimeter questions?

**Back:** Underline if the question asks for area or perimeter.Write the formula before substituting numbers.Check the units (e.g., cm vs cm²).

**Choices:**

- Underline if the question asks for area or perimeter.Write the formula before substituting numbers.Check the units (e.g., cm vs cm²). ✔
  - _why:_ Identifying area or perimeter selects the correct method, writing the formula makes the calculation clear, and checking units catches errors such as using cm instead of cm².
- Underline if the question asks for area or perimeter.Write the answer before substituting numbers.Check the units (e.g., cm vs cm²).
  - _why:_ Writing the answer before calculating skips the essential formula step and encourages unsupported answers. Write the formula first, then substitute values and check the units.
- Circle if the question asks for area or perimeter.Write the formula before substituting numbers.Check the units (e.g., cm vs cm²).
  - _why:_ Circling can help with reading, but the stated technique is to underline whether area or perimeter is required. This helps choose the correct formula before calculating.
- Underline if the question asks for area or perimeter.Write the formula before substituting numbers.Ignore the units (e.g., cm vs cm²).
  - _why:_ Ignoring units can hide errors between lengths and areas. Area needs squared units such as cm², while perimeter needs ordinary length units such as cm.
