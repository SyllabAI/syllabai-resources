## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** The main routine is: make the [ ___ ] match first, then calculate, then answer in the units asked for.

**Back:** The main routine is: make the units match first, then calculate, then answer in the units asked for.

**Choices:**

- units ✔
  - _why:_ Quantities must use matching units before they can be combined or compared. Converting first prevents errors such as directly mixing pounds with pence.
- digits
  - _why:_ Matching digits may help with written arithmetic, but it does not make differently measured quantities compatible. The units must match before calculating.
- operations
  - _why:_ Choosing an operation is important, but operations do not need to “match”. Convert the units to the same form first, then perform the required calculation.
- answers
  - _why:_ Answers are found after the calculation rather than matched beforehand. The units must be made consistent first so the arithmetic compares equivalent quantities.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In money questions, [ ___ ] makes £1.

**Back:** In money questions, 100p makes £1.

**Choices:**

- 100p ✔
  - _why:_ There are 100 pence in one pound, so amounts can be converted from pounds to pence by multiplying the number of pounds by 100.
- 10p
  - _why:_ This confuses pounds with a smaller decimal subdivision. One pound contains 100p, not 10p, because one penny is one hundredth of a pound.
- 50p
  - _why:_ This treats half a pound as a whole pound. Since 50p equals £0.50, the amount required to make £1 is 100p.
- 1000p
  - _why:_ This adds an extra factor of ten when converting currency. Since £1 equals 100p, 1000p is actually equal to £10.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you not subtract 434p directly from £10?

**Back:** Because the units differ; convert to pence or pounds first.

**Choices:**

- Because the units differ; convert to pence or pounds first. ✔
  - _why:_ Pounds and pence are different units, so direct subtraction would compare unlike quantities. Convert £10 to 1000p, or 434p to £4.34, first.
- Because the decimal places differ; round both amounts first.
  - _why:_ The issue is not differing decimal places or a need to round. The units differ, so both amounts must first be expressed in pounds or pence.
- Because the amounts differ; add them together before subtracting.
  - _why:_ Different monetary amounts can be subtracted, so their unequal values are not the problem. Their units differ and must be converted to pounds or pence first.
- Because the currency differs; convert both amounts into euros first.
  - _why:_ Both amounts use the same currency, sterling, so converting to euros is unnecessary. The mismatch is between pounds and pence, which must be made consistent.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** 7 cakes cost 62p each. What is the total cost?

**Back:** The total cost is 434p.

**Choices:**

- The total cost is 434p. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying the number of cakes by the price of each gives $7 \times 62 = 434$, so the seven cakes cost 434p altogether.
- The total cost is 424p.
  - _why:_ This results from an arithmetic error in multiplying 62 by 7. The correct calculation is $7 \times 62 = 434$, giving a total of 434p.
- The total cost is 372p.
  - _why:_ This calculates the cost of only six cakes because $6 \times 62 = 372$. Seven cakes cost $7 \times 62 = 434$p.
- The total cost is 496p.
  - _why:_ This calculates the cost of eight cakes because $8 \times 62 = 496$. The question asks for seven, so the total is 434p.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Pay £10 for goods costing 434p. What change is given?

**Back:** The change is £5.66.

**Choices:**

- The change is £5.66. ✔
  - _why:_ Convert £10 to 1000p, then subtract: $1000 - 434 = 566$p. Converting 566p back to pounds gives £5.66.
- The change is £6.34.
  - _why:_ This commonly comes from mishandling the subtraction across place values. Correctly calculating $£10.00 - £4.34$ gives £5.66 change.
- The change is £5.76.
  - _why:_ This is a ten-pence subtraction error. Writing both amounts with two decimal places gives $£10.00 - £4.34 = £5.66$, not £5.76.
- The change is £56.60.
  - _why:_ This misplaces the decimal point by one place when converting pence back to pounds. The 566p change equals £5.66, not £56.60.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** When dividing but another full group cannot be made, the leftover amount is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** When dividing but another full group cannot be made, the leftover amount is the remainder.

**Choices:**

- remainder ✔
  - _why:_ The remainder is the amount left after forming as many complete equal groups as possible. It is smaller than the size of one full group.
- quotient
  - _why:_ The quotient is the result of division, representing the number of groups rather than the leftover amount. Any amount left over is the remainder.
- divisor
  - _why:_ The divisor is the number by which another number is divided, often representing group size. The leftover amount after making full groups is the remainder.
- multiple
  - _why:_ A multiple is produced by multiplying a number by an integer; it is not the leftover from division. The leftover amount is called the remainder.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** In as many as possible questions, which operation finds the number of full items?

**Back:** Use division.

**Choices:**

- Use division. ✔
  - _why:_ Division determines how many times the cost or size of one item fits into the total available amount, giving the number of complete items.
- Use multiplication.
  - _why:_ Multiplication finds the total for a known number of items, rather than how many items can be made. Finding the number of full items requires division.
- Use addition.
  - _why:_ Addition could repeatedly combine item costs, but it is not the direct operation for finding how many equal groups fit. Division gives the number of full items.
- Use subtraction.
  - _why:_ Subtraction may be repeated until insufficient money remains, but the direct operation for finding the number of complete equal groups is division.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** £4 is spent on 35p bars. How many full bars can be bought?

**Back:** 11 bars.

**Choices:**

- 11 bars. ✔
  - _why:_ Convert £4 to 400p and divide: $400 \div 35 = 11$ remainder 15. Therefore, 11 complete bars can be bought.
- 10 bars.
  - _why:_ This stops one group too soon. Ten bars cost 350p, leaving 50p, which is enough for one more bar, so 11 can be bought.
- 12 bars.
  - _why:_ This rounds $400 \div 35 \approx 11.43$ up, but only complete affordable bars count. Twelve bars cost 420p, so only 11 can be bought.
- 15 bars.
  - _why:_ This confuses the 15p remainder with the number of bars. Dividing 400p by 35p gives 11 complete bars with 15p left.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** £4 is spent on 35p bars as many as possible. What change is left?

**Back:** The change is 15p.

**Choices:**

- The change is 15p. ✔
  - _why:_ £4 is 400p, and 11 bars cost 385p. A twelfth bar would exceed the budget, so the change is 400p - 385p = 15p.
- The change is 50p.
  - _why:_ This stops after buying only 10 bars for 350p. Another 35p bar can still be afforded, so 11 bars are bought and 15p remains.
- The change is 5p.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats 395p as a possible total cost, but 395 is not a multiple of 35. Eleven bars cost 385p, leaving 15p.
- The change is 0p.
  - _why:_ This assumes the whole £4 can be spent exactly. Since 400p is not divisible by 35p, 11 bars cost 385p and leave 15p.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** For full cups or cut lengths, should you round a remainder up or down?

**Back:** Round down.

**Choices:**

- Round down. ✔
  - _why:_ Only complete cups or lengths can be counted. Any remainder represents an incomplete item, so the quotient must be rounded down to the number of full items.
- Round up.
  - _why:_ Rounding up would count an incomplete cup or cut length as a complete one. Only full items count, so a remainder must be rounded down.
- Round to the nearest whole number.
  - _why:_ Ordinary rounding could count an incomplete item when the remainder is large. The requirement for full cups or lengths means always rounding down.
- Do not round.
  - _why:_ A decimal answer would include part of a cup or length, rather than counting complete items. The result must be a whole number rounded down.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** For buying enough packs, should you round a remainder up or down?

**Back:** Round up.

**Choices:**

- Round up. ✔
  - _why:_ A partly needed pack still requires buying one complete additional pack. Therefore, any non-zero remainder means the number of packs must be rounded up.
- Round down.
  - _why:_ Rounding down would leave some items uncovered because too few packs would be bought. Any remainder requires an additional pack, so round up.
- Round to the nearest whole number.
  - _why:_ Rounding to the nearest integer can round down when the remainder is small, leaving too few items. Buying enough packs always requires rounding up.
- Do not round.
  - _why:_ Shops sell complete packs, so a decimal number of packs cannot be purchased. Any remainder requires one more whole pack, meaning round up.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a group price, work out how many [ ___ ] are needed, then multiply by the group price.

**Back:** For a group price, work out how many groups are needed, then multiply by the group price.

**Choices:**

- groups ✔
  - _why:_ A group price applies once to each complete group. Find the number of groups required, then multiply that number by the stated price per group.
- items
  - _why:_ Counting individual items and multiplying by a group price charges the group rate once per item. The items must first be organised into groups.
- prices
  - _why:_ The price is the amount multiplied, not the quantity being counted. Work out how many groups are needed, then multiply by the group price.
- remainders
  - _why:_ A remainder only shows whether an additional group may be needed; it is not the quantity multiplied by the price. The required quantity is groups.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** 20 pens are sold at 5 for 80p. How many groups of 5 are needed?

**Back:** 4 groups.

**Choices:**

- 4 groups. ✔
  - _why:_ Each group contains 5 pens, so the number of groups is 20 ÷ 5 = 4. Four groups contain all 20 pens.
- 5 groups.
  - _why:_ This confuses the number of pens in each group with the number of groups. Dividing 20 pens by 5 pens per group gives 4 groups.
- 20 groups.
  - _why:_ This counts each pen as a separate group, ignoring that every group contains 5 pens. The correct calculation is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 groups.
- 100 groups.
  - _why:_ This multiplies 20 by 5 instead of dividing by the group size. Splitting 20 pens into groups of 5 produces 4 groups.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Costs are 320p, 720p and 2250p. What is the total in pounds?

**Back:** The total is £32.90.

**Choices:**

- The total is £32.90. ✔
  - _why:_ The costs total 320p + 720p + 2250p = 3290p. Dividing by 100 converts pence to pounds, giving £32.90.
- The total is £3290.00.
  - _why:_ This labels the total number of pence as pounds without converting it. The sum is 3290p, which equals £32.90 after dividing by 100.
- The total is £329.00.
  - _why:_ This moves the decimal point only one place, effectively dividing pence by 10. Converting 3290p to pounds requires division by 100, giving £32.90.
- The total is £3.29.
  - _why:_ This moves the decimal point three places and makes the total ten times too small. Since 100p equals £1, 3290p is £32.90.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find equal instalments, subtract the [ ___ ] first, then divide what is left.

**Back:** To find equal instalments, subtract the deposit first, then divide what is left.

**Choices:**

- deposit ✔
  - _why:_ The deposit is paid immediately, so it is removed from the full price first. The remaining balance is then divided equally among the instalments.
- instalment
  - _why:_ An instalment is one of the later equal payments, not the initial amount removed from the price. Subtract the deposit before dividing the balance.
- interest
  - _why:_ Interest may be added in some finance arrangements, but it is not the initial payment described here. The deposit is subtracted before calculating equal instalments.
- price
  - _why:_ Subtracting the full price from itself would leave nothing to divide. The correct method subtracts the deposit from the price, then divides the balance.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** A bike costs £540 with a £120 deposit and 12 equal payments. What is each payment?

**Back:** Each payment is £35.

**Choices:**

- Each payment is £35. ✔
  - _why:_ After the £120 deposit, the unpaid balance is £540 - £120 = £420. Dividing £420 equally across 12 payments gives £35 each.
- Each payment is £45.
  - _why:_ This divides the full £540 price by 12 and ignores the deposit already paid. The remaining £420 divided by 12 gives £35 per payment.
- Each payment is £55.
  - _why:_ This adds the £120 deposit to the price before dividing: £660 ÷ 12 = £55. The deposit must instead be subtracted, giving £35.
- Each payment is £420.
  - _why:_ This is the full balance remaining after the deposit, not one instalment. The £420 balance must be divided by 12, producing payments of £35.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** If several identical items have a known total cost, [ ___ ] to find the unit cost.

**Back:** If several identical items have a known total cost, divide to find the unit cost.

**Choices:**

- divide ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing the total cost by the number of identical items gives the cost of one item, which is the unit cost.
- multiply
  - _why:_ Multiplying would increase the total rather than find the cost of one item. Divide the total cost by the number of identical items.
- add
  - _why:_ Adding is used to combine separate costs, not to calculate a unit cost. Divide the known total by the number of identical items.
- subtract
  - _why:_ Subtracting finds a difference or removes an amount; it does not share the total equally. Divide by the number of items to find the unit cost.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Back:** 1 pen costs 30p.

**Choices:**

- 1 pen costs 30p. ✔
  - _why:_ Convert £1.20 to 120p, then divide by 4. Since 120 ÷ 4 = 30, each pen costs 30p.
- 1 pen costs 20p.
  - _why:_ This results from dividing 120p by 6 rather than by the 4 pens. The correct calculation is 120 ÷ 4 = 30p.
- 1 pen costs 24p.
  - _why:_ This results from dividing 120p by 5, an off-by-one error in the number of pens. Dividing by 4 gives 30p per pen.

  - _why:_ This results from dividing the total by 3 instead of 4. There are four pens, so 120p ÷ 4 = 30p.

  - _why:_ This includes only one pencil with the pen. Two pencils cost 30p altogether, so adding the 30p pen gives 60p.

  - _why:_ This effectively counts three pencils instead of two. One pen and two pencils cost 30p + 30p = 60p.

## Card 25 — `basic`

**Front:** A jug has 1.6 litres and each cup needs 180 ml. How many full cups?

**Back:** 8 full cups.

**Choices:**

- 8 full cups. ✔
  - _why:_ Convert 1.6 litres to 1600 ml, then calculate $1600 \div 180 = 8.88\ldots$. Only eight complete 180 ml cups can be filled.
- 9 full cups.
  - _why:_ Rounding $1600 \div 180 = 8.88\ldots$ up gives nine, but the ninth cup would not be full. Therefore, only eight full cups can be filled.
- 7 full cups.
  - _why:_ This results from underestimating the quotient after converting 1.6 litres to 1600 ml. Since $1600 \div 180 = 8.88\ldots$, eight full cups can be filled.
- 6 full cups.
  - _why:_ This may come from treating 1.6 litres as a smaller volume than 1600 ml. Dividing 1600 ml by 180 ml gives eight full cups, with some liquid remaining.

## Card 26 — `basic`

**Front:** Sam needs 900g of powder. Each pack has 600g. How many packs are needed?

**Back:** 2 packs.

**Choices:**

- 2 packs. ✔
  - _why:_ Calculate $900 \div 600 = 1.5$ packs. Since packs must be bought whole, Sam must round up to two packs to obtain at least 900g.
- 1 pack.
  - _why:_ One pack contains only 600g, leaving Sam 300g short of the required 900g. He therefore needs to buy two complete packs.
- 1.5 packs.
  - _why:_ Dividing $900 \div 600$ gives 1.5, but packs are bought as whole items rather than fractions. Sam must round up and buy two packs.
- 3 packs.
  - _why:_ Three packs would provide 1800g, which is twice the required amount. As 1.5 packs are mathematically required, rounding up means buying two packs.
