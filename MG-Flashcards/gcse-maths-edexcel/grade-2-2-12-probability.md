## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What interval must every probability lie in?

**Back:** From 0 to 1 inclusive.

**Choices:**

- From 0 to 1 inclusive. ✔
  - _why:_ A probability cannot be less than 0 or greater than 1. Probability 0 represents an impossible event, while probability 1 represents a certain event.
- From -1 to 1 inclusive.
  - _why:_ Including negative values confuses probability with scales that permit signed numbers. Every probability lies from 0 to 1 inclusive, because chances cannot be below impossibility.
- From 0 to 0.5 inclusive.
  - _why:_ This excludes probabilities greater than one half, including likely and certain events. The correct interval is from 0 to 1 inclusive.
- From 0 to 100 inclusive.
  - _why:_ Probabilities are not percentages unless converted, so the upper bound is 1 rather than 100. The correct interval is from 0 to 1 inclusive.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** Rolling a 5 is an [ ___ ]; rolling an even number is an [ ___ ].

**Back:** Rolling a 5 is an outcome; rolling an even number is an event.

**Choices:**

- Rolling a 5 is an outcome; rolling an even number is an event. ✔
  - _why:_ An outcome is one specific possible result, such as rolling 5. An event is a set of outcomes, such as rolling any even number.
- Rolling a 5 is an event; rolling an even number is an outcome.
  - _why:_ This reverses the definitions. Rolling 5 is one specific outcome, whereas rolling an even number describes the event containing outcomes 2, 4 and 6.
- Rolling a 5 is a trial; rolling an even number is an outcome.
  - _why:_ A trial is the action of rolling the die, not the result obtained. Rolling 5 is an outcome, while rolling an even number is an event.
- Rolling a 5 is a sample space; rolling an even number is a sample space.
  - _why:_ A sample space contains all possible results, so neither description is a sample space. Rolling 5 is an outcome, and rolling an even number is an event.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** A result that is not chosen on purpose is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A result that is not chosen on purpose is at random.

**Choices:**

- at random ✔
  - _why:_ A result obtained without deliberately selecting it is described as being at random. Random selection is intended to avoid purposeful choice influencing the result.
- biased
  - _why:_ A biased result is affected by an unfair method or unequal chances, not merely unchosen. A result not chosen on purpose is described as being at random.
- systematic
  - _why:_ A systematic result follows a planned rule or pattern, which conflicts with selection without purposeful choice. The required description is at random.
- certain
  - _why:_ A certain result must occur, whereas lack of purposeful selection does not guarantee any particular result. The correct description is at random.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** If each outcome has the same chance, the outcomes are [ ___ ].

**Back:** If each outcome has the same chance, the outcomes are equally likely.

**Choices:**

- equally likely ✔
  - _why:_ Outcomes are equally likely when every one has the same probability of occurring. For example, each face of a fair six-sided die has probability $\frac{1}{6}$.
- unequally likely
  - _why:_ Unequally likely means that some outcomes have different probabilities, directly contradicting the condition that each has the same chance. The outcomes are equally likely.
- certain
  - _why:_ Certain means having probability 1, rather than sharing the same chance with other possible outcomes. Outcomes with the same probability are equally likely.
- impossible
  - _why:_ Impossible outcomes each have probability 0 and cannot occur; equal non-zero chances are not described this way. Outcomes with the same chance are equally likely.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** For equally likely outcomes, what calculation gives the probability of an event?

**Back:** $\frac{\text{wanted outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{\text{wanted outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$ ✔
  - _why:_ For equally likely outcomes, probability is found by dividing the number of outcomes that satisfy the event by the total number of possible outcomes.
- $\frac{\text{total outcomes}}{\text{wanted outcomes}}$
  - _why:_ Reversing the fraction can produce a value greater than 1, which cannot be a probability. Divide wanted outcomes by total outcomes instead.
- $\frac{\text{unwanted outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$
  - _why:_ This calculates the probability of the complementary event by counting outcomes that are not wanted. The required probability uses wanted outcomes over total outcomes.
- $\frac{\text{wanted outcomes}}{\text{unwanted outcomes}}$
  - _why:_ Comparing wanted outcomes only with unwanted outcomes omits part of the sample space from the denominator. The denominator must be the total number of outcomes.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Cards show 2, 4, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9. What is the probability of picking a 4?

**Back:** $\frac{2}{7}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{7}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are seven cards in total and two of them show 4. Therefore, the probability of selecting a 4 is $\frac{2}{7}$.
- $\frac{1}{7}$
  - _why:_ This counts the value 4 only once because it treats repeated values as a single outcome. Two of the seven cards show 4, giving $\frac{2}{7}$.
- $\frac{2}{6}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly removes one of the two matching cards from the total after counting it. All seven cards remain possible selections, so the probability is $\frac{2}{7}$.
- $\frac{4}{7}$
  - _why:_ This uses the number printed on the desired cards as the numerator instead of counting those cards. There are two matching cards out of seven, giving $\frac{2}{7}$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a probability scale, 0 means [ ___ ] and 1 means [ ___ ].

**Back:** On a probability scale, 0 means impossible and 1 means certain.

**Choices:**

- On a probability scale, 0 means impossible and 1 means certain. ✔
  - _why:_ Probability 0 represents an event that cannot happen, so it is impossible. Probability 1 represents an event that must happen, so it is certain.
- On a probability scale, 0 means certain and 1 means impossible.
  - _why:_ This reverses the endpoints of the probability scale. Probability 0 means impossible because the event cannot occur, while probability 1 means certain.
- On a probability scale, 0 means unlikely and 1 means likely.
  - _why:_ Unlikely and likely describe probabilities near the endpoints, not exactly at them. Probability 0 means impossible, and probability 1 means certain.
- On a probability scale, 0 means an even chance and 1 means an even chance.
  - _why:_ An even chance corresponds to probability $\frac{1}{2}$, so it cannot describe either endpoint. At 0 an event is impossible, and at 1 it is certain.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a probability scale, [ ___ ] means an even chance.

**Back:** On a probability scale, $\frac{1}{2}$ means an even chance.

**Choices:**

- $$\frac{1}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ An even chance means that an event is equally likely to happen or not happen. Its probability is therefore one half, written $\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$0$
  - _why:_ A probability of 0 represents an impossible event, not an even chance. An event with an even chance has probability $\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$1$
  - _why:_ A probability of 1 represents a certain event, not one equally likely to happen or not happen. An even chance is represented by $\frac{1}{2}$.
- $$\frac{1}{4}$
  - _why:_ This gives a one-in-four chance, so the event is less likely to happen than not happen. An even chance has probability $\frac{1}{2}$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** On a probability scale, where is $\frac{1}{4}$ placed?

**Back:** Halfway between 0 and $\frac{1}{2}$.

**Choices:**

- Halfway between 0 and $\frac{1}{2}$. ✔
  - _why:_ The midpoint of 0 and $\frac{1}{2}$ is $\frac{1}{4}$, because half of $\frac{1}{2}$ is $\frac{1}{4}$.
- Halfway between 0 and 1.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of 0 and 1 is $\frac{1}{2}$, not $\frac{1}{4}$. The value $\frac{1}{4}$ is halfway between 0 and $\frac{1}{2}$.
- Halfway between $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ is $\frac{3}{8}$. The value $\frac{1}{4}$ is halfway between 0 and $\frac{1}{2}$.
- Halfway between 0 and $\frac{1}{4}$.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of 0 and $\frac{1}{4}$ is $\frac{1}{8}$, which is too small. The value $\frac{1}{4}$ lies halfway between 0 and $\frac{1}{2}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** On a probability scale, where is $\frac{3}{4}$ placed?

**Back:** Halfway between $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1.

**Choices:**

- Halfway between $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1. ✔
  - _why:_ The gap from $\frac{1}{2}$ to 1 is $\frac{1}{2}$, and moving halfway across it gives $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{4}$.
- Halfway between 0 and 1.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of the full probability scale from 0 to 1 is $\frac{1}{2}$. The value $\frac{3}{4}$ is halfway between $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1.
- Halfway between 0 and $\frac{3}{4}$.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of 0 and $\frac{3}{4}$ is $\frac{3}{8}$, not $\frac{3}{4}$. The correct endpoints are $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1.
- Halfway between $\frac{3}{4}$ and 1.
  - _why:_ The midpoint of $\frac{3}{4}$ and 1 is $\frac{7}{8}$, so this places the point too high. $\frac{3}{4}$ is halfway between $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** A fair dice is rolled once. What is the probability of rolling an 8?

**Back:** 0

**Choices:**

- 0 ✔
  - _why:_ A standard fair dice has outcomes 1 to 6, so rolling an 8 is impossible. An impossible event has probability 0.
- $\frac{1}{6}$
  - _why:_ The probability $\frac{1}{6}$ applies to one possible face, such as rolling a 4. Since 8 is not on a standard dice, its probability is 0.
- $\frac{1}{8}$
  - _why:_ Using 8 as the denominator mistakes the requested number for the number of possible outcomes. A dice has six outcomes and none is 8, so the probability is 0.
- 1
  - _why:_ A probability of 1 describes a certain event, but rolling an 8 on a standard six-sided dice is impossible. Its probability is therefore 0.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A fair dice is rolled once. What is the probability of rolling a number less than 7?

**Back:** 1

**Choices:**

- 1 ✔
  - _why:_ Every possible result on a standard dice - 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 - is less than 7, so the event is certain and has probability 1.
- $\frac{5}{6}$
  - _why:_ This commonly comes from leaving out 6, but 6 is less than 7 and must be included. All six outcomes qualify, giving $\frac{6}{6}=1$.
- $\frac{6}{7}$
  - _why:_ Using 7 as the denominator confuses the comparison value with the number of dice outcomes. All six possible outcomes are less than 7, so the probability is 1.
- $\frac{1}{6}$
  - _why:_ This counts only one successful outcome, but all six faces are numbered below 7. The event is certain, so its probability is $\frac{6}{6}=1$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** A fair dice is rolled once. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than 3?

**Back:** $\frac{1}{2}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{1}{2}$ ✔
  - _why:_ The results greater than 3 are 4, 5 and 6. That gives three favourable outcomes out of six, so the probability is $\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\frac{1}{3}$
  - _why:_ This counts only two successful outcomes, often by overlooking 4. The results greater than 3 are 4, 5 and 6, giving $\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\frac{2}{3}$
  - _why:_ This usually comes from including 3 among the successful outcomes. Greater than 3 excludes 3, leaving 4, 5 and 6, so the probability is $\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ The denominator must include all six possible dice outcomes, not only five of them. Three outcomes are greater than 3, so the probability is $\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}$.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** In probability, less than 3 means 1 and 2, so it [ ___ ].

**Back:** In probability, less than 3 means 1 and 2, so it excludes 3.

**Choices:**

- excludes 3 ✔
  - _why:_ The phrase “less than 3” includes only values strictly below 3. For whole-number dice outcomes, these are 1 and 2, so 3 itself is excluded.
- includes 3
  - _why:_ Including 3 confuses “less than” with “less than or equal to”. A strict inequality does not include its boundary value, so less than 3 excludes 3.
- excludes 2
  - _why:_ The number 2 is below 3, so it must be included. Less than 3 includes 1 and 2 but excludes the boundary value 3.
- includes only 3
  - _why:_ This confuses values below 3 with the value equal to 3. Less than 3 includes 1 and 2 and excludes 3 itself.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A box has 25 counters: 9 red, 6 blue and the rest green. How many are green?

**Back:** 10

**Choices:**

- 10 ✔
  - _why:_ The red and blue counters total $9+6=15$. Subtracting these from the 25 counters leaves $25-15=10$ green counters.
- 9
  - _why:_ This repeats the number of red counters instead of finding the remainder. After accounting for 9 red and 6 blue counters, $25-9-6=10$ are green.
- 15
  - _why:_ This is the combined number of red and blue counters, not the number of green counters. Subtracting it from 25 gives $25-15=10$ green counters.
- 16
  - _why:_ This comes from subtracting only the 9 red counters and forgetting the 6 blue counters. Both known colours must be removed, giving $25-9-6=10$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** A box has 25 counters: 9 red, 6 blue and the rest green. What is the probability of green?

**Back:** $\frac{2}{5}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{2}{5}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are $25-9-6=10$ green counters, so the probability is $\frac{10}{25}$. Dividing numerator and denominator by 5 simplifies this to $\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{3}{5}$
  - _why:_ This is the probability of selecting a counter that is not green, since there are 15 red or blue counters. Green has probability $\frac{10}{25}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{2}{3}$
  - _why:_ This incorrectly uses the 15 red and blue counters as the total. The denominator must be all 25 counters, so green has probability $\frac{10}{25}=\frac{2}{5}$.
- $\frac{2}{25}$
  - _why:_ This simplifies the numerator from 10 to 2 without also simplifying the denominator. Dividing both parts of $\frac{10}{25}$ by 5 gives $\frac{2}{5}$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** A bag has 5 red counters and 1 blue counter. What is the probability of red?

**Back:** $\frac{5}{6}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{5}{6}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are 6 counters altogether, and 5 of them are red. Therefore, the probability of selecting red is $\frac{5}{6}$.
- $\frac{1}{6}$
  - _why:_ This is the probability of selecting the single blue counter. Red has 5 favourable outcomes out of 6 counters, so its probability is $\frac{5}{6}$.
- $\frac{5}{5}$
  - _why:_ This uses only the five red counters as the denominator, incorrectly suggesting that every counter is red. The denominator must be the total of 6, giving $\frac{5}{6}$.
- $\frac{6}{5}$
  - _why:_ This reverses the required fraction, placing the total number above the number of favourable outcomes. Probability is favourable outcomes over total outcomes, so it is $\frac{5}{6}$.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** An event and its complement have probabilities that add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** An event and its complement have probabilities that add to 1.

**Choices:**

- 1 ✔
  - _why:_ An event and its complement cover all possible outcomes without overlapping. Their probabilities must therefore add to 1, representing certainty.
- 0
  - _why:_ A total probability of 0 represents an impossible event, not all possible outcomes. An event and its complement together are certain, so their probabilities add to 1.
- 2
  - _why:_ Probabilities across all mutually exclusive possible outcomes cannot total 2. An event and its complement exhaust the sample space, so their probabilities add to 1.
- 0.5
  - _why:_ A total of 0.5 would represent only half of the total probability. An event and its complement include every outcome, so their probabilities add to 1.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A player has probability 0.7 of winning. What is the probability of not winning?

**Back:** 0.3

**Choices:**

- 0.3 ✔
  - _why:_ Winning and not winning are complementary events, so their probabilities add to 1. Therefore, the probability of not winning is $1 - 0.7 = 0.3$.
- 0.7
  - _why:_ This repeats the probability of winning rather than finding its complement. Subtracting the winning probability from 1 gives $1 - 0.7 = 0.3$.
- 1.7
  - _why:_ This adds the winning probability to 1, producing a value greater than the maximum possible probability. The complement is found by $1 - 0.7 = 0.3$.
- 0.03
  - _why:_ This introduces an unnecessary decimal-place shift after finding the complement. The correct subtraction is $1.0 - 0.7 = 0.3$, not 0.03.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** A table shows 6 red, 4 blue, 5 yellow and 3 green counters. What is the probability of not blue?

**Back:** $\frac{7}{9}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{7}{9}$ ✔
  - _why:_ There are 18 counters in total and 14 are not blue. Thus the probability is $\frac{14}{18}$, which simplifies to $\frac{7}{9}$.
- $\frac{2}{9}$
  - _why:_ This is the probability of selecting blue, since 4 of the 18 counters are blue. The complement is $1 - \frac{2}{9} = \frac{7}{9}$.
- $\frac{7}{18}$
  - _why:_ This correctly identifies 14 non-blue counters but incorrectly uses 36 rather than 18 as the total before simplifying. The correct probability is $\frac{14}{18} = \frac{7}{9}$.
- $\frac{4}{9}$
  - _why:_ This uses the 4 blue counters as the numerator while retaining the simplified denominator incorrectly. There are 14 non-blue counters out of 18, giving $\frac{7}{9}$.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** A probability table has red 0.25, blue 0.15 and yellow 0.40. What is the missing green probability?

**Back:** 0.20

**Choices:**

- 0.20 ✔
  - _why:_ The probabilities must total 1. The known probabilities sum to $0.25 + 0.15 + 0.40 = 0.80$, leaving $1 - 0.80 = 0.20$.
- 0.80
  - _why:_ This is the sum of the three known probabilities, not the missing probability. Subtracting that sum from 1 gives $1 - 0.80 = 0.20$.
- 0.10
  - _why:_ This results from an arithmetic error when subtracting the known total from 1. Since the known probabilities total 0.80, the missing probability is 0.20.
- 0.60
  - _why:_ This subtracts only the red and blue probabilities and overlooks yellow. All known probabilities must be included, so $1 - 0.25 - 0.15 - 0.40 = 0.20$.
