## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In an equation, the left and right sides must have the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In an equation, the left and right sides must have the same value.

**Choices:**

- same value ✔
  - _why:_ An equation states that two expressions are equal, so both sides must represent the same value even when their forms look different.
- same number of terms
  - _why:_ The two sides do not need the same number of terms. They must have the same value because the equals sign states that they are equal.
- same operation
  - _why:_ Matching operations are not required on the two sides of an equation. The essential condition is that both sides have the same value.
- same variables
  - _why:_ The variables can differ between the two sides while the equation remains valid. It is their value, not their variables, that must be the same.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** If you subtract 6 from the left side of an equation, what must you do to the right side?

**Back:** Subtract 6 from the right side too.

**Choices:**

- Subtract 6 from the right side too. ✔
  - _why:_ Performing the same subtraction on both sides preserves equality. Subtracting 6 from each side keeps the equation balanced while helping to isolate the unknown.
- Add 6 to the right side too.
  - _why:_ Adding 6 uses the inverse operation but does not balance the subtraction performed on the left. You must subtract 6 from the right side too.
- Divide the right side by 6 too.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 6 confuses balancing an equation with undoing multiplication. Since 6 was subtracted on the left, subtract 6 from the right side too.
- Subtract 12 from the right side too.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 12 doubles the change made on the left, so equality is not preserved. You must subtract exactly 6 from the right side too.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** Inverse operations undo each other: adding ↔ [ ___ ] and multiplying ↔ [ ___ ].

**Back:** Inverse operations undo each other: adding ↔ subtracting and multiplying ↔ dividing.

**Choices:**

- Inverse operations undo each other: adding ↔ subtracting and multiplying ↔ dividing. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtraction reverses addition, while division reverses multiplication. Applying each inverse operation undoes the original operation and helps isolate an unknown.
- Inverse operations undo each other: adding ↔ multiplying and multiplying ↔ adding.
  - _why:_ Adding and multiplying are not inverse operations because one does not undo the other. Adding is undone by subtracting, and multiplying is undone by dividing.
- Inverse operations undo each other: adding ↔ subtracting and multiplying ↔ subtracting.
  - _why:_ The first pair is correct, but multiplying is not undone by subtracting. Multiplication is reversed by division, so the second missing term must be dividing.
- Inverse operations undo each other: adding ↔ dividing and multiplying ↔ dividing.
  - _why:_ Dividing does not reverse addition, although it does reverse multiplication. Addition is undone by subtraction, so the completed pairs are subtracting and dividing.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What does $7y$ mean?

**Back:** $7\times y$.

**Choices:**

- $7\times y$. ✔
  - _why:_ Writing a number directly beside a variable denotes multiplication. Therefore, $7y$ is the product of 7 and the value represented by $y$.
- $7+y$.
  - _why:_ Placing a coefficient beside a variable indicates multiplication, not addition. Therefore, $7y$ means $7\times y$, rather than adding 7 and $y$.
- $7-y$.
  - _why:_ Adjacent notation does not indicate subtraction. The 7 is a coefficient multiplying the variable, so $7y$ means $7\times y$.
- $7\div y$.
  - _why:_ The notation does not represent division by the variable. A number written directly before a variable multiplies it, so $7y$ means $7\times y$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** In $7y$, what is the coefficient?

**Back:** $7$.

**Choices:**

- $7$. ✔
  - _why:_ A coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable. In $7y$, the variable $y$ is multiplied by 7, so the coefficient is 7.
- $y$.
  - _why:_ The symbol $y$ is the variable, not the coefficient. The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying it, which is 7 in $7y$.
- $1$.
  - _why:_ A missing written coefficient would imply 1, but this expression explicitly shows 7 multiplying $y$. Therefore, the coefficient in $7y$ is 7.
- $-7$.
  - _why:_ Treating the coefficient as negative introduces a sign that is not present. Since $7y$ means 7 multiplied by $y$, its coefficient is 7.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $x+6=18$.

**Back:** $x=12$.

**Choices:**

- $x=12$. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting 6 from both sides gives $x=18-6=12$. Substitution confirms the result because $12+6=18$.
- $x=24$.
  - _why:_ Adding 6 instead of subtracting it applies the operation in the wrong direction. Subtracting 6 from both sides gives $x=18-6=12$.
- $x=3$.
  - _why:_ Dividing 18 by 6 confuses addition with multiplication. Since 6 is added to $x$, subtract 6 from 18 to obtain $x=12$.
- $x=-12$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting the larger number from the smaller reverses the intended calculation and creates a negative answer. The correct calculation is $18-6=12$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $p-7=11$.

**Back:** $p=18$.

**Choices:**

- $p=18$. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding 7 to both sides undoes the subtraction, giving $p=11+7=18$. Checking gives $18-7=11$, so the solution is valid.
- $p=4$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 7 again fails to undo the original subtraction. Adding 7 to both sides gives $p=11+7=18$.
- $p=-11$.
  - _why:_ Changing the sign of 11 does not isolate the variable and reflects an incorrect sign manipulation. Add 7 to 11 to obtain $p=18$.
- $p=77$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 11 by 7 confuses subtraction with multiplication. Because 7 is subtracted from $p$, add 7 to both sides to get $p=18$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $8a=56$.

**Back:** $a=7$.

**Choices:**

- $a=7$. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by 8 isolates $a$, giving $a=56\div8=7$. Checking confirms that $8\times7=56$.
- $a=448$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 56 by 8 repeats the multiplication rather than undoing it. Divide both sides by 8 to obtain $a=56\div8=7$.
- $a=48$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 8 treats $8a$ as though it meant $a+8$. Since $8a$ means $8\times a$, divide 56 by 8 to get 7.
- $a=\frac{1}{7}$.
  - _why:_ Reversing the division produces the reciprocal and confuses $56\div8$ with $8\div56$. Dividing 56 by 8 correctly gives $a=7$.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** A fraction bar means [ ___ ], so $\frac{y}{3}$ means $y\div3$.

**Back:** A fraction bar means division, so $\frac{y}{3}$ means $y\div3$.

**Choices:**

- division ✔
  - _why:_ A fraction bar represents division: the numerator is divided by the denominator. Therefore, $\frac{y}{3}$ means $y\div3$.
- multiplication
  - _why:_ Multiplication is indicated by symbols such as $\times$ or by adjacent factors. A fraction bar means division, so $\frac{y}{3}$ is $y\div3$.
- addition
  - _why:_ Addition combines quantities using a plus sign; it is not represented by a fraction bar. The fraction bar means division, giving $y\div3$.
- subtraction
  - _why:_ Subtraction requires a minus sign rather than a fraction bar. A fraction bar denotes division, so the denominator 3 divides the numerator $y$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $\frac{d}{2}=8.5$.

**Back:** $d=17$.

**Choices:**

- $d=17$. ✔
  - _why:_ Multiplying both sides of $\frac{d}{2}=8.5$ by 2 gives $d=8.5\times2=17$, preserving the equality.
- $d=4.25$.
  - _why:_ Dividing 8.5 by 2 repeats the existing division instead of undoing it. Multiply both sides by 2 to obtain $d=17$.
- $d=10.5$.
  - _why:_ Adding 2 treats the denominator as a subtracted term. Since $d$ is divided by 2, multiplication by 2 gives $d=17$.
- $d=-17$.
  - _why:_ Introducing a negative sign has no basis because both 8.5 and the divisor are positive. Multiplying by 2 gives the positive result $d=17$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** If $-m=8$, what is $m$?

**Back:** $m=-8$.

**Choices:**

- $m=-8$. ✔
  - _why:_ The expression $-m$ is $m$ multiplied by $-1$. Multiplying both sides by $-1$ gives $m=-8$, since $-(-8)=8$.
- $m=8$.
  - _why:_ Leaving the sign unchanged overlooks that $-m$ is the opposite of $m$. Multiplying both sides by $-1$ gives $m=-8$.
- $m=-\frac{1}{8}$.
  - _why:_ Negation does not mean taking a reciprocal; it means multiplying by $-1$. Therefore, $-m=8$ gives $m=-8$, not a fraction.
- $m=0$.
  - _why:_ Zero cannot satisfy the equation because $-0=0$, not 8. Reversing the sign on both sides gives the correct value $m=-8$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $6-m=14$.

**Back:** $m=-8$.

**Choices:**

- $m=-8$. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting 6 from both sides gives $-m=8$. Multiplying by $-1$ then gives $m=-8$, which satisfies $6-(-8)=14$.
- $m=8$.
  - _why:_ Stopping at $-m=8$ and dropping the negative sign changes the expression incorrectly. Since $m$ is negated, its value must be $-8$.
- $m=20$.
  - _why:_ This comes from treating the equation as $m-6=14$, reversing the original subtraction. Correctly isolating $-m$ gives $-m=8$, so $m=-8$.
- $m=-20$.
  - _why:_ Adding 6 to 14 and then attaching a negative sign does not preserve the equation. Subtracting 6 first gives $-m=8$, hence $m=-8$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a two-step equation, usually undo [ ___ ] before [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a two-step equation, usually undo adding or subtracting before multiplying or dividing.

**Choices:**

- In a two-step equation, usually undo adding or subtracting before multiplying or dividing. ✔
  - _why:_ Solving reverses the order in which operations act on the unknown. The outer addition or subtraction is undone first, followed by the multiplication or division.
- In a two-step equation, usually undo multiplying or dividing before adding or subtracting.
  - _why:_ This follows the usual order of operations rather than reversing it. When solving, undo the outer addition or subtraction first, then undo multiplication or division.
- In a two-step equation, usually undo the first operation shown before multiplying or dividing.
  - _why:_ The operation written first is not necessarily the outermost operation affecting the unknown. Usually, undo adding or subtracting before multiplying or dividing.
- In a two-step equation, usually undo adding or subtracting before powers or roots.
  - _why:_ Powers and roots are not normally the second operation in a linear two-step equation. After undoing addition or subtraction, undo multiplication or division.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $3h-5=16$.

**Back:** $h=7$.

**Choices:**

- $h=7$. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding 5 to both sides gives $3h=21$. Dividing both sides by 3 then gives $h=7$, and $3(7)-5=16$.
- $h=\frac{11}{3}$.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 5 from 16 repeats the subtraction instead of undoing it, producing $3h=11$. The correct first step is adding 5, giving $h=7$.
- $h=11$.
  - _why:_ This subtracts 5 from 16 but then fails to divide the coefficient of $h$. Adding 5 and dividing 21 by 3 gives $h=7$.
- $h=\frac{21}{5}$.
  - _why:_ Dividing by the constant 5 confuses the subtracted term with the coefficient. Add 5 first, then divide by the coefficient 3 to obtain $h=7$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $\frac{y}{3}-4=6$.

**Back:** $y=30$.

**Choices:**

- $y=30$. ✔
  - _why:_ Adding 4 to both sides gives $\frac{y}{3}=10$. Multiplying both sides by 3 then gives $y=30$, which makes the original left side 6.
- $y=10$.
  - _why:_ This stops after adding 4, when the equation is only $\frac{y}{3}=10$. Multiplying by 3 is still required, giving $y=30$.
- $y=18$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying 6 by 3 immediately ignores the subtracted 4. Add 4 first to obtain 10, then multiply by 3 to get $y=30$.
- $y=22$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 3 must affect every term, so treating the equation as $y-4=18$ is invalid. Add 4 first and then multiply, giving $y=30$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** In $4(x-3)$, the [ ___ ] is multiplied by 4.

**Back:** In $4(x-3)$, the whole bracket is multiplied by 4.

**Choices:**

- whole bracket ✔
  - _why:_ The factor 4 applies to everything enclosed by the brackets. By the distributive law, $4(x-3)=4x-12$, so both terms are multiplied.
- letter x
  - _why:_ Multiplying only $x$ ignores the second term inside the brackets. The factor 4 multiplies the whole bracket, producing $4x-12$.
- number 3
  - _why:_ The factor outside the brackets does not apply only to the constant. It multiplies the whole bracket, so both $x$ and $-3$ are affected.
- first term only
  - _why:_ Restricting multiplication to the first term is the common failure to distribute fully. The whole bracket is multiplied by 4, giving $4x-12$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $5(x-4)=35$.

**Back:** $x=11$.

**Choices:**

- $x=11$. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both sides by $5$ gives $x-4=7$. Adding $4$ to both sides then gives $x=11$.
- $x=7$.
  - _why:_ This stops after dividing both sides by $5$, leaving $x-4=7$. Adding $4$ is still required, so the correct solution is $x=11$.
- $x=3$.
  - _why:_ This subtracts $4$ from $7$ instead of adding it when undoing $x-4$. The inverse operation gives $x=7+4=11$.
- $x=39$.
  - _why:_ This treats the equation as though $5$ affects only $x$ and then adds $4$ incorrectly. Dividing by $5$ first gives $x-4=7$, so $x=11$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $20=4(2x-1)$.

**Back:** $x=3$.

**Choices:**

- $x=3$. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing by $4$ gives $5=2x-1$. Adding $1$ gives $6=2x$, and dividing by $2$ gives $x=3$.
- $x=2$.
  - _why:_ This subtracts $1$ rather than adding $1$ when rearranging $5=2x-1$. The correct next step is $6=2x$, giving $x=3$.

  - _why:_ This comes from distributing the bracket incorrectly. Expanding correctly gives $20=8x-4$, so $24=8x$ and therefore $x=3$.

  - _why:_ This finds $2x=6$ but forgets to divide by the coefficient $2$. Completing that final inverse operation gives $x=3$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a fraction, the top is the [ ___ ] and the bottom is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a fraction, the top is the numerator and the bottom is the denominator.

**Choices:**

- In a fraction, the top is the numerator and the bottom is the denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator records the number of parts being considered and is written above the fraction bar. The denominator is written below and gives the total equal parts.
- In a fraction, the top is the denominator and the bottom is the numerator.
  - _why:_ This reverses the two fraction terms. The numerator is above the fraction bar, while the denominator is below it.
- In a fraction, the top is the numerator and the bottom is the divisor.
  - _why:_ A fraction can represent division, making “divisor” tempting, but the standard name for the bottom number is the denominator; the top is the numerator.
- In a fraction, the top is the dividend and the bottom is the denominator.
  - _why:_ “Dividend” belongs to division terminology rather than naming a fraction’s positions. The top number is the numerator, and the bottom number is the denominator.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** With unknowns on both sides, collect [ ___ ] on one side and [ ___ ] on the other.

**Back:** With unknowns on both sides, collect unknown terms on one side and number terms on the other.

**Choices:**

- With unknowns on both sides, collect unknown terms on one side and number terms on the other. ✔
  - _why:_ Moving all terms containing the unknown to one side and constant number terms to the other isolates the variable, allowing its value to be found.
- With unknowns on both sides, collect positive terms on one side and negative terms on the other.
  - _why:_ Sorting terms by sign does not isolate the variable, and signs can change when terms cross the equals sign. Collect unknown terms on one side and number terms on the other.
- With unknowns on both sides, collect bracketed terms on one side and number terms on the other.
  - _why:_ Whether a term is bracketed does not determine which side it belongs on; brackets should usually be expanded first. Collect unknown terms on one side and number terms on the other.
- With unknowns on both sides, collect unknown terms on one side and coefficient terms on the other.
  - _why:_ A coefficient is attached to an unknown, so it cannot be separated into a different category of term. Collect whole unknown terms on one side and number terms on the other.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $6w+9=3w+24$.

**Back:** $w=5$.

**Choices:**

- $w=5$. ✔
  - _why:_ Subtracting $3w$ from both sides gives $3w+9=24$. Subtracting $9$ gives $3w=15$, and dividing by $3$ gives $w=5$.
- $w=11$.
  - _why:_ This combines unlike terms or moves them without changing their signs correctly. Rearranging gives $6w-3w=24-9$, so $3w=15$ and $w=5$.
- $w=-5$.
  - _why:_ This introduces a negative sign when subtracting terms across the equation. The correct rearrangement is $3w=15$, which gives the positive solution $w=5$.
- $w=3$.
  - _why:_ This confuses the remaining coefficient with the value of the unknown. After collecting terms, $3w=15$, so dividing both sides by $3$ gives $w=5$.
