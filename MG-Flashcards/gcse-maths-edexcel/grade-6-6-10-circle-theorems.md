# Circle Theorems Flashcards

_Lane: `gcse-maths-edexcel` · grade 6 · 22 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is a straight line joining two points on the circumference.

**Back:** A chord is a straight line joining two points on the circumference.

**Choices:**

- chord ✔
  - _why:_ A chord is a line segment whose two endpoints lie on the circumference of a circle.
- radius
  - _why:_ A radius joins the centre to the circumference, not two points on the circumference. The required line segment is a chord.
- diameter
  - _why:_ A diameter passes through the centre, which is not required here. A line segment joining any two points on the circumference is a chord.
- tangent
  - _why:_ A tangent meets the circle at exactly one point rather than joining two points on it. The correct term is chord.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] touches a circle at exactly one point.

**Back:** A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point.

**Choices:**

- tangent ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent is a straight line that meets a circle at exactly one point, called the point of contact.
- chord
  - _why:_ A chord is a line segment joining two points on the circumference. A line touching the circle at exactly one point is a tangent.
- secant
  - _why:_ A secant crosses the circle and meets its circumference at two points. A line meeting it at exactly one point is a tangent.
- radius
  - _why:_ A radius joins the centre of the circle to its circumference. The line that touches the circle at one point is a tangent.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What fact lets you mark OA and OB equal when O is the centre and A and B lie on the circle?

**Back:** All radii in the same circle are equal.

**Choices:**

- All radii in the same circle are equal. ✔
  - _why:_ OA and OB both run from the centre O to the circumference, so both are radii of the same circle and must have equal lengths.
- All chords in the same circle are equal.
  - _why:_ Chords in the same circle can have different lengths depending on their distance from the centre. OA and OB are equal because they are radii.
- All radii in different circles are equal.
  - _why:_ Radii are equal only when they belong to the same circle; different circles may have different sizes. OA and OB are radii of one circle.
- All lines from the centre are equal.
  - _why:_ Only segments from the centre to the circumference are necessarily radii. OA and OB are equal specifically because both are radii of the same circle.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** If two sides of a triangle are radii of the same circle, what type of triangle is it?

**Back:** An isosceles triangle.

**Choices:**

- An isosceles triangle. ✔
  - _why:_ Two radii of the same circle have equal lengths, so the triangle has two equal sides and is therefore an isosceles triangle.
- An equilateral triangle.
  - _why:_ Two equal radii guarantee only two equal sides, not all three. The triangle must be isosceles, but it is not necessarily equilateral.
- A scalene triangle.
  - _why:_ A scalene triangle has three unequal sides, which contradicts the fact that the two radii are equal. The triangle is isosceles.
- A right-angled triangle.
  - _why:_ Equal radii do not guarantee a 90° angle. They create two equal sides, so the triangle is isosceles rather than necessarily right-angled.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** In triangle AOB, OA=OB and ∠ABO=42°. What is ∠OAB?

**Back:** 42°.

**Choices:**

- 42°. ✔
  - _why:_ OA and OB are equal radii, making AOB isosceles. Its base angles at A and B are therefore equal, so ∠OAB is 42°.
- 48°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting 42° from 90° incorrectly assumes the triangle is right-angled. Since OA and OB are equal, both base angles are 42°.
- 84°.
  - _why:_ Doubling the given base angle does not find the other base angle. In an isosceles triangle the base angles are equal, so ∠OAB is 42°.
- 96°.
  - _why:_ This is the angle at O after subtracting both 42° base angles from 180°. The requested base angle ∠OAB is 42°.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In triangle AOB, the base angles are 42° and 42°. What is ∠AOB?

**Back:** 96°.

**Choices:**

- 96°. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles in a triangle total 180°. Subtracting both base angles gives $180°-42°-42°=96°$, so ∠AOB is 96°.
- 84°.
  - _why:_ Adding the two base angles gives 84°, but the question asks for the remaining angle. Subtracting 84° from 180° gives 96°.
- 138°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting only one 42° angle overlooks the second base angle. Both must be removed from 180°, giving ∠AOB as 96°.
- 42°.
  - _why:_ The equal base angles are each 42°, but the angle at O is different. Triangle angles total 180°, so ∠AOB is 96°.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** A tangent to a circle is [ ___ ] to the radius at the point of contact.

**Back:** A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

**Choices:**

- perpendicular ✔
  - _why:_ At the point of contact, a tangent and the radius drawn to that point meet at a right angle, so they are perpendicular.
- parallel
  - _why:_ A tangent cannot be parallel to the radius at their shared point of contact. They meet at 90°, so the correct relationship is perpendicular.
- oblique
  - _why:_ An oblique line meets another at an angle other than 90°. The tangent-radius theorem states that these lines are perpendicular at the contact point.
- equal in length
  - _why:_ The theorem concerns the angle between the tangent and radius, not their lengths. They are perpendicular at the point of contact.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** A tangent touches the circle at A and OA is a radius. What is the angle between OA and the tangent?

**Back:** 90°.

**Choices:**

- 90°. ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, which measures 90°.
- 45°.
  - _why:_ An angle of 45° is half a right angle and does not satisfy the tangent-radius theorem. OA and the tangent meet at 90°.
- 180°.
  - _why:_ An angle of 180° would place OA and the tangent on one straight line. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius, giving 90°.
- 0°.
  - _why:_ An angle of 0° would mean OA and the tangent point in the same direction. They are perpendicular at A, so the angle is 90°.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** A tangent touches the circle at A. Which radius makes the 90° angle with the tangent?

**Back:** The radius to the point of contact.

**Choices:**

- The radius to the point of contact. ✔
  - _why:_ A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point where it touches the circle, so this radius forms the 90° angle.
- The radius to the opposite point.
  - _why:_ The radius to the opposite side lies on the same diameter, but it does not meet the tangent. The perpendicular radius ends at the point of contact.
- The radius parallel to the tangent.
  - _why:_ Parallel lines do not form a 90° angle. The radius drawn to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
- The radius to the centre of the chord.
  - _why:_ A radius may be perpendicular to a chord at its midpoint, but the stated line is a tangent. The relevant radius goes to the point of contact.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** If two angles stand on the same chord or arc, the angle at the centre is [ ___ ] the angle at the circumference.

**Back:** If two angles stand on the same chord or arc, the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

**Choices:**

- twice ✔
  - _why:_ For angles subtended by the same chord or arc, the angle at the centre is twice the corresponding angle at the circumference.
- half
  - _why:_ This reverses the circle theorem. The circumference angle is half the centre angle, so the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
- equal to
  - _why:_ Equal angles arise in the same segment, not when comparing centre and circumference angles. The centre angle is twice the corresponding circumference angle.
- three times
  - _why:_ The multiplier in the centre-angle theorem is 2, not 3. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** ∠BOC at the centre is 74°. ∠BAC at the circumference stands on the same chord BC. What is ∠BAC?

**Back:** 37°.

**Choices:**

- 37°. ✔
  - _why:_ The angle at the circumference is half the angle at the centre when both stand on chord BC, so $74° \div 2 = 37°$.
- 74°.
  - _why:_ This incorrectly treats the centre and circumference angles as equal. The circumference angle is half of 74°, giving the correct answer of 37°.
- 148°.
  - _why:_ This doubles the centre angle instead of halving it. Since the circumference angle is half the 74° centre angle, the correct answer is 37°.
- 53°.
  - _why:_ This results from subtracting from 180°, which is not the relevant theorem. Halving the 74° centre angle gives the circumference angle of 37°.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A reflex centre angle is 220°. What is the smaller centre angle?

**Back:** 360° - 220° = 140°.

**Choices:**

- 360° - 220° = 140°. ✔
  - _why:_ Angles around the centre total 360°. Subtracting the 220° reflex angle leaves the smaller centre angle: $360° - 220° = 140°$.
- 220° - 180° = 40°.
  - _why:_ Subtracting a straight angle does not find the remaining angle around a point. The two centre angles total 360°, so the smaller angle is 140°.
- 360° - 220° = 180°.
  - _why:_ The correct method is shown, but the subtraction is inaccurate. Since $360 - 220 = 140$, the smaller centre angle is 140°.
- 360° - 140° = 220°.
  - _why:_ This calculation returns the given reflex angle rather than the requested smaller angle. Subtracting 220° from 360° gives the smaller angle of 140°.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** Angles in the [ ___ ] are equal.

**Back:** Angles in the same segment are equal.

**Choices:**

- same segment ✔
  - _why:_ Angles subtended by the same chord at points on the same side of that chord are equal, which is the same-segment circle theorem.
- same sector
  - _why:_ Being within the same sector does not guarantee equal angles. The theorem applies to angles standing on the same chord in the same segment.
- same circle
  - _why:_ Angles located in the same circle can have many different sizes. They are equal when they stand on the same chord in the same segment.
- opposite segment
  - _why:_ Angles on opposite sides of a chord are generally supplementary rather than equal. The equal-angle theorem applies to angles in the same segment.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to [ ___ ].

**Back:** Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.

**Choices:**

- 180° ✔
  - _why:_ A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on a circle is cyclic, and each pair of opposite interior angles is supplementary, adding to 180°.
- 360°
  - _why:_ The four interior angles of any quadrilateral total 360°, but one opposite pair in a cyclic quadrilateral adds to 180°.
- 90°
  - _why:_ An angle in a semicircle is 90°, but that is a different circle theorem. Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.
- 270°
  - _why:_ This confuses the supplementary relationship with a reflex total. Each pair of opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral adds to 180°, not 270°.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** The [ ___ ] links a tangent-chord angle to the angle in the opposite segment.

**Back:** The alternate segment theorem links a tangent-chord angle to the angle in the opposite segment.

**Choices:**

- alternate segment theorem ✔
  - _why:_ The alternate segment theorem states that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle subtended by that chord in the opposite segment.
- same segment theorem
  - _why:_ The same segment theorem compares two angles subtended by the same chord within a circle. The tangent-chord relationship is given by the alternate segment theorem.
- angle at the centre theorem
  - _why:_ The angle at the centre theorem relates central and circumferential angles subtended by the same arc. A tangent-chord angle uses the alternate segment theorem.
- cyclic quadrilateral theorem
  - _why:_ The cyclic quadrilateral theorem states that opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°. The tangent-chord relationship is the alternate segment theorem.

## Card 18 — `basic`

- 51°. ✔

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** In right-angled triangle OAC, OA=6 cm and AC=8 cm. What is OC?

**Back:** 10 cm.

  - _why:_ Adding the two shorter sides directly does not calculate a hypotenuse. Pythagoras’ theorem gives $OC=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10$ cm.

  - _why:_ Averaging the two shorter sides is not valid for finding the hypotenuse. Using Pythagoras’ theorem gives $OC=\sqrt{36+64}=10$ cm.
- 12 cm.
  - _why:_ This can result from mishandling the squared side lengths. The correct calculation is $OC=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=\sqrt{100}=10$ cm.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** O, B and C are collinear, with OC=10 cm and OB=6 cm. What is BC?

**Back:** 4 cm.

**Choices:**

- 4 cm. ✔
  - _why:_ B lies between O and C, so $OC=OB+BC$. Therefore $BC=10-6=4$ cm.
- 16 cm.
  - _why:_ Adding OC and OB ignores that OB is already part of the full distance OC. Since B lies between O and C, $BC=10-6=4$ cm.
- 5 cm.
  - _why:_ Halving OC would only work if B were the midpoint, which is not stated and conflicts with $OB=6$ cm. Subtraction gives $BC=4$ cm.
- 6 cm.
  - _why:_ This repeats the length OB rather than finding the remaining section of OC. Since $OC=OB+BC$, $BC=10-6=4$ cm.
