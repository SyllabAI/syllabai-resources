## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** When labelling a right-angled triangle, which side is fixed no matter which angle $\theta$ you choose?

**Back:** Hypotenuse.

**Choices:**

- Hypotenuse. ✔
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse is fixed because it is always the side opposite the right angle, regardless of which acute angle is chosen as $\theta$.
- Opposite.
  - _why:_ The opposite side changes when the chosen acute angle changes. The hypotenuse remains fixed because it is always directly opposite the right angle.
- Adjacent.
  - _why:_ The adjacent side depends on which acute angle is labelled $\theta$. The hypotenuse is fixed because it is always the side opposite the right angle.
- Perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular describes lines meeting at a right angle rather than a SOHCAHTOA side label. The fixed side is the hypotenuse, opposite the right angle.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The hypotenuse is always the [ ___ ] side and lies opposite the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The hypotenuse is always the longest side and lies opposite the right angle.

**Choices:**

- The hypotenuse is always the longest side and lies opposite the right angle. ✔
  - _why:_ The right angle is the triangle’s largest angle, so the side opposite it is the longest. That side is defined as the hypotenuse.
- The hypotenuse is always the shortest side and lies opposite the right angle.
  - _why:_ Calling the hypotenuse the shortest side reverses a key property of right-angled triangles. It is always the longest side and lies opposite the right angle.
- The hypotenuse is always the longest side and lies opposite the chosen angle.
  - _why:_ The side opposite the chosen angle is labelled opposite, not necessarily hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is the longest side and always lies opposite the right angle.
- The hypotenuse is always the adjacent side and lies opposite the acute angle.
  - _why:_ Adjacent is a position relative to a chosen acute angle, not a fixed length property. The hypotenuse is the longest side and lies opposite the right angle.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** For a chosen angle $\theta$, which label goes on the side directly across from $\theta$?

**Back:** Opposite.

**Choices:**

- Opposite. ✔
  - _why:_ The side directly across the triangle from the chosen angle $\theta$ is called the opposite side. Its label changes if a different acute angle is chosen.
- Adjacent.
  - _why:_ The adjacent side touches $\theta$ and the right angle, rather than lying directly across from $\theta$. The side across from $\theta$ is labelled opposite.
- Hypotenuse.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse lies opposite the right angle, not necessarily opposite the chosen angle $\theta$. The side directly across from $\theta$ is labelled opposite.
- Perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular describes two lines meeting at $90^\circ$ and is not the required side label. The side directly across from $\theta$ is called opposite.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** For a chosen angle $\theta$, which label goes on the side connecting $\theta$ to the right angle?

**Back:** Adjacent.

**Choices:**

- Adjacent. ✔
  - _why:_ The adjacent side is the non-hypotenuse side next to $\theta$. It runs from the chosen angle to the right angle of the triangle.
- Hypotenuse.
  - _why:_ The hypotenuse touches $\theta$, but it does not connect $\theta$ to the right angle as a triangle side. That side is labelled adjacent.
- Opposite.
  - _why:_ The opposite side lies directly across from $\theta$, so it does not touch that angle. The side connecting $\theta$ to the right angle is adjacent.
- Perpendicular.
  - _why:_ Perpendicular describes the relationship between the two shorter sides at the right angle. The side joining $\theta$ to the right angle is labelled adjacent.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** The [ ___ ] and [ ___ ] labels depend on the chosen angle $\theta$.

**Back:** The opposite and adjacent labels depend on the chosen angle $\theta$.

**Choices:**

- The opposite and adjacent labels depend on the chosen angle $\theta$. ✔
  - _why:_ Choosing the other acute angle swaps which shorter side is opposite and which is adjacent. The hypotenuse remains fixed because it stays opposite the right angle.
- The opposite and hypotenuse labels depend on the chosen angle $\theta$.
  - _why:_ The opposite label changes with the chosen acute angle, but the hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle. Opposite and adjacent are the two angle-dependent labels.
- The hypotenuse and adjacent labels depend on the chosen angle $\theta$.
  - _why:_ The adjacent label changes with $\theta$, but the hypotenuse does not. The two labels that depend on the chosen angle are opposite and adjacent.
- The hypotenuse and right angle labels depend on the chosen angle $\theta$.
  - _why:_ Neither the hypotenuse nor the right angle changes when another acute angle is chosen. The labels that swap according to $\theta$ are opposite and adjacent.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** SOH: sin uses [ ___ ] over [ ___ ].

**Back:** SOH: sin uses Opposite over Hypotenuse.

**Choices:**

- SOH: sin uses Opposite over Hypotenuse. ✔
  - _why:_ SOH links sine with opposite and hypotenuse: $\sin\theta$ equals the length of the opposite side divided by the length of the hypotenuse.
- SOH: sin uses Adjacent over Hypotenuse.
  - _why:_ Adjacent over hypotenuse is the cosine ratio, represented by CAH. SOH states that sine equals the opposite side divided by the hypotenuse.
- SOH: sin uses Opposite over Adjacent.
  - _why:_ Opposite over adjacent is the tangent ratio, represented by TOA. For sine, SOH gives opposite divided by hypotenuse.
- SOH: sin uses Hypotenuse over Opposite.
  - _why:_ This reverses the sine ratio, producing its reciprocal rather than sine. SOH places opposite in the numerator and hypotenuse in the denominator.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** With a formula triangle, cover [ ___ ] to get the [ ___ ].

**Back:** With a formula triangle, cover what you want to find to get the rearrangement.

**Choices:**

- With a formula triangle, cover what you want to find to get the rearrangement. ✔
  - _why:_ In a formula triangle, the covered part is the unknown you are trying to make the subject. The uncovered parts then show the correct rearrangement.
- With a formula triangle, cover what you already know to get the rearrangement.
  - _why:_ Covering known values is a common mix-up, but the triangle is used by covering the unknown. You cover what you want to find to reveal the rearrangement.
- With a formula triangle, cover what you want to find to get the substitution.
  - _why:_ The triangle does not substitute numbers for you; it shows how the formula should be rearranged. After covering the unknown, you then substitute values into that rearrangement.
- With a formula triangle, cover the largest number to get the calculation.
  - _why:_ Choosing the largest number is not part of using a formula triangle. You cover the quantity you want to find, and the uncovered parts give the rearrangement.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** For a 35° angle with hypotenuse 20 cm and opposite side $x$, what equation starts SOH?

**Back:** Use SOH: $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$.

**Choices:**

- Use SOH: $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$. ✔
  - _why:_ SOH means sine equals opposite over hypotenuse. Here the opposite side is $x$ and the hypotenuse is 20, so $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$.
- Use CAH: $\cos(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$.
  - _why:_ Cosine uses adjacent over hypotenuse, not opposite over hypotenuse. Since $x$ is opposite the angle and 20 is the hypotenuse, the correct ratio is $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$.
- Use SOH: $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{20}{x}$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the fraction in SOH. Sine is opposite over hypotenuse, so the opposite side $x$ goes on top and the hypotenuse 20 goes on the bottom.
- Use TOA: $\tan(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$.
  - _why:_ Tangent uses opposite over adjacent, but 20 is the hypotenuse, not the adjacent side. With opposite and hypotenuse, the correct choice is SOH.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** From $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$, how do you isolate $x$?

**Back:** Multiply by 20: $x=20\sin(35^\circ)$.

**Choices:**

- Multiply by 20: $x=20\sin(35^\circ)$. ✔
  - _why:_ Since $x$ is divided by 20, multiplying both sides by 20 cancels the denominator. This leaves $x=20\sin(35^\circ)$.
- Divide by 20: $x=\frac{\sin(35^\circ)}{20}$.
  - _why:_ Dividing by 20 makes the denominator problem worse instead of cancelling it. Because $x$ is already over 20, you must multiply by 20 to isolate $x$.
- Divide by $\sin(35^\circ)$: $x=\frac{20}{\sin(35^\circ)}$.
  - _why:_ This treats the equation as if 20 were on top and $x$ were connected by multiplication. From $\sin(35^\circ)=\frac{x}{20}$, multiplying by 20 gives $x=20\sin(35^\circ)$.
- Subtract 20: $x=\sin(35^\circ)-20$.
  - _why:_ The 20 is a divisor, not a term being added. To undo division by 20, use multiplication, giving $x=20\sin(35^\circ)$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** In $\tan(42^\circ)=\frac{11}{x}$, what first step moves $x$ off the bottom?

**Back:** Multiply by $x$: $x\tan(42^\circ)=11$.

**Choices:**

- Multiply by $x$: $x\tan(42^\circ)=11$. ✔
  - _why:_ Because $x$ is in the denominator, multiplying both sides by $x$ cancels the denominator. This moves $x$ to the left as $x\tan(42^\circ)=11$.
- Divide by $x$: $\frac{\tan(42^\circ)}{x}=11$.
  - _why:_ Dividing by $x$ does not remove $x$ from the denominator; it introduces another division by $x$. To move $x$ off the bottom, multiply both sides by $x$.
- Multiply by $11$: $11\tan(42^\circ)=\frac{121}{x}$.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 11 changes the numerator but leaves $x$ in the denominator. The step that cancels the denominator is multiplying both sides by $x$.
- Divide by $\tan(42^\circ)$: $1=\frac{11}{x\tan(42^\circ)}$.
  - _why:_ Dividing by tangent isolates neither $x$ nor removes it from the denominator. First multiply by $x$ to get $x\tan(42^\circ)=11$.

**Back:** Use TOA: $\tan(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$.

**Choices:**

- Use TOA: $\tan(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$. ✔
  - _why:_ TOA means tangent equals opposite over adjacent. With opposite side 5 cm and adjacent side 9 cm, $\tan(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$.
- Use TOA: $\tan(\theta)=\frac{9}{5}$.
  - _why:_ This reverses the tangent ratio. Tangent is opposite over adjacent, so the opposite side 5 goes on top and the adjacent side 9 goes underneath.
- Use SOH: $\sin(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Given $\tan(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$, what expression gives $\theta$?

**Back:** Inverse tan: $\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.

**Choices:**

- Inverse tan: $\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$. ✔
  - _why:_ Inverse tangent reverses the tangent function. Applying it to both sides isolates the angle, giving $\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.
- Inverse sin: $\theta=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.
  - _why:_ Inverse sine would apply if $\sin(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$. Because the given ratio is tangent, the correct expression is $\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.
- Inverse cos: $\theta=\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.
  - _why:_ Inverse cosine reverses cosine, not tangent. Since $\tan(\theta)=\frac{5}{9}$, applying inverse tangent gives $\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.
- Reciprocal tan: $\theta=\frac{1}{\tan\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)}$.
  - _why:_ The superscript $-1$ on $\tan^{-1}$ means inverse function here, not reciprocal. The angle is found using $\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{9}\right)$.

  - _why:_ Tangent is a trigonometric function, not the reciprocal of an angle. Applying tangent again does not undo it; inverse tangent must be used to find $\theta$.

  - _why:_ This treats $\tan(\theta)$ like a product with a cancellable factor. Because tangent is a function, the correct inverse operation is inverse tangent, not multiplication.
