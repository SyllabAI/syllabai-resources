## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a [ ___ ]; the whole is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a part of a whole; the whole is the full amount.

**Choices:**

- Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a part of a whole; the whole is the full amount. ✔
  - _why:_ These forms express how much of a complete quantity is being considered. The whole represents the entire original quantity, while the fraction, decimal or percentage represents a part of it.
- Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a part of a whole; the whole is the half amount.
  - _why:_ Half is only one possible part, not the definition of the whole. The whole is the full amount, and fractions, decimals and percentages describe parts of that amount.
- Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a full amount; the whole is the full amount.
  - _why:_ This confuses the represented part with the complete quantity. Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a part of a whole, while the whole itself is the full amount.
- Fractions, decimals and percentages describe a part of a whole; the whole is the numerator.
  - _why:_ The numerator is the top number in a fraction, not the entire quantity. The whole is the full amount from which the represented part is taken.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a fraction, the top number is the [ ___ ] and the bottom number is the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In a fraction, the top number is the numerator and the bottom number is the denominator.

**Choices:**

- In a fraction, the top number is the numerator and the bottom number is the denominator. ✔
  - _why:_ The numerator counts the selected parts, while the denominator shows how many equal parts make one whole. Their positions are fixed at the top and bottom respectively.
- In a fraction, the top number is the denominator and the bottom number is the numerator.
  - _why:_ This reverses the two fraction terms. The numerator is the top number and counts selected parts, while the denominator is the bottom number and gives the total equal parts.
- In a fraction, the top number is the numerator and the bottom number is the numerator.
  - _why:_ Only the top number is called the numerator. The bottom number is the denominator, which states how many equal parts form the whole.
- In a fraction, the top number is the denominator and the bottom number is the denominator.
  - _why:_ Only the bottom number is called the denominator. The top number is the numerator, which states how many of the equal parts are being considered.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** A percentage means [ ___ ].

**Back:** A percentage means out of 100.

**Choices:**

- out of 100 ✔
  - _why:_ Percent literally means per hundred, so a percentage states how many parts there are out of 100. For example, 37% means 37 out of 100.
- out of 10
  - _why:_ This confuses percentages with tenths. A percentage always means out of 100, although a fraction with denominator 10 can be converted to an equivalent fraction with denominator 100.
- out of 1
  - _why:_ Decimals often express a proportion relative to 1, which makes this tempting. A percentage instead means out of 100, so 0.37 is equivalent to 37%.
- out of 1000
  - _why:_ This confuses percentages with parts per thousand. A percentage means out of 100; a quantity stated out of 1000 must first be converted to an equivalent amount out of 100.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** To simplify a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the [ ___ ] without changing its [ ___ ].

**Back:** To simplify a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the same number without changing its value.

**Choices:**

- To simplify a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the same number without changing its value. ✔
  - _why:_ Dividing both parts of a fraction by the same common factor produces an equivalent fraction. Its numbers become smaller, but the proportion and therefore its value remain unchanged.
- To simplify a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the different numbers without changing its value.
  - _why:_ Dividing the numerator and denominator by different numbers usually changes the fraction's value. Both must be divided by the same common factor to produce an equivalent simplified fraction.
- To simplify a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the same number without changing its denominator.
  - _why:_ The denominator normally becomes smaller during simplification, so it does change. Dividing both numbers by the same common factor leaves the fraction's value unchanged instead.
- To simplify a fraction, divide numerator and denominator by the sum of the numbers without changing its value.
  - _why:_ The sum of the numerator and denominator is not generally a factor of either number. Simplification requires dividing both by the same common factor, which preserves the fraction's value.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Back:** $\frac{7}{50}$

**Choices:**

- $\frac{7}{50}$ ✔
  - _why:_ Both 14 and 100 are divisible by 2, giving $14 \div 2 = 7$ and $100 \div 2 = 50$. No larger common factor remains.
- $\frac{7}{100}$
  - _why:_ Only dividing the numerator by 2 halves the fraction's value rather than simplifying it equivalently. Divide both numerator and denominator by 2 to obtain $\frac{7}{50}$.
- $\frac{14}{50}$
  - _why:_ Only dividing the denominator by 2 doubles the fraction's value rather than preserving it. Both parts must be divided by 2, producing $\frac{7}{50}$.
- $\frac{1}{7}$
  - _why:_ This results from incorrectly cancelling digits or treating the numbers separately rather than using a common factor. Dividing both 14 and 100 by 2 gives $\frac{7}{50}$.

  - _why:_ The stated operation and movement contradict each other: multiplying by 100 moves the decimal point right, not left. A decimal becomes a percentage by moving it two places right.
- To change a decimal to a percentage, multiply by 10; the decimal point moves one place right.
  - _why:_ Multiplying by 10 only moves the decimal point one place and gives a result ten times too small. Percentage conversion requires multiplying by 100 and moving two places right.

**Back:** Divide

**Choices:**

  - _why:_ A fraction bar instructs you to divide the numerator by the denominator. For example, $\frac{6}{3}$ means $6 \div 3$, which equals 2.

  - _why:_ Multiplication may be used when working with fractions, but the fraction bar itself represents division. The numerator is divided by the denominator, so $\frac{6}{3}$ means $6 \div 3$.

  - _why:_ The numerator and denominator are not added together. A fraction bar means division, with the value found by dividing the numerator by the denominator.
- Subtract
  - _why:_ The fraction bar does not mean finding the difference between the numerator and denominator. It represents division, so the numerator must be divided by the denominator.
