# Correlation Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 22 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is bivariate data?

**Back:** Data involving two variables measured in pairs.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a scatter graph, the [ ___ ] variable is plotted on the horizontal axis, and the response variable is plotted on the vertical axis.

**Back:** On a scatter graph, the explanatory variable is plotted on the horizontal axis, and the response variable is plotted on the vertical axis.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a scatter graph, the explanatory variable is plotted on the horizontal axis, and the [ ___ ] variable is plotted on the vertical axis.

**Back:** On a scatter graph, the explanatory variable is plotted on the horizontal axis, and the response variable is plotted on the vertical axis.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the difference between positive and negative correlation?

**Back:** Positive: as one variable increases, the other tends to increase.Negative: as one variable increases, the other tends to decrease.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How is strong correlation identified on a scatter graph?

**Back:** The plotted points lie close to a straight line.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** When describing correlation in an exam, what three components must be included?

**Back:** The strength (e.g. strong/weak)The direction (e.g. positive/negative)The context of the variables

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Correlation does not prove [ ___ ], because a third factor might be influencing both variables.

**Back:** Correlation does not prove causation, because a third factor might be influencing both variables.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Give a reason why ice cream sales and suncream sales might be correlated but not causal.

**Back:** Both are independently caused by a third factor: temperature (or hot weather).

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the lower outlier boundary is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The formula for the lower outlier boundary is $\boxed{Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}}$.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the upper outlier boundary is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The formula for the upper outlier boundary is $\boxed{Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}}$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the product moment correlation coefficient ($r$) measure?

**Back:** The strength and direction of a linear relationship.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the maximum and minimum possible values for $r$?

**Back:** Between -1 and 1.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** A value of $r$ close to [ ___ ] suggests there is no linear correlation, though a curved relationship may still exist.

**Back:** A value of $r$ close to 0 suggests there is no linear correlation, though a curved relationship may still exist.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** In the regression line equation $y = a + bx$, what does the gradient $b$ represent?

**Back:** The change in the predicted response ($y$) for every 1 unit increase in the explanatory variable ($x$).

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** A regression line for weight ($w$) on height ($h$) is $w = 1.3h - 150$. Interpret the gradient 1.3.

**Back:** For every 1 cm increase in height, the predicted weight increases by 1.3 kg.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Define interpolation in the context of regression.

**Back:** Predicting a value within the range of the original data.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Define extrapolation and state its main risk.

**Back:** Predicting outside the range of the original data.Risk: the linear trend may not continue.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** A regression estimate is more reliable if the scatter graph shows [ ___ ] correlation and the value used is an interpolation.

**Back:** A regression estimate is more reliable if the scatter graph shows strong correlation and the value used is an interpolation.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** A regression estimate is more reliable if the scatter graph shows strong correlation and the value used is an [ ___ ].

**Back:** A regression estimate is more reliable if the scatter graph shows strong correlation and the value used is an interpolation.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you avoid rearranging a regression line of $y$ on $x$ to predict $x$?

**Back:** A regression line is specifically designed to predict the response variable from the explanatory variable, not vice-versa.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** What effect can an outlier have on a regression line?

**Back:** It can distort the gradient/intercept or make the line unreliable for predictions.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Explain why a linear regression model is not suitable if the PMCC is $0.05$.

**Back:** The correlation is very weak, meaning there is no clear linear pattern to model.
