# Equations and Inequalities Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 23 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In set notation, what does the expression $\{x:x>3\}$ mean?

**Back:** The set of values of $x$ such that $x$ is greater than 3.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** When solving a linear inequality, what operation requires reversing the inequality sign?

**Back:** Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** If you [ ___ ] both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the inequality sign reverses.

**Back:** If you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the inequality sign reverses.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** If you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a [ ___ ] number, the inequality sign reverses.

**Back:** If you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the inequality sign reverses.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step when solving a quadratic inequality like $x^2+6x>16$?

**Back:** Rearrange the expression so it is compared to zero (e.g. $x^2+6x-16>0$).

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What do the roots of the equation $ax^2+bx+c=0$ represent when solving the inequality $ax^2+bx+c>0$?

**Back:** They are the boundary values where the expression changes sign.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** For a quadratic where the $x^2$ coefficient is positive, where is the expression greater than zero?

**Back:** In the regions outside the roots (the two outer intervals).

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** For a quadratic where the $x^2$ coefficient is positive, where is the expression less than zero?

**Back:** In the region between the roots (the single middle interval).

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a quadratic with a [ ___ ] $x^2$ coefficient, the parabola opens upwards.

**Back:** For a quadratic with a positive $x^2$ coefficient, the parabola opens upwards.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a quadratic with a positive $x^2$ coefficient, the parabola opens [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a quadratic with a positive $x^2$ coefficient, the parabola opens upwards.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** How do strict and non-strict inequalities differ regarding boundary values?

**Back:** Strict ($$) excludes boundary values.Non-strict ($\le$ or $\ge$) includes boundary values.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] is the collection of all possible values that make an inequality true.

**Back:** A solution set is the collection of all possible values that make an inequality true.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the final solution set for two combined inequalities?

**Back:** Solve each separately and find the overlap (intersection) of the two sets.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What do the solutions to a pair of simultaneous equations represent on a graph?

**Back:** The coordinates of the intersection points where the graphs meet.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the standard algebraic method for finding the intersection of a line and a curve?

**Back:** Substitute the linear equation into the curve equation to form a quadratic.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** If you are unsure which interval to choose for a quadratic inequality, you should [ ___ ] an easy value (like $x=0$) in the expression.

**Back:** If you are unsure which interval to choose for a quadratic inequality, you should test an easy value (like $x=0$) in the expression.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** The [ ___ ] of a quadratic is called the discriminant.

**Back:** The $b^2-4ac$ of a quadratic is called the discriminant.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** The $b^2-4ac$ of a quadratic is called the [ ___ ].

**Back:** The $b^2-4ac$ of a quadratic is called the discriminant.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** If a line and a curve intersect at two distinct points, what is the value of the discriminant?

**Back:** $$$b^2-4ac>0$$

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a discriminant of $b^2-4ac=0$ indicate about the intersection of a line and a curve?

**Back:** The line is a tangent to the curve (they touch at one repeated point).

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** If the discriminant of the quadratic formed by substitution is negative, what does this mean for the line and curve?

**Back:** They do not intersect (no real roots).

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** Why should you solve $x^2-x-6 \le 0$ by testing intervals or sketching rather than just taking square roots?

**Back:** Quadratic solutions are ranges of values determined by the shape of the parabola relative to the x-axis.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve the linear inequality $20-x < 4x+5$.

**Back:** $$x > 3$
