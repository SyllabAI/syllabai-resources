## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In the polynomial $f(x) = 3x^3 - 2x + 5$, what is the coefficient of $x^3$?

**Back:** 3

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A root (or zero) of $f(x)$ is a value of $x$ that makes $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** A root (or zero) of $f(x)$ is a value of $x$ that makes $\boxed{f(x) = 0}$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What happens to the sign of a negative value like $x = -3$ when it is squared vs cubed?

**Back:** $(-3)^2$ becomes positive (9)$(-3)^3$ stays negative (-27)

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** According to the factor theorem, $(x - a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ if and only if $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** According to the factor theorem, $(x - a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ if and only if $\boxed{f(a) = 0}$.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** To test if $(2x + 3)$ is a factor of a polynomial, what value of $x$ should be substituted?

**Back:** $$x = -\frac{3}{2}$

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** If $f(x) = (x - 2)(Ax^2 + Bx + C)$, how are $A$, $B$, and $C$ typically found at AS level?

**Back:** Comparing coefficients after expanding the right-hand side.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Where should you look for possible rational roots of an integer cubic polynomial?

**Back:** Factors of the constant term divided by factors of the leading coefficient.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c$ has no real roots, its discriminant $\text{[...]}$ must be $< 0$.

**Back:** If a quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c$ has no real roots, its discriminant $\boxed{b^2 - 4ac}$ must be $< 0$.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c$ has no real roots, its discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ must be $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If a quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c$ has no real roots, its discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ must be $\boxed{< 0}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** How does a repeated root appear on a cubic graph?

**Back:** The graph touches the $x$-axis and turns around (does not cross).

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you show a cubic equation has exactly one real root?

**Back:** Show one linear factor $(x - a)$ gives $f(a) = 0$Show the remaining quadratic factor has a negative discriminant

**Back:** In algebraic proofs, an even integer is written as $\boxed{2n}$ and an odd integer as $2n + 1$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** In algebraic proofs, an even integer is written as $2n$ and an odd integer as $\text{[...]}$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you disprove the claim: "$n^2 - n + 3$ is prime for every positive integer $n$"?

**Back:** Find a counterexample (e.g., if $n=3$, $3^2 - 3 + 3 = 9$, which is not prime).

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Prove that the sum of two neighbouring odd integers is a multiple of 4.

**Back:** Let integers be $2n + 1$ and $2n + 3$Sum: $(2n + 1) + (2n + 3) = 4n + 4$Factorise: $4(n + 1)$, which is a multiple of 4

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** When proving a quadratic inequality is positive for all real $x$, what technique is most effective?

**Back:** Completing the square.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** To show $x^2 + 4x + 7 > 0$ for all $x$, complete the square to get $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** To show $x^2 + 4x + 7 > 0$ for all $x$, complete the square to get $\boxed{(x + 2)^2 + 3}$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Why does $(x + 2)^2 + 3 > 0$ prove the expression is always positive?

**Back:** Because $(x + 2)^2 \ge 0$ for all real $x$, so adding 3 makes the result strictly greater than zero.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the danger of dividing an inequality by a variable expression?

**Back:** The sign of the expression might be negative, which would reverse the inequality symbol.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If $n$ is an even integer ($n = 2k$), what is the algebraic form of $n^2 + 1$?

**Back:** $$4k^2 + 1$

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you show that $4k^2 + 1$ is not a multiple of 4?

**Back:** It is one more than a multiple of 4 ($4(k^2) + 1$).
