# Measures of Location and Spread Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Distinguish between a measure of location and a measure of spread.

**Back:** Location: describes where the data is centred (e.g. mean).Spread: describes how varied or consistent the data is (e.g. standard deviation).

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for the mean $\bar{x}$ of $n$ data values is $\bar{x} = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The formula for the mean $\bar{x}$ of $n$ data values is $\bar{x} = \boxed{\frac{\sum x}{n}}$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What must be done to raw data before calculating the median or quartiles?

**Back:** The data must be placed in ascending order.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a large standard deviation indicate about a data set?

**Back:** The data values are more spread out and less consistent.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** Using summary statistics, the formula for standard deviation is $\text{sd} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \text{[...]}}$.

**Back:** Using summary statistics, the formula for standard deviation is $\text{sd} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \boxed{\bar{x}^2}}$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Define the statistic $S_{xx}$ in terms of $\sum x^2$, $\sum x$, and $n$.

**Back:** $$S_{xx} = \sum x^2 - \frac{(\sum x)^2}{n}$

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the summary statistic $S_{xx}$ is known, the standard deviation is calculated as $\sqrt{\text{[...]}}$.

**Back:** If the summary statistic $S_{xx}$ is known, the standard deviation is calculated as $\sqrt{\boxed{\frac{S_{xx}}{n}}}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are the mean and standard deviation calculated from frequency tables called estimates?

**Back:** The exact original values within the classes are unknown.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What value is used to represent a class interval when estimating a grouped mean?

**Back:** The class midpoint.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** How is $\sum fx^2$ calculated for a grouped frequency table?

**Back:** Multiply each frequency by the square of its class midpoint, then sum the results.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** In the interpolation formula $L + \frac{\frac{n}{2} - c}{f} \times w$, what do $c$ and $w$ represent?

**Back:** $c$: cumulative frequency before the median class.$w$: class width.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** In the interpolation formula $L + \frac{\frac{n}{2} - c}{f} \times w$, what do $L$ and $f$ represent?

**Back:** $L$: lower boundary of the median class.$f$: frequency of the median class.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** If data is recorded to the nearest whole unit, what are the real class boundaries for the group 20-29?

**Back:** 19.5 and 29.5.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** If data $x$ is coded as $x = \frac{t - 20}{2}$, how is the original mean $\bar{t}$ found?

**Back:** Rearrange the formula to undo the coding: $\bar{t} = 2\bar{x} + 20$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** If data $x$ is coded as $x = \frac{t - 20}{2}$, how is the original standard deviation $\sigma_t$ found?

**Back:** Multiply by the scale factor only: $\sigma_t = 2\sigma_x$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Why does adding a constant $b$ in the coding $t = ax + b$ not change the standard deviation?

**Back:** Adding a constant shifts all values by the same amount, so the spread remains the same.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Define the Interquartile Range (IQR).

**Back:** $$Q_3 - Q_1$

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What assumption is made about data within a class when using linear interpolation?

**Back:** Data values are evenly spread throughout the class.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the median class identified in a grouped frequency table of $n$ values?

**Back:** It is the class that contains the $(\frac{n}{2})^{th}$ value.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a calculator, the standard deviation calculated with divisor $n$ is typically labelled $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** On a calculator, the standard deviation calculated with divisor $n$ is typically labelled $\boxed{\sigma_x}$.
