# Representations of Data Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the true boundaries for the class 10-14 if data is rounded to the nearest whole number?

**Back:** 9.5 and 14.5

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** In a histogram, what does the area of each bar represent?

**Back:** Frequency

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for frequency density?

**Back:** $$$\frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}$$

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a histogram, the vertical axis represents [ ___ ], not frequency.

**Back:** In a histogram, the vertical axis represents frequency density, not frequency.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate the horizontal scale on a drawn histogram (cm per unit)?

**Back:** $$$\frac{\text{drawn width in cm}}{\text{true class width}}$$

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are histogram height and width scales calculated independently?

**Back:** Frequency is represented by area, so height must represent frequency density specifically.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** The interquartile range (IQR) represents the spread of the middle [ ___ ] of the data.

**Back:** The interquartile range (IQR) represents the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate the IQR from quartiles?

**Back:** $$Q_3 - Q_1$

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the lower fence formula for identifying outliers?

**Back:** $$Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the upper fence formula for identifying outliers?

**Back:** $$Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** Where do the whiskers stop on a box plot if outliers are present?

**Back:** At the smallest and largest non-outlier values.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a box plot, outliers are typically plotted separately using [ ___ ].

**Back:** On a box plot, outliers are typically plotted separately using crosses.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** List three features to compare when describing two distributions using box plots.

**Back:** Location (compare medians)Spread (compare IQRs or ranges)Outliers (mention any in context)

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** When are the median and IQR preferred over the mean and standard deviation?

**Back:** When data are skewed or contain outliers.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for standard deviation using summary statistics?

**Back:** $$$\sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{n} - \bar{x}^2}$$

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** A variable is measured to the nearest whole number. The class 11-18 has true boundaries of [ ___ ] and 18.5.

**Back:** A variable is measured to the nearest whole number. The class 11-18 has true boundaries of 10.5 and 18.5.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** A variable is measured to the nearest whole number. The class 11-18 has true boundaries of 10.5 and [ ___ ].

**Back:** A variable is measured to the nearest whole number. The class 11-18 has true boundaries of 10.5 and 18.5.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** When finding quartiles from an odd number of data values, do not include the [ ___ ] in either half for calculation.

**Back:** When finding quartiles from an odd number of data values, do not include the median in either half for calculation.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you estimate probability from a histogram if the total frequency is known?

**Back:** $$$\frac{\text{area of required section}}{\text{total area}} \times \text{total frequency}$$ (then divide by total frequency) or simply the ratio of areas.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** Mean and standard deviation are most useful when data are [ ___ ] and have no major outliers.

**Back:** Mean and standard deviation are most useful when data are symmetrical and have no major outliers.
