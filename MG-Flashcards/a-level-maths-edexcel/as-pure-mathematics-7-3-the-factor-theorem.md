# 7.3 The Factor Theorem Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the degree of a polynomial determined?

**Back:** By the highest power of the variable.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the relationship between a root ($x=a$) and a factor of a polynomial?

**Back:** If $x=a$ is a root, then $(x-a)$ is a factor.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $x=-3$ is a root of a polynomial, the corresponding linear factor is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If $x=-3$ is a root of a polynomial, the corresponding linear factor is $\boxed{x+3}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** If $x = \frac{1}{2}$ is a root of $f(x)$, what is the equivalent linear factor with integer coefficients?

**Back:** $$2x-1$

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** The Factor Theorem states that the bracket $(x-a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ if and only if $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The Factor Theorem states that the bracket $(x-a)$ is a factor of $f(x)$ if and only if $\boxed{f(a)=0}$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** To use the Factor Theorem to show $(x+2)$ is a factor of $f(x)$, what specific value must be substituted for $x$?

**Back:** $$-2$

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** Where are the roots of a polynomial found on a graph of $y=f(x)$?

**Back:** The x-intercepts (where the curve crosses or touches the x-axis).

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In a cubic with integer coefficients, which term provides the candidate values for integer roots?

**Back:** The constant term (the number without an $x$).

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** For the polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x + 6$, possible integer roots must be factors of [ ___ ].

**Back:** For the polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 5x + 6$, possible integer roots must be factors of 6.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** According to exam technique, what two things must be shown to prove $(x-a)$ is a factor?

**Back:** The substitution $f(a)$ calculated to equal 0A conclusion stating that $(x-a)$ is a factor

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the result of dividing a cubic polynomial by a linear factor?

**Back:** A quadratic expression.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** When factorising $f(x) = 2x^3 + ...$ into $(x-a)(Ax^2+Bx+C)$, how is the coefficient $A$ determined?

**Back:** By comparing the $x^3$ coefficients (in this case, $A=2$).

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** When substituting negative numbers into a polynomial, you should always use [ ___ ] to avoid accuracy errors.

**Back:** When substituting negative numbers into a polynomial, you should always use brackets to avoid accuracy errors.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** If $f(x)$ contains two unknown constants $p$ and $q$, and you are given $f(1)=0$ and $f(2)=5$, what method is used to find $p$ and $q$?

**Back:** Simultaneous equations.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the 3 standard steps to solve a cubic equation $f(x)=0$.

**Back:** Find one root using the factor theoremDivide by the matching linear factor to get a quadraticSolve the quadratic (factorising or formula)

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic factor cannot be factorised using integers, use the [ ___ ] to find the remaining roots.

**Back:** If a quadratic factor cannot be factorised using integers, use the quadratic formula to find the remaining roots.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** When asked to "factorise completely", what should you do if the remaining quadratic factor cannot be factorised into integers?

**Back:** Leave the answer as (linear factor)(quadratic factor).

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** If $f(2) = -12$, what does the factor theorem tell us about the expression $(x-2)$?

**Back:** $$(x-2)$ is not a factor of $f(x)$.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a cubic $f(x)$, if $x=a$ is the only root that results in $f(x)=0$, the curve will cross the x-axis [ ___ ].

**Back:** For a cubic $f(x)$, if $x=a$ is the only root that results in $f(x)=0$, the curve will cross the x-axis once.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the degree of a cubic polynomial?

**Back:** Degree 3.
