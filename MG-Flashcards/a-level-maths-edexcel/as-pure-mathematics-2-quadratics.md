## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** For a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, the discriminant $\Delta$ is given by the formula $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** For a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, the discriminant $\Delta$ is given by the formula $\boxed{\Delta = b^2 - 4ac}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** How many real roots exist if the discriminant $\Delta > 0$?

**Back:** Two distinct real roots.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a quadratic has a repeated root, its discriminant must be $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If a quadratic has a repeated root, its discriminant must be $\boxed{\Delta = 0}$.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What does $\Delta < 0$ imply about the graph of a quadratic?

**Back:** The curve never crosses the $x$-axis (no real roots).

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the graphical behavior of a quadratic at its x-intercept when $\Delta = 0$?

**Back:** The curve touches the $x$-axis at its turning point.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** When solving for a parameter $k$ in $(k+1)x^2 + 2x + k = 0$, you must exclude the value $\text{[...]}$ because the equation would no longer be quadratic.

**Back:** When solving for a parameter $k$ in $(k+1)x^2 + 2x + k = 0$, you must exclude the value $\boxed{k = -1}$ because the equation would no longer be quadratic.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the turning point of $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$.

**Back:** $$(h, k)$

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the form $y = a(x + b)^2 + c$, the turning point is located at $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** In the form $y = a(x + b)^2 + c$, the turning point is located at $\boxed{(-b, c)}$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the equation for the axis of symmetry of $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$?

**Back:** $$x = h$

## Card 16 — `cloze`

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** A square of a real expression (e.g., $(x - h)^2$) is always $non-negative$ or $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** A square of a real expression (e.g., $(x - h)^2$) is always $non-negative$ or $\boxed{\ge 0}$.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** Why does $(x - 3)^2 + 5 = 0$ have no real roots?

**Back:** $$(x - 3)^2$ must be $\ge 0$, so $(x - 3)^2 + 5$ must be $\ge 5$; it can never equal $0$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step before calculating the discriminant of $3x^2 + 7 = 2x$?

**Back:** Rearrange to standard form: $3x^2 - 2x + 7 = 0$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the relationship between the turning point and the normal to the curve at that point?

**Back:** The normal is a vertical line ($x = x_{turning\,point}$).
