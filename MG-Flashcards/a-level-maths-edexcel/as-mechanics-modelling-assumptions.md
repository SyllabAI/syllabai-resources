# Modelling Assumptions Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 22 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In mechanics, what is a mathematical model?

**Back:** A simplified version of a real-world situation used to make mathematics manageable.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** When using a particle model, the object's mass is treated as [ ___ ].

**Back:** When using a particle model, the object's mass is treated as concentrated at a single point.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What three physical properties are ignored when an object is modelled as a particle?

**Back:** SizeShapeRotation / turning effects

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Why might a particle model be unsuitable for a car in a turning maneuver?

**Back:** It cannot account for tilting, spinning, or forces acting on different parts of the car.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the definition of constant acceleration?

**Back:** The rate of change of velocity remains the same throughout the motion.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What two conditions must be met to use SUVAT (constant-acceleration) equations?

**Back:** Acceleration is constantMotion is in a straight line

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Near the Earth's surface, the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) is taken as $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** Near the Earth's surface, the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) is taken as $\boxed{9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** If 'upwards' is the positive direction, what is the value of acceleration for an object in free fall?

**Back:** $$a = -9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What does it mean for an object to move freely under gravity?

**Back:** The only force acting on the object is its weight.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** When is the assumption of negligible air resistance likely to be unsuitable?

**Back:** When the object has a large surface area relative to its mass (e.g., a feather or parachute).

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** If air resistance is ignored in a model, how will the calculated final speed of a falling object compare to its real speed?

**Back:** The calculated speed will be higher than the real speed.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] surface has no friction, while a rough surface includes friction.

**Back:** A smooth surface has no friction, while a rough surface includes friction.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** A smooth surface has no friction, while a [ ___ ] surface includes friction.

**Back:** A smooth surface has no friction, while a rough surface includes friction.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is assumed about a light object (like a string or rod) in a mechanics model?

**Back:** It has zero mass and its weight is ignored.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the physical consequence of modelling a string as inextensible?

**Back:** Connected objects have the same magnitude of acceleration (if the string is taut).

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** For connected objects to share the same acceleration magnitude, the inextensible string must be [ ___ ].

**Back:** For connected objects to share the same acceleration magnitude, the inextensible string must be taut.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the consequence of modelling a pulley as smooth and light?

**Back:** The tension in the string is the same on both sides of the pulley.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** State three assumptions made when an object is modelled as a rod.

**Back:** Mass is concentrated along a lineIt has no thicknessIt is rigid (does not bend)

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** A rod is assumed to be [ ___ ], meaning it does not bend.

**Back:** A rod is assumed to be rigid, meaning it does not bend.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** A rod is assumed to be rigid, meaning it does not [ ___ ].

**Back:** A rod is assumed to be rigid, meaning it does not bend.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is the particle model suitable for a small parcel sliding a long distance?

**Back:** The parcel's size is small compared to the distance travelled and it does not rotate.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** How should you justify the suitability of a model in an exam?

**Back:** Identify the modelling assumption and link it to a specific physical effect in the real situation.
