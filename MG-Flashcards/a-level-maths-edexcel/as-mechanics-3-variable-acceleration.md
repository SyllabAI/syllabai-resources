# Variable Acceleration Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can SUVAT formulae not be used for variable acceleration?

**Back:** SUVAT only applies when acceleration is constant.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find velocity ($v$) from displacement ($s$), you must [ ___ ] with respect to time ($t$).

**Back:** To find velocity ($v$) from displacement ($s$), you must differentiate with respect to time ($t$).

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find acceleration ($a$) from velocity ($v$), you must [ ___ ] with respect to time ($t$).

**Back:** To find acceleration ($a$) from velocity ($v$), you must differentiate with respect to time ($t$).

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate velocity from acceleration?

**Back:** Integrate acceleration with respect to time: $v = \int a \, dt$Include a constant of integration ($+C$)

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** To find displacement ($s$) from velocity ($v$), you must [ ___ ] with respect to time ($t$).

**Back:** To find displacement ($s$) from velocity ($v$), you must integrate with respect to time ($t$).

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In mechanics, what does the term initially imply about the variable $t$?

**Back:** $$t = 0$

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** When integrating to find displacement or velocity, you must always include a [ ___ ] (e.g., $+C$).

**Back:** When integrating to find displacement or velocity, you must always include a constant of integration (e.g., $+C$).

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** What condition is met when a particle is instantaneously at rest?

**Back:** $$v = 0$

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the physical difference between displacement and distance?

**Back:** Displacement: signed position relative to a fixed point.Distance: total length of the path travelled (always positive).

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a velocity-time graph, displacement is the [ ___ ] under the graph.

**Back:** On a velocity-time graph, displacement is the signed area under the graph.

## Card 11 — `cloze`

**Front:** On a velocity-time graph, total distance is the [ ___ ] (treating negative areas as positive).

**Back:** On a velocity-time graph, total distance is the total area (treating negative areas as positive).

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the distance between two positions where a particle is at rest?

**Back:** Find times $t_1$ and $t_2$ where $v=0$.Calculate the magnitude of the displacement change: $|\int_{t_1}^{t_2} v \, dt|$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What must be done to calculate total distance if velocity changes sign during the interval?

**Back:** Solve $v = 0$ to find turning points.Split the integral into sections at these times.Add the absolute values of the displacement for each section.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the calculus relationship between displacement ($s$) and acceleration ($a$)?

**Back:** $$a = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}$ (Differentiate displacement twice).

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the time when a particle's acceleration is zero given its velocity function?

**Back:** Differentiate $v$ to find $a = \frac{dv}{dt}$.Set the expression for $a$ to zero and solve for $t$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** What units are typically used for acceleration in variable acceleration problems?

**Back:** m s$^{-2}$

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** In piecewise motion, how is the constant of integration found for the second time interval?

**Back:** Use the final velocity/displacement from the first interval as the starting condition for the second.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** A negative displacement value means the particle is in the [ ___ ] relative to the origin.

**Back:** A negative displacement value means the particle is in the negative direction relative to the origin.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** If $a = 6t - 12$ and $v(0) = 9$, what is the velocity function?

**Back:** $$v = 3t^2 - 12t + 9$

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it incorrect to use a piecewise formula for $t > 3$ to calculate displacement at $t = 2$?

**Back:** Formulae are only valid within their specified time intervals.
