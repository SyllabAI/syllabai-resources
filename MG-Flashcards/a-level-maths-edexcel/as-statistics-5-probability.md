# Probability Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** The [ ___ ] is the set of all possible outcomes in a probability experiment.

**Back:** The sample space is the set of all possible outcomes in a probability experiment.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What range of values can a probability $P(A)$ take?

**Back:** $$0 \le P(A) \le 1$

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** For any event $A$, the probability of its complement $A'$ is calculated as $P(A') = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** For any event $A$, the probability of its complement $A'$ is calculated as $P(A') = \boxed{1 - P(A)}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** In set notation, what symbol represents the intersection (A and B)?

**Back:** $$A \cap B$

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** In set notation, what symbol represents the union (A or B)?

**Back:** $$A \cup B$

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In a Venn diagram, why is $P(A \cap B)$ subtracted in the formula for $P(A \cup B)$?

**Back:** To avoid double-counting the overlap between the two events.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** The addition rule for the union of two events is $P(A \cup B) = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The addition rule for the union of two events is $P(A \cup B) = \boxed{P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** Define mutually exclusive events.

**Back:** Events that cannot happen at the same time.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** If events $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, then $P(A \cap B) = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If events $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, then $P(A \cap B) = \boxed{0}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** If two events are mutually exclusive and have non-zero probabilities, can they be independent?

**Back:** No; knowing one occurs makes the other impossible, so they affect each other.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step when filling in a three-set Venn diagram?

**Back:** Place the value for the center region (all three events) first.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Define independent events.

**Back:** The outcome of one event does not affect the probability of the other.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** Events $A$ and $B$ are independent if and only if $P(A \cap B) = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** Events $A$ and $B$ are independent if and only if $P(A \cap B) = \boxed{P(A)P(B)}$.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** When asked to test for independence in an exam, what must your answer include?

**Back:** A comparison showing whether $P(A \cap B)$ equals $P(A)P(B)$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** In a tree diagram, how do you calculate the probability of a specific combined outcome (e.g., A and then B)?

**Back:** Multiply the probabilities along the branches.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** When multiple different routes on a tree diagram satisfy a condition, you [ ___ ] their individual probabilities.碎

**Back:** When multiple different routes on a tree diagram satisfy a condition, you add their individual probabilities.碎

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the notation $P(B|A)$ represent?

**Back:** The conditional probability of $B$ given that $A$ has occurred.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** How does "without replacement" affect a tree diagram for a second selection?

**Back:** The total outcomes and the favourable outcomes must both be updated (decremented).

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** When calculating the probability of picking "one of each" type from a tree, which routes must be considered?

**Back:** All possible orders (e.g., Red then Blue AND Blue then Red).

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the total probability of an event that can occur via different machines or categories?

**Back:** Sum the products of the branches for every route leading to that event.
