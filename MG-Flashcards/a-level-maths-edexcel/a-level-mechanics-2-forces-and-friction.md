## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the key assumption of the particle model in mechanics?

**Back:** Size and shape are ignored, so all forces act through a single point.

**Choices:**

- Size and shape are ignored, so all forces act through a single point. ✔
  - _why:_ The particle model treats an object as having mass but negligible dimensions, allowing every force to be considered as acting through one point and ignoring rotational effects.
- Mass and weight are equal, so all forces act through the centre of mass.
  - _why:_ Mass and weight are different physical quantities, and the particle model does not require forces to act through the centre of mass. It ignores size and shape and uses a single point.
- Size and shape are important, so all forces act through a single point.
  - _why:_ Treating size and shape as important contradicts the particle assumption, which removes dimensions from the model. The correct simplification is to ignore them and make forces act through one point.
- Size and shape are ignored, so all forces act along a single line.
  - _why:_ A particle model specifies one point of action, not merely one line. Confusing these ideas can lead to incorrect torque reasoning; the correct assumption ignores size and shape.
