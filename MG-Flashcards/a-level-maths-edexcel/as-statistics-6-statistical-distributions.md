# Statistical Distributions Flashcards

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** For any discrete random variable, the sum of all probabilities is: $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** For any discrete random variable, the sum of all probabilities is: $\boxed{\sum P(X=x)=1}$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What defines a random variable as discrete?

**Back:** It can only take separate, countable values (e.g., 0, 1, 2).

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** If $X$ is a discrete random variable taking integer values, what is the meaning of $P(X > 1.4)$?

**Back:** $$P(X \ge 2)$

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Define the cumulative distribution function $F(x)$ for a random variable $X$.

**Back:** The probability that $X$ is less than or equal to $x$: $F(x) = P(X \le x)$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** A cumulative distribution function $F(x)$ must never $\text{[...]}$ and its final value must be $1$.

**Back:** A cumulative distribution function $F(x)$ must never $\boxed{decrease}$ and its final value must be $1$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** A cumulative distribution function $F(x)$ must never $decrease$ and its final value must be $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** A cumulative distribution function $F(x)$ must never $decrease$ and its final value must be $\boxed{1}$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What characterizes a discrete uniform distribution with $n$ outcomes?

**Back:** Every outcome is equally likely, each with probability $1/n$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** State the four conditions required for a binomial distribution $X \sim B(n, p)$.

**Back:** Fixed number of trials ($n$)Two outcomes: success or failureConstant probability of success ($p$)Trials are independent

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** In the notation $X \sim B(n, p)$, what do $n$ and $p$ represent?

**Back:** $n$: number of trials$p$: probability of success

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The probability of exactly $r$ successes in a binomial distribution is $P(X=r) = \text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The probability of exactly $r$ successes in a binomial distribution is $P(X=r) = \boxed{\binom{n}{r}p^r(1-p)^{n-r}}$.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the binomial inequality $P(X < 3)$ rewritten for calculator entry (using cumulative functions)?

**Back:** $$P(X \le 2)$

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** How is $P(X \ge 5)$ calculated using the complement rule in a binomial model?

**Back:** $$1 - P(X \le 4)$

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Why might "number of goals in a football match" not be well-modelled by a binomial distribution?

**Back:** Events are not independentProbability of scoring is not constant throughout the match

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the expected number of successes if an event with probability $p$ is repeated $N$ times?

**Back:** $$Np$

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** An expected value $Np$ is a $\text{[...]}$ and does not have to be a $whole number$.

**Back:** An expected value $Np$ is a $\boxed{long-run average}$ and does not have to be a $whole number$.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** An expected value $Np$ is a $long-run average$ and does not have to be a $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** An expected value $Np$ is a $long-run average$ and does not have to be a $\boxed{whole number}$.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** If two games are independent, how is the probability of a specific outcome in both games calculated?

**Back:** Multiply the individual probabilities of those outcomes.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A player throws balls with success probability 0.4. What is the calculation for the first success being on the 4th throw?

**Back:** $$(0.6)^3 \times 0.4$ (three failures then one success)

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** When solving an inequality for a transformation like $Y = 9 - 3X$, what must happen when dividing by $-3$?

**Back:** The inequality sign must be reversed.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the primary method for finding a missing constant $k$ in a discrete probability table?

**Back:** Set the sum of all probabilities equal to 1 and solve for $k$.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a binomial distribution, the probability of failure is often written as $\text{[...]}$ or represented by the letter $q$.

**Back:** In a binomial distribution, the probability of failure is often written as $\boxed{1-p}$ or represented by the letter $q$.

## Card 22 — `cloze`

**Front:** In a binomial distribution, the probability of failure is often written as $1-p$ or represented by the letter $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** In a binomial distribution, the probability of failure is often written as $1-p$ or represented by the letter $\boxed{q}$.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the probability of a total score from two independent games?

**Back:** List all pairs of scores that reach that totalMultiply probabilities within each pairAdd the results of all pairs
