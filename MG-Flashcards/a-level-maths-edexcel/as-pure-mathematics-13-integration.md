# Integration Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 23 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the relationship between integration and differentiation?

**Back:** Integration is reverse differentiation (finding the antiderivative).

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** To integrate a power of $x$ using the reverse power rule: [ ___ ], then divide by the new power.

**Back:** To integrate a power of $x$ using the reverse power rule: increase the power by 1, then divide by the new power.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** To integrate a power of $x$ using the reverse power rule: increase the power by 1, then [ ___ ].

**Back:** To integrate a power of $x$ using the reverse power rule: increase the power by 1, then divide by the new power.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** State the general rule for $\int ax^n \, dx$ where $n \neq -1$.

**Back:** $$$\frac{a}{n+1}x^{n+1}+C$$

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Why must a constant of integration ($C$) be added to indefinite integrals?

**Back:** Differentiating any constant results in zero, so the original constant cannot be known from the gradient function alone.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you verify if an integration result is correct?

**Back:** Differentiate the answer; it should return the original expression.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** When integrating an expression like $(x+5)(x-2)$, you must [ ___ ] before applying the power rule.

**Back:** When integrating an expression like $(x+5)(x-2)$, you must expand the brackets before applying the power rule.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** How is a constant term $k$ integrated with respect to $x$?

**Back:** It becomes a multiple of $x$: $kx + C$.

**Back:** To integrate roots or fractions, rewrite them as powers: $\sqrt{x} = $ $\boxed{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}$$\frac{1}{x^n} = $ $x^{-n}$

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What specific power of $x$ cannot be integrated using the reverse power rule?

**Back:** $$x^{-1}$ (or $\frac{1}{x}$)

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the steps to find the specific constant of integration $C$.

**Back:** Integrate the derivative to find the general solution.Substitute the coordinates of a known point $(x, y)$.Solve the resulting equation for $C$.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the primary difference between the output of an indefinite integral and a definite integral?

**Back:** Indefinite: A function (family of curves).Definite: A number (often representing area).

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** The notation $[F(x)]_a^b$ indicates the calculation $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The notation $[F(x)]_a^b$ indicates the calculation $\boxed{F(b) - F(a)}$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Geometrically, what does the definite integral $\int_a^b f(x) \, dx$ represent if the curve is above the $x$-axis?

**Back:** The area under the curve between $x=a$ and $x=b$.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you determine the limits of integration for the area enclosed between two curves?

**Back:** Find the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points by setting the two equations equal to each other.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** State the formula for the area between two curves where $f(x)$ is above $g(x)$ between $a$ and $b$.

**Back:** $$$\text{Area} = \int_a^b (f(x) - g(x)) \, dx$$

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a curve crosses the $x$-axis, you must [ ___ ] at the roots and treat the area below the axis as positive.

**Back:** If a curve crosses the $x$-axis, you must split the integral at the roots and treat the area below the axis as positive.

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** If a curve crosses the $x$-axis, you must split the integral at the roots and treat the area [ ___ ] as positive.

**Back:** If a curve crosses the $x$-axis, you must split the integral at the roots and treat the area below the axis as positive.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the $dx$ in the symbol $\int f(x) \, dx$ signify?

**Back:** The variable you are integrating with respect to.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Front:** Before integrating $\frac{4}{x^2}$, you should rewrite it as $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** Before integrating $\frac{4}{x^2}$, you should rewrite it as $\boxed{4x^{-2}}$.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** In a definite integral calculation, when is the constant of integration $C$ omitted?

**Back:** Always; it cancels out during the subtraction $F(b) - F(a)$.

## Card 23 — `cloze`

**Front:** To decide which curve is "top" for an area calculation, [ ___ ] between the limits into both functions and compare the $y$-values.

**Back:** To decide which curve is "top" for an area calculation, substitute an x-value between the limits into both functions and compare the $y$-values.
