# 12.2 Differentiation from first principles Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the average gradient between two points on a curve defined?

**Back:** The gradient of the chord (or secant) joining those two points.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the instantaneous gradient at a specific point on a curve defined?

**Back:** The gradient of the tangent at that point.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The derivative from first principles is defined by the formula: $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \text{[...]}$

**Back:** The derivative from first principles is defined by the formula: $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \boxed{\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}}$

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What does the symbol $\lim_{h \to 0}$ represent in the first principles formula?

**Back:** The value the expression approaches as $h$ gets closer and closer to 0.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it mathematically impossible to substitute $h=0$ into the first principles formula immediately?

**Back:** It would result in division by zero.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** When expanding $(x+h)^2$ for a first principles proof, the result is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** When expanding $(x+h)^2$ for a first principles proof, the result is $\boxed{x^2 + 2xh + h^2}$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** The expansion for a cubic term $(x+h)^3$ is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The expansion for a cubic term $(x+h)^3$ is $\boxed{x^3 + 3x^2h + 3xh^2 + h^3}$.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** In a first principles proof, what must be true of every term remaining in the numerator after subtracting $f(x)$?

**Back:** Every term must contain a factor of $h$.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What algebraic step must occur before letting $h \to 0$ in a first principles proof?

**Back:** Factorising out and cancelling $h$ from the numerator and denominator.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** When proving the derivative of $3x^2$ from first principles, what is the expression for $f(x+h)$?

**Back:** $$3(x+h)^2 = 3x^2 + 6xh + 3h^2$

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** If an exam question asks to differentiate "from first principles", can you just use the power rule?

**Back:** No; the full limit definition and algebraic proof must be shown for marks.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the common mistake when subtracting $f(x)$ from $f(x+h)$ in complex expressions?

**Back:** Forgetting to subtract the whole function (not using brackets), which leads to sign errors.

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** How does the constant $a$ behave when differentiating $f(x) = ax^n$ from first principles?

**Back:** It remains as a multiplier for every term in the expansion and the final derivative.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the context of first principles, $h$ represents a [ ___ ] in $x$.

**Back:** In the context of first principles, $h$ represents a small change in $x$.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the final three steps of the core method for differentiation from first principles.

**Back:** Simplify the numeratorCancel the factor of $h$Let $h \to 0$

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it useful to use the power rule when answering a first principles question?

**Back:** To check your final answer is correct.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $f(x) = 6x$, the simplified numerator $f(x+h) - f(x)$ becomes $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If $f(x) = 6x$, the simplified numerator $f(x+h) - f(x)$ becomes $\boxed{6h}$.

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** If $f(x) = 3x^2$, the simplified numerator $f(x+h) - f(x)$ becomes $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** If $f(x) = 3x^2$, the simplified numerator $f(x+h) - f(x)$ becomes $\boxed{6xh + 3h^2}$.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** What does a derivative $f'(x)$ represent on a graph?

**Back:** The instantaneous gradient of the curve at any point $x$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the simplified form of $\frac{h(12x^2 + 12xh + 4h^2)}{h}$ before taking the limit?

**Back:** $$12x^2 + 12xh + 4h^2$
