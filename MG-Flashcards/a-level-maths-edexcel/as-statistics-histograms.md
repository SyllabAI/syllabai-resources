# Histograms Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 21 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In a histogram, what does the area of each bar represent?

**Back:** The frequency of the class.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The formula for frequency density is $$\text{[...]}$$.

**Back:** The formula for frequency density is $$\boxed{\text{frequency density} = \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}}}$$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** What is represented on the vertical axis (y-axis) of a histogram?

**Back:** Frequency density.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** If data is measured to the nearest whole number, the class width of the interval 10–14 is [ ___ ].

**Back:** If data is measured to the nearest whole number, the class width of the interval 10–14 is 5.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** Why do the bars in a histogram typically touch?

**Back:** The data is continuous (or treated as continuous).

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** How is the class width calculated for the interval 9–13 when data is rounded to the nearest integer?

**Back:** Use class boundaries 8.5 to 13.5$13.5 - 8.5 = 5$

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** If two classes have the same frequency but class A is wider than class B, which bar is taller?

**Back:** Class B (the narrower class has a higher frequency density).

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you calculate frequency from a histogram bar?

**Back:** $$\text{class width} \times \text{frequency density}$

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a histogram bar is measured in cm on paper, the horizontal scale links class width to [ ___ ].

**Back:** When a histogram bar is measured in cm on paper, the horizontal scale links class width to drawn width.

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** When a histogram bar is measured in cm on paper, the vertical scale links frequency density to [ ___ ].

**Back:** When a histogram bar is measured in cm on paper, the vertical scale links frequency density to drawn height.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** A variable is measured to the nearest whole number. What are the class boundaries for the interval 20–22?

**Back:** 19.5 and 22.5

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A class 4–8 (nearest whole number) has a frequency of 10. What is its frequency density?

**Back:** Class width = 5$10 \div 5 = 2$

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** To estimate the probability of an event from a histogram, you divide the [ ___ ] by the total frequency.

**Back:** To estimate the probability of an event from a histogram, you divide the estimated frequency for the event by the total frequency.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** To estimate the probability of an event from a histogram, you divide the estimated frequency for the event by the [ ___ ].

**Back:** To estimate the probability of an event from a histogram, you divide the estimated frequency for the event by the total frequency.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** What assumption is made when a probability boundary cuts through a histogram class?

**Back:** Data is evenly spread (distributed) within that class.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you estimate the frequency of a partial class where the boundary cuts through it?

**Back:** Take the fraction of the width covered by the event and multiply by the total class frequency.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** A bar has a width of 5 units and a frequency density of 2.8. What is the frequency?

**Back:** 14 ($5 \times 2.8$)

## Card 18 — `cloze`

**Front:** The probability calculated from a histogram where boundaries cut through classes is only an [ ___ ] because the exact distribution of values within classes is unknown.

**Back:** The probability calculated from a histogram where boundaries cut through classes is only an estimate because the exact distribution of values within classes is unknown.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** In a histogram, the bar for 9–13 has a class width of 5 and a drawn width of 2.5 cm. What is the horizontal scale?

**Back:** 0.5 cm per unit ($2.5 \div 5$)

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** If frequency density 2.4 is represented by a drawn height of 3.6 cm, what is the vertical scale?

**Back:** 1.5 cm per unit ($3.6 \div 2.4$)

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the primary reason for using a histogram instead of a standard bar chart?

**Back:** The data is grouped and the class widths are unequal.
