# Data Collection Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 24 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** Contrast a population, a sample, and a census.

**Back:** Population: the whole group of interest.Sample: a subset of the population.Census: data collected from every population member.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** A [ ___ ] sample reflects the population fairly, whereas a biased sample systematically favours certain outcomes.

**Back:** A representative sample reflects the population fairly, whereas a biased sample systematically favours certain outcomes.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** A representative sample reflects the population fairly, whereas a [ ___ ] sample systematically favours certain outcomes.

**Back:** A representative sample reflects the population fairly, whereas a biased sample systematically favours certain outcomes.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** What is a sampling frame?

**Back:** A list of all members of the population used to select a sample.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What defines a simple random sample?

**Back:** Every possible sample of that size has an equal chance of being selected.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** Outline the steps for taking a simple random sample from a list of $N$ members.

**Back:** Number the members from $1$ to $N$.Generate random numbers using a random number generator.Ignore repeats (sampling without replacement).

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** Sampling is usually done without replacement, meaning if a number is [ ___ ], the researcher must ignore it and generate another.

**Back:** Sampling is usually done without replacement, meaning if a number is repeated, the researcher must ignore it and generate another.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** Sampling is usually done without replacement, meaning if a number is repeated, the researcher must [ ___ ] and generate another.

**Back:** Sampling is usually done without replacement, meaning if a number is repeated, the researcher must ignore it and generate another.

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the formula for the number to sample from a stratum in a stratified random sample?

**Back:** $$$\frac{\text{stratum size}}{\text{population size}} \times \text{overall sample size}$$

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** A population has $96$ Year 12s and $84$ Year 13s. Calculate the number of Year 12s needed for a stratified sample of $30$.

**Back:** $$$\frac{96}{180} \times 30 = 16$$

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the essential final step of stratified random sampling after calculating the size of each group?

**Back:** Perform a simple random sample within each stratum.

## Card 12 — `cloze`

**Front:** In systematic sampling, the sampling interval $k$ is calculated by the formula $k = \text{[...]}$ (Population size / Sample size).

**Back:** In systematic sampling, the sampling interval $k$ is calculated by the formula $k = \boxed{\frac{N}{n}}$ (Population size / Sample size).

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** Describe the process of systematic sampling.

**Back:** Choose a random starting point between 1 and the interval $k$.Select every $k$th member of the sampling frame.

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the sampling interval is $k=5$ and the random start is $4$, the first three members selected in a systematic sample are [ ___ ], 9, and 14.

**Back:** If the sampling interval is $k=5$ and the random start is $4$, the first three members selected in a systematic sample are 4, 9, and 14.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the sampling interval is $k=5$ and the random start is $4$, the first three members selected in a systematic sample are 4, [ ___ ], and 14.

**Back:** If the sampling interval is $k=5$ and the random start is $4$, the first three members selected in a systematic sample are 4, 9, and 14.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** If the sampling interval is $k=5$ and the random start is $4$, the first three members selected in a systematic sample are 4, 9, and [ ___ ].

**Back:** If the sampling interval is $k=5$ and the random start is $4$, the first three members selected in a systematic sample are 4, 9, and 14.

## Card 17 — `basic`

**Front:** Why might stratified random sampling be impossible for fish in a lake?

**Back:** There is no sampling frame (list of individuals) to assign numbers for random selection.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** What is quota sampling?

**Back:** A non-random method where a researcher collects data from groups until set proportions are filled.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify two types of non-random sampling other than quota sampling.

**Back:** Opportunity (convenience) sampling.Volunteer (self-selected) sampling.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** What is opportunity sampling?

**Back:** Choosing individuals who are easiest to access at the time of the study.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is standing at a leisure centre entrance in the afternoon to ask the first 35 people an example of biased sampling?

**Back:** It only includes people available at that specific time (e.g., excludes those working 9-5).

## Card 22 — `cloze`

**Front:** In systematic sampling, the random start must be between 1 and the [ ___ ] to ensure the sample is properly random.

**Back:** In systematic sampling, the random start must be between 1 and the sampling interval (k) to ensure the sample is properly random.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the sampling method: A researcher numbers every item in a list and uses a random number generator to pick 20 items.

**Back:** Simple random sampling.

## Card 24 — `basic`

**Front:** Identify the sampling method: A student posts a survey link on social media for anyone to complete.

**Back:** Volunteer (or self-selected) sampling.
