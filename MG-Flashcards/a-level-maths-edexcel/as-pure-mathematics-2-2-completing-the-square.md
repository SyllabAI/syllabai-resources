# 2.2 Completing the Square Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 20 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the expansion of $(x+a)^2$, the coefficient of $x$ is always $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** In the expansion of $(x+a)^2$, the coefficient of $x$ is always $\boxed{2a}$.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step in completing the square for the expression $x^2 + bx + c$?

**Back:** Halve the coefficient of $x$ to get the value inside the bracket: $(x + \frac{b}{2})^2$.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** Express $x^2 + 10x + 7$ in completed-square form.

**Back:** $$(x+5)^2 - 18$

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** Why are fractions common when completing the square for $x^2 - 7x + 4$?

**Back:** Because the coefficient of $x$ ($-7$) is odd.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** What defines a non-monic quadratic?

**Back:** The coefficient of $x^2$ is not 1.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step to complete the square for $3x^2 - 12x + 5$?

**Back:** Factor out 3 from the $x^2$ and $x$ terms: $3(x^2 - 4x) + 5$.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** When completing the square for $a(x+B)^2+C$, the term $C$ is found by expanding the bracket and adjusting the [ ___ ].

**Back:** When completing the square for $a(x+B)^2+C$, the term $C$ is found by expanding the bracket and adjusting the constant.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** For a curve $y = (x - a)^2 + b$, what are the coordinates of the minimum point?

**Back:** $$(a, b)$

## Card 9 — `basic`

**Front:** State the minimum point of $y = (x + 6)^2 - 11$.

**Back:** $$(-6, -11)$

## Card 10 — `cloze`

**Front:** The expression $(x+2)^2$ can never be negative because the result of squaring any real number is always [ ___ ].

**Back:** The expression $(x+2)^2$ can never be negative because the result of squaring any real number is always $\ge 0$ (greater than or equal to zero).

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find the $x$-coordinate of the minimum point of $y = (x-4)^2 - 5$?

**Back:** Set the squared bracket to zero: $x-4=0$, so $x=4$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A quadratic has a minimum point at $(3, -4)$. What is its equation in completed-square form?

**Back:** $$y = (x-3)^2 - 4$

## Card 13 — `basic`

**Front:** How can completed-square form prove $x^2 - 10x + 28 > 0$ for all real $x$?

**Back:** Rewrite as $(x-5)^2 + 3$.Since $(x-5)^2 \ge 0$, the minimum value is 3.Therefore, the expression is always positive.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** What is the first step to prove $x^2 + 3x + 9 > x + 1$ for all real $x$?

**Back:** Rearrange to one side so the expression is compared to zero: $x^2 + 2x + 8 > 0$.

## Card 15 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the context of quadratic equations, the roots are the $x$-coordinates where the parabola [ ___ ].

**Back:** In the context of quadratic equations, the roots are the $x$-coordinates where the parabola crosses the $x$-axis.

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** Complete the square for $x^2 + px + 5$.

**Back:** $$(x + \frac{p}{2})^2 - \frac{p^2}{4} + 5$

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** A quadratic equation has no real roots if, after completing the square and isolating the bracket, the value on the other side is [ ___ ].

**Back:** A quadratic equation has no real roots if, after completing the square and isolating the bracket, the value on the other side is negative.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** If solving $x^2 + px + 5 = 0$ leads to $x + \frac{p}{2} = \pm \sqrt{\frac{p^2}{4} - 5}$, what condition ensures no real roots exist?

**Back:** $$\frac{p^2}{4} - 5 < 0$ (or $p^2 - 20 < 0$)

## Card 19 — `cloze`

**Front:** To solve $p^2 < 20$, the range for $p$ is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** To solve $p^2 < 20$, the range for $p$ is $\boxed{-2\sqrt{5} < p < 2\sqrt{5}}$.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it important to expand your completed-square form mentally during an exam?

**Back:** To check it simplifies back to the original quadratic and catch constant errors.
