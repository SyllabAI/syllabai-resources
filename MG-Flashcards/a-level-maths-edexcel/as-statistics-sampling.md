# Sampling Flashcards

_Lane: `a-level-maths-edexcel` · grade — · 24 cards (source: MathsGenie flashcards)_

## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** For a survey about Year 12 students in one school, what is the population?

**Back:** Year 12 students in that school.

## Card 2 — `basic`

**Front:** A principal asks every student in a college about a timetable. What method is this?

**Back:** Census.

## Card 3 — `basic`

**Front:** If some groups are systematically more likely to be selected, what problem is present?

**Back:** Bias.

## Card 4 — `cloze`

**Front:** A sample is chosen from the [ ___ ] and used to draw conclusions about that larger group.

**Back:** A sample is chosen from the population and used to draw conclusions about that larger group.

## Card 5 — `basic`

**Front:** For a gym surveying active members, what is a suitable sampling frame?

**Back:** An up-to-date list of active members.

## Card 6 — `basic`

**Front:** In a sports centre membership database, what are the sampling units?

**Back:** The individual members.

## Card 7 — `cloze`

**Front:** A sampling frame should match the [ ___ ] as closely as possible.

**Back:** A sampling frame should match the population as closely as possible.

## Card 8 — `basic`

**Front:** A computer selects donor numbers at random from a list. What sampling method is this?

**Back:** Simple random sampling.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** In simple random sampling, every member has an [ ___ ] of being selected.

**Back:** In simple random sampling, every member has an equal chance of being selected.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** What must be complete for simple random sampling to be relatively unbiased?

**Back:** The sampling frame.

## Card 11 — `basic`

**Front:** What can still happen by chance in a simple random sample?

**Back:** Some groups may be under-represented.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** A teacher chooses every 6th name from an ordered list. What sampling method is this?

**Back:** Systematic sampling.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** Systematic sampling selects at [ ___ ] from an [ ___ ].

**Back:** Systematic sampling selects at regular intervals from an ordered list.

## Card 14 — `basic`

**Front:** When can systematic sampling become biased?

**Back:** When a list pattern or cycle matches the interval.

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** The population is split into groups and proportional numbers are chosen. What method is this?

**Back:** Stratified sampling.

## Card 16 — `cloze`

**Front:** In stratified sampling, bigger strata should contribute [ ___ ] than smaller strata.

**Back:** In stratified sampling, bigger strata should contribute more people than smaller strata.

## Card 17 — `cloze`

**Front:** For stratified sampling: number from stratum = [ ___ ] ÷ population size × [ ___ ].

**Back:** For stratified sampling: number from stratum = stratum size ÷ population size × sample size.

## Card 18 — `basic`

**Front:** A school has 640 pupils, 72 in a stratum, and wants a sample of 50. How many from that stratum?

**Back:** $$\frac{72}{640}\times 50=5.625$, so 6.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** A student asks pupils until he has 20 boys and 20 girls. What sampling method is this?

**Back:** Quota sampling.

## Card 20 — `cloze`

**Front:** Quota sampling fills [ ___ ] from groups, usually without [ ___ ] selection.

**Back:** Quota sampling fills fixed numbers from groups, usually without random selection.

## Card 21 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can quota sampling be biased?

**Back:** The researcher may choose convenient people.

## Card 22 — `basic`

**Front:** A manager asks the first 30 gym members entering on Tuesday morning. What method is this?

**Back:** Opportunity sampling.

## Card 23 — `cloze`

**Front:** Opportunity sampling is also called [ ___ ].

**Back:** Opportunity sampling is also called convenience sampling.

## Card 24 — `basic`

**Front:** Why may opportunity sampling fail to represent the population?

**Back:** It only uses people easiest to reach.
