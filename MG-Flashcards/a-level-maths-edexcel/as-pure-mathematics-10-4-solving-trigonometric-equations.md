## Card 1 — `basic`

**Front:** In trigonometric equations, what term describes the first angle solution provided by a calculator?

**Back:** The principal value.

## Card 2 — `cloze`

**Front:** The period of sine and cosine is $\text{[...]}$, whereas the period of tangent is $180^\circ$.

**Back:** The period of sine and cosine is $\boxed{360^\circ}$, whereas the period of tangent is $180^\circ$.

## Card 3 — `cloze`

**Front:** The period of sine and cosine is $360^\circ$, whereas the period of tangent is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The period of sine and cosine is $360^\circ$, whereas the period of tangent is $\boxed{180^\circ}$.

## Card 4 — `basic`

**Front:** When solving $\cos(2x+10^\circ)=0.4$ for $0 \le x < 180^\circ$, what is the first step regarding the interval?

**Back:** Transform the interval for the inside angle: $10^\circ \le (2x+10^\circ) < 370^\circ$.

## Card 5 — `cloze`

**Front:** For real angles, the values of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ must always lie between $\text{[...]}$ and $1$.

**Back:** For real angles, the values of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ must always lie between $\boxed{-1}$ and $1$.

## Card 6 — `cloze`

**Front:** For real angles, the values of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ must always lie between $-1$ and $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** For real angles, the values of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ must always lie between $-1$ and $\boxed{1}$.

## Card 7 — `basic`

**Front:** What must you do when taking the square root to solve an equation like $\tan^2 x = 5$?

**Back:** Account for both the positive and negative roots: $\tan x = \pm\sqrt{5}$.

## Card 8 — `cloze`

**Front:** The trigonometric identity used to convert $\sin^2 x$ into terms of cosine is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The trigonometric identity used to convert $\sin^2 x$ into terms of cosine is $\boxed{\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x}$.

## Card 9 — `cloze`

**Front:** The trigonometric identity used to convert $\cos^2 x$ into terms of sine is $\text{[...]}$.

**Back:** The trigonometric identity used to convert $\cos^2 x$ into terms of sine is $\boxed{\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x}$.

## Card 10 — `basic`

**Front:** Why is it dangerous to divide both sides of $2\sin x = 3\sin x\cos x$ by $\sin x$?

**Back:** It results in the loss of valid solutions where $\sin x = 0$.

## Card 12 — `basic`

**Front:** If a quadratic trig equation yields $\cos x = -2$, what action should be taken?

**Back:** Reject that solution because cosine cannot be less than $-1$.

## Card 13 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the expression $\tan^2 x$, the [ ___ ] is squared, not the angle ($x$).

**Back:** In the expression $\tan^2 x$, the ratio (tangent value) is squared, not the angle ($x$).

## Card 14 — `cloze`

**Front:** In the expression $\tan^2 x$, the ratio (tangent value) is squared, not the [ ___ ].

**Back:** In the expression $\tan^2 x$, the ratio (tangent value) is squared, not the angle ($x$).

## Card 15 — `basic`

**Front:** How do you find multiple solutions for a tangent equation once you have the principal value?

**Back:** Add or subtract multiples of $180^\circ$ (the period).

## Card 16 — `basic`

**Front:** When solving $\sin(3\theta-20^\circ)=0.5$, at what stage should you add $20^\circ$ and divide by $3$?

**Back:** Only after finding all possible values for the whole angle $(3\theta-20^\circ)$ in the transformed interval.

## Card 19 — `basic`

**Front:** Solve $3\cos^2 x + 5\cos x - 2 = 0$ given $(3\cos x - 1)(\cos x + 2) = 0$. Which factor provides valid solutions?

**Back:** $$3\cos x - 1 = 0$ (or $\cos x = \frac{1}{3}$), because $\cos x = -2$ is impossible.

## Card 20 — `basic`

**Front:** How should you treat $2x$ when solving $2\sin(2x)\tan(2x)=\cos(2x)+1$ for $0 \le x < 180^\circ$?

**Back:** Solve for the angle $u = 2x$ using the interval $0 \le u < 360^\circ$, then divide by $2$.

## Card 21 — `cloze`

**Back:** To solve equations mixing $\sin^2 x$ and $\cos x$, use the identity $\boxed{\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1}$ to create a quadratic in $\cos x$.

## Card 23 — `basic`

**Front:** What calculator mode is required for the angles discussed in this topic?

**Back:** Degree mode.

## Card 24 — `basic`

**Front:** Why can a single trig equation have multiple solutions within an interval?

**Back:** Because trigonometric graphs are periodic (they repeat).

## Card 25 — `basic`

**Front:** What are the two components used to find solutions beyond the principal value?

**Back:** Symmetry and periodicity of the trig graph.
