# Worksheet 4CH1-S1-g — Covalent bonding
SME group `1-7-covalent-bonding` · notes: 4 · spec points: 8

## Spec points in this subsection

- **4CH1-1.44** (1.44): know that a covalent bond is formed between atoms by the sharing of a pair of electrons
- **4CH1-1.45** (1.45): understand covalent bonds in terms of electrostatic attractions
- **4CH1-1.46** (1.46): understand how to use dot-and-cross diagrams to represent covalent bonds in: • diatomic molecules, including hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, halogens and hydrogen halides • inorganic molecules including water, ammonia and carbon dioxide • organic molecules containing up to two carbon atoms, including methane, ethane, ethene and those containing halogen atoms.
- **4CH1-1.47** (1.47): explain why substances with a simple molecular structures are gases or liquids, or solids with low melting and boiling points the term intermolecular forces of attraction can be used to represent all forces between molecules
- **4CH1-1.48** (1.48): explain why the melting and boiling points of substances with simple molecular structures increase, in general, with increasing relative molecular mass
- **4CH1-1.49** (1.49): explain why substances with giant covalent structures are solids with high melting and boiling points
- **4CH1-1.50** (1.50) · DAMAGE: explain how the structures of diamond, graphite and C60 fullerene influence their physical properties, including electrical conductivity and hardness
- **4CH1-1.51** (1.51): know that covalent compounds do not usually conduct electricity
