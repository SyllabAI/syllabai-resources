## Solving problems with Kirchhoff's laws

- Kirchhoff’s laws can be used to solve simple circuit problems

> **Worked Example**
> For the circuit below, state the readings of ammeters A<sub>1</sub>, A<sub>2</sub>  and A<sub>3</sub>.
> 
> ![WE - Kirchoff's first law question image, downloadable AS & A Level Physics revision notes](assets/0652cacefa6a-we-kirchoffs-first-law-question-image.png)
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1: Calculate the current through ammeter A**<sub>**1**</sub>
> 
> - The 0.270 A and A<sub>1</sub> are connected in series
> - The current entering the cell must equal the current out of it at A<sub>1</sub>
> - Therefore A<sub>1</sub> = 0.270 A
> 
> **Step 2: Calculate the current through ammeter A**<sub>**2**</sub>
> 
> - From Kirchhoff's first law, the total current into a junction must equal the total current out of it
> 
> ![10-2-5-we-kirchhoff-step-1---cie-new](assets/fb6eb55f01b9-10-2-5-we-kirchhoff-step-1-cie-new.png)
> 
> $80\mathrm{mA}=27\mathrm{mA}+A_{2}$
> 
> $A_{2}=80-27=53\mathrm{mA}$
> 
> **Step 3: Calculate the current through ammeter A**<sub>**3**</sub>
> 
> - Applying Kirchhoff's first law again gives:
> 
> ![10-2-5-we-kirchhoff-step-2---cie-new](assets/47578c2fded0-10-2-5-we-kirchhoff-step-2-cie-new.png)
> 
> $270\mathrm{mA}=A_{3}+80\mathrm{mA}$
> 
> $A_{3}=270-80=190\mathrm{mA}$

> **Worked Example**
> For the circuit below, state the readings of the voltmeters V<sub>1</sub>, V<sub>2</sub>  and V<sub>3</sub>.
> 
> All the lamps and resistors have the same resistance.
> 
> ![WE - kirchoff's second law question image, downloadable AS & A Level Physics revision notes](assets/b8d065366a55-we-kirchoffs-second-law-question-image.png)
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1: Calculate the potential difference shown by voltmeter V**<sub>**1**</sub>
> 
> - Kirchhoff's second law states that the sum of the three components is equal to the e.m.f of the supply
> 
> ![10-2-5-we2-kirchhoff-step-1-cie-new](assets/31234bc8dd14-10-2-5-we2-kirchhoff-step-1-cie-new.png)
> 
> $2V+V_{1}+12V=24V$
> 
> $V_{1}=24-12-2=10V$
> 
> **Step 2: Calculate the potential difference shown by voltmeter V**<sub>**2**</sub>
> 
> - Applying Kirchhoff's second law to this loop
> - Both lamps have the same resistance *R* and the same current *I *through them so they share the energy equally and have the same potential difference
> 
> ![10-2-5-we2-kirchhoff-step-2-cie-new](assets/468ff31cb66c-10-2-5-we2-kirchhoff-step-2-cie-new.png)
> 
> $24 \mathrm{V} = 2 \mathrm{V} + 8 \mathrm{V} + ( 2 \mathrm{V}_{2} )$
> 
> $2V_{2}=24-2-8=14V$
> 
> $V_{2}=\frac{14}{2}=7V$
> 
> **Step 3: Calculate the potential difference shown by voltmeter V**<sub>**3**</sub>
> 
> - Applying Kirchhoff's second law to this loop
> 
> ![10-2-5-we2-kirchhoff-step-3-cie-new](assets/19018e0551fc-10-2-5-we2-kirchhoff-step-3-cie-new.png)
> 
> $24V=2V+V_{3}$
> 
> $V_{3}=24-2=22V$
