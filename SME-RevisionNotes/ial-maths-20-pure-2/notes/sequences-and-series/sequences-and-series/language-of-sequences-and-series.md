## Language of sequences & series

### What is a sequence?

- A **sequence** is an *ordered* set of numbers with a rule for finding all the numbers in the sequence

![Lang Seq Ser Illustr 1, A Level & AS Level Pure Maths Revision Notes](../../../assets/b5e38daf593b-4-3-1-lang-seq-ser-illustr-1.png)

- The numbers in a sequence are called **terms**
- The terms of a sequence are often referred to by letters with a subscript

![Lang Seq Ser Illustr 2, A Level & AS Level Pure Maths Revision Notes](../../../assets/53a83194430f-4-3-1-lang-seq-ser-illustr-2.png)

### What is a series?

- You get a **series** by summing up the terms in a sequence

![Lang Seq Ser Illustr 3, A Level & AS Level Pure Maths Revision Notes](../../../assets/e2d03dfd52a1-4-3-1-lang-seq-ser-illustr-3.png)

*** ***

- We use the notation ***S***<sub>***n ***</sub>to refer to the sum of the first ***n*** terms in the series i.e.   ***S***<sub>***n***</sub>** =** ***u***<sub>**1**</sub>** +***** u***<sub>**2**</sub>*** *****+ *****u***<sub>**3**</sub>** + … + *****u***<sub>**n**</sub>

![Lang Seq Ser Illustr 4, A Level & AS Level Pure Maths Revision Notes](../../../assets/f2637451ad8e-4-3-1-lang-seq-ser-illustr-4.png)

### What are increasing, decreasing and periodic sequences?

- A sequence is **increasing** if ***u***<sub>***n***</sub><sub>**+1**</sub>** > *****u***<sub>***n***</sub> for all positive integers ***n***

  - i.e. if every term is greater than the term before it
- A sequence is **decreasing** if ***u***<sub>***n***</sub><sub>**+1**</sub>** < *****u***<sub>***n***</sub> for all positive integers ***n***

  - i.e. if every term is less than the term before it

![Lang Seq Ser Illustr 5, A Level & AS Level Pure Maths Revision Notes](../../../assets/af5ea821d48e-4-3-1-lang-seq-ser-illustr-5.png)

- A sequence is **periodic** if the terms repeat in a cycle
- The **order** (or **period**) of a periodic sequence is the number of terms in each repeating cycle

![Lang Seq Ser Illustr 6, A Level & AS Level Pure Maths Revision Notes](../../../assets/ff791110c382-4-3-1-lang-seq-ser-illustr-6.png)

> **Exam Hint**
> Look out for sequences defined by trigonometric functions – this can be a way of 'hiding' a periodic function.
