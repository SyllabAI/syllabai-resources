# Oxidation Number Rules

## Oxidation Numbers 

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_HmMgqHYYJMyF6nVN`

## Oxidation Numbers

- The oxidation number (also known as oxidation state) is a number given to each atom or ion in a compound to keep track of how many electrons they have
- In a single ion or molecular ion, the oxidation number tells us how many electrons have been lost or gained
- Positive oxidation number = loss of electrons
- Negative oxidation number = gain of electrons

### Oxidation number rules

- The oxidation number refers to a *single* atom in a compound

|   | **Rule** | **Example** |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The oxidation number of any uncombined element is zero | H<sub>2</sub>  <br>Zn <br>O<sub>2</sub> |
| **2** | Many atoms or ions have fixed oxidation numbers in compounds | Group 1 elements are always +1 <br>Group 2 elements are always +2 Fluorine is always –1 <br>Hydrogen is +1, except in hydrides like NaH where it is –1 Oxygen is –2, except in peroxides where it is in –1 and in F<sub>2</sub>O where it is +2 |
| **3** | The oxidation number of an element in a monoatomic ion is always the same as the charge | Zn<sup>2+</sup> = +2 <br>Fe<sup>3+</sup> = +3 <br>Cl<sup>–</sup> = –1 |
| **4** | The sum of the oxidation numbers in a compound is zero | NaCl <br>Na = +1 <br>Cl = –1 <br>Sum of oxidation numbers = 1 – 1 = 0 |
| **5** | The sum of the oxidation numbers in an ion is equal to the charge on the ion | SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2– </sup><br>S = +6 <br>Four O atoms = 4 x (–2) = –8 <br>Sum of oxidation numbers = 6 – 8 = –2 |
| **6** | In either a compound or an ion, the more electronegative element is given the negative oxidation number | F<sub>2</sub>O <br>Two F atoms = 2 x (–1) = –2 <br>O = +2 |

> **Worked Example**
> **Deducing oxidation numbers**
> 
> State the oxidation number of the bold atoms in these compounds or ions.
> 
> 1. **P**<sub>2</sub>O<sub>5</sub>
> 2. **S**O<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup>
> 3. H<sub>2</sub>**S**
> 4. **Al**<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>6</sub>
> 5. **N**H<sub>3</sub>
> 6. **Cl**O<sub>2</sub><sup>–</sup>
> 7. Ca**C**O<sub>3</sub>
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> 1. **P**<sub>2</sub>O<sub>5</sub>
> 
>   - 5 O atoms = 5 x (–2) = –10
>   - The overall charge of the compound = 0
>   - 2 P atoms = +10
>   - Oxidation number of 1 P atom = (+10) / 2 = +5
> 2. **S**O<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup>
> 
>   - 4 O atoms = 4 x (–2) = –8
>   - The overall charge of the compound = –2
>   - The oxidation number of 1 S atom = +6
> 3. H<sub>2</sub>**S**
> 
>   - 2 H atoms = 2 x (+1) = +2
>   - The overall charge of the compound = 0
>   - The oxidation number of 1 S atom = –2
> 4. **Al**<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>6</sub>
> 
>   - 6 Cl atoms = 6 x (–1) = –6
>   - The overall charge of the compound = 0
>   - 2 Al atoms = +6
>   - The oxidation number of 1 Al atom = (+6) / 2 = +3
> 5. **N**H<sub>3</sub>
> 
>   - 3 H atoms = 3 x (+1) = +3
>   - The overall charge of the compound = 0
>   - The oxidation number of 1 N atom = –3
> 6. **Cl**O<sub>2</sub><sup>–</sup>
> 
>   - 2 O atoms = 2 x (–2) = –4
>   - The overall charge of the compound = –1
>   - The oxidation number of 1 Cl atom = +3
> 7. Ca**C**O<sub>3</sub>
> 
>   - 3 O atoms = 3 x (–2) = –6
>   - 1 Ca atom = +2
>   - The overall charge of the compound = 0
>   - The oxidation number of 1 C atom = +4
