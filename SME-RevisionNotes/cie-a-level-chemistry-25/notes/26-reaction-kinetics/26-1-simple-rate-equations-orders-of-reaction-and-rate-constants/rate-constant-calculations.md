## Rate Constant Calculations

- The **rate** **constant** (*k*) of a reaction can be calculated using:

  - The **initial** **rates** and the rate equation
  - The **half-life**

### Calculating the rate constant from the initial rate

- The reaction of calcium carbonate (CaCO<sub>3</sub>) with chloride (Cl<sup>–</sup>) ions to form calcium chloride (CaCl<sub>2</sub>) will be used as an example to calculate the rate constant from the **initial rate and initial concentrations**
- The reaction and rate equation are as follows:

**CaCO**<sub>**3**</sub>** (s) + 2Cl**<sup>**–**</sup>** (aq) + 2H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) → CaCl**<sub>**2**</sub>** (aq) + CO**<sub>**2**</sub>** (g) + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l)**

**Rate = *****k***** [CaCO**<sub>**3**</sub>**] [Cl**<sup>**–**</sup>**]**

- The **progress** of the reaction can be followed by measuring the **initial** **rates** of the reaction using various **initial concentrations **of each reactant

#### Experimental results of concentrations & initial rates table

|   | **[CaCO**<sub>**3**</sub>**]** **(mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>**)** | **[Cl**<sup>**–**</sup>**]** **(mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>**)** | **[H**<sup>**+**</sup>**]** **(mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>**)** | **Initial rate of reaction** **(mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>** s**<sup>**-1**</sup>**)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 0.0250 | 0.0125 | 0.0125 | 4.38 x 10<sup>-6</sup> |
| **2** | 0.0375 | 0.0125 | 0.0125 | 6.63 x 10<sup>-6</sup> |
| **3** | 0.00625 | 0.0250 | 0.0250 | 2.19 x 10<sup>-6</sup> |

- To find the rate constant (*k*):

  - Rearrange the rate equation to find *k*:
  - Rate = *k* [CaCO<sub>3</sub>] [Cl<sup>–</sup>] → *k* = $\frac{rate}{[ CaCO_{3} ] [ Cl^{-} ]}$
- Substitute the values of one of the experiments to find *k*:

  - For example, using the measurements from experiment 1
  - *k* = $\frac{4.38 \times 10^{- 6} }{[ 0.0250 ] [ 0.0125 ]}$
  - *k* = 1.40 x 10<sup>-2</sup> dm<sup>3</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup> s<sup>-1</sup>
- The measurements from experiments 2 or 3** **could also have been used to find *k*

  - They would also give the same result of 1.40 x 10<sup>-2</sup> dm<sup>3</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup> s<sup>-1</sup>

### Calculating the rate constant from the half-life

- The rate constant (*k*) can also be calculated from the half-life of a reaction
- You are only expected to deduce *k* from the half-life of a **first-order **reaction as the calculations for **second **and **zero-order **reactions are more complicated
- For a **first-order **reaction, the **half-life **is related to the rate constant by the following expression:

$t_{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{0.693}{k}$

- Rearranging the equation to find *k* gives:

$k=\frac{0.693}{t_{\frac{1}{2}}}$

- So, for a **first-order **reaction such as the **methyl **(CH<sub>3</sub>) **rearrangement** in **ethanenitrile **(CH<sub>3</sub>CN) with a half-life of 10.0 minutes the rate constant is:

  - $k=\frac{0.693}{10.0\times60}$ = 1.16 x 10<sup>-3</sup> dm<sup>3</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup> s<sup>-1</sup>
