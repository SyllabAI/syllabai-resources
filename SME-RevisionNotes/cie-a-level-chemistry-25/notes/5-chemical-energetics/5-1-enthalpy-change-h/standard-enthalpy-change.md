# Standard Enthalpy Change

## Defining Enthalpy Changes 

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_sxWZPvSF3Y2Jk76v`

## Defining Enthalpy Changes

- To fairly compare the changes in enthalpy between reactions, all reactions should be carried out under standard conditions
- These standard conditions are:

  - A **pressure** of 101 kPa
  - A **temperature** of 298 K (25 <sup>o</sup>C)
  - Each substance involved in the reaction is in its normal physical state (solid, gas or liquid)
- To show that a reaction has been carried out under standard conditions, the symbol θ is used

  - Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup> = the standard enthalpy change
- These are a number of key definitions for common language relating to enthalpy change that all chemists need to know

#### Enthalpy definitions

- All standard enthalpies are performed under standard conditions
- **Standard enthalpy change of reaction, **Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*r*</sub>

  - The enthalpy change when the reactants in the **stoichiometric equation** react to form the products, with all species in their standard states
  - Can be **both** exothermic and endothermic
- **Standard enthalpy change of formation, **Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>

  - The enthalpy change when **one mole **of a compound is **formed** from its **elements** in their standard states
  - Can be **both** exothermic and endothermic
- **Standard enthalpy change of combustion, **Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*c*</sub>

  - The enthalpy change when **one mole **of a substance, in its standard state, is **burnt** in excess oxygen
  - It is an** exothermic** change
- **Standard enthalpy change of neutralisation, **Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*neut*</sub>

  - The enthalpy change when **one mole **of **water** is **formed** by reacting an acid and an alkali
  - It is an **exothermic** change

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the enthalpy change of reaction of water**
> 
> One mole of water is formed from hydrogen and oxygen, releasing 286 kJ of energy.
> 
> H<sub>2</sub> (g) + ½O<sub>2</sub> (g) → H<sub>2</sub>O (l)   Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*r*</sub>* *= –286 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> Calculate Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>* *for the reaction below:
> 
> 2H<sub>2</sub> (g) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l)
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - The Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*r*</sub>* *value of –286 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> is for one mole of water being formed
> - Two moles of water molecules are formed for the equation in question
> - So, the energy released is simply:
> 
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>* * = 2 mol x (–286 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>)
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>* * = –572 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the enthalpy change of formation**
> 
> Calculate Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>* *of the reaction below:
> 
> 4Fe (s) + 3O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s)   Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>* *[Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s)]* *= –824.2 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - The Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>* *value of –824.2 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> is for one mole of Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s)being formed
> - Two moles of Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s) are formed for the equation in question
> - So, the energy released is simply:
> 
>   - Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>* * = 2 mol x (–824.2kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>)
>   - Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>* * = –1648.4 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating enthalpy changes**
> 
> Identify each of the following as Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*r*</sub>*, *Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>*, *Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*c*</sub>* *or Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*neut*</sub>:
> 
> 1. MgCO<sub>3</sub> (s) → MgO (s) + CO<sub>2</sub> (g)
> 2. C (graphite) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → CO<sub>2</sub> (g)
> 3. HCl (aq) + NaOH (aq) → NaCl (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)
> 
> **Answers:**
> 
> **Answer 1**
> 
> - It cannot be Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub> as there is more than one compound being formed
> - It cannot be Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*c*</sub> as there is no reaction with oxygen
> - It cannot be Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*neut*</sub> as there is no acid or alkali involved* *
> - This is just a chemical reaction, therefore, it is Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*r*</sub>
> 
> **Answer 2**
> 
> - Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub> as one mole of CO<sub>2</sub> is formed from its elements in their standard states **AND **Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*c*</sub> as one mole of carbon is burnt completely in oxygen
> 
> **Answer 3**
> 
> - Δ*H*<sub>*neut*</sub><sup>Ꝋ</sup> as one mole of water is formed from the reaction of an acid and alkali

> **Exam Hint**
> The Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub> of an **element** in its standard state is zero. For example, Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub> of O<sub>2</sub> (g) is 0 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
