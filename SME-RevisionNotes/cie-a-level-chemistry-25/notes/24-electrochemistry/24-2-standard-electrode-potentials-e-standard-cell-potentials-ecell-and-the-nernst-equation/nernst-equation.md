# Nernst Equation

## The Nernst Equation

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_PmQxN44zb7TySVjZ`

## The Nernst Equation

- Under non-standard conditions, the cell potential of the half-cells is shown by the symbol *E*<sub>*cell*</sub>
- The effect of changes in **temperature** and **ion concentration** on the *E*<sub>*cell*</sub> can be deduced using the **Nernst equation**

$E = E^{ } \Theta + \frac{ R T}{ z F} l n \frac{ \text{stretchy} [ o x i d i s e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}{ \text{stretchy} [ r e d u c e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}$

- ***E*** = electrode potential under nonstandard conditions

  - ***E***<sup>**θ**</sup>** **= standard electrode potential
  - ***R*** = gas constant (8.31 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>)
  - ***T*** = temperature (kelvin, K)
  - **z** = number of electrons transferred in the reaction
  - ***F***** **= Faraday constant (96 500 C mol<sup>-1</sup>)
  - **ln** = natural logarithm

- This equation can be simplified to

$E = E^{ } \Theta + \frac{ 0 . 059}{ z} log_{ } 10 \frac{ \text{stretchy} [ o x i d i s e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}{ \text{stretchy} [ r e d u c e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}$

- At standard temperature, *R, T *and *F* are constant

  - ln x = 2.303 log<sub>10</sub> x
- The Nernst equation only depends on **aqueous ions **and **not solids or gases**
- The concentrations of solids and gases are therefore set to 1.0 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the electrode potential of a Fe**<sup>**3+**</sup>** / Fe**<sup>**2+**</sup>** half-cell**
> 
> Calculate the electrode potential at 298K of a Fe<sup>3+</sup> / Fe<sup>2+</sup> half-cell.
> 
> Fe<sup>3+</sup> (aq) + e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Fe<sup>2+</sup> (aq)
> 
> - [Fe<sup>3+</sup>] = 0.034 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> - [Fe<sup>2+</sup>] = 0.64 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> - *E*<sup>θ</sup> = +0.77 V
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - From the question, the relevant values for the Fe<sup>3+ </sup>/ Fe<sup>2+</sup> half-cell are:
> 
>   - [Fe<sup>3+</sup>] = 0.034 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
>   - [Fe<sup>2+</sup>] = 0.64 mol dm<sup>-3 </sup>
>   - *E*<sup>Θ</sup> = + 0.77 V
> - The oxidised species is Fe<sup>3+</sup> as it has a higher oxidation number (+3)
> - The reduced species is Fe<sup>2+</sup> as it has a lower oxidation number (+2)
> - *z* is 1 as only one electron is transferred in this reaction
> - The Nernst equation for this half-reaction is, therefore:
> 
>   - $E = 0 . 77 + \frac{ 0 . 059}{ 1} log_{ } 10 \frac{ [ 0.034 ] }{ [ 0.64 ] }$
>   - ***E *****= (+0.77) + (-0.075)**
>   - ***E *****= +0.69 V**

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the electrode potential of a Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** / Cu half-cell**
> 
> Calculate the electrode potential at 298K of a Cu<sup>2+</sup> / Cu half-cell.
> 
> Cu<sup>2+</sup> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Cu (s)
> 
> - [Cu<sup>2+</sup>] = 0.001 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> - *E*<sup>θ</sup> = +0.34 V
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - From the question, the relevant values for the Cu<sup>2+</sup> / Cu half-cell are:
> 
>   - [Cu<sup>2+</sup>] = 0.0010 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
>   - *E*<sup>Θ</sup> = + 0.34 V
> - The oxidised species is Cu<sup>2+</sup> as it has a higher oxidation number (+2)
> - The reduced species is Cu as it has a lower oxidation number (0)
> - Cu is solid which means that it is not included in the Nernst equation
> 
>   - Its concentration does not change and is, therefore, fixed at 1.0
> - *z* is 2 as 2 electrons are transferred in this reaction
> - The Nernst equation for this half-reaction is, therefore:
> 
>   - $E = E^{ } \Theta + \frac{ 0 . 059}{ z} log_{ } 10 \frac{ \text{stretchy} [ o x i d i s e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}{ \text{stretchy} [ r e d u c e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}$
>   - $E = 0 . 34 + \frac{ 0 . 059}{ 2} log_{ } 10 \frac{ [ 0.0010 ] }{ [ 1.0 ] }$
>   - ***E ***** = (+ 0.34) + (– 0.089)**
>   - ***E *****= + 0.25 V**

> **Exam Hint**
> - You need to know the Nernst equation, so make sure you learn it
> 
>   - CIE specifically ask students to learn use this version:
> 
> $E = E^{ } \Theta + \frac{ 0 . 059}{ z} log_{ } 10 \frac{ \text{stretchy} [ o x i d i s e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}{ \text{stretchy} [ r e d u c e d s p e c i e s \text{stretchy} ]}$
> 
> - Make sure you always check what the temperature is
> - If the temperature is **not** 298 K (or 25 <sup>o</sup>C) the full Nernst equation should be used
> - You don’t need to know how to **simplify **the Nernst equation
> - You are only expected to use the equation when the temperature is 298 K (or 25 <sup>o</sup>C)
