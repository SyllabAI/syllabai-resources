## Calculating pH, Ka, pKA & Kw

### pH

- The **pH** indicates the **acidity** or **basicity** of an acid or alkali
- The pH scale goes from 0.0 to 14.0

  - Acids have a pH below 7.0
  - Pure water is **neutral **with a pH of 7.0
  - Bases and alkalis have a pH above 7.0
- pH can be calculated using:

**pH = -log**<sub>**10**</sub>** [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**]**

- where [H<sup>+</sup>] = concentration of H<sup>+</sup> ions (mol dm<sup>-3</sup>)
- The pH can also be used to calculate the concentration of H<sup>+</sup> ions in solution by rearranging the equation to:

**[H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] = 10**<sup>**-pH**</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the pH of acids**
> 
> Calculate the pH of ethanoic acid, at 298K, when the hydrogen ion concentration is 1.32 x 10<sup>-3</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> pH = -log [H<sup>+</sup>]
> 
> pH = -log 1.32 x 10<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> pH = 2.9

### K<sub>a</sub> & pK<sub>a</sub>

- The *K*<sub>a</sub> is the **acidic dissociation constant**

  - It is the **equilibrium constant **for the dissociation of a **weak** **acid** at 298 K
- For the **partial** **ionisation** of a weak acid HA, the equilibrium expression to find *K*<sub>a</sub> is:

**HA (aq) ⇌ H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + A**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**

***K***<sub>**a**</sub>*** = ***$\frac{[ H + ] [ A^{-} ]}{[ \text{HA} ]}$

- When writing the equilibrium expression for weak acids, the following assumptions are made:

  - The concentration of hydrogen ions due to the ionisation of water is negligible
  - The dissociation of the weak acid is so small that the concentration of HA can be approximated by its initial value
  
    - This is calculated from the number of moles divided by the volume of solvent
- The value of *K*<sub>a</sub> indicates the extent of dissociation

  - A high value of *K*<sub>a</sub> means that:
  
    - The equilibrium position lies to the right
    - The acid is **almost** **completely** **ionised**
    - The acid is **strongly acidic**
  - A low value of *K*<sub>a</sub> means that:
  
    - The equilibrium position lies to the left
    - The acid is **only slightly ionised** (there are mainly HA and only a few H<sup>+</sup> and A<sup>-</sup> ions)
    - The acid is **weakly acidic**
- Since *K*<sub>a</sub> values of many weak acids are **very low**, p*K*<sub>a</sub>* *values are used instead to compare the strengths of weak acids with each other

**p*****K***<sub>**a**</sub>** = -log**<sub>**10**</sub>** *****K***<sub>**a**</sub>

- The **less** **positive** the p*K*<sub>a</sub> value the **more** **acidic** the acid is

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the *****K***<sub>***a***</sub>*** *****& p*****K***<sub>***a***</sub>** of weak acids**
> 
> Calculate the *K*<sub>a</sub>* *and p*K*<sub>a</sub> values of 0.100 mol dm<sup>-3</sup> ethanoic acid at 298K which forms 1.32 x 10<sup>-3</sup> of H<sup>+</sup> ions in solution.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write down the equation for the partial dissociation of ethanoic acid:
> 
> CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (aq) ⇌ H<sup>+</sup> (aq) + CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> (aq)
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write down the equilibrium expression to find *K*<sub>a</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = $\frac{[ \mathrm{H}^{+} ] [ CH_{3} COO^{-} ]}{[ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 3: **Simplify the expression:
> 
> The ratio of H<sup>+</sup> to CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> is 1:1
> 
> The concentration of H<sup>+</sup> and CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> is, therefore, the same
> 
> The equilibrium expression can be simplified to:
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = $\frac{[ \mathrm{H}^{+} ]^{2}}{[ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 4: **Substitute the values into the expression to find *K*<sub>a</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = $\frac{[ 1.32 \times 10^{- 3} ]^{2}}{[ 0.100 ]}$
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = 1.74 x 10<sup>-5</sup>
> 
> - **Step 5: **Determine the units of *K*<sub>a</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = $\frac{[ \text{mol} dm^{- 3} ]^{2}}{[ \text{mol} dm^{- 3} ]}$= mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>a</sub> is 1.74 x 10<sup>-5</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> - **Step 6: **Find p*K*<sub>a</sub>:
> 
> p*K*<sub>a</sub> = - log<sub>10 </sub>*K*<sub>a</sub>
> 
> p*K*<sub>a</sub><sub>* *</sub>*=* - log<sub>10</sub> (1.74 x 10<sup>-5</sup>)
> 
> p*K*<sub>a</sub> = 4.76

### K<sub>w</sub>

- The *K*<sub>w</sub> is the **ionic product of water**

  - It is the **equilibrium constant **for the dissociation of **water** at 298 K
  - Its value is 1.00 x 10<sup>-14</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>
- For the **ionisation** of water, the equilibrium expression to find *K*<sub>w</sub> is:

**H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l) ⇌ H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + OH**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**

***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = **$\frac{[ H^{+} ] [ OH^{-} ]}{[ H_{2} O ]}$

- As the **extent of ionisation **is very low, only small amounts of H<sup>+</sup> and  OH<sup>- </sup>ions are formed
- The concentration of H<sub>2</sub>O can therefore be regarded as constant and removed from the *K*<sub>*w*</sub> expression
- The equilibrium expression therefore becomes:

***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] [OH**<sup>**-**</sup>**]**

- As the [H<sup>+</sup>] = [OH<sup>+</sup>] in pure water, the equilibrium expression can be further simplified to:

***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**]**<sup>**2**</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the concentration of H**<sup>**+**</sup>** of pure water**
> 
> Calculate the concentration of H<sup>+</sup> in pure water, using the ionic product of water
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write down the equation for the partial dissociation of water:
> 
>   - In pure water, the following equilibrium exists:
> 
> **H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l) ⇌ H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + OH**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write down the equilibrium expression to find *K*<sub>w</sub>:
> 
> ***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = **$\frac{[ H^{+} ] [ OH^{-} ]}{[ H_{2} O ]}$
> 
> - **Step 3: **Simplify the expression:
> 
>   - Since the concentration of H<sub>2</sub>O is constant, this expression can be simplified to:
> 
> ***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] [OH**<sup>**-**</sup>**]**
> 
> - **Step 4: **Further simplify the expression:
> 
>   - The ratio of H<sup>+</sup> to OH<sup>-</sup> is 1:1
>   - The concentration of H<sup>+</sup> and OH<sup>-</sup> is, therefore, the same and the equilibrium expression can be further simplified to:
> 
> ***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**]**<sup>**2**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 5: **Rearrange the equation to find [H<sup>+</sup>]:
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = $\sqrt{K_{w}}$
> 
> - **Step 6: **Substitute the values into the expression to find *K*<sub>w</sub>:
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = $\sqrt{1.00\times10^{-14}}$
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 1.00 x 10<sup>-7</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
