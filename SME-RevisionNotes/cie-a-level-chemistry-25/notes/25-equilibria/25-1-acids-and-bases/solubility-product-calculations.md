# Solubility Product Calculations

## Solubility Product Calculations

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_NjwGX5WsGm4tfWS7`

## Solubility Product Calculations

- Calculations involving the **solubility product **(*K*<sub>*sp*</sub>) may include::

  - Calculating the solubility product of a compound from its **solubility**
  - Calculating the solubility of a compound from the **solubility** **product**

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the solubility product of a compound from its solubility**
> 
> Calculate the solubility product of a saturated solution of lead(II) bromide, PbBr<sub>2</sub>, with a solubility of 1.39 x 10<sup>-3</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write down the equilibrium equation:
> 
> **PbBr**<sub>**2**</sub>** (s) ⇌ Pb**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) + 2Br**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write down the equilibrium expression:
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = [Pb**<sup>**2+**</sup>**(aq)] [Br**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)]**<sup>**2**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 3: **Calculate the ion concentrations in the solution:
> 
>   - **[PbBr**<sub>**2**</sub>**(s)] = 1.39 x 10**<sup>**-3**</sup>** mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>
>   - The ratio of PbBr<sub>2</sub> to Pb<sup>2+</sup> is 1:1
> 
> **[Pb**<sup>**2+**</sup>**(aq)] = [PbBr**<sub>**2**</sub>**(s)] = 1.39 x 10**<sup>**-3**</sup>** mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>
> 
> - The ratio of PbBr<sub>2</sub> to Br<sup>-</sup> is 1:2
> 
> **[Br**<sup>**-**</sup>**(aq)] = 2 x [PbBr**<sub>**2**</sub>**(s)] = 2 x 1.39 x 10**<sup>**-3**</sup>** mol dm**<sup>**-3 **</sup>**= 2.78 x 10**<sup>**-3**</sup>** mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 4: **Substitute the values into the expression to find the solubility product:
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = (1.39 x 10**<sup>**-3**</sup>**) x (2.78 x 10**<sup>**-3**</sup>**)**<sup>**2**</sup>
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>*** *****= 1.07 x 10**<sup>**-8**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 6:  **Determine the correct units of *K*<sub>*sp*</sub>:
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = (mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>**) x (mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>**)**<sup>**2**</sup>
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = mol**<sup>**3**</sup>** dm**<sup>**-9**</sup>
> 
> - Therefore, the solubility product is 1.07 x 10<sup>-8</sup> mol<sup>3</sup> dm<sup>-9</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculating the solubility of a compound from its solubility product**
> 
> Calculate the solubility of a saturated solution of copper(II) oxide, CuO, with a solubility product of 5.9 x 10<sup>-36</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write down the equilibrium equation:
> 
> **CuO (s) ⇌ Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) + O**<sup>**2-**</sup>** (aq)**
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write down the equilibrium expression:
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = [Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)] [O**<sup>**2-**</sup>** (aq)]**
> 
> - **Step 3: **Simplify the equilibrium expression:
> 
>   - The ratio of Cu<sup>2+</sup> to O<sup>2-</sup> is 1:1
>   - [Cu<sup>2+</sup>(aq)] = [O<sup>2-</sup>(aq)] so the expression can be simplified to:
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = [Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)]**<sup>**2**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 4: **Substitute the value of *K*<sub>*sp*</sub> into the expression to find the concentration:
> 
> **5.9 x 10**<sup>**-36**</sup>** = [Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)]**<sup>**2**</sup>
> 
> **[Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)] = **$\sqrt{5.9\times10^{-36}}$
> 
> **[Cu**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)] = 2.4 x 10**<sup>**-18**</sup>** mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>
> 
> - Since [CuO (s)] = [Cu<sup>2+</sup> (aq)], the solubility of copper oxide is 2.4 x 10<sup>-18</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

> **Exam Hint**
> Remember that the solubility product is only applicable to very slightly soluble salts and cannot be used for soluble salts such as:
> 
> - Group 1 element salts
> - All nitrate salts
> - All ammonium salts
> - Many sulfate salts
> - Many halide salts (except for lead(II) halides and silver halides)
