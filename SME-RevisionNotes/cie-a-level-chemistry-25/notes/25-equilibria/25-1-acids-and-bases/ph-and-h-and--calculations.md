# pH & [H+] Calculations

## [H+] & pH Calculations

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_rcYTTx2Xn8VynVS7`

## [H+] & pH Calculations

- If the concentration of H<sup>+</sup> of an acid or alkali is known, the **pH** can be calculated using the equation:

**pH = -log [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**]**

- Similarly, the **concentration** **of** **H**<sup>**+**</sup>** **of a solution can be calculated if the pH is known by rearranging the above equation to:

**[H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] = 10**<sup>**-pH**</sup>

### Strong acids

- **Strong acids** are completely **ionised **in solution

**HA (aq) → H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + A**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**

- Therefore, the concentration of hydrogen ions ([H<sup>+</sup>]) is **equal **to the concentration of acid ([HA])
- The number of hydrogen ions ([H<sup>+</sup>]) formed from the ionisation of water is **very small **relative to the [H<sup>+</sup>] due to the ionisation of the strong acid and can therefore be **neglected**
- The **total **[H<sup>+</sup>] is therefore the same as the [HA]

> **Worked Example**
> **pH calculations of a strong acid**
> 
> For a solution of hydrochloric acid, calculate the following:
> 
> 1. The pH when the hydrogen ion concentration is 1.6 x 10<sup>-4</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>.
> 2. The hydrogen ion concentration when the pH is 3.1.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - Hydrochloric acid is a strong monobasic acid
> 
> **HCl (aq) → H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + Cl**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**
> 
> **Answer 1**
> 
> - The pH of the solution is:
> 
> pH = -log [H<sup>+</sup>]
> 
> pH = -log 1.6 x 10<sup>-4</sup>
> 
> pH = 3.80
> 
> **Answer 2**
> 
> - The hydrogen concentration can be calculated by rearranging the equation for pH:
> 
> pH = -log [H<sup>+</sup>]
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 10<sup>-pH</sup>
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 10<sup>-3.1</sup>
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 7.9 x 10<sup>-4</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

### Strong alkalis

- **Strong alkalis **are completely **ionised **in solution

**BOH (aq) → B**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + OH**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**

- Therefore, the concentration of hydroxide ions ([OH<sup>-</sup>]) is **equal **to the concentration of base ([BOH])

  - Even strong alkalis have small amounts of H<sup>+</sup> in solution which is due to the ionisation of water
- The concentration of OH<sup>-</sup> in solution can be used to calculate the pH using the **ionic product of water**

***K***<sub>**w**</sub>** = [H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] [OH**<sup>**-**</sup>**]**

**[H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] = **$\frac{ K_{ } w}{ \text{stretchy} [ OH^{ - } \text{stretchy} ]}$

- Since *K*<sub>w</sub> is 1.00 x 10<sup>-14</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>

**[H**<sup>**+**</sup>**] = **$\frac{1 . 00 \times 10^{- 14} }{[ OH^{-} ]}$

- Once the [H<sup>+</sup>] has been determined, the pH of the strong alkali can be found using pH = -log[H<sup>+</sup>]
- Similarly, the ionic product of water can be used to find the concentration of OH<sup>-</sup> ions in solution if [H<sup>+</sup>] is known

**[OH**<sub>**–**</sub>**] = **$\frac{1 . 00 \times 10^{- 14} }{[ H^{+} ]}$

> **Worked Example**
> **pH calculations of a strong alkali**
> 
> For a solution of sodium hydroxide, calculate the following:
> 
> 1. The pH when the hydrogen ion concentration is 3.5 x 10<sup>-11</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>.
> 2. The hydroxide ion concentration when the pH is 12.3.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - Sodium hydroxide is a strong base which ionises as follows:
> 
> **NaOH (aq) → Na**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + OH**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq) **
> 
> **Answer 1**
> 
> - The pH of the solution is:
> 
> pH = -log [H<sup>+</sup>]
> 
> pH = -log 3.5 x 10<sup>-11</sup>
> 
> pH = 10.5
> 
> **Answer 2**
> 
> - **Step 1:** Calculate the hydrogen concentration by rearranging the equation for pH:
> 
> pH = -log [H<sup>+</sup>]
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 10<sup>-pH</sup>
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 10<sup>-12.3</sup>
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 5.01 x 10<sup>-13</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> - **Step 2: **Rearrange the **ionic product of water**  to find the concentration of hydroxide ions:
> 
> *K*<sub>w</sub> = [H<sup>+</sup>] [OH<sup>-</sup>]
> 
> [OH<sup>–</sup>] = $\frac{K_{w} }{[ \mathrm{H}^{+} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 3: **Substitute the values into the expression to find the concentration of hydroxide ions:
> 
>   - Since *K*<sub>*w*</sub> is 1.00 x 10<sup>-14</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>
> - [OH<sup>–</sup>] = $\frac{1.00\times10^{-14}}{5.01\times10^{-13}}$
> - [OH<sup>–</sup>] = 0.0199<sup> </sup>mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

### Weak acids

- The pH of **weak acids** can be calculated when the following is known:

  - The **concentration** of the acid
  - The ***K***<sub>***a***</sub> value of the acid

> **Worked Example**
> **pH calculations of weak acids**
> 
> Calculate the pH of 0.100 mol dm<sup>-3</sup> ethanoic acid at 298K with a *K*<sub>a</sub> value of 1.74 x 10<sup>-5</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - Ethanoic acid is a weak acid which ionises as follows:
> 
> **CH**<sub>**3**</sub>**COOH (aq) ⇌ H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + CH**<sub>**3**</sub>**COO**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write down the equilibrium expression to find *K*<sub>a</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = $\frac{[ \mathrm{H} + ] [ CH_{3} COO^{-} ]}{[ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 2: **Simplify the expression:
> 
>   - The ratio of H<sup>+</sup> to CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> ions is 1:1
>   - Therefore the concentration of H<sup>+</sup> and CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> ions are the same
>   - So, the expression can be simplified to:
> 
> *K*<sub>a</sub> = $\frac{[ \mathrm{H} + ]^{2}}{[ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 3: **Rearrange the expression to find [H<sup>+</sup>]:
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = $\sqrt{K_{a} \times [ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 4: **Substitute the values into the expression to find [H<sup>+</sup>]:
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = $\sqrt{( 1.74 \times 10^{- 5} ) \times 0.100}$
> 
> [H<sup>+</sup>] = 1.32 x 10<sup>-3</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> - **Step 5: **Find the pH:
> 
> pH = -log<sub>10</sub> [H<sup>+</sup>]
> 
> pH = -log<sub>10</sub> 1.32 x 10<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> pH = 2.88
