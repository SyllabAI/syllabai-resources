# The Common Ion Effect

## The Solubility Product & the Common Ion Effect

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_mjhMDJVnvhydvMWZ`

## The Solubility Product & the Common Ion Effect

- A **saturated solution **is a solution that contains the **maximum **amount of dissolved salt
- If a second compound, which has an ion **in common **with the dissolved salt, is added to the saturated solution, the solubility of the salt reduces, and a solid **precipitate **will be formed
- This is also known as the **common ion effect**
- For example, if a solution of **potassium chloride **(KCl) is added to a **saturated solution of silver chloride** (AgCl) a precipitate of silver chloride will be formed

  - The chloride ion is the common ion
- The **solubility product** can be used to predict whether a precipitate will form or not

  - A precipitate will form if the product of the ion concentrations is **greater than** the solubility product (*K*<sub>*sp*</sub>)

### Common ion effect in silver chloride

- When a **KCl** solution is added to a saturated solution of **AgCl**, an AgCl precipitate forms
- In a **saturated** AgCl solution, the silver chloride is in equilibrium with its ions

**AgCl (s) ⇌ Ag**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + Cl**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq)**

- When a solution of potassium chloride is added:

  - Both KCl and AgCl have the common Cl<sup>-</sup> ion
  - There is an increased Cl<sup>-</sup> concentration so the equilibrium position shifts to the left
  - The increase in Cl<sup>-</sup> concentration also means that [Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)] [Cl<sup>-</sup>(aq)] is **greater** than the *K*<sub>*sp*</sub> for AgCl
  - As a result, the AgCl is **precipitated**

> **Worked Example**
> **Calculations using the *****K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** values and the concentration of the common ion**
> 
> Predict whether a precipitate of CaSO<sub>4</sub> will form if a saturated solution of 1.0 x 10<sup>-3</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup> CaSO<sub>4</sub> is mixed with an equal volume of 1.0 x 10<sup>-3</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup> Na<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>.
> 
> *K*<sub>*sp*</sub>* *CaSO<sub>4</sub> = 2.0 x 10<sup>-5</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Determine the equilibrium reaction of CaSO<sub>4</sub>:
> 
> **CaSO**<sub>**4**</sub>** (s) ⇌ Ca**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) + SO**<sub>**4**</sub><sup>**2-**</sup>** (aq)**
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write down the equilibrium expression for *K*<sub>*sp*</sub>:
> 
> ***K***<sub>***sp***</sub>** = [Ca**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)] [SO**<sub>**4**</sub><sup>**2-**</sup>** (aq)]**
> 
> - **Step 3: **Determine the concentrations of the ions:
> 
>   - There are equal volumes of each solution
>   - This means that the total solution was diluted by a factor of 2
>   - The new concentration of the Ca<sup>2+</sup> ion is **halved**:
>   
>     - [Ca<sup>2+</sup>] =  $\frac{1.0\times10^{-3}}{2}$
>     - [Ca<sup>2+</sup>] = 5.0 x 10<sup>-4</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
>   - The sulfate ion concentration remains the same as it is a common ion and its concentration is the same in both solutions
> 
> - **Step 4: **Substitute the values into the expression:
> 
>   - Product of the ion concentrations = [Ca<sup>2+</sup> (aq)] x [SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> (aq)]
>   - Product of the ion concentrations = (5.0 x 10<sup>-4</sup>) x (1.0 x 10<sup>-3</sup>)
>   - Product of the ion concentrations = 5.0 x 10<sup>-7</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>
> 
> - **Step 5: **Determine if a precipitate will form:
> 
>   - As the product of the ion concentration (5.0 x 10<sup>-7</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>) is smaller than the *K*<sub>*sp*</sub> value (2.0 x 10<sup>-5</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>), the CaSO<sub>4</sub> precipitate will **not** be formed
