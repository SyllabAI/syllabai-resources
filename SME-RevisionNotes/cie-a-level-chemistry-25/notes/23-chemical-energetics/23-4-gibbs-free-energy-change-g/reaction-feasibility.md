## Reaction Feasibility

- The Gibbs equation can be used to calculate whether a reaction is **feasible **or not

**Δ*****G***<sup>**θ**</sup>** = Δ*****H***<sub>***reaction***</sub><sup>**θ**</sup>** - TΔ*****S***<sub>***system***</sub><sup>**θ**</sup>

- When Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup> is **negative**, the reaction is **feasible **and likely to occur
- When Δ*G*<sup>θ </sup>is **positive**, the reaction is **not feasible** and unlikely to occur

> **Worked Example**
> Calculate the Gibbs free energy for the following reaction at 298 K and determine whether the reaction is feasible.
> 
> 2Ca (s) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2CaO (s)   Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup> = -635.5 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> - *S*<sup>θ</sup>[Ca (s)] = 41.00 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>
> - *S*<sup>θ</sup>[O<sub>2</sub> (s)] = 205.0 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>
> - *S*<sup>θ</sup>[CaO (s)] = 40.00 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Calculate Δ*S*<sub>system</sub><sup>θ</sup>
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ </sup>= ΣΔ*S*<sub>*products*</sub><sup>θ</sup> - ΣΔ*S*<sub>*reactants*</sub><sup>θ</sup>
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ </sup>= (2 x Δ*S*<sup>θ</sup> [CaO(s)]) -  (2 x Δ*S*<sup>θ</sup> [Ca(s)] + Δ*S*<sup>*θ*</sup><sup> </sup>[O<sub>2</sub>(g)])
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ </sup>= (2 x 40.00) - (2 x 41.00 + 205.0)
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ </sup>= -207.0 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> - **Step 2: **Convert Δ*S*<sup>θ </sup>to kJ K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> Δ*S*<sup>θ </sup>= $\frac{-207.0}{1000}$ = -0.207 kJ K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> - **Step 3: **Calculate Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup>
> 
> Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup> = Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup> - TΔ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ</sup>
> 
> Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup> = -635.5 - (298 x -0.207)
> 
> Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup> = -573.8 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> - **Step 4: **Determine whether the reaction is feasible
> 
>   - Since the Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup>*** ***is **negative **the reaction is feasible and likely to occur

## Reaction Feasibility & Temperature Changes

- The **feasibility **of a reaction can be affected by the **temperature**
- The Gibbs equation will be used to explain what will affect the feasibility of a reaction for exothermic and endothermic reactions

![Entropy - Gibbs Equation, downloadable AS & A Level Chemistry revision notes](../../../assets/79edb59f3b46-5-2-entropy-gibbs-equation.png)

### Exothermic reactions

- In exothermic reactions, Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup>** **is **negative**
- If the Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ</sup> is **positive:**

  - Both the first and second term will be **negative**
  - Resulting in a **negative** Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup> so the reaction is **feasible**
  - Therefore, regardless of the temperature, an exothermic reaction with a positive Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ</sup> will **always be feasible**
- If the Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ</sup> is **negative:**

  - The first term is **negative **and the second term is **positive**
  - At high temperatures, the -*T*Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ </sup>will be very **large** and **positive** and will overcome Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup>
  - Therefore, at **high temperatures** Δ*G*<sup>θ </sup>is **positive** and the reaction is not feasible
  - The reaction is more **feasible** at low temperatures, as the second term will not be large enough to overcome Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup> resulting in a negative Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup>
- This corresponds to Le Chatelier’s principle which states that for **exothermic reactions **an increase in temperature will cause the equilibrium to shift position in favour of the reactants, i.e. in the endothermic direction

  - In other words, for exothermic reactions, the products will **not be formed **at high temperatures
  - The reaction is **not feasible **at high temperatures

### Endothermic reactions

- In endothermic reactions, Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup> is **positive**
- If the Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ</sup> is **negative:**

  - Both the first and second term will be **positive**
  - Resulting in a **positive** Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup> so the reaction is **not feasible**
  - Therefore, regardless of the temperature, endothermic with a negative Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ</sup> will **never be feasible**
- If the Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>ꝋ</sup> is **positive:**

  - The first term is **positive **and the second term is **negative**
  - At low temperatures, the -*T*Δ*S*<sub>*system*</sub><sup>θ </sup>will be **small** and **negative** and will not overcome the larger Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup>
  - Therefore, at low temperatures Δ*G*<sup>θ </sup>is **positive** and the reaction is less feasible
  - The reaction is **more** **feasible** at **high temperatures** as the second term will become negative enough to overcome the Δ*H*<sub>*reaction*</sub><sup>θ</sup> resulting in a negative Δ*G*<sup>θ</sup>
- This again corresponds to Le Chatelier’s principle which states that for **endothermic reactions **an increase in temperature will cause the equilibrium to shift position in favour of the products

  - In other words, for endothermic reactions, the products will **be formed **at high temperatures
  - The reaction is therefore **feasible**
