## Define & Write a Stability Constant for a Complex

- When transition element ions are in aqueous solutions, they will automatically become hydrated

  - Water molecules will surround the ion and act as **ligands** by forming dative covalent bonds to the central metal ion
- When there are other potential ligands present in the solution, there is a **competing equilibrium **in **ligand exchange** and the most stable complex will be formed
- For example, a Co(II) ion in solution will form a [Co(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>6</sub>]<sup>2+</sup> complex
- Adding ammonia results in the stepwise substitution of the water ligands by ammonia ligands until a stable complex of [Co(NH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>4</sub>(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2+</sup> is formed

**[Cu(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**6**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + 4NH**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⇌ [Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**4**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**2**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + 4H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**

- For the substitution reaction above, there are **four **stepwise constants:

**[Cu(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**6**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + NH**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⇌ [Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**5**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O              *****K***<sub>***1***</sub>

**[Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**5**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + NH**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⇌ [Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**2**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**4**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O             *****K***<sub>***2***</sub>

**[Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**2**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**4**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + NH**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⇌ [Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**3**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**3**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O             *****K***<sub>***3***</sub>

**[Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**3**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**3**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + NH**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⇌ [Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**4**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**2**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O             *****K***<sub>***4***</sub>

- These stepwise constants are summarised in the **overall stability constant**, *K*<sub>*stab*</sub>
- The **stability constant** is the **equilibrium constant** for the formation of the complex ion in a solvent from its constituent ions or molecules

### Expression of K<sub>stab</sub>

- The expression for *K*<sub>*stab*</sub> can be deduced in a similar way as the expression for the equilibrium constant (*K*<sub>*c*</sub>)
- For example, the equilibrium expression for the substitution of water ligands by ammonia ligands in the Co(II) complex is:

**[Cu(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**6**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + 4NH**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⇌ [Cu(NH**<sub>**3**</sub>**)**<sub>**4**</sub>**(H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O)**<sub>**2**</sub>**]**<sup>**2+**</sup>** + 4H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O **

- The concentration of **water** is **not** included in the expression as the water is in **excess**
- Therefore, any water **produced** in the reaction is **negligible** compared to the water that is already present
- The units of the *K*<sub>*stab*</sub> can be deduced from the expression in a similar way to the units of *K*<sub>*c*</sub>
- The stability constants can be used to **compare **the **stability **of ligands relative to the **aqueous metal ion** where the ligand is water
- The **larger **the *K*<sub>*stab*</sub> value, the **more stable **the complex formed is

## Calculations Involving Stability Constants

- If the concentrations of the transition element complex and the reacting ligands are known, the expression for the stability constant (*K*<sub>*stab*</sub>) can be used to determine which complex is **more** **stable**
- The **greater** the value of *K*<sub>*stab*</sub> the **more stable **the complex is
