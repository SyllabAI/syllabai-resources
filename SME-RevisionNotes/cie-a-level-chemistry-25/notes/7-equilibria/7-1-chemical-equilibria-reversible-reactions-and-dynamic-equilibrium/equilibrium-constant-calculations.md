## Equilibrium Constant: Calculations

### Calculations involving K<sub>c</sub>

- In the equilibrium expression, each figure within a square bracket represents the concentration in **mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>
- The **units **of *K*<sub>*c*</sub> therefore depend on the form of the equilibrium expression
- Some questions give the **number of moles **of each of the reactants and products at equilibrium together with the volume of the reaction mixture
- The concentrations of the reactants and products can then be calculated from the number of moles and total volume using:

$\mathrm{concentration}(\mathrm{mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3})=\frac{\mathrm{number} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{moles}}{\mathrm{volume}(\mathrm{dm}^{3})}$

> **Worked Example**
> At equilibrium, 500 cm<sup>3</sup> of the following reaction mixture contains 0.235 mol of ethanoic acid, 0.0350 mol of ethanol, 0.182 mol of ethyl ethanoate and 0.182 mol of water.
> 
> CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (l) + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH (l) $\rightleftharpoons$ CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> (l) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)
> 
> Use this information to calculate a value of *K*<sub>c</sub> for this reaction.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Calculate the concentrations of the reactants and products:
> 
> [CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (l)] = $\frac{0.235}{0.500\mathrm{dm}^{3}}$ = 0.470 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> [C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH (l)] = $\frac{0.350}{0.500\mathrm{dm}^{3}}$ = 0.070 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> [CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> (l)] = $\frac{0.182}{0.500\mathrm{dm}^{3}}$ = 0.364 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> [H<sub>2</sub>O (l)] = $\frac{0.182}{0.500\mathrm{dm}^{3}}$ = 0.364 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write out the balanced chemical equation with the calculated concentrations beneath each substance:
> 
> CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (l) + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH (l) ⇌ CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> (l) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)
> 
> 0.470 mol dm<sup>-3 </sup>0.070 mol dm<sup>-3 </sup>0.364 mol dm<sup>-3 </sup>0.364 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> - **Step 3: **Write the equilibrium constant for this reaction in terms of concentration:
> 
> *K*<sub>c</sub> = $\frac{[ \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O} ] [ CH_{3} COOC_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} ]}{[ \mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} \text{OH} ] [ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 4: **Substitute the equilibrium concentrations into the expression:
> 
> *K*<sub>c</sub> = $\frac{0.364\times0.364}{0.070\times0.470}$
> 
> *K*<sub>c</sub> = 4.03
> 
> - **Step 5: **Deduce the correct units for *K*<sub>c</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>c</sub> = $\frac{( \text{mol} dm^{- 3} ) ( \text{mol} dm^{- 3} )}{( \text{mol} dm^{- 3} ) ( \text{mol} dm^{- 3} )}$
> 
> All units cancel out
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>*c*</sub>* *= 4.03
> 
> - Note that the smallest number of significant figures used in the question is 3, so the final answer should also be given to 3 significant figures

- Some questions give the **initial and equilibrium concentrations **of the reactants but not products
- An initial, change and equilibrium table should be used to determine the equilibrium concentration of the products **using the molar ratio of reactants and products in the stoichiometric equation**

> **Worked Example**
> Ethyl ethanoate is hydrolysed by water:
> 
> **CH**<sub>**3**</sub>**COOC**<sub>**2**</sub>**H**<sub>**5**</sub>** (I) + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (I) ⇌ CH**<sub>**3**</sub>**COOH (I) + C**<sub>**2**</sub>**H**<sub>**5**</sub>**OH (I)**
> 
> 0.1000 mol of ethyl ethanoate is added to 0.1000 mol of water. A little acid catalyst is added and the mixture is made up to 1 dm<sup>3</sup>. At equilibrium 0.0654 mol of water are present. Use this data to calculate a value of *K*<sub>*c*</sub> for this reaction.
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> - **Step 1:** Complete the ICE table for the reaction:
> 
>   - Write out the balanced chemical equation with the number of moles of each substance given in the question beneath using an initial, change and equilibrium table:
> 
> - Calculate the change in moles of water and add to the table (an increase is shown by + and a decrease is shown by -)
> 
>   - Equilibrium amount = Initial amount + Change in amount
>   - 0.0654 = 0.100 + Change in amount
>   - Change in amount = 0.0654 - 0.100 = *–0.0346*
> 
> - Use the stoichiometry of the equation to calculate the change in amounts of the remaining reactants/products and add to the table
> 
>   - There is a 1 : 1 reacting ratio between H<sub>2</sub>O and all other reactants/products
>   - As H<sub>2</sub>O has **decreased **by **0.0346 mol**, the other reactant CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub><sub>** **</sub>will **decrease **by **0.0346 mol**
>   - Since CH<sub>3</sub>COOH and C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH are products, they will both **increase** by **0.0346 mol**
> 
> - Calculate the number of moles at equilibrium of the remaining reactants / products to complete the table
> 
>   - Equilibrium amount = Initial amount + Change in amount
>   - Equilibrium amount of CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>  = 0.100 + (-0.0346) = ***0.0654 mol***
>   - Equilibrium amount of CH<sub>3</sub>COOH  = 0.000 + 0.0346 = ***0.0346 mol***
>   - Equilibrium amount of C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH = 0.000 + 0.0346 = ***0.0346 mol***
> 
> | ** ** | **CH**<sub>**3**</sub>**COOC**<sub>**2**</sub>**H**<sub>**5**</sub>** (I)   +** | **H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (I)          ⇌** | **CH**<sub>**3**</sub>**COOH (I)     +** | **C**<sub>**2**</sub>**H**<sub>**5**</sub>**OH (I)** |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Initial moles** | 0.100 | 0.100 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
> | **Change** | **-0.0346** | > *-0.0346* | **+0.0346** | **+0.0346** |
> | **Equilibrium moles** | ***0.0654*** | 0.0654 | ***0.0346*** | ***0.0346*** |
> 
> - **Step 2:** Calculate the concentrations of the reactants and products:
> 
> [CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (l)] = $\frac{0.0346}{1.0}$ = 0.0346 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> [C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH (l)] = $\frac{0.0346}{1.0}$ = 0.0346 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> [CH<sub>3</sub>COOC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub> (l)] = $\frac{0.0654}{1.0}$ = 0.0654 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> [H<sub>2</sub>O (l)] = $\frac{0.0654}{1.0}$ = 0.0654 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> - **Step 3**: Write the equilibrium constant for this reaction in terms of concentration:
> 
> *K*<sub>*c*</sub>* *= $\frac{[ \mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} \text{OH} ] [ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}{[ \mathrm{H}_{2} O ] [ CH_{3} COOC_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} ]}$
> 
> - **Step 4:** Substitute the equilibrium concentrations into the expression:
> 
> *K*<sub>*c*</sub>* *= $\frac{0.0346\times0.0346}{0.0654\times0.0654}$ = **0.280**
> 
> - **Step 5: **Deduce the correct units for *K*<sub>c</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>*c*</sub>* *= $\frac{[ \mathrm{C}_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} \text{OH} ] [ CH_{3} \text{COOH} ]}{[ \mathrm{H}_{2} O ] [ CH_{3} COOC_{2} \mathrm{H}_{5} ]}$
> 
> All units cancel out
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>*c*</sub>* *= 0.280 **(no units)**

### Calculations involving K<sub>p</sub>

- In the equilibrium expression the *p* represent the partial pressure of the reactants and products in **Pa**
- The **units** of *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *therefore depend on the form of the equilibrium expression

> **Worked Example**
> The equilibrium between sulfur dioxide, oxygen and sulfur trioxide is as follows:
> 
> 2SO<sub>2</sub> (g) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) $\rightleftharpoons$ 2SO<sub>3</sub> (g)
> 
> At constant temperature, the equilibrium partial pressures are:
> 
> - SO<sub>2</sub> = 1.0 x 10<sup>6</sup> Pa
> - O<sub>2</sub> = 7.0 x 10<sup>6</sup> Pa
> - SO<sub>3</sub> = 8.0 x 10<sup>6</sup> Pa
> 
> Calculate the value of *K*<sub>p</sub> for this reaction.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write the equilibrium constant for the reaction in terms of partial pressures:
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = $\frac{p^{2}\mathrm{SO}_{3}}{p^{2}\mathrm{SO}_{2}\timespO_{2}}$
> 
> - **Step 2: **Substitute the equilibrium concentrations into the expression:
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = $\frac{( 8.0 \times 10^{6} )^{2}}{( 1.0 \times 10^{6} )^{2} \times ( 7.0 \times 10^{6} )}$
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = 9.1 x 10<sup>–6</sup>
> 
> - **Step 3: **Deduce the correct units of *K*<sub>p</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = $\frac{\mathrm{Pa}^{2}}{\mathrm{Pa}^{2}\times\mathrm{Pa}}$
> 
> So, the units of *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *are Pa<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *= 9.1 x 10<sup>-6</sup> Pa<sup>-1</sup>

- Some questions only give the **number of moles** of gases present and the total pressure
- The number of moles of each gas should be used to first calculate the **mole fractions**
- The mole fractions are then used to calculate the **partial pressures**
- The values of the partial pressures are then substituted in the **equilibrium expression**

> **Worked Example**
> The equilibrium between hydrogen, iodine and hydrogen iodide is as follows:
> 
> H<sub>2</sub> (g) + I<sub>2</sub> (g) $\rightleftharpoons$ 2HI (g)
> 
> At constant temperature, the equilibrium moles are:
> 
> - H<sub>2</sub> = 1.71 x 10<sup>–3</sup>
> - I<sub>2</sub> = 2.91 x 10<sup>–3</sup>
> - HI = 1.65 x 10<sup>–2</sup>
> 
> The total pressure is 100 kPa.
> 
> Calculate the value of *K*<sub>p</sub> for this reaction.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Calculate the total number of moles:
> 
> Total number of moles = 1.71 x 10<sup>-3 </sup>+ 2.91 x 10<sup>-3 </sup>+ 1.65 x 10<sup>-2</sup>
> 
> Total number of moles = 2.112 x 10<sup>-2</sup>
> 
> 1. **Step 2: **Calculate the mole fraction of each gas:
> 
> H<sub>2</sub> = $\frac{1.71\times10^{-3}}{2.112\times10^{-2}}$ = 0.0810
> 
> I<sub>2</sub> = $\frac{2.91\times10^{-3}}{2.112\times10^{-2}}$ = 0.1378
> 
> HI = $\frac{1.65\times10^{-2}}{2.112\times10^{-2}}$ = 0.7813
> 
> - **Step 3: **Calculate the partial pressure of each gas:
> 
> H<sub>2</sub> = 0.0810 x 100 = 8.10 kPa
> 
> I<sub>2</sub> = 0.1378 x 100 = 13.78 kPa
> 
> HI = 0.7813 x 100 = 78.13 kPa
> 
> - **Step 4: **Write the equilibrium constant in terms of partial pressure:
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = $\frac{p^{2}\mathrm{HI}}{pH_{2}\timespI_{2}}$
> 
> - **Step 5: **Substitute the values into the equilibrium expression:
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = $\frac{78.13^{2}}{8.10\times13.78}$
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = 54.7
> 
> - **Step 6: **Deduce the correct units for *K*<sub>p</sub>:
> 
> *K*<sub>p</sub> = $\frac{\mathrm{Pa}^{2}}{\mathrm{Pa}\times\mathrm{Pa}}$
> 
> All units cancel out
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *= 54.7

- Other questions related to equilibrium expressions may involve calculating quantities present at equilibrium given appropriate data

> **Worked Example**
> An equilibrium is set up in a closed container between equal volumes of gaseous reactants **A** and **B** to form a gaseous product **C**.
> 
> A (g) + B (g) $\rightleftharpoons$ 2C (g)
> 
> The total pressure within the container, at 50 <sup>o</sup>C, is 3 atm.
> 
> The equilibrium partial pressure of **A**, at 50 <sup>o</sup>C, is 0.5 atm.
> 
> What is the equilibrium partial pressure of **C** at this temperature?
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - There are equal volumes of reactants **A** and **B** in a 1 : 1 molar ratio
> 
>   - This means their partial pressures will be the same.
>   - **B** therefore also has an equilibrium partial pressure of 0.5 atm
> - **Total pressure = Σ (equilibrium partial pressures)**
> 
>   - Therefore, the sum of all the partial pressures must equal to 3 atm
>   - 0.5 + 0.5 + *p*<sub>*c*</sub> = 3 atm
>   - *p*<sub>*c*</sub> = 2 atm
