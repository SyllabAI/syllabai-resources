# Hess’s Law

## Hess Cycles

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_mqCCKGsGRn8qBZ3X`

## Hess Cycles

- **Hess’s Law **states that:

***"The total enthalpy change in a chemical reaction is independent of the route by which the chemical reaction takes place as long as the initial and final conditions are the same."***

- This means that whether the reaction takes place in one or two steps, the total enthalpy change of the reaction will still be the same

#### Illustration of Hess's Law

![Flowchart with three routes: direct from A+B to C, indirect route 1 via F+G, indirect route 2 via X and Y+Z. Arrows indicate direction.](../../../assets/c8529df9c3c1-1-5-chemical-energetics-hess-cycles.png)

*According to Hess’ Law, the enthalpy change of the direct route, going from reactants (A+B) to product (C), is equal to the enthalpy change of the indirect routes*

- Hess’ Law is used to calculate enthalpy changes which can’t be found experimentally using **calorimetry**, e.g.:

**3C (s) + 4H**<sub>**2 **</sub>**(g) → C**<sub>**3**</sub>**H**<sub>**8**</sub>**(g)**

- ΔH<sub>f</sub> (propane) can’t be found experimentally as hydrogen and carbon don’t react under standard conditions

### Calculating ΔH<sub>r</sub> from ΔH<sub>f</sub> using Hess’s Law energy cycles

- The products can be directly formed from the elements = **Δ*****H***<sub>**2**</sub> **OR **The products can be indirectly formed from the elements = **Δ*****H***<sub>**1**</sub>** + Δ*****H***<sub>**r**</sub>

#### Applying Hess's Law

![Diagram illustrating Hess's Law with rectangles labelled reactants, products, and elements, showing direct and indirect routes with enthalpy changes.](../../../assets/028e7723c154-1-5-5-enthalpy-change-of-reaction-from-formation.png)

*The enthalpy change from elements to products (direct route) is equal to the enthalpy change of elements forming reactants and then products (indirect route)*

- **Equation**

**Δ*****H***<sub>**2**</sub>** = Δ*****H***<sub>**1**</sub>** + Δ*****H***<sub>**r**</sub>

Therefore,

**Δ*****H***<sub>**r **</sub>**= Δ*****H***<sub>**2**</sub>** – Δ*****H***<sub>**1**</sub>** **

> **Worked Example**
> Calculate Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>* *for the following reaction:
> 
> 2NaHCO<sub>3</sub> (s) → Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> (s) + CO<sub>2</sub> (g) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)
> 
> The table shows the standard enthalpy of formations, Δ*H*<sup>*θ*</sup><sub>*f*</sub>, relevant to this reaction.
> 
> | **Molecule** | **Δ*****H***<sup>***θ***</sup><sub>***f***</sub> **kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>** ** |
> |---|---|
> | NaHCO<sub>3</sub> (s) | –950.8 |
> | Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> (s) | –1130.7 |
> | CO<sub>2</sub> (g) | –393.5 |
> | H<sub>2</sub>O (l) | –285.8 |
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write the balanced equation at the top
> 
> ![Hess cycle step 1: the equation 2NaHCO3(s) → Na2CO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(l) written at the top of the cycle, reactants on the left and products on the right.](assets/f8ab9c92af39-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-1-calculating-the-e.png)
> 
> - **Step 2: **Draw the cycle with the elements at the bottom
> 
> ![Hess cycle step 2: 2NaHCO3(s) and Na2CO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(l) at the top, with the elements 2Na(s) + 2C(graphite) + 3O2(g) + H2(g) drawn below them.](assets/3115f71a9055-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-2-calculating-the-e.png)
> 
> - **Step 3: **Draw in all arrows, making sure they go in the correct directions. Write the standard enthalpy of formations
> 
> ![Completed Hess cycle: arrow from 2NaHCO3(s) to Na2CO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(l) is the enthalpy change of reaction; arrow from the elements 2Na(s) + 2C(graphite) + 3O2(g) + H2(g) up to 2NaHCO3(s) is ΔH1 = 2 x ΔHf[NaHCO3(s)]; arrow from the elements to the products is ΔH2 = ΔHf[Na2CO3(s)] + ΔHf[CO2(g)] + ΔHf[H2O(l)].](assets/55982c353dc9-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-3-calculating-the-e.png)
> 
> - **Step 4: **Apply Hess’s Law:
> 
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>*= ΔH*<sub>*2*</sub>*–* Δ*H*<sub>*1*</sub>
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>*= (* Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>* *[Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> (s)] + Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>* *[CO<sub>2</sub> (g)]*+* Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>* *[H<sub>2</sub>O (l)] ) – 2Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>* *[NaHCO<sub>3</sub> (s)]
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>* *= ( (–1130.7) + (–393.5) + (–285.8) ) – ( 2 x –950.8)
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*r*</sub>* *= +91.6 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>

### Calculating ΔH<sub>f</sub> <sub> </sub>from ΔH<sub>c</sub> using Hess’s Law energy cycles

- The combustion products can be formed directly from elements to combustion products =** ΔH**<sub>**1**</sub> **OR **The combustion products can be formed indirectly from elements to compounds to combustion products =** ΔH**<sub>**f **</sub>**+ ΔH**<sub>**2**</sub>

#### Hess's Law and combustion enthalpies

![Flowchart illustrating combustion: arrows connect "Elements + Oxygen," "Compound Formed + Oxygen," and "Combustion Products" with ΔH annotations.](../../../assets/54b3e81aefb9-1-5-chemical-energetics-enthalpy-change-elements.jpg)

*The enthalpy change going from elements to products (direct route) is equal to the enthalpy change of elements forming reactants and then products (indirect route)*

- **Equation**

**Δ*****H***<sub>**1**</sub>** = Δ*****H***<sub>**f**</sub>** + Δ*****H***<sub>**2**</sub>

- Therefore,

**Δ*****H***<sub>**f  **</sub>**= Δ*****H***<sub>**1 **</sub>**– Δ*****H***<sub>**2**</sub>

> **Worked Example**
> Calculate, using the standard enthalpy change of combustion values given, the enthalpy of formation of ethane.
> 
> | **Reaction** | **Δ*****H***<sub>***c***</sub> **kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>** ** |
> |---|---|
> | **C (graphite) + O**<sub>**2**</sub>** (g) → CO**<sub>**2**</sub>** (g)** | –393.5 |
> | H<sub>2</sub> (g) + ½O<sub>2</sub> (g) → H<sub>2</sub>O (l) | –285.8 |
> | C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> (g) + 3½O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2CO<sub>2</sub> (g) + 3H<sub>2</sub>O (l) | –1559.7 |
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write the equation for the enthalpy change of formation at the top and add oxygen on both sides
> 
> ![Combustion cycle step 1: the formation equation for ethane at the top, 2C(graphite) + 3H2(g) + 3½O2(g) → C2H6(g) + 3½O2(g), with oxygen added to both sides.](assets/93f6694e8128-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-1-calculating-the-e.png)
> 
> - **Step 2: **Draw the cycle with the combustion products at the bottom
> 
> ![Combustion cycle step 2: the elements plus oxygen, 2C(graphite) + 3H2(g) + 3½O2(g), and ethane plus oxygen, C2H6(g) + 3½O2(g), at the top, with the combustion products 2CO2(g) + 3H2O(l) at the bottom.](assets/624b5637c134-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-2-calculating-the-e.png)
> 
> - **Step 3: **Draw all arrows in the correct direction
> 
> ![Completed combustion cycle: ΔHf arrow from the elements plus oxygen to ethane plus oxygen; ΔH1 = 2ΔHc[C(graphite)] + 3ΔHc[H2(g)] from the elements plus oxygen down to 2CO2(g) + 3H2O(l); ΔH2 = ΔHc[C2H6(g)] from ethane plus oxygen down to the same products.](assets/f89fbd58b537-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-3-calculating-the-e.png)
> 
> - **Step 4: **Apply Hess’s Law:
> 
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>*= ΔH*<sub>*1*</sub>*–* Δ*H*<sub>*2*</sub>
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>* *= ( (2 x –393.5) + (3 x –285.8) ) – ( –1559.7)
>   - Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>* *= –84.7 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>

#### Calculating average bond energies using Hess's cycles

- Bond energies cannot be found directly so enthalpy cycles are used to find the **average bond energy**
- This can be done using enthalpy changes of **atomisation **and **combustion **or **formation**
- The **enthalpy change of** **atomisation **(Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>at</sub> ) is the enthalpy change when **one mole of gaseous **atoms is **formed **from its elements under standard conditions. *(A Level only)*

  - E.g.* *Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*at*</sub> [H<sub>2</sub>] relates to the equation:

**½H**<sub>**2 **</sub>**(g) → H (g)**

> **Worked Example**
> Calculate the average bond energy of the C–H bond, using the relevant Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub> and Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*at*</sub> values shown in the table.
> 
> |   | **Energy** **kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>** ** |
> |---|---|
> | Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*f*</sub>  [CH<sub>4</sub> (g)] | –74.8 |
> | Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*at*</sub> [½H<sub>2</sub> (g)] | +218 |
> | Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*at*</sub> [C (graphite)] | +717.7 |
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write down the equation for the dissociation of methane at the top
> 
> ![Bond energy cycle step 1: the dissociation of methane at the top, CH4(g) → C(g) + 4H(g).](assets/8def51508339-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-1-calculating-avera.png)
> 
> - **Step 2: **Write down the elements at the bottom
> 
> ![Bond energy cycle step 2: CH4(g) and C(g) + 4H(g) at the top, with the elements C(graphite) + 2H2(g) drawn below.](assets/0ee686144b4c-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-2-calculating-avera.png)
> 
> - **Step 3: **Draw all arrows in the correct direction
> 
> ![Completed bond energy cycle: ΔH from CH4(g) to C(g) + 4H(g); ΔHf[CH4(g)] from the elements C(graphite) + 2H2(g) up to CH4(g); ΔHat[C(graphite)] + 4 x ΔHat[½H2(g)] from the elements up to C(g) + 4H(g).](assets/054be44abe11-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-3-calculating-avera.png)
> 
> - **Step 4: **Apply Hess’s Law:
> 
>   - Δ*H =* Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>*at*</sub>*–* Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub>
>   - Δ*H* = ( (+717.7) + (4 x 218) ) – ( –74.8)
>   - Δ*H* = +1664.5 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> - **Step 5: **Calculate the bond energy for **one **C–H bond
> 
>   - There are 4 C-H bonds in methane
>   - Average bond enthalpy (C–H) = $\frac{+1664.5}{4}$
>   - Average bond energy (C–H) = +416 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> (to 3 significant figures)

> **Exam Hint**
> - Remember to take into account the number of moles of each reactant and product.
> - For example, there are two moles of NaHCO<sub>3</sub> (s) so the Δ*H*<sub>*f*</sub> value is multiplied by 2.
