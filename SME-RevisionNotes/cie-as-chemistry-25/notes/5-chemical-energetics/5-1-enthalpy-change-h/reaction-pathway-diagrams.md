# Reaction Pathway Diagrams

## Reaction Pathway Diagrams

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_qD2VzcDXjsjC5k9j`

## Reaction Pathway Diagrams

- A reaction pathway diagram shows the energies of the reactants, the transition state(s) and the products of the reaction with time

  - They are sometimes called energy profile diagrams
- The **transition state** is a stage during the reaction at which chemical bonds are partially broken and formed
- The transition state is very unstable – it cannot be isolated and is higher in energy than the reactants and products
- The activation energy (*E*<sub>*a*</sub>) is the energy needed to reach the transition state
- We can define the activation energy as ‘*the minimum amount of energy needed for reactant molecules to have a successful collision and start the reaction’*

#### Example reaction pathway diagram

![Energy profile diagram showing the reaction of Cl₂ and H₂ to form HCl. Includes reactants, transition state, products, activation energy, and enthalpy change.](../../../assets/1c725045e204-1-6-23-2-1-energy-level-diagram-for-hcl.png)

*The reaction pathway diagram for the reaction of hydrogen with chlorine to form hydrogen chloride gas*

### Exothermic reaction

- In an exothermic reaction, the reactants are higher in energy than the products
- The reactants are therefore closer in energy to the transition state
- This means that exothermic reactions have a lower activation energy compared to endothermic reactions

#### The reaction pathway diagram for exothermic reactions

![Graph of an exothermic reaction showing reactants, transition state, and products. Energy decreases, with negative ΔH, ending lower than reactants.](../../../assets/d16a9b3115ee-5-1-2-exo-reaction-profile.png)

*Exothermic reaction pathway diagrams always have the products lower in energy than the reactants*

### Endothermic reaction

- In an endothermic reaction, the reactants are lower in energy than the products
- The reactants are therefore further away in energy to the transition state
- This means that endothermic reactions have a higher activation energy compared to exothermic reactions

#### The reaction pathway diagram for endothermic reactions

![Graph of an endothermic reaction showing energy versus reaction progress. Curve rises to transition state, then falls to products higher than reactants.](../../../assets/3d9d0cb88922-5-1-2-endo-reaction-profile.png)

*Endothermic reaction pathway diagrams always have the products higher in energy than the reactants*

> **Worked Example**
> **Drawing energy level diagrams of the combustion of methane**
> 
> The *E*<sub>a</sub> and Δ*H* for the complete combustion of methane are +2653 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> and –890 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> respectively.
> 
> Draw the reaction pathway diagram for this reaction
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **The chemical equation for the complete combustion of methane is:
> 
> **CH**<sub>**4**</sub>** (g) + 2O**<sub>**2**</sub>** (g) → CO**<sub>**2**</sub>** (g) + 2H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l)**
> 
> - **Step 2: **Combustion reactions are always exothermic (Δ*H* is negative) so the reactants should be drawn higher in energy than the products
> 
> ![Energy level diagram step 2: axes of energy against extent of reaction, with CH4(g) + 2O2(g) as a higher level on the left and CO2(g) + 2H2O(l) as a lower level on the right.](assets/f74a5aa61f39-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-2-drawing-energy-le.png)
> 
> - **Step 3: **Draw the curve in the energy level diagram, clearly showing the transition state
> 
> ![Energy level diagram step 3: a curve rising from CH4(g) + 2O2(g) up to a transition state and falling to the lower level CO2(g) + 2H2O(l).](assets/dd9510599a13-1-5-chemical-energetics-step-3-drawing-energy-le.png)
> 
> - **Step 4: **Draw arrows to show the *E*<sub>*a*</sub>* *and Δ*H* including their values
> 
> ![Completed reaction pathway diagram for the combustion of methane: the curve rises from CH4(g) + 2O2(g) to a transition state and falls to CO2(g) + 2H2O(l), with the activation energy Ea and the enthalpy change ΔH marked with their values.](assets/2d4ca0c0e3c1-1-6-2-we-1.png)

> **Worked Example**
> **Determining the activation energy**
> 
> Determine, using the reaction pathway diagram, the *E*<sub>a</sub> for the forward and reverse reaction. The diagram shows the products 70 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> above the reactants, and the transition state 20 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> above the products.
> 
> ![Reaction pathway diagram for an endothermic reaction: the products are 70 kJ per mol above the reactants and the transition state is 20 kJ per mol above the products.](assets/69cb9cb7a3b6-1-6-2-we-2.png)
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - The *E*<sub>*a*</sub> is the energy difference from the energy level of the reactants to the top of the ‘hump’
> - *E*<sub>*a*</sub> (forward reaction) = (+70 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>) + (+ 20 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> ) = **+90 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>
> - As the question is asking for the **reverse reaction, **the *E*<sub>*a*</sub>* *<sub> </sub>is the energy difference from the energy level of the products to the ‘hump’
> - ***E***<sub>***a***</sub>** (reverse reaction) = +20 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>

> **Exam Hint**
> - The activation energy is the energy difference from **reactants **to the **transition state.**
> - The enthalpy change of the reaction is the energy difference from **reactants **to **products.**
> - Remember to label the axis of the reaction pathway diagrams!
