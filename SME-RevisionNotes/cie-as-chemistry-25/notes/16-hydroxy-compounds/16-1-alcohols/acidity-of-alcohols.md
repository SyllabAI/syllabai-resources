## Acidity of Alcohols

- Alcohols have a **low degree of dissociation**

  - This means, that when dissolved in water, alcohol molecules do not **dissociate **(split up) to a great extent

ROH (aq) <sup>**           **</sup>$\rightleftharpoons$<sup>**       **</sup>RO<sup>- </sup>(aq) + H<sup>+</sup> (aq)

<sup>alcohol                               alkoxide ion</sup>

- The **position **of the equilibrium lies to the left, meaning that there are far more alcohol molecules than RO<sup>-</sup> and H<sup>+</sup> ions
- When water dissociates, the position of the equilibrium still lies to the **left**, but there are more H<sup>+</sup> ions compared to the dissociation of alcohols

H<sub>2</sub>O (l) $\rightleftharpoons$ OH<sup>–</sup> (aq) + H<sup>+</sup> (aq)

- As alcohols have a lower [H<sup>+ </sup>(aq)] in solution compared to water, **alcohols are weaker acids than water**

### The inductive effect in alcohols

- **Electron-donating species such as alkyl groups push electrons into a covalent bond and are said to have a positive inductive effect**
- In alcohols, the **oxygen** atom in the **alkoxide ion **is bonded to an **electron-donating **alkyl group
- This means that there is more electron density on the O<sup>-</sup> atom
- The alkoxide ion is, therefore, more likely to **accept **an H<sup>+</sup> ion and form the alcohol again

#### The inductive effect in alcohols

![Ethanol equilibrium reaction showing equilibrium leftwards, forming ethoxide ion and hydrogen ion. Alkyl group donates electron density to oxygen.](../../../assets/9cd2f0918cbd-3-4-hydroxy-compounds-positive-inductive-effect-.png)

*Alkyl groups in the alkoxide ion donate electron density to the negatively charged oxygen, causing it to more readily accept a proton and form the alcohol again*

- When water dissociates, the **hydroxide ion** only has one other **hydrogen atom**
- There is no extra **electron density **on the oxygen which is less likely to **accept **an H<sup>+</sup> ion
- Water is therefore a **stronger **acid than alcohols

#### The equilibrium of water and hydroxide ions

![Dissociation of water, H2O(l) in equilibrium with OH-(aq) + H+(aq), with the position lying to the left but less far left than for ethanol; the hydroxide ion has no alkyl group, so less electron density on oxygen, and it accepts H+ less readily than an alkoxide ion](../../../assets/5e5bf42ff4a9-3-4-hydroxy-compounds-acid-behaviour-of-water.png)

*Water is a stronger acid than alcohols as there are no electron-donating groups in the hydroxide ion, causing the O- to be less likely to accept a proton and reform water*
