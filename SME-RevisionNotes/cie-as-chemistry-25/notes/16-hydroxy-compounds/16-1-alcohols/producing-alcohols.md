# Producing Alcohols

## Production of Alcohols

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_MhBYBMKn8wCVYzfn`

## Production of Alcohols

- **Alcohols **are compounds that contain at least one **hydroxy **(-OH) group
- The general formula of alcohols is **C**<sub>**n**</sub>**H**<sub>**2n+1**</sub>**OH**
- Alcohols can be prepared by a wide range of chemical reactions

### Electrophilic addition of alkenes

- When hot steam is reacted with an alkene, using concentrated phosphoric(V) acid (**H**<sub>**3**</sub>**PO**<sub>**4**</sub>**) **as a catalyst, ***electrophilic addition*** takes place to form an **alcohol**

#### The electrophilic addition of ethene using water

![Chemical reaction showing ethene and steam forming ethanol, with H₃PO₄ and heat as catalysts. Ethene and ethanol molecular structures depicted.](../../../assets/379a58aa6bc6-3-4-hydroxy-compounds-electrophilic-addition.png)

*Electrophilic addition of steam to alkenes forms alcohols*

### Oxidation of alkenes

- Cold, dilute KMnO<sub>4</sub> is a **mild oxidising agent** and oxidises **alkenes**
- The C-C double bond is not fully broken and a diol is formed

  - A diol is a compound with two hydroxy, -OH, groups

#### Oxidation of ethene using cold, dilute KMnO<sub>4</sub>

![Hydroxy Compounds Oxidation, downloadable AS & A Level Chemistry revision notes](../../../assets/d975171ddee5-3-4-hydroxy-compounds-oxidation.png)

*The oxidation of alkenes using cold, dilute KMnO4 forms a diol*

### Nucleophilic substitution of halogenoalkanes

- The halide atom in halogenoalkanes can be substituted when heated with aqueous NaOH in a ***nucleophilic substitution*** reaction

#### Nucleophilic substitution of bromoethane using NaOH

![Ethene reacts with water and an oxidising agent, [O], to form ethane-1,2-diol (reaction with cold dilute KMnO4)](../../../assets/f0cbcddd904b-3-4-hydroxy-compounds-nucleophilic-substitution.png)

*The nucleophilic substitution of halogenoalkanes using NaOH forms an alcohol*

### Reduction of aldehyde & ketones

- **Aldehydes **and **ketones **can be reduced by reducing agents such as NaBH<sub>4</sub> or LiAlH<sub>4</sub>

- Aldehydes are reduced to **primary alcohols**

  - The carbon attached to the hydroxy group is bonded to one other alkyl group
- Ketones are reduced to **secondary alcohols**

  - The carbon attached to the hydroxy group is bonded to two other alkyl groups

#### The reduction of ethanal and propanone

![Chemical equations showing ethanal to ethanol and propanone to propan-2-ol, both with hydrogen addition. Ethanol is a primary alcohol, propan-2-ol secondary.](../../../assets/13d059b24a31-3-4-hydroxy-compounds-reduction-1.png)

*The reduction of aldehydes and ketones forms primary and secondary alcohols respectively*

### Reduction of carboxylic acids

- Similarly, carboxylic acids are reduced by LiAlH<sub>4</sub> to primary alcohols
- NaBH<sub>4</sub> is not used as it is not a strong enough reducing agent for this reaction

#### The reduction of ethanoic acid

![Ethanoic acid is reduced by 4[H] (from LiAlH4) to ethanol and water](../../../assets/f1557c87e780-ethanoic-acid-reduction-lialh4.png)

*The reduction of carboxylic acids forms primary alcohols*

### Hydrolysis of ester

- Esters are made by a **condensation reaction** between an alcohol and a carboxylic acid
- When an ester is heated with dilute acid or alkali, **hydrolysis** will take place and the carboxylic acid and alcohol will be reformed

#### Acidic and alkaline hydrolysis of ethyl ethanoate

![Chemical reactions of ethyl ethanoate: hydrolysis forming ethanoic acid and ethanol, and saponification forming sodium ethanoate and ethanol.](../../../assets/286423bc0e30-acidic-and-alkaline-hydrolysis-of-ethyl-ethanoat.png)

*The hydrolysis of esters forms alcohols*

| **Reaction Type** | **Reagents** | **Conditions ** | **Product** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrophilic addition | Alkene Steam | Heat<br><br>H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> catalyst | Alcohol |
| Oxidation | Alkenes Cold, dilute KMnO<sub>4</sub> | Shaking of reagents | Diol |
| Nucleophilic substitution | Halogenoalkanes Aqueous NaOH | Heat | Alcohol |
| Reduction | Aldehydes / ketones | LiAlH<sub>4</sub> or NaBH<sub>4</sub> | Primary / secondary alcohol |
| Reduction | Carboxylic acid | LiAlH<sub>4</sub> | Primary alcohol |
| Hydrolysis | Esters | Dilute acid or alkali<br><br>Heat | Alcohol |

> **Exam Hint**
