## Common Acids

- An **acid** is a substance that **neutralises** a base forming a **salt** and **water**:

**acid + base **$\rightleftharpoons$**salt + water**

**2HCl (aq) + CaO (s) **$\rightleftharpoons$**CaCl**<sub>**2**</sub>** (aq) + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l)**

- Acids are also substances that release **hydrogen ions** when they dissolve in water:

**HCl(g) → H**<sup>**+**</sup>**(aq) + Cl**<sup>**- **</sup>**(aq)**

#### Acid dissociation

![Chemical equation showing HA dissociating into H⁺ and A⁻ ions; HA in red and green circles, arrow indicating reaction direction.](../../../assets/ee190122870f-1-7-equilibria-acid-dissociation.png)

*Acids dissociate in water to release a hydrogen ion*

#### Common acids

- **Hydrochloric acid**

  - HCl forms H<sup>+</sup> + Cl<sup>–</sup> in water
- **Nitric acid**

  - HNO<sub>3</sub> forms H<sup>+</sup> + NO<sub>3</sub><sup>–</sup> in water
- **Sulfuric acid**

  - H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> forms 2H<sup>+</sup> + SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup> in water
- **Ethanoic acid**

  - CH<sub>3</sub>COOH forms H<sup>+</sup> + CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>–</sup> in water

- Monoprotic inorganic acids, such as hydrochloric acid, fully dissociate into their ions
- Organic acids, such as **carboxylic acids**, do not fully dissociate into their ions

  - Only some of the hydrogen atoms can form ions

## Common Alkalis

- A **base** is a compound that **neutralises **an acid forming a **salt **and **water**

**acid + base **$\rightleftharpoons$**salt + water**

**2HCl (aq) + CaO (s) **$\rightleftharpoons$**CaCl**<sub>**2**</sub>** (aq) + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l)**

- A base is a substance that **accepts** hydrogen ions or a compound that contains **oxide** or **hydroxide** ions

  - For example, when the base ammonia is added to water, the ammonium ion and hydroxide ions are formed:

NH<sub>3</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l) ⇌ NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> (aq) + OH<sup>– </sup>(aq)

- For example, when sodium hydroxide is dissolved in solution, sodium ions and hydroxide ions are formed:

NaOH (s) + aq → Na<sup>+ </sup>(aq) + OH<sup>– </sup>(aq)

- A base that is **soluble **in water is called an **alkali**

#### Common alkalis table

- **Sodium hydroxide**

  - NaOH forms Na<sup>+</sup> + OH<sup>–</sup> in water
- **Potassium hydroxide**

  - KOH forms K<sup>+</sup> + OH<sup>–</sup>  in water
- **Aqueous ammonia**

  - NH<sub>3</sub> forms NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> + OH<sup>–</sup> in water
