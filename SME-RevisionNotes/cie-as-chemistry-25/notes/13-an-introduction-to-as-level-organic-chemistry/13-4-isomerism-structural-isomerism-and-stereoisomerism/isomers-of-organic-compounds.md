# Isomers of Organic Compounds

## Deducing Isomers of a Compound

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_b4cNQNCfhNWb2vQw`

## Deducing Isomers of a Compound

- You should be able to deduce all possible isomers for organic compounds knowing their molecular formula

> **Worked Example**
> How many isomers are there of dibromopropane, C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub>Br<sub>2</sub>?
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> **Step 1:** Draw the structural formula of the compound
> 
> ![Displayed formula of 1,1-dibromopropane, a three-carbon chain with two bromine atoms on the end carbon](assets/c81d9f7176a6-3-1-an-introduction-to-as-level-organic-chemistr.png)
> 
> **Step 2: **Determine whether it is a stereo or structural isomer
> 
> There is no restricted bond rotation around the C-C bond, so there is no geometrical isomerism. Next, check each structural isomer for a chiral centre: in 1,2-dibromopropane, CH<sub>3</sub>CHBrCH<sub>2</sub>Br, carbon 2 is bonded to four different groups (H, Br, CH<sub>3</sub> and CH<sub>2</sub>Br), so it exists as two optical isomers. C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub>Br<sub>2</sub> therefore has **5** isomers in total: the 4 structural isomers shown below, one of which has two optical isomers
> 
> **Step 3:** Determine whether it is a functional group, chain or positional isomerism
> 
> - Functional group? No, as Br is the only functional group possible
> - Chain? No, as the longest chain can only be 3
> - Positional? Yes, as the two bromine atoms can be bonded to different carbon atoms
> 
> ![Carbon skeleton of a three-carbon chain drawn three ways (bent, straight and with the end carbon turned) to show they are the same chain, so the longest chain can only be three carbons](assets/189a4c06eac3-3-1-an-introduction-to-as-level-organic-chemistr.png)
> 
> ![Diagram showing four structural isomers of C3H6Br2: 1,1-dibromopropane, 2,2-dibromopropane, 1,2-dibromopropane, and 1,3-dibromopropane.](assets/ecc6dcc56bdd-10-1-2-isomers-of-dibromopropane-worked-example-.png)

> **Worked Example**
> How many isomers are there of the compound with molecular formula C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>?
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> - **Step 1:** Draw the structural formula of the compound
> 
> ![Displayed formula of butane, a straight chain of four carbon atoms](assets/4b0c40e5c853-3-1-an-introduction-to-as-level-organic-chemistr.png)
> 
> - **Step 2: **Determine whether it is a stereo or structural isomer. There is no restricted bond rotation around the C-C bond and there is no chiral centre so it is structural isomerism
> 
> - **Step 3:** Determine whether it is a functional group, chain or positional isomerism
> 
>   - Functional group? No, as there are no functional groups
>   - Positional? No, as there are no functional groups which can be positioned on different carbon atoms
>   - Chain? Yes!
> 
> ![The two structural isomers of C4H10, butane and 2-methylpropane, shown as displayed formulae, with butane drawn three equivalent ways](assets/4fba09aa9f97-3-1-an-introduction-to-as-level-organic-chemistr.png)

> **Worked Example**
> How many **stereoisomers** are there of the compound with molecular formula C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>?
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> - **Step 1:** Draw the possible structural formula of the compound
> 
> ![Structural formula of dichloroethene molecule showing two carbon atoms double-bonded, with each carbon bonded to one hydrogen and one chlorine atom.](assets/6b9b9020c68f-3-1-an-introduction-to-as-level-organic-chemistr.png)
> 
> - **Step 2: **Determine whether it is a stereo or structural isomer
> 
>   - The compound has to be unsaturated for it to have molecular formula C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>
>   
>     - Due to the double bond there is restricted rotation about the C-C bond; This compound will therefore display geometrical isomerism
>   - **1,1-dichloroethene** is a structural isomer of 1,2 dichloroethene and does not exhibit stereoisomerism
> - **Step 3: **Determine whether it is optical or geometrical isomerism ![Trans-1,2-dichloroethene and cis-1,2-dichloroethene structural diagrams show geometric isomers of C2H2Cl2, highlighting different chlorine and hydrogen positions.](assets/70777e269198-3-1-an-introduction-to-as-level-organic-chemistr.png)
> 
>   - Optical? No, as there are no chiral centres
>   - Geometric? Yes!
