# Magnification Formula

Calculate magnification and size of biological specimens using millimetres as units

## Magnification formula

### Calculating magnification and specimen size using millimetres as units

- Magnification is calculated using the following formula:

**Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size**

- A better way to remember the formula is using a formula triangle

![Magnification formula triangle showing I above A and M, with text explaining I = image size, A = actual size, M = magnification](../../../assets/31f280b4b7df-magnification-equation.png)

*Magnification formula triangle*

- Rearranging the formula to find things other than magnification becomes easy when using the triangle. Whatever value is unknown gets covered with a finger, and whatever is left is what gets calculated:

  - Magnification = image size ÷ actual size
  - Actual size = image size ÷ magnification
  - Image size = magnification × actual size

Remember magnification does not have any units and is just written as ‘x 10’ or ‘x 5000’

> **Worked Example**
> An image of an animal cell is 30 mm in size and it has been magnified by a factor of x 3000. What is the actual size of the cell?
> 
> To find the actual size of the cell:
> 
> ![Diagram showing the magnification formula triangle, with A (actual size) covered by a finger, leaving  I (image size) over M (magnification) and the  worked example showing that in this example,  A = I ÷ M = 30 mm ÷ 3000 = 0.01 mm = 10 μm](assets/a9d8883434d4-worked-example-using-magnification-equation.png)

> **Exam Hint**
> This skill most frequently comes up in paper 5 and 6 (although it also comes up in the multiple choice and occasionally the theory paper) and you will definitely have to **calculate either magnification, drawing size or actual size** in a least one paper. To ensure you are awarded marks:
> 
> 1. Always **look at the units** that have been given in the question - if you are asked to measure something, most often you will be expected to measure it in millimetres NOT in centimetres - double check the question to see!
> 2. **Learn the equation triangle for magnification** and write it on the page straight away
> 3. Don’t forget that **magnification has NO UNITS** - students often lose a mark because they put one in
