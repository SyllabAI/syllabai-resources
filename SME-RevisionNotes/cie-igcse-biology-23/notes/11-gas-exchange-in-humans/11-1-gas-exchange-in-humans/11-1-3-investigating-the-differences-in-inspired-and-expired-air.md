# Investigating the Differences in Inspired & Expired Air

## Investigating the differences in inspired & expired air

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_C3TS2DRykHdZgS9x` · Investigate the differences in composition between inspired and expired air using limewater as a test for carbon dioxide

## Investigating the differences in inspired & expired air

- A simple experimental setup can be used to investigate the differences between inspired and expired air

![Diagram of two limewater boiling tubes, A and B, linked by rubber tubing to the mouth to compare exhaled carbon dioxide effects on limewater.](../../../assets/a1303f581cd8-the-limewater-test.png)

*The limewater test*

- When air is breathed in, it is drawn through boiling tube A
- When air is breathed out, the air is blown into boiling tube B
- **Limewater** is **clear** but becomes **cloudy** (or milky) when carbon dioxide is bubbled through it

  - The limewater in **boiling tube A will remain clear**, as inhaled air is atmospheric air, which contains a very low percentage of carbon dioxide The limewater in **boiling tube B will become cloudy**, as exhaled air has passed through the lungs (where gas exchange has taken place) and contains a higher percentage of carbon dioxide
- This shows us that the **percentage of carbon dioxide in exhaled air is higher than in inhaled air**
