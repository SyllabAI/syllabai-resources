# Differences in Inspired & Expired Air

## Differences in Inspired & Expired Air: Extended

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_9MryhxnfY32CS4CQ` · Explain the differences in composition between inspired and expired air

## Differences in inspired & expired air: Extended

### Extended Tier Only

#### Reason for differences between inspired and expired air table

| Gas | Inspired air (%) | Expired air (%) | Reason for difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen (O<sub>2</sub>) | 21 | 16 | Oxygen is removed from blood by respiring cells, so blood returning to the lungs has a lower oxygen concentration than the air in the alveoli, which means oxygen diffuses into the blood in the lungs |
| Carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) | 0.04 | 4 | Carbon dioxide is produced by respiration and diffuses into blood from respiring cells; the blood transports the carbon dioxide to the lungs, where it diffuses into the alveoli as it is in a higher concentration in the blood than in the air in the alveoli |
| Water vapour (H<sub>2</sub>O) | Lower | Higher | Water evaporates from the moist lining of the alveoli into the expired air as a result of the warmth of the body |
| Nitrogen (N<sub>2</sub>) | 78 | 78 | Nitrogen gas is very stable and so cannot be used by the body; for this reason, its concentration does not change in inspired or expired air |

> **Exam Hint**
> Make sure you can explain the *reasons* behind the differences between inspired and expired air, not just recall the differences themselves, this is a common Extended Tier requirement.
