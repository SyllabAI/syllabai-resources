# Explaining the Link Between Physical Activity & Breathing

## Explaining the link between physical activity & breathing: Extended

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_7cCBKxVPTTxM67s8` · Explain the link between physical activity and the rate and depth of breathing in terms of: an increased carbon dioxide concentration in the blood, which is detected by the brain, leading to an increased rate and greater depth of breathing

## Explaining the link between physical activity & breathing: Extended

### Extended Tier Only

- Frequency and depth of breathing **increase when exercising**
- This is because muscles are working harder and respiring aerobically at a faster rate, so they **need more oxygen delivered to them** (and carbon dioxide removed) to keep up with the increased energy demand
- If they cannot meet the energy demand they will also [respire anaerobically](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/biology/cie/23/revision-notes/12-respiration/12-1-respiration/12-1-3-anaerobic-respiration/?_ai=internal-linking), producing **lactic acid**
- After exercise has finished, the lactic acid that has built up in muscles needs to be **removed** as it **lowers the pH of cells** and can **denature enzymes** catalysing cell reactions
- Lactic acid can only be removed using oxygen — this is known as '**repaying the oxygen debt**'
- Oxygen debt can be measured by timing how long it takes for breathing rate and depth to return to normal after exercise - **the longer this takes, the more lactic acid was produced during exercise, and the greater the oxygen debt that needs to be repaid**

#### Mechanism for increasing breathing during exercise

- The **rate of respiration** increases in muscle cells when exercising heavily
- Carbon dioxide is a **product** of aerobic respiration, so **carbon dioxide levels increase** in the muscle cells
- This carbon dioxide diffuses out of the cells into the **blood plasma**

  - Carbon dioxide in solution causes a slight drop in pH so the blood becomes slightly more acidic
- The blood flows around the circulatory system and passes to the brain where the increased carbon dioxide levels are detected by **chemoreceptors** in the brain

  - Chemoreceptors are cells that detect chemical changes in the body
  - They can detect changes in blood gas levels, as well as changes in pH
  - The chemoreceptors are located in the **medulla oblongata** of the brain
- The brain sends nerve impulses to the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles to **increase the** **rate and depth of muscle contraction**
- The **rate of inspiration increases**, along with the **volume of air** moved in and out with each breath
- The result is **greater absorption of oxygen** and **removal rate of carbon dioxide**

  - This supports the **increased rate of respiration** in the exercising muscle cells

> **Exam Hint**
> Be sure not to confuse the terms **respiration** and **breathing** in this topic:
> 
> - respiration is a series of chemical reactions that releases energy from food
> - breathing is the mechanism for moving air in and out of the lungs; this supports the chemical reactions of respiration
