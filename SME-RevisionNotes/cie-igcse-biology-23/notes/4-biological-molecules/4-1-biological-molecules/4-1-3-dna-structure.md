## Structure of a DNA molecule: Extended

### Extended Tier Only

- DNA, or deoxyribonucleic acid, is the molecule that contains the instructions for the growth and development of all organisms
- It consists of two strands of DNA wound around each other in what is called a **double helix**

![Diagram showing DNA double helix forming a DNA strand, packed into a chromosome (with further detail in a label that a chromosome is a long strand of DNA wrapped around proteins) inside the nucleus of a cell with other organelles present (mitochondria, cytoplasm and cell membrane) ](../../../assets/82b5c2dd0530-dna-chromosomes-and-the-nucleus.png)

*DNA, chromosomes and the nucleus*

- The individual units of DNA are called **nucleotides**

![Diagram of a DNA nucleotide labelled with a phosphate group and deoxyribose sugar which forms the DNA strand backbone, and an attached nitrogenous base A in this image (with a label that the nitrogenous base could be A, T, C or G) ](../../../assets/201dcf7e54c3-a-nucleotide.png)

*A nucleotide*

- All nucleotides contain the same phosphate and deoxyribose sugar, but differ from each other in the **base** attached
- There are four different bases, **Adenine (A), Cytosine (C), Thymine (T) and Guanine (G)**
- The bases on each strand pair up with each other, holding the two strands of DNA in the double helix
- The bases always pair up in the same way:

  - Adenine always pairs with Thymine **(A-T)**
  - Cytosine always pairs with Guanine **(C-G)**

![Diagram of DNA double helix showing coloured base pairs A–T and C–G joined by hydrogen bonds, and a labelled sugar–phosphate backbone on the outer edge of each strand](../../../assets/0f748c670d13-dna-base-pairs.png)

*DNA base pairs*

- The phosphate and sugar section of the nucleotides form the ‘backbone’ of the DNA strand (like the sides of a ladder) and the base pairs of each strand connect to form the rungs of the ladder

![Diagram of DNA showing on the left an unwound DNA double helix, illustrating a sequence of base pairs (with A always paired with T and C paired with G) on two antiparallel strands, and the image on the right shows a double helix, there is a note that genes are sequences of bases, not base pairs](../../../assets/a5734f79b5e2-the-dna-helix-is-made-from-two-strands-of-dna-he.png)

*The DNA helix is made from two strands of DNA held together by hydrogen bonds*

- It is this sequence of bases that holds the code for the formation of proteins

> **Exam Hint**
> You do not need to learn the names of the bases, **just their letter**. Make sure you **know which bonds with which**, as this is the most commonly asked question about this topic.
