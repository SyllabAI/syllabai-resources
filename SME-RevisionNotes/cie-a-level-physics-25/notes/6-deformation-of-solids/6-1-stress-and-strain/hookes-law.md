# Hooke's Law

## Hooke's Law

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_Nj3pK6T5CnZC8qr4`

## Hooke's law

- A material demonstrating elastic behaviour obeys Hooke’s Law if **its extension is directly proportional to the applied force (load)**
- The [Force-extension graph](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/physics/cie/25/revision-notes/6-deformation-of-solids/6-1-stress-and-strain/extension-and-compression/) of an object obeying Hooke's law is a straight line through the origin
- This linear relationship is represented by the Hooke’s law equation

$F=kx$

- Where:

  - *F* is force applied in N
  - *k* is the spring constant in N m<sup>−1</sup>
  - *x* is the extension of the spring

## The Spring Constant

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_wdkFMpP4xk9MCYth`

## The spring constant

- *k* is the **spring constant** of the spring and is a measure of the **stiffness** of a spring

  - A stiffer spring will have a larger value of *k*

- *k* is defined as the **force per unit extension** up to the limit of proportionality
- The SI unit for the spring constant is **N m**<sup>**-1**</sup>
- Rearranging the **Hooke’s law equation** shows the equation for the spring constant is

$k=\frac{F}{x}$

- Therefore, the spring constant *k* is the **gradient** of the** linear part** of a **force-extension graph**

#### Gradient of force-extension graph

![Spring constant on graph, downloadable AS & A Level Physics revision notes](../../../assets/981e921fad02-6-1-3-spring-constant-on-graph.png)

***Spring constant is the gradient of a force vs extension graph***

> **Worked Example**
> A spring was stretched with increasing load.
> 
> The graph of the results is shown below.
> 
> ![WE - hookes law question image, downloadable AS & A Level Physics revision notes](assets/ad976fdb449c-we-hookes-law-question-image.png)
> 
> Determine the value of the spring constant.
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1: Rearrange Hooke's Law:**
> 
> - Spring constant, *k*, is:
> 
> $k=\frac{F}{x}$
> 
> **Step 2: Relate the gradient of this graph to *****k *****:**
> 
> - The y axis of this graph is length *L* and the x axis is load *F*
> - Gradient is change in y over change in x:
> 
> $\mathrm{gradient}=\frac{ΔL}{ΔF}=\frac{x}{F}$
> 
> - The change in length is the extension *x*
> - Therefore:
> 
> $\mathrm{gradient}=\frac{1}{k}$
> 
> **Step 3: Determine the gradient of the graph:**
> 
> - Choose a large section of the graph line to determine the changes in the x and y axes
> 
> ![6-1-2-we-hookes-law-cie-new](assets/6119ae92ab39-6-1-2-we-hookes-law-cie-new.png)
> 
> - Convert the extension from cm to m
> 
> $\mathrm{gradient}=\frac{0.145-0.100}{0.36}=0.125mN^{-1}$
> 
> **Step 4: Calculate the spring constant:**
> 
> - The spring constant is
> 
> $k=\frac{1}{\mathrm{gradient}}=\frac{1}{0.125}=8.0Nm^{-1}$

> **Exam Hint**
> Double check the axes before finding the spring constant as the gradient of a force-extension graph. Exam questions often swap the load onto the x-axis and length on the y-axis. In this case, the spring constant is $\frac{1}{\mathrm{gradient}}$ and not the spring constant.
