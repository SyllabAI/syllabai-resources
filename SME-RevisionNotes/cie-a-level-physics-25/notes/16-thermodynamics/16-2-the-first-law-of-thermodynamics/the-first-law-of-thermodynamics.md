# The First Law of Thermodynamics

## The First Law of Thermodynamics

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_JkqD5M7D8cFv9jTc`

## The first law of thermodynamics

- The first law of thermodynamics is based on the principle of conservation of energy
- Heating a gas and doing work on it both increase its internal energy; ∆*U* is the net result of both:

**The increase in internal energy of a system is equal to the sum of the thermal energy added to the system and the work done on it**

- The first law of thermodynamics is therefore defined as:

$\DeltaU=q+W$

- Where:

  - ∆*U* = increase in *internal energy* (J)
  - *q* = thermal energy added to the system (J)
  - *W* = work done on the system (J)

- The first law of thermodynamics applies to **all** thermodynamic systems, not just gases

  - There is an important sign convention used for this equation
- A **positive **value for internal energy (+∆*U*) means:

  - The internal energy ∆*U *of the system **increases**
  - Heat (+*q*) is **added** to the system
  - Work (+*W*) is done **on **the system
- A **negative **value for internal energy (−∆*U*) means:

  - The internal energy ∆*U* of the system **decreases**
  - Heat (-*q*)* *is **taken away** from the system
  - Work (-*W*)* *is done **by **the system

> **Worked Example**
> The volume occupied by 1.00 mol of a liquid at 50 °C is 2.4 × 10<sup>-5</sup> m<sup>3</sup>. When the liquid is vaporised at an atmospheric pressure of 1.03 × 10<sup>5</sup> Pa, the vapour has a volume of 5.9 × 10<sup>-2 </sup>m<sup>3</sup>. The latent heat to vaporise 1.00 mol of this liquid at 50 °C at atmospheric pressure is 3.48 × 10<sup>4</sup> J.
> 
> Determine, for this change of state, the change in the internal energy of the system.
> 
> **Answer: **
> 
> **Step 1: Write down the first law of thermodynamics**
> 
> $\DeltaU=q+W$
> 
> **Step 2: Write the value of heating *****q***** of the system**
> 
> - This is the latent heat, the heat required to vaporise the liquid =** **3.48 × 10<sup>4</sup> J
> 
> **Step 3: Calculate the work done *****W***
> 
> $W=p\DeltaV$
> 
> - ∆*V *= final volume − initial volume
> 
> ∆*V = *5.9 × 10<sup>-2</sup> − 2.4 × 10<sup>-5</sup> = 0.058976 m<sup>3</sup>
> 
> - *p* = atmospheric pressure  = 1.03 × 10<sup>5</sup> Pa
> - *W *= work done
> 
> *W* = (1.03 × 10<sup>5</sup>) × 0.058976 = 6074.528 = 6.07 × 10<sup>3</sup> J
> 
> - Since the gas is **expanding**, the work done is **negative**
> 
> *W* = −6.07 × 10<sup>3</sup> J
> 
> **Step 4: Substitute the values into the first law of thermodynamics**
> 
> ∆*U* = 3.48 × 10<sup>4</sup>  + (−6.07 × 10<sup>3</sup>) = 28 730 = 29 000 J (2 s.f.)
