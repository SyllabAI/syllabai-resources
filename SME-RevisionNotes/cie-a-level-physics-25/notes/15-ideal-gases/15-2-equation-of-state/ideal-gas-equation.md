# Ideal Gas Equation

## Ideal Gas Equation

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_yBrgNYY2b47JJzdD`

## Ideal gas equation

- The **equation of state for an ideal gas** (or the ideal gas equation) can be expressed as:

$pV=nRT$

- Where:

  - *p* = pressure (Pa)
  - *V* = volume (m<sup>3</sup>)
  - *n* = amount of substance / [number of moles](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/physics/cie/25/revision-notes/15-ideal-gases/15-1-the-mole/the-mole/) (mol)
  - *R* = molar gas constant (8.31 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>)
  - *T* = temperature (K)

- The ideal gas equation can also be written in the form:

$pV=NkT$

- Where:

  - *N* = number of molecules
  - *k* = Boltzmann constant (1.38 × 10<sup>-23</sup> J K<sup>–1</sup>)
- Remember that the number of moles and the number of molecules are related by [Avogadro's constant](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/physics/cie/25/revision-notes/15-ideal-gases/15-1-the-mole/the-mole/)
- An ideal gas is therefore defined as:

**A gas which obeys the equation of state pV = nRT at all pressures, volumes and temperatures**

> **Worked Example**
> A storage cylinder of an ideal gas has a volume of 8.3 × 10<sup>3</sup> cm<sup>3</sup>. The gas is at a temperature of 15°C and a pressure of 4.5 × 10<sup>7</sup> Pa.
> 
> Calculate the amount of gas in the cylinder, in moles.
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1: Write down the ideal gas equation**
> 
> - Since the number of moles (*n*) is required, use the equation:
> 
> $pV=nRT$
> 
> **Step 2: Rearrange for the number of moles***** n***
> 
> $n=\frac{pV}{RT}$
> 
> **Step 3:** **Substitute in values**
> 
> $V = 8.3 \times 10^{3} cm^{3} = ( 8.3 \times 10^{3} ) \times 10^{- 6} = 8.3 \times 10^{- 3} \mathrm{m}^{3}$
> 
> $T=15^{\circ}C+273.15=288.15K$
> 
> $n = \frac{( 4.5 \times 10^{7 ) \times ( 8.3 \times 10^{-3} } ) }{8.31 \times 288.15} = 155.98 = 160 \text{moles}$

> **Exam Hint**
> Don’t worry about remembering the values of R and k, they will both be given in the equation sheet in your exam.

## The Boltzmann constant

- The **Boltzmann constant, *****k,**** *is used in the ideal gas equation and is defined by the equation:

$k=\frac{R}{N_{A}}$

- Where:

  - *R* = *molar gas constant*
  - *N*<sub>*A*</sub> = *Avogadro’s constant*

- Boltzmann’s constant therefore has a value of:

$k=\frac{8.31}{6.02\times10^{23}}=1.38\times10^{-23}JK^{-1}$

- The Boltzmann constant has the** units J K**<sup>**-1**</sup><sup> </sup>because

  - it relates the properties of **microscopic particles**, e.g. kinetic energy of gas molecules
  - to their **macroscopic properties**, e.g. temperature

- The Boltzmann constant is **very small** because the increase in kinetic energy of a molecule is very small for every incremental increase in temperature
