# Average Kinetic Energy of a Molecule

## Average Kinetic Energy of a Molecule

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_HRPRbn6dzzsWw4Pw`

## Average kinetic energy of a molecule

- An important property of molecules in a gas is their **average translational kinetic energy**
- For a gas, **translational kinetic energy** is defined as:

**The kinetic energy of the molecules as determined by their temperature**

- The average translational kinetic energy of **one particle** in a gas can be deduced from the ideal gas equations relating pressure, volume, temperature and speed

### Deducing the equation for translational kinetic energy

- Recall the [ideal gas equation](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/physics/cie/25/revision-notes/15-ideal-gases/15-2-equation-of-state/ideal-gas-equation/):

$pV=NkT$

- Also, recall the equation for the [kinetic theory of gases](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/physics/cie/25/revision-notes/15-ideal-gases/15-3-kinetic-theory-of-gases/derivation-of-the-kinetic-theory-of-gases-equation/):

$pV=\frac{1}{3}Nm<c^{2}>$

- The left-hand sides of the equations are equal to *pV*, therefore, they can be equated:

$\frac{1}{3}Nm<c^{2}>=NkT$

- Simplify the equation by:

  - dividing both sides by *N*
  - multiplying both sides by 3

$m<c^{2}>=3kT$

- Recall the familiar [kinetic energy equation](https://www.savemyexams.com/a-level/physics/cie/25/revision-notes/5-work-energy-and-power/5-2-gravitational-potential-energy-and-kinetic-energy/kinetic-energy/) from mechanics:

kinetic energy = $\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$

- In translational kinetic energy, the mean-square speed of all the molecules, <*c*<sup>2</sup>> replaces *v*<sup>2</sup> for a single particle

- Multiplying both sides by ½ gives the equation for the **average translational kinetic energy **of **one molecule** of an ideal gas:

$E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}m<c^{2}>=\frac{3}{2}kT$

- Where:

  - *E*<sub>k </sub>= kinetic energy of a molecule (J)
  - *m* = mass of one molecule (kg)
  - <c<sup>2</sup>> = mean square speed of a molecule (m<sup>2</sup> s<sup>-2</sup>)
  - *k* = *Boltzmann constant*
  - *T* = temperature of the gas (K)
- To calculate the average translational kinetic energy for all the molecules in a gas, multiply the kinetic energy by the number of molecules in the gas, *N*:

$E_{k-total}=\frac{3}{2}NkT$

> **Worked Example**
> Helium can be treated like an ideal gas. Helium molecules have a root-mean-square (r.m.s.) speed of 730 m s<sup>-1</sup> at a temperature of 45 °C.
> 
> Calculate the r.m.s speed of the molecules at a temperature of 80 °C.
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1: Write down the equation for the average translational kinetic energy**
> 
> $E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}m<c^{2}>=\frac{3}{2}kT$
> 
> **Step 2: Find the relation between *****c***<sub>**r.m.s**</sub>** and temperature *****T***
> 
> - Since *m* and *k* are constant, <*c*<sup>2</sup>> is directly proportional to *T*
> 
> $<c^{2}>\proptoT$
> 
> - Therefore, the relation between *c*<sub>r.m.s</sub> and *T* is:
> 
> $c_{r.m.s}=\sqrt{<c^{2}>}\propto\sqrt{T}$
> 
> **Step 3: Write the equation in full**
> 
> $c_{r.m.s}=a\sqrt{T}$
> 
> - where a is the constant of proportionality
> 
> **Step 4: Calculate the constant of proportionality from values given by rearranging for a**
> 
> T = 45 °C + 273.15 = 318.15 K
> 
> $a=\frac{c_{r.m.s}}{\sqrt{T}}=\frac{730}{\sqrt{318.15}}=40.92...$
> 
> **Step 5: Calculate***** c***<sub>**r.m.s**</sub>**  at 80 °C by substituting the value of a and new value of *****T***
> 
> $T=80^{\circ}C+273.15=353.15K$
> 
> $c_{r.m.s}=\frac{730}{\sqrt{318.15}}\times\sqrt{353.15}=769=770ms^{-1}$

> **Exam Hint**
> Keep in mind this particular equation for kinetic energy is only for **one** molecule in the gas. If you want to find the kinetic energy for all the molecules, remember to multiply by ***N***, the total number of molecules. You can remember the equation through the rhyme ‘Average K.E is three-halves kT’.
