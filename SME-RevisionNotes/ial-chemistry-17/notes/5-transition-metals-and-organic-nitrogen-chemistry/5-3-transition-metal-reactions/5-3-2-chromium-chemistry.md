## Chromium - Reduction & Oxidation

- For chromium we need to consider the following standard electrode potential values

![reduction-and-oxidation-of-chromium-species-table](../../../assets/5b16b35816fe-reduction-and-oxidation-of-chromium-species-tabl.png)

- The half equations are arranged from the most negative *E*<sup>θ </sup>at the top to the most positive *E*<sup>θ </sup>at the bottom

  - The strongest oxidising agent is the reactant from the electrode reaction with the most positive standard electrode potential
  
    - In this case, the most positive value is + 1.33 V, which means that Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2–</sup> (aq)  is the strongest oxidising agent
  - The strongest reducing agent is the product from the electrode reaction with the most negative standard electrode potential
  
    - In this case, the most negative value is – 0.76 V, which means that Zn (s) is the strongest reducing agent

#### Oxidation of Cr from +3 to +6 by H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>

- The two half equations we need to consider are 3 and 4
- Chromium is being oxidised from an oxidation number of +6 to +3 in half equation 3
- So, the two half equations we need to consider are:

  - 3. CrO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup> (aq) + 4H<sub>2</sub>O (l) + 3e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Cr(OH)<sub>3</sub> (aq) + 5OH<sup>–</sup> (aq)     *E*<sup>θ</sup> = – 0.13 V
  - 4. H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ 2OH<sup>–</sup> (aq)     *E*<sup>θ</sup> = + 1.24 V

- Half equation 4 has the most positive standard electrode potential, *E*<sup>θ</sup>, value

  - Therefore, this is the reduction reaction
  - H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ 2OH<sup>–</sup> (aq)
- Half equation 3 has the least positive standard electrode potential, *E*<sup>θ</sup>, value

  - Therefore, this is the oxidation reaction and needs to be reversed
  - Cr(OH)<sub>3</sub> (aq) + 5OH<sup>–</sup> (aq) $\rightleftharpoons$CrO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup> (aq) + 4H<sub>2</sub>O (l) + 3e
- We can obtain the overall equation by combining the reduction equation (half equation 4) and the oxidation equation (the reversed half equation 3)

  - **Remember: **When combining half equations, they must have the same number of electrons in both equations | **3H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**<sub>**2**</sub>** (aq) + 6e**<sup>**–**</sup>** ** | $\rightleftharpoons$** ** | **6OH**<sup>**–**</sup>** (aq)** |
  |---|---|---|
  | **2Cr(OH)**<sub>**3**</sub>** (aq) + 10OH**<sup>**–**</sup>** (aq)** | $\rightleftharpoons$ | **2CrO**<sub>**4**</sub><sup>**2–**</sup>** (aq) + 8H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l) + 6e**<sup>**–**</sup> |
  - The equal number of electrons on both sides of the equation cancel out and 6OH<sup>–</sup> (aq) cancel out on both sides to give the overall equation:

** 2Cr(OH)**<sub>**3 **</sub>**(aq) + 4OH**<sup>**-**</sup>** (aq) + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**<sub>**2 **</sub>**(aq) **$\rightleftharpoons$** CrO**<sub>**4**</sub><sup>**2- **</sup>**(aq) + 8H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l)**

- This reaction is carried out in alkaline conditions due to the presence of OH<sup>-</sup> ions in the equation

#### Reduction of Cr from +6 to +3 by Zn

- Chromium is being reduced from an oxidation number of +6 to +3 in half equation 5
- So, the two half equations we need to consider are:

  - 1. Zn<sup>2+</sup> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Zn (s)     *E*<sup>θ</sup> = – 0.76 V
  - 5. Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2–</sup> (aq) + 14H<sup>+</sup> (aq) + 6e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ 2Cr<sup>3+</sup> (aq) + 7H<sub>2</sub>O (l)     *E*<sup>θ</sup> = + 1.33 V
- Half equation 5 has the most positive standard electrode potential, *E*<sup>θ</sup>, value

  - Therefore, this is the reduction reaction
  - Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2–</sup> (aq) + 14H<sup>+</sup> (aq) + 6e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ 2Cr<sup>3+</sup> (aq) + 7H<sub>2</sub>O (l)
- Half equation 1 has the least positive / most negative standard electrode potential, *E*<sup>θ</sup>, value

  - Therefore, this is the oxidation reaction and needs to be reversed
  - Zn (s) → Zn<sup>2+</sup> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup>
- We can obtain the overall equation by combining the reduction equation (half equation 3) and the oxidation equation (the reversed half equation 1)

  - **Remember: **When combining half equations, they must have the same number of electrons in both equations | ** Cr**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**<sub>**7**</sub><sup>**2–**</sup>** (aq) + 14H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + 6e**<sup>**–**</sup>** ** | $\rightleftharpoons$** ** | **2Cr**<sup>**3+**</sup>** (aq) + 7H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l)** |
  |---|---|---|
  | **3Zn (s)** | $\rightleftharpoons$** ** | **3Zn**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) + 6e**<sup>**–**</sup>** ** |
  - The equal number of electrons on both sides of the equation cancel out to give the overall equation:

** Cr**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**<sub>**7**</sub><sup>**2-**</sup><sub>** **</sub>**(aq) + 14H**<sup>**+**</sup>** (aq) + 3Zn (s) **$\rightleftharpoons$** 2Cr**<sup>**3+ **</sup>**(aq) + 7H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l) + 3Zn**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq)**

- This reaction is carried out under acidic conditions due to the presence of H<sup>+</sup> in the equation

#### Reduction of Cr from +3 to +2 by Zn

- Chromium is being reduced from an oxidation number of +3 to +2 in half equation 2
- So, the two half equations we need to consider are:

  - 1. Zn<sup>2+</sup> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Zn (s)     *E*<sup>θ</sup> = – 0.76 V
  - 2. Cr<sup>3+</sup> (aq) + e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Cr<sup>2+</sup> (aq)     *E*<sup>θ</sup> = – 0.41 V
- Half equation 2 has the most positive / least negative standard electrode potential, *E*<sup>θ</sup>, value

  - Therefore, this is the reduction reaction
  - Cr<sup>3+</sup> (aq) + e<sup>–</sup> $\rightleftharpoons$ Cr<sup>2+</sup> (aq)
- Half equation 2 has the least positive standard electrode potential, *E*<sup>θ</sup>, value

  - Therefore, this is the oxidation reaction and needs to be reversed
  - Zn (s) $\rightleftharpoons$ Zn<sup>2+</sup> (aq) + 2e<sup>–</sup>
- We can obtain the overall equation by combining the reduction equation (half equation 2) and the oxidation equation (the reversed half equation 1)

  - **Remember: **When combining half equations, they must have the same number of electrons in both equations | **2Cr**<sup>**3+**</sup>** (aq) + 2e**<sup>**–**</sup> | $\rightleftharpoons$** ** | **2Cr**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) ** |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Zn (s)** | $\rightleftharpoons$** ** | **Zn**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) + 2e**<sup>**–**</sup>** ** |
  - The equal number of electrons on both sides of the equation cancel out to give the overall equation:

**2Cr**<sup>**3+ **</sup>**(aq) + Zn (s) **$\rightleftharpoons$** 2Cr**<sup>**2+**</sup>** (aq) + Zn**<sup>**2+ **</sup>**(aq) **

- As this reaction is a further step from the previous reduction this reaction is also carried out under acidic conditions

## Dichromate(VI) & Chromate(VI) Equilibrium

- The chromate CrO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> and dichromate Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2- </sup>ions can be converted from one to the other by the following equilibrium reaction

**2CrO**<sub>**4**</sub><sup>**2-**</sup>** (aq) + 2H**<sup>**+ **</sup>**(aq) ⇌ Cr**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**<sub>**7**</sub><sup>**2-**</sup>** (aq) + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O (l) **

- Chromate(IV) ions are stable in alkaline solution, but in acidic conditions the dichromate(VI) ion is more stable
- Addition of acid will  push the equilibrium to the dichromate

  - This results in a colour change from yellow to orange
- Addition of alkali will remove the H<sup>+</sup> ions and push the equilibrium to the chromate
- This is not a redox reaction as both the chromate and dichromate ions have an oxidation number of +6

  - This is an acid base reaction
