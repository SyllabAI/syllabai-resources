## Reacting Mass Calculations

- Chemical equations can be used to calculate the** moles** or **masses** of reactants and products
- To do this, information given in the question is used to find the amount in moles of the substances being considered
- Then, the **ratio** between the substances is identified using the balanced chemical equation
- Once the moles have been determined they can then be converted into grams using the relative atomic or relative formula masses

> **Worked Example**
> **Example 1**
> 
> Calculate the maximum mass of magnesium oxide that can be produced by completely burning 7.5 g of magnesium in oxygen.
> 
> magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> 1. Write the balanced chemical equation:
> 
> 2Mg (s) + O<sub>2 </sub>(g) → 2MgO (s)
> 
> 2. Determine the relative atomic and formula masses:
> 
>   - Magnesium, Mg = 24.3 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Oxygen, O<sub>2</sub> = 32.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Magnesium oxide, MgO = 40.3 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 3. Calculate the moles of magnesium used in the reaction:
> 
> *n*(Mg) = $\frac{7.5g}{24.3g\mathrm{mol}^{-1}}$ = 0.3086 moles
> 
> 4. Deduce the number of moles of magnesium oxide, using the balanced chemical equation:
> 
>   - 2 moles of magnesium form 2 moles of magnesium oxide
>   
>     - The ratio is 1 : 1
>   - Therefore, *n*(MgO) = 0.3086 moles
> 5. Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide:
> 
> Mass = moles x *M*<sub>r</sub>
> 
> Mass = 0.3086 mol x 40.3 g mol<sup>-1</sup> = 12.4 g
> 
> 6. Therefore, the mass of magnesium oxide produced is **12.4 g**

> **Worked Example**
> **Example 2**
> 
> Calculate the mass of aluminium, in tonnes, that can be produced from 51 tonnes of aluminium oxide. The equation for the reaction is:
> 
> aluminium oxide ⟶ aluminium + oxygen
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> 1. Write the balanced chemical equation:
> 
> 2Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>  ⟶  4Al +  3O<sub>2</sub>** **
> 
> 2. Determine the relative atomic and formula masses:
> 
>   - Aluminium oxide, Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>  = 102.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Aluminium, Al = 27.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Oxygen, O<sub>2</sub> = 32.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 3. Calculate the mass, in g, of aluminium oxide used in the reaction:
> 
> 51 tonnes x 10<sup>6</sup> = 51 000 000 g
> 
> 4. Calculate the moles of aluminium oxide used in the reaction:
> 
> *n*(Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> ) = $\frac{51000000g}{102.0g\mathrm{mol}^{-1}}$ = 500 000 mol
> 
> 5. Deduce the number of moles of aluminium, using the balanced chemical equation:
> 
>   - 2 moles of aluminium oxide form 4  moles of aluminium
>   
>     - The ratio is 1:2
>   - Therefore, *n*(Al) = 500 000 x 2 = 1 000 000 mol
> 6. Calculate the mass of aluminium:
> 
> mass = moles x *M*<sub>r</sub>
> 
> mass =  1 000 000 mol x 27.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup> = 27 000 000 g
> 
> 7. Convert the mass to tonnes:
> 
> mass = $\frac{27000000}{10^{6}}$
> 
> mass = **27 tonnes**

> **Exam Hint**
> As long as you are consistent it doesn't matter whether you work in grams or tonnes or any other mass unit as the reacting masses will always be in proportion to the balanced equation.

#### Balancing Equations using Reacting Masses

- If the masses of reactants and products of a reaction are known then we can use them to write a balanced equation for that reaction
- This is done by converting the **masses **to **moles** and simplifying to find the **molar ratios**

> **Worked Example**
> **Example 3**
> 
> A student reacts 1.2 g of carbon with 16.2 g of zinc oxide. The resulting products are 4.4 g of carbon dioxide and 13.0 g of zinc.
> 
> Determine the balanced equation for the reaction.
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> 1. Write the unbalanced chemical equation:
> 
> C + ZnO ⟶ Zn + CO<sub>2</sub>
> 
> 2. Determine the relative atomic and formula masses:
> 
>   - Carbon, C = 12.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Zinc, Zn = 65.4 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Zinc oxide, ZnO = 81.4 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
>   - Carbon dioxide, CO<sub>2</sub> = 44.0 g mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 3. Calculate the moles of each substance
> 
> moles = $\frac{\mathrm{mass}}{M_{r}}$
> 
> *n*(C) = $\frac{1.2}{12.0}$ = 0.1 mol
> 
> *n*(Zn) = $\frac{13.0}{65.4}$ = 0.2 mol
> 
> *n*(ZnO) = $\frac{16.2}{81.4}$ = 0.2 mol
> 
> *n*(CO<sub>2</sub>) = $\frac{4.4}{44.0}$ = 0.1 mol
> 
> 4. Determine the ratio (divide by the smallest value)
> 
> C = $\frac{0.1}{0.1}$ = 1
> 
> Zn = $\frac{0.2}{0.1}$ = 2
> 
> ZnO = $\frac{0.2}{0.1}$ = 2
> 
> CO<sub>2</sub> = $\frac{0.1}{0.1}$ = 1
> 
> 5. Apply the values to the unbalanced equation:
> 
> **1**C + **2**ZnO ⟶ **2**Zn + **1**CO<sub>2</sub>
> 
> 6. Simplify:
> 
> C + **2**ZnO ⟶ **2**Zn + CO<sub>2</sub>

> **Exam Hint**
> These questions look hard but they are actually quite easy to do, as long as you follow the steps and organise your work neatly.
> 
> **Remember: **The molar ratio of a balanced equation gives you the ratio of the amounts of each substance in the reaction.
