## Concentration Calculations

#### Amount of substance calculations

- As previously discussed, the amount of substance can be defined as the number of particles in a substance, *n*, measured in moles (often abbreviated to mol)
- In reality, amount of substance is used as a blanket term to cover most chemical calculations, especially those that involve moles
- The two main calculations for amount of substance are:

Moles, *n* = $\frac{mass,m}{molarmass,M}$

Moles, *n* = concentration x volume

- Other common calculations for amount of substance include:

Moles, *n* = $\frac{numberofparticles}{Avogadro'sconstant}$

Moles, *n* =$\frac{PV}{RT}$from the ideal gas equation (*PV = nRT*)

#### Concentration calculations

- Concentration can be defined as the amount of a substance dissolved in a quantity of liquid
- When a mass concentration is calculated, the units are usually g dm<sup>-3</sup>

  - Other units are possible such as g cm<sup>-3</sup>, kg m<sup>-3</sup> and sometimes in medicines you might see them as mg / ml
  - The equation to calculate mass concentration is:

mass concentration in g dm<sup>3</sup> = $\frac{\mathrm{mass} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{solute} \mathrm{in}g}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{solution} \mathrm{in} \mathrm{dm}^{3}}$

- When a molar concentration is calculated, the units are mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

  - Calculating molar concentrations requires the use of two equations:

number of moles (or amount) = $\frac{\mathrm{mass}}{\mathrm{molar} \mathrm{mass}}$

- Molar mass is the mass per mole of a substance in g mol<sup>-1</sup>

molar concentration in mol dm<sup>-3</sup> = $\frac{\mathrm{number} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{moles}(\mathrm{or} \mathrm{amount})}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{solution} \mathrm{in} \mathrm{dm}^{3}}$

> **Worked Example**
> **Mass concentration calculations**
> 
> 1. What is the mass concentration when 6.34 g of sodium chloride is dissolved into a 0.250 dm<sup>3</sup> solution?
> 2. A sodium carbonate solution has a mass concentration of 5.2 g dm<sup>-3</sup>. What is the volume of the solution made when 250 g of sodium carbonate is used?
> 3. The mass concentration of a solution is 26.7 g dm<sup>-3</sup>. What is the mass of sodium bromide in 500 cm<sup>3</sup> of solution?
> 
> **Answer 1**
> 
> - Mass concentration $=\frac{\mathrm{mass}(g)}{\mathrm{volume}(\mathrm{dm}^{3})}=\frac{6.34}{0.250}=$25.4 g dm<sup>-3</sup> (to 3 s.f.)
> 
> **   Answer 2**
> 
> - Volume of solution $=\frac{\mathrm{mass}(g)}{\mathrm{mass} \mathrm{concentration}(g\mathrm{dm}^{3})}=\frac{250}{5.2}=$48 dm<sup>3</sup>
> 
>   - This answer should be given to 2 s.f. as there is a value in the question with only 2 significant figures
> 
> **   Answer 3**
> 
> - Mass = mass concentration (g dm<sup>-3</sup>) x volume of solution (dm<sup>3</sup>) = 26.7 x 0.5 = 13.4 g (to 3 s.f.)
> 
>   - The 500 cm<sup>3</sup> in the question has to be converted into dm<sup>3</sup>
>   - $\frac{500}{1000}$ = 0.5 dm<sup>3</sup>

> **Worked Example**
> **Molar concentration calculations**
> 
> 1. What is the molar concentration when 6.34 g of sodium chloride is dissolved into a 250 cm<sup>3</sup> solution?
> 2. A sodium carbonate solution has a molar concentration of 1.25 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>. What is the volume of the solution made when 250 g of sodium carbonate is used?
> 3. The molar concentration of a sodium bromide solution is 0.250 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>. What is the mass of sodium bromide in 500 cm<sup>3</sup> of this solution?
> 
> **Answer 1**
> 
> - Number of moles of NaCl $=\frac{\mathrm{mass}}{\mathrm{molar} \mathrm{mass}}=\frac{6.34}{(23.0+35.5)}=$0.1084 moles
> 
>   - Molar concentration in mol dm<sup>-3</sup> $=\frac{\mathrm{number} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{moles}(\mathrm{or} \mathrm{amount})}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{solution} \mathrm{in} \mathrm{dm}^{3}}=\frac{0.1084}{0.250}=$0.434 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> **   Answer 2**
> 
> - Number of moles of Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> $=\frac{\mathrm{mass}}{\mathrm{molar} \mathrm{mass}}=\frac{250}{(23.0\times2)+12.0+(16.0\times3)}=$2.358 moles
> 
>   - Volume of solution in dm<sup>3</sup>
> 
> $=\frac{\mathrm{number} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{moles}(\mathrm{or} \mathrm{amount})}{\mathrm{Molar} \mathrm{concentration} \mathrm{in} \mathrm{mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}}=\frac{2.358}{1.25}=$1.89 dm<sup>3</sup>
> 
> **   Answer 3**
> 
> - Number of moles of NaBr = molar concentration x volume of solution
> 
> = 0.250 x 0.500 = 0.125 moles
> 
> - Mass of NaBr = number of moles of NaBr x molar mass
> 
> = 0.125 x (23.0 + 79.9) = 12.9 g

#### Parts per million

- When expressing extremely low concentrations a unit that can be used is **parts per million** or **ppm**
- This is useful when giving the concentration of a pollutant in water or the air when the absolute amount is tiny compared the the volume of water or air
- **1 ppm** is defined as

  - A mass of **1 mg** dissolved in **1 dm**<sup>**3**</sup> of water
- Since 1 dm<sup>3</sup> weighs 1 kg we can also say it is

  - A mass of **1 mg** dissolved in **1 kg** of water, or 10<sup>-3</sup> g in 10<sup>3</sup> g which is the same as saying the concentration is **1 in 10**<sup>**6 **</sup>or **1 in a million**

> **Worked Example**
> The concentration of chlorine in a swimming pool should between between 1 and 3 ppm. Calculate the maximum mass, in kg, of chlorine that should be present in an olympic swimming pool of size 2.5 million litres.
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1**: calculate the total mass in mg assuming 3ppm(1 litre is the same as 1 dm<sup>3</sup>)
> 
> - 3 x 2.5 x 10<sup>6 </sup>= 7.5 x 10<sup>6 </sup>mg
> 
> **Step 2**: convert the mass into kilograms (1 mg = 10<sup>-6 </sup>kg)
> 
> - 7.5 x 10<sup>6 </sup> x 10<sup>-6  </sup>kg = **7.5 kg**

#### Atmospheric gas concentration

- The concentration of atmospheric gases, particularly pollutants, can be measured in **parts per million, ppm**
- Instead of using mass, the comparison of gas is done by volume

  - You might see the values quoted in **ppmv** - the v shows that the value relates to concentration by volume
- A concentration of 1 ppmv means that there is 1 cm<sup>3</sup> of a particular gas in 1000000cm<sup>3</sup> or 1000 dm<sup>3</sup>
- The equation to calculate the concentration of a gas in ppm is:

$\mathrm{concentration} \mathrm{in} \mathrm{ppm}=\frac{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{gas}\times1000000}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{air}}$

- The volumes can be given in any units but they must be the same units, otherwise one of them will need to be converted

> **Worked Example**
> **Atmospheric gas concentration calculations**
> 
> Calculate the concentration, in ppm, of the following:
> 
> 1. A volume of 2.5 dm<sup>3</sup> of carbon dioxide in 10000 dm<sup>3</sup> of air
> 2. A volume of 2.5 dm<sup>3</sup> of sulfur dioxide in 4000 dm<sup>3</sup> of air
> 3. A volume of 152 cm<sup>3</sup> of ozone in 112 dm<sup>3</sup> of air
> 
> **  Answer 1**
> 
> - Concentration in ppm
> 
> $=\frac{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{gas}\times1000000}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{air}}=\frac{2.5\times1000000}{10000}=$ 250 ppm
> 
> **   Answer 2**
> 
> - Concentration in ppm
> 
> $=\frac{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{gas}\times1000000}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{air}}=\frac{2.5\times1000000}{4000}=$ 625 ppm
> 
> **   Answer 3**
> 
> A volume of 152 cm<sup>3</sup> of ozone in 112 dm<sup>3</sup> of air
> 
> - Concentration in ppm $=\frac{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{gas}\times1000000}{\mathrm{volume} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{air}}=\frac{0.152\times1000000}{112.0}=$1360 ppm

> **Exam Hint**
> When completing atmospheric gas calculations, the gas involved does not affect the calculation as shown by worked examples 1 and 2
