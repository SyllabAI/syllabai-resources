## Kp Expressions - Deduction

We have seen previously that equilibrium reactions can be quantified by reference to an equilibrium expression and equilibrium constant The **equilibrium expression **links the **equilibrium constant**, ***K***<sub>***c***</sub>, to the **concentrations of reactants and products** at equilibrium taking the **stoichiometry** of the equation into account So, for a given reaction:

**aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD**

*K*<sub>*c*</sub>* *is defined as follows:

![Equilibria Equilibrium Expression, downloadable AS & A Level Chemistry revision notes](../../../assets/095ad550300b-1-7-equilibria-equilibrium-expression.png)

***Equilibrium expression linking the equilibrium concentration of reactants and products at equilibrium***

- **Solids** are ignored in equilibrium expressions
- The *K*<sub>*c*</sub> of a reaction is constant and only changes if the **temperature** of the reaction changes

#### Homogeneous reactions

- In the generic example above, if all the substances are gases, we can show the equation with that state symbol

**aA (g) + bB (g) ⇌ cC (g) + dD (g)**

- We can write a different equilibrium expression in terms of the **partial pressure** of the gases
- This equilibrium constant is called ***K***<sub>***p ***</sub>and is defined as follows

![Kp Expressions, downloadable AS & A Level Chemistry revision notes](../../../assets/bad3d0150b4f-5-3-1-kp-expressions.png)

***Equilibrium expression linking the partial pressures of reactants and products at equilibrium***

#### Heterogeneous reactions

- For heterogenous reactions**, solids** and **liquids** are ignored in *K*<sub>*p *</sub>equilibrium expressions
- The *K*<sub>*p*</sub> of a reaction is constant and only changes if the **temperature** of the reaction changes

## Kp Expressions - Calculations

#### Calculations involving K<sub>p</sub>

- In the equilibrium expression, the *p* represents the partial pressure of the reactants and products in **Pa**
- The **units** of *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *therefore depend on the form of the equilibrium expression

> **Worked Example**
> #### Calculating K<sub>p</sub> of a gaseous reaction
> 
> Sulfur dioxide and oxygen react according to the following equation:
> 
> 2SO<sub>2</sub> (g) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) ⇌ 2SO<sub>3</sub> (g)
> 
> The equilibrium partial pressures at constant temperature are:
> 
> - SO<sub>2</sub> =1.0 x 10<sup>6</sup> Pa
> - O<sub>2</sub> = 7.0 x 10<sup>6</sup> Pa
> - SO<sub>3</sub> = 8.0 x 10<sup>6</sup> Pa
> 
> Calculate the value of *K*<sub>p</sub> for this reaction.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Write the equilibrium constant for the reaction in terms of partial pressures
> 
> $K_{p}=\frac{p^{2}\mathrm{SO}_{3}}{p^{2}\mathrm{SO}_{2}\timespO_{2}}$
> 
> - **Step 2: **Substitute the equilibrium concentrations into the expression
> 
> $K_{p}=\frac{(8.0\times10^{6})^{2}}{(1.0\times10^{6})^{2}\times(7.0\times10^{6})}$
> 
> ***   K***<sub>***p***</sub><sub>** **</sub>**= 9.1 x 10**<sup>**-6**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 3: **Deduce the correct units of *K*<sub>*p*</sub>
> 
> <sub>*   *</sub>$K_{p}=\frac{Pa^{2}}{Pa^{2}\timesPa}$
> 
> The units of *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *are Pa<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *= 9.1 x 10<sup>-6</sup> Pa<sup>-1</sup>

- Some questions only give the **number of moles** of gases present and the total pressure
- The number of moles of each gas should be used to first calculate the **mole fractions**
- The mole fractions are then used to calculate the **partial pressures**
- The values of the partial pressures are then substituted in the **equilibrium expression**

> **Worked Example**
> #### Calculating K<sub>p</sub> of hydrogen iodide equilibrium reaction
> 
> The equilibrium between hydrogen, iodine and hydrogen iodide, at 600 K, is as follows:
> 
> H<sub>2</sub> (g) + I<sub>2</sub> (g) ⇌ 2HI (g)
> 
> At equilibrium, the number of moles present are:
> 
> - H<sub>2</sub> = 1.71 x 10<sup>-3</sup>
> - I<sub>2</sub> = 2.91 x 10<sup>-3</sup>
> - HI = 1.65 x 10<sup>-2</sup>
> 
> The total pressure is 100 kPa. Calculate the value of *K*<sub>p</sub> for this reaction.
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> - **Step 1: **Calculate the total number of moles
> 
> **Total number of moles = 1.71 x 10**<sup>**-3 **</sup>**+ 2.91 x 10**<sup>**-3 **</sup>**+ 1.65 x 10**<sup>**-2**</sup>
> 
> **Total number of moles = 2.112 x 10**<sup>**-2**</sup>
> 
> - **Step 2: **Calculate the mole fraction of each gas
> 
> $H_{2}=\frac{1.71\times10^{-3}}{2.112\times10^{-2}}=0.0810\,I_{2}=\frac{2.91\times10^{-3}}{2.112\times10^{-2}}=0.1378\,\mathrm{HI}=\frac{1.65\times10^{-2}}{2.112\times10^{-2}}=0.7813$
> 
> - **Step 3: **Calculate the partial pressure of each gas
> 
> **H**<sub>**2**</sub>** = 0.0810 x 100 = 8.10 kPa**
> 
> **I**<sub>**2**</sub>** = 0.1378 x 100 = 13.78 kPa**
> 
> **HI = 0.7813 x 100 = 78.13 kPa**
> 
> - **Step 4: **Write the equilibrium constant in terms of partial pressure
> 
> $K_{p}=\frac{p^{2}\mathrm{HI}}{pH_{2}\timespI_{2}}$
> 
> - **Step 5: **Substitute the values into the equilibrium expression
> 
> $K_{p}=\frac{78.13^{2}}{8.10\times13.78}$
> 
> ***   K***<sub>***p***</sub><sub>** **</sub>**= 54.7**
> 
> - **Step 6: **Deduce the correct units for *K*<sub>*p*</sub>
> 
> *   *$K_{p}=\frac{Pa^{2}}{Pa\timesPa}$
> 
> All units cancel out
> 
> Therefore, *K*<sub>*p*</sub>* *= 54.7

- Other questions related to equilibrium expressions may involve calculating quantities present at equilibrium given appropriate data
