# On & Off the job Training

## On-the-job training methods

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_F592ZMk3FY6WYY4K` · Importance of training to a business and workers: 
different types of training and the main purpose:
• on-the-job 
• off-the-job

## On-the-job training methods

- **On-the-job training** takes place **while employees are working** in their job roles

  - Employees **learn skills and knowledge** from colleagues while performing their job duties
  - For example, a sous chef at The Ivy Restaurant in York receives **on-the-job training **from the Head Chef to learn how to prepare new dishes, **use equipment** or improve cooking techniques

#### Advantages and disadvantages of on-the-job training

| **Advantages** | **Disadvantages** |
|---|---|
| - Employees learn new skills and knowledge while performing their job duties<br>- **Training is tailored** to the employee's specific job role and responsibilities<br>- Training is often **practical and relevant** to the employee's job duties<br>- **Can be cost-effective** as it takes place during working hours | - Employees may make mistakes while learning or trainers could pass on bad habits which may **impact productivity and quality**<br>- Can be disruptive to the workplace as it **requires the trainer to devote time** to training the employee<br>- May not be effective in all cases, leading to **employee dissatisfaction** and higher turnover rates |

## Off-the-job training methods

- **Off-the-job training** takes place away from or **outside of the workplace**
- It can be in the form of workshops, seminars, conferences or online courses

  - For example, teachers attend exam board **training days, **where they learn how to deliver the syllabus and help students to prepare for exams

#### Advantages and disadvantages of off-the-job training

| **Advantages** | **Disadvantages** |
|---|---|
| - Employees learn new skills and knowledge outside of the workplace, which can **bring fresh ideas and perspectives** to the workplace<br>- **Training can be tailored** to the employee's specific needs and interests<br>- Training can be **used as a reward** or incentive for high-performing employees<br>- **Can be cost-effective** if training is provided online or through webinars | - Can be expensive to organise, especially if** travel and accommodation** are required<br>- Employees may** miss work** while attending training, which can impact productivity<br>- The training **may not be directly applicable** to the employee's job role or the needs of the firm |

> **Exam Hint**
> You may need to evaluate a particular type of training or recommend one to a business
> 
> Think about the nature of the role and whether on or off-the-job training would be the most appropriate.
> 
> E.g. On-the-job training may be suitable for a hotel porter who has many hands on aspects to the role and needs to learn quickly. In comparison, employees that need training in a new software may need training sessions from an expert so show step by step how to use it in their role
