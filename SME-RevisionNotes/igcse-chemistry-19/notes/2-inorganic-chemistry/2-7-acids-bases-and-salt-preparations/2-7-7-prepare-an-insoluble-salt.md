# Prepare an Insoluble Salt

## Prepare an Insoluble Salt

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_CVjbGJzh8gK3DxHZ` · describe an experiment to prepare a pure, dry sample of an insoluble salt, starting from two soluble reactants

## Prepare an insoluble salt

- Insoluble salts can be prepared using a ***precipitation reaction***
- The solid salt obtained is the precipitate, thus in order to successfully use this method the solid salt being formed must be insoluble in water
- The preparation of an insoluble salt follows this pattern:

**soluble salt 1 + soluble salt 2 ⟶  insoluble salt + soluble salt 3**

**AB + CD ⟶ AD + CB**

- The method involves measuring out a fixed volume of one salt solution and then adding the second salt solution until it is in a slight excess

  - This ensures the maximum amount of precipitate will be obtained
- The precipitate is recovered by filtration

  - Then it must be washed with distilled water remove reactants that are contaminating the residue (recovered solid)
  - It is then left to dry

- This method is a good way to [prepare silver and lead(II) salts](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/chemistry/edexcel/19/revision-notes/2-inorganic-chemistry/2-7-acids-bases--salt-preparations/2-7-9-practical-prepare-leadiisulfate/) which are often insoluble; the starting material will usually be the nitrate of silver or lead(II) since all nitrates are soluble

> **Exam Hint**
> This reaction is also known as a double decomposition reaction.
