## Powers & roots

### What are powers (indices)?

- **Powers (or indices)** are the small 'floating' values that are used when a number is multiplied by itself repeatedly

  - 6<sup>1</sup> means 6
  - 6<sup>2</sup> means 6 × 6
  - 6<sup>3</sup> means 6 × 6 × 6
- The big number at the **bottom** is called the **base**
- The small number that is **raised** is called the **index, power,** or **exponent**
- Any non-zero number to the **power of 0** is **equal to 1**

  - 3<sup>0</sup> = 1
- Any number to the **power of 1** is **equal to itself**

  - 3<sup>1</sup>=3

### What are square roots?

- **Roots** are the **reverse of powers**
- A **square root** of 25 is a number that when squared equals 25

  - The **two square roots** of 25 are 5 and -5
  
    - 5<sup>2</sup> = 25 and (-5)<sup>2</sup> = 25
- Every **positive number** has **two square roots**

  - One is **positive** and one is **negative**
  - Negative numbers do not have a real square root
- The notation $\sqrt{}$  refers to the **positive square root** of a number

  - $\sqrt{25}=5$
  - You can show both roots at once using the **plus or minus symbol **±
  - Square roots of 25 are $\pm\sqrt{25}=\pm5$

### What are cube roots?

- A **cube root** of 125 is a number that when **cubed **equals 125

  - The cube root of 125 is 5
  
    - 5<sup>3</sup> = 125
  - Unlike square roots, each number only has** one cube root**
  - Every **positive** and **negative** **number** has** one real cube root**
  - The notation `cube root of blank` refers to the **cube root** of a number
  
    - `cube root of 125 equals 5`

### What are n<sup>th</sup> roots?

- An ***n*****th root** of a number is a value that when raised to the power *n* equals the original number

  - 3<sup>5</sup>=243 therefore 3 is a 5th root of 243
- If *n* is **even**, there will be a **positive and negative** *n*th root

  - The 6th roots of 64 are 2 and -2
  
    - 2<sup>6</sup> = 64 and (-2)<sup>6</sup> = 64
  - The notation `n-th root of blank` refers to the **positive *****n*****th root** of a number
  
    - `root index 6 of 64 equals 2`
  - Negative numbers do not have a real *n*th root if *n* is even
- If *n* is **odd** then there will only be **one ***n*th root

  - The 5th root of -32 is -2
  
    - (-2)<sup>5</sup> = -32`equation`
  - Every **positive and negative** number will have one real *n*th root

### How do I estimate a root?

- You can **estimate roots** by finding the **closest integer **roots

  - To estimate $\sqrt{20}$
  
    - We know that $\sqrt{16}=4$ and $\sqrt{25}=5$
    - So $\sqrt{20}$ must be between 4 and 5

### What are reciprocals?

- The **reciprocal** of a number is **1 divided** by the number

  - The reciprocal of 2 is $\frac{1}{2}$
- A number and its reciprocal **multiply **together to give **1**
- You **switch **the numerator and denominator of a fraction to find its reciprocal

  - The reciprocal of $\frac{1}{4}$ is 4
  - The reciprocal of $\frac{3}{2}$ is $\frac{2}{3}$
- The **reciprocal** of a number can be written as an **index of -1**

  - 5<sup>-1</sup> is the reciprocal of 5, so $\frac{1}{5}$
- This can be extended to other **negative indices**

  - 5<sup>-2</sup> means the reciprocal of 5<sup>2</sup>, so $\frac{1}{5^{2}}$ or $\frac{1}{25}$

> **Exam Hint**
> If your calculator shows "Math Error" or similar when finding a square root, this is probably because you have accidentally entered a negative number!
