# Price Elasticity of Supply (PES)

## The Definition & Calculation of PES

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_BgdkJ3r82hbNJdBP`

## The Definition & Calculation of PES

- The **law of supply** states that when there is an increase in price (***ceteris paribus***), producers will increase the quantity supplied and vice versa

  - Economists are interested **by how much** the **quantity supplied will increase**

- **Price elasticity of supply** **(PES)** reveals how **responsive** the change in **quantity supplied** is to a change in **price**

  - The responsiveness is different for different types of products

- **PES can be calculated** using the following formula

$\text{PES = } \frac{\% \text{change} \text{in} \text{quantity} supplied}{\% \text{change} \text{in} price} = \frac{\% \Delta \text{in} QS}{\% \Delta \text{in} \mathrm{P} }$

- **To calculate a % change**, use the following formula

$\% \text{Change} = \frac{new \text{value} - \text{old} value}{old value} \times 100$

> **Worked Example**
> In recent months, the **price of avocados** has increased from AU\$ 0.90 to AU\$ 1.45. *Bewdley Farm Shop* in Margaret River has sought to **maximise their profits** by increasing the **quantity supplied **to the market. They have been able to increase sales from 110 units a week to 120 units a week. Calculate the **PES of avocados** and explain one reason for the value
> 
> **Step 1:  Calculate the % change in QS**
> 
> **     **$\% \Delta Q S = \frac{120 - 110}{110} \times 100
> 
> \% \Delta Q S = 9.1 \%$** **
> 
> **Step 2: Calculate the % change in P**
> 
> $\% \Delta \mathrm{P} = \frac{1.45 - 0.90}{0.90} \mathrm{x} 100
> 
> \% \Delta \mathrm{P} = 61 \%$
> 
> **Step 3: Insert the above values in the PES formula**
> 
> $\text{PE} S = \frac{\% \Delta \text{in} Q S}{\% \Delta \text{in} \mathrm{P} } 
> 
> \text{PE} S = \frac{9.1 \%}{61 \%} 
> 
> \text{PE} S = 0.15
>$
> 
> **Step 4: Explain one reason for the value**
> 
> The PES value of 0.15 indicates that **avocados are very price inelastic in supply**. Even with a significant increase in price, suppliers are **unable to supply more**, likely due to the time it takes to grow additional avocados

## Interpreting PES Values

**The Values of PES Vary From 0 To Infinity (∞) & They Are Classified As Follows**

| **Value and Name** | **Explanation** | **Diagram** |
|---|---|---|
| **0**<br><br>**Perfectly Inelastic** | - The QS is **completely unresponsive** to a change in P (e.g. fixed number of seats in a theatre) | ![2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance-of-pes---perfectly-inelastic](assets/16f5968fa6b2-2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance.png) |
| **0 → 1**<br><br>**Relatively Inelastic** | - The %∆ in QS is **less than** proportional to the %∆ in P (e.g agricultural products) | ![2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance-of-pes---relatively-inelastic](assets/9ae9e3eb89b9-2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance.png) |
| **1 → ∞**<br><br>**Relatively Elastic** | - The %∆ in QS is **more than** proportional to the %∆ in P (e.g t-shirts) | ![2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance-of-pes----relatively-elastic](assets/6837fcce21ed-2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance.png) |
| **∞**<br><br>**Perfectly Elastic** | - The %∆ in QS will **fall to zero** with any %∆ in P. However, supply is unlimited at a particular price. This is a very theoretical scenario | ![2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance-of-pes---perfectly-elastic](assets/207e8dc3ebff-2-8-1-calculation-determination-and-significance.png) |
| **1**<br><br>**Unitary Elasticity** | - Any supply curve that starts at **the origin** (e.g. S1, S2 or S3) has a PES value equal to 1.
