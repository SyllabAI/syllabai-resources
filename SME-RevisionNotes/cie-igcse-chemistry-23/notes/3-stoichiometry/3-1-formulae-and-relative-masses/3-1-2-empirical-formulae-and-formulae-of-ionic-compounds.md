# Empirical Formulae & Formulae of Ionic Compounds

## Empirical formulae

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_TM7nqQNY64FxC6QT` · Define the empirical formula of a compound as the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms or ions in a compound

## Empirical formulae

#### Extended tier only

- The **empirical formula **is the simplest whole number ratio of the atoms of each element present in one molecule or formula unit of the compound
- The empirical formula of an organic molecule is often different to its molecular / chemical formula

  - For example, ethanoic acid has the chemical formula CH<sub>3</sub>COOH or C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>O<sub>2</sub> but its empirical formula is CH<sub>2</sub>O
- The molecular / chemical formula of an ionic compound is always its empirical formula

  - For example, sodium chloride has the chemical formula NaCl, which is also its empirical formula

## Deducing formulae of ionic compounds

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_xg7Xrk26Mjgw9Xh4` · Deduce the formula of an ionic compound from the relative numbers of the ions present in a model or a diagrammatic representation or from the charges on the ions

## Deducing formulae of ionic compounds

#### Extended tier only

- Metals and non-metals react together to form [ionic](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/chemistry/cie/23/revision-notes/2-atoms-elements-and-compounds/2-2-ions-and-ionic-bonds/2-2-1-ions-and-ionic-bonds/) compounds

  - Ionic compounds are **not** simple molecules
  - **Remember: **Simple molecules are formed when non-metals react together to form compounds
- Ionic compounds involve the metal losing electrons and the non-metal gaining electrons to form ions

- Some ions that you will be expected to be able to use, because they are stated in the exam specification, include:

  - Hydrogen ions, H<sup>+</sup> - sometimes referred to as protons
  - Group 1 ions, e.g. Li<sup>+</sup>, Na<sup>+</sup>, K<sup>+</sup>
  - Group 7 ions, F<sup>–</sup>, Cl<sup>–</sup>, Br<sup>–</sup>
  - Copper(II) ions, Cu<sup>2+</sup>
  - Iron(II) ions, Fe<sup>2+</sup>
  - Iron(III) ions, Fe<sup>3+</sup>
- There are some polyatomic (containing more than one atom) ions stated in the exam specification:

  - Carbonate ions, CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2–</sup>
  - Sulfate ions, SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup>
  - Hydroxide ions, OH<sup>–</sup>
  - Nitrate ions, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>–</sup>
  - Ammonium ions, NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>

### How to determine the formulae of ionic compounds

- Ionic compounds typically have **no overall charge**

  - This means that the size of any positively charged ion is cancelled by the size of any negatively charged ion
  - **Careful: **This should not be confused with an atom having no overall charge

#### Direct comparison

- The formula of an ionic compound can be determined by directly comparing the charges of the ions:

  - For example, iron(II) sulfate
  
    - The iron(II) ion is Fe<sup>2+</sup>, which means that it has a 2+ or +2 charge
    - The sulfate ion is SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup>, which means that it has a 2– or –2 charge
    - The charges cancel each other out Mathematically, (+2) + (–2) = 0
    - This means that one SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup> ion is needed to cancel the +2 charge on Fe<sup>2+</sup>
    - Therefore, the formula of iron(II) sulfate is FeSO<sub>4</sub>

#### The swap-and-drop method

- When the ions in the ionic compound have **different charges**, it can be easier to use the **swap-and-drop** method

  - **Careful: **If you use this method with ions that have the same charge, then you must give the simplest whole number ratio to get the correct answer
- For example, copper(II) chloride:

  - The copper(II) ion is Cu<sup>2+</sup>, which means that it has a 2+ or +2 charge
  - The chloride ion is Cl<sup>–</sup>, which means that it has a 1– or –1 charge
  - The size of the charge on the copper(II) ion indicates the number of chloride ions needed, and the size of the charge on the chloride ion indicates the number of copper(II) ions needed

#### Determining the formula of copper(II) chloride

![Diagram showing electron transfer in Cu and Cl ions forming CuCl2, with colour-coded charges and arrows indicating electron flow.](../../../assets/66c18e9ad879-26547-httpscdn-savemyexams-comuploads2024025rmsb.png)

***The charges swap from element to element and drop down. The positive and negative signs are removed and there is no need for the number 1.***

- This gives the overall formula of copper(II) chloride as CuCl<sub>2</sub>

> **Worked Example**
> The compound produced in the reaction between iron wool and chlorine contains the ions Fe<sup>3+</sup> and Cl<sup>–</sup>.
> 
> a) Give the formula of this compound.
> 
> b) State the name of this compound.
> 
> **Answers:**
> 
> **Part a)**
> 
> - **Direct comparison method:**
> 
>   - The iron ion is Fe<sup>3+</sup>, which means that it has a 3+ or +3 charge
>   - The chloride ion is Cl<sup>–</sup>, which means that it has a 1– or –1 charge
>   - The charges do not cancel each other out
>   
>     - Mathematically, (+3) + (–1) ≠ 0
>   - Three Cl<sup>–</sup> ions are needed to cancel the +3 charge on Fe<sup>3+</sup>
>   - Therefore, the formula is **FeCl**<sub>**3**</sub>** **
> - **Swap-and-drop method**
> 
> ![Diagram showing the electron transfer in forming iron(III) chloride: from Fe to Cl, resulting in the compound FeCl₃, highlighting changes in charges.](assets/9398774363a5-49761-httpscdn-savemyexams-comuploads202402d5o2d.png)
> 
> - The formula is **FeCl**<sub>**3**</sub>** **
> 
> **Part b)**
> 
> - The metal is iron and the chlorine will change to chloride
> - Therefore the name is **iron chloride**
