## Loci in Argand diagrams

### How do I sketch $\mathrm{Re}z=k$ or $\mathrm{Im}z=k$ on an Argand diagram?

- All complex numbers, $z=x+iy$, that satisfy the equation $\mathrm{Re}z=k$ lie on a **vertical line** with Cartesian equation $x=k$

  - Any complex number along this vertical line will have a **real part **of $k$
- All complex numbers, $z=x+iy$, that satisfy the equation $\mathrm{Im}z=k$ lie on a **horizontal line** with Cartesian equation $y=k$

  - Any complex number along this horizontal line will have an **imaginary part **of $k$
- E.g. The loci $\mathrm{Re}z=4$ and $\mathrm{Im}z=-3$ are represented by the vertical line $x=4$ and the horizontal line $y=-3$

### How do I sketch $\left| z - a \right| = k$ on an Argand diagram?

- All complex numbers, $z$, that satisfy the equation $\left| z \right| = k$ lie on a **circle **of **radius** $k$ about the **origin**

  - E.g. the locus of $\left| z \right| = 10$ is a circle of radius 10, centred at the origin, as every complex number on that circle has a modulus of 10
- For a given complex number, $a$, all complex numbers, $z$, that satisfy the equation $\left| z - a \right| = k$ lie on a **circle** of** radius **$k$ about the **centre **$a$

  - This is because $\left| z - a \right|$ represents the distance between complex numbers $z$ and $a$
  - E.g. the locus of $\left| z - ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) \right| = 10$ is a circle of radius 10 about $(3+4i)$
- Many equations need to be adjusted algebraically into the correct $\left| z - a \right|$ form

  - E.g. to find the centre of the circle $\left| z - 8 \mathrm{i} + 3 \right| = 12$, first rewrite it as $\left| z - ( - 3 + 8 \mathrm{i} ) \right| = 12$, giving the centre as $-3+8i$
  - E.g. to find the centre of the circle $\left| z + \mathrm{i} \right| = 2$, first rewrite it as $\left| z - ( - \mathrm{i} ) \right| = 2$ , giving the centre as $( - \mathrm{i} )$
  - Note that the centre of the circle $\left| z \right| = 5$ is the origin (it can be thought of as `open vertical bar z minus 0 close vertical bar equals 5`)
- In order to sketch correctly, **check** whether the **origin** lies **outside**, **on** or **inside** the circle

  - E.g. for the locus of $\left| z - ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) \right| = 10 ,$ the distance from the centre of the circle, $3+4i$, to the origin is 5 (by Pythagoras), which is **less **than the radius of 10; a sketch must therefore show the origin** inside** the circle
- By knowing the radius and centre of a circle, the Cartesian equation of the circle can be found

  - The circle $\left| z - ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) \right| = 10$ has a radius of 10 and centre of $(3,4)$ in coordinates, so the equation of the circle is $( x - 3 )^{2} + ( y - 4 )^{2} = 100$

### How do I sketch $\left| z - a \right| = \vert z - b \vert$ on an Argand diagram?

- For two given complex numbers, $a$ and $b$, all complex numbers, $z$, that satisfy the equation $\left| z - a \right| = \vert z - b \vert$ lie on the **perpendicular bisector** of $a$ and $b$

  - This is because the distance from $z$ to $a$ must equal the distance from $z$ to $b$
  
    - a condition that is satisfied by all the complex numbers, $z$, on the perpendicular bisector of $a$ and $b$
  - E.g. the locus of $\left| z - 3 + 2 \mathrm{i} \right| = \vert z + 8 \vert$ can be rewritten as $\left| z - ( 3 - 2 \mathrm{i} ) \right| = \left| z - ( - 8 ) \right|$ which is the perpendicular bisector of the points $3-2i$ and $-8$
- A **sketch **of the perpendicular bisector is sufficient, without finding its exact equation (though this could be found using coordinate geometry methods)

### How do I sketch $\mathrm{arg}(z-a)=α$  on an Argand diagram?

- All complex numbers, $z$, that satisfy the equation $\mathrm{arg}z=α$ lie on a **half-line **from the **origin** at an angle of $α$ to the positive real axis

  - Although the half-line starts at the origin, the origin itself ($z=0$) does not satisfy the equation $\mathrm{arg}z=α$  as $\mathrm{arg}0$ is undefined (there is no angle at the origin)
  - To show the** exclusion** of $z=0$ from the locus of $\mathrm{arg}z=α$, a small **open circle** at the origin is used
  - E.g. the locus of $\mathrm{arg}z=\frac{π}{4}$ is a half-line of angle $\frac{π}{4}$ to the positive real axis, starting from the origin, with an open circle at the origin
- For a given complex number, $a$, all complex numbers, $z$, that satisfy the equation $\mathrm{arg}(z-a)=α$ lie on a ***half-line*** from the **point** $a$ at an **angle** of $α$ to the positive real axis, with an open circle to show the **exclusion** of $z=a$

  - E.g. the locus of $\text{arg} ( z - 1 - 5 \mathrm{i} ) = \frac{2 \pi }{3}$ can be rewritten as $\text{arg} ( z - ( 1 + 5 \mathrm{i} ) ) = \frac{2 \pi }{3}$, which is a half-line of angle $\frac{2π}{3}$measured from the point $1+5i$, with an open circle at $1+5i$ to show its exclusion
- In some cases, the **equation** of the half-line can be found using a** sketch** to help

  - E.g. the locus of $\mathrm{arg}z=\frac{π}{4}$is the half-line $y=x$ for $x>0$
  - E.g. the locus of $\text{arg} ( z - ( 8 + 5 \mathrm{i} ) = - \frac{\pi }{4}$ can be thought of, in coordinate geometry, as the half-line through $(8,5)$with gradient -1, giving $y=-x+13$  for  $x>8$
  - Whilst **not examinable**, the half-line equation for a more general angle, $\mathrm{arg}z=α$, is $y = ( \tan \\alpha ) x$ for $x>0$, as the $\mathrm{gradient}=\frac{\mathrm{opposite}}{\mathrm{adjacent}}=\mathrm{tan}α$
