# Motivation Theories: Herzberg, McClelland and Vroom

## Herzberg's Two-Factor theory

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_M8ByqhVGYsRdykNs`

## Herzberg's Two-Factor theory

- **Herzberg's theory** suggests that two factors  affect employee motivation and job satisfaction

  - **Hygiene factors**
  
    - Elements that do not necessarily lead to job satisfaction, but their absence can cause dissatisfaction
  - **Motivators**
  
    - Elements that lead to job satisfaction and motivation

#### Herzberg's Two Factor theory

![Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory diagram showing job dissatisfaction linked to hygiene factors and job satisfaction linked to motivator factors.](../../../assets/3ceaa1c8d154-32572-herzbergs-two-factor-theory.bin)

*The lack of hygiene factors causes dissatisfaction while addressing motivators increases satisfaction*

### How businesses use hygiene factors to decrease dissatisfaction

- **Pay fair wages and salaries**

  - If an employee is not paid a fair rate for their work, they may become dissatisfied
- **Offer excellent working conditions**

  - If the workplace is dirty, unsafe, or uncomfortable, employees may become demotivated
  - E.g. **Google**'s workplaces include gourmet restaurants, laundry services and dog care
- **Offer employment contracts which provide job security**

  - If employees feel that their job is not secure, they may become anxious and contribute less to business goals

### How businesses use motivating factors to increase satisfaction

- **Build a recognition and rewards culture**

  - When employees are recognised and rewarded for their hard work, they are motivated to perform well
- **Offer opportunities for growth and development**

  - When employees are given opportunities to learn new skills and advance in their careers, they are motivated to continue working for the business
- **Provide challenging work that requires problem-solving.**

  - When employees are given challenging work that allows them to use their skills and abilities, they are motivated to continue performing well

## McClelland’s Acquired Needs theory

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_K7CbDChdkWtD5GBS`

## McClelland’s Acquired Needs theory

- McClelland's **Acquired Needs theory **focuses on **three key needs** that individuals develop over time and how these needs influence their actions and choices in the workplace and in life

#### Summary of McLelland's Acquired Needs

| **Type of need** | **Achievement need (nAch)** | **Affiliation need (nAff)** | **Power need (nPow)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Motivations** | - Desire to excel and achieve challenging tasks<br>- Setting & achieving personal goals | - Forming positive relationships<br>- Being happy within the workplace | - Desire to influence & control others or situations<br>- Bring out the best in others in teams |
| **Behaviours** | - Seek success and take calculated risks<br>- Appreciate feedback on their performance<br>- Enjoy tasks that require problem-solving<br>- Driven by a sense of accomplishment | - Seek approval, acceptance and social interaction<br>- Avoid conflict<br>- Prioritise harmony in their interactions<br>- Desire for cooperation and *collaboration* | - Seek leadership positions<br>- Enjoy being in control of their environment<br><br>- McClelland identified **two types of power needs:**<br><br>  - **Personal power** is the desire to control others<br>  - **Institutional power **is the desire to influence and shape organisational systems |

- The relative strength of these needs **varies among individuals**
- They can be developed over time through **life experiences**, **upbringing **and ***cultural influences***
- Understanding individuals' dominant need can **help employers tailor their approach to employee motivation** and management

### Strengths and weaknesses of McLelland's Acquired Needs theory

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| - The model is **easy to understand and apply**<br><br>  - Helps managers to motivate employees in various situations<br>- It recognises that people have **varying needs and motivations**<br><br>  - Managers can create more effective motivational strategies<br>- It is based on **extensive research**<br><br>  - McClelland's research was conducted in various contexts so it can be applied in different settings | - Does not account for the **complexity of human behaviour**<br><br>  - Can be influenced by a wide range of personal and environmental factors<br>- The model may **oversimplify** workers' motivations<br><br>  - People often have a combination of the three needs<br>- **Limited evidence** supports the specific needs identified in the theory<br><br>  - Achievement, affiliation and power are not universally accepted as the main drivers of human behaviour |

## Vroom's Expectancy theory

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_JjG2J79DxS3NVRfV`

## Vroom's Expectancy theory

- Vroom's Expectancy theory seeks to explain **why people make choices and behave in specific ways**

  - It assumes that people will choose to **maximise pleasure **and **minimise pain** and will behave in a way that results in the **best outcome or reward**
- It suggests that motivation depends on these three factors working together

  - **Expectancy **is the belief that effort leads to performance
  - **Instrumentality** is the belief that performance leads to outcomes
  - **Valence** is the value or desirability of those outcomes

#### Vrooms Expectancy theory

![Person climbing steps labelled Expectancy, Instrumentality, and Valence towards a trophy, representing progression or achievement.](../../../assets/51b0b8726cf0-33955-expectancy-theory.bin)

* Vroom suggests that motivation depends on the three factors of expectancy, instrumentality and valence *

| **Expectancy** | **Instrumentality** | **Valence** |
|---|---|---|
| - **Effort will lead to successful performance**<br><br>  - If a worker believes that hard work will result in achieving a specific goal, they are likely to be motivated to put in that effort | - **Successful performance will be rewarded**<br><br>  - If a worker is confident that hard work will lead to a desirable outcome or reward, they are more likely to be motivated to perform well | - **The potential outcome is desirable**<br><br>  - The more valuable or appealing the outcome, the more motivated workers are to achieve it |

- Where low motivation is identified, managers may **take steps to improve one or more of the components for individuals**
- These steps may include

  - **Improve training** and development to increase the expectancy score
  - **Increase trust **between management and workers by ensuring that promises are kept to increase the instrumentality score
  - Increase the **range of rewards** available for good performance to improve the valence score

### Strengths and weaknesses of Vroom's Expectancy theory

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| - The theory takes into account **personal differences** and considers them when explaining motivation<br>- A clear and logical framework for understanding motivation by** breaking it down into three key components**<br>- It emphasises the **importance of involving employees** in the goal-setting process | - The theory is based on ***subjective**** *perceptions and beliefs so it may not always accurately predict behaviour<br>- Individuals' **perceptions **and **values can vary**<br>- It provides a **framework** for understanding motivation **rather than making precise predictions** |

> **Exam Hint**
> Case studies often contain clues to the motivational approach adopted by a business. Consider how much or how little control over their working patterns employees have. Weigh up which needs are - or are not being met. Look for indications of how challenging or engaging the work is likely to be.
