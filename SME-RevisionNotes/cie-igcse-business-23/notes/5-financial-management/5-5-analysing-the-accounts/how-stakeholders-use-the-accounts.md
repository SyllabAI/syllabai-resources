# How Stakeholders Use the Accounts

## Using financial accounts to make decisions

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_nmmJt59TXVGyCHfk` · "• Needs of different users of accounts and ratio analysis
• How users of accounts and ratio results might use information to help make decisions, e.g. whether to lend to or invest in the business"

## Using financial accounts to make decisions

- The financial accounts of a ***limited liability*** business need to be submitted to Companies House each year

  - ***Public Limited Companies*** need to have their accounts ***audited*** before they publish them
- **Different stakeholders** use the business accounts for different purposes

#### Stakeholder use of the financial accounts

| **Stakeholder** | **How they use the statement of financial position** | **How they use the income statement** |
|---|---|---|
| **Investors and shareholders** | - Used to identify the **asset structure **of the business and how their investment has been put to use<br>- Used to calculate the **working capital** of the business and determine its solvency<br>- Used to determine the rough **value of a business,** which helps make a judgement on **whether their investment is growing** | - Interested in revenues, costs and profits earned, business growth and **dividend** payments<br>- Shareholders may use **ratio analysis** tools to identify profit margins and returns on investment |
| **Management** | - Used to identify the **financial position** of the business at a given point in time<br>- It is useful to assess the **working capital** position of the business and determine if there are enough liquid assets **to pay its bills**<br>- Provides information on the **capital structure **of the business which helps guide decisions on whether to raise further funds through **borrowing or via other means** (e.g. share issue) | - Interested in key performance data **such as an improvement in sales revenue and net profit**<br><br>  - This data can aid in business decision-making<br>  - Financial data can provide evidence to support the payment of bonuses |
| **Lenders and creditors** | - Look at the balance sheet to evaluate the company's ***solvency***** **<br>- They are interested in the company's **ability to pay back its debts** | - Consider **profit generated,** as this could indicate the stability of the business and **level of risk** it presents |
| **Suppliers** | - Businesses with **low levels of working capital** may find it difficult to pay short-term debts and so suppliers **may offer trade credit, but with stricter terms** | - Interested in the **continued success of the company**<br>- This information is also used by suppliers to determine the level of **trade credit** offered to businesses |
| **Employees** | - Used to answer questions such as:<br><br>  - Is the business financially stable or are jobs at risk?<br>  - Has the businesses performance improved or worsened?<br>  - What is the business spending its money on?<br>  - How much are senior executives paid?<br>  - How much tax is the business paying? | - Interested in profits earned, the **potential for wage increases,** and job stability<br>- Employees may look at **notes to the accounts** that detail levels of executive pay |
| **Regulatory bodies and tax authorities** | - Regulatory bodies use financial statements to ensure a business is **complying with accounting standards and regulations** | - Tax authorities use financial statements to check the accuracy of tax returns and assess **how much tax a business is liable to pay**<br>- The Income Statement can provide an insight into whether the business will** continue to provide employment**, place orders with other businesses and supply goods and services to the public sector |
| **Local community** | - Interested in the **stability **of the business and what this may mean for jobs in the community | - Another interest is to see if the firm is generating enough profit to perhaps approach them for local sponsorship |
