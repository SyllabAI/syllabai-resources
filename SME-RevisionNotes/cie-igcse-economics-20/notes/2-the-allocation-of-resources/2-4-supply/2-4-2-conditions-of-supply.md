# Conditions of Supply

## Shifts of the Supply Curve

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_x668fBxfB2zFg4jx` · A supply curve to be drawn and used to illustrate movements along a supply curve with appropriate terminology, for example extensions and contractions in supply.

## Shifts of the Supply Curve

- There are several factors that will **change the supply** of a good/service, **irrespective of the price level**. Collectively these factors are called the **conditions of supply**
- Changes to any of the conditions of supply **shifts the entire supply curve** (as opposed to a movement along the supply curve)

![Graph showing supply curves shifting. Initial curve S shifts left to S1 at reduced quantity and right to S2 at increased quantity, price remains at £7.](../../../assets/4afebd254b89-19171-1-2-4-cie-igcse-economics-shifts-in-the-su.png)

*A graph that shows how changes to any of the conditions of supply shifts the entire supply curve left or right, irrespective of the price level*

- For example, if a firm's cost of production increases due to the increase in price of a key resource, then there will be a** decrease in supply** as the firm can now only afford to produce fewer products

  - This is a **shift in supply **from S to S<sub>1</sub>. The price remains unchanged at £7 but the **supply has decreased** from 10 to 2 units

**How Each of the Conditions of Supply Shifts the Entire Supply Curve**

| **Costs of Production (COP)** |   |
|---|---|
| - If the price of **raw materials** or other **costs of production** change, firms respond by changing supply. | - **COP increases**<br><br>  - S decreases<br>  <br>    - Shifting left<br>    - (S→S<sub>1</sub>)<br>- **COP decreases**<br><br>  - S increases<br>  <br>    - Shifting right<br>    - (S→S<sub>2</sub>) |
| **Indirect Taxes** |   |
| - Any changes to ***indirect taxes*** change the **cost of production **for a firm and impact supply. | - **Taxes increase**<br><br>  - S decreases<br>  <br>    - Shifting left<br>    - (S→S<sub>1</sub>)<br>- **Taxes decrease**<br><br>  - ** **S increases<br>  <br>    - Shifting right<br>    - (S→S<sub>2</sub>) |
| **Subsidies** |   |
| - Changes to ***producer subsidies*** directly impact the **cost of production** for the firm. | - **Subsidy increases**<br><br>  - S increases<br>  <br>    - Shifting right<br>    - (S→S<sub>2</sub>)<br>- **Subsidy decreases**<br><br>  - S decreases<br>  <br>    - Shifting left<br>    - (S→S<sub>1</sub>) |
| **New Technology** |   |
| - New technology increases ***productivity*** and **lowers costs** of production.<br>- **Ageing technology** can have the opposite effect. | - **Technology increases**<br><br>  - S increases<br>  <br>    - Shifting right<br>    - (S→S<sub>2</sub>)<br>- **Technology decreases**<br><br>  - S decreases<br>  <br>    - shifting left<br>    - (S→S<sub>1</sub>) |
| **Change in the number of firms in the industry** |   |
| - The **entry and exit** of firms into the market has a direct impact on the supply.<br>- If ten new firms start selling building materials in Hanoi, the supply of building material will **increase.** | - **No. of firms increases**<br><br>  - S increases<br>  <br>    - Shifting right<br>    - (S→S<sub>2</sub>)<br>- **No. of firms decreases**<br><br>  - S decreases<br>  <br>    - Shifting left<br>    - (S→S<sub>1</sub>) |
| **Weather Events** |   |
| - Droughts or flooding can cause a **supply shock** in agricultural markets.<br>- A drought will cause **supply to decrease**.<br>- Unexpectedly good growing conditions can cause supply to increase. | - **Drought**<br><br>  - S decreases<br>  <br>    - Shifting left<br>    - (S→S<sub>1</sub>)<br>- **Good weather**<br><br>  - S increases<br>  <br>    - Shifting right<br>    - (S→S<sub>2</sub>) |

> **Exam Hint**
> Several of the **conditions of supply** change the costs of production. However, be sure to explain each condition as its own point before linking it to the **cost of production** (for example, a change in indirect taxation).
> 
> A common error by students is to explain that a **subsidy** (for example, £3,000 subsidy for each electric vehicle produced) shifts the demand curve for electric vehicles to the right. This is incorrect. The subsidy will shift the **supply curve** to the right. Then due to the lower price, there will be a **movement along the demand curve** (extension of quantity demanded) to create a new market equilibrium.
