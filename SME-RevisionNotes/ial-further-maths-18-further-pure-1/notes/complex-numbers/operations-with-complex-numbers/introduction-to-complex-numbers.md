## Cartesian Form of Complex Numbers

#### What is an imaginary number?

- Equations like $x^{2}=-9$ have **no real **solutions

  - **Squaring** real numbers always gives a **positive **value
  
    - No real number squared could give  -9
  - $x=\pm3$ are real numbers, but **neither** work
  
    - They give +9
- To get round this, mathematicians introduce the **imaginary number**, $i$, as follows:

  - $i^{2}=-1$
  
    - This can be thought of as $i=\sqrt{-1}$
- Rules for **surds** and **indices **can be used

  - $x^{2}=-9$ means $x^{2 } = 9 \times ( - 1 ) = 9 \mathrm{i}^{2}$
  
    - The** imaginary solutions** are $x=\pm3i$

#### What is a complex number?

- **Complex numbers **have both a **real part **and an** imaginary part**

  - For example, $3+4i$
  
    - The real part is 3
    - The imaginary part is 4
- This is called **Cartesian form**
- In general, Cartesian form is written using the **notation**:

  - $z=x+yi$
  - $\text{Re} ( z ) = x$
  - $\text{Im} ( z ) = y$

#### How are real numbers and complex numbers related?

- The letter $ℂ$ stands for all complex numbers

  - $z\inℂ$
- Complex numbers with **no imaginary parts** are **real numbers**

  - The **real **numbers, $ℝ$, are a **subset** of the **complex** numbers, $ℂ$
  
    - $ℝ\subsetℂ$
- In Cartesian form $z=x+yi\inℂ$

  - $x\inℝ$
  - $y\inℝ$ as $y$  itself takes real values
  
    - Multiplying it by $i$ makes it imaginary: $yi$
- Complex numbers with **no real parts** are called **imaginary numbers**

#### How do I add or subtract complex numbers?

- **Add** or **subtract** their** real** parts and** imaginary** parts **separately**

  - $\begin{gathered} ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) + ( 2 + 8 \mathrm{i} ) & = ( 3 + 2 ) + ( 4 + 8 ) \mathrm{i} = 5 + 12 \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$
  - $\begin{gathered} ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) - ( 2 + 8 \mathrm{i} ) & = ( 3 - 2 ) + ( 4 - 8 ) \mathrm{i} = 1 - 4 \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$

#### How do I multiply or divide complex numbers by real numbers?

- **Multiply** or **divide** their** real** parts and** imaginary** parts **separately**

  - $\begin{gathered} 10 ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) & = 30 + 40 \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$
  - $\begin{gathered} ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) \div 10 & = 0.3 + 0.4 \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$
  
    - This can also be written $\begin{gathered} \frac{1}{10} ( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} ) & = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{4}{10} \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$

> **Exam Hint**
> - Avoid these handwriting misinterpretations when writing $i$ in the exam:
> 
>   - $2\sqrt{5}i$ can look like $2\sqrt{5i}$
>   
>     - Alternatives are $( 2 \sqrt{5} ) \mathrm{i}$ or $2i\sqrt{5}$
>   - $\frac{3}{2}i$ can look like $\frac{3}{2i}$
>   
>     - An alternative is $\frac{3i}{2}$

> **Worked Example**
> Two complex numbers are given by $z_{1}=p+2i$ and $z_{2}=-7+qi$, where $p$ and $q$ are real.
> 
> Given that $z_{1}+2z_{2}=4-8i$, find $p$ and $q$.
> 
> > *Substitute the complex numbers into the left-hand side*
> 
> $( p + 2 \mathrm{i} ) + 2 ( - 7 + q \mathrm{i} )$
> 
> > *Expand the brackets and collect real and imaginary terms*
> 
> $\begin{gathered} = p + 2 \mathrm{i} - 14 + 2 q \mathrm{i} \\ = ( \mathrm{p} - 14 ) + ( 2 + 2 q ) \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$
> 
> > *Compare this to the right-hand side*
> *Set the real parts equal to each other and solve*
> 
> $\begin{gathered} p - 14 & = 4 \\ p = 18 \end{gathered}$
> 
> > *Set the imaginary parts equal to each other and solve*
> 
> $\begin{gathered} 2 + 2 q & = - 8 \\ 2 q & = - 10 \\ q = - 5 \end{gathered}$
> 
> **Final answer:** $p=18$** and **$q=-5$

## Multiplying Complex Numbers

### How do I multiply complex numbers?

- All rules of** expanding brackets** still work

  - You need to remember that $i^{2}=-1$
- For example, $( a + b \mathrm{i} ) ( c + d \mathrm{i} ) = a c + a d \mathrm{i} + b c \mathrm{i} + b d \mathrm{i}^{2}$

  - Use $i^{2}=-1$ in the last term
  
    - $ac+adi+bci-bd$
  - Then group **real **and **imaginary** parts
  
    - **Factorise **out the $i$
    - $a c - b d + ( a d + b c ) \mathrm{i}$
- Note that the **difference between two squares** becomes

  - $( a + b \mathrm{i} ) ( a - b \mathrm{i} ) = a^{2} - b^{2} \mathrm{i}^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}$

### How do I find powers of i?

- Use the fact that $i^{2}=-1$

  - Below are the first few powers of $i$:
  
    - $i^{0}=1$
    - $i^{1}=i$
    - $i^{2}=-1$
    - $i^{3}=-i$ from $\mathrm{i}^{2} \times \mathrm{i} = ( - 1 ) \times \mathrm{i}$
    - $i^{4}=1$ from $( \mathrm{i}^{2} )^{2} = ( - 1 )^{2} = 1$
    - $i^{5}=i$ from $i^{5}=(i^{2})^{2}\timesi=i$
  - The **pattern** above continues
  
    - $i^{6}=-1$
    - $i^{7}=-i$
- Find **higher powers** of $i$ using a **base** of $i^{2}$

  - Remember that **-1 **to an **even **power** **is 1 (or to an **odd** power is -1)
  
    - $\mathrm{i}^{23} = ( \mathrm{i}^{2} )^{11} \times \mathrm{i} = ( - 1 )^{11} \times \mathrm{i} = - \mathrm{i}$

> **Exam Hint**
> Questions that say "show your working clearly" won't accept answers written down from a calculator.

> **Worked Example**
> Showing your working clearly, find and simplify:
> 
> (a)  $( 4 + \mathrm{i} ) ( 2 + 9 \mathrm{i} )$
> 
> > *Expand the brackets*
> 
> $=4\times2+4\times9i+i\times2+i\times9i\,=8+36i+2i+9i^{2}$
> 
> > *Collect the imaginary parts*
> *Use that *$i^{2}=-1$* then collect the real parts*
> 
> $\begin{gathered} = 8 + 38 \mathrm{i} + 9 \times ( - 1 ) \\ = 8 + 38 \mathrm{i} - 9 \end{gathered}$
> 
> $-1+38i$
> 
> (b)  $( 3 - 4 \mathrm{i} )^{2}$
> 
> > *Write using double brackets then expand*
> 
> $= ( 3 - 4 \mathrm{i} ) ( 3 - 4 \mathrm{i} )
> = 9 - 12 \mathrm{i} - 12 \mathrm{i} + 16 \mathrm{i}^{2}$
> 
> > *Collect the imaginary parts*
> *Use that *$i^{2}=-1$* then collect the real parts*
> 
> $\begin{gathered} = 9 - 24 \mathrm{i} + 16 \times ( - 1 ) \\ = 9 - 24 \mathrm{i} - 16 \end{gathered}$
> 
> $\begin{gathered} - 7 - 24 \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$
> 
> (c)  $( - 2 \mathrm{i} )^{11}$
> 
> > *Use index laws to move the power onto the individual terms*
> 
> $( - 2 )^{11} \times \mathrm{i}^{11}$
> 
> > *Work out *$( - 2 )^{11}$
> 
> $( - 2 )^{11} = - 2048$
> 
> > *Work out *$i^{11}$
> *It helps to write it in terms of *$i^{10}$* then *$i^{2}$
> *Use *$i^{2}=-1$
> 
> $\begin{gathered} \mathrm{i}^{11} & = \mathrm{i}^{10} \times \mathrm{i} \\ = ( \mathrm{i}^{2} )^{5} \times \mathrm{i} \\ = ( - 1 )^{5} \times \mathrm{i} \\ = - 1 \times \mathrm{i} \\ = - \mathrm{i} \end{gathered}$
> 
> > *Multiply both parts together*
> *Two minus signs make a plus*
> 
> $- 2048 \times ( - \mathrm{i} )$
> 
> $2048i$

## Complex Conjugates & Division

### What is a complex conjugate?

- If $z=x+yi$ then the **complex conjugate** of $z$ is $z\ast=x-yi$

  - The **sign** of the **imaginary part** changes
- Note that

  - $z+z\ast$ is always **real**
  
    - since $x+yi+x-yi=2x$
  - $z-z\ast$ is always **imaginary**
  
    - since $x + y \mathrm{i} - ( x - y \mathrm{i} ) = 2 y \mathrm{i}$
  - $zz\ast$ is always **real** (and non-negative)
  
    - since $( x + y \mathrm{i} ) ( x - y \mathrm{i} ) = x^{2} - y^{2} \mathrm{i}^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2}$
    - $zz\ast=z\astz$

### How do I divide complex numbers?

- To **divide** $z_{1}$ by $z_{2}$, **multiply top and bottom** of $\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}$ by $z_{2}\ast$

  - $z_{2}\ast$ is the **complex conjugate **of the **denominator**
- This makes the denominator a **real **number

  - which allows you to write the final answer in Cartesian form, $x+yi$
- The process is called **realising the denominator**

  - It is a very similar to rationalising the denominator with surds
- For example, to work out $\frac{50+75i}{3+4i}$

  - calculate $\frac{( 50 + 75 \mathrm{i} )}{( 3 + 4 \mathrm{i} )} \times \frac{( 3 - 4 \mathrm{i} )}{( 3 - 4 \mathrm{i} )}$
  
    - It helps to write the **brackets** in
  - then **expand** and **simplify**

> **Exam Hint**
> To check your answer in an exam, multiply it by the denominator and see if you get the numerator.

> **Worked Example**
> Let $z_{1}=1+7i$ and $z_{2}=3-i$.
> 
> Find and simplify  $\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}$, giving your answer in the form $x+yi$ where $x$ and $y$ are real numbers.
> 
> > *Find the complex conjugate of the denominator*
> 
> $z_{2}\ast=3+i$
> 
> > *Multiply the top and bottom of *$\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}$* by *$z_{2}\ast$
> 
> $\frac{( 1 + 7 \mathrm{i} )}{( 3 - \mathrm{i} )} \times \frac{( 3 + \mathrm{i} )}{( 3 + \mathrm{i} )}$
> 
> > *Write as one single fraction then expand top and bottom separately*
> 
> $\frac{( 1 + 7 \mathrm{i} ) ( 3 + \mathrm{i} )}{( 3 - \mathrm{i} ) ( 3 + \mathrm{i} )} = \frac{3 + \mathrm{i} + 21 \mathrm{i} + 7 \mathrm{i}^{2}}{9 - \mathrm{i}^{2}}$
> 
> > *Use that *$i^{2}=-1$*then collect real and imaginary parts*
> 
> $\begin{gathered} = \frac{3 + \mathrm{i} + 21 \mathrm{i} - 7}{9 - ( - 1 )} \\ = \frac{- 4 + 22 \mathrm{i} }{10} \end{gathered}$
> 
> > *To give your answer in the form *$x+yi$*, split the fraction then simplify*
> 
> $-\frac{4}{10}+\frac{22}{10}i$
> 
> $-\frac{2}{5}+\frac{11}{5}i$
> 
> $-0.4+2.2i$** is also accepted**
