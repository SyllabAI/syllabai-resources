# Answering 4-Mark Questions

## How do I answer IGCSE economics 4-mark questions?

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_HgnNWzqytJhJQsqR`

## How do I answer IGCSE economics 4-mark questions?

- To answer IGCSE Economics 4-mark questions, consider the following:

  - 4-mark questions appear in **both Section A and Section B** of Paper 2.
  - The most common command terms used for these questions are:
  
    - Explain
    - Analyse
    - Draw

### 1. Explain

- These questions require **two explanations**, each developed with a **cause → effect** chain

  - Each explanation can score up to **2 marks**:
  
    - **1 mark** for the cause/reason
    - **1 mark** for development
- They test **AO2 analysis**, not evaluation

> **Worked Example**
> **Explain two **causes of an increase in the price elasticity of demand for rice.
> 
> **Model Answer**
> 
> Consumers may have more substitutes available, such as pasta or potatoes **(1)**,
> making them more responsive to a price change in rice **(1).**
> 
> Rice may now account for a smaller proportion of consumer income **(1)**, meaning households can more easily change quantity demanded when price changes **(1)**

> **Exam Hint**
> - Each point must be different
> - Do not write the same idea twice with different wording

### 2. Analyse

- These questions test deeper analytical thinking about **relationships between variables**, **economic behaviour**, or **data patterns**
- They require **two chains of analysis**, each linking cause → effect

  - **1 mark** for the analytical point
  - **1 mark** for development
  - × 2 = **4 marks**

> **Worked Example**
> **Analyse **the relationship between life expectancy and the net migration rate
> 
> **Model Answer**
> 
> Higher life expectancy suggests better healthcare and living standards** (1)**,
> attracting migrants looking for improved welfare **(1)**
> 
> A higher net migration rate increases the working-age population **(1)**,
> creating more tax revenue that can improve healthcare and raise life expectancy **(1)**

> **Exam Hint**
> - Always show a directional relationship: *“as X increases, Y tends to…”*

### 3. Draw

- These questions assess diagram accuracy and understanding
- You must draw a **fully labelled diagram** showing the correct economic change

  - No written explanation is required unless explicitly stated
  - The marks allocation is usually as follows:
  
    - **1 mark** for correct axes
    - **1 mark** for correct original curves
    - **1 mark** for correct shift
    - **1 mark** for new equilibrium

> **Worked Example**
> **Draw **a demand and supply diagram to show the effect of an increase in vegetarianism on the market for tomatoes
>

> *How to score 4 marks for a 'draw' question*
> 
> - **1 mark** for correct axes
> - **1 mark** for correct original curves
> - **1 mark** for rightward shift to **D₂**
> - **1 mark** for new equilibrium **P₂** and quantity **Q₂**

> **Exam Hint**
> - A missing label = a lost mark
> - Neatness is not marked; **accuracy** is – but neatness helps you spot inaccuracies!
