# How to use Detailed Specific Examples in 7 Mark Questions

## What is a detailed specific example?

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_2NpRHTCfWhgm8SF7`

## What is a detailed specific example?

- A **Detailed Specific Example (DSE)** is a real, named **case study** you use to answer certain questions on Paper 1 and Paper 2

  - This is a specific river, country, city, volcano or company, rather than a general description
- The syllabus does **not** name these examples for you: your teacher chooses which ones you study

  - You need to be able to name them precisely and use real detail from them in the exam

### Where are detailed specific examples required?

- There are **16 parts of the specification** across the ten topics that require a detailed specific example

  - Eight on Paper 1 (Physical Geography)
  - Eight on Paper 2 (Human Geography)

#### Paper 1

- **Changing river environments**

  - The causes, impacts and management of a flood for a named river
  - The causes, impacts and management of pollution in a named river

- **Changing coastal environments**

  - Coastal erosion and storm protection for a named country or coastal area
  - The importance, threats to, and protection of a named coral reef

- **Changing ecosystems**

  - Deforestation impacts and management for a named country or tropical rainforest area
- **Tectonic hazards**

  - The causes, impacts, responses and management of an earthquake for a named country or area
  - The causes, impacts, responses and management of an eruption for a named volcano

- **Climate change**

  - The impacts, responses and management of climate change for a named country or region

#### Paper 2

- **Changing populations **

  - The reasons for growth/decline and impacts of a population policy for a named country
  - The push and pull factors of a named international migration (named origin and destination country)
- **Changing towns and cities **

  - The opportunities, challenges and management of rapid urban growth for a named urban area
- **Development**

  - The level and strategies to raise development for a named MIC or LIC
- **Changing economies**

  - The impacts of globalisation and of a named TNC in a named country or area
  - The growth, benefits, problems and management of tourism in a named country or area
- **Resource provision**

  - The factors, problems and strategies around food supply in a named country or area
  - The energy mix, impacts and management of energy supply for a named country
- **The Antarctic ecosystem** is the one exception

  - The syllabus specifically does not require a DSE here, since the whole sub-topic is already based on Antarctica itself

## What is an 'explain' or 'describe' 7 mark question?

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_HGBHf7j3cxbP7CT5`

## What is an 'explain' or 'describe' 7 mark question?

- In CIE IGCSE Geography (0460), there are two types of 7 mark question

  - In** Section A** on each of Paper 1 and 2 there is a 7 mark "Assess the extent to which" question which requires an** evaluative judgement **
  - In** Section B **(Questions 2–4(c)), the 7 mark questions use "**Explain**" or "**Describe**" and only test knowledge
  
    - No judgement is required in these questions
- This guide will explain how to complete the **Section B** 7 mark questions

### Command phrases and assessment objectives

- This question type uses command phrases '**explain**' and '**describe**'
- The question tests:

  - **AO1 (Knowledge and understanding) **

### How are these questions marked?

- This question is **not point-marked,** it is a** levelled response **
- The examiner reads your whole answer and decides which level it best fits, then places it within that level's mark range
- Within a level examiners award:

  - The top mark if your answer **convincingly** meets the level statement
  - The middle mark if it **adequately** meets it
  - The bottom mark if it **just** meets it

#### Level 3 (6-7 marks)

- Uses named example (when appropriate)
- A comprehensive answer with developed statements showing detailed knowledge and understanding which is relevant to the question
- Answer includes specific details about place or process

#### Level 2 (3-5 marks)

- Developed statements showing good knowledge and understanding which is relevant to the question

#### Level 1 (1-2 marks)

- Simple statements with limited detail, showing basic knowledge and understanding which is relevant to the question

#### Level 0 (0 marks)

- No creditable response

## How to use a detailed specific example well

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_ZwMv5bcX3gMG5b9f`

## How to use a detailed specific example well

- **Name it precisely**

  - A real place, not a vague description
  - "Bangladesh" and "the River Ganges/Brahmaputra" score higher than "a poor country" or "a river in Asia"
- **Use specific facts**

  - Figures, dates, place names within the example
  - The mark schemes for these questions specifically reward "specific details about place or process," not general statements that could apply to anywhere

- **Match the example to the question**

  - Know what your named example is meant to illustrate (e.g. is it your flood example, or your pollution example, for that same river?) so you don't just use whichever example you remember best
- Examples from the **2000s onwards** tend to work best, since more recent data and reporting is usually available to draw on

### How to answer an 'in an area you have studied' question

#### Step 1

- Read the question carefully and underline the **command words**

*"*<u>**Explain**</u>** the importance of coral reefs to a coastal area you have studied.**"

- The command word "**explain**" means that you need to say why and/or how and support with relevant evidence

#### Step 2

- Underline the key words.

*"***Explain the **<u>**importance**</u>** of **<u>**coral reefs **</u>**to a coastal **<u>**area you have studied**</u>**.**"

- In this example, you need to focus on why coral reefs are important using your detailed specific example

#### Step 3

- Plan the information you are going to include. This can be a short list of bullet points. For example:

  - Where the coral reef is located
  - Reasons that it is important including:
  
    - Storm protection
    - Ecosystem
    - Reducing erosion
    - Tourism
    - Cultural significance

#### Step 4

- Write your answer

  - To achieve 7 marks, you need to write at least 3 detailed statements
  - Do not make general statements
  - Be specific, for example:

*'*the reef supports tourism by attracting millions of visitors for diving and snorkelling, which contributes over AU\$6 billion (about US\$4 billion) to the Australian economy each year.'*     *

> **Worked Example**
> **Explain the importance of coral reefs to a coastal area you have studied.**
> 
> [7 marks]
> 
> **Answer**
> 
> The Great Barrier Reef, off the coast of northeast Australia, is important for many reasons. Located off the coast of Queensland, Australia, it is the largest coral reef system in the world. **[KU] **The reef system acts as natural walls that absorb wave energy, protecting coastal towns from erosion and storm damage. **[KU]**
> 
> The reef is home to around 9,000 known marine species, so it helps biodiversity. **[KU] **This is important for the fishing industry, as many fish breed and live in the reef, providing jobs and food for local communities. **[KU]**
> 
> The reef has cultural significance, as Indigenous Australian groups like the Yirrganydji and Gurang people **[KU]** have cared for it for thousands of years using their traditional ecological knowledge. **[KU]** The reef's diversity has helped scientists to find medicines to treat diseases like cancer and arthritis. **[KU]**
> 
> In addition, the reef supports tourism by attracting millions of visitors for diving and snorkelling, **[KU]** which contributes over AU\$6 billion (about US\$4 billion) to the Australian economy each year. **[KU]** The reef, through tourism, recreation, and fishing, supports over 64,000 jobs. **[KU]**
> 
> Without the reef, Australia's coast would have less protection, which could potentially result in the loss of many jobs. **[KU]**
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This is a level of response answer. Each point made in the answer does not equal a mark. Marks are allocated against one assessment objective:
> 
> - **AO1 Knowledge and Understanding** **[KU]**
> 
> This is a **'level of response'** answer. Each point made in the answer does not equal a mark.
> 
> - A Level 3 answer will use specific examples that demonstrate detailed, developed knowledge and understanding **[KU]** of the importance of coral reefs in a specified coastal area.
> 
> **Alternative** **content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of a response to this question. Other information that could be used in the answer includes:
> 
> - The coast receives protection from tsunamis.
> - Provides sheltered anchorage and harbours.
> - It maintains marine ecosystems and food chains.

> **Exam Hint**
> Notice this question doesn't use "evaluate" or "assess" — it says "explain," so there's no judgement mark here, and the mark scheme (Table B) rewards knowledge and specific detail, not a balanced argument.
> 
> The biggest difference between a Level 2 and a Level 3 answer is specificity: naming "the Great Barrier Reef" and describing an actual named benefit scores far better than writing generically about "a coral reef." Build a bank of 3–4 real facts for each of your detailed specific examples well before the exam, rather than trying to recall detail under pressure.
