# How to Answer a 5 mark "Evaluate" Question

## How to answer 5-Mark "Evaluate" questions

> **Spec point** — `spcpt_7bbFDxX72qMWq6ts`

## How to answer 5-Mark questions

- In CIE IGCSE Geography (0460), **"Evaluate"**, **"To what extent" or "Assess the extent" **questions appear as the **(b)(ii)** part of **Section B** (Questions 2 and 4)

  - They are found in both Paper 1 and Paper 2 and are worth **5 marks**
- Unlike the longer 7-mark questions on this paper, these are **point-marked, not levels-marked**

  - There is no best-fit judgement made by the examiner
  - You are awarded marks for specific content points, with **one mark specifically reserved for your evaluation or judgement**

### How the marks are awarded

- Up to **4 marks** for relevant points

  - Either four simple points, or fewer points which are developed further (a point plus its development counts as 2 marks)
- **1 mark** is reserved specifically for a stated evaluation or judgement

  - This means you cannot get full marks from a list of facts alone, however accurate they are
  - **The judgement mark only comes from an evaluative statement**

### Structuring your answer

- Give three to four relevant points (strategies, factors, or ideas), briefly developed rather than just named
- Finish your answer with a clear evaluative sentence

  - A word like **"however"**, **"overall"** or **"ultimately"** is often the signal that you have reached your judgement
- Common judgement you may need to make for management/strategy questions include:

  - Which option works best and why
  - What determines success (e.g. level of economic development, available technology or finance),
  - A limitation shared by all the strategies

### Assessment objectives

- These 5 mark questions are used to assess **AO3: Evaluation and decision-making**
- This means you are expected to**:**

  - Evaluating the importance of geographical factors, processes and change
  - Recognise the limitations of data, theories and models
  - Evaluate the impact of different values and attitudes in environmental management; making and justifying decisions
  - Weigh up the options available to decision-makers
  - Evaluating the success of management strategies, including sustainable approaches

> **Worked Example**
> **Evaluate the different ways in which the impacts of tropical storms (cyclones, hurricanes and typhoons) can be managed.**
> 
> [5]
> 
> **Answer using four simple points and a final evaluation/judgement**
> 
> Shelters for people to take refuge in during a storm **[1]** Warning systems (e.g. satellite tracking) to alert people in advance **[1] **Evacuation planning — moving people away from high-risk areas before landfall **[1]** Storm defences built to protect coastal areas. **[1] **However, storms cannot be prevented, only managed. The success of strategies often depends on a country's wealth, as HICs can afford better warning systems and defences than LICs. **[1]**
> 
> **An alternative answer with development of two points rather than four simple points**
> 
> Shelters for people to take refuge in during a storm** [1] t**hese are effective at managing the impacts because they are built to withstand the strong winds and high rainfall**. [d]  **Warning systems (e.g satellite tracking) to alert people ahead of the storm **[1] **which means that they can evacuate to safety or prepare their homes to reduce the impact.** [d] **However, storms cannot be prevented, only managed. The success of strategies often depends on a country's wealth, as HICs can afford better warning systems and defences than LICs. **[1]**
> 
> **Mark Scheme and Guidance**
> 
> - 4 marks for any four relevant strategies, one mark each
> 
> - 1 mark is reserved specifically for evaluation or judgement
> 
> - Fewer, more developed points also work: a point plus its development can earn 2 marks, in any combination that reaches the 4-mark cap for content

> **Exam Hint**
> Do not spend all five minutes listing strategies — the fourth mark for content is easy to reach, but many students lose the fifth mark entirely by never writing a judgement. Build in your evaluation from the start: as you name each strategy, keep asking yourself "but does this actually work, and for whom?" One clear evaluative sentence at the end is enough to secure that mark.
