## Reactions of Acids

### Reactions of acids with metals

- Only metals **above hydrogen** in the reactivity series will react with dilute acids
- The more reactive the metal then the more vigorous the reaction will be
- Metals that are placed high on the reactivity series such as potassium and sodium are very dangerous and react **explosively** with acids
- When acids react with metals they form a** salt** and [hydrogen gas](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/science/edexcel/double-award-modular/24/chemistry-unit-2/revision-notes/inorganic-chemistry/chemical-tests/tests-gases/)
- The general equation is:

**metal + acid ⟶ salt + hydrogen**

- Some examples of metal-acid reactions and their equations are given below:

#### Table of acid-metal reactions

| **Metal** | **Sulfuric acid** | **Hydrochloric acid** |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium | Mg (s) + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq) → MgSO<sub>4</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) | Mg (s) + 2HCl (aq) → MgCl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) |
| Zinc | Zn (s) + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq) → ZnSO<sub>4</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) | Zn (s) + 2HCl (aq) → ZnCl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) |
| Iron | Fe (s) + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq) → FeSO<sub>4</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) | Fe (s) + 2HCl (aq) → FeCl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) |

- In general, we can summarise the reaction of a metal that forms a +2 ion as follows:

#### Acids-Metals Summary Table

| **Acid** | **Name of products** | **Equation for reaction** |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric acid | Metal chloride and hydrogen | M + 2HCl → MCl<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub> |
| Sulfuric acid | Metal sulfate and hydrogen | M + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> → MSO<sub>4</sub> + H<sub>2</sub> |

### Reaction of acids with bases

- When an acid reacts with a [base](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/science/edexcel/double-award-modular/24/chemistry-unit-1/revision-notes/inorganic-chemistry/salt-preparations/bases/), a **neutralisation** reaction occurs
- In all acid-base neutralisation reactions, a **salt** and **water** are produced:

**acid + base ⟶ salt + water**

- The identity of the salt produced depends on the** acid** used and the **positive ions** in the base
- Hydrochloric acid produces** chlorides**, sulfuric acid produces** sulfate** salts and nitric acid produces **nitrates**
- Metal **oxide**s and metal **hydroxides** act as** bases**
- The following are some specific examples of reactions  between acids and metal oxides / hydroxides:

**2HCl + CuO ⟶ CuCl**<sub>**2**</sub>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**

**H**<sub>**2**</sub>**SO**<sub>**4**</sub>** + 2NaOH ⟶ Na**<sub>**2**</sub>**SO**<sub>**4**</sub>** + 2H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**

**HNO**<sub>**3**</sub>** + KOH ⟶ KNO**<sub>**3**</sub>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**

- In general, we can summarise the reaction of metals and bases as follows:

#### Acids and Metals Oxides or Hydroxides Summary Table

| **Acid** | **Name of products** | **Equation for reaction** |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric acid | Metal chloride and water | MOH + HCl ⟶ MCl + H<sub>2</sub>O |
| Sulfuric acid | Metal sulfate and water | MO + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> ⟶ MSO<sub>4</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O |
| Nitric acid | Metal nitrate and water | MO + HNO<sub>3</sub> ⟶ MNO<sub>3</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O |

### Reactions of Acids with Metal Carbonates

- Acids will react with metal carbonates to form the corresponding metal** salt**, [carbon dioxide](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/science/edexcel/double-award-modular/24/chemistry-unit-2/revision-notes/inorganic-chemistry/chemical-tests/tests-gases/) and **water**
- These reactions are easily distinguishable from acid – metal oxide/hydroxide reactions due to the presence of **effervescence** caused by the carbon dioxide gas

#### Acids & Metal Carbonates Reactions Table

| **Acid** | **Name of Products** | **Equation for Reaction** |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric acid | Metal chloride, carbon dioxide and water | MCO<sub>3</sub> + 2HCl ⟶ MCl<sub>2</sub> + CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O |
| Sulfuric acid | Metal sulfate, carbon dioxide and water | MCO<sub>3</sub> + 2H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> ⟶ MSO<sub>4</sub> + CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O |
| Nitric acid | Metal nitrate, carbon dioxide and water | MCO<sub>3</sub> + HNO<sub>3</sub> ⟶ M(NO<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub> + CO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O |

- The following are some specific examples of reactions between acids and metal carbonates:

**2HCl + Na**<sub>**2**</sub>**CO**<sub>**3**</sub>** ⟶ 2NaCl + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O + CO**<sub>**2**</sub>

**H**<sub>**2**</sub>**SO**<sub>**4**</sub>** + CaCO**<sub>**3**</sub>**⟶ CaSO**<sub>**4**</sub>** + H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O + CO**<sub>**2**</sub>

> **Exam Hint**
> If in an acid-base reaction there is effervescence produced then the base must be a metal carbonate which produces carbon dioxide gas.
