## Trigonometric Identities

### What is a trigonometric identity?

- **Trigonometric identities** are statements about **trigonometric functions** like $\mathrm{sin}θ$, $\mathrm{cos}θ$ and $\mathrm{tan}θ$

  - They are **true** for **all values** of the angle $θ$
  - They can be used to help **simplify** trigonometric equations before solving them
- Sometimes you may see identities written with the **symbol ** $\equiv$  instead of an equals sign

  - This means 'identical to' or 'equivalent to'

### What trigonometric identities do I need to know?

- You must know these **two trigonometric identities**:

  - $\mathrm{tan}θ=\frac{\mathrm{sin}θ}{\mathrm{cos}θ}$
  
    - This is the identity for $\mathrm{tan}θ$
  - $\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ+\mathrm{cos}^{2}θ=1$
  
    - This is sometimes called the Pythagorean identity
    - Note that the notation $\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ$ is the same thing as $(\mathrm{sin}θ)^{2}$
- The second identity is often used in one of its **rearranged forms**

  - $\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ=1-\mathrm{cos}^{2}θ$
  - $\mathrm{cos}^{2}θ=1-\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ$

> **Exam Hint**
> - When asked to show that one thing is equal or identical to another, look at what parts are 'missing'
> 
>   - This can help you spot which identity to use

> **Worked Example**
> Show that the equation $2\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ-\mathrm{cos}θ=0$ can be written in the form  $a\mathrm{cos}^{2}θ+b\mathrm{cos}θ+c=0$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are integers to be found with $a>0$.

> > *Note that in the 'target' form there is no *$\mathrm{sin}θ$* or *$\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ$
> 
> > *That means we want to use a substitution to get rid of the *$\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ$* in the original form*
> 
> > *We can do this using the identity *$\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ+\mathrm{cos}^{2}θ=1$*, rearranged as  *$\mathrm{sin}^{2}θ=1-\mathrm{cos}^{2}θ$
>
