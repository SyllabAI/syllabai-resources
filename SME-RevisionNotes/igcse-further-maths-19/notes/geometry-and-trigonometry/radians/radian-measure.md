## Radian Measure

### What are radians?

- **Radians** are an alternative to degrees for measuring angles
- 1 radian is **defined** as the angle in a circle **sector** of radius 1 and arc length 1
- Radians are often given in terms of $π$

  - $2π$ radians = 360°
  - $π$ radians = 180°
- The **symbol** for radians is <sup>c</sup> but it is more usual to see **rad**

  - When $π$ is involved, **no symbol** for radians is needed
  
    - With $π$ it is obvious it is in radians
  - You should **never** omit the symbol for degrees
- In the exam a question will usually tell you **which angle measure** to use

  - But radians **must** be used if calculus with the trigonometric functions is involved

![1 radian shown on a unit circle](../../../assets/7b5234c20288-5-4-1-radian-measure-notes-diagram-1.png)

### How do I convert between radians and degrees?

- Use $π$* *radians<sup> </sup>= 180° to **convert** between radians and degrees

  - To convert **from radians to degrees** multiply by $\frac{180}{π}$
  - To convert **from degrees to radians** multiply by $\frac{π}{180}$
- Some of the **common equivalents** are:

  - $2π\mathrm{rad}=360^{\circ}$
  - $π\mathrm{rad}=180^{\circ}$
  - $\frac{π}{2}\mathrm{rad}=90^{\circ}$
  - $\frac{π}{3}\mathrm{rad}=60^{\circ}$
  - $\frac{π}{4}\mathrm{rad}=45^{\circ}$
  - $\frac{π}{6}\mathrm{rad}=30^{\circ}$
- It is useful to **remember** these and use them to work out other conversions
- Your **calculator** will be able to work with both radians and degrees

![using multiples or fractions of pi when converting between radians and degrees](../../../assets/bb6a9c0e8fdb-5-4-1-radian-measure-notes-diagram-2.png)

> **Exam Hint**
> - If the question specifies a unit of angle measure (radians or degrees) be sure to use it!
> - Make sure that your calculator is in the correct mode for the angle measure you are working with

> **Worked Example**
> (a) Convert 43.8° to radians, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures.
> 
> > *Multiply by  *$\frac{π}{180}$
> 
> $43.8\times\frac{π}{180}=\frac{73π}{300}=0.764454...$
> 
> > *Note that *$\frac{73π}{300}$* is the exact value answer*
> 
> **Final answer:** **0.764 rad (3 s.f.)**
> 
> (b) Convert $\frac{5π}{4}$ radians to degrees.
> 
> > *If you remember that *$\frac{π}{4}$* radians is 45°, then you just need to multiply that by 5 to get the answer.*
> 
> > *Otherwise, multiply by  *$\frac{180}{π}$
> 
> $\frac{5π}{4}\times\frac{180}{π}=\frac{5}{4}\times180=5\times45=225$
> 
> **Final answer:** **225°**
