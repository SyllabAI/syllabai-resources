## Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

- The gravitational force between two masses, e.g., between the Earth and the Sun, is defined by Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation
- Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation states: **The gravitational force *****F***<sub>**G**</sub>** between two masses *****m***<sub>**1**</sub>** and *****m***<sub>**2**</sub>** is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation, *****r***
- In equation form, this is written as:

![Newton's Law of Gravitation equation 1](../../../assets/bdd47f5bb9fa-4-newtons-law-of-gravitation-equation-1.png)

- Where:

  - *F*<sub>*G*</sub> = gravitational force between two masses (N)
  - *G* = *Newton’s gravitational constant*
  - *m*<sub>*1*</sub> and *m*<sub>*2*</sub> = mass of body 1 and mass of body 2 (kg)
  - *r* = distance between the centre of the two masses (m)
- The 1/*r*<sup>2</sup> relation is called the ‘inverse square law’

  - This means that if the distance between two masses **doubles**, *r* becomes *2r*
  - Therefore, 1/*r*<sup>2</sup> becomes 1/(2*r*)<sup>2</sup>, which is equal to 1/4*r*<sup>2</sup>
  - Hence, the gravitational force between the two masses **reduces** by a factor of **four**

> **Exam Hint**
> A common mistake in exams is to forget to **add together** the distance from the surface of the planet and its radius to obtain the value of *r*. The distance *r* is measured between the **centre** of each mass, which is from the **centre** of the planet to the centre of the satellite!
> 
> Another common mistake is forgetting that the distance between masses *m*<sub>1</sub> and *m*<sub>2</sub> is **squared**. Remember this whenever you use Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation!
