## Electric Force between Two Charges

- All charged particles produce an electric field around them

  - This field exerts a force on any other charged particle within range
- The electrostatic force between two charges is defined by **Coulomb’s Law**

  - Recall that the charge of a uniform spherical conductor can be considered as a *point charge* at its centre
- Coulomb’s Law states that: **The electrostatic force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation**
- The force *F*<sub>E</sub> between two charges as expressed by Coulomb's Law is given by the equation:

![Coulombs Law diagram, downloadable AS & A Level Physics revision notes](../../../assets/5bbb3bd8c0b8-18-1-coulombs-law-diagram.png)

***The electrostatic force between two charges is defined by Coulomb’s Law***

- Where:

  - *F*<sub>*E*</sub> = electrostatic force between two charges (N)
  - *Q*<sub>*1*</sub> and *Q*<sub>*2*</sub> = two point charges (C)
  - *ε*<sub>*0*</sub> = *permittivity of free space*
  - *r* = distance between the centre of the charges (m)

- The 1/*r*<sup>*2*</sup> relation is called the *inverse square law*
- This means that when the separation of two charges doubles, the electrostatic force between them reduces to (½)<sup>2</sup> = ¼ of its original size
- *ε*<sub>*0*</sub> is a physical constant used to show the capability of a vacuum to permit electric fields

- If *Q*<sub>1</sub> and *Q*<sub>2</sub> are oppositely charged, then the electrostatic force *F*<sub>E</sub> is negative

  - This can be interpreted as an **attractive force **between** ***Q*<sub>1</sub> and *Q*<sub>2</sub>
- If *Q*<sub>1</sub> and *Q*<sub>2</sub> are the same charge, then the electrostatic force *F*<sub>E</sub> is positive

  - This can be interpreted as a **repulsive force **between** ***Q*<sub>1</sub> and *Q*<sub>2</sub>

> **Worked Example**
> An alpha particle is situated 2.0 mm away from a gold nucleus in a vacuum. Assuming they are point charges, calculate the magnitude of the force acting on each of the charges.
> 
> Atomic number of helium = 2
> 
> Atomic number of gold = 79
> 
> Charge of an electron = 1.60 × 10<sup>-19</sup> C
> 
> **Answer:**
> 
> **Step 1: Write down the known quantities**
> 
> - Distance, *r* = 2.0 mm = 2.0 × 10<sup>-3</sup> m
> 
> The charge of one proton = +1.60 × 10<sup>-19</sup> C
> 
> An alpha particle (helium nucleus) has 2 protons
> 
> - Charge of alpha particle, *Q*<sub>*1*</sub> = 2 × 1.60 × 10<sup>-19</sup> = +3.2 × 10<sup>-19</sup> C
> 
> The gold nucleus has 79 protons
> 
> - Charge of gold nucleus, *Q*<sub>*2*</sub> = 79 × 1.60 × 10<sup>-19</sup> = +1.264 × 10<sup>-17</sup> C
> 
> **Step 2: The electrostatic force between two point charges is given by Coulomb’s Law**

> **Exam Hint**
> Remember to always **square** the distance, *r* between the charges!
> 
> Always look out for unit prefixes when substituting values into an equation. Check whether the charge has been converted into C instead of nC or µC, or the distance in mm to m to get a force *F* of newtons, N.
