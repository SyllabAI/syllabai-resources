You now hold the complete [[ester]] story. The family is
defined by the –COO– functional group (4.38C); its simplest
synthesis is the [[esterification]] of ethanol with ethanoic
acid over a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst, reversible
and therefore yield-managed (4.39C); every member can be built
from its parents and split back to them, with the
alkyl-alkanoate naming rule reading in both directions
(4.40C, 4.41C); the family's volatility and distinctive smells
make it the flavour-and-perfume workhorse (4.42C); and the
test-tube preparation — hot-water bath, carbonate cleanup,
wafted smelling — turns all of it into a procedure you can run
(4.43C). Two equations anchor the chapter: the esterification
of ethanol with ethanoic acid, and its reverse, the
water-split of [[ethyl-ethanoate]].

Every specification statement of this chapter — 4.38C to 4.43C —
has been taught, and each now joins the platform question bank
through the links below, with practice items keyed to exactly
those statements.

Before opening the bank, audit yourself against the chapter's
core moves. Can you draw –COO– and write the esterification
equation with word labels? Can you build any grid ester from
named parents and decode any ester back to them? Can you argue
the uses from volatility and the purification steps from their
chemistry? Any wobble is a section to revisit, not a reason to
start over.

The question bank for this chapter trains a predictable set of
shapes, so rehearse the moves rather than the answers. Formula
items open the set: structural formulae for named esters,
[[displayed-formula]] drawings with every bond explicit, and
naming from condensed structures — all three formula levels the
drawing section drilled. A long planning item hands you two
alcohols and two acids and asks for every ester they can make
(the grid does the counting), then the method, the detection of
the product, and two uses. Decode items run the naming rule in
reverse: named esters and drawn structures to be split back
into their alcohol and carboxylic acid parents. A synthesis
route item starts from ethanol as the only organic starting
material and builds ethyl ethanoate through it — the displayed
formula, the acid required, the oxidation that makes that acid
from ethanol (u4:c26 called back), the balanced equation and
the conditions. A closing problem hands you three unlabelled
samples — ethanol, ethanoic acid, ethyl ethanoate — and bans
smelling, so the identification must run on reactions and
physical tests you can defend. The shapes are the chapter; the
esters are just costumes.

:::exam-hint
Two habits pay in this bank. In naming and drawing items, find
the C=O first — it fixes the alkanoate word and its carbon
count, and everything beyond the single-bonded oxygen is the
alkyl word. In route items, the conditions line is worth
explicit marks: concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst named but
kept out of the equation, heat applied through a water bath,
and the yield argument (pure, water-free reagents) if the
question pushes one step further.
:::

:::misconception
"The question bank rewards memorising the answers." It rewards
the transferable routines: the C=O-first decode, the
grid build-and-split, the property-to-use argument, the
step-justified practical. The same skills re-ask through
propyl propanoate instead of ethyl ethanoate, a drawn parent
instead of a named one, a planning frame instead of an
identification frame. Run the method and the costume does not
matter.
:::

:::summary
- **The functional group (4.38C)**: esters carry –COO– — a
  carbon with C=O and C–O — the signature shared by fats, oils
  and every artificial flavouring.
- **Making ethyl ethanoate (4.39C)**:
  CH₃COOH(l) + CH₃CH₂OH(l) → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃(l) + H₂O(l) —
  esterification and condensation in one; concentrated sulfuric
  acid catalyst in the conditions, never in the equation; pure
  (glacial) reagents guard the equilibrium.
- **Drawing and naming (4.40C, 4.41C)**: draw the parents with
  –OH groups adjacent, remove H₂O, join the leftovers; name
  alkyl-then-alkanoate ('yl' from the alcohol, 'oate' from the
  acid); split any ester between the group's C and O, adding H
  and OH, to recover the parents.
- **Uses (4.42C)**: volatile liquids with distinctive smells —
  solvents (nail polish), food flavourings and perfumes; hardly
  soluble in water because no O–H remains to hydrogen-bond.
- **The practical (4.43C)**: 1 cm³ acid + 1 cm³ alcohol, few
  drops of concentrated sulfuric acid, 80 °C water bath five
  minutes, cool, pour into sodium carbonate solution, waft the
  floating ester layer — butyl butanoate (pineapple), propyl
  ethanoate (pear).
:::
