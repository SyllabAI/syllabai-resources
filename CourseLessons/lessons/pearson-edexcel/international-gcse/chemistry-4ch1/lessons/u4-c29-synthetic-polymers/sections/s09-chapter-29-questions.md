You now hold the complete synthetic-polymers story. The
addition half: alkenes open their C=C bonds and add on, an initiator starting chains of 4000 to 40 000 carbons, with
nothing lost (4.44); the repeat-unit routine runs forwards for
poly(ethene), poly(propene), PVC and PTFE (4.45) and
backwards from any drawn polymer to its monomer (4.46); and
the disposal bill follows from the chemistry — strong covalent
bonds make the [[polymer]] inert and non-biodegradable, and
burning it trades landfill for carbon dioxide and toxic gases
(4.47). The condensation half, Paper 2C only: a diol and a
dicarboxylic acid alternate down a chain, water expelled at
every seam, an ester link at every junction (4.48C); the
repeat unit and balanced equation for the ethanedioic acid +
ethane-1,2-diol polyester (4.49C); and the biopolyesters,
PLA from lactic acid, that break down far faster than their
addition cousins (4.50C). Two equations anchor the chapter —
the ethene-to-poly(ethene) addition and the
diol-plus-dioic-acid condensation with its 2n waters — and one
routine runs the whole show: open the double bond going in,
restore it coming out.

Every specification statement of this chapter — 4.44 to 4.50C —
has been taught, and each now joins the platform question bank
through the links below, with practice items keyed to exactly
those statements.

Before opening the bank, rehearse the shapes the
questions wear. The addition half asks for terms defined
precisely (unsaturated, hydrocarbon, polymerisation, addition),
a balanced combustion equation for ethene, displayed formulae
and three-repeat-unit chain diagrams, the styrene
polymerisation with the C₆H₅ group carried whole, and the
monomer decode behind a Perspex-style chain fragment — the
4.46 routine run on unfamiliar furniture. The decision question
asks you to take a side on landfill versus incineration and
defend it with two reasons from the trade-off table. The
condensation half, Paper 2C only, asks you to identify a
polyester from a block diagram and draw its monomers, to build
a PET/Terylene chain two repeat units long with its balanced
equation from ethane-1,2-diol and terephthalic acid, and to
extend the whole pattern to nylon — diamine plus dioic acid,
amide links instead of ester links, the same remove-water
logic. Shapes, not scripts: every one of them is answered by
the routines already in your hands.

:::exam-hint
Read every bank question with the paper route in mind: the
addition-polymer items can reach you on Paper 1C as well as
2C, while anything asking for a polyester, a PET chain or a
nylon block diagram is C-suffixed territory — Paper 2C only.
And when a question shows a polymer fragment you have never
seen, that is not a new topic: it is the 4.46 routine wearing
a costume, so find two adjacent chain carbons and put the
double bond back.
:::

:::misconception
"The final chapter must be the hardest, so polymer questions
are where to expect defeat." This chapter is the most

to one drawing routine read forwards or backwards, one

you have already written out. Difficulty in polymer questions
comes from unfamiliar drawings, not unfamiliar chemistry —
and the routine dissolves unfamiliarity on contact.
:::

:::summary
Addition polymerisation joins many small alkene [[monomer]]s
into one giant molecule with nothing expelled; the
[[repeat-unit]] sits in square brackets with continuation
bonds crossing the walls and a subscript n. The routine runs
forwards from any alkene (ethene, propene, chloroethene,
tetrafluoroethene) and backwards from any polymer to its
monomer. The chains are saturated, inert and
non-biodegradable, so disposal is landfill versus incineration
with real trade-offs on both sides. [[condensation-polymerisation]]
alternates two monomers — a diol and a
dicarboxylic acid — expelling water at every junction and
building [[polyester]] chains stitched by ester links; its
equation balances with 2n waters per n repeat units. Because
the ester links are attackable, condensation polymers break
down eventually, and the biopolyesters — PLA from corn-starch
lactic acid — break down much faster.
:::

:::
