Here is the book's worked example (Example 1), which turns §s07's
definition into a routine. A naturally occurring sample of boron contains
**20% ¹⁰B and 80% ¹¹B**. Calculate the relative atomic mass.

The book's method — **imagine there are exactly 100 atoms** — makes the
percentages do the work:

- 20% of 100 atoms = 20 atoms, each of mass 10 → total mass 20 × 10
- 80% of 100 atoms = 80 atoms, each of mass 11 → total mass 80 × 11

Add the masses, divide by the number of atoms, and the average falls out:

> relative atomic mass = (20 × 10 + 80 × 11) ÷ 100 = (200 + 880) ÷ 100 =
> **10.8**

Therefore the relative atomic mass of boron is **10.8**. Notice what the
number tells you before you check it: 10.8 sits much closer to 11 than to
10, exactly as §s07 predicts for a sample that is 80% ¹¹B.

The book adds that the same method works even if there are **three or
four different isotopes**: multiply each isotope's mass by its abundance,
add all the products, divide by 100 (when abundances are percentages). The
imagine-100-atoms framing survives every exam version of this question.

:::worked-example
Boron: 20% ¹⁰B, 80% ¹¹B. Imagine 100 atoms → 20 atoms of mass 10, 80 of
mass 11. Total mass = 20 × 10 + 80 × 11 = 1080. Divide by 100 atoms:
Aᵣ = **10.8**. Mark lines: (1) multiply each mass by its abundance;
(2) add the products; (3) divide by 100; (4) quote 10.8 (no units — Aᵣ is
a relative quantity).
:::

:::maths-skills
The skill is the **weighted mean**, in the exact form maths uses: multiply
each value by its weight, add, divide by the total weight. Here the values
are isotope masses (10, 11) and the weights are the abundances (20, 80).
The imagine-100 trick converts percentages into counts so you never have
to manipulate percentage fractions under exam pressure.
:::

:::misconception
"The relative atomic mass of boron should be a whole number, like a mass
number." No — Aᵣ is an average over a mixture, so it is usually not a
whole number (boron 10.8, chlorine 35.5, copper 63.5). Whole numbers
belong to individual isotopes (mass numbers 10 and 11); the element's
Aᵣ averages across them.
:::

:::exam-hint
Show the full weighted-mean line — (20 × 10 + 80 × 11) ÷ 100 — not just
the answer. The three method steps carry the marks; a bare "10.8" with no
working risks losing method marks if any slip occurs. Quote the final
value **without units**.
:::
