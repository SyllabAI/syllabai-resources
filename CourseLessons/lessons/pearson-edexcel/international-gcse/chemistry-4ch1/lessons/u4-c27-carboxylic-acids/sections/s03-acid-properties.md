Ethanoic acid and the other carboxylic acids are **[[weak-acid]]s**
— a label this section uses and Section 5 finally explains. In
practical terms: their solutions have a pH of about 3–5 (the
exact value depends on the concentration), they turn blue litmus
paper red, and they react with everything you expect an acid to
react with. Weak does not mean inactive — the family passes every
classical test of an acid; it just runs the reactions more
gently than hydrochloric or sulfuric acid do.

Everything that follows takes [[ethanoic-acid]] as the worked
example, because it is the member you are most likely to meet in
a laboratory or a kitchen. The other members behave the same way:
if a question switches the acid, all you change is the formula —
CH₃COOH becomes CH₃CH₂COOH, and the reaction writing itself is
unchanged. That is the practical payoff of a homologous series:
learn one member's reactions, own the family's.

Two reaction families deliver Statement 4.36C, and each gets its
own section: the reaction with **metals** (next section, salts
plus hydrogen) and the reaction with **metal carbonates** (the
section after, salts plus carbon dioxide plus water). Both are
re-runs of acid chemistry met in Unit 2 — what is new here is
only the acid, and the ethanoate salts it leaves behind.

:::exam-hint
"State two ways ethanoic acid behaves like other acids" is a
gift item if you keep the acid-test checklist ready: turns blue
litmus red, pH below 7 (about 3–5 here), reacts with metals
above hydrogen, reacts with carbonates giving carbon dioxide.
Any two score. The trap is overclaiming — the family is weak,
so "reacts violently" or "pH about 1" statements contradict the
chemistry and cost the mark.
:::

:::misconception
"A weak acid is a harmless acid." Weak describes how far the
acid ionises in water, not how safe it is. Ethanoic acid still
turns litmus red, still fizzes with carbonates, and concentrated
solutions are corrosive. What weak actually changes is the
*rate and extent* of those reactions — Section 5 gives the
dissociation picture behind the word.
:::

:::
