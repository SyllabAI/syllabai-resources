You now hold the complete [[carboxylic-acid]] story. The family
is defined by the carboxyl group –COOH (4.34C); its four named
members up to four carbons come with the three-level formula
discipline and the count-them-all naming rule (4.35C); as
[[weak-acid]]s they react with metals and metal carbonates —
slower than the strong acids, leaving [[ethanoate-ion]] salts
and running the same charge arithmetic every time (4.36C); and
[[vinegar]], the everyday solution of [[ethanoic-acid]], is the
family's flagship (4.37C). Three equations anchor the chapter:
the magnesium reaction, the sodium carbonate reaction and the
calcium carbonate reaction.

Every specification statement of this chapter — 4.34C to 4.37C —
has been taught, and each now joins the platform question bank
through the links below, with practice items keyed to exactly
those statements.

Before opening the bank, audit yourself against the chapter's
core moves. Can you draw the carboxyl group with both bonds
explicit? Can you produce molecular, structural and displayed
levels for the four named acids? Can you write any metal
ethanoate or hydrogen-carbonate product from ion charges alone?
Can you explain the weak-acid rate difference through
dissociation? Any wobble is a section to revisit, not a reason
to start over.

The question bank for this chapter trains a predictable set of
shapes, so rehearse the moves rather than the answers. Drawing
and naming items open the set: structural formulae for named
acids, [[displayed-formula]] drawings with the COOH bonding
explicit, and naming from condensed structures. Identification items follow:
a drawn structure to be tested against a tick-table of the
alkene, alcohol and carboxylic-acid groups — read the drawing
bond by bond, because a molecule can legitimately tick two
boxes at once. A long oxidation item then reaches back to the
alcohols lesson: propan-1-ol heated under reflux with potassium
dichromate(VI) in dilute sulfuric acid, asking for the product's
full displayed formula (the oxidation lands on this chapter's
family), the orange-to-green colour change, a hazard critique of
an unsafe apparatus choice, and then the product tested in two
test-tubes — one with magnesium ribbon, one with sodium
carbonate solution — so both of this chapter's reaction families
run inside a single item. A finishing item balances the
vinegar-plus-sodium-hydrogencarbonate frame (a salt plus carbon
dioxide plus water) and asks for the reaction type and the
observation. The shapes are the chapter; the acid names are
just costumes.

:::exam-hint
Two habits pay in this bank. In equation items, the ethanoate
ion is always 1− — subscript the metal after the charges are
counted, and the coefficient of CH₃COOH follows the same count.
In explanation items, dissociation is the argument: weak means
partially ionised, lower H⁺ concentration, slower reaction —
"it is a weak acid so it is slower" without the mechanism earns
one mark where the dissociation sentence earns both.
:::

:::misconception
"The question bank rewards memorising these printed answers."
It rewards the transferable routines: the count-them-all naming
decode, the three-level formula ladder, the charge-first salt
arithmetic, the dissociation argument for rate differences. The
same skills re-ask through propanoic acid instead of ethanoic,
sodium carbonate instead of calcium, a drawn alcohol instead of
a drawn acid. Run the method and the costume does not matter.
:::

:::summary
- **The functional group (4.34C)**: carboxylic acids contain
  –COOH — carbonyl C=O and hydroxyl O–H on the same end carbon;
  built by oxidising the corresponding alcohol (microbial or
  dichromate(VI) routes).
- **Drawing and naming (4.35C)**: methanoic HCOOH (CH₂O₂),
  ethanoic CH₃COOH (C₂H₄O₂), propanoic CH₃CH₂COOH (C₃H₆O₂),
  butanoic CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH (C₄H₈O₂); count total carbons including
  the –COOH carbon; no locant is ever needed; displayed formulae
  show the C=O and O–H explicitly.
- **Weak-acid behaviour (4.36C)**: pH about 3–5, blue litmus to
  red, partial dissociation (about 1 in 75 molecules) — slower
  than strong acids at equal concentration because the H⁺
  concentration is lower.
- **With metals**: salt + hydrogen —
  Mg(s) + 2CH₃COOH(aq) → (CH₃COO)₂Mg(aq) + H₂(g); sodium
  dangerously reactive, zinc slower, below-hydrogen metals not
  at all; zinc ethanoate (CH₃COO)₂Zn.
- **With carbonates**: salt + carbon dioxide + water —
  Na₂CO₃(s) + 2CH₃COOH(aq) → 2CH₃COONa(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) and
  2CH₃COOH(aq) + CaCO₃(s) → (CH₃COO)₂Ca(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l);
  two protons per carbonate ion.
- **Vinegar (4.37C)**: an aqueous solution containing ethanoic
  acid — the family's everyday representative.
:::
