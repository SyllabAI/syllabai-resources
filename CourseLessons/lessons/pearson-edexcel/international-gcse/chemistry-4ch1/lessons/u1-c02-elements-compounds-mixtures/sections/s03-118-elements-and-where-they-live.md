So how many elements are there? **118**, and every single one of them lives
on the **[[periodic-table]]** — the chart that hangs on every chemistry
laboratory wall. Most of them occur naturally on Earth: hydrogen, helium
and sulfur are everyday examples you will meet throughout your studies. A
few do not occur naturally at all and have to be made artificially, in
particle accelerators and nuclear reactors; einsteinium is one of these.

For this lesson the count itself is not the point — the point is that the
periodic table is the *complete catalogue* of elements. Every substance
that is not on it is either a compound built from two or more of its
entries (§s04) or a mixture of such substances (§s05).

:::key-point
There are 118 elements, all displayed in the periodic table. Most occur
naturally (hydrogen, helium, sulfur); a handful are made artificially
(einsteinium).
:::

:::misconception
"Artificial elements are not real chemicals." No — an element is defined
by its atoms (same atomic number), not by where it came from. Einsteinium
is as much an element as hydrogen; it is simply one humans have to
manufacture because it does not occur naturally.
:::

:::exam-hint
"State the number of elements shown in the periodic table" or "give the
name of an element that occurs naturally" (1 mark): **118**; any natural
element named from the periodic table. Keep it short — command word
"state" wants one line.
:::
