Solids, liquids and gases are known as **the three states of matter**. That
is the whole definition — but it is worth pausing on it, because it is
beautifully sweeping: everything you look at is a solid, a liquid or a gas. Water is our everyday demonstration substance: ice
(solid), tap water (liquid) and steam (gas) are the same substance existing
in all three states at different temperatures.

Which state a substance is in is not random — it is decided by how its
particles are arranged, how far apart they are, and how they move. That is
exactly what §s04 puts on the table, so if you want the full particle
pictures, read on and we will build them properly there.

:::key-point
The three states of matter are **solid, liquid and gas**. Every substance
you will meet is one of the three (or changing between two
of them).
:::

:::misconception
"Clouds are a special fourth state of matter — they are not solid, liquid
or gas." No — a cloud is made of tiny liquid water droplets suspended in
air (a gas mixture). Everything is one of the three states.
:::

:::exam-hint
"State the three states of matter" (1 mark): list all three — solid,
liquid, gas — in any order. Mark schemes accept the three words alone; no
explanation is needed for a "State" command word.
:::
