:::key-point
The three states of matter are solid, liquid and gas. The [[particle-model]]
explains their properties in terms of the arrangement, separation and motion
of particles.
:::

:::prior-knowledge
Before this lesson you should be comfortable with: **elements, compounds and
mixtures** vocabulary from Key Stage 3, and reading simple particle diagrams.
This is the first chapter of Unit 1 — there is no earlier lesson in this
course to link back to.
:::

:::cross-curricular
**Maths:** comparing densities involves ratio and proportion reasoning.
**Physics:** changes of state reappear in thermal-energy transfer — the same
[[particle-model]] vocabulary is used there.
:::

:::misconception
"Heating makes particles expand." No — when a solid is heated and melts, the
**spaces between** particles increase and particles move faster; the particles
themselves do not get bigger.
:::

:::exam-hint
Command word **"State"** (1 mark): one short line, no explanation needed.
Mark schemes typically accept "particles move faster" for melting/evaporation
questions and avoid answers claiming particles "expand" — always answer in
terms of arrangement, separation and motion.
:::
