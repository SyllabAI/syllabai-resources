The book now states the two facts that turn the Periodic Table into a
shortcut for electronic configurations, and they are worth quoting
exactly:

- **Elements in the same group in the Periodic Table have the same
  number of electrons in their [[outer-shell]].**
- **The number of electrons in the outer shell is the same as the group
  number, for Groups 1 to 7.**

The book then shows the shortcut in action with three elements you will
never need a diagram for again: barium sits in **Group 2**, so it has
**2 electrons in its outer shell** — the same as every other Group 2
element; **iodine** (Group 7) has **7** outer-shell electrons;
**lead** (Group 4) has **4**. One look at the column, one answer — no
shell-filling required. (The book adds the boundary honestly: working
out what is in the *inner* shells is much more difficult, because the
simple patterns do not work beyond calcium.)

Notice what the first fact really says: a group is a column of atoms
whose outer shells match. That is why group membership — not mass, not
density, not colour — is the deep classifier of chemical behaviour. The
next section adds the period side of the story (shells), and then the
whole "configuration ↔ position" link of spec 1.22 is in your hands.

:::worked-example
Deduce the outer-shell electrons without any shell-filling: barium —
read its column, Group 2 → **2 outer electrons**. Iodine — Group 7 →
**7 outer electrons**. Lead — Group 4 → **4 outer electrons**. Mark
lines: (1) identifies the group number; (2) states outer electrons =
group number (Groups 1–7 only); (3) does not attempt inner shells.
:::

:::key-point
Same group ⇒ same number of outer-shell electrons; and for Groups 1–7,
outer-shell electrons = group number. Group 2 means 2 outer electrons,
Group 7 means 7 — for any element in the column, however heavy.
:::

:::misconception
"Barium is in Period 6, so it has 6 outer-shell electrons." That
confuses the two axes: **period counts shells, group counts outer-shell
electrons** (Groups 1–7). Barium: Group 2 → 2 outer electrons; Period 6
→ 6 *shells* in use. The next section (§s07) pins the period side down.
:::

:::exam-hint
"State the number of electrons in the outer shell of X" for any Group
1–7 element is a one-step mark: the group number. The examiner is
testing the shortcut, not a diagram — drawing the full shells for, say,
iodine wastes time and invites arithmetic slips.
:::
