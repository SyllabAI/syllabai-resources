The book's Figure 4.7 lays out the **electronic configurations of the
first 20 elements** in the Periodic Table — hydrogen and helium filling
the first shell, lithium to neon filling the second (2,1 up to 2,8), and
sodium to argon filling the third in exactly the same pattern (2,8,1 up
to 2,8,8). The figure's caption ends with the phrase "10 more elements":
potassium and calcium, the 19th and 20th, start a new shell — 2,8,8,1 and
2,8,8,2 — because the third shell is full at 8 *at this level*.

That is the whole content of spec 1.19: given any of the first 20
elements, you can deduce its electronic configuration from its position
in the Periodic Table — atomic number from the table, then fill shells
2, 8, 8, then the fourth. The figure is worth internalising rather than
memorising: the pattern *is* the table, one electron at a time.

Two honest boundaries, both stated by the book. First, the simple
filling pattern works only up to calcium: "working out what is in their
inner shells is much more difficult — the simple patterns we have
described don't work beyond calcium" (that is why this spec stops at 20).
Second, the book's extension note reveals the real machinery — the third
shell can actually hold up to 18 electrons, and 2 electrons go into the
fourth shell *before* the third is complete because the 4s subshell sits
lower in energy than the 3d. You do not need subshells at this level;
you need to know that the simple 2, 8, 8 picture is the model the exam
wants for the first 20 elements.

:::key-point
First 20 elements, one pattern: shells fill 2, 8, 8, then 2 — H/He
(1st shell), Li→Ne (2nd, 2,1…2,8), Na→Ar (3rd, 2,8,1…2,8,8), K and Ca
start the fourth shell (2,8,8,1 and 2,8,8,2). The simple pattern stops
at calcium; the exam tests exactly the first 20.
:::

:::misconception
"Potassium's configuration is 2, 8, 9, 1 — the third shell holds 18, so
why not keep filling it?" At International GCSE the third shell is
treated as full at **8**: potassium is **2, 8, 8, 1** and calcium is
**2, 8, 8, 2**. (The deeper story — 4s before 3d, a third shell that can
truly hold 18 — is extension material and *not* part of the model the
mark scheme uses for the first 20 elements.)
:::

:::exam-hint
"Deduce the electronic configuration of X (Z ≤ 20)" is a two-step
answer: atomic number from the Periodic Table, then fill 2, 8, 8, then
the fourth shell — and show the commas (2, 8, 1). Write the numbers with
commas exactly; a string like "281" is ambiguous and can lose the mark.
:::
