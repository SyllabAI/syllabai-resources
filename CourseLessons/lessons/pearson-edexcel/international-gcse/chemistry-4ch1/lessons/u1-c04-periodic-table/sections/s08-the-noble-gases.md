The **Group 0** elements are known as the **noble gases** because they
are almost completely unreactive — in fact the two at the top of the
group, **helium and neon, don't react with anything**. The
electron-counting picture explains the group at a glance: the elements of
Group 0 have **8 electrons in their outer shell** (apart from helium,
which has **2**) — and either way, the outer shell is **full**.

That fullness is the whole explanation, stated as the exam answer: the lack of reactivity of the noble gases is associated with
their electronic configurations — **the outer shell is full, and so there
is no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons in a chemical reaction**.
Every reaction you will meet in Chapters 7 and 8 happens because an atom
is chasing a full outer shell (losing, gaining or sharing electrons to
get there). The noble gases start with the prize; they have nothing to
chase.

An extension honesty box for the curious:
in reality the outer shells of argon, krypton, xenon and radon are *not*
full — only helium and neon have truly full outer shells, because the
third shell can hold up to 18 electrons and the fourth up to 32. That is
extension work, not exam material — but it explains why "full outer
shell" is a *model* that happens to be exactly right for the two
lightest noble gases and right-in-effect for the rest at this level.

:::key-point
Noble gases (Group 0): 8 outer-shell electrons except helium (2). They
are almost completely unreactive — helium and neon react with nothing —
because the outer shell is full: no tendency to lose, gain or share
electrons. That is the whole answer to spec 1.24.
:::

:::misconception
"Helium must have 8 outer electrons to be a noble gas." Helium has
**2** — and that is a full first shell (the first shell only holds 2),
which is why helium sits in Group 0 despite never having an octet. The
unreactivity rule is **full outer shell**, not "8 electrons" as such;
8 is just what a full outer shell looks like for every noble gas except
the first.
:::

:::exam-hint
"Why are the noble gases unreactive?" is a one-cause answer and the
cause must be electronic: **full outer shell → no tendency to lose,
gain or share electrons**. Answers based on "they are stable" or "they
are gases" earn nothing — name the electron arrangement. If the question
adds "(except helium)", show you know why: 2 electrons already fill the
first shell.
:::
