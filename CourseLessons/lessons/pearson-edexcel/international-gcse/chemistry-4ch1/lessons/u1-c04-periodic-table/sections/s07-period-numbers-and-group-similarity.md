The period axis gets its own rule: **the period number gives the number
of occupied shells, or the highest occupied shell**.
So calcium (electronic configuration 2, 8, 8, 2) is in **Period 4** and
has four shells occupied, with its outermost electron in the fourth
shell. Iodine is in **Period 5** — five occupied shells — and because it
is *also* in **Group 7**, you can deduce it has **7 electrons in the
fifth shell**. Group gives the outer count, period gives the shell
count: together they locate an element's chemistry exactly.

Now the deeper question — why do the columns behave like families? The
answer is the section's own heading: **elements in the same group in the
Periodic Table have similar chemical properties**. The evidence is a
trio of reaction families: *all* Group 1 elements react vigorously with
water to form hydrogen and hydroxides with similar formulae — LiOH,
NaOH, KOH; *all* Group 7 elements react with hydrogen to form compounds
with similar formulae — HF, HCl, HBr; *all* Group 2 elements form
chlorides with similar formulae — MgCl₂, CaCl₂. Similar formulas come
from similar outer-shell electron counts.

And the *reason* reactions match is the electron bookkeeping you built
in §s04: **the reactions of atoms depend on how many electrons there are
in their outer shell** — those are the electrons that get involved when
elements bond. Elements in the same group (apart from helium in Group 0)
have the same number of outer-shell electrons, **therefore they react in
similar ways**. A historical wrinkle: the
noble-gas group is called Group 0 (not Group 8) because when those gases
were first discovered they were believed to combine with nothing —
**zero combining power ([[valency]])**. It is the only group whose
number does not tell you the outer-electron count: noble gases (except
helium) have **8** outer electrons. In Chapters 7 and 8 you will see
atoms losing, gaining or sharing electrons to reach 8 — the noble gases
already have 8, so they do not bother.

:::key-point
Period number = number of occupied shells (calcium: 2, 8, 8, 2 →
Period 4). Same group ⇒ same outer-shell count ⇒ similar chemical
properties — Group 1 hydroxides LiOH/NaOH/KOH, Group 7 hydrides
HF/HCl/HBr, Group 2 chlorides MgCl₂/CaCl₂. Reactions depend on
outer-shell electrons; helium is the one Group 0 exception (2 outer
electrons).
:::

:::misconception
"Group 0 elements have 0 electrons in their outer shell — the number is
the count, like Groups 1–7." No: Group 0 is the one group where the
number is *not* the outer-electron count. The name comes from the old
idea of zero combining power (valency). Noble gases other than helium
have **8** outer-shell electrons (a full shell), which is precisely why
they were thought to have zero combining power.
:::

:::exam-hint
To deduce a configuration from position alone, use **both** axes:
iodine is Period 5 *and* Group 7, so its outer electrons are 7 *in the
fifth shell*, and the full stack is 2, 8, 8, 8, 7. "Explain why
elements in the same group have similar chemical properties" wants the
causal chain: same group → same number of outer-shell electrons →
outer electrons are the ones involved in reactions → similar reactions.
:::
