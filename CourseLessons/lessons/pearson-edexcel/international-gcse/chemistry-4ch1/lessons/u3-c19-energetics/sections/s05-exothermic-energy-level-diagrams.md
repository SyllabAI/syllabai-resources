An [[energy-level-diagram]] is the standard picture for the energy story of a
reaction. The vertical axis is energy (label it!), the horizontal axis is
the progress of the reaction, and the reactants and products each sit on
their own horizontal level.

For an **exothermic** reaction the reactants sit **higher** than the
products — they have more chemical energy — so the arrow from reactants to
products points **down**. The drop between the two levels is the energy that
has been released to the surroundings as heat. Because the products have less
energy, we say the products are **more stable** than the reactants. As the
reaction runs, the temperature of both the reaction mixture and its
surroundings goes up — that warming is the released energy showing itself.

Draw the diagram in this order and you cannot lose the labels:

1. Two horizontal levels — reactants on the upper level, products on the
   lower level.
2. Vertical axis labelled "energy", horizontal axis labelled "progress of
   the reaction".
3. An arrow pointing down from reactants to products.
4. ∆H written next to the arrow with its **negative** sign — the drop is the
   magnitude, the sign records the direction.

:::exam-hint
Paper 2C loves three details on this diagram. Label both axes — an unlabelled
energy axis costs the mark even if the shape is perfect. Put the reactants
above the products for an exothermic change, with a downward arrow. And
write ∆H beside the arrow **with its minus sign**; "∆H" alone, or a positive
value on a downward diagram, contradicts itself and loses the mark. If the
question asks you to *explain* the shape, one sentence does it: the products
have less chemical energy than the reactants, so the difference is released
as heat.
:::

:::misconception
"The products are drawn lower because the reaction mixture ended up colder."
The levels show **chemical energy stored in the chemicals**, not the
temperature at the end. An exothermic reaction leaves the *surroundings*
hotter precisely because the *chemicals* ended with less energy — the two
observations point in opposite directions, and the diagram records the
chemicals.
:::
