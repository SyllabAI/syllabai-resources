Every chemical reaction involves an energy change. Some reactions pour heat
out into the surroundings; others pull heat in from them. This chapter is
about measuring those changes, calculating them, and explaining them through
the bonds that break and form while reactions happen.

The chapter runs on two tracks. The **core track** (assessed in both Paper 1C
and Paper 2C) covers the exothermic/endothermic split, the calorimetry
experiments that measure heat changes for combustion, displacement, dissolving
and neutralisation, the equation Q = mc∆T, and the calculation of molar
enthalpy changes. The **chemistry-only track** (Paper 2C, sections marked
3.5C/3.6C/3.7C) adds energy level diagrams and bond-energy calculations. The
practical work — investigating temperature changes when salts dissolve,
neutralisations happen, metals displace and fuels burn — is core practical
work, and the examiners ask about its method, its errors and its
improvements as often as they ask for the numbers.

Two words carry the whole chapter. A reaction that gives out heat is
[[exothermic]]; a reaction that takes heat in is [[endothermic]]. Everything
else — the diagrams, the equations, the experiments — exists to measure,
compare and explain those two directions of energy flow.

:::prior-knowledge
You have already met energy changes without naming them: burning fuels
(Unit 1, states of matter and fuels), metals reacting with acids and the
displacement reactions of the reactivity series (u2:c14), neutralisation
(u2:c16), and the thermal decomposition of carbonates (u2:c10's electrolysis
neighbour, u1:c08's reactions). The mole and molar calculations from
u1:c05/u1:c06 are used in every worked example here — if mol/dm³
concentration sums feel shaky, revise them first.
:::

:::cross-curricular
Physics shares the specific heat capacity idea — the same J/g/°C quantity
appears in thermal physics. Design technology meets it in hand warmers and
self-heating meal packs. Geography uses heat of combustion when comparing
fuels and carbon footprints.
:::

:::exam-hint
In the exam, the command words for this chapter are *know*, *describe*,
*calculate*, *draw* and *explain*. "Know" questions want the exothermic or
endothermic definition in one precise sentence — direction of heat flow
relative to the surroundings. "Describe" questions want a method in logical
order. "Calculate" questions want Q = mc∆T and ΔH = Q ÷ n set out with units.
"Draw" questions (Paper 2C) want labelled energy level diagrams. "Explain"
questions want bonds broken (energy in) versus bonds made (energy out).
:::

:::misconception
"Energy changes only matter for dramatic reactions like explosions." Every
reaction carries an energy change — dissolving a pinch of ammonium chloride
in water lowers the temperature measurably, and neutralising dilute acid with
dilute alkali warms the mixture by several degrees even though nothing
appears to happen. The size differs; the direction of energy flow is always
there.
:::
