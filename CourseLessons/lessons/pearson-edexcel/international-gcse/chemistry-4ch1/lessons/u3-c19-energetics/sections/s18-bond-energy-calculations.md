The calculation recipe has one sign rule and two sums. When energy is given
out you show it with a **negative** sign; when energy is taken in, positive.
Then:

- **energy needed to break all the bonds in the reactants** — every term
  positive;
- **energy released when the new bonds are made in the products** — every
  term negative;
- **overall change = (energy in to break) + (energy out to make)** — a
  positive total means the reaction is endothermic, a negative total
  exothermic.

A quick illustration of the rule: if breaking all the reactant bonds costs
+1000 kJ and making the product bonds releases −1200 kJ, the overall change
is +1000 + (−1200) = **−200 kJ** — the reaction releases 200 kJ of energy
overall, so it is exothermic.

:::worked-example
**Methane reacts with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light to
produce chloromethane and hydrogen chloride.** You can imagine all the bonds
being broken in the methane and chlorine, and then being reformed in new
ways in the products. Calculate the overall energy change.

Bonds that need to be broken (endothermic):

| Bond | Count | Sum |
|---|---|---|
| 4 C–H | 4 × (+413) | +1652 kJ |
| 1 Cl–Cl | 1 × (+243) | +243 kJ |
| | **total in** | **+1895 kJ** |

New bonds made (exothermic):

| Bond | Count | Sum |
|---|---|---|
| 3 C–H | 3 × (−413) | −1239 kJ |
| 1 C–Cl | 1 × (−346) | −346 kJ |
| 1 H–Cl | 1 × (−432) | −432 kJ |
| | **total out** | **−2017 kJ** |

More energy is released when bonds are formed than is required to break
them, so the reaction releases energy overall: it is exothermic.

Overall energy change = +1895 + (−2017) = **−122 kJ**

The negative sign of the answer shows that, overall, heat is given out as
the bonds rearrange.
:::

Two honesty notes belong beside every bond-energy answer. First, [[bond-energy]]
calculations only ever give **estimates**: the strength of a bond varies
slightly depending on what else is around it in the molecule, so quoted bond
energies are **average** values — good, but not perfect. Enthalpy changes
measured by the calorimetry experiments of this chapter are more specific for
particular compounds. Second, the "break everything, rebuild everything"
route is an **imaginary** one — the real reaction does not actually happen
by all the bonds being broken this way. That does not matter: the overall
amount of heat released or absorbed is the same however you do the reaction,
as long as the reactants and products are the same. That fact is
[[hesss-law]], which you will meet again if you take chemistry further.

:::worked-example
**Combustion of methane.** The equation is:

CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(g)

| Bond | C–H | O=O | C=O | O–H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bond energy / kJ/mol | 413 | 498 | 743 | 464 |

Start by drawing out the structures showing all the bonds, so the bond
counts are right: CH₄ holds 4 C–H; the two O₂ molecules hold 2 O=O
double bonds (498 kJ/mol is the energy to break the **whole** double bond);
CO₂ holds 2 C=O; the two H₂O molecules hold 4 O–H.

Bonds that need to be broken (endothermic):

4 C–H = 4 × (+413) = +1652 kJ
2 O=O = 2 × (+498) = +996 kJ
**total in = +2648 kJ**

New bonds made (exothermic):

2 C=O = 2 × (−743) = −1486 kJ
4 O–H = 4 × (−464) = −1856 kJ
**total out = −3342 kJ**

Enthalpy change = +2648 + (−3342) = **−694 kJ/mol**

More energy is released when bonds are formed than is required to break
them: the reaction is exothermic. Energy is given out as the reactants are
converted to the products, so the products must have less energy than the
reactants — the products are more stable, and an energy level diagram for
this reaction shows the products level sitting below the reactants level,
with a downward arrow and ∆H = −694 kJ/mol.

The reaction is written with H₂O(g) rather than H₂O(l) deliberately: bond
energies can only be used for **gaseous** molecules. Finding the enthalpy
change for the same reaction forming liquid water would need the energy
released by H₂O(g) → H₂O(l) as well — beyond International GCSE.
:::

:::exam-hint
Paper 2C marks these in four slots: correct bond **counts** from the drawn-out
structures (the balancing coefficients multiply the bonds — 2O₂ means 2
O=O, and 2H₂O means 4 O–H), correct signs (+ in, − out), the addition to a
total, and the exothermic/endothermic verdict tied to the sign. Draw the
molecules first: every miscounted bond in this question family traces back
to skipping the structures. If an energy level diagram is required, the
sign of your total dictates which level the products occupy.
:::

:::misconception
"O=O at 498 kJ/mol breaks into two O– bonds worth 249 kJ each, so count the
double bond as two single bonds." The 498 kJ/mol is the energy to break the
**whole double bond at once** — count one O=O per oxygen molecule. Bond
tables list whole bonds (single or double as printed); inventing half-values
or counting double bonds twice is the standard arithmetic corruption in this
question.
:::
