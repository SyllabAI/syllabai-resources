You now hold the complete [[alcohol]] story. The family is defined
by the –OH functional group on a saturated chain (4.29C); its four
named members — methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol, butan-1-ol — come
with the draw-and-name discipline and the propan-1-ol/propan-2-ol
[[isomer]] distinction (4.30C); and ethanol, the family's
representative, does three oxidations — burning (Section 3),
microbial souring (Section 4), and the dichromate(VI) reflux that
turns orange to green (Section 5) — all landing on the same
product, [[ethanoic-acid]] (4.31C). Its two industrial routes,
[[fermentation]] (enzymes, ~30 °C, anaerobic — with the *why* of
each condition, 4.32C + 4.33C) and [[hydration]] (addition, H₃PO₄,
300 °C, 60–70 atm, making [[ethanol]]), close the chapter in a
five-row comparison.
Every specification statement of this chapter — 4.29C to 4.33C —
has been taught, and each now joins the platform question bank
through the links below, with practice items keyed to exactly
those statements.

Before opening the bank, audit yourself against the chapter's core
moves. Can you draw the four alcohols with the O–H bond shown? Can
you run all three oxidations — reagents, conditions, colour change
where there is one, product? Can you write the three key equations
(combustion, [O] oxidation, glucose fermentation)? Can you argue
both sides of the fermentation-vs-hydration comparison in table
language? Any wobble is a section to revisit, not a reason to
start over.

The question bank for this chapter trains a predictable set of
shapes, so rehearse the moves rather than the answers. Drawing
items open the set: structural formulae for named alcohols,
displayed formulae with the O–H bond explicit, and naming from
condensed structures. Bonding items then ask for a dot-and-cross
diagram of ethanol — the covalent model (u1:c08) applied at a
functional group. Conditions items follow: two conditions of the
catalytic hydration, then the fermentation trio (yeast, 30–40 °C,
absence of air) with each condition *explained* in the Section 6
language, plus the Brazil economics. Equation items close the set:
the complete combustion of ethanol, the glucose-to-ethanol
conversion, the dichromate oxidation with [O] and its orange-to-
green observation, and a finishing item that runs ethanol
backwards to ethene — the dehydration completion — by supplying
the missing product. The shapes are the chapter; the alcohol names
are just costumes.

:::exam-hint
Two mark-dense habits for this bank. In oxidation items, the
product is always **ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH** — name it with its
formula, and pair the [O] shorthand with the named oxidising agent
in prose. In explanation items, mechanisms beat adjectives: "the
enzymes denature above 40 °C" earns, "it's too hot" does not. And
in any "state two conditions" item, take them verbatim from the
process boxes — specific numbers and named catalysts are what the
mark scheme stores.
:::

:::summary
- **The functional group (4.29C)**: alcohols contain –OH
  covalently bonded to a carbon chain; "alcohol" in everyday
  speech is ethanol, one member of the homologous series.
- **Drawing and naming (4.30C)**: methanol CH₃OH, ethanol
  CH₃CH₂OH, propan-1-ol CH₃CH₂CH₂OH, butan-1-ol
  CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH; displayed formulae must show the O–H bond;
  propanol/butanol are acceptable names; the locant reports the
  –OH position and generates propan-1-ol/propan-2-ol isomers.
- **Three oxidations (4.31C)**: burning in air (complete
  combustion, C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l)); microbial
  oxidation by air + microorganisms (wine → vinegar, ethanoic
  acid CH₃COOH); heating with potassium dichromate(VI) in dilute
  sulfuric acid under reflux (orange → green, Cr³⁺, [O]
  shorthand: CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O).
- **Fermentation (4.32C + 4.33C)**: yeast enzymes convert glucose
  C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ at ~30 °C, anaerobic; no air because
  aerobic enzymes make CO₂ + water instead; 30–40 °C because the
  protein enzymes denature above ~40 °C and stall below; capped
  at ~15% alcohol (yeast dies), purified by fractional
  distillation (ethanol 78 °C vs water 100 °C).
- **Hydration (4.32C)**: ethene + steam, ~300 °C, ~60–70 atm,
  phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) catalyst; an addition reaction; recycle
  loop for the unreacted ethene.
- **The comparison**: renewable/batch/slow/impure/gentle vs
  finite/continuous/quick/purer/high-energy — and the long-run
  case for the renewable route when oil runs out.
:::
