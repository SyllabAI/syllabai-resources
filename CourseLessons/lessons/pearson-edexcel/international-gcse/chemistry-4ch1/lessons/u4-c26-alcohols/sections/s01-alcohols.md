The word "alcohol" in everyday speech means one specific liquid —
but in chemistry it names an entire family. **[[alcohol]]s** are an
important class of organic compounds: a **[[homologous-series]]**
whose members all carry the **–OH functional group** covalently
bonded to a carbon chain. What people commonly call "alcohol" is
just one member of that family — the drink industry's ethanol —
and even that molecule should properly be called
**[[ethanol]]**, C₂H₅OH, or better, showing the structure,
CH₃CH₂OH. Every chemistry idea you already own from the organic
chapters — formula types, series behaviour, the reactivity that a
[[functional-group]] confers — transfers directly to this family;
the –OH group simply replaces the C=C handle of the alkenes
(u4:c25) as the reactive site everything else happens at.

The family is small at this level and its chemistry is organised
around one member. You will draw and name the first four alcohols,
then watch ethanol — the family's representative — do three
characteristic things: burn, oxidise to ethanoic acid (three
different ways, one of them in a wine bottle), and get manufactured
industrially by two competing routes whose trade-offs the
comparison section sets side by side. Every specification statement
of this chapter — 4.29C to 4.33C — is chemistry-only content,
assessed in Paper 2C alone, so this lesson is paper-2 territory
through and through.

:::exam-hint
The functional group of the alcohols is the **–OH (hydroxyl)
group**. Write it with the dash: –OH, "covalently bonded to a
carbon chain" when the question asks for the full statement
(4.29C). Do not write just "O" or "H" — and do not confuse the
family name (alcohols) with the one member everyone has heard of
(ethanol).
:::

:::misconception
"'Alcohol' is a single substance." It is a family — a homologous
series — of which ethanol is merely the most familiar member.
Methanol, propan-1-ol and butan-1-ol are all alcohols too, with
the same –OH group and their own properties; the drinks-cabinet
meaning of the word is a chemistry accident, not a definition.
:::

Can you name the four alcohols this chapter covers? Then the
family portrait is hung — move on to drawing it.
:::
