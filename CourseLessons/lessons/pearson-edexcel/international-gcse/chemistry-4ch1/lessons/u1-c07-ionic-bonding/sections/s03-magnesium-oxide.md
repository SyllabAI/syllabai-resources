Magnesium oxide (MgO) is the second worked example, and it adds two new ideas
at once: a **2+/2−** ion pair, and the charge labels that make a
**[[dot-and-cross-diagram]]** complete. Magnesium ([2, 8, 2]) has two outer
electrons; oxygen ([2, 6]) has room for exactly two. **Two electrons are
transferred** from a magnesium atom to an oxygen atom, forming Mg²⁺ and O²⁻.

The book's Figure 7.4 draws the diagram: magnesium's two outer electrons
become crosses inside oxygen's outer shell (or dots-and-crosses in any
colour convention you like — the marks scheme cares about the electron
*count* and the *charges*, not the symbol you used). The caption carries the
single most common lost mark: **don't forget the charges on the ions** — a
correct diagram labelled without Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ is an incomplete answer.

:::key-point
The book's word of the day (chunk 0310, doubled sentence deduplicated):
**isoelectronic** means *have the same number of electrons*. When sodium
forms its ion, electronic configuration [2, 8], it becomes isoelectronic
with neon, [2, 8]. The same idea runs through this whole chapter: Mg²⁺ is
isoelectronic with neon, and O²⁻ is isoelectronic with neon too — three
different particles, one electron count.
:::

Notice what the transfer did *not* do: it did not make a molecule. One Mg²⁺
paired with one O²⁻ is the *formula unit* the diagram shows — the actual
compound (section 12) is a giant lattice of very many Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions. The
diagram is the bookkeeping for one transfer; the compound is the sum of
enormous numbers of those transfers' results.

:::exam-hint
Dot-and-cross mark schemes for 1.40 are nearly always the same three lines:
outer shells only (spec words: *only outer electrons need be shown*);
correct electron counts in each ion's outer shell; **charges on both ions**.
For MgO: magnesium bracket [2, 8]²⁺ with empty outer shell, oxygen bracket
[2, 8]²⁻ with eight outer electrons — two of them crosses. Draw big, clear
square brackets; tiny cramped brackets are where marks go missing.
:::

:::misconception
"Mg²⁺ means the magnesium atom gained two electrons, and O²⁻ lost two." Read
the charges against the process: losing electrons leaves *more* protons than
electrons, so **loss makes positive** (Mg lost 2 → 2+); gaining electrons
makes the balance negative (O gained 2 → 2−). The superscript records the
balance after the transfer — never the direction of the move itself.
:::
