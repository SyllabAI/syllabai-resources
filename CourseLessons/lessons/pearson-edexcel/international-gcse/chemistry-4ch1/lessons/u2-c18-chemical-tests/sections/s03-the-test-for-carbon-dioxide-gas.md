Bubble the gas through **limewater**. Carbon dioxide turns limewater **milky**
— some mark schemes say chalky or cloudy; all three words describe the same
thing, a fine white solid forming and scattering the light.

[[limewater]] is calcium hydroxide solution, and the milkiness is a genuine
precipitation reaction. The carbon dioxide reacts with the calcium hydroxide
to form a white precipitate of calcium carbonate:

Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)

This is the same carbonate chemistry you met in the salt preparations: an
alkaline solution meeting an acidic oxide, with an insoluble salt as the
product. The observation and the equation are two halves of one mark scheme
line, so learn them joined.

:::key-point
If you keep bubbling the carbon dioxide through the limewater, the white
precipitate will eventually disappear again. This is because the CO₂ reacts
with the calcium carbonate to form calcium [[hydrogencarbonate]], which is
soluble in water:

CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) → Ca(HCO₃)₂(aq)

So a positive test has a shelf life: stop bubbling once the milkiness appears,
or over-running the test dissolves your own evidence.
:::

:::exam-hint
The full script is: bubble the gas through limewater; the limewater turns milky
or cloudy; therefore carbon dioxide. The follow-up asks are predictable and
worth preparing: give the equation for the precipitate (Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃
+ H₂O, with state symbols); name the white precipitate (calcium carbonate); and
explain why the milkiness vanishes if bubbling continues (soluble calcium
hydrogencarbonate forms). That last explanation is the distinguisher between a
competent answer and a top answer — quote the hydrogencarbonate equation if you
want the respect line.
:::

:::misconception
"Limewater is lime juice, or somehow the same as water." Limewater is calcium
hydroxide solution, Ca(OH)₂(aq) — an alkali, nothing like fruit juice. The test
works *because* the calcium hydroxide reacts with the acidic oxide CO₂ to make
the insoluble carbonate; plain water would form no precipitate and prove
nothing. Also resist saying "limewater turns carbon dioxide milky" — the gas
turns the *limewater* milky; keep the subject and object the right way round.
:::
