Hold a piece of **damp litmus paper** (or damp universal indicator paper) at
the mouth of the tube or inside it. Chlorine **bleaches** the damp litmus
paper — it turns the paper white. To [[bleach]] is to strip colour, and
chlorine is the only gas on this course that does it.

The dampness matters. Chlorine dissolves in the water on the wet paper and
reacts with it; dry paper is a much weaker positive result. And the order of
colour changes tells you two things at once. If **blue** litmus or universal
indicator paper is used, it goes **red first** — the chlorine dissolves in the
water to form an acidic solution — and **then white**, as the bleaching
follows. Red-then-white on blue litmus is the complete chlorine signature:
acidic *and* bleaching in one observation.

Chlorine is a green gas, so the colour of the gas itself is a supporting clue,
but never a conclusion: name gases by their tests, not by their colour.

:::safety
Wear eye protection. Avoid inhaling or "sniffing" chlorine or ammonia,
especially if you have a breathing problem such as asthma. Both gases are
tested at the mouth of the tube, not by leaning over it.
:::

:::exam-hint
Write the script in order: damp litmus paper at the mouth of the tube; blue
litmus goes red first then white (or colourless litmus simply turns white);
therefore chlorine. The two-stage colour change is the mark-winner — answers
that only say "bleaches litmus" drop the acidity half. A classic distractor
pairs this test with the ammonia test: ammonia turns damp *red* litmus *blue*
(alkaline), chlorine turns damp *blue* litmus *red then white* (acidic then
bleached). Learn the pair together and neither can be confused with the other.
:::

:::misconception
"Chlorine turns litmus white, so it is an alkali like ammonia." The bleaching
is not an acid-base colour flip — it is a chemical destruction of the dye,
which is why the paper does not turn back. Chlorine's first effect on damp
blue litmus is actually acidic (red), from the acidic solution it forms with
the water on the paper; the whitening that follows is the bleach. Alkalis
turn litmus blue without destroying it. Two different mechanisms, two
different colours, one discipline: quote both stages.
:::
