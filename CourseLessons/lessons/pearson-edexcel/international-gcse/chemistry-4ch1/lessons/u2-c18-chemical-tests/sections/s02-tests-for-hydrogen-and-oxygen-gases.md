Two gases in this chapter announce themselves with a flame. Hydrogen is
identified with a **lighted splint** held to the mouth of the tube: the
hydrogen explodes with a **squeaky pop**. The pop is genuine combustion — the
hydrogen combines explosively with oxygen from the air to make water, and it
is the rapid expansion of the burning gas mixture that you hear:

2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)

Oxygen is identified with a **glowing splint** placed inside the tube of gas:
oxygen **relights a glowing splint**. The reasoning is the same chemistry from
the other side — combustion needs oxygen, so a gas that rekindles a flame that
had almost died is oxygen-rich. Note the pairing of the two tests: a *lighted*
splint finds the gas that burns, a *glowing* splint finds the gas that lets
other things burn. Mixing the splints up is the classic lost mark, so anchor
each test to its adverb.

Both tests are safe on test-tube quantities, but the hydrogen pop is a genuine
mini-explosion, so the tube mouth must point away from faces and the splint
must be held at the mouth, not lowered inside. If the pop is faint or absent,
the gas may have escaped or been displaced by air — collect a fresh sample
rather than concluding the gas is not hydrogen.

:::exam-hint
The examiner expects both tests word-perfect: hydrogen — lighted splint,
squeaky pop; oxygen — glowing splint, relights. A favourite extended version
gives you three colourless gases (hydrogen, oxygen and air) and asks you to
identify each with the splint tests: the pop identifies hydrogen, the relight
identifies oxygen, and the splint that simply goes out leaves air, whose 21%
oxygen is too dilute to relight the splint. If asked for the equation, the
hydrogen pop gives 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l) — balanced, with state symbols.
:::

:::misconception
"A positive squeaky pop proves the tube contains *pure* hydrogen." A pop
confirms hydrogen is present, but hydrogen only explodes cleanly when mixed
with air — pure hydrogen in a tube that never met air burns quietly or not at
all. Equally, the pop test does not identify hydrogen uniquely among all
conceivable gases; it identifies hydrogen among the gases of this course. Say
"squeaky pop with a lighted splint, so hydrogen" — the full script is what
earns the mark.
:::
