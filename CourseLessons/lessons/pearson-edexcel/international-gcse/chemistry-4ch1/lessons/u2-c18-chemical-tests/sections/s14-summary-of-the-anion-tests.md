Three [[anion]] families, three scripts, one table. Everything the carbonate,
sulfate and halide sections taught compresses into this summary, and the
compression is itself the revision: cover the right-hand columns, rebuild
each row, and the whole of objective 2.48 is one artefact.

| Ion | Test | Positive result | Product of reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO₃²⁻ | add dilute hydrochloric acid, then bubble any gas produced through limewater | fizzing; limewater goes cloudy (white precipitate) | CO₂; CaCO₃ (with limewater) |
| SO₄²⁻ | add dilute hydrochloric acid followed by barium chloride solution | white precipitate | BaSO₄ |
| Cl⁻ | add dilute nitric acid followed by silver nitrate solution | white precipitate | AgCl |
| Br⁻ | add dilute nitric acid followed by silver nitrate solution | cream precipitate | AgBr |
| I⁻ | add dilute nitric acid followed by silver nitrate solution | yellow precipitate | AgI |

Read the table by columns as well as by rows. The **test** column has a
pattern: the acid always comes first (bodyguard duty, removing interfering
ions), then the specific reagent names the ion. The **result** column has
two whites — carbonate (through limewater's CaCO₃) and chloride (AgCl) —
which is why the acid-then-reagent order is not optional: the carbonate's
white precipitate only forms with limewater *after* the acid has already
signalled it by fizzing. The **product** column is the ionic-equation
vocabulary: CO₂, CaCO₃, BaSO₄, AgCl, AgBr, AgI.

The full-anion discipline the table encodes: acid first, reagent second,
colour third, equation fourth. The same discipline reappears if the question
hands you an *unknown* and two or three test results at once — work row by
row, eliminate as you go, and name the compound only when every row agrees.

:::exam-hint
Table-reconstruction questions on 2.48 are free marks if the rows live in
your hand; interpretation questions are the real game. A typical setup gives
a table of results for an unknown compound — sodium hydroxide added (no
visible reaction), dilute hydrochloric acid (no reaction), hydrochloric acid
then barium chloride (no reaction), nitric acid then silver nitrate (white
precipitate) — and asks what it proves. Row by row: not NH₄⁺ (no ammonia), no
carbonate (no fizz), no sulfate (no white BaSO₄), chloride present (white
AgCl). The answer is a chloride whose cation is not ammonium — for example
sodium chloride. Practise that elimination reading until the table reads like
a sentence.
:::

:::misconception
"Two white precipitates means the tests are unreliable." The two whites belong
to different scripts: carbonate's white is calcium carbonate *in limewater*,
after fizzing with hydrochloric acid; chloride's white is silver chloride
*in silver nitrate*, after acidifying with nitric acid. Different acids,
different reagents, different confirmations. An unknown cannot fake its way
across both scripts at once — which is exactly why the exam table asks for
the reagent column as well as the result column.
:::

and interpret this mini-set cold — no fizz with HCl, no precipitate with HCl

:::
