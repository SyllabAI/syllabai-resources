Hold a piece of **damp universal indicator paper or red litmus paper** at the
mouth of the test-tube. Ammonia turns the paper **blue**. It is the only gas
you will meet at International GCSE that is alkaline, so a damp indicator
paper turning blue at the mouth of a tube is ammonia and nothing else.

The chemistry is the ammonia-as-[[alkali]] story from the acids and bases
chapter: dissolved ammonia reacts reversibly with water to generate hydroxide
ions, NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻, and it is those hydroxide ions that flip the
indicator. The dampness of the paper is essential for the same reason as in
the chlorine test — the gas must dissolve in the water on the paper before it
can show its alkaline character.

Keep the two damp-paper tests locked together in your memory: ammonia turns
damp **red** litmus **blue** (alkaline); chlorine turns damp **blue** litmus
**red, then white** (acidic, then bleached). One gas goes up the pH scale,
the other crashes down it and destroys the dye on the way.

:::exam-hint
The script: hold damp red litmus paper (or universal indicator paper) at the
mouth of the tube; it turns blue; therefore ammonia. The examiner's favourite
extension connects this test to the cation tests later in the chapter — when
you warm an ammonium compound with sodium hydroxide solution, the ammonia gas
evolved is identified by exactly this test. So the damp-red-litmus script
appears twice in exam questions: once as the gas test here, once as the
confirmation step in the ammonium-ion test. Write it identically both times.
:::

:::misconception
"Ammonia solution and ammonia gas are the same thing, so the gas test needs a
solution." The gas is tested at the mouth of the tube with damp paper; the
*solution* is the laboratory reagent you met in titrations. They share the
alkaline character because the same reversible reaction with water happens in
both — on the wet paper and in the bottle — but the test for the *gas* never
involves dissolving the sample first. Also, "ammonia turns litmus blue" is
incomplete without *damp*: dry paper gives a weak or absent result.
:::
