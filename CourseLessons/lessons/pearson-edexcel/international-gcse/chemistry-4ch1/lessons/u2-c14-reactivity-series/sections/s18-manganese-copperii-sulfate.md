**Part (a), answered.** Manganese is above copper in the reactivity
series — it lies between aluminium and zinc, and copper is below iron —
so it will react, displacing copper from the copper(II) sulfate
solution. This is a [[displacement-reaction]], and every observation in
it follows from who is gaining and who is losing:

- a pink-brown deposit of copper forms on the manganese;
- the blue colour of the copper(II) sulfate solution fades, ending as a
  very pale pink — virtually colourless — solution of manganese(II)
  sulfate;
- the equation, built from Mn²⁺ and the sulfate ion:

Mn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → MnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

Notice how the setup facts did all the work. "Between aluminium and
zinc" placed manganese above copper; "forms a 2+ ion" named the
products (manganese(II) sulfate, MnSO₄); "very pale pink, almost
colourless" gave the final solution colour. Nothing was measured — the
prediction was executed from position alone, exactly as the reactivity
series intends.

:::exam-hint
Full-mark answers to part (a) carry four marking points: the decision
(will react, because manganese is above copper), the observation pair
(pink-brown deposit AND fading blue solution), the product names
(copper + manganese(II) sulfate) and the balanced equation. The
observation pair is the most commonly dropped — candidates who write
"it reacts" without saying what appears have banked the decision and
abandoned the description marks.
:::

:::misconception
"The product is manganese(III) sulfate or manganese(IV) sulfate —
transition metals have lots of charges, so any of them could be right."
The setup specified manganese forms a 2+ ion, which fixes the formula
as MnSO₄ and the name as manganese(II) sulfate — one manganese ion
balancing one 2− sulfate ion. Reading the given ion charge before
naming products is the difference between a prediction and a guess;
for unfamiliar metals, the charge is always supplied for exactly this
reason.
:::
