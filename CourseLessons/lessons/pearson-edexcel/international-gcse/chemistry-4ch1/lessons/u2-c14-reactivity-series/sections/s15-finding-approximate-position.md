Everything the chapter has built — the water reactions, the acid
reactions, the vigour gradient — compresses into a four-step decision
procedure that places an unknown metal approximately in the
**reactivity series** using nothing but water and dilute acid:

1. Add a very small piece of the metal to some cold water. Any rapid
   reaction places the metal above magnesium in the series.
2. If there is no reaction, add a small amount of the metal to some
   dilute hydrochloric acid (or dilute sulfuric acid), cold.
3. If there is still no reaction in the cold acid, warm it carefully.
   A reaction at this stage places the metal somewhere between
   magnesium and hydrogen.
4. If even the warmed acid does nothing, the metal is probably below
   hydrogen.

The procedure is a bisection: water cuts the series at magnesium,
cold acid cuts it again, warm acid sweeps out the sluggish middle, and
silence everywhere leaves only the below-hydrogen neighbourhood. It
gives a bracket, not a coordinate — "between magnesium and hydrogen" or
"above magnesium" — but a bracket is usually all a prediction needs,
and the safety ordering is built into the sequence: water before acid,
cold before warm, tiny pieces throughout.

:::exam-hint
Describe-the-method questions on this procedure are marked on ORDER.
Water before acid and cold before warm are not stylistic preferences —
they are the safety logic and the discrimination logic of the test at
once. An answer that starts with warmed concentrated acid has invented
a hazard and lost the sequence marks; the procedure only needs the two
reagents named in the chapter and a stepwise "if no reaction, then…"
chain.
:::

:::misconception
"A metal that fizzes in cold dilute acid must be above magnesium,
because fizzing means very reactive." Fizzing in acid only proves the
metal is above hydrogen — magnesium is the reference point the cold
water test uses, not the acid test. Zinc fizzes steadily in dilute acid
and sits below magnesium; iron fizzes slowly and sits lower still. The
two tests work together: water brackets the top of the series, acid
sorts everything between magnesium and hydrogen.
:::

:::
