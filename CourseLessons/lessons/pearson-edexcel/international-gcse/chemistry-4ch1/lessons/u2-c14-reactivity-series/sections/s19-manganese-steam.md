**Part (b), answered.** Manganese sits above hydrogen in the reactivity
series, and metals above hydrogen react with steam to give hydrogen and
the metal oxide — so manganese is expected to react. The products are
hydrogen and the metal oxide, which for a 2+ ion is manganese(II)
oxide:

Mn(s) + H₂O(g) → MnO(s) + H₂(g)

One sentence in the model answer deserves as much attention as the
chemistry: you could not predict the colour of the manganese(II) oxide,
and the question does not ask you to do this. Position predicts reaction
behaviour — that a reaction happens and what the products are. It does
not hand you every physical property of the products, and inventing a
colour for an oxide you have never seen is not prediction; it is
fabrication with confidence. A prediction answer that stops exactly
where its evidence stops is a complete answer.

:::exam-hint
The steam products rule does the work here: metal + steam → metal oxide
+ hydrogen, with the oxidation number supplied by the setup (2+ ion →
MnO). The discipline to copy is the stopping rule: answer what was
asked. If a question wants products and an equation, properties not
requested — colour, density, smell — add nothing and risk everything.
:::

:::misconception
"Metals above hydrogen make hydroxides with steam, just like they do
with cold water." Cold water gives the hydroxide; steam gives the
oxide — the state of the water decides the product family. Writing
Mn(OH)₂ plus hydrogen for a steam reaction mixes the two product rules
and loses the product mark even though the decision to react was
correct. Watch the state symbol on the water: (g) means steam rules.
:::
