The [[ph-scale]] runs from about 0 to about 14, and each number is a verdict
on a solution: how acidic or how alkaline it is. The scale pivots on 7. Below
7 the solution is acidic, and the further below, the more acidic; above 7 it
is alkaline, and the further above, the more alkaline; exactly 7 is neutral.
Vinegar, for example — a solution of ethanoic acid — sits a few units below
7, while drain-clearing sodium hydroxide sits near the top.

The scale is not just a number line: the specification wants you to sort
solutions into five named bands. Learn the boundaries and an everyday example
of each, because classification questions ask for the band by name.

| Classification | pH | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| [[strongly-acidic]] | 0–3 | hydrochloric acid |
| [[weakly-acidic]] | 4–6 | ethanoic acid (vinegar) |
| neutral | 7 | sodium chloride (solution) |
| [[weakly-alkaline]] | 8–10 | ammonia (solution) |
| [[strongly-alkaline]] | 11–14 | sodium hydroxide |

Two conventions earn easy marks. When you write pH, it is a small p and a
capital H — the H is the symbol for hydrogen, and by the end of this chapter
you will see exactly why the symbol belongs there. And when a question gives
you a pH value, answer with the band word ("weakly acidic"), not just
"acidic": the band is the classification, and the mark scheme wants it.

[[litmus]] is the indicator attached to this band system. It works either as
a solution or soaked into litmus paper, and it sorts any solution for you:
red in acid, blue in alkali.

:::key-point
Litmus can be used as a solution or as litmus paper — either form gives the
same red-in-acid, blue-in-alkali verdict. When writing pH, it is a small p
and a big H (the symbol for hydrogen).
:::

:::exam-hint
A pH meter measures pH far more accurately than any indicator — in method
questions, naming a pH meter (with its digital readout) is the accurate
instrument, while universal indicator is the approximate one. And treat the
five bands as approximate boundaries, not walls: the edges depend on
concentration, so a 0.10 mol/dm³ solution of ethanoic acid already measures
pH 2.88 — inside the strongly acidic band even though it is a modest
concentration of a weak acid.
:::

:::misconception
"'Strongly acidic' means a high concentration of acid has been dissolved."
The band describes the pH of the solution, not the amount of acid dissolved
in it. A modest 0.10 mol/dm³ solution of ethanoic acid sits at pH 2.88,
deep in the strongly acidic band, because pH tracks the hydrogen ions in
solution — not the mass of acid you started with. Concentration and pH are
related, but they are not the same measurement.
:::

:::quick-check
Question-bank join: spec 2.29 classification items. Classify each by band:
hydrochloric acid, vinegar, sodium chloride solution, ammonia solution,
sodium hydroxide solution. Then write the symbol correctly from memory —
small p, capital H. If all five bands came out right, you are ready to
measure.
:::
