Suppose a calculation says you should get **12.5 g of copper sulfate
crystals**, but the experiment delivers only **11.2 g**. Name both
numbers:

- **12.5 g is the [[theoretical-yield]]** — obtained by carrying out a
  moles calculation from the equation;
- **11.2 g is the [[actual-yield]]** — what the experiment actually
  produced, and it will be **given to you in the question**.

The formula is then one division:

$$\text{percentage yield} = \frac{\text{actual yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}} \times 100$$

Here: (11.2 / 12.5) × 100 = **89.6%**.

The sanity bound every marker looks for: **the
percentage yield should always come out as less than 100%**. If it comes
out above 100%, either you have made a mistake in the calculation, or the
question is deliberately continuing by asking what went wrong
experimentally. Its own example: if you are making a salt (Chapter 17),
a percentage yield above 100% can mean the salt **was not completely
dry** — extra water inflating the "product" mass.


:::plain-words
One division, one discipline: [[percentage-yield]] = actual ÷ theoretical × 100 — 11.2/12.5
gives 89.6%. The [[actual-yield]] is always the number the question gave you; the
[[theoretical-yield]] is always the one you calculated. Above 100% means a mistake or wet
product — never better-than-perfect chemistry.
:::
:::key-point
Percentage yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100. The actual yield is
always the given number; the theoretical yield is always the one *you*
calculate from the equation. Never divide the other way.
:::

:::exam-hint
Identify which number is which before dividing — that is the entire
skill in this calculation type. Clues: the theoretical yield's context is
"calculate/could/might produce"; the actual yield's context is "she
made/obtained/collected". Worked example 7 (next section) shows the
theoretical yield being *derived* through a full two-equation mole
chain before the division — that derivation is where most of the marks
live. Real mark scheme of record (4CH1/1C JUN 2019 Q14(f)(ii), actual yield 23.92 g of copper(II) sulfate crystals): M1 “(23.92 ÷ 29.9) × 100” — “OR (23.92 ÷ M3 from (i)) × 100”, the mark scheme explicitly crediting the earlier theoretical-yield answer — M2 “= 80(%)” with “ALLOW any number of sig figs” and “correct answer without working scores 2”.
:::

:::misconception
"A percentage yield of 89.6% means 10.4% of the atoms vanished."
Conservation of mass never breaks: the "missing" 10.4% of product is
unreacted starting material, product lost in transfers or purification,
or mass diverted into **side-reaction products** you did not count.
The yield measures how much of your product *ended up in the bottle*,
not whether atoms were destroyed.
:::
