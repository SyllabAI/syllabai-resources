**To find the percentage by mass of oxygen in sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃**
(Aᵣ values: Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16).

Hold one picture beside this calculation: 1-mole quantities of carbon
(12 g), sulfur (32 g), iron (56 g), copper (63.5 g) and magnesium (24 g)
— the bridge from formula-mass arithmetic to the mole, which the very
next section makes explicit. Here, the
calculation runs:

1. **Mᵣ(Na₂CO₃)** = (2 × 23) + 12 + (3 × 16) = 46 + 12 + 48 = **106**
2. **Oxygen's contribution**: there are 3 oxygens in the formula, so the
   total mass of oxygen is 3 × 16 = **48**
3. **Percentage**: (48 / 106) × 100 ≈ **45.3%**

Step 2 in words: "48 of this 106 is due to the oxygen (remember that
there are 3 oxygens in the formula, so the total mass of oxygen is
3 × 16 = 48)." The multiply-before-you-divide structure is the
whole method: count the element's atoms, total their mass, divide by the
compound's Mᵣ.

:::exam-hint
Carry the "total mass of the element" as its own named step (3 × 16 = 48)
before dividing by 106. That presentation is also self-checking: the three element percentages (Na, C, O) must sum
to 100% — a quick sanity check that catches subscript slips on the spot.
:::

:::misconception
"Oxygen is 16/106 ≈ 15% of the compound." That divides *one* oxygen's mass
by the whole. The formula has **three** oxygens: 48/106 ≈ 45.3%. The
subscript is part of the mass arithmetic, not decoration — every atom in
the formula contributes its Aᵣ to the element's share.
:::
