Heat a salt that contains water of crystallisation and the water is
driven off, leaving the **[[anhydrous]]** salt behind. Barium chloride —
a **[[hydrated-salt]]** — is the commonly used example, because the
barium chloride itself does not decompose even on quite strong heating —
only the water leaves.

**The experiment** (heating the crystals in a crucible) hands you three
masses:

- mass of crucible = **30.00 g**
- mass of crucible + barium chloride crystals, BaCl₂·nH₂O = **32.44 g**
- mass of crucible + anhydrous barium chloride, BaCl₂ = **32.08 g**

**Finding n** means finding the ratio of the number of moles of BaCl₂ to
the number of moles of water — "it's just another empirical formula
calculation" (Aᵣ: H = 1, O = 16, Cl = 35.5, Ba = 137; Mᵣ of BaCl₂ = 137 +
2 × 35.5 = 208, Mᵣ of H₂O = 18):

- mass of BaCl₂ = 32.08 − 30.00 = **2.08 g**
- mass of water = 32.44 − 32.08 = **0.36 g**

The mole table:

|                       | BaCl₂       | H₂O       |
|-----------------------|-------------|-----------|
| masses/g              | 2.08        | 0.36      |
| ÷ Mᵣ for moles        | 2.08/208    | 0.36/18   |
| number of moles       | 0.0100      | 0.020     |
| ratio of moles        | 1           | 2         |
| empirical formula     | **BaCl₂·2H₂O** |        |

The unknown n was 2 — found by the identical divide-by-Mᵣ,
divide-by-smallest machinery as every other empirical formula in this
chapter. The only novelty is that one of the "elements" is a whole water
molecule.

:::exam-hint
Two Mᵣ values must be *assembled* before the table can start: BaCl₂ =
208 (137 + 2 × 35.5) and H₂O = 18. Show both constructions — the marks
sit in the assembly, and the table cannot misfire if 208 and 18 are
written explicitly. Note also which masses produce which subtraction:
crystals-minus-crucible gives the *hydrate*, anhydrous-minus-crucible
gives the *salt*, and their difference is the *water*.
:::

:::misconception
"n is found by dividing the mass of crystals by the mass of water." That
ignores the salt entirely: n is the ratio of **moles** — 0.020 mol water
to 0.010 mol BaCl₂ — not of masses (0.36/2.08 = 0.17 would be nonsense).
Convert each mass to moles with its own Mᵣ *first*; the ratio comes only
after both conversions.
:::
