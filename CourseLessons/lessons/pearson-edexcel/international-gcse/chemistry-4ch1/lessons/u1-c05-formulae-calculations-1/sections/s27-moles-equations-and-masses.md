Section 25 taught you to read an equation in moles. This section cashes
that in for **masses**. Take the methane equation again:

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Interpreted in moles: **1 mol CH₄ reacts with 2 mol O₂** to form 1 mol
CO₂ and 2 mol H₂O. The book's KEY POINT adds the molecular bookkeeping
beneath it: 2 mol of O₂ contains twice as many molecules as 1 mol of
CH₄ — the proportions you weigh out are the proportions that the
molecules react in.

Now attach masses, using the Mᵣ values (CH₄ = 16, O₂ = 32, CO₂ = 44,
H₂O = 18):

- 1 mol CH₄ = **16 g** and 2 mol O₂ = **64 g** react exactly...
- ...to form 1 mol CO₂ = **44 g** and 2 mol H₂O = **36 g**

Check the bookkeeping with the conservation instinct from section 4:
16 + 64 = 80 g on the left; 44 + 36 = 80 g on the right. Mass conserved,
moles proportioned — the equation is now a quantitative recipe, and the
book's Figure 5.16 (glowing limestone) previews the first full problem
in the next section: limestone, impure calcium carbonate, decomposes on
heating to calcium oxide (quicklime) and carbon dioxide — a **thermal
decomposition** with Mᵣ(CaCO₃) = 100 and Mᵣ(CaO) = 56, the two numbers
you will need.

:::key-point
The mass scale of any reaction comes from its mole recipe: multiply each
mole count by the substance's mass of 1 mole. Conservation is the built-in
check — total mass of products must equal total mass of reactants.
:::

:::exam-hint
The four masses (16 g, 64 g, 44 g, 36 g) are worth deriving rather than
memorising: each is moles × Mᵣ, straight from the equation's big numbers.
Examiners ask this pattern constantly — "what mass of oxygen reacts with
16 g of methane?" — and the answer is always the same three moves:
equation → moles → mass.
:::

:::misconception
"If the equation says CH₄ + 2O₂, I need equal *masses* of methane and
oxygen." The equation proportions are moles (1 : 2), not grams. Converting
to masses gives 16 g : 64 g — oxygen's extra factor of 4 comes from its
Mᵣ being double methane's while you need double the moles. Confusing the
mole row with a mass row is the error that makes conservation look
broken.
:::
