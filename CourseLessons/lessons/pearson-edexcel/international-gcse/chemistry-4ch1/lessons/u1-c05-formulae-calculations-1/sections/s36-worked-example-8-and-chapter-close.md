**Example 8.** Copper reacts with concentrated nitric acid according to
the equation:

Cu(s) + 4HNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l) + 2NO₂(g)

**3.2 g of copper** is reacted with **0.40 mol** of concentrated nitric
acid. Work out which reagent is in excess.

**Convert the copper mass to moles first** (Aᵣ: Cu = 63.5):

number of moles of Cu = 3.2 / 63.5 ≈ **0.050 mol**

**Run each reagent through the equation.** The equation says 1 mol Cu
reacts with **4 mol HNO₃**, so:

0.050 mol Cu reacts with 4 × 0.050 = **0.20 mol HNO₃**

We have **more** than 0.20 mol HNO₃ (we have 0.40 mol) — therefore
**HNO₃ is in excess** (and the copper is the limiting reagent).

The book's follow-through: if the question went further and asked for the
amount of NO₂ produced *using these quantities*, you would have to use
the **copper's** moles — not all the HNO₃ is used up. From the equation,
2 mol Cu produce 2 mol NO₂, so 0.050 mol Cu gives 2 × 0.050 =
**0.10 mol NO₂**. This is exactly the section 35 law: the limiting
reagent feeds the product calculation.

:::summary
- Equations must **balance** — same atoms out as in. Balance with big
  numbers only; **never change a formula**. State symbols: (s), (l),
  (g), (aq).
- Big numbers = coefficients = **moles**; subscripts belong to the atom
  (or bracket) before them (2H₂SO₄ = 4 H, 2 S, 8 O).
- **Mᵣ** = sum of the Aᵣ values (hydrated salts include their waters:
  CuSO₄·5H₂O = 159.5 + 90 = 249.5); **mass of 1 mole = Mᵣ in grams**.
- **1 mol = 6 × 10²³ particles** (the Avogadro constant); moles =
  mass ÷ mass of 1 mole.
- **Empirical formula** = simplest whole-number ratio (from masses or
  percentages → assume 100 g); **molecular formula** = empirical × n,
  n = Mᵣ(compound) ÷ Mᵣ(empirical).
- Formulae *from experiments*: metal oxides by combustion or reduction
  (spec 1.36); water from collected masses; n in BaCl₂·nH₂O.
- Reacting masses: **mass → moles → equation ratio → moles → mass**.
  The limiting reagent (never the excess one) feeds the product.
- **Percentage yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100** — always < 100%
  unless something went wrong (or the product is wet).
:::
