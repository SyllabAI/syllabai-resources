**Example 7.** A student reacted **2.40 g of copper(II) oxide (CuO)** with
hot sulfuric acid. She made **5.21 g of copper(II) sulfate crystals
(CuSO₄·5H₂O)**. The two reactions run in sequence: the copper(II) oxide
reacts with the sulfuric acid to form copper(II) sulfate in solution, and
the copper(II) sulfate then crystallises from that solution with five
waters of crystallisation attached — CuSO₄ → CuSO₄·5H₂O, one formula unit
giving one formula unit of crystals.

**Deriving the theoretical yield.** First, the moles of copper(II)
oxide — we have enough information to calculate them. Mᵣ(CuO) = 63.5 + 16
= 79.5:

number of moles of CuO = 2.40 / 79.5 = **0.0302 mol**

From the first reaction, **0.0302 mol CuO produces 0.0302 mol CuSO₄**
(one formula unit → one formula unit). From the crystallisation step,
**0.0302 mol CuSO₄ produces 0.0302 mol CuSO₄·5H₂O**.

Now convert to mass — the Mᵣ of the crystals is the one you built in
section 12: Mᵣ(CuSO₄·5H₂O) = **249.5**.

mass = 0.0302 × 249.5 = **7.53 g**

This is the **theoretical yield** of copper(II) sulfate crystals.

**The percentage yield.** The actual yield is what the experiment
obtained: **5.21 g**.

percentage yield = (5.21 / 7.53) × 100 = **69.2%**

:::key-point
A 1 : 1 : 1 mole chain (CuO → CuSO₄ → CuSO₄·5H₂O) means the *moles never
change* through the two steps — only the mass does, when each substance's
own Mᵣ is applied. The chain's job is to get you from the starting mass
to the theoretical yield of the *product as collected* (crystals
included).
:::

:::exam-hint
Two details make this example exam-hard. First, the product's Mᵣ must
include the **water of crystallisation** (249.5, not 159.5 for CuSO₄) —
use the wrong one and the whole calculation dies at the last step
(despite correct moles). Second, number discipline: 0.0302 mol carries
three significant figures because the mass data does.
The 69.2% then lands below 100% as it should (section 33's bound).
:::

:::misconception
"The theoretical yield is 2.40 g — the copper(II) oxide all becomes
product, so the masses match." The *moles* carry over 1 : 1, not the
masses: 0.0302 mol of CuO weighs 2.40 g, but the same 0.0302 mol of
CuSO₄·5H₂O weighs 0.0302 × 249.5 = 7.53 g — the sulfate and the five
waters add enormous mass. Each step multiplies by its own substance's
mass of 1 mole; only the mole count is conserved.
:::
