This chapter opens with one of the biggest numbers you will meet in
chemistry: a single gold bar contains almost **4 × 10²⁵ gold atoms** — that is 4 followed by 25 noughts. Counting
atoms one by one is obviously hopeless, so chemistry needs a way of
counting particles *by weighing*. That is what this chapter builds: the
**[[mole]]**, the unit chemists use for amount of substance, and the
calculations that flow from it — formulae, balanced equations, reacting
masses and yields.

:::prior-knowledge
You will use ideas from earlier chapters throughout: **balancing and
reading formulae** builds on writing word and symbol equations (Chapter 1);
the **relative atomic mass** recap in this chapter leans directly on
isotopes and weighted averages from Chapter 3 (page 26); and the
**Periodic Table** supplies the Aᵣ values you will be given in every
calculation.
:::

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

- write **word equations** and **balanced chemical equations**, including
  **state symbols**, for familiar and unfamiliar reactions;
- calculate **relative formula masses (Mᵣ)** from relative atomic masses
  (Aᵣ), including for hydrated crystals such as CuSO₄·5H₂O;
- interconvert **masses in grams** and **amounts in moles**, and use the
  **[[avogadro-constant]]**;
- determine **empirical and molecular formulae** from experimental data —
  including by combustion and reduction of metal oxides, and water of
  crystallisation experiments;
- calculate **reacting masses** from chemical equations, decide which
  reagent is **in excess**, and calculate **percentage yields**.

:::exam-hint
The chapter's own objectives list is your revision checklist: every one of
its bullets is an examinable skill. Notice what the first objective
goes out of its way to emphasise — you must be able to write balanced
equations **including state symbols** both for reactions you have studied
*and* for unfamiliar reactions where you are given suitable information.
The unfamiliar-reaction half is where marks are commonly lost.
:::

:::misconception
"It says moles — so this chapter is about small furry animals burrowing
in the soil." The [[mole]] is a *counting unit*, like "a dozen" but for
6 × 10²³ particles. Nothing about it is furry; everything about it is
arithmetic. Chapter 3 gave you a taste of huge numbers (6 × 10²³ atoms in
the sports-hall thought experiment); this chapter turns that number into
a working tool.
:::
