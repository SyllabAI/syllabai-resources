The specification asks you to describe two reversible reactions in
detail, and they make a matched pair: one change driven by heat acting
on a solid crystal, one change driven by heat acting on a white powder
that splits into two gases.

**Reaction 1 — hydrated copper(II) sulfate.** On gentle heating the blue
crystals split into white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate and water; on
adding water the change runs backwards and blue crystals form again,
releasing heat. The forward direction needs heat supplied; the reverse
direction gives heat out. Both directions have been observed directly
in s02, and the equation carries the double arrow to show it:

CuSO4·5H2O(s) ⇌ CuSO4(s) + 5H2O(l)

The classification matters as much as the observation. The forward
change — one substance broken into simpler substances by heat alone —
is an example of [[thermal-decomposition]]. Any time heat alone pulls a
compound apart, that word is available, and examiners like to see it
attached to this reaction.

**Reaction 2 — ammonium chloride.** Warm a tube containing white
ammonium chloride crystals and they vanish from the bottom of the tube
and reappear as a white solid further up, where the glass is cooler.
Heating ammonium chloride splits it into two colourless gases, ammonia
and hydrogen chloride. The equations tell the story in three lines.
Splitting needs heat:

NH4Cl(s) → NH3(g) + HCl(g)  (heat needed)

The gases recombine further up the tube, where it is cooler:

NH3(g) + HCl(g) → NH4Cl(s)  (heat released)

And because the whole thing reverses when the conditions change from hot
to cool, the summary equation carries the reversible arrow:

NH3(g) + HCl(g) ⇌ NH4Cl(s)

The recombination step is a reaction between an acid and a base:
hydrogen chloride is the acid and ammonia is the base, meeting to make
the white solid ammonium chloride. The two reactions also hand you the
chapter's directional vocabulary. In an equation as written, the
[[forward-reaction]] runs left to right and the [[reverse-reaction]]
runs right to left — and for the ammonium chloride system, which
direction runs depends entirely on whether the surroundings are hotter
or cooler than the reaction needs.

:::key-point
The forward change in the copper(II) sulfate reaction — one substance
broken into simpler substances by heat alone — is an example of thermal
decomposition.
:::

:::key-point
The recombination step of the ammonium chloride reaction is a reaction
between an acid (HCl) and a base (NH3), producing the salt ammonium
chloride.
:::

:::misconception
"The ammonium chloride disappeared, so it vaporised like dry ice."
Vaporising is a physical change — the substance would condense back in
the same place it left. Here the white solid returns in a *different
place* (further up the tube, where it is cooler) and only after its
molecules were split into two different gases and recombined. Movement
plus splitting plus re-forming is a chemical reversal, which is exactly
why this reaction is a named reversible reaction and not an evaporation
demo.
:::

:::exam-hint
Describe questions on heating ammonium chloride want the full narrative:
white crystals disappear from the bottom of the tube and reappear
further up; the solid splits into the colourless gases ammonia and
hydrogen chloride; the gases recombine where it is cooler. The mark
scheme's favourite discriminator is *where* the solid reappears and why
— cooler glass, further up — so tie the position of the new white solid
to the temperature change when you write.
:::

:::quick-check
From memory: write the splitting equation for ammonium chloride and say
which direction needs heat and which releases it. Then classify the
copper(II) sulfate forward change with its proper name. If you can also
state which gas is the acid and which the base in the recombination
step, the whole of the core track's reaction list is secure.
:::
