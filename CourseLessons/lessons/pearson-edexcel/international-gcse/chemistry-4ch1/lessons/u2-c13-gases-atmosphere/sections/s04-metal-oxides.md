When an element burns in oxygen the product is an **[[oxide]]**, and
each element announces its combustion with a characteristic flame and a
characteristic product. Three elements are named by the specification —
magnesium, hydrogen and sulfur — and you should be able to give the
observation, the equation and the product for each.

**Magnesium** burns in oxygen with an **extremely bright white flame**,
leaving a white, powdery ash of magnesium oxide:

2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s)

The white powder is not very soluble in water, but a very small amount
does dissolve, and the tiny quantity that does reacts to give an
**alkaline** solution of magnesium hydroxide:

MgO(s) + H₂O(l) → Mg(OH)₂(aq)

That slant — metal oxide meeting water and coming out alkaline — is the
first hint of the metal/non-metal oxide split, which the next section
turns into a full set of rules.

**Sulfur** burns in oxygen with a **blue flame**, producing a
poisonous, **colourless** gas, sulfur dioxide:

S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)

The gas dissolves in water to form an acidic solution of sulfurous
acid:

SO₂(g) + H₂O(l) → H₂SO₃(aq)

(Section 3's key point is worth repeating in equation form: the acid
here is sulfurous acid, H₂SO₃ — sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, is a different
substance with two more oxygens.)

**Hydrogen** burns in oxygen with a **pale blue flame**, and the only
product is water:

2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)

Hydrogen's combustion has a second face: ignite a *mixture* of hydrogen
and oxygen — rather than burning hydrogen in a jar of oxygen — and it
explodes with a characteristic squeaky pop. That explosion is the basis
of the standard laboratory test for hydrogen gas, which lives in
Chapter 18 (u2:c18) with the rest of the gas tests.

:::exam-hint
Combustion questions are observation-plus-equation questions, and the
marks split between them. The observation bank to memorise: magnesium —
*extremely bright white flame, white powder*; sulfur — *blue flame,
poisonous colourless gas*; hydrogen — *pale blue flame, water formed*.
Note the deliberate contrast: sulfur's **flame** is blue and hydrogen's
**flame** is pale blue, but sulfur's product is a colourless gas while
hydrogen's product is a colourless liquid. Writing "yellow" or "green"
for sulfur dioxide is a lost mark — the yellow you may be picturing is
the sulfur element itself.
:::

:::misconception
"Burning is the same as heating — both need a flame." A flame is the
*sign* of combustion, not its cause. **Combustion** is a chemical
reaction: the element combining with oxygen, with energy released.
Heating magnesium gently in air *without* ignition does nothing; ignite
it, and the magnesium atoms react with oxygen molecules to build magnesium
oxide. In equations, the oxygen is a **reactant** — if O₂ is not on the
left-hand side, the reaction is not combustion.
:::
