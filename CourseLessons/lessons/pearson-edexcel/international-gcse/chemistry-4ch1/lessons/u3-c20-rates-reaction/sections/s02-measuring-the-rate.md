The [[rate-of-reaction]] is the speed at which the amount of reactant
decreases, or the amount of product increases, during a chemical reaction.
It is measured as a change in the concentration (or amount) of a reactant
or product per unit time — per second, or per minute.

rate of reaction = (change in concentration, volume or mass) ÷ (time)

Because you can follow either side of the equation, there are two families
of measurement. Follow a **reactant disappearing**: the mass of a solid as
it dissolves away, or the concentration of an acid as it is used up. Or
follow a **product appearing**: the volume of gas collected in a syringe,
or the mass lost as a gas escapes an open flask. Whichever quantity you
choose, the arithmetic is the same — divide the change by the time it took.

The choice of what to measure is decided by the reaction itself. A reaction
that gives off a gas invites you to measure gas volume or mass loss; a
reaction that changes colour or opacity invites you to time how long a
marker takes to disappear from view. In the next section you will put this
into practice with the chapter's core experiment, following a gas being
lost from a reacting flask on a balance.

:::exam-hint
In the exam, rate questions are nearly always "calculate the average rate
between…" items. Write the definition chain first: rate = change ÷ time;
identify the change from the data (mass lost, volume collected,
concentration fallen); identify the time interval. Quote units with every
answer — g/s, cm³/s or mol/dm³ per second — because an unnumbered rate
loses the unit mark even when the arithmetic is perfect.
:::

:::misconception
"Rate is a property of the reaction, so it stays the same from start to
finish." Almost no reaction behaves that way. The rate at the start — when
reactant is plentiful — is the fastest the reaction will ever be, and it
falls as reactants are consumed. That is why every rate value must name its
time interval: "0.47 g in the first minute" and "0.20 g in the second"
describe the same reaction at different moments.
:::
