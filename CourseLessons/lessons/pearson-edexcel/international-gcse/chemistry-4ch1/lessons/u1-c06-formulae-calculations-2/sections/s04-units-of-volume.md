Volumes of gases *and* of solutions are measured in cubic centimetres (cm³)
or cubic decimetres (dm³) — the book also lists the litre, but the Key Point
in the last section already told you the house preference: **1 litre =
1 dm³ = 1000 cm³**, and you should quote cubic decimetres rather than litres.
Everything in this chapter runs on that one conversion, so it is worth being
automatic about it.

The book's Figure 6.3 makes the direction of the conversion visual. Because
1 dm³ is equivalent to 1000 cm³, converting **dm³ to cm³ means going from 1
to 1000, so you multiply by 1000**; converting **cm³ to dm³ means going from
1000 down to 1, so you divide by 1000**. The book says the most important
thing you need to be able to do here is exactly this conversion — the molar
volume comes in two sizes (24 dm³ and 24 000 cm³) and the concentration unit
is built on the dm³, so every calculation in this chapter will ask you to
pick the right one.

:::key-point
1 dm³ = 1000 cm³ = 1 litre. To convert dm³ → cm³, multiply by 1000; to
convert cm³ → dm³, divide by 1000. (Chunk 0270; the Figure 6.3 ladder.)
:::

:::exam-hint
The lost-mark scan in every gas-volume or concentration answer: **which unit
is my volume in, and which unit does my constant want?** Molar volume 24 dm³
pairs with volumes in dm³; 24 000 cm³ pairs with volumes in cm³. Mixing them
throws answers out by a factor of 1000 — the book's own warning later in the
chapter ("Look carefully: is the volume given in cm³ or dm³?") exists because
that error is the most common one in the paper.
:::

:::misconception
"dm³ means cubic metres." It doesn't — the *d* stands for **deci-**, a tenth:
a decimetre is one tenth of a metre, so a cubic decimetre is (0.1 m)³ = one
thousandth of a cubic metre. The cubic metre (m³) is 1000 dm³ — a thousand
times bigger again. Confusing dm³ with m³ is a factor-of-1000 error in the
opposite direction from the cm³ slip.
:::

:::maths-skills
The conversion is a unit-prefix ladder, the same skill as mm → cm → m in
physics: each step of 10³. Write the ladder once (cm³ —×1000→ dm³ —×1000→ m³)
and read off the direction for any problem. Exam-style: convert 350 cm³ to
dm³ (350 ÷ 1000 = 0.350 dm³); convert 2.4 dm³ to cm³ (2.4 × 1000 = 2400 cm³).
Quote the unit in the answer — an unlabelled number is a lost mark in every
calculation question of this chapter.
:::
