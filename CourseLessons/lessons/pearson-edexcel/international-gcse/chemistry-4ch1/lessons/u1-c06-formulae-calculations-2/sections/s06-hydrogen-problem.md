The chapter's first full gas-volume problem runs the three stages in the
direction that feels backwards: from a **volume** of gas wanted to a **mass**
of solid needed. Aluminium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid:

2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂(g)

*What mass of aluminium would you need to add to an [[excess]] of dilute
hydrochloric acid so that you produced 100 cm³ of hydrogen at room
temperature and pressure? (Aᵣ of Al = 27; molar volume = 24 000 cm³ at rtp.)*

**Stage 1 — moles of what we can.** We can only calculate the moles of the
hydrogen; the mass of aluminium is the unknown. The volume is in cm³, so use
the molar volume as 24 000 cm³: number of moles of H₂ = 100 ÷ 24 000 =
**0.00417 mol** (which the book also writes in standard form as
4.17 × 10⁻³ mol).

**Stage 2 — equation ratio.** The big numbers say 3 mol of H₂ are produced
from 2 mol of Al. So the moles of Al are **two-thirds** of the moles of H₂:
0.00417 × 2/3 = **0.00278 mol** of aluminium.

**Stage 3 — convert to the asked quantity.** mass = number of moles × mass of
1 mol = 0.00278 × 27 = **0.075 g of Al**.

The book's marginal HINT and REMINDER on this problem both earn their place
as examiner advice — they are quoted below as boxes, and they target the two
classic slips: mangling the 3-to-2 ratio, and then double-counting the 2 in
the final mass step.

:::exam-hint
The book's HINT on the ratio step: to go from 3 (H₂) to 2 (Al), divide the 3
by 3 to get 1 and multiply by 2 to get 2 — in other words, multiply the moles
of H₂ by **2/3**. The same divide-then-multiply routine works for any ratio
the equation hands you, forwards or backwards.
:::

:::misconception
"Moles of Al = 0.00417 × 54, because the equation has 2Al and Aᵣ of Al is 27."
That double-counts the 2 — the book's REMINDER says exactly this: **multiply
by 27, not 54**. The 2 in front of the Al was already used in the ratio step
(2 mol Al per 3 mol H₂). The mass of 0.00278 mol of Al is fixed at 0.00278 ×
27: the mass of a given number of moles cannot depend on what the aluminium
is reacting with.
:::

:::cross-curricular
Maths — standard form and ratios. The intermediate answer 0.00417 mol is
written in the book as 4.17 × 10⁻³ mol: small mole counts in gas problems
regularly demand confident standard form, and the "divide by 3, multiply by
2" ratio move is the same proportion skill as map scales and recipe
scaling. Both are Paper-2 quantitative skills shared with the maths
qualification.
:::
