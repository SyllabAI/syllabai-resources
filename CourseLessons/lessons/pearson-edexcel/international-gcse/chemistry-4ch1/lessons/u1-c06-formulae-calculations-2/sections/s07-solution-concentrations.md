The solutions half of the chapter begins with the two concentration units.
Concentrations can be measured in **g/dm³** or in **mol/dm³** — also written g dm⁻³ and
mol dm⁻³, and read as "grams per cubic decimetre" and "moles per cubic
decimetre". Both units ride on the **[[cubic-decimetre]]** from the last
unit section: remember that 1 dm³ is the same as 1 litre. (You may also meet
the symbol **[[molarity]]** — a concentration quoted as "2 M" means
2 mol/dm³.)

The core skill is converting **between g/dm³ and mol/dm³**, and the point
is reassuring: *this is no different from converting moles into
grams and vice versa* — multiply or divide by the Mᵣ. It makes no difference
that the substance is dissolved in 1 dm³ of solution; the arithmetic is the
Chapter-5 mass↔moles ladder wearing a per-dm³ hat.

The worked case: sea water with a sodium chloride concentration of
**35.1 g/dm³** — find the concentration in mol/dm³ (Aᵣ: Na = 23, Cl = 35.5).
1 mol of NaCl has a mass of 58.5 g. Every dm³ of solution holds 35.1 g, so
the moles in every dm³ are 35.1 ÷ 58.5 = **0.600 mol**. The concentration is
therefore **0.600 mol/dm³** — sea water holds about 0.6 moles of NaCl per
cubic decimetre.

:::key-point
Two concentration units, one bridge: **g/dm³ → mol/dm³ is ÷ Mᵣ; mol/dm³ →
g/dm³ is × Mᵣ** (per dm³ of solution, exactly as mass ↔ moles in Chapter 5).
:::

:::exam-hint
Quote concentration answers **with the unit the question asked for** — a bare
"0.600" scores nothing when the question said mol/dm³. And when a question
hands you a concentration in one unit and the equation mole-ratio arithmetic
needs moles, convert to mol/dm³ first: mole ratios only ever apply to
moles, never to grams.
:::

:::misconception
"Concentration in g/dm³ tells you how many moles you have." Not until you
divide by Mᵣ — 35.1 g/dm³ of NaCl and 35.1 g/dm³ of NaHCO₃ are different
mole counts, because the formula units have different masses. The g/dm³
number is a mass in every dm³; only ÷ Mᵣ turns it into an amount of
substance.
:::

:::maths-skills
The two-unit ladder is a proportional-reasoning chain: g/dm³ —(÷ Mᵣ)→
mol/dm³, and dm³ of solution —(× concentration)→ moles. Exam-style with those
numbers: 35.1 g/dm³ of NaCl (Mᵣ 58.5) = 0.600 mol/dm³; then 2.0 dm³
of that sea water holds 2.0 × 0.600 = 1.2 mol of NaCl. Three conversions,
one skill: keep the units attached to every number and let them cancel like
fractions.
:::
