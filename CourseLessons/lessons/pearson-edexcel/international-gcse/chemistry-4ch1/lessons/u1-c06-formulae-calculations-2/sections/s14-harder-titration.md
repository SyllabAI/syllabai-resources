Same apparatus, same stages, one twist: the equation's ratio is not 1 : 1.
*25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution of unknown concentration was titrated
with dilute sulfuric acid of concentration 0.050 mol/dm³. 20.0 cm³ of the
acid was required to neutralise the alkali. Find the concentration of the
sodium hydroxide solution in mol/dm³.*

2NaOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)

**Stage 1 — moles of what we can.** This time everything is known about the
sulfuric acid: moles of H₂SO₄ = (20.0 ÷ 1000) × 0.050 = **0.0010 mol**.

**Stage 2 — the ratio is the whole difficulty.** As the book puts it, *the
equation proportions aren't 1 : 1 this time — that's what makes the
calculation slightly different from the last one.* The equation shows 1 mol
of sulfuric acid reacts with **2 mol** of sodium hydroxide, so moles of NaOH
= 2 × 0.0010 = **0.0020 mol**.

**Stage 3 — concentration.** That 0.0020 mol must have been in the 25.0 cm³
(25.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.025 dm³) of sodium hydroxide: concentration =
0.0020 ÷ 0.025 = **0.080 mol/dm³**.

The book also flags the reverse family — *instead of calculating a
concentration from titration results, you may be asked to calculate what
volume of a solution is needed to neutralise something else* — and hands the
shape to Example 4 in the next section.

:::exam-hint
When the ratio is 2 : 1, say which way it doubles *in words* before
multiplying: "1 mol acid reacts with 2 mol alkali, so moles NaOH = 2 × moles
H₂SO₄". Mark schemes routinely place the ratio mark before the arithmetic —
and note the trap direction: here the *unknown* (NaOH) gets the 2, so the
answer 0.080 mol/dm³ is **bigger** than the acid's 0.050 mol/dm³. A quick
proportion sanity check (more concentrated alkali for the same neutralising
power in less volume... here slightly more volume, so mildly more
concentrated) catches inverted ratios.
:::

:::misconception
"Balance the equation to make the ratio 1 : 1, then divide evenly." You
cannot re-arrange the chemistry to suit the arithmetic — 2NaOH + H₂SO₄ is
the reaction; the 2 : 1 mole ratio is a fact of the substances, not a
nuisance. Doubling the NaOH data or halving the acid data to "simplify" the
ratio fabricates a different experiment. Apply the ratio; don't remove it.
:::
