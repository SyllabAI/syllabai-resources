Example 2 runs the ÷ Mᵣ direction, with one extra twist: the solution volume
is not 1 dm³. *What is the concentration in mol/dm³ of a solution containing
2.1 g of sodium hydrogencarbonate, NaHCO₃, in 250 cm³ of solution? (Aᵣ:
H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Na = 23.)*

1. Mᵣ of NaHCO₃ = 23 + 1 + 12 + (3 × 16) = **84**, so 1 mol has a mass of 84 g.
2. 2.1 g is 2.1 ÷ 84 = **0.025 mol**.
3. That 0.025 mol sits in 250 cm³ — but concentration wants *per 1000 cm³*.
   There are four lots of 250 cm³ in 1000 cm³, so scale up: 4 × 0.025 =
   **0.10 mol** in 1 dm³.
4. The concentration is **0.10 mol/dm³**.

The solution triangle is the shortcut for steps 3 and 4 — the same
triangle the next section turns into the single most-used equation of the
topic.

That hint is aimed at the questions you will actually meet:
*"when doing calculations involving solutions you will most often be asked to
calculate a number of moles given the volume of the solution and its
concentration"*, via:

**number of moles = volume of solution (dm³) × concentration (mol/dm³)**

— and because concentrations come in mol/dm³ while volumes usually come in
cm³, you **usually have to convert the volume to dm³ by dividing by 1000**
first. The HINT's reassurance about unfamiliar formulae is worth keeping too:
meet an equation containing, say, CH₃COOH (ethanoic acid) in a later
question, and the concentration arithmetic does not care — it only needs the
volume, the concentration and the big numbers.

:::exam-hint
The scaling shortcut generalises: whatever the volume, dividing by it and
multiplying by 1000 is the same as "per dm³". 250 cm³ → ×4; 200 cm³ → ×5;
50 cm³ → ×20. But in the exam the safest habit is the triangle's algebra:
moles = (volume ÷ 1000) × concentration — write the ÷1000 line visibly,
because that is the mark the marker looks for.
:::

:::misconception
"0.025 mol in 250 cm³ means a concentration of 0.025 mol/dm³." That ignores
the per-dm³ scale-up: the concentration must state what is in **1000 cm³**.
The tell is the unit sanity check — a solution made from 2.1 g in a quarter
of a dm³ reading "0.025 mol/dm³" would hold just 0.025 mol in a full dm³,
i.e. less in more, which contradicts dissolving the same solid. Scale to the
dm³ before quoting the unit.
:::
