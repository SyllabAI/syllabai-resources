Part 2 of the formulae-and-calculations chapter extends the mole toolkit you
built in Chapter 5 from **masses** to **gases and solutions**. The book is
explicit that this material stands on part 1: everything here is the same
"calculate the moles, use the equation, convert to what's asked" three-stage
method, applied to two new ways of quoting an amount. By the end you will
learn to calculate what volume of gas a reaction produces, and how to work
with solution concentrations quoted in mol/dm³.

The two objectives of record for this lesson are both **Chemistry-only**
specification statements — they are assessed in **Paper 2 only** and are not
shared with Science Double Award:

- calculations involving amount of substance, volume and **concentration (in
  mol/dm³)** of solution (spec 1.34C);
- calculations involving **gas volumes and the molar volume of a gas**
  (24 dm³ and 24 000 cm³ at room temperature and pressure, rtp) (spec 1.35C).

The chapter runs pp. 64–71 and falls into two clean halves: gas volumes first
(Avogadro's law, the molar volume, and the worked problems on pp. 64–67),
then solutions (concentration units, the four worked examples, and titration
calculations on pp. 67–71). The book's own end-of-chapter question bank
(pp. 72–74) stays outside this Lesson — open the student book PDF for those.

:::prior-knowledge
You need the part-1 toolkit before starting — the book says so in its opening
line. Concretely, from Chapter 5 (u1:c05): the mole as the unit for amount of
substance (spec 1.27); moles from mass via mass ÷ mass of 1 mol (spec 1.28);
calculating Mᵣ from Aᵣ values (spec 1.26); the big-numbers-in-front-of-formulae
mole ratios from a balanced equation; and the three-stage method for reacting-mass
problems (spec 1.29). If any of those feel shaky, revisit the relevant sections
of Lesson u1:c05 before going further — every calculation here leans on them.
:::

:::exam-hint
Both of this chapter's spec points carry the **C suffix** (1.34C, 1.35C), which
in Edexcel IGCSE Chemistry means: examined in **Paper 2 (2C) only**, and never
in Science Double Award. Practically: gas-volume and concentration calculations
are Paper-2 bankable marks — the standard titration calculation in particular
appears nearly every series, and it is the same three-stage method every time.
:::

:::misconception
"Part 2 is a new topic, so it needs new methods." It doesn't — it is the same
mole ladder with two new rungs. Mass, gas volume and concentration are three
different currencies for "amount of substance"; the exchange rates are Mᵣ (for
mass), the molar volume 24 dm³ (for gases at rtp) and 1 dm³ of solution (for
concentration). Learners who treat gases and solutions as separate topics
memorise twice; learners who see them as conversions solve unfamiliar problems.
:::
