Here is the shape of *a standard titration problem* — learn this
layout and nearly every titration question in the paper is a re-skin:

*25.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide solution required 23.50 cm³ of
dilute hydrochloric acid for neutralisation. Calculate the concentration of
the hydrochloric acid.*

NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

**Stage 1 — moles of what we can.** We know both the volume and the
concentration of the sodium hydroxide: number of moles of NaOH =
(25.00 ÷ 1000) × 0.100 = **0.00250 mol**.

**Stage 2 — equation ratio.** The equation says 1 mol of NaOH reacts with
1 mol of HCl, so 0.00250 mol of NaOH reacts with **0.00250 mol of HCl**.

**Stage 3 — convert to the asked quantity.** That 0.00250 mol of HCl is what
was in the 23.50 cm³ the titration measured out — so the acid's
concentration = moles ÷ volume in dm³ = 0.00250 ÷ (23.50 ÷ 1000) =
**0.106 mol/dm³**.

The closing line for this example is the checkpoint: *we have the volume
and number of moles of HCl and can work out the concentration*. Stage
3 in a titration is always the concentration rearrangement — moles ÷ (volume
÷ 1000) — rather than a mass conversion, which is the only structural
difference from s11's limescale problem.

:::exam-hint
The titration mark scheme, in this order every time: **M1** moles of the
known solution (volume ÷ 1000 × concentration); **M1** the equation ratio
quoted and applied; **M1** concentration = moles ÷ volume with the *other*
volume converted to dm³; **A1** the value with mol/dm³. Carry at least
3 significant figures through the intermediate steps (0.00250, not 0.003) —
rounding before the last line is the classic titration mark leak. Note the
data style too: 25.00 cm³ and 23.50 cm³ — burette readings to 2 decimal
places, exactly what Chapter 16's technique will demand.
:::

:::misconception
"The concentration of the acid is 0.00250 ÷ 25.00..." — mixing up *which*
volume pairs with *which* moles. The 0.00250 mol of HCl sits in the acid's
23.50 cm³, not in the alkali's 25.00 cm³; the alkali's data was already used
in stage 1. Anchor each intermediate to its substance ("0.00250 mol **of
HCl** in **23.50 cm³ of acid**") and the rearrangement cannot pick the wrong
volume.
:::
