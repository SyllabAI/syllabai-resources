Every salt preparation in the course is one of three methods, and the choice is
never a matter of taste — two solubility questions decide it. Ask them in
order:

**Question 1: is the salt you want [[soluble]]?** If it is **[[insoluble]]**
(silver chloride, barium sulfate, lead(II) sulfate, most carbonates and
hydroxides), the method is a **[[precipitation-reaction]]**: mix two soluble
solutions — the
metal part as its nitrate, the non-metal part as its sodium or potassium salt —
then filter, wash with distilled water and dry. No acid is needed and no
crystallisation follows, because the product comes out of the reaction as a
solid, not from a solution.

**Question 2 (if the salt is [[soluble]]): is there a suitable solid reactant?**
If the salt can be made from a solid whose excess is visible — a metal from
magnesium to iron, a metal oxide, a metal hydroxide or a carbonate — the method
is the **excess-solid route**: warm the acid, add the solid in excess, filter
off the leftover, crystallise the filtrate. Copper(II) sulfate from copper(II)
oxide and magnesium sulfate from magnesium both ride this route.

**If the salt is soluble but every reactant would be a solution** — the sodium,
potassium and ammonium salts, all of whose compounds dissolve — the method is
the **titration route**: titrate with an indicator to find the reacting
volumes, then mix those volumes again without the indicator and crystallise.
Sodium sulfate from sodium hydroxide solution and sulfuric acid is the
template.

Three examples pin the logic down. Magnesium sulfate: soluble, and magnesium is
a suitable solid — excess-solid route. Sodium nitrate: soluble, but sodium
hydroxide is a solution, so no solid excess is possible — titration route.
Calcium carbonate: insoluble — precipitation route, calcium ions from a soluble
calcium salt (its nitrate or chloride) and carbonate ions from sodium
carbonate solution. The chapter-question bank at this point in the course
drills exactly this classification, salt by salt, with equations — the page
anchor above takes you there for practice.

:::exam-hint
When a question hands you a named salt and asks for the method, answer with
the branch *and* the reagents in one breath: "insoluble, so mix [metal]
nitrate solution with [sodium/potassium] [non-metal part] solution";
"soluble, from excess [solid] with warm [acid]"; "soluble, by titrating
[alkali] against [acid], then repeating without indicator". The follow-up
marks are always the equation and the purification beats — but the branch
choice is the gate every other mark sits behind.
:::

:::misconception
"Acid + metal, acid + base and acid + carbonate are three *methods* of salt
preparation." They are three *reactions* — all inside one single method. The
three methods are the branches above, sorted by solubility: excess-solid,
titration, precipitation. A question asking you to choose a method wants the
branch; a question asking for an equation wants the reaction inside it.
Answering "acid + carbonate" to a method question is like answering "a
spatula" to a question about the plan.
:::
