Dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with ammonia solution in a neutralisation
reaction to form a salt — ammonium chloride:

NH₃(aq) + HCl(aq) → NH₄Cl(aq)

Ammonium chloride is an ionic salt, so the ions present can be shown
explicitly:

NH₃(aq) + HCl(aq) → NH₄⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

Now the [[proton-transfer]] reading falls out of the equation. In order to form
ammonium chloride, the HCl must donate a proton (H⁺) to the NH₃. The HCl is an
acid because it donates a proton; the NH₃ is a [[base]] because it accepts one.
Ammonia — a molecule with no hydroxide anywhere in it — neutralises an acid
exactly as a hydroxide solution would, and the product is an
[[ammonium-salt]]: the chloride of the ammonium ion.

This one reaction ties three threads of the chapter together. Metal hydroxides
neutralise acids because their OH⁻ accepts H⁺; ammonia neutralises acids
because the nitrogen's lone pair accepts H⁺ directly; and both routes land on
the same ionic product, an ammonium or water molecule plus the acid's anion.
Ammonia's basicity is therefore not a special case bolted onto the rules — it
is the proton-transfer definition doing its job on a molecule the Arrhenius
picture cannot classify on its own.

:::exam-hint
Two sentences win these marks. Product identification: "ammonium chloride, an
ammonium salt" — with the formula NH₄Cl, four hydrogens, not NH₃Cl. Definition
application: "HCl is the acid because it donates a proton; NH₃ is the base
because it accepts one." Examiners also accept the ionic form
NH₄⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) as the products — quoting both the formula-unit equation
and the ionic version shows you know the salt is ionic.
:::

:::misconception
"Ammonium chloride must contain ammonium hydroxide, because that's what an
acid plus an alkali gives." The products of this neutralisation are the
ammonium ion and the chloride ion — there is no NH₄OH species hiding in the
equation. Ammonia solution does contain some hydroxide ions (from NH₃ + H₂O ⇌
NH₄⁺ + OH⁻), but the cleanest description writes the direct proton transfer:
NH₃ accepts H⁺ from HCl, and the anion tags along as the counter-ion.
:::
