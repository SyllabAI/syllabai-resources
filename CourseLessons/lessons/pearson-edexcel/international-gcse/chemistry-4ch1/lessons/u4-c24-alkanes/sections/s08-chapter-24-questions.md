You now hold the complete [[alkane]] story. The family is the simplest
set of [[hydrocarbon]]s: C–C and C–H single bonds only
([[saturated-hydrocarbon]], 4.20), organised as a homologous series
whose members step up by –CH₂– units, all obeying one
[[general-formula]] (4.19), learnable through the first five named
members and their molecular, structural and displayed formulae
(4.21). Physically the members grade smoothly — gases up to butane,
liquids through the IGCSE range, solids from about C₁₈H₃₈ — because
bigger molecules mean stronger intermolecular forces. Chemically
the family is quiet: two reactions only, combustion (complete and
incomplete, with the [[carbon-monoxide]] hazard) and UV-light
[[substitution-reaction]] with halogens (4.22). Every specification
statement of this chapter — 4.19 to 4.22 — has been taught, and
each now joins the platform question bank through the links below,
with practice items keyed to exactly those statements.

Before opening the bank, audit yourself against the chapter's core
moves. Can you write the saturated-hydrocarbon definition in
bonding language? Can you apply CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ forwards (formula from
carbon count) and backwards (family membership from formula)? Can
you draw a branched-chain [[isomer]] with every carbon's four bonds
accounted for? Can you derive — not recall — a complete-combustion
equation for any alkane you are handed, and state what changes when
the oxygen runs short? Can you name the condition for substitution
and explain why its gaseous acid fume is not "an acid"? Any wobble
is a section to revisit, not a reason to start over.

The question bank for this chapter trains a predictable set of
shapes, so rehearse the moves rather than the answers. Definition
items open the set: the saturated-hydrocarbon explanation in
bonding language, exactly as Section 1 drilled it. Formula items
then hand you named alkanes whose molecular formulae must come from
the general formula alone — undecane and friends — sometimes with a
physical-state prediction attached, which Section 5's trend answers
without a table. Drawing items ask for pairs of isomers of a given
alkane, where Section 2's backbone-then-fill method and atom count
earn the marks. Equation items run the full combustion dial — a
complete equation, then an incomplete one, then the *why* behind
the hazard of the carbon-monoxide product — and the substitution
block checks the condition word, the reaction type, a displayed
equation and the name of the organic product. A closing item asks
for two isomeric products from a single mono-substitution of a
branched precursor — the isomer skill and the substitution skill
shaking hands. The shapes are the chapter; the fuel names are just
costumes.

:::exam-hint
Balance the practice time against the mark structure: the
definition and formula items are fast marks — bank them first and
refuse to overthink them. The drawing items are where precision
pays: draw the backbone, fill the bonds, count the atoms, then
move on. For the equation items, derive in the fixed order
(hydrogens to water first, carbons second, balance oxygen last) —
the order never changes, and neither do the marks it earns. And in
substitution items, write the condition *before* the equation; it
is the one-mark sentence people forget under time pressure.
:::

:::misconception
"The question bank rewards knowing the printed answers." It rewards
knowing the *chemistry* — the same reaction can be asked about
through a different fuel, a different halogen or a different
product, and a memorised answer to a different question will not
fit. That is why this lesson trained methods (the general-formula
substitution, the backbone-and-fill drawing routine, the
derive-don't-memorise combustion order, the oxygen dial) rather
than specific answers. If you can run the method, the question's
costume does not matter.
:::

:::summary
- **Alkanes and saturation (4.20)**: the simplest hydrocarbon
  family — C–C and C–H single bonds only, no C=C or C≡C; saturated
  because every bond is single; fairly unreactive as a
  consequence, with strong bonds and no reactive handle.
- **The general formula (4.19)**: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ — n carbons carry
  2n + 2 hydrogens; each member differs from the next by –CH₂–;
  membership is proved by fitting the formula; undecane is
  C₁₁H₂₄ by one line of arithmetic.
- **The first five members (4.21)**: methane CH₄, ethane C₂H₆,
  propane C₃H₈, butane C₄H₁₀, pentane C₅H₁₂ — draw structural and
  displayed formulae up to five carbons and name the unbranched
  chain; isomers share a molecular formula but differ in
  structural formula (butane and pentane have branched forms).
- **The homologous series frame**: same functional group (the
  C–C/C–H skeleton), similar chemical properties, a gradation in
  physical properties, one general formula, –CH₂– steps.
- **The physical trend**: boiling point rises regularly with size —
  stronger intermolecular forces need more energy to overcome;
  first four members are gases, mid-range are liquids, solids from
  about C₁₈H₃₈.
- **Combustion**: complete gives CO₂ + H₂O (e.g. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ +
  2H₂O); incomplete gives CO and/or soot instead of CO₂, plus
  water (e.g. 2C₂H₆ + 5O₂ → 4CO + 6H₂O); carbon monoxide is toxic
  because it reduces the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity.
- **Substitution (4.22)**: alkane + halogen, UV light required;
  one H replaced by one halogen (CH₄ + Br₂ → CH₃Br + HBr);
  products are halogenoalkanes named x-haloalkane; the HCl(g)
  co-product is hydrogen chloride, not hydrochloric acid, until it
  dissolves in water.
:::

:::can-you
- define a hydrocarbon with *only* in place, and an alkane as the
  simplest member of that family?
- state why alkanes are saturated in bonding language — and why
  that saturation explains the family's low reactivity?
- write the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ and use it in both directions —
  formula from carbon count, membership from formula?
- draw and name the structural formulae of the first five unbranched
  alkanes, and draw a branched isomer of butane or pentane with
  every bond accounted for?
- reproduce the five-bullet homologous-series definition and deploy
  the one-sentence properties answer?
- explain the boiling-point trend through intermolecular forces and
  place the state boundaries (gases to C₄, solids at about C₁₈H₃₈)?
- derive complete- and incomplete-combustion equations in the fixed
  order and explain why carbon monoxide harms humans?
- give the UV condition for substitution, write the methane–bromine
  and ethane–chlorine equations, and name halogenoalkane products
  correctly?
:::
