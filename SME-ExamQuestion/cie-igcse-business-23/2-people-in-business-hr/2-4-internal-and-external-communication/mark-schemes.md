### Q6
A problem with the medium used could arise if SSE relies too heavily on email for urgent messages. For example, if the head office needs to quickly inform a new factory about a change in helmet design specifications **[Ap]**, an email could get lost in a spam folder or not be seen immediately. This could lead to production delays or mistakes **[An]**

The sender of messages from SSE's head office may create barriers by using technical jargon about helmet manufacturing **[Ap] **that managers in the new factories don't understand. Or they might send overly long messages with too much information, causing important points about safety standards to be overlooked and helmets failing to meet them, causing wastage **[An]**

Receivers in the new factories could cause communication problems if they don't carefully read messages from head office. They may also misinterpret instructions about helmet production targets **[Ap]** if they speak a different language and don't fully grasp the nuances, giving SSE less output to sell **[An]**

The easiest barrier to overcome is likely the problem with the sender **[E]**. SSE could provide training to head office staff in country Z **[Ap]** on writing clear, concise messages without jargon. They could also create templates for common communications such as emails or staff notices to ensure important information is highlighted. Addressing these sender issues would improve communication regardless of the medium used or receiver's situation **[E]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Structure**
> 
> - Write one PEA paragraph for each point identified in the question:
> 
>   - Point: Identify the problem
>   - Evidence (Ap): Apply case study detail
>   - Analysis (An): Explain business impact
> - Add a final Evaluation (E):
> 
>   - Decide which problem is easiest/hardest to overcome
>   - Justify your choice
>   - Compare and reject alternatives
> 
> **Marking approach**
> 
> - This is a levelled response answer in which the examiner determines how well you have demonstrated skills of application, analysis and evaluation
> 
>   - Application (4) – Use case study facts
>   - Analysis (4) – Chain effects clearly, using business terms
>   - Evaluation (4) – Balanced judgement, rejects others with reasoning
> 
> **Mark scheme**
> 
> - **Problems with the medium used**
> 
>   - Too many links in the chain → each person may change the wording → final message is distorted
>   - Message can be lost → receiver never reads it → task (e.g. making helmets) is not done
>   - Wrong channel for the situation → slow delivery when speed is vital (letter vs phone)
>   - Technical breakdown → no internet / dead battery → message never reaches receiver
> - **Problems with the sender**
> 
>   - Unclear wording → jargon or speaking too fast → receiver misunderstands
>   - Message is overloaded → key facts about, say, cricket helmets are hidden → action not taken
>   - Sent to the wrong person or wrong file → message ignored → decision delayed
> - **Problems with the receiver**
> 
>   - Not listening → misses phone instructions about component orders
>   - Does not trust the sender → hesitates to act on the information
>   - Poor attitude / low urgency → delays action
>   - Language barrier → misunderstands key points
> 
> **Conclusion**
> 
> - **Medium issues** are often the easiest to fix: ask for quick feedback; if none arrives, resend the message
> - **Sender issues** can be reduced with clear, short language guidelines for everyone
> - **Receiver issues** may need culture-building (e.g. flagging urgent messages) but can still be overcome with clear priority labels

### Q7
Email is quick and can reach multiple suppliers at once. FO could easily send details about missing wood, nails or paint **[Ap] **to several suppliers. However, emails may go unread or end up in junk folders, potentially delaying responses about late materials **[An]**

Mobile phones allow for immediate two-way communication. FO could call suppliers to urgently discuss late deliveries of brackets or other components needed for manufacturing outdoor furniture **[Ap]**. However, it may be time-consuming to reach the right person, especially if FO needs to contact multiple suppliers about various raw materials **[An]**

Letters provide a formal record, which could be useful if there are ongoing issues with late deliveries affecting FO's production schedule and it needs to seek compensation **[An]**. However, letters are slow, which is problematic when FO needs materials quickly to fulfil its online **[Ap]** customer orders **[An]**

Email would likely be most suitable for contacting suppliers about late deliveries **[E]**. It balances speed and detail, so FO can quickly send specific information about missing materials to multiple suppliers. This is crucial, as FO needs to manage its resources carefully to avoid production delays and ensure online customers **[Ap]** receive orders promptly **[E]**. While a phone call might be faster for a single supplier, email is more efficient for contacting several suppliers about various late materials. Email also provides a written record FO can reference, unlike phone calls **[E]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Structure**
> 
> - Write one PEA paragraph for each point identified in the question:
> 
>   - Point: Identify the problem
>   - Evidence (Ap): Apply case study detail
>   - Analysis (An): Explain business impact
> - Add a final Evaluation (E):
> 
>   - Decide which problem is easiest/hardest to overcome
>   - Justify your choice
>   - Compare and reject alternatives
> 
> **Marking approach**
> 
> - This is a levelled response answer in which the examiner determines how well you have demonstrated skills of application, analysis and evaluation
> 
>   - Application (4) – Use case study facts
>   - Analysis (4) – Chain effects clearly, using business terms
>   - Evaluation (4) – Balanced judgement, rejects others with reasoning
> 
> **Mark scheme**
> 
> - **Email**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Sent in seconds → suppliers get the message fast, even in another country.
>     - One click reaches many suppliers at once → saves time compared with phoning each one.
>     - Can be printed or saved → FO keeps a hard-copy record for later checks.
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - No guarantee the supplier opens it **→** message may sit in a junk folder.
>     - Needs a good internet link **→** delays if the network is down.
>     - High inbox traffic **→** email could be lost among other messages.
> - **Mobile (cell) phone**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Call is heard instantly **→** FO knows the message reached the right person.
>     - Two-way conversation **→** supplier can answer questions about brackets or delivery times.
>     - Most people keep phones with them **→** easier to reach busy suppliers.
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - May ring several numbers before finding the right contact **→** time-consuming.
>     - International calls and time-zone differences **→** can be costly or inconvenient.
>     - No written record **→** details could be forgotten or disputed later.
> - **Letter**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Provides a formal, signed hard copy **→** useful for legal or large orders.
>     - Plenty of space to explain problems, e.g. late nails and paint.
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - Slowest method **→** may add days to order resolution.
>     - Printing and postage costs higher than email or quick calls.
>     - Still no certainty the letter is received unless sent “signed-for”.
> 
> **Conclusion**
> 
> - **Email**: best when many suppliers must be updated quickly and FO needs a written record.
> - **Mobile phone**: gives instant feedback for urgent questions but is slower for multiple suppliers and leaves no paper trail.
> - **Letter: **suits detailed or formal issues yet is too slow for day-to-day delays.
> - FO should match the method to the situation: urgent delivery queries → email or phone; formal contract changes → letter.
