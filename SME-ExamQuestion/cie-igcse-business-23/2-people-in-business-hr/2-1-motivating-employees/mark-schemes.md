### Q1
Introducing a performance-related bonus scheme could motivate employees to work harder and be more productive. This financial reward might encourage staff to stay with the company longer **[An] **to earn bonuses, which would reverse the increase in staff turnover **[An]**. However, it could create unhealthy competition between workers or cause stress if targets are too high **[An]**

Implementing a staff training programme would help employees gain new skills and feel more valued. This could increase job satisfaction and make workers want to stay at GNP **[An]**. Training could also improve efficiency and product quality. But it would be expensive and time-consuming to run courses. Given that GNP's profit margins are under pressure **[Ap]**, this option may not be acceptable **[An]**

Offering flexible working hours would give employees a better work-life balance. This could reduce stress and make staff happier in their jobs, encouraging them to stay **[An]**. It may also help GNP attract new employees. However, it could be difficult to manage production schedules for GNP's sportswear range **[Ap] **with varied working hours **[An]**

Overall, the staff training programme is likely to be most effective **[E]**. GNP has experienced increased employee turnover **[Ap]**, so developing workers' skills would make them feel more valued and less likely to leave. Training would also help GNP adapt to changing consumer preferences for eco-friendly products **[Ap]**. While expensive initially, the long-term benefits of increased productivity and retention would outweigh the costs **[E]**. The other options may help, but they don't address the core issue **[E] **of making employees feel invested in the company's ambitions for the future **[Ap]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Structure**
> 
> - Write one PEA paragraph for each point identified in the question:
> 
>   - Point: Identify the problem
>   - Evidence (Ap): Apply case study detail
>   - Analysis (An): Explain business impact
> - Add a final Evaluation (E):
> 
>   - Decide which problem is easiest/hardest to overcome
>   - Justify your choice
>   - Compare and reject alternatives
> 
> **Marking approach**
> 
> - This is a levelled response answer in which the examiner determines how well you have demonstrated skills of application, analysis and evaluation
> 
>   - Application (4) – Use case study facts
>   - Analysis (4) – Chain effects clearly, using business terms
>   - Evaluation (4) – Balanced judgement, rejects others with reasoning
> 
> **Mark scheme**
> 
> - **Performance-related bonus scheme**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Links pay to individual or team performance → staff see direct reward for meeting targets → boosts motivation and productivity
>     - Can reinforce company goals (e.g. quality, efficiency) → aligns employee effort with GNP’s objectives
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - Adds variable labour cost → reduces net margin on \$2.4 m profit
>     - May demotivate staff if targets are perceived as unattainable or unfair
>     - Administrative complexity in measuring and distributing bonuses → HR burden
> - **Staff training and development programme**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Up-skills 250 production and batch-line workers → increases job competence and confidence
>     - Demonstrates investment in employee career growth → improves retention and loyalty
>     - Helps GNP adapt to eco-friendly production methods → supports strategic shift toward sustainable sportswear
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - Training time off the line → temporary dip in output and potential backlog
>     - Requires upfront investment in trainers and materials → short-term cost without immediate sales impact
> - **Flexible working hours**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Better work–life balance → reduces absenteeism and turnover in a developing-economy labour market
>     - Can attract diverse talent (e.g. parents, students) → widens recruitment pool
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - Factory batch-production schedules may limit flexibility → operational complexity
>     - Difficult to coordinate shifts → risk of understaffing or overtime pay
> - **Conclusion**
> 
>   - Training and development is the most effective method for GNP because it directly addresses skill gaps, supports the company’s sustainability goals and shows long-term commitment to employees, driving retention in a competitive market
>   - Performance bonuses can boost productivity but add variable costs and risk uneven motivation
>   - Flexible hours improve work–life balance but are hard to implement in batch-production factories and may disrupt scheduling

### Q14
Job rotation is the process of switching an employee between jobs **[K]**, increasing variety in their jobs **[K]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> - Knowledge 2
> 
>   - Give a clearly-stated, valid definition to achieve both knowledge [K] marks
>   - 2 marks for a full definition, 1 mark for a partial definition
> 
> **Mark scheme**
> 
> - Employees swapping around and doing each specific task for only a limited time [2]
> 
>   - OR Process of switching an employee between jobs over a period of time [2]
>   - OR Increasing variety in the workplace by allowing workers to switch from one task to another [2]
> - Partial definition e.g. workers do a variety of tasks [1]

> **[exam-tip]**
> Do not confuse job rotation, which is switching between tasks with the same level of challenge, with job enlargement, which is switching between tasks of varying levels of challenge.
