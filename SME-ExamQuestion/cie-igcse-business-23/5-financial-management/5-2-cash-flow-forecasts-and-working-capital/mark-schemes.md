### Q2
A 20% increase in monthly cash inflows would significantly improve HHP's working capital. Given current inflows of around \$1.5-2 million per month, this could provide an extra \$300,000-\$400,000 monthly **[Ap]**. This additional cash would help cover short-term debts and potentially eliminate the projected negative balances in November and December **[An]. **However, such an increase in sales** **may be unrealistic given HHP's recent 10% sales decline **[Ap]**

A 15% decrease in monthly cash outflows, currently around \$2 million, could save HHP approximately \$300,000 per month **[Ap]**. This would positively impact working capital by reducing cash leaving the business, helping maintain a positive balance and help HHP avoid liquidity issues **[An]**. However, it might involve job cuts, hurting employee morale and productivity

A \$1 million capital injection in July would immediately boost HHP's cash position and working capital. This large one-time inflow would provide a buffer against projected negative balances and support investments **[An]** in growth initiatives like the proposed \$2 million e-commerce website **[Ap]**

The \$1 million capital injection would likely have the greatest impact on HHP's working capital. While the other changes would improve cash flow incrementally, the large upfront boost would immediately improve HHP's liquidity **[E]**. Increasing monthly cash flows would be more gradual, so wouldn't solve HHP's immediate cash flow problems, and reducing cash outflows could negatively impact operations, potentially affect customer service **[E]**

> - **20% increase in monthly cash inflows**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - 20 % rise in inflows → each month’s net cash flow improves → boosts working capital buffer
>     - July inflows jump from \$2 000 k to \$2 400 k → July net cash moves from \$100 k to \$500 k → avoids early liquidity strain
>     - Higher inflows reduce risk of consecutive negative closing balances in Sept–Dec
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - Sales growth needed to sustain 20 % increase may not materialise
>     - If outflows remain high, stronger inflows alone might not prevent liquidity shortages
> - **15% decrease in monthly cash outflows**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - Cutting outflows by 15 % lowers monthly cash drain → net cash improves across all months
>     - October–December outflows of \$1 950–2 050 k fall by about \$292–308 k → negative months turn closer to break-even or positive
>     - Reduces need for external finance and interest costs
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - May require cuts to essential spending (staff, inventory) → risks harming operations
>     - Some costs (rent, utilities) are fixed and cannot be fully reduced
> - **\$1 million injection of capital in July**
> 
>   - **Advantages**
>   
>     - One-off \$1 000 k boost → July closing balance rises to \$1 600 k → strong cushion before downturn
>     - Covers shortfalls in Aug–Dec without changing day-to-day operations
>   - **Disadvantages**
>   
>     - Does not address underlying cash flow issues → future months still run negative if operations unchanged
>     - Injection may come as debt or equity dilution → long-term financial cost
> - **Conclusion**
> 
>   - 15% reduction in outflows has the greatest impact on working capital by sustainably improving monthly net cash and reducing recurring cash deficits
>   - 20% inflow increase also helps but relies on uncertain revenue growth and may lag in effect
>   - \$1 million capital injection solves short-term gaps but fails to improve ongoing cash generation and leaves future deficits untouched
