### Q4
Firstly, it would allow GNP to reach more customers directly **[K]**. GNP currently has a small but growing online presence, with 10% of its sales now coming from its e-commerce website **[Ap]**. Expanding this channel could significantly increase GNP's customer base without relying on third-party retailers **[An]**. This would give GNP more control over its sales process and potentially increase profits by eliminating intermediaries **[An]**

Secondly, it would help reduce costs **[K]**, a key issue GNP is currently exploring **[Ap]**. By selling more products online, GNP can avoid the costs associated with supplying physical retailers, such as transportation and storage fees **[An]**. This could help improve GNP's profit margins, which are currently under pressure due to rising costs of raw materials and increased competition from cheaper imported brands **[An]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> - Write two full paragraphs, one for each reason/point
> - In each paragraph:
> 
>   - State one valid point relevant to the question → 1 K mark
>   - Apply it clearly to the case study → 1 Ap mark
>   - Explain how it works using a cause–effect chain → up to 2 An marks
> 
> **Mark scheme**
> 
> - Relevant answers might include
> 
>   - To reach more customers directly – selling online allows GNP to target a wider customer base without relying on retailers – which gives GNP more control over the sales process and increases profit margins
>   - To reduce distribution and retailer-related costs – avoids transportation, storage, and wholesale discounts typically required when using third-party retailers – this helps improve profit margins, which are currently under pressure
>   - To grow the existing online sales channel – 10% of GNP’s sales are already online – building on this success could be a more effective and lower-risk growth strategy than seeking new retailers
>   - To increase control over pricing and marketing – GNP can set its own prices and use promotional strategies on its own website – which could improve brand positioning and customer loyalty
>   - To improve customer data collection and feedback – selling directly allows GNP to gather customer insights – which can help tailor products and marketing to consumer preferences
>   - To strengthen brand awareness and visibility – GNP can showcase its eco-friendly values and high-quality sportswear directly to consumers – which supports its premium positioning
> - Application could include:
> 
>   - GNP is a medium-sized sportswear and athletic shoe manufacturer
>   - Based in Country X; been in business for 15 years
>   - Currently sells mainly through sports retailers
>   - 10% of sales already come from online channel
>   - Annual revenue: \$30 million; profit: \$2.4 million
>   - Facing rising raw material costs and pressure on margins
>   - Increased competition from cheaper imported brands
>   - Considering eco-friendly product development
>   - Uses batch production
>   - Management exploring cost reduction and efficiency
>   - Developing country context; lower import tariffs increasing competition

### Q5
Entering foreign markets allows businesses to reach new customers **[K],** which can increase sales and profits through greater demand and economies of scale **[An]**

However, businesses may not understand local customer preferences** [K]**, which could lead to poor sales if cultural or market differences are ignored **[An]**

Overall, entering foreign markets can bring rewards, but the risks don’t always make it worthwhile** [E]**. Success depends on the specific situation and how well the business prepares **[E]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> - You are writing a mini-argument with a decision
> 
>   - Give one relevant reason to support your view → 1 K mark
>   - Explain why this reason helps the business (cause-effect chain) →  1 An mark
>   - Give a different possible reason or viewpoint → 1 K mark
>   - Explain why this could also help the business → 1 An mark
>   - Make a decision — which argument is stronger or more important overall? → up to 2 E marks
> 
> **Mark scheme**
> 
> - Points might include:
> 
>   - Access to new customers [k]; can increase sales and revenue [an]
>   - Greater production/output [k]; can lead to economies of scale and lower average costs [an]
>   - Diversification [k]; reduces dependence on one domestic market [an]
>   - Lack of local market knowledge [k]; may result in poor marketing or unmet customer needs [an]
>   - Cultural or legal barriers [k]; may increase operating costs or create compliance issues [an]
>   - Strong local competitors [k]; may reduce chances of success or lower market share [an]
>   - High initial investment required [k]; may be risky if returns are uncertain [an]
> - Candidates must explore both potential rewards and potential risks, and evaluate whether the rewards always outweigh the risks.
> Some points may be rewarded as [k] or [an], but do not award the same point twice.
> - Examples help demonstrate understanding, but evaluation must be clear to access full marks
