> Answers may include some of the following ideas. All valid material must be credited.
> 
> The Western Wall is important in the history of Judaism because:
> 
> - Jews believe that the Temple was built according to the instructions given by G-d so that the chosen People could worship as G-d required.
> - The Western Wall was part of the second Temple in Jerusalem and is considered holy because it is so close to the Temple site.
> - It is one of the only parts to survive when the Temple was destroyed in 70CE.
> - As a last remaining piece of the place which was once the centre of the religion the Western Wall is now a site of pilgrimage and prayer.
> 
> However, it might be considered to have less religious importance today because:
> 
> - While the whole site is holy, the historic Temple consisted of various areas, which were considered more sacred as they moved in towards the Holy of Holies at the heart of the Temple; the Western Wall was part of the outside structure.
> - Many Jewish sources regard the Foundation or Pierced Stone within the Dome of the Rock as the site of the Holy of Holies, making that location more significant in terms of its connection to G-d and Jewish history.
> - As the Temple no longer exists and the forms of worship there are impossible, some Jews might not consider what is left of it to be of great spiritual significance to them personally, being more concerned with living an halakhic life and obeying the mitzvot.
