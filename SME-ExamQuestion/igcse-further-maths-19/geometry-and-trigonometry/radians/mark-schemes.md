## Q1 — medium — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 2((b)) — 3 marks
The shape $A B C$ is made of two pieces that do not overlap, so find each area and add them

- Triangle $A O B$
- Sector $O B C$

Both pieces need an angle at $O$, so start with the arc length formula $s = r θ$, whose angle is in radians

$π = 6 θ$

$θ = \frac{π}{6}$

$A O C$ is a straight line, so the angles at $O$ add up to $π$

`\angle A O B = \frac{5 \pi}{6}`

The triangle has two sides and the angle between them, so use $\frac{1}{2} a b \text{sin} C$

`\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 6 \times \text{sin} \left(\frac{5 \pi}{6}\right) = 15`

**[M1]**

The sector uses $\frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$, again with the angle in radians

$\frac{1}{2} \times 6^{2} \times \frac{π}{6} = 3 π$

**[M1]**

Add the two areas

$15 + 3 π = 24 . 4247 \dots$

`\text{Area} = 24 . 4 \textrm{ } \text{cm}^{2} \textrm{ } \left(3 s . f .\right)`

**[A1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **M1**: A correct expression for the area of triangle $A O B$, using $\frac{1}{2} a b \text{sin} C$ with 10, 6 and your angle $A O B$.
> 
> **M1**: A correct expression for the area of sector $O B C$, using $\frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$ with $r = 6$ and your $θ$.
> 
> **A1**: $24 . 4$ cm<sup>2</sup>. Accept answers which round to this value.
> 
> Finding the sector area as $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{arc} \times \text{radius}$ is equally acceptable, giving $\frac{1}{2} \times π \times 6 = 3 π$, and earns the second method mark in the same way.
> 
> Working in degrees throughout is equally acceptable, using `\angle A O B = 150 \circ` and a sector angle of $30 \circ$ with $\frac{θ}{360} \times π r^{2}$.
> 
> The angle at $O$ inside the triangle is the obtuse one. Note that `\text{sin} \left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)` and `\text{sin} \left(\frac{5 \pi}{6}\right)` are both $\frac{1}{2}$, so the triangle's area of 15 is reached either way and the first method mark is not lost by using the wrong angle here.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Split a compound shape into pieces you have a formula for.
> 
> - Here that is a triangle and a sector, and they meet along $O B$ without overlapping, so the two areas simply add
> 
> Both radian formulas need the angle in radians, so leave $θ$ as $\frac{π}{6}$.
> 
> - $\frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$ gives $3 π$ directly
> - Converting to degrees means switching to $\frac{θ}{360} \times π r^{2}$ as well, which is more work for the same answer
> 
> The sector area can also be found straight from the arc.
> 
> - $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{arc} \times \text{radius}$ is $\frac{1}{2} \times π \times 6$, which is $3 π$ again
> - Useful when the arc is given and the angle is not
> 
> Keep the exact values until the very end.
> 
> - The exact area is $15 + 3 π$, so round only once
> - Rounding $3 π$ to $9 . 4$ first still gives $24 . 4$ here, but the habit costs marks on questions where it does not
> 
> Sine does not distinguish the two possible angles at $O$.
> 
> - `\text{sin} \left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)` and `\text{sin} \left(\frac{5 \pi}{6}\right)` are both $\frac{1}{2}$, so the triangle's area comes out as 15 either way
> - That is a quirk of this question rather than a rule, and it would not save you in the length calculation

## Q2 — medium — 6 marks · exam-questions

### 3((a)) — 2 marks
The small circle touches both $O A$ and $O B$, and a circle touching two lines has its centre on the line that bisects the angle between them

Call that centre $M$, and drop a perpendicular from $M$ to $O A$

That perpendicular is a radius of the small circle, so it has length $r$

The small circle also touches the arc $A B$, so $O$, $M$ and the point where they touch all lie on one straight line of length $3 r$

$O M = 3 r - r$

$O M = 2 r$

The perpendicular creates a right-angled triangle in which the angle at $O$ is half of $θ$, the opposite side is $r$ and the hypotenuse is $O M$

$\text{sin} \frac{θ}{2} = \frac{r}{2r}$

**[M1]**

The $r$ cancels, so the angle does not depend on the size of the circles at all

$\text{sin} \frac{θ}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$

$\frac{θ}{2} = \frac{π}{6}$

$θ = \frac{π}{3}$

**[A1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **M1**: Uses the sine ratio in a right-angled triangle to reach $\frac{θ}{2} = \frac{π}{6}$. Working in degrees is equally acceptable, so $\frac{θ}{2} = 30 ^{\circ}$ earns this mark.
> 
> **A1**: $θ = \frac{π}{3}$ only.
> 
> A method that does not use trigonometry at all earns nothing here, even if it happens to reach the right value.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Two tangents from the same point mean the centre sits on the angle bisector.
> 
> - That is what puts half of $θ$ into the triangle rather than all of it
> - Forgetting the halving gives $θ = \frac{π}{6}$, which is the commonest wrong answer
> 
> The radius drawn to a point of contact is always perpendicular to what it touches.
> 
> - So the perpendicular from $M$ to $O A$ has length exactly $r$, which is the opposite side of the triangle
> - Marking the right angle on your own sketch makes the triangle much easier to see
> 
> The distance $O M$ is a subtraction, not the radius.
> 
> - The small circle touches the arc from the inside, so $O M$ plus $r$ makes up the full $3 r$
> - That gives $O M = 2 r$, and the ratio $\frac{r}{2r}$ is what makes the answer so clean
> 
> Every length here is a multiple of $r$, so $r$ cancels.
> 
> - The angle is fixed by the shape alone, which is why part (a) can be answered before $r$ is known

> - Dividing through by $π$ early keeps the arithmetic in whole numbers

so $a = 1$ and $b = 3 . 8$

> 
> - The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord and the angle, so half the chord is $x \text{sin} 0 . 9$
