> **[mark-scheme]**

> **[exam-tip]**

> Once you have the correct formulae, you can balance the equation.

### 2 — 5 marks
i) The curve using half the concentration of acid is:

![Graph showing hydrogen volume in cm³ over time in minutes with two curves; the upper curve has a marked point at 8 minutes and 55 cm³.](assets/005-graph-showing-hydrogen-volume-in-cm-over-time-in.png)

**[2 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 1 mark for a curve that starts at the origin and is less steep than the original curve
> 
> 1 mark for a curve that levels off at half the volume in the original experiment (~38cm<sup>3</sup>)

> **[mark-scheme]**
> ii) How halving the concentration of acid affects the reaction rate:
> 
> - There are  fewer particles/molecules (in the same volume)  **[1 mark]**
> - There are fewer collisions per unit time 
> **OR**
> There are less frequent collisions **[1 mark]**
> - So, the rate of  reaction is slower **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **For the graph:** Remember that changing the concentration of a reactant affects two things:
> 
> 1. **The Rate (the slope)**
> 
>   - Lower concentration = slower rate = less steep curve.
> 2. **The Amount of Product (the final height):**
> 
>   - Halving the amount of the limiting reactant (the acid) will halve the amount of product formed.
> 
> **For the explanation:** This is a collision theory question. To get full marks, you must link the concepts in the correct order:
> 
> 1. **Fewer particles** in the same volume.
> 2. Leads to **less frequent collisions**.
> 3. Results in a **slower rate**.
> 
> Simply stating "fewer collisions" without mentioning the frequency (per unit time) is a common mistake and will not score a mark.
