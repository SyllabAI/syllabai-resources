## Exam Questions (27 questions)


### Q1 — hard — 12 marks

**Q1** [2 marks]

Phoebe has a faulty smoke detector which rings at an average rate of 5 times per day.

State two conditions necessary for a Poisson distribution to be a suitable model for the number of times that the smoke detector rings.

**Q1** [6 marks]

Assume now that a Poisson distribution is a suitable model.

(i) Find the probability that the smoke detector rings exactly 3 times on each day of a randomly chosen 4-day period.

(ii) Find the probability that the smoke detector rings exactly 12 times during a randomly chosen 4-day period.

(iii) Explain why the answers to part (b)(i) and part (b)(ii) are different.

**Q1** [4 marks]

Given that the smoke detector rings at least once during a randomly chosen 6-hour period, find the probability that it rings no more than 4 times during this period.

### Q2 — hard — 7 marks

**Q2** [7 marks]

The random variables `X \sim \text{Po}(\alpha)`, `Y \sim \text{Po}(\beta)` and `Z \sim \text{Po}(\gamma)`.

(i) Given that $\text{P} ( X = 4 ) = \text{P} ( X = 2 ) + \text{P} ( X = 3 )$, find the value of $α$.

(ii) Given that $\text{P} ( Y \geq 1 ) = 0 . 314$, find the value of $β$.

(iii) Given that $\text{P} ( Z = k + 1 ) = \text{P} ( Z = k - 1 )$ where $k$ is a positive integer, find an expression for $γ$ in terms of $k$.

### Q3 — medium — 6 marks

**Q3** [6 marks]

Roger is investigating historical data from World War II. In south London, an area of 144 km² was divided into 576 equal squares, and the number of times each square was hit by a flying bomb over a period of time was recorded. During this time, a total of 537 bombs landed in the area. The results are shown in the table below.

| **Number of times hit by a flying bomb** | **Number of squares** |
|---|---|
| 0 | 229 |
| 1 | 211 |
| 2 | 93 |
| 3 | 35 |
| 4 | 7 |
| 5 or more | 1 |

Roger suggests that a Poisson distribution with mean $\frac{537}{576}$ would be an appropriate model for the number of times a square was hit by a flying bomb.

(i) By finding probabilities using Roger's model, estimate the expected number of squares that would be hit, respectively, by 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 or more flying bombs.

(ii) By comparing the actual number of squares in the table with the expected numbers of squares from part (i), state whether Roger's model is appropriate.

### Q4 — very_hard — 10 marks

**Q4** [4 marks]

**Q4** [6 marks]

Find the probability that all three essays are returned.

### Q5 — very_hard — 13 marks

**Q5** [4 marks]

**Q5** [2 marks]

Given instead that Priya makes exactly 5 mistakes in a randomly chosen 1-hour revision period, find the probability that Priya and Qays make a total of exactly 9 mistakes in that period.

**Q5** [3 marks]
