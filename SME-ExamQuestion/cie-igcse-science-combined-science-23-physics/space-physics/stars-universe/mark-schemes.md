**5. Space Physics** · Cambridge (CIE) IGCSE science (0653)


## Exam Questions (4 questions)


### Q1
(i)

> **[mark-scheme]**
> - red giant **[1 mark]**

(ii)

> **[mark-scheme]**
> - the Sun does not have enough mass / the Sun is a small mass star / only very large mass stars become black holes **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Only stars with a much greater mass than the Sun end their life cycle as a black hole (via a supernova). A star the size of the Sun ends up as a white dwarf instead, after passing through the red giant and planetary nebula stages.

### Q2
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - Milky Way **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Don't confuse the Milky Way (our own galaxy) with the Solar System (the Sun and the objects that orbit it) — the Solar System is just one small part of the Milky Way galaxy.

### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- A star about the same size as the Sun follows the sequence: main sequence → red giant → planetary nebula + white dwarf

**B** is incorrect because supernovae and black holes are associated with the life cycle of massive stars (red supergiants), not stars the size of the Sun

**C** is incorrect because it describes a red supergiant, which forms from a massive star, not a star the size of the Sun

**D** is incorrect because it describes a red supergiant ending in a supernova, both stages in the life cycle of a massive star, not a star the size of the Sun

### Q4
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- A light-year is a unit of distance, not time — it is the distance that light travels in a vacuum in one year (approximately 9.46 × 10<sup>15</sup> m)

**B** is incorrect because a light-year is defined using a single year, not 100 000 years — 100 000 light-years is closer to the diameter of the Milky Way galaxy

**C** is incorrect because it describes a light-year as a unit of time, when a light-year measures distance, not time

**D** is incorrect because it also describes a light-year as a unit of time, when a light-year measures distance, not time
