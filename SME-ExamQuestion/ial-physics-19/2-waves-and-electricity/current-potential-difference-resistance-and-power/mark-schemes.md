## Q9 — easy — 1 marks · multiple-choice-questions

### 1() — 1 marks
**Correct answer: B** — filament lamp

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- The gradient of a $V$–$I$ graph at any point is equal to resistance

$R = \frac{V}{I}$

- The curve becomes steeper as $I$ increases, so the resistance increases with current
- As current rises, the filament heats up and the increased lattice vibrations raise the metal's resistance

  - This is characteristic of a filament lamp

**A** is incorrect because an ohmic resistor (at constant temperature) obeys Ohm's law — $V$ is directly proportional to $I$ and the graph would be a straight line through the origin, not a curve.

**C** is incorrect because the resistance of a thermistor (NTC) *decreases* as temperature (and current) increases, so the $V$–$I$ curve would become less steep at higher currents — concave down, not concave up.

**D** is incorrect because a forward-biased diode conducts negligible current until $V$ reaches the threshold (~0.7 V), then $I$ rises sharply for very small further increases in $V$ — on a $V$–$I$ plot the curve is approximately flat above the threshold, not a steeply rising curve through the origin.

> **[exam-tip]**
> For $V$–$I$ graphs, the gradient at any point is the resistance ($R = V / I$). A curve that gets steeper means resistance is increasing — typical of a filament lamp warming up. A curve that gets shallower means resistance is decreasing — typical of an NTC thermistor. Always check the axis order: $I$–$V$ graphs (with $I$ on the vertical axis) and $V$–$I$ graphs (with $V$ on the vertical axis) flip the curvature for the same component.
