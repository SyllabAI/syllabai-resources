### 2() — 5 marks
To explain how SOHO stays in a fixed position relative to the Earth:

- List the known quantities:

  - Distance of SOHO from the Earth, $r_{E}=1.51\times10^{9}\text{m}$ (from part a)
  - Distance from the Earth to the Sun $=1.496\times10^{11}\text{m}$
  - Mass of Sun, $M_{S}=1.989\times10^{30}\text{kg}$
  - Mass of Earth, $M_{E}=5.972\times10^{24}\text{kg}$
  - Mass of SOHO, $m=1862\text{kg}$
  - Gravitational constant, $G=6.67\times10^{-11}\text{N m}^{2}\text{kg}^{-2}$
- Determine the orbital radius of SOHO (distance from the Sun):

$r_{S}=1.496\times10^{11}-1.51\times10^{9}=1.481\times10^{11}\text{m}$

- Determine the net gravitational force on SOHO:

  - Since SOHO orbits the Sun, there must be a centripetal force acting towards the Sun
  - At L1, the gravitational forces from the Sun and the Earth act in opposite directions, with a net force towards the Sun providing the centripetal force

$F_{net}=\frac{GM_{S}m}{r_{S}^{2}}-\frac{GM_{E}m}{r_{E}^{2}}$

$F_{n e t} = G m ( \frac{M_{S} }{r_{S}^{2} } - \frac{M_{E} }{r_{E}^{2} } )$

$F_{n e t} = 6.67 \times 10^{- 11} \times 1862 \times ( \frac{1.989 \times 10^{30} }{( 1.481 \times 10^{11} )^{2}} - \frac{5.972 \times 10^{24} }{( 1.51 \times 10^{9} )^{2}} )$

$F_{net}=10.94\text{N}$ **[1 mark]**

- Use the equation for centripetal force to determine $ω$:

$F=mω^{2}r$

$ω=\sqrt{\frac{F_{net}}{mr}}$

$ω=\sqrt{\frac{10.94}{1862\times1.481\times10^{11}}}$

$ω=1.992\times10^{-7}\text{rad s}^{-1}$ **[1 mark]**

- Calculate the orbital period:

$T=\frac{2π}{ω}$

$T=\frac{2π}{1.992\times10^{-7}}=3.154\times10^{7}\text{s}$ **[1 mark]**

$T=\frac{3.154\times10^{7}}{60\times60\times24}\approx365\text{days}\approx1\text{year}$

- Explanation:

For SOHO to remain in a fixed position relative to the Earth, this means it must have the same period and angular velocity as the Earth **[1 mark]**

The net gravitational force (`tilde 11 text  N end text`) provides the centripetal force required to enable SOHO to orbit in 1 Earth year **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 1 mark for stating net gravitational force on SOHO = Sun's gravitational force − Earth's gravitational force
> 
> - Accept $F_{n e t} = G m ( M_{S} \div r_{S}^{2} - M_{E} \div r_{E}^{2} )$ or equivalent
> 
> 1 mark for use of $F=mω^{2}r$ to calculate $ω$ ($1.99\times10^{-7}\text{rad s}^{-1}$)
> 
> 1 mark for use of $ω=2π/T$ to calculate $T$
> 
> 1 mark for stating SOHO must orbit the Sun with the same period / angular velocity as the Earth
> 
> 1 mark for showing $T\approx3.15\times10^{7}\text{s}\approx1\text{year}$, concluding this matches the Earth's period

> **[exam-tip]**
> A common error is to add the two gravitational forces at L1. Because L1 lies between the Earth and the Sun, the Earth's pull points away from the Sun and must be subtracted. The Earth's term is tiny compared with the Sun's, but it is exactly what slows the orbit from just under a year to precisely one year, and keeps SOHO at a fixed position on the Sun–Earth line.

$\frac{g_{1}}{g_{2}} = ( \frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}} ) ^{2}$

$g_\frac{{s}}{g} = ( \frac{R}{R + h} ) ^{2}$

$g_{s} = g ( \frac{R}{R + h} )^{2}$
