## Structured Questions (1 questions)


### Q1
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Any **two** from:
> 
> - The system is subjected to a periodic driving force with a frequency equal to the natural frequency of the system
> - There is a maximum transfer of energy from the driver to the oscillating system
> - The amplitude of the oscillation becomes a maximum
> 
> **[2 marks]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Resonance is three linked ideas — the frequency condition (driving frequency equal to natural frequency), maximum energy transfer and maximum amplitude — and any two of them earn the marks here. Lead with the frequency condition: it is the defining feature, and answers built on "large amplitude" alone, with no reference to the natural frequency, score zero.
To calculate the rotation rate at which resonance occurs:

- List the known quantities:

  - Mass of wet clothes, $\Delta m = 8 . 0 \text{kg}$
  - Additional compression of the mounts, $\Delta x = 2 . 0 \text{mm} = 2 . 0 \times 10^{-3} \text{m}$
  - Total oscillating mass, $m = 72 + 8 . 0 = 80 \text{kg}$
- Use Hooke's law to determine the stiffness of the spring mounts:

  - The additional weight is supported by the additional compression of the mounts

$\DeltaF=k\Deltax=\Deltamg$

$k=\frac{\Deltamg}{\Deltax}$

$k=\frac{8.0\times9.81}{2.0\times10^{-3}}$

$k = 3 . 9 \times 10^{4} \text{N m}^{-1}$ **[1 mark]**

- The machine and its contents oscillate as a mass–spring system, so combine $T = 2 π \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$ with $f = \frac{1}{T}$, using the total mass:

$T=\frac{1}{f}=2π\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$

$f_{0}=\frac{1}{2π}\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$

$f_{0}=\frac{1}{2π}\sqrt{\frac{3.9\times10^{4}}{80}}$ **[1 mark]**

$f_{0} = 3 . 5 \text{Hz}$ **[1 mark]**

- At resonance, the rotation frequency of the drum is equal to the natural frequency, so convert to revolutions per minute:

$\text{rotation rate} = 3 . 5 \times 60$ **[1 mark]**

$\text{rotation rate} =$$210 \text{rev min}^{-1}$ **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 1 mark for use of $\Delta F = k \Delta x$ with the additional weight and the compression converted to metres, giving $k = 3 . 9 \times 10^{4} \text{N m}^{-1}$
> 
> - Accept the substitution shown as `k = \left(8.0 \times 9.81\right) \div 0.0020`
> - Do not accept the total weight of the machine and clothes in this step
> 
> 1 mark for use of $T=2π\sqrt{m/k}$ with $f = 1 / T$ and the total mass of $80 \text{kg}$
> 
> 1 mark for $f_{0} = 3 . 5 \text{Hz}$
> 
> - Accept 3.52–3.53 Hz
> 
> 1 mark for equating the drum rotation frequency to the natural frequency and multiplying by 60
> 
> 1 mark for a correct answer of $210 \text{rev min}^{-1}$
> 
> - Accept answers in the range 210–212 rev min<sup>-1</sup>
> - Must include a correct unit for this mark

> **[exam-tip]**
> Two traps sit in this calculation.
> 
> First, the stiffness comes only from the additional load — the machine sinks a *further* 2.0 mm under the 8.0 kg of clothes, so 8.0 kg belongs in the Hooke's law step.
> 
> Second, the oscillating system is the whole machine plus its contents, so 80 kg belongs in the frequency step.
> 
> Using the same mass in both steps is wrong physics, and if you use 8.0 kg throughout you should notice the answer of about 670 rev min⁻¹ fails a sense check against part (c)'s spin cycle.
> 
> Convert 2.0 mm to metres before substituting — leaving it in mm makes the stiffness a factor of 1000 too small.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - As the drum speeds up, its rotation frequency passes through the natural frequency of the machine, so resonance occurs only momentarily **[1 mark]**
> - At higher rotation rates, the driving frequency is much greater than the natural frequency, so the amplitude of the oscillation is small **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Picture the amplitude–driving frequency curve: as the drum accelerates from rest to 1200 rev min⁻¹, the driving frequency sweeps along the frequency axis, passing through the resonance peak at about 210 rev min⁻¹. The violent vibration occurs when the sweep crosses the peak; at full speed, the machine is far beyond it, where the curve is low. Answers that only say "resonance occurs" without explaining why it stops score one mark, not two.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - The damping force opposes the motion of the machine, so energy is transferred from the oscillating system (to the surroundings) **[1 mark]**
> - The maximum amplitude at resonance is reduced **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> "Explain" needs a mechanism, not just an outcome. Say where the energy goes — the damping force opposes the motion, so energy is transferred out of the oscillating system — and then state the effect on the resonance amplitude. A common weak answer names the effect ("smaller amplitude") with no energy argument, which earns only one of the two marks.
