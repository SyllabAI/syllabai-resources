**Extract:**

When marking this question:

- Accept "forcing frequency" or "frequency of the applied force" for "driving frequency"
- Accept "the frequency at which the system oscillates freely" as a description of natural frequency
- Do not accept "large amplitude" without reference to a maximum

A washing machine rests on spring mounts. During the spin cycle, the wet clothes in the rotating drum are not evenly distributed, so the drum exerts a periodic force on the machine. The frequency of this force is equal to the rotation frequency of the drum.

The machine and its contents can be modelled as a mass–spring system oscillating vertically. The mounts obey Hooke's law.

Explain what is meant by resonance.

Calculate the rotation rate of the drum, in revolutions per minute, at which resonance occurs.

**Extract:**

Calculate the rotation rate of the drum, in revolutions per minute, at which resonance occurs.

Mark scheme result from (b): rotation rate = 210 rev min<sup>-1</sup> (the natural frequency of the machine as a mass-spring system, f₀ = 3.5 Hz).

- For the first mark, accept: the rotation rate is (briefly) equal to about 210 rev min<sup>-1</sup> (allow ecf of the value from (b))
- For the second mark, accept: answers argued from the shape of an amplitude–driving frequency graph, i.e. the amplitude falls away either side of the peak

During the spin cycle, the drum accelerates from rest to a maximum rotation rate of 1200 revolutions per minute.

Suggest why the machine vibrates violently only for a short time.
