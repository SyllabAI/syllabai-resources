# Mark Schemes — Ethics & Ownership
**7. Ethics & Ownership** · Cambridge (CIE) A-LEVEL computer-science (9618)


## Exam Questions (28 questions)


### Q1
The AI system needs a large amount of computing power to be trained and to run, so it uses a large amount of electricity, and where that electricity does not come from a renewable source it produces more carbon emissions. **[1]**

The system also needs specialised hardware, which is replaced as the technology improves, so it adds to the amount of electronic waste. **[1]**

Against this, the system can switch off heating, cooling and lighting in rooms that are not being used, and can learn the pattern of use of each building, so less energy is wasted, and that saving is repeated every day across many buildings. **[1]**

The overall effect therefore depends on whether the energy the system saves is greater than the energy it uses, and that cannot be known until the system has run for a period of time. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark each, to a maximum of 4; maximum 2 from any one section:**
> 
> Environmental cost of running the system:
> 
> - The system needs a large amount of computing power, so it uses a large amount of electricity
> - Training the system on stored data uses further electricity
> - ... and where that electricity is not from a renewable source it produces more carbon emissions
> - The system needs specialised hardware, which is replaced as the technology improves
> - ... which adds to the amount of electronic waste
> 
> Environmental saving made by the system:
> 
> - Heating, cooling and lighting can be switched off in rooms that are not in use
> - The system can learn the pattern of use of a building and adjust the settings before they are needed
> - ... so less energy is wasted
> - The saving is repeated every day, and across every building the organisation owns
> 
> Why the answer is not certain:
> 
> - The overall effect depends on whether the energy saved is greater than the energy used
> - ... which is not known until the system has run for a period of time
> - The answer changes if the electricity supply changes to a renewable source
> 
> **[4 marks]**
> 
> Accept "power" in place of "electricity".
> 
> Accept the electricity used to keep the computers cool as a further cost.
> 
> Do not accept "AI is bad for the environment" or "AI helps the environment" with no reason given.
> 
> Do not accept an answer that gives only costs or only savings, because the question asks why the impact is difficult to predict.

> **[exam-tip]**
> The environmental strand is the one students revise least, and the usual answer is one sided: three or four sentences all saying that AI uses a lot of electricity. That answer cannot reach full marks here however detailed it is, because the question asks why the impact is hard to predict. Give at least one cost, at least one saving, and finish with the point that the overall effect depends on which of the two is larger.

### Q2
The bank's costs fall. **[1]**

Fewer members of staff are needed to read every application in full, because the system decides which applications need attention first. **[1]**

The bank therefore spends less on wages for the same number of applications. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for identifying one correct economic impact
> 
> **1 mark** each, to a maximum of 2, for description points that follow from that impact
> 
> Examples:
> 
> Costs fall for the bank
> 
> - fewer staff are needed to read every application in full
> - so less is spent on wages
> - staff time is spent only on the applications that need a decision
> 
> Income rises for the bank
> 
> - more applications are dealt with each day
> - so more loans are agreed in the same time
> - so the bank earns more in interest
> 
> Costs rise for the bank
> 
> - the system is expensive to buy, and has to be paid for again each time it is updated
> - staff have to be retrained to work with it
> - so any saving may take several years to appear
> 
> Accept a description built on a rise in costs just as readily as one built on a saving.
> 
> Do not accept "customers get a decision faster" on its own, because that is a social impact unless it is linked to the bank's income or costs.
> 
> Do not accept "the bank makes more money" with no reason given.

> **[exam-tip]**
> An economic impact needs a direction and a reason. "The bank saves money" is worth one mark at most; the other marks come from saying what the saving is, such as fewer staff hours, and why it happens, such as the system doing the first read. Note also that a rise in costs is just as valid an answer as a saving. AI systems are expensive to buy and to keep up to date, and an answer built on that side scores full marks if it is described just as carefully.

### Q3
Licence: a shareware software licence. **[1]**

The company's research shows that many customers will not pay for a program they have not used, and a shareware licence lets those customers install and use the program free of charge for a trial period first. **[1]**

Customers who find the program useful then pay for the full version, so the company earns the money it needs to pay its programmers. **[1]**

The source code is not made available under a shareware licence and the program stays copyrighted, so nobody outside the company can legally change the program code. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for naming an appropriate licence, then **1 mark** for each justification point that fits the licence named, to a maximum of 3.
> 
> The licence mark is awarded for either of the following.
> 
> - Shareware software licence
> - Commercial software licence
> 
> **Justification, if shareware is named. Any three from:**
> 
> - A trial version can be installed and used free of charge, which answers the finding that customers will not pay for a program they have not used
> - Users who find the program useful pay for the full version, so the company earns money to pay its programmers
> - More people try the program, so more people may go on to buy it
> - The source code is not made available and the program is copyrighted, so nobody outside the company can legally change the code
> 
> **Justification, if commercial is named. Any three from:**
> 
> - Every user has to pay before using the program, so the company is certain of an income from every copy
> - With a shareware trial some users would use the free version and never pay, so a commercial licence protects the company's income better
> - The source code is not made available and the program is copyrighted, so nobody outside the company can legally change the code
> - A commercial licence usually includes support from the developer, which can persuade a customer to buy
> 
> Accept either licence. What is marked is whether the justification fits the licence you named. A shareware justification written under the heading "commercial" earns the justification marks only if the points are true of commercial software.
> 
> Do not accept the Open Source Initiative or the Free Software Foundation. Under both of those the source code is available and anyone may change the program, which the company's third need forbids. If you name either, you score zero for the licence and can score justification marks only for points that are actually true of that licence.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Only one of the four marks is for the licence name. The other three are for the justification, so a one-word answer throws away most of the question.
> 
> Every justification point must be tied to something the company actually said it needs. Naming shareware and then writing a general definition of shareware earns very little. Quote the need, then say how the licence meets it.
> 
> More than one licence can be correct in a question like this. The examiner is not marking whether you picked their favourite, they are marking whether your reasons match the licence you named.

### Q4
Patients are given a result more quickly, so their treatment can start sooner. **[1]**

If the system gives an incorrect result, a patient may be given the wrong treatment. **[1]**

**[2 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any two from:**
> 
> - Patients receive a result more quickly, so treatment starts sooner
> - More patients can be assessed, including in places where there are few specialist doctors
> - An incorrect result may lead to a patient being given the wrong treatment
> - Patients may not trust a decision that a machine has been involved in
> - It is unclear who is responsible when the system is wrong
> - Medical images have to be stored, which raises concerns about privacy
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> Accept two benefits, two drawbacks, or one of each.
> 
> Do not accept "the hospital spends less on staff" or "the system is expensive to buy", because those are economic impacts and not social ones.
> 
> Do not accept "AI is bad for people" with no effect stated.

> **[exam-tip]**
> The syllabus names three kinds of impact, social, economic and environmental, and the question tells you which one is being marked. Social means the effect on people: their health, their safety, their privacy, their trust. An answer about cost, wages or profit is economic and earns nothing here, even though it is true. Read the word in the question before you start writing.

### Q5
Under both licences the user must pay for the full program, so both give the developer an income. **[1]**

Under neither licence is the source code made available, so under both the user may not legally change the program. **[1]**

A shareware licence lets the user try the program before paying, but a commercial licence requires the user to pay before the program can be used at all. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any three from, of which at least one must be a difference:**
> 
> - Similarity: under both licences the user has to pay for the full program // both give the developer an income
> - Similarity: neither licence gives the user access to the source code // under both, the user may not legally change the program
> - Similarity: both programs are copyrighted, so the developer keeps ownership
> - Difference: shareware lets the user try the program first, but a commercial licence requires payment before use
> - Difference: a shareware trial may have features missing, but a commercial licence gives the full program
> - Difference: the user may pass copies of shareware on to other people, but may not pass on commercial software
> - Difference: a commercial licence usually includes support from the developer, which a shareware trial does not
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> Accept a difference written as a single sentence covering both licences, for example "shareware can be tried first but commercial software cannot".
> 
> Do not accept a point made about only one licence, such as "shareware has a trial period", with no statement of what the commercial licence does. A comparison must refer to both.
> 
> Do not accept "shareware is free and commercial software costs money". Shareware is free only during the trial.

> **[exam-tip]**
> "Compare" means both licences in every point. The single most common way to lose marks here is to write a paragraph about shareware, then a paragraph about commercial software, and leave the examiner to spot the link. Use the words "whereas" or "but" in each point and the comparison becomes explicit.
> 
> These two licences are more alike than students expect. Both are paid, both are closed source, and both are copyrighted. The trial is the real difference.

### Q6
The system analyses the words the user has typed and identifies the key words. **[1]**

It compares those key words with the words stored for each help page, and suggests the page with the highest probability of a match. **[1]**

Each time a user says whether the page was helpful, that result is stored together with the words the user typed. **[1]**

The system uses the stored results to change its own rules and data, so a page that users report as helpful becomes more likely to be suggested for those words in future. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark each, to a maximum of 4; maximum 2 from either section:**
> 
> Suggesting a page:
> 
> - Analyses the text the user has typed
> - Identifies the key words in it
> - Compares the key words with the words stored for each help page
> - Suggests the page with the highest probability of a match
> 
> Becoming more accurate:
> 
> - The user's response, saying whether the page was helpful, is stored
> - ... together with the words that user typed
> - The system uses the stored responses to change its own rules and data
> - A page reported as helpful becomes more likely to be suggested for those words
> - ... and a page reported as unhelpful becomes less likely to be suggested
> - The system therefore improves without a programmer rewriting the rules
> 
> **[4 marks]**
> 
> Accept "most likely match" in place of "highest probability of a match".
> 
> Accept "it learns from its mistakes" or "it learns how to improve" for one mark in the second section. Do not accept "it gets more intelligent" on its own.
> 
> Do not accept an answer that says a programmer updates the rules, because the system changes its own rules and data.

> **[exam-tip]**
> The usual mistake here is to answer only the first half. Students describe how the system picks a page and then stop, which caps the answer at two marks however well it is written. The question also asks how the suggestions get better, so at least two sentences must say what is stored after each use and what the system changes as a result. When a question asks two things, write two labelled halves and check you have marks in both.

### Q7
The user can install and use the program free of charge for a trial period. **[1]**

The user must pay for the full version in order to keep using the program after the trial ends. **[1]**

**[2 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any two from:**
> 
> - The user can install and use the program free of charge for a trial period
> - The trial version may be limited // some features may not be available until the user pays
> - The user must pay to get the full version // to keep using the program after the trial period ends
> - The source code is not made available, so the user may not legally change the program
> - The user may pass copies of the trial version on to other people
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> Accept "try before you buy" for the first point.
> 
> Do not accept "the software is free". Shareware is free only for the trial period, and free software is a different licence type on this syllabus.
> 
> Do not accept "the user can change the source code". That describes the Free Software Foundation and the Open Source Initiative, not shareware.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Shareware and free software sound similar and are not the same thing. Shareware is paid software with a free trial, and its source code stays closed. Free software and open source are about access to the source code, not about price.
> 
> The word "trial" is what an examiner is looking for here. An answer that only says "the user does not pay at first" is unclear about whether the program ever has to be paid for, and often scores nothing.

### Q8
For releasing the program: the company keeps the promise it made, so the client can begin using the program on the date they planned for. **[1]**

For releasing the program: missing the date may lose the company the contract, which could cost the programmer's colleagues their work. **[1]**

Against releasing the program: untested software may contain faults that lose the client's data or put users at risk, and the programmer must protect the health, safety and welfare of the public. **[1]**

Against releasing the program: releasing software the programmer knows has not been tested is not an honest claim about what the program does, so trust in the company is lost when the faults appear. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for each bullet point, max 2 for releasing and max 2 for not releasing
> 
> For releasing:
> 
> **1 mark** for the company keeps the promise it made to the client
> 
> **1 mark** for the client can start using the program on the date they planned for
> 
> **1 mark** for missing the date may lose the contract, which affects the company and the jobs of colleagues
> 
> **1 mark** for any remaining faults may be small and can be corrected in a later update
> 
> Against releasing:
> 
> **1 mark** for untested software may contain faults that lose data or put users at risk
> 
> **1 mark** for the programmer must maintain the health, safety and welfare of the public
> 
> **1 mark** for releasing software known to be untested is not an honest or realistic claim about the program
> 
> **1 mark** for public trust in the company is lost when the faults are found, which costs more than the delay would have
> 
> Accept "the client could be told about the missed date and asked to agree a new one" as a point against releasing.
> 
> Do not accept an answer that only says the programmer should refuse. The question asks for reasons on both sides, so a one-sided answer is limited to 2 marks.

> **[exam-tip]**
> This is the shape of ethics question that catches you out. It looks like it has an obvious answer, so it is tempting to write four reasons for refusing and score two.
> 
> Read the tariff and the wording together. Reasons for and against with 4 marks almost always means a maximum of 2 on each side. Write two points, change side, write two more, and label which side each point is on so the examiner cannot miss it.

### Q9
The camera captures an image of each piece of fruit as it passes. **[1]**

Image recognition is used to analyse the pixels in the image and find the pattern of a damaged area, such as a bruise or a mark. **[1]**

The pattern is compared with the patterns the system has learned from a large number of images of good fruit and damaged fruit, and the fruit is removed when the probability of a match to damaged fruit is high enough. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark each, to a maximum of 3:**
> 
> - Captures or takes an image of each piece of fruit
> - Uses image recognition
> - Analyses the pixels in the image
> - Identifies features or patterns, such as a bruise, a mark or an unusual shape
> - The system has been trained using a large number of images
> - ... of both good fruit and damaged fruit
> - Compares the features found with the patterns it has stored
> - Uses the probability of a match to make the decision
> - Fruit identified as damaged is removed from the belt
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> Accept "video frame" in place of "image".
> 
> Accept "most likely match" in place of "probability of a match".
> 
> Do not accept "the system learns" on its own, with no statement of what it learns from.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Every mark here comes from one step of the process: capture the image, analyse the pixels, compare with what the system was trained on, decide. The common way to lose marks is to write three sentences that all say the same thing, such as "it recognises the fruit, it knows which ones are bad, it identifies the damage". That is one marking point written three times, so it scores one mark. Move forward a step in each sentence.

### Q10
The programmer is the legal owner of the program, because the program is the programmer's intellectual property. **[1]**

Copyright legislation protects the program against unauthorised reproduction, so making the extra copies is illegal. **[1]**

This is true whether or not the person making the copies charges for them, because it is the act of copying without permission that breaks copyright, not the act of selling. **[1]**

The friends have not bought their own copies, so the programmer loses the income from them, and the programmer has a legal right of redress against the user. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any four from:**
> 
> - The programmer is the legal owner of the program // the program is the programmer's intellectual property
> - Copyright protects the work against unauthorised reproduction, so making the copies is illegal
> - Payment is not what decides whether copyright is broken // copyright is broken by copying without permission, not by making money
> - Only the copyright owner may give permission for copies to be made or distributed
> - Buying one copy gives the user the right to use that copy, not the right to produce more copies
> - The friends have not bought the program, so the programmer loses the income from those copies
> - The programmer has a legal right of redress // the programmer can take legal action against the user
> 
> **[4 marks]**
> 
> Accept "the user only paid for one copy, so only one copy is allowed" for the fifth point.
> 
> Do not accept "the user did not make a profit, so the harm is small". That is the argument the question asks you to reject, not a marking point.
> 
> Do not accept "the friends should have paid" on its own. It states the consequence without saying which right has been broken.

> **[exam-tip]**
> This question has two halves, and it is easy to answer only one of them. The legal half says which right was broken: the programmer owns the program and nobody may reproduce it without permission. The practical half says what the harm is: the lost income. Four marks needs both.
> 
> Watch the trap in the user's claim. Money is not what makes copying illegal, so an answer built entirely around "the user did not sell them" is answering the wrong question.

### Q11
Customers are protected from the fault, so the company maintains the health, safety and welfare of the public. **[1]**

Customers see that the company is honest about its own mistake, so public trust in the company is maintained and they are more likely to buy from it again. **[1]**

The company loses money, because it pays for the update and the staff time and receives nothing in return. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for each point below, to a maximum of 3
> 
> **1 mark** for customers are not harmed by the fault // the health, safety and welfare of the public is maintained
> 
> **1 mark** for the company is seen to be honest, so public trust is maintained
> 
> **1 mark** for the company becomes known as reputable, so customers buy from it again or recommend it
> 
> **1 mark** for the company loses money, because it pays for the update and receives nothing for it
> 
> **1 mark** for sales may fall in the short term, because some customers will avoid a program that has been reported as faulty
> 
> Accept "existing customers keep using the program instead of moving to a competitor".
> 
> Do not accept "the company obeys the law". No copyright or licensing law is involved here, and the question is about the impact of an ethical choice.

> **[exam-tip]**
> The word impact does not mean benefit. Acting ethically here costs the company real money, so a strong answer looks at that cost as well as the good outcomes.
> 
> Name the group affected each time, and consider both the good outcomes and the costs.

### Q12
Speech recognition, used to identify the words a person has spoken. **[1]**

Self-driving cars, used to control a vehicle without a driver. **[1]**

**[2 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any two from:**
> 
> - Facial recognition, used to identify a person from an image
> - Speech recognition or voice recognition
> - A natural language interface, such as a spoken assistant
> - Registration number or number plate recognition
> - Self-driving cars
> - Game playing, such as a computer opponent in a board game
> - Medical diagnosis from images or from a list of symptoms
> - Fault diagnosis, such as identifying a fault in a vehicle
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> Accept any other application where a task is named and a machine does that task in place of a person.
> 
> Do not accept the name of a machine on its own, for example "a robot", because no task is given.
> 
> Do not accept two answers that are the same task written twice, for example facial recognition and unlocking a phone with a face.

> **[exam-tip]**
> An application is a job that the AI system does, not a piece of hardware. Writing "a robot" or "a smartphone" earns nothing, because the marks are for the task. Name the task instead: "a robot that recognises spoken orders from customers".

### Q13
A program is the intellectual property of the person or company that wrote it, and copyright legislation makes them its legal owner. **[1]**

Without copyright legislation, anyone could copy a program and sell or give away work that they did not write. **[1]**

The developer would then earn no income from the program, so they could not afford to keep writing and supporting software. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any three from:**
> 
> - A program is the intellectual property of the person or company that wrote it // copyright legislation identifies the legal owner of the program
> - Writing a program takes a large amount of time and skill, but a copy of it costs nothing to make
> - Without the legislation anyone could copy a program and sell or distribute work they did not write
> - The developer would earn no income from the program // the developer could not recover the cost of writing it
> - ... so fewer developers would be willing to write and publish new programs
> - Copyright legislation gives the owner a legal right of redress // the owner can take legal action against anyone who copies the program
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> Accept "author", "programmer", "developer" or "software company" for the owner.
> 
> Do not accept "so that the program is safe" or "so that the program cannot be hacked". Those describe security, not ownership.
> 
> Do not accept an answer that only names a licence type. The question asks why the legislation is needed, not which licence a developer should choose.

> **[exam-tip]**
> The word "need" in the question is doing the work. Examiners want the reason the law has to exist, so the strongest answers say what would happen if it did not. A program is expensive to write and free to copy, and that gap is exactly what the law closes.
> 
> Many students answer this by defining copyright instead of explaining why it is needed. A definition on its own will usually earn one mark at most.

### Q14
The programmer has a duty to keep the personal data of the public secure, and opening the record breaks that duty. **[1]**

The record is not needed for the programmer's work, so there is no reason to open it. **[1]**

**[2 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any two from:**
> 
> - The programmer has a duty to maintain the security and privacy of the public's data
> - The customer has not given permission for their record to be seen
> - The record is not needed for the work, so there is no reason to open it
> - Public trust in the company and in the programmer is lost if this becomes known
> - Other staff may copy the behaviour if it is allowed to pass
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> Accept "the customer would object if they knew" as an alternative to the permission point.
> 
> Do not accept "the programmer might be sacked" or "the programmer could be fined". These are consequences for the programmer, not reasons the action is unethical.
> 
> Do not accept "the data could be stolen". Nothing in the question says the programmer copied or shared the record.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Students often answer this by describing a punishment. A punishment is what happens afterwards. The question asks why the action is wrong, so each mark must name a duty that has been broken or a person who has been harmed.
> 
> Notice also that the programmer did have the technical ability to open the record. Being able to do something is not the same as being allowed to do it, and that gap is exactly what ethics covers.

### Q15
Copyright legislation protects the programmer against anyone copying the program without permission. **[1]**

Copyright legislation protects the programmer against anyone selling or giving away the program without permission. **[1]**

**[2 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Any two from:**
> 
> - Unauthorised copying / reproduction of the program
> - Unauthorised selling or distribution of the program
> - Another person claiming to be the owner or author of the program
> - Illegal modification of the program // another person changing the program without permission
> - Another person using the program code inside their own program without permission
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> Accept "someone stealing the program" for the first point, and "someone pretending they wrote it" for the third.
> 
> Do not accept "stops other people using the program". A copyright owner sells other people the right to use the program. What copyright protects is the right to copy, change and distribute it.
> 
> Do not accept "protects the program from hackers" or "protects the program from viruses". Copyright is a legal right, not a security measure.

> **[exam-tip]**
> The commonest error on this topic is to describe copyright as a piece of technology, such as a password, a licence key or encryption. Copyright is a law. It does not physically stop anyone copying the program. It makes the copying illegal and gives the owner the right to take legal action.
> 
> Two marks means two separate protections. Writing the same idea twice in different words, for example "stops copying" and "stops people making copies", scores one mark only.

### Q16
The body publishes ethical guidelines, so the programmer does not have to decide alone what is ethical in this situation. **[1]**

The guidelines are written down, so the programmer can show the client the standard that every member must apply instead of making it a personal disagreement. **[1]**

The body provides help and support, for example legal advice, if the client disputes the programmer's decision. **[1]**

Refusing may cost the programmer this contract, but being a member shows other clients that the programmer is reputable, which may bring further work. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for each bullet point (max 4)
> 
> **1 mark** for the body has ethical guidelines the programmer must follow
> 
> **1 mark** for so the programmer does not have to decide alone what is ethical // it is written down
> 
> **1 mark** for the programmer can show the client an agreed standard rather than a personal opinion
> 
> **1 mark** for the body provides help and support, for example legal advice, if the client objects
> 
> **1 mark** for the body runs training courses, so the programmer can explain why the security checks are still needed
> 
> **1 mark** for membership shows other clients the programmer is reputable, which may replace work that is lost
> 
> Accept "the client can read the guidelines for themselves, so they can see the programmer is not simply being difficult".
> 
> Do not accept "the body will force the client to change their mind". A professional ethical body sets standards for its own members. It has no authority over a client.

> **[exam-tip]**
> There is a real cost to the programmer here, and the strongest answers say so. Refusing the request may lose the contract. A question that asks how membership helps is not asking you to pretend the decision is free.
> 
> Avoid writing that the body would punish or expel the programmer. Cambridge mark schemes for this topic list guidelines, entry requirements, support and training. Discipline is not on that list, so build your answer from the four things that are.

### Q17
The member has a set of ethical guidelines to follow, so the business knows the standards that will be applied to its work. **[1]**

The body may set an entry requirement or a test, so the business knows the programmer has been judged competent. **[1]**

Membership gives recognition of the programmer's skills and knowledge, so the business knows the programmer is reputable. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for each bullet point (max 3)
> 
> **1 mark** for the member has ethical guidelines to follow
> 
> **1 mark** for so the business knows what standards are being applied to the work
> 
> **1 mark** for there may be an entry requirement or test for membership
> 
> **1 mark** for so the business knows the programmer is competent // gives recognition of the programmer's skills and knowledge
> 
> **1 mark** for the business knows the programmer is reputable
> 
> **1 mark** for the body runs training courses, so the programmer's skills are kept up to date
> 
> Accept "the business has somewhere to turn for help if the programmer behaves badly".
> 
> Do not accept "the member is a better programmer". Membership is evidence that a standard is being applied, not proof of higher skill, and no mark scheme credits it.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Most students learn this topic from the programmer's point of view, so under pressure they write "it helps the programmer keep their skills up to date". Here the marks are awarded from the business's point of view, so that answer needs one more step: the skills are up to date, so the business gets work done to a current standard.
> 
> Whenever a question names who benefits, finish every point with the effect on that person or organisation.

### Q19
The professional has access to the personal data of every passenger, so they have a duty to keep that data secure. **[1]**

Passengers cannot see the program code, so they cannot check for themselves how their data is used and have to trust the professional instead. **[1]**

A very large number of passengers rely on the software, so one careless decision affects many people at the same time. **[1]**

Transport software that fails can put people in danger, so the professional must protect the health, safety and welfare of the public. **[1]**

**[4 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for each bullet point (max 4)
> 
> **1 mark** for the professional holds a large amount of personal data and must maintain its security
> 
> **1 mark** for passengers cannot inspect the code, so they have no way to check the professional's work
> 
> **1 mark** for passengers therefore have to trust the professional
> 
> **1 mark** for many people rely on the software, so a single decision affects a large number of them
> 
> **1 mark** for the professional must maintain the health, safety and welfare of the public
> 
> **1 mark** for the professional must be honest and realistic about what the software does, so the company and its passengers are not misled
> 
> Accept "the transport company also relies on the professional's judgement, because its own staff cannot check the code".
> 
> Do not accept "because it is illegal to lose data". This question asks about ethics, not legislation, and a general appeal to the law names no duty and no affected group.

> **[exam-tip]**
> A four-mark ethics question needs four separate points, not one point written four times. "It is important to be trustworthy", "people trust you" and "trust matters" are the same mark, so they score one between them.

### Q20
A customer may buy the program because of the claim, and then store valuable data on it that is not as safe as they were told. **[1]**

The programmer has a duty to be honest and realistic about what the software can do, so that public trust in the company is maintained. **[1]**

When the claim is shown to be untrue the company loses its reputation, so customers will not buy from it again. **[1]**

**[3 marks]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **1 mark** for each bullet point (max 3)
> 
> **1 mark** for customers may rely on the claim and store data that is then not protected
> 
> **1 mark** for a professional must be honest and realistic in claims made about software
> 
> **1 mark** for public trust in the company is lost when the claim is shown to be false
> 
> **1 mark** for the company loses its reputation, so customers do not buy from it again
> 
> **1 mark** for users could be put at risk or harmed if they act on a claim that is not true
> 
> Accept "the company could face legal action" as an alternative to the loss of trust point.
> 
> Do not accept "it is against the law" on its own, because it names no duty or affected group.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Weak answers stop at "because lying is wrong". That restates the question and earns nothing. Every mark here comes from naming a consequence: who is affected, and what happens to them.
>

### Q21
