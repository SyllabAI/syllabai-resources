### Q1
The skeletal formula of the intermediate is:

![Skeletal formula of 1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene, showing a benzene ring with a methyl group at position 1 and a nitro group (NO2) at position 4](assets/001-skeletal-formula-of-1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene-show.png)

**[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 1 mark for the correct skeletal formula of 1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene

> **[exam-tip]**
> To prepare an aromatic amine, the benzene ring must first be nitrated to introduce a –NO<sub>2</sub> group, which is then reduced to –NH<sub>2</sub> in the second step.
> 
> The methyl group on methylbenzene directs the incoming electrophile to the ortho and para positions. The para product, 1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene, is the major product due to steric factors.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The reagents and conditions for step 1 are:
> 
> - Concentrated nitric acid / conc HNO<sub>3</sub>
> **AND**
> concentrated sulfuric acid / conc H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> **[1 mark]**
> - Heat / reflux between 30–50 °C **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> A nitrating mixture of concentrated HNO<sub>3</sub> and concentrated H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> generates the nitronium ion (NO<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup>), which acts as the electrophile in the electrophilic substitution reaction.
> 
> Because the methyl group activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic substitution, nitration of methylbenzene requires a lower temperature than for plain benzene (55 °C). A temperature of 30–50 °C achieves mono-nitration while reducing the risk of a di-nitrated product forming.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Sodium hydroxide is required because:
> 
> - The reduction in strongly acidic conditions produces the protonated amine / 4-methylphenylammonium salt rather than the free amine **[1 mark]**
> - Aqueous sodium hydroxide deprotonates the ammonium salt / acts as a base to neutralise the acid and liberate the free amine **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> When the nitro group is reduced using Sn and concentrated HCl, the acidic conditions immediately protonate the –NH<sub>2</sub> group as it forms, producing a 4-methylphenylammonium salt. The nitrogen's lone pair accepts H<sup>+</sup> from the acid.
> 
> To obtain the free amine, aqueous NaOH is added after the reduction step. It accepts the H<sup>+</sup> from the ammonium ion (acting as a Brønsted–Lowry base), releasing the neutral 4-methylphenylamine.

> 
> - Phenol / C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> 4-hydroxyazobenzene contains an –OH group on one of the aromatic rings, which originates from phenol, the coupling partner. Phenol is dissolved in alkaline conditions (NaOH (aq)) to form the more reactive phenoxide ion (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sup>−</sup>) before coupling.
> 
> A common error is to suggest a compound that already contains nitrogen. The diazonium salt provides both nitrogen atoms in the –N=N– azo group. The coupling reagent simply needs to be an electron-rich aromatic compound activated by an electron-donating group such as –OH or –NR<sub>2</sub>.

### Q4
> **[mark-scheme]**
> i) The relative order of basicity, from least to most basic, is:
> 
> - Phenylamine < ammonia < *N*-methylethanamine **[1 mark]**
> 
> ii) The explanation for the order of basicity is:
> 
> - Alkyl groups donate electron density towards the N atom in *N*-methylethanamine / the methyl and ethyl groups have a positive inductive effect **[1 mark]**
> - The lone pair on N in *N*-methylethanamine is more available to accept a proton / bond with H<sup>+</sup> **[1 mark]**
> - In phenylamine, the lone pair on N overlaps with / becomes delocalised into the π system of the benzene ring **[1 mark]**
> - The lone pair on N in phenylamine is therefore less available to accept a proton **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Base strength depends on the availability of the lone pair on the nitrogen atom to accept H<sup>+</sup>.
> 
> For *N*-methylethanamine: two alkyl groups donate electron density towards N through the positive inductive effect, making the lone pair more available. This makes it the strongest base of the three.
> 
> For phenylamine: N is bonded directly to the benzene ring. The lone pair overlaps with the delocalised π electron system, partially withdrawing it from N and reducing its availability to bond with H<sup>+</sup>. This makes phenylamine the weakest base, weaker even than ammonia, which has no interaction with an aromatic ring system.

> - Reagents: Tin / Sn
