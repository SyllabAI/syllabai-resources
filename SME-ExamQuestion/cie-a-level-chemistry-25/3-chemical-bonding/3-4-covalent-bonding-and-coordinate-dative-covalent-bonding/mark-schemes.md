> **[exam-tip]**
> Every bond (single, double, or triple) contains exactly one σ bond.
> 
> A double bond adds 1 π bond.
> 
> A triple bond adds 2 π bonds.
> 
> Count all σ bonds first, then count π bonds separately from any multiple bonds.

### Q9

### Q14
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- An O<sub>2</sub> molecule contains a double bond (4 shared electrons, bond order 2).
- An N<sub>2</sub> molecule contains a triple bond (6 shared electrons, bond order 3)v
- The greater number of shared electrons in the N<sub>2</sub> triple bond creates a stronger electrostatic attraction between the shared electrons and the two nitrogen nuclei.

  - This pulls the atoms closer together and results in a shorter bond length.

**A** is incorrect as nitrogen (Group 15) actually has a larger atomic radius than oxygen (Group 16). Both are in Period 2, but oxygen has a greater nuclear charge (8 protons vs 7 protons), which pulls its outer electrons closer to the nucleus. A larger atomic radius would tend to increase bond length.

**B** is incorrect as it is true that oxygen is more electronegative than nitrogen. However, higher electronegativity tends to shorten bonds, not lengthen them. The fact that N<sub>2</sub> is shorter than O<sub>2</sub> demonstrates that bond order overrides electronegativity in this comparison.

**C** is incorrect as the O–O double bond in O<sub>2</sub> is not stronger than the N≡N triple bond. The triple bond (bond order 3) is stronger than the double bond (bond order 2).
