### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- The correct formulas are:

  - Phosphate PO<sub>4</sub><sup>3-</sup>
  - Ammonium NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>
  - Ethanoate CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>
  - Nitrite NO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup> - Note the distinction between nitrate (NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>) and nitrite (NO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup>).
- The ethanoate ion is derived from the dissociation of ethanoic acid:

CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (aq) ⇌ CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> (aq) + H<sup>+</sup> (aq)

**A** is incorrect as the ethanoate ion is an anion (CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>), not a cation (CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>+</sup>); and NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> is the nitrate ion, not the nitrite ion.

**B** is incorrect as the phosphate ion has the formula PO<sub>4</sub><sup>3-</sup>, not PO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>; and the ammonium ion is a cation (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>), not an anion.

**D** is incorrect as the phosphate ion has a 3- charge (PO<sub>4</sub><sup>3-</sup>), not 2-; and CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> is the propanoate ion, not ethanoate.

### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Gallium is in Group 13 and loses three valence electrons to form a Ga<sup>3+</sup> cation.
- Phosphorus is in Group 15 and gains three electrons to form a P<sup>3-</sup> anion.
- The 3+ and 3- charges balance in a 1:1 ratio, giving the formula GaP.

**B** is incorrect as GaP<sub>2</sub> would require gallium to form a 2+ ion and phosphorus to form a 1- ion, which does not match their Group 13 and 15 positions.

**C** is incorrect as Ga<sub>2</sub>P would imply gallium has a 1+ charge and phosphorus has a 2- charge, which is inconsistent with their respective group positions.

**D** is incorrect as Ga<sub>3</sub>P<sub>2</sub> implies gallium forms a 2+ ion and phosphorus a 3- ion. This is a common trap for students who confuse gallium with a Group 2 metal.

### Q4
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Ionic bonding is the strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions (cations and anions) in a crystal lattice.

**B** is incorrect as the electrostatic attraction between positive ions and a sea of delocalised electrons is the definition of metallic bonding, not ionic bonding.

**C** is incorrect as nuclei are positively charged, so two nuclei would experience electrostatic repulsion, not attraction.

**D** is incorrect as the electrostatic attraction between shared pairs of electrons and positively charged nuclei is the definition of covalent bonding.

### Q5

SiCl<sub>4</sub> + 2H<sub>2</sub>O → SiO<sub>2</sub> + 4HCl

### Q6
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Aluminium is in Group 13 and loses three electrons to form Al<sup>3+</sup>.
- Chloride is a 1- ion (Cl<sup>-</sup>).
- Three chloride ions are required to balance one aluminium ion, giving the correct formula AlCl<sub>3</sub>, not AlCl<sub>2</sub>.

**B** is not the answer as (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> is the correct formula for ammonium phosphate. The ammonium ion has a charge of 1+ (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>) and the phosphate ion has a charge of 3- (PO<sub>4</sub><sup>3-</sup>), so three ammonium ions balance one phosphate ion. This statement is true.

**C** is not the answer as nitrogen is in Group 15 and gains three electrons to achieve a stable octet, forming the nitride ion N<sup>3-</sup>. This statement is true.

**D** is not the answer as potassium is in Group 1 and loses one electron to form K<sup>+</sup>, a cation. This statement is true.

### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- CH<sub>3</sub>OH contains only non-metal atoms (carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen) with no metal involved.
- The atoms share electrons to form covalent bonds only.

  - No electron transfer occurs.

**A** is incorrect as barium is a metal and chloride is a non-metal ion. BaCl<sub>2</sub> forms a giant ionic lattice with ionic bonds between Ba<sup>2+</sup> and Cl<sup>-</sup> ions.

**B** is incorrect as CaCO<sub>3</sub> contains ionic bonds between Ca<sup>2+</sup> and CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup> ions. The carbonate ion also contains covalent bonds, but the compound is held together by ionic bonding.

**D** is incorrect as NaOH contains ionic bonds between Na<sup>+</sup> and OH<sup>-</sup> ions. The hydroxide ion also contains a covalent O–H bond.

### Q8
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Magnesium is a metal and oxygen is a non-metal.
- Magnesium loses two electrons to form Mg<sup>2+</sup> ions and oxygen gains two electrons to form O<sup>2-</sup> ions.
- These oppositely charged ions are held together by strong electrostatic attraction in a giant ionic lattice.

**B** is incorrect as giant metallic bonding involves metal ions surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons. It is only present in pure metals or alloys, not in metal–non-metal compounds such as MgO.

**C** is incorrect as macromolecular (giant covalent) structures consist of a network of atoms linked by covalent bonds. Examples include elements such as silicon, diamond, and graphite, and compounds such as silicon dioxide (SiO<sub>2</sub>). MgO does not contain covalent bonds.

**D** is incorrect as simple molecular structures consist of discrete molecules held together by weak intermolecular forces, giving low melting points. Iodine (I<sub>2</sub>) is a typical example.

### Q9
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Aluminium is in Group 13 and forms a 3+ ion (Al<sup>3+</sup>).
- The sulfate ion has a fixed charge of 2- (SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>).

![Diagram showing the criss-cross method: Al³⁺ and SO₄²⁻ charges swap to form the ionic compound formula Al₂(SO₄)₃.](assets/001-diagram-showing-the-criss-cross-method-al-and-so.jpg)

- To balance: 2 x Al<sup>3+</sup> gives 6+ total; 3 x SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> gives 6- total.
- The formula is Al<sub>2</sub>(SO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub>.

**A** is incorrect as AlSO<sub>4</sub> would require aluminium to have a charge equal in magnitude to sulfate, which is not consistent with Group 13 chemistry.

**B** is incorrect as Al(SO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub> would imply aluminium has a 4+ charge, which is not consistent with aluminium being in Group 13.

**D** is incorrect as Al<sub>3</sub>(SO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub> implies aluminium forms a 2+ ion — a common trap for students who confuse aluminium with a Group 2 metal such as magnesium.
