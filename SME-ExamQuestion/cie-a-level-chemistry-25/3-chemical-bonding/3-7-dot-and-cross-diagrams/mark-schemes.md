### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Both **X** (5 outer-shell electrons, Group 15) and **Y** (7 outer-shell electrons, Group 17) are non-metals.
- Neither element will transfer electrons to the other, as both strongly attract electrons to complete their octets. They must share electrons to form a **covalent** bond.
- **X** requires 3 more electrons to complete its octet, so it forms 3 covalent bonds.
- **Y** requires 1 more electron to complete its octet, so each **Y** atom forms 1 covalent bond.
- One atom of **X** therefore bonds with 3 atoms of **Y**, giving the formula **XY**<sub>3</sub>.

![Dot-and-cross diagram of a molecule with the structure of XY~3~, showing the central X atom with 3 bond pairs and 1 lone pair](assets/011-dot-and-cross-diagram-of-a-molecule-with-the-str.jpg)

**A** and **B** are incorrect as the formula **X**<sub>3</sub>**Y** inverts the combining ratios. **X** needs 3 more electrons and forms 3 bonds, while **Y** needs only 1. One atom of **X** bonds with 3 atoms of **Y**, not the reverse.

**D** is incorrect as ionic bonding requires electron transfer from a metal to a non-metal. Both **X** and **Y** are non-metals with high numbers of outer-shell electrons. Neither element will donate electrons to the other, instead they must share them.

> **[mark-scheme]**

>

> **[exam-tip]**
> The NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> ion has three different types of N-O bond. To construct the diagram:
> 
> 1. Start with nitrogen: 5 outer electrons.
> 2. Double bond: 2 electrons from N, 2 from O — this oxygen now has a complete octet.
> 3. Single bond: 1 from N, 1 from O — this oxygen needs 1 extra electron to complete its octet. That extra electron accounts for the 1- charge on the ion.
> 4. Coordinate bond: both electrons donated from N's lone pair to the third O atom.
> 5. Complete the lone pairs on each oxygen.
> 6. Enclose in square brackets and place the 1- charge as a superscript outside.
> 
> Without the square brackets and charge, you will lose the second mark.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The ions present in H<sub>4</sub>N<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> are:

> **[exam-tip]**

> 
> Learn the formulae and charges of all common polyatomic ions as CIE will not provide them. Key ions to know: NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>, NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>, CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>, OH<sup>-</sup>, and PO<sub>4</sub><sup>3-</sup>.

> Comparing the C≡N bond in the cyanide ion with the C-N bond in methylamine:
> 
> - The bond energy of C≡N in CN<sup>-</sup> is greater than the bond energy of C-N in methylamine **[1 mark]**

>

> - Bond length: the internuclear distance between two covalently bonded atoms

> **[mark-scheme]**

> 
> 1 mark for a correct dot-and-cross diagram of SF<sub>4</sub>: central S atom with 4 bond pairs (one to each F), 1 lone pair on S; 3 lone pairs on each F atom (expanded octet on S)

> Both PBr<sub>3</sub> and SF<sub>4</sub> have central atoms from Period 3, so they can accommodate more than 8 electrons in their outer shell (expanded octet). This is only possible for elements in Period 3 and beyond, because they have vacant d orbitals available.
> 
> For PBr<sub>3</sub>: phosphorus is in Group 15 (5 outer electrons) and each bromine is in Group 17 (7 outer electrons). Draw 3 bond pairs between P and each Br, plus 1 lone pair on P. Each Br has 3 lone pairs.
> 
> For SF<sub>4</sub>: sulfur is in Group 16 (6 outer electrons) and each fluorine is in Group 17 (7 outer electrons). Draw 4 bond pairs between S and each F, plus 1 lone pair on S (giving S 10 electrons in total). Each F has 3 lone pairs.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The shape and bond angle of PBr<sub>3</sub> are:
> 
> - Pyramidal / trigonal pyramidal **[1 mark]**
> - 107° / approximately 107° **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> PBr<sub>3</sub> has 3 bonding pairs and 1 lone pair around the central phosphorus atom. The lone pair exerts greater repulsion than a bonding pair, pushing the three P-Br bonds downward and reducing the bond angle from the ideal tetrahedral value of 109.5° to approximately 107°.
> 
> This is the same electron arrangement as NH<sub>3</sub>, PCl<sub>3</sub>, and NF<sub>3</sub>: all have a pyramidal shape and a bond angle of approximately 107°.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> PBr<sub>3</sub> and SF<sub>4</sub> are polar because:
> 
> - P-Br and S-F bonds are polar / there is a difference in electronegativity between the bonded atoms **[1 mark]**
> - The molecules have non-symmetrical shapes so the bond dipoles do not cancel **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Both PBr<sub>3</sub> (pyramidal) and SF<sub>4</sub> (see-saw) have unsymmetrical shapes due to the lone pair(s) on the central atom. Even though each individual P-Br or S-F bond is polar, the key point is that the bond dipoles do not point in equal and opposite directions, so they cannot cancel.
> 
> Compare this with a non-polar molecule such as BF<sub>3</sub> (trigonal planar, no lone pairs). In BF<sub>3</sub> the three equal B-F dipoles cancel exactly because the molecule is perfectly symmetrical. The absence of lone pairs on the central atom is what allows full cancellation.
