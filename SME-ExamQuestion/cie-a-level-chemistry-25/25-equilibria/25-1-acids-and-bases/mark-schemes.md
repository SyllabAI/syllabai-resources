[H<sup>+</sup>] = 1.12 x 10<sup>-5</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

- Calculate the pH:

pH = –log(1.12 x 10<sup>-5</sup>)

pH = **4.95**

> **[exam-tip]**
> When NaOH is added to the buffer, OH<sup>-</sup> reacts with the weak acid:
> 
> CH<sub>3</sub>COOH + OH<sup>-</sup> → CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> + H<sub>2</sub>O.
> 
> This decreases the moles of acid and increases the moles of salt by the same amount. Since both components change by only 1.00 x 10<sup>-3</sup> mol, and the initial moles are much larger, the pH changes by only ~0.02. This is the buffer in action.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Equation 1: addition of acid to the buffer
> 
> - CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> (aq) + H<sup>+</sup> (aq) → CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (aq)
> **OR**
> CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> (aq) + H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup> (aq) → CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l) **[1 mark]**
> 
> Equation 2: addition of base to the buffer
> 
> - CH<sub>3</sub>COOH (aq) + OH<sup>-</sup> (aq) → CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l) **[1 mark]**
> 
> State symbols are required for each equation

> **[exam-tip]**
> Buffers resist pH change by reacting with both added acid and added base. The conjugate base (CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>) mops up added H<sup>+</sup> ions, and the weak acid (CH<sub>3</sub>COOH) reacts with added OH<sup>-</sup> ions. Both equations must include state symbols. Note: adding H<sub>3</sub>O<sup>+</sup> and H<sub>2</sub>O as a product is also accepted for equation 1.
