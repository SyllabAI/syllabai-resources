**Q1** [1 mark]

Calcium phosphate is a sparingly soluble naturally occurring mineral that is a large component of bones and teeth. The *K*<sub>sp</sub> value at 25 °C for calcium phosphate is 2.07 x 10<sup>-33</sup>.

Construct an equation to show the equilibrium present in a saturated solution of calcium phosphate. Include state symbols.

**Q1** [4 marks]

**Extract:**

Calcium phosphate is a sparingly soluble naturally occurring mineral that is a large component of bones and teeth. The Ksp value at 25 °C for calcium phosphate is 2.07 x 10^-33.

The equilibrium equation for the dissolution of calcium phosphate is:
Ca3(PO4)2 (s) ⇌ 3Ca^2+ (aq) + 2PO4^3- (aq)

Use your answer to part **(a)** to calculate the concentration, in mol dm<sup>-3</sup>, of the calcium and phosphate ions in solution. Show your working.

**Q1** [2 marks]

**Extract:**

Let x = solubility of Ca~3~(PO~4~)~2~, so [Ca^2+^ (aq)] = 3x and [PO~4~^3-^ (aq)] = 2x

x = 1.14 x 10^-7^ mol dm^-3^

[Ca^2+^ (aq)] = 3 x 1.14 x 10^-7^ = 3.42 x 10^-7^ mol dm^-3^

[PO~4~^3-^ (aq)] = 2 x 1.14 x 10^-7^ = 2.28 x 10^-7^ mol dm^-3^

Use your answer to part **(b)** to calculate the mass of solute, in grams, in 150 cm<sup>3</sup> of solution at 25 °C. Show your working.

*K*<sub>pc</sub> = `\frac{\left[\text{equilibrium concentration in organic layer / mol dm}^{-3}\right]}{\left[\text{equilibrium concentration in aqueous layer / mol dm}^{-3}\right]}`

CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> (aq) ⇌ CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>NH<sub>2</sub> (organic solvent)
