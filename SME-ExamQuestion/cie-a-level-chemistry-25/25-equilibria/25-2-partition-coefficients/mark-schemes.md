### Q2
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The equilibrium equation for a saturated solution of silver bromide is:
> 
> - AgBr (s) ⇌ Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq) + Br<sup>–</sup> (aq) **[1 mark]**
> 
> State symbols are required

> **[exam-tip]**
> Silver bromide dissolves to give one Ag<sup>+</sup> ion and one Br<sup>–</sup> ion per formula unit. Including state symbols, (s) for the undissolved solid and (aq) for the dissolved ions, is required for the mark.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The effect on the concentration of dissolved silver ions when sodium bromide solution is added:
> 
> - Decreases **[1 mark]**
> - The increase in [Br<sup>–</sup> (aq)] shifts the equilibrium to the left (towards AgBr (s)) **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> This is an application of Le Chatelier’s principle. Adding sodium bromide increases the Br<sup>–</sup> concentration. To oppose this change, the equilibrium shifts left, consuming both Ag<sup>+</sup> and Br<sup>–</sup> ions and forming more AgBr (s), so [Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)] decreases.
To calculate the concentration of silver ions in a saturated solution of silver bromide:

- Let *s* = solubility of AgBr = [Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)] = [Br<sup>–</sup> (aq)]

- Substitute into the *K*<sub>sp</sub> expression:

*K*<sub>sp</sub> = [Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)][Br<sup>–</sup> (aq)] = *s*<sup>2</sup> = 3.3 x 10<sup>-13</sup> **[1 mark]**

- Solve for *s*:

[Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)] = $\sqrt{3.3\times10^{-13}}$

[Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)] = **5.74 x 10**<sup>**-7**</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup> **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 1 mark for *K*<sub>sp</sub> = *s*<sup>2</sup> = 3.3 x 10<sup>-13</sup>
> 
> 1 mark for [Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq)] = 5.74 x 10<sup>-7</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup>
> 
> Allow 5.7 x 10<sup>-7</sup> mol dm<sup>-3</sup> (2 sf)

> **[exam-tip]**
> For a 1:1 salt such as AgBr, the two ion concentrations in a saturated solution are equal (both equal to the solubility *s*), so *K*<sub>sp</sub> = *s*<sup>2</sup>. Taking the square root gives the concentration of each ion.
