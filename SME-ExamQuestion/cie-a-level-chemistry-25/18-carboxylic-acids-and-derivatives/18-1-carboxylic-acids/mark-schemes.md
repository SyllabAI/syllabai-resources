### Q4
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Calculate moles of NaOH required:

- Citric acid contains 3 carboxylic acid groups, each neutralised by one NaOH molecule.
- Therefore, for every 1 mole of citric acid, 3 moles of NaOH are required.
- 0.008 moles of citric acid is used.

0.008 x 3 = 0.024 moles of NaOH are required.

- Calculate volume of NaOH required:

moles = concentration x volume

volume = $\frac{\mathrm{moles}}{\mathrm{concentration}}$

volume = $\frac{0.024}{0.2}$

volume = 0.12 dm<sup>3</sup>

- Convert from dm<sup>3</sup> to cm<sup>3</sup>:

volume = 0.12 × 1000 = 120 cm<sup>3</sup>

**A** is incorrect as it assumes a 1:1 molar ratio between citric acid and NaOH, giving 40 cm<sup>3</sup>. The word "acid" does not always mean one acidic group.

**B** is incorrect as it assumes a 1:2 molar ratio. This occurs when only two of the three carboxylic acid groups are identified, giving 80 cm<sup>3</sup>. The most common mistake is missing the one written as CO<sub>2</sub>H in the central bracket

**D** is incorrect as it assumes a 1:4 molar ratio by incorrectly treating the alcohol –OH group as acidic. The –OH group is an alcohol, not a phenol, and does not react with NaOH. Counting all four –OH-containing groups gives 160 cm<sup>3</sup>.

### Q10
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- A 'hydroxyl' group is an '–OH' group, which in malic acid appears in both carboxylic acid groups and the alcohol group.
- If the substance only reacts with one hydroxyl group, this must be the alcohol group.

  - So, we must identify what does not react with a carboxylic acid group.
- The dichromate ion (Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2-</sup>), produced when K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub> is dissolved in acidified aqueous solution, acts as an oxidising agent.
- Carboxylic acids are already at their highest oxidation state and therefore will not react with acidified dichromate ions.
- The secondary alcohol in malic acid is oxidised to a ketone, which is not oxidised further.

**B** is incorrect as sodium metal reacts with **both** the carboxylic acid groups and the alcohol group in redox reactions, producing a salt and hydrogen gas:

2RCOOH + 2Na $\rightarrow$ 2RCOONa + H<sub>2</sub>

2ROH + 2Na $\rightarrow$ 2RONa + H<sub>2</sub>

**C** is incorrect as sodium hydroxide reacts with **both** carboxylic acid groups in a neutralisation reaction:

RCOOH + NaOH $\rightarrow$ RCOONa + H<sub>2</sub>O

**D** is incorrect as PCl<sub>5</sub> reacts with **both** the carboxylic acid groups (producing an acyl chloride) and the alcohol group (producing a haloalkane):

RCOOH + PCl<sub>5</sub> $\rightarrow$ RCOCl + POCl<sub>3</sub> + HCl

ROH + PCl<sub>5</sub> $\rightarrow$ RCl + POCl<sub>3</sub> + HCl
