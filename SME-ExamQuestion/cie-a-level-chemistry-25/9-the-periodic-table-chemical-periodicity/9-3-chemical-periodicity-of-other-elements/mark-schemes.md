### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- The oxide of element **Z** can react with both acids and bases, meaning it is amphoteric
- Aluminium oxide (Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) is the Period 3 oxide that is amphoteric:
- Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> reacts with acids:

Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s) + 6HCl (aq) → 2AlCl<sub>3</sub> (aq) + 3H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

- Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> reacts with bases:

Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s) + 2NaOH (aq) + 3H<sub>2</sub>O (l) → 2NaAl(OH)<sub>4</sub> (aq)

- Therefore, element **Z** must be Al

**A** is incorrect as silicon dioxide (SiO<sub>2</sub>) is an acidic oxide. It reacts with bases but not with acids.

**B** is incorrect as magnesium oxide (MgO) is a basic oxide. It reacts with acids but not with bases.

**C** is incorrect as chlorine forms simple molecular oxides (such as Cl<sub>2</sub>O) that are gases or liquids at room temperature, not solids. These are strictly acidic oxides.

### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- When 1.0 mol of SO<sub>3</sub> is added to water, it produces exactly 1.0 mol of sulfuric acid:

SO<sub>3</sub> (s) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l) → H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq)

- 1.0 mol of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> is then neutralised by exactly 1.0 mol of Na<sub>2</sub>O:

H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq) + Na<sub>2</sub>O (s) → Na<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

- The 1:1 molar ratio is satisfied

**B** is incorrect as 1.0 mol of P<sub>4</sub>O<sub>10</sub> produces 4.0 mol of phosphoric(V) acid:

P<sub>4</sub>O<sub>10</sub> + 6H<sub>2</sub>O → 4H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>.

Neutralising 4.0 mol of H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> with Na<sub>2</sub>O requires 6.0 mol of Na<sub>2</sub>O, not 1.0 mol.

**C** is incorrect as 1.0 mol of SO<sub>3</sub> produces 1.0 mol of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>, but 1.0 mol of Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> neutralises only 3.0 mol of HCl (not 2.0 mol of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>):

3H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> + Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> → Al<sub>2</sub>(SO<sub>4</sub>)<sub>3</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>O.

The molar ratio is 3:1, not 1:1.

**D** is incorrect as 1.0 mol of P<sub>4</sub>O<sub>10</sub> produces 4.0 mol of H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>, which requires 2.0 mol of Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> to neutralise:

4H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub> + 2Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> → 4AlPO<sub>4</sub> + 6H<sub>2</sub>O.

> **[exam-tip]**
> This question requires two separate balanced equations: one for the hydrolysis of J (forming an acid) and one for the neutralisation of that acid by G.
> 
> Work through each option systematically:
> 
> 1. Write the hydrolysis equation for J to find how many moles of acid are produced per mole of J
> 2. Write the neutralisation equation to find how many moles of G are needed per mole of acid
> 3. Multiply to find moles of G needed per mole of J

### Q4
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- PCl<sub>3</sub> and SiCl<sub>4</sub> are both simple covalent liquids at room temperature
- Simple covalent liquids contain no ions or delocalised electrons, so they do not conduct electricity in the liquid state
- When added to water, both undergo vigorous hydrolysis:

PCl<sub>3</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>O → H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>3</sub> (aq) + 3HCl (aq)

SiCl<sub>4</sub> + 2H<sub>2</sub>O → SiO<sub>2</sub> (s) + 4HCl (aq)

- The HCl produced fully dissociates to give H<sup>+</sup> and Cl<sup>-</sup> ions, so the resulting solutions conduct electricity

**A** is incorrect as MgCl<sub>2</sub> is a giant ionic lattice. When molten, the ions become free to move, so MgCl<sub>2</sub> does conduct electricity in its liquid state. A common trap is to only consider aqueous conductivity and overlook the molten state condition.

**B** is incorrect as MgCl<sub>2</sub> conducts electricity when molten (see above), so it does not satisfy the condition of not conducting in the liquid state.

**D** is incorrect as NaCl is a giant ionic lattice that also conducts electricity when molten. Like MgCl<sub>2</sub>, it does not satisfy the condition.

### Q6
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- Atomic radius (measured as covalent radius) decreases across a period due to increasing nuclear charge pulling electrons in the same shell closer to the nucleus
- Chlorine has the smallest atomic radius in Period 3 because it has the highest nuclear charge of the non-noble gas elements
- Therefore, element **X** is chlorine

- Phosphorus (P<sub>4</sub>) and sulfur (S<sub>8</sub>) are simple molecular substances whose melting points depend on the strength of instantaneous dipole-induced dipole forces between molecules
- S<sub>8</sub> has more electrons than P<sub>4</sub>, so sulfur has stronger intermolecular forces and a higher melting point than phosphorus
- The Period 3 melting point order for simple molecular non-metals is:

Cl<sub>2</sub> < P<sub>4</sub> < S<sub>8</sub>

- Apart from argon, only chlorine has a lower melting point than phosphorus
- Therefore, element **Y** is phosphorus, and compound **Z** is PCl<sub>3</sub>

**A** is incorrect as Na<sub>2</sub>S would require X to be Na and Y to be S. Sodium does not have the smallest atomic radius in Period 3.

**B** is incorrect as MgS would require X to be Mg and Y to be S. Magnesium does not have the smallest atomic radius in Period 3. Sulfur also has a higher melting point than phosphorus, so it does not fit the melting point clue.

**C** is incorrect as MgCl<sub>2</sub> would require X to be Mg and Y to be Cl. Magnesium does not have the smallest atomic radius in Period 3.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Argon is a noble gas and its atomic radius is measured as a van der Waals radius (around 188 pm), which is much larger than the covalent radius of chlorine (around 99 pm). Argon does not have a smaller covalent radius than chlorine.

### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- A melting point of 1710 °C indicates a giant covalent (macromolecular) structure, requiring large amounts of energy to break covalent bonds throughout the lattice
- Silicon reacts with oxygen to form silicon dioxide, which has a giant covalent structure:

Si (s) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → SiO<sub>2</sub> (s)

- SiO<sub>2</sub> is an acidic oxide and reacts with hot aqueous sodium hydroxide:

SiO<sub>2</sub> (s) + 2NaOH (aq) → Na<sub>2</sub>SiO<sub>3</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

- Therefore, element **X** is silicon

**B** is incorrect as sodium reacts with oxygen to form sodium oxide (Na<sub>2</sub>O), which is a giant ionic lattice. It is a basic oxide. Basic oxides react with acids, not with other bases, so Na<sub>2</sub>O will not react with sodium hydroxide.

**C** is incorrect as chlorine forms simple molecular oxides (such as Cl<sub>2</sub>O) with low melting points, because only weak intermolecular forces need to be overcome. This does not satisfy the 1710 °C clue.

**D** is incorrect as sulfur forms simple molecular oxides (SO<sub>2</sub>, SO<sub>3</sub>) with low melting points for the same reason. This does not satisfy the 1710 °C clue.

### Q8
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- Iron commonly exists as Fe<sup>2+</sup> and Fe<sup>3+</sup>.
- Aluminium forms a 3+ ion (Al<sup>3+</sup>), which matches iron's common +3 oxidation state
- Al<sup>3+</sup> has 10 electrons, which is the same number as neon
- Aluminium oxide (Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) is insoluble in water
- Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to produce aluminium chloride:

Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> (s) + 6HCl (aq) → 2AlCl<sub>3</sub> (aq) + 3H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

- Therefore, element **Z** is aluminium

**A** is incorrect as magnesium forms a 2+ ion (Mg<sup>2+</sup>), which matches iron's +2 oxidation state and has 10 electrons (isoelectronic with Ne). However, magnesium oxide is sparingly soluble in water, forming a weakly alkaline solution. It does not satisfy the condition of being insoluble in water.

**B** is incorrect as silicon(IV) oxide (SiO<sub>2</sub>) is insoluble in water, but it is an acidic oxide. It will not react with hydrochloric acid to produce a salt.

**C** is incorrect as sodium forms a 1+ ion (Na<sup>+</sup>). +1 is not a common oxidation state for iron.

> **[exam-tip]**
> A common trap is to select magnesium because it satisfies both the electron configuration and oxidation state clues, but MgO is sparingly soluble in water rather than truly insoluble.

### Q9
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- For element **X**, there is a large jump in ionisation energy between the 2nd and 3rd electrons.

  - This means that **X** must be in Group 2 with two outer electrons
- Argon has 18 electrons.
- The Group 2 element that forms a 2+ ion with 18 electrons is calcium:

20 − 2 = 18 electrons → Ca<sup>2+</sup> is isoelectronic with Ar

- For element **Y**, there is a large jump in ionisation energy between the 5th and 6th electrons

  - This means that **Y** must be in Group 15 with five outer electrons
- Argon has 18 electrons.
- The Group 15 element that forms a 3− ion with 18 electrons is phosphorus:

15 + 3 = 18 electrons → P<sup>3-</sup> is isoelectronic with Ar

**B** is incorrect as a sodium ion (Na<sup>+</sup>) has 10 electrons and an oxygen ion (O<sup>2-</sup>) has 10 electrons, so neither is isoelectronic with argon (18 electrons). They are isoelectronic with neon.

**C** is incorrect as a magnesium ion (Mg<sup>2+</sup>) has 10 electrons and a nitrogen ion (N<sup>3-</sup>) has 10 electrons, so neither is isoelectronic with argon. They are isoelectronic with neon.

**D** is incorrect as potassium is in Group 1, not Group 2, so there is no large jump between the 2nd and 3rd ionisation energies. The elements are also written in the wrong order.

> **[exam-tip]**
> A common trap is to confuse argon (18 electrons) with neon (10 electrons). Sodium, oxygen, magnesium, and nitrogen all form ions with 10 electrons. They are isoelectronic with neon, not argon.
