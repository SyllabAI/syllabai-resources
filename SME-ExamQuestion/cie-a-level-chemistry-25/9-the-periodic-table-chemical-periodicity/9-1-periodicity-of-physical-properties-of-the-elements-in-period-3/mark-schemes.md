X (g) → X<sup>+</sup> (g) + e<sup>-</sup>

| S | ↑↓ | ↑ | ↑ |

- Phosphorus (P<sub>4</sub>) and sulfur (S<sub>8</sub>) are both **simple covalent molecules**.

### Q10
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- Both sodium and sulfur are in **Period 3.**
- **Atomic radius**:

  - Decreases from left to right across a period.
  - The nuclear charge increases from Na (+11) to S (+16).
  - But, electrons are added to the same principal quantum shell (n = 3), so shielding remains relatively constant.
  - The increased nuclear attraction pulls the outer electrons closer.
  - This means that the atomic radius of Na is greater than that of S.
- **Ionic radius:**

  - Sodium forms Na<sup>+</sup> by losing its outer 3s electron.
  - Electronic configuration of Na<sup>+</sup>: 1s<sup>2</sup> 2s<sup>2</sup> 2p<sup>6</sup> (2 principal quantum shells)
  - Sulfur forms S<sup>2-</sup> by gaining two electrons to complete its 3p subshell.
  - Electronic configuration of S<sup>2-</sup>: 1s<sup>2</sup> 2s<sup>2</sup> 2p<sup>6</sup> 3s<sup>2</sup> 3p<sup>6</sup> (3 principal quantum shells)
  - S<sup>2-</sup> has one more principal quantum shell than Na<sup>+</sup>.
  - This means that the ionic radius of Na<sup>+</sup> is less than that of S<sup>2-</sup>.

**A** is incorrect as the ionic comparison is correct but the atomic radius of Na is greater than S, not less.

**C** is incorrect as the atomic comparison is correct but Na<sup>+</sup> has fewer principal quantum shells than S<sup>2-</sup>, so the ionic radius of Na<sup>+</sup> is less than that of S<sup>2-</sup>, not greater.

**D** is incorrect as both comparisons are reversed.
