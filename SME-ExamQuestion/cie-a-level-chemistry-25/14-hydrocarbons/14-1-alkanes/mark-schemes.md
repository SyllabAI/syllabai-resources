### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- Cracking is a reaction in which larger alkane molecules are broken down into smaller alkane and alkene molecules.
- For C<sub>16</sub>H<sub>34</sub> to be produced from C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>36</sub>, the remaining fragment would be CH<sub>2</sub>:

C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>36</sub> → C<sub>16</sub>H<sub>34</sub> + CH<sub>2</sub>

- CH<sub>2</sub> cannot exist as a stable molecule.
- The minimum stable alkene requires at least two carbon atoms to form a C=C double bond (ethene, C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>).
- Therefore, C<sub>16</sub>H<sub>34</sub> cannot be produced by cracking.

**A**, **B** and **C** are incorrect as these compounds can all be produced by valid cracking equations that leave a stable alkane or alkene as the other fragment:

C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>36</sub> → C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + C<sub>14</sub>H<sub>28</sub> (alkane + alkene)

C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>36</sub> → C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + C<sub>13</sub>H<sub>28</sub> (alkene + alkane)

C<sub>17</sub>H<sub>36</sub> → C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>16</sub> + C<sub>9</sub>H<sub>20</sub> (alkene + alkane)

### Q4
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- A propagation step involves a free radical reacting with a stable molecule to produce a new radical and a new stable molecule, keeping the chain reaction going.
- **A** shows how a chlorine radical abstracts a hydrogen from chloroethane, producing a chloroethyl radical and HCl.

  - The number of free radicals is unchanged, so this is a valid propagation step.
- The standard propagation steps for the ethane and chlorine reaction are:

C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> + Cl• → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>• + HCl

C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>• + Cl<sub>2</sub> → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>Cl + Cl•

- Further substitution of the product C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>Cl is also a valid propagation step.

**B** is incorrect as it shows two radicals combining to form one stable molecule, which is a termination step (net destruction of radicals).

**C** is incorrect as it produces an H• radical. Hydrogen radicals are not formed in this mechanism; Cl• abstracts H to form HCl, not H•.

**D** is incorrect as the equation is unbalanced. Chlorine appears in the product (HCl) but not in the reactants.

### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Initiation = net formation of radicals
- In the initiation step, free radical Cl• atoms are formed:

Cl<sub>2</sub> → 2Cl•

- Propagation = no change in the number of free radicals
- These free radical Cl• atoms remove a hydrogen from the ethane making an ethyl free radical, C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>•:

C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> + Cl• → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>• + HCl

- Termination = net destruction of free radicals
- Two of these radicals can then pair up in a termination step to form butane, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> (C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>):

C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>• + C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>• → C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>

- Therefore, option **C** is the only compound that could be present in the mixture.

**A** is incorrect as CH<sub>3</sub>Cl cannot be formed from the reaction between ethane and chlorine. All carbon-containing products must retain the two-carbon skeleton of ethane; a one-carbon product such as chloromethane is not possible.

**B** is incorrect as CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Cl (1-chloropropane) contains three carbon atoms and cannot be formed from ethane and chlorine. No three-carbon products can arise from a two-carbon starting material.

**D** is incorrect as CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> (pentane) contains five carbon atoms. The termination step joining two ethyl radicals (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>•) produces butane (four carbons), not pentane.

### Q8
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- The highest demand hydrocarbon for transportation is octane (C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>18</sub>).
- Subtract octane from eicosane to find the remaining fragment:

C<sub>20</sub>H<sub>42</sub> - C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>18</sub> = C<sub>12</sub>H<sub>24</sub>

- C<sub>12</sub>H<sub>24</sub> has the general formula C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n</sub>, so the remaining three products are alkenes.
- Let the smallest alkene have n carbons.

  - Three consecutive alkenes total 12 carbons:

n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 12

3n + 3 = 12

n = 3

- The three consecutive alkenes are propene (C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub>), butene (C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub>) and pentene (C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub>).
- Overall cracking equation:
- C<sub>20</sub>H<sub>42</sub> → C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>18</sub> + C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub> + C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + C<sub>5</sub>H<sub>10</sub>

**A** is incorrect as the carbon count of the products (4 + 4 + 6 + 8 = 22) exceeds the 20 carbons in eicosane, so it is stoichiometrically impossible.

**C** is incorrect as ethane is not a hydrocarbon in high demand for transportation (it is a gas at room temperature, not a liquid fuel). The remaining alkenes in this option are also not consecutive members of the same homologous series.

**D** is incorrect as ethene is not a hydrocarbon in high demand for transportation, and the three alkenes (ethene, pentene, heptene) are not consecutive members of the same homologous series.

### Q9

  - mass of CO<sub>2</sub> = 35.2 g, *M*<sub>r</sub>(CO<sub>2</sub>) = 44

### Q10

- The first four members of the alkane series are methane (CH<sub>4</sub>), ethane (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub>), propane (C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub>) and butane (C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub>).

- Methane:

CH<sub>4</sub> + 2O<sub>2</sub> → CO<sub>2</sub> + 2H<sub>2</sub>O

O<sub>2</sub> consumed = 2 x 10 = 20 cm<sup>3</sup>; CO<sub>2</sub> produced = 10 cm<sup>3</sup>

Residual volume = (70 − 20) + 10 = 60 cm<sup>3</sup>

C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> + $\frac{7}{2}$O<sub>2</sub> → 2CO<sub>2</sub> + 3H<sub>2</sub>O

O<sub>2</sub> consumed = 3.5 x 10 = 35 cm<sup>3</sup>; CO<sub>2</sub> produced = 20 cm<sup>3</sup>

Residual volume = (70 − 35) + 20 = 55 cm<sup>3</sup>

- Propane:

C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub> + 5O<sub>2</sub> → 3CO<sub>2</sub> + 4H<sub>2</sub>O

O<sub>2</sub> consumed = 5 x 10 = 50 cm<sup>3</sup>; CO<sub>2</sub> produced = 30 cm<sup>3</sup>

Residual volume = (70 − 50) + 30 = 50 cm<sup>3</sup>

C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> + $\frac{13}{2}$O<sub>2</sub> → 4CO<sub>2</sub> + 5H<sub>2</sub>O

- The four values (60, 55, 50, 45 cm<sup>3</sup>) decrease linearly by 5 cm<sup>3</sup> per member, as shown in graph **B**.

### Q11
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Cracking an alkane to produce exactly two molecules with the same number of carbon atoms yields one alkane and one alkene.

  - For n = 8, C<sub>8</sub>H<sub>18</sub> cracks to give C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> (butane) and C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub> (butene).

- Both products must have non-cyclic structural isomers:

  - C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>10</sub> has two non-cyclic structural isomers: butane and methylpropane
  - C<sub>4</sub>H<sub>8</sub> has three non-cyclic structural isomers: but-1-ene, but-2-ene, and 2-methylpropene
  - Cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene are E/Z stereoisomers of but-2-ene, not separate structural isomers.
- Both products satisfy the condition, so n = 8.

**A** is incorrect as when n = 4, cracking gives ethane (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub>) and ethene (C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>). Neither 2-carbon compound has any structural isomers.

**B** is incorrect as when n = 6, cracking gives propane (C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub>) and propene (C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>6</sub>). Propane has no structural isomers, and propene's only structural isomer is cyclopropane, which is cyclic. Neither product satisfies the condition.

**D** is incorrect as n = 9 is odd. It is not possible to split an odd number of carbon atoms into two products with the same number of carbons each.
