### Q6
> **[mark-scheme]**
> i) The equation for the reaction between magnesium and steam:
> 
> - Mg (s) + H<sub>2</sub>O (g) → MgO (s) + H<sub>2</sub> (g) **[1 mark]**
> 
> ii) The equation for the reaction between magnesium and sulfuric acid:

> 
> State symbols must be correct in both equations to score the marks

> **[exam-tip]**
> Magnesium reacts with steam (H<sub>2</sub>O (g)) to form magnesium oxide and hydrogen gas. With dilute acids, the general equation is:
> 
> metal + acid → salt + hydrogen.
> 
> Group 2 elements are expected to react with oxygen, water/steam, dilute HCl, and dilute H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The trend in solubility of Group 2 sulfates going down the group:
> 
> - Solubility decreases / the sulfates become less soluble **[1 mark]**
> - Both lattice energy and enthalpy of hydration decrease / become less negative **[1 mark]**
> - The enthalpy of hydration decreases by more than the lattice energy / the decrease in hydration enthalpy is greater than the decrease in lattice energy **[1 mark]**
> - So Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> becomes more endothermic / more positive going down the group **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Although Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> can be positive (endothermic) or negative (exothermic), a compound is more likely to be soluble when Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> is negative or has a small positive value. As Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> becomes more positive going down the group, solubility decreases.
> 
> Note that the trend for Group 2 hydroxides is the opposite: hydroxide solubility increases going down the group because the lattice energy decreases by more than the hydration enthalpy.
To calculate Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> for each compound:

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>hyd</sub>(total) − Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>latt</sub>

- For Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub>:

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = [(−1890) + 2(−550)] − (−2993)

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = −2990 − (−2993)

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = **+3 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup> **[1 mark]**

- For Sr(OH)<sub>2</sub>:

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = [(−1414) + 2(−550)] − (−2467)

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = −2514 − (−2467)

Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = **-47 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup> **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 1 mark for Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub>(Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub>) = +3 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>; allow ±1 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> 1 mark for Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub>(Sr(OH)<sub>2</sub>) = −47 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>; allow ±1 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> 
> In both cases, the hydration enthalpy of OH<sup>-</sup> must be multiplied by 2 as there are two hydroxide ions in M(OH)<sub>2</sub>

> **[exam-tip]**
> The formula for enthalpy change of solution:
> 
> Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = sum of all Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>hyd</sub> values − Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>latt</sub>
> 
> Remember to multiply the hydration enthalpy of OH<sup>-</sup> by 2 as there are two OH<sup>-</sup> ions in each formula unit of M(OH)<sub>2</sub>.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Whether Sr(OH)<sub>2</sub> is more or less soluble than Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub>:
> 
> - Sr(OH)<sub>2</sub> is more soluble (than Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub>) **[1 mark]**
> - Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> for Sr(OH)<sub>2</sub> is negative / exothermic, while Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> for Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub> is positive / endothermic **[1 mark]**
> 
> Allow error carried forward from incorrect values in part (c) provided the conclusion is consistent with the calculated values

> **[exam-tip]**
> A compound is more likely to be soluble when Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> is negative (exothermic). Sr(OH)<sub>2</sub> has Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = −47 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> (negative, more soluble), while Mg(OH)<sub>2</sub> has Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>sol</sub> = +3 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> (positive, less soluble).
> 
> This deduction is consistent with the trend in Group 2 hydroxide solubility increasing down the group.
