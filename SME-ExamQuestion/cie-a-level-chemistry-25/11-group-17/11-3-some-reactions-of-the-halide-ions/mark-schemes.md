### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Down Group 17, the atomic radius increases as extra principal quantum shells are added.
- The outer shell is further from the nucleus and experiences greater shielding from inner electrons.
- An incoming electron therefore experiences less electrostatic attraction from the nucleus.
- The halogens' ability to attract and gain an electron (their oxidising power) decreases down the group.

**B** is incorrect as atomic radius increases down the group, not decreases.

**C** is incorrect as donating an electron describes oxidation, not reduction. This statement describes increasing reducing power, not decreasing oxidising power.

**D** is incorrect as electronegativity decreases down the group.

### Q9
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- Concentrated sulfuric acid can act both as an acid and as an oxidising agent
- When it acts as an acid, it produces the hydrogen halide; all halide salts react in this way
- The reactions of solid sodium fluoride with both acids are acid-base reactions only
- The reaction of solid sodium bromide with concentrated phosphoric acid is also an acid-base reaction only
- When concentrated sulfuric acid reacts with solid sodium bromide:

  - Bromide ions are strong enough reducing agents to reduce H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>
  - Bromide ions are oxidised to bromine:

2HBr (g) + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (l) → Br<sub>2</sub> (g) + SO<sub>2</sub> (g) + 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

- The brown vapour of bromine confirms that concentrated sulfuric acid is a stronger oxidising agent than bromine

**A** is incorrect as concentrated phosphoric acid only acts as an acid in these reactions. It is not an oxidising agent and is therefore a weaker oxidising agent than bromine

**B** is incorrect as concentrated sulfuric acid oxidises bromide ions but concentrated phosphoric acid does not. Concentrated sulfuric acid is therefore the stronger oxidising agent

**C** is incorrect as concentrated sulfuric acid is not a strong enough oxidising agent to oxidise fluoride ions. No redox reaction occurs with sodium fluoride under these conditions

**B** is incorrect as a yellow solid of sulfur is only produced in the reaction with sodium iodide, not sodium bromide. The iodide ion is a stronger reducing agent than bromide.

**C** is incorrect as a yellow solid indicates iodide chemistry, not bromide chemistry.

### Q12
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- Down Group 17, the reducing power of the halide ions increases as atomic radius increases and shielding increases, making it easier for the ion to lose an electron.
- The astatide ion (At<sup>-</sup>) is therefore a very strong reducing agent.
- When solid sodium astatide reacts with hot concentrated sulfuric acid, the astatide ion reduces the H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> and is itself oxidised to form elemental astatine (At<sub>2</sub>).

**A** is incorrect as the solubility of silver halides in ammonia decreases down the group. AgAt will be even less soluble than AgI, which is insoluble in both dilute and concentrated aqueous ammonia.

**C** is incorrect as astatine is a weaker oxidising agent than chlorine. A halogen can only displace a halide ion of a weaker oxidising agent, so no reaction would take place.

**D** is incorrect as astatide ions are strong enough reducing agents to also reduce sulfuric acid, so the reaction produces H<sub>2</sub>S and At<sub>2</sub> alongside other products, not just hydrogen astatide fumes.
