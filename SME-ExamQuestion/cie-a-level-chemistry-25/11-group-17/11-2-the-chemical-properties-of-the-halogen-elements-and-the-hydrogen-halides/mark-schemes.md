### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- A stronger oxidising agent will displace a weaker oxidising agent from its halide solution.

  - Oxidising ability decreases down Group 17 as atomic radius increases and electron shielding increases.

- From the table:

  - Y<sub>2</sub> displaces both X<sup>−</sup> and Z<sup>−</sup>, so Y<sub>2</sub> is the strongest oxidising agent (Cl<sub>2</sub>).

  - X<sub>2</sub> displaces nothing, so X<sub>2</sub> is the weakest oxidising agent (I<sub>2</sub>).

X<sup>−</sup> (I<sup>−</sup>) > Z<sup>−</sup> (Br<sup>−</sup>) > Y<sup>−</sup> (Cl<sup>−</sup>).

### Q5
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Concentrated sulfuric acid can act both as a Brønsted–Lowry acid and as an oxidising agent.
- As an acid, it reacts with all three sodium halides to produce the hydrogen halide gas and sodium hydrogensulfate.

  - This acid–base reaction occurs with all halide ions.
- As an oxidising agent, it is only reduced by halide ions that are strong enough reducing agents.

  - Reducing ability increases down Group 17, as ionic radius increases and electron shielding increases, making it easier to lose the outermost electron.
- Bromide ions (Br<sup>−</sup>) reduce concentrated sulfuric acid to SO<sub>2</sub> while being oxidised to Br<sub>2</sub>.
- Iodide ions (I<sup>−</sup>) are even stronger reducing agents and reduce sulfuric acid further, producing SO<sub>2</sub>, S, and H<sub>2</sub>S.
- Chloride ions (Cl<sup>−</sup>) are too weak as reducing agents.

  - Only the acid–base reaction occurs with NaCl, producing steamy fumes of HCl gas.

**B** is incorrect as chloride ions are not strong enough to reduce concentrated sulfuric acid. Only an acid–base reaction occurs with sodium chloride.

**C** is incorrect for the same reason. Cl<sup>−</sup> does not act as a reducing agent with concentrated sulfuric acid.

**D** is incorrect as bromide ions also reduce concentrated sulfuric acid, producing SO<sub>2</sub>. It is not iodide only.
