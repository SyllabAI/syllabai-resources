### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Calcium oxide (CaO) has the highest melting point (2614 °C) because it forms a **giant ionic lattice** with highly charged ions, Ca<sup>2+</sup> and O<sup>2-</sup>.
- The high charges on these ions, combined with their small ionic radii, result in a large **lattice energy**
- The electrostatic forces of attraction between the ions are very strong and require the most thermal energy to overcome.

**B** is incorrect as lithium forms a **giant metallic lattice**. The singly charged Li<sup>+</sup> ions with one delocalised electron per atom produce relatively weak metallic bonding, giving it a low melting point (181 °C).

**C** is incorrect as sodium fluoride also forms a **giant ionic lattice**, but the Na<sup>+</sup> and F<sup>-</sup> ions carry only +1 and −1 charges respectively. The lower ionic charges result in a smaller lattice energy than CaO, so sodium fluoride has a lower melting point (993 °C).

**D** is incorrect as sulfur forms a **simple molecular** structure (S<sub>8</sub> molecules). Only weak instantaneous dipole-induced dipole forces exist between molecules, requiring very little thermal energy to overcome, giving sulfur a low melting point (113 °C).

### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- All three metal oxides form **giant ionic lattices**.

  - The melting point depends on the amount of thermal energy required to overcome the electrostatic forces of attraction between the oppositely charged ions, which is directly related to the magnitude of the **lattice energy**.

- **MgO > CaO**:

  - Both lattices contain +2 cations and the O<sup>2-</sup> anion.
  - However, the Mg<sup>2+</sup> cation has a smaller ionic radius than Ca<sup>2+</sup>, giving it a higher charge density.
  - This results in a greater lattice energy for MgO, so MgO has the higher melting point.

- **CaO > Na**<sub>**2**</sub>**O**:

  - The Ca<sup>2+</sup> cation carries a +2 charge, whereas the Na<sup>+</sup> cation carries only a +1 charge.
  - The higher ionic charge on Ca<sup>2+</sup> results in a significantly greater lattice energy for CaO compared to Na<sub>2</sub>O, giving CaO the higher melting point.

- The correct decreasing order is therefore MgO > CaO > Na<sub>2</sub>O.

**A**, **C** and **D** are incorrect as they place Na<sub>2</sub>O above one or both of the Group 2 metal oxides. Since Na<sup>+</sup> has a lower charge than Ca<sup>2+</sup> and Mg<sup>2+</sup>, Na<sub>2</sub>O has the smallest lattice energy and therefore the lowest melting point of the three.

### Q8
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- **Contains localised covalent bonds** (✓):

  - The single covalent bond in HF consists of a shared pair of electrons localised between the H nucleus and F nucleus only.
  - There are no delocalised electrons.

- **Highly soluble in polar solvents** (✓):

  - The large difference in electronegativity between H and F makes the bond highly polar (H<sup>δ+</sup>-F<sup>δ-</sup>). HF dissolves readily in polar solvents such as water by forming hydrogen bonds with solvent molecules.

- **Lowest bond energy of the hydrogen halides** (✗):

  - HF actually has the highest bond energy of the hydrogen halides.
  - Fluorine is the smallest halogen, making the H−F bond the shortest and allowing the most effective orbital overlap.
  - Bond energy decreases down the group as the halogen atom increases in size and bond length increases.

- **Highest boiling point of the hydrogen halides** (✓):

  - HF has the highest boiling point of the hydrogen halides because it is the only one capable of forming hydrogen bonds between its molecules.
  - Hydrogen bonding is significantly stronger than the permanent dipole−permanent dipole forces and instantaneous dipole-induced dipole forces present in HCl, HBr and HI.

**A** is incorrect as it states that HF does not contain localised covalent bonds.

**C** is incorrect as it states that the H−F bond has the lowest bond energy of the hydrogen halides.

**D** is incorrect as it states that HF does not have the highest boiling point and is not highly soluble in polar solvents. Both are incorrect.

### Q10
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- **Ammonium sulfate**, (NH<sub>4</sub>)<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>:

  - This consists of polyatomic ammonium cations (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>) and sulfate anions (SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>) held together by electrostatic forces of attraction.
  - Although it contains only non-metal elements, the presence of oppositely charged ions means it forms a **giant ionic lattice**.

- **Potassium iodide**, KI:

  - This consists of K<sup>+</sup> cations and I<sup>-</sup> anions held together by electrostatic forces of attraction.
  - It forms a **giant ionic lattice**.

- **Silicon dioxide**, SiO<sub>2</sub>:

  - This does not contain ions.
  - Each Si atom is covalently bonded to 4 O atoms in a continuous network.
  - It is a **giant covalent** lattice.

**A** and **B** are incorrect as they both identify silicon dioxide as having an ionic lattice. SiO<sub>2</sub> has no ions; it has a giant covalent structure.

**D** is incorrect as ammonium sulfate does form a giant ionic lattice. Ionic compounds are not restricted to metal + non-metal combinations, and polyatomic ions such as NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> and SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> also form ionic lattices.
