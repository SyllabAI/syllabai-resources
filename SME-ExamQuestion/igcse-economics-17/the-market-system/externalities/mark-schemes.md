### 2((g)) — 9 marks
A negative externality is a spillover cost imposed on third parties by production or consumption, and pollution is a clear example because the harm falls on people who neither made nor used the product.** [K]** Kenya's government has responded with regulation, banning single-use plastic bags outright and backing the ban with penalties for anyone who breaks it.** [Ap]** Because firms and shoppers face punishment, the ban directly cuts the quantity of bags in circulation rather than relying on people to change their behaviour voluntarily.** [An]** More than five years on the environment is measurably cleaner, with less visual pollution and fewer livestock dying after eating discarded bags, so the regulation has been reasonably effective at reducing the externality.** [An]**

However, the ban has not removed the problem entirely.** [Ev]** Regulation only works if it is enforced, and monitoring illegal use costs the Kenyan government money and staff time that could have been spent on healthcare or education instead, which is a real opportunity cost.** [An]** Its success also depends on the public cooperating, and plastic bags are only one source of pollution in Kenya, so other causes remain untouched.** [An]** Alternative measures such as fines or taxation might tackle the externality more cheaply, by making polluters pay the external cost themselves rather than requiring the state to police a ban.** [An]**

> - Valid knowledge point → Application → Analysis

> 
> **Mark allocation**

> A 9 mark (Level 3) answer will include examples which demonstrate:
> 
> - **Application [Ap]**: clear knowledge and understanding by developing relevant points. Appropriate application of economic terms, concepts, theories and calculations
> - **Analysis [An]**: information presented will demonstrate excellent selectivity and organisation. Interpretation of economic information will be excellent, with a thorough analysis of issues
> - **Evaluation [Ev]**: offers more than one viewpoint. The argument is well balanced and coherent, leading to an evaluation that demonstrates full understanding and awareness
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Additional information which could be used in the answer includes:
> 
> - Negative externalities are spillover effects on third parties due to consumption or production
> - Pollution is an example of a negative externality because its effects are felt by those not producing or using the plastic bags
> - One method of government intervention to reduce negative externalities is the use of regulation
> - Regulation such as the ban on single-use plastic bags limits the amount of pollution produced and reduces the externality, because there are penalties if it is not followed
> - The ban in Kenya has led to a cleaner environment, as reduced consumption means the visual pollution and danger to livestock is less of a problem
> - The regulation has therefore been quite effective in reducing the negative externality

> - Any other relevant and valid analysis is acceptable

> **[exam-tip]**

## Q4 — easy — 1 marks · exam-questions

### 3((b)) — 1 marks
**Correct answer: A** — Education

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

Educating one person benefits others who did not pay for it, because a better educated workforce raises productivity and the wider economy gains through higher output and tax revenue. Those gains fall on third parties, which makes them an external benefit

**B** is incorrect as air pollution harms third parties rather than benefiting them, which makes it an external cost

**C** is incorrect as overcrowding imposes inconvenience and delay on people who did not cause it, so it is also an external cost

**D** is incorrect as resource depletion leaves fewer resources for future generations, which is a negative effect on third parties rather than a benefit

## Q5 — easy — 1 marks · exam-questions

### 2((b)) — 1 marks
**Correct answer: C** — Private benefits + external benefits

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

Social benefits are the total gains to society from an activity, which means the benefits enjoyed by the people directly involved plus the benefits that spill over to third parties

**A** is incorrect as adding private costs to private benefits mixes two different things together and leaves out third party effects altogether

**B** is incorrect as subtracting private costs from private benefits gives the net private gain, not the benefit to society as a whole

**D** is incorrect as it works only with costs, so it cannot measure benefits at all, and subtracting external effects would remove the third party impact rather than include it
