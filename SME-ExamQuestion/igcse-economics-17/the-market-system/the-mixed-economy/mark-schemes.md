## Q10 — easy — 1 marks · exam-questions

### 2((a)) — 1 marks
**Correct answer: B** — What to produce

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

Because resources are scarce but wants are unlimited, every economy has to answer three fundamental questions: what to produce, how to produce it, and who to produce it for. 'What to produce' is one of those three

**A** is incorrect as when to produce is a timing decision for an individual firm, not one of the three fundamental questions scarcity forces on an economy

**C** is incorrect as whether to produce is not one of the three questions either. Scarcity assumes production takes place; the problem is choosing between competing uses of resources

**D** is incorrect as where to produce is a location decision for a firm, which does not follow from scarcity itself

> - The public sector is the part of the economy owned by the government and operating in the public interest
> - By providing funding for roads in Cambodia, repairs could be made to the more than half of the 6,000km of roads in need of maintenance
> - This could improve the safety standard of roads and lead to fewer accidents, meaning more people are available to work in the Cambodian economy
> - It could also allow construction of new roads able to cope with increased business in Cambodia
> - This would most likely create positive externalities for Cambodian businesses that benefit from cheaper and quicker transportation of goods
> - Business could therefore continue to grow in Cambodia, providing more revenue for the government through taxes
> - However, the cost of improving the roads means less government revenue would be available for other things such as health and education
> - The Cambodian economy may therefore not benefit as much from this investment as it would if the money were invested elsewhere
> - There is a high number of roads of poor quality, so it may take a long time for the economy to benefit from the investment
> - If the economy continues to grow, further road construction may be needed and public sector investment may not keep up with that growth
> - Any other relevant and valid analysis is acceptable

> **[exam-tip]**
> - 'Assess' requires a balanced, two-sided argument which is applied. Examiner reports state that there is no requirement for a judgement or a conclusion, but both sides should be developed, thorough and in context
> - Opportunity cost is the strongest counter-argument here, because every dollar spent on roads is a dollar not spent on health or education. Naming it explicitly earns credit
> - Use the scale in the data. More than half of 6,000km is over 3,000km, and doing that arithmetic shows the examiner you have used the evidence rather than repeated it
