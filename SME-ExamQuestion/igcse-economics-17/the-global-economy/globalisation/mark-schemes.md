### Q1
The Irish government should have done more to prevent this tax avoidance, given the scale of revenue lost.** [K]** The European Court of Justice found that Apple had been paying an effective corporation tax rate as low as 0.005%, resulting in the €13 billion in back taxes now ordered to be repaid.** [Ap]** This represents an enormous amount of government revenue that Ireland was not collecting for years, revenue that could otherwise have funded public services such as healthcare, education and infrastructure.** [An]** This means that ordinary taxpayers effectively bore a greater share of the overall tax burden during this period, while one of the world's most profitable companies paid an almost negligible rate of tax on its profits.** [An]**

However, the Irish government may have been right to be cautious, given the value of the jobs and investment at stake.** [Ev]** Apple's European headquarters in Cork employs over 6,000 people, representing a significant source of direct employment in Ireland.** [Ap]** Had the Irish government imposed stricter, higher tax rules on Apple from the outset, Apple may have chosen to base its European operations in a different country offering more attractive tax arrangements instead, which could have cost Ireland these 6,000-plus jobs and the wider economic activity and tax revenue this investment generates.** [An]** This loss would have occurred immediately rather than being uncertain, as the eventual €13 billion recovery turned out to be.** [An]**

Overall, whether the Irish government should have done more depends on how the value of the eventual recovered tax compares with the value of the jobs and investment that might otherwise have been lost.** [Ev]** In this case, the €13 billion recovered is now very large relative to the tax and wage-related revenue generated by 6,000 employees, suggesting that, with hindsight, the long-run cost of allowing this level of avoidance was substantial.** [Ev]** However, this scale of cost only became clear after a legal process lasting around a decade; at the time the original arrangements were made, the certain, immediate benefit of securing thousands of jobs may reasonably have seemed to outweigh an uncertain, long-term legal risk, which helps explain why the government behaved as it did at the time.** [Ev]**

> - Any other relevant and valid evaluation is acceptable
