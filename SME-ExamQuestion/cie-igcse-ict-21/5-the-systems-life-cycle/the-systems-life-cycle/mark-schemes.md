### Q11
Both questionnaires and interviews are useful methods for researching an existing system, but each has its advantages and disadvantages.

One advantage of questionnaires is that they are **quicker to complete**, allowing multiple people to respond at the same time. Additionally, questionnaires can be **completed in the respondent’s own time**, providing flexibility compared to interviews, which must be scheduled. Another key benefit is that responses can **remain anonymous**, making people more likely to give honest feedback.

However, questionnaires are** inflexible**, as they only allow for fixed responses, whereas interviews can be adjusted based on previous answers. Another drawback is that **respondents may not return questionnaires**, leading to a low response rate. Additionally, if a question is unclear, there is no way for the respondent to ask for clarification, which could result in misleading or inaccurate answers.

On the other hand, interviews allow for deeper discussions, as the interviewer can ask follow-up questions and adapt them based on previous responses. This makes them more flexible and allows for more detailed insights. However, interviews are **more expensive**, as businesses must pay for an interviewer and compensate employees for their time. Additionally, interviews are not anonymous, which may lead some respondents to withhold honest opinions.

Overall, questionnaires are more efficient for collecting large amounts of data, while interviews provide more detailed, flexible responses. The choice of method depends on the time, budget, and depth of information required.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Response includes at least three advantages and three disadvantages, ensuring a balanced evaluation.
> 
> - Questionnaires are quicker to complete **[1 mark]**
> - Questionnaires can be completed in the respondent’s own time **[1 mark]**
> - Questionnaires allow for anonymous responses **[1 mark]**
> - Questionnaires are inflexible, whereas interviews allow follow-up questions **[1 mark]**
> - Respondents may not return questionnaires **[1 mark]**
> - Interviews are more expensive due to staffing costs **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Five **top tips for answering this 6-mark "evaluate" question effectively:
> 
> 1. **Compare both methods equally**
> 
>   1. To get full marks, ensure you discuss three points for each method rather than focusing too much on one.
> 2. **Explain how interviews provide deeper insights**
> 
>   1. Example: Instead of just saying "interviews allow more detail," write "interviews allow follow-up questions, helping to explore answers in greater depth."
> 3. **Highlight why questionnaires are faster but less flexible**
> 
>   1. Mention that questionnaires allow responses from many people at once, but lack adaptability when answers need further explanation.
> 4. **Discuss time, cost, and accuracy factors**
> 
>   1. Example: "Interviews are expensive because the interviewer must be paid, whereas questionnaires can be distributed at a lower cost."
> 5. **Avoid vague statements**
> 
>   1. Instead of "people don’t always answer questionnaires," say "people may not return questionnaires, leading to a lower response rate."

### Q12
Analysing a system by observing staff instead of examining documents has several benefits. One advantage is that the **analyst sees the system in action**, allowing them to understand how staff interact with it in real time. Additionally, observation **provides a complete picture** of the system, including any inefficiencies that might not be recorded in documents. Another benefit is that it is less disruptive to workers, as they can continue their tasks while the analyst observes, **making it a cheaper method of analysis**.

However, observation also has drawbacks. The **quality and accuracy of the findings depend on the skill of the observer**, meaning mistakes or biases could affect the results. Another issue is the Hawthorne effect, where staff may change their behaviour when they know they are being watched, leading to inaccurate data.

On the other hand, examining existing documentation has advantages, such as **allowing the analyst to see exact details of inputs, processing, and outputs**, which can be more precise than observation. Additionally, previous system analysis reports may already exist, saving time in the research process. However, looking through documents is **time-consuming**, and hiring an analyst to examine records can be expensive.

Overall, while observation provides real-world insight into system usage, examining documents offers structured and precise information, though it requires more time and cost.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The response includes at least three advantages and three disadvantages, ensuring a balanced discussion.
> 
> - The analyst sees the system in action **[1 mark]**
> - Observation provides a complete picture **[1 mark]**
> - Cheaper as it does not take the worker away from work **[1 mark]**
> - The quality and accuracy of information is highly dependent on the skill of the observer **[1 mark]**
> - Examining documents shows exact details of inputs, processing, and outputs **[1 mark]**
> - Very time-consuming to look through all the documents **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Five **top tips for answering this 6-mark "discuss" question effectively:
> 
> 1. **Include at least three points for both observation and examining documents**
> 
>   1. A balanced response is necessary to get full marks.
> 2. **Explain why observation is useful but not always reliable**
> 
>   1. Example: Instead of just saying "observation provides a complete picture," write "observation helps analysts see real-world system usage, but staff may alter their behaviour due to the Hawthorne effect."
> 3. **Highlight why examining documents can be more precise**
> 
>   1. Mention that documents provide exact details about inputs, processing, and outputs, which may not be as clear from observation.
> 4. **Consider time and cost factors**
> 
>   1. Example: "Examining documents can be time-consuming and expensive, whereas observation is cheaper as it does not disrupt staff."
> 5. **Avoid vague points**
> 
>   1. Instead of "documents take a long time," explain "analysing large amounts of paperwork is slow and costly, as it requires hiring an experienced analyst."
