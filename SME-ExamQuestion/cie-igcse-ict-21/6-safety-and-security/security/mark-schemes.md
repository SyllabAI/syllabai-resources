### Q9
There are several methods to increase the security of data on a computer system, each with varying levels of effectiveness.

One effective method is using **strong passwords**, as they are harder to crack than simple passwords. Additionally, **biometric authentication is even more secure** since it relies on unique human characteristics, making it nearly impossible to duplicate. **Two-factor authentication (2FA) **further improves security by requiring an extra layer of verification, such as a code sent to a mobile device. **Firewalls** also play a key role by **blocking unauthorised access** and preventing fraudulent websites from attacking the computer. Lastly, anti-spyware software helps prevent keyloggers from capturing passwords, reducing the risk of credential theft.

However, security methods also have limitations. Overly** complex passwords can be easily forgotten**, leading users to write them down or reset them frequently. Additionally, r**egularly changing passwords can make them easier to guess** if users follow predictable patterns. **Firewalls**, while essential for security, **can sometimes block legitimate software and may need to be temporarily disabled**, making the system vulnerable during updates. Lastly, **hackers can still breach even strong passwords**, particularly if a user reuses them across multiple accounts.

Overall, while strong passwords, biometric authentication, firewalls, and 2FA improve security, they also introduce challenges related to usability and system performance. A balanced approach that includes multiple security layers is the most effective way to protect sensitive data.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> You must include at least four advantages and four disadvantages, ensuring a balanced discussion
> 
> - Strong passwords are difficult to crack **[1 mark]**
> - Biometric passwords are harder to crack **[1 mark]**
> - Two-factor authentication adds extra security **[1 mark]**
> - Firewalls prevent unauthorised access **[1 mark]**
> - Too complex passwords can be easily forgotten **[1 mark]**
> - Regularly changing passwords makes them easier to guess **[1 mark]**
> - Firewalls may block legitimate software and require disabling for updates **[1 mark]**
> - Hackers can still breach strong passwords **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Five **top tips for answering this 8-mark "discuss" question effectively:
> 
> 1. **Include both advantages and disadvantages**
> 
>   1. To achieve full marks, discuss at least four points from each side.
> 2. **Explain why certain methods improve security**
> 
>   1. Example: Instead of just saying "2FA increases security," write "2FA adds an extra layer of protection by requiring a second form of authentication, making it harder for hackers to gain access."
> 3. **Acknowledge the limitations of security measures**
> 
>   1. Mention real-world issues like "firewalls blocking legitimate software" and "passwords being difficult to remember if changed too often."
> 4. **Compare effectiveness vs. practicality**
> 
>   1. Example: "While biometric authentication is highly secure, it requires expensive hardware and may not always work reliably."
> 5. **Avoid vague points**
> 
>   1. Instead of saying "passwords are important," explain how "longer, complex passwords are harder to guess but can also be difficult to remember."
