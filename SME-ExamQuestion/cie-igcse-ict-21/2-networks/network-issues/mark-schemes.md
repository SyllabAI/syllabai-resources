### Q10
One disadvantage of using video-conferencing for business meetings is that it **requires a fast and reliable internet connection**. If the connection is weak, **documents and images may take longer to load**, or participants may **experience lag and audio delays**, making communication difficult.

Another issue is that** time zone differences can make scheduling difficult**, especially when **employees are in different parts of the world**. This can lead to meetings being held at inconvenient times for some participants, reducing productivity.

Additionally, video-conferencing may **require specialist hardware and software**, which **can be expensive for companies to purchase and maintain**. Businesses may also need to invest in training staff to use the system effectively, which takes time and resources.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Three **matched **pairs:
> 
> - Requires a good/fast/reliable internet connection → documents/images may require more bandwidth **[2 marks]**
> - Time zone differences → staff work in other parts of the world/time of meeting may be difficult **[2 marks]**
> - Requires specialist hardware/software → can be expensive to purchase **[2 marks]**
> 
> **[Total marks: 6/6]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Five **top tips for answering this 6-mark "describe" question effectively:
> 
> 1. **Use a clear structure with matched pairs**
> 
>   1. Each disadvantage should be followed by a reason/explanation.
>   2. Example: "Time zone differences can make scheduling difficult, as staff work in different parts of the world."
> 2. **Avoid vague or one-word answers**
> 
>   1. Instead of just saying "internet issues", explain "a slow or unstable internet connection can cause delays, affecting communication."
> 3. **Use business-related terminology where possible**
> 
>   1. Terms like "bandwidth," "productivity," "specialist hardware," and "real-time communication" demonstrate a stronger understanding of the topic.
> 4. **Prioritise practical challenges businesses face**
> 
>   1. Think about real-world workplace issues, such as team collaboration, costs, and technical difficulties.
> 5. **Check that each point is unique**
> 
>   1. Avoid writing two points that overlap too much (e.g., "internet connection issues" and "disconnection issues" could be too similar).

### Q11
The student’s password Px2zY!Cg3?bd is **strong and secure** because it is long and contains a mix of uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters, making it **harder to crack** using brute force attacks. Additionally, it **does not contain personal information, which reduces the risk of someone guessing it**. The password’s** length also makes shoulder surfing more difficult**, as it is harder for someone nearby to memorise.

However, the **complexity of the password makes it difficult to remember,** which could lead to the **user writing it down, increasing the risk of unauthorised access**. The **mix of letters, numbers, and symbols may also cause confusion**, such as mistaking ‘2’ for ‘Z’ or ‘1’ for ‘I’, making it **easier to mistype**. Additionally, the user may need to look at the keyboard while typing, slowing down the login process.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> To gain full marks you **must **have correct answers for both benefits and drawbacks
> 
> - Strong password that is hard to crack **[1 mark]**
> - More secure as it does not contain personal information **[1 mark]**
> - Longer password reduces the risk of shoulder surfing **[1 mark]**
> - Difficult to remember due to complexity **[1 mark]**
> - May need to be written down, increasing security risks **[1 mark]**
> - Letters and numbers may be confused, leading to typing errors **[1 mark]**
> 
> **[Total marks: 6/6]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Five **top tips for answering this 6-mark "Discuss" question effectively:
> 
> 1. **Include both benefits and drawbacks**
> 
>   1. The mark scheme requires both positive and negative aspects, so make sure to balance your answer.
> 2. **Be specific about security advantages**
> 
>   1. Instead of saying "it is secure," explain why: "It includes a mix of characters, making brute force attacks more difficult."
> 3. **Consider real-world usability issues**
> 
>   1. Mention practical problems, such as difficulty remembering the password or the need to look at the keyboard while typing.
> 4. **Use comparative phrases to strengthen the discussion**
> 
>   1. Example: "Although the password is strong, its complexity may lead to the user writing it down, reducing security."
>   2. This links the benefits and drawbacks, showing a balanced discussion.
> 5. **Avoid vague points and repetition**
> 
>   1. Instead of "it is hard to remember," specify why: "Its combination of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols makes it harder to memorise."
>   2. Cover distinct issues instead of repeating the same idea in different words.
