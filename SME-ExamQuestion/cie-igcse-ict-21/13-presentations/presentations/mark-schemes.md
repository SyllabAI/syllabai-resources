### Q2
Using a **multimedia presentation** instead of printed leaflets has several advantages for a sales representative. One key benefit is that **multimedia allows for the use of sound, video, animations, and transitions**, making the presentation more engaging and helping to capture the audience’s attention. It can also be **more interactive**, with the use of hyperlinks or clickable elements, allowing it to be shared online or used on an interactive whiteboard. Another advantage is that **there are no printing costs**, and the presentation is **easier to update**, especially if product information changes. Multimedia presentations can also be **more accessible**, for example, by using text-to-speech features for visually impaired users.

However, there are also disadvantages. **Special equipment is needed**, such as a computer, projector, or speakers, which may not always be available. **Technical issues** like power cuts, equipment failure, or corrupted files could disrupt the presentation. Additionally, **some users may focus more on the visuals and animations rather than the product content**. Unlike leaflets, **audience members need to be present** to see the presentation, whereas a leaflet **can be read at any time** and taken away for future reference. Furthermore, **presentations cannot be produced in braille**, making printed leaflets more suitable for certain accessibility needs.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Response includes at least four advantages and four disadvantages, ensuring a balanced discussion
> 
> - Allows the use of sound/animation/video/transitions **[1 mark]**
> - Can be more interactive (used on website/whiteboard) **[1 mark]**
> - No printing costs **[1 mark]**
> - Easier to update **[1 mark]**
> - More accessible (speak text, etc.) **[1 mark]**
> - Special equipment is needed **[1 mark]**
> - Power/equipment failure/corruption can be an issue **[1 mark]**
> - Presentations cannot be written in braille **[1 mark]**
> 
> **[Total marks: 8/8]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Five **top tips for answering this 8-mark "discuss" question effectively:
> 
> 1. **Include both advantages and disadvantages**
> 
>   1. To get full marks, include at least four clear points from each side.
> 2. **Be specific about multimedia benefits**
> 
>   1. Example: Instead of saying "presentations are better," write "multimedia presentations can include sound and animation, making the information more engaging."
> 3. **Acknowledge practical limitations**
> 
>   1. Mention technical issues, accessibility, and the need for equipment, which are real concerns for presentations.
> 4. **Compare flexibility and usability**
> 
>   1. Example: "Leaflets can be kept and read later, whereas presentations usually require the audience to be present in the room."
> 5. **Avoid vague statements**
> 
>   1. Rather than saying "it might not work," be clear: "corrupted software or power failure can prevent the presentation from being delivered at all."
