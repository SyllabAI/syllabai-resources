### Q2
Policing social networking sites has several benefits. One key advantage is that it can **reduce cyberbullying and hate speech**, making online platforms safer for users. Additionally, policing can **prevent social media from being used for illegal activities**, such as fraud or illegal sales. Another benefit is that it can **reduce the ability to incite violence and trolling**, helping to prevent harm caused by online conflicts. Finally, it could **prevent users from being subjected to inappropriate messages**, such as harassment or offensive content.

However, there are also drawbacks to policing social networks. One major issue is that **freedom of speech is reduced**, as platforms may remove content based on their own policies, potentially leading to bias. Another problem is that **policing would be very difficult to enforce, as social media generates a massive amount of content every second**. Additionally, **privacy concerns arise**, as monitoring content could involve reading private messages, making users feel like they are being watched. Finally, **different countries have different rules**, meaning that what is considered acceptable in one country may not be in another, creating inconsistencies in enforcement.

Overall, while policing social networks can create a safer online space, it also introduces challenges related to privacy, enforcement, and freedom of speech.

> Response includes at least four benefits and four drawbacks, ensuring a balanced discussion.
> 
> - Can reduce cyberbullying/hate speech **[1 mark]**
> - Can reduce social media from being used for illegal activities **[1 mark]**
> - Can reduce the ability to incite violence/trolling **[1 mark]**
> - Could prevent people from being subjected to inappropriate messages **[1 mark]**
> - Freedom of speech is reduced **[1 mark]**
> - Very difficult to police due to the large amount of information **[1 mark]**
> - Privacy issues as people checking could read messages **[1 mark]**
> - Different rules in different countries and social media crosses borders **[1 mark]**
> 
> **[Total marks: 8/8]**

> **[exam-tip]**

> 
> 1. **Include both benefits and drawbacks**
> 
>   1. To achieve full marks, discuss at least four points from each category.
> 2. **Explain why policing social networks improves safety**
> 
>   1. Example: Instead of just saying "it stops cyberbullying," write "policing can reduce cyberbullying by removing harmful posts and preventing online harassment."
> 3. **Discuss enforcement challenges**
> 
>   1. Mention that "due to the massive amount of content posted every second, it would be extremely difficult to monitor everything effectively."
> 4. **Compare privacy concerns with security benefits**
> 
>   1. Example: "While policing can prevent illegal activities, it also raises privacy issues, as monitoring could involve reading personal messages."
> 5. **Avoid vague or repetitive points**
> 
>   1. Instead of "it would be hard to police," explain "enforcing rules would be difficult because different countries have different laws on what content is acceptable."

### Q3
Policing the internet has several disadvantages. One major concern is that it **removes freedom of speech**, as governments or organisations may decide what content is allowed, potentially leading to biased restrictions. Additionally, the** internet is international, making it difficult to police effectively**, as laws vary between countries and enforcement would be inconsistent.

Another issue is that **criminal activity may shift to the dark net**, **where it is even harder to track**. Similarly, **electronic crimes could move away from the internet, making them more difficult for law enforcement to intercept**. Furthermore, internet users can remain anonymous, meaning tracking illegal activity is challenging and often ineffective.

Policing the internet also raises privacy concerns, as constant monitoring could make users feel like they are always being watched. Additionally, **the cost of security would increase**, requiring significant investment in surveillance and enforcement.

Overall, while policing the internet aims to improve security, it presents significant challenges related to freedom, enforcement, cost, and privacy.
