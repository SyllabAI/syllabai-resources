## Q1 — easy — 1 marks · exam-questions

### 1((a)) — 1 marks
**Correct answer: B** — Force

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is ****B**** because:**

- A vector quantity is any that has a **magnitude** (size) and **direction**

  - This means that a force of 10 N and –10 N are in opposite directions
- Only force can be represented with both a magnitude and direction

One way to know whether a quantity is scalar or vector is thinking about the equations that are associated with it. For example:

- Density = $\frac{mass}{volume}$ Since mass and volume are both scalar, density is also a scalar
- Mass is scalar, since you cannot have 'negative' mass
- Speed is the scalar version of velocity
- Force = mass × acceleration. Since acceleration is a vector, and mass is a scalar, force must therefore also be a vector.

In summary:

- vector × scalar = vector
- scalar × scalar = scalar

One way to know whether a quantity is scalar or vector is thinking about the equations that are associated with it. For example:

- Momentum = mass × velocity. Since velocity is a vector, and mass is a scalar, momentum must therefore also be a vector.
- Acceleration = $\frac{velocity}{time}$. Since velocity is a vector, and time is a scalar, acceleration must therefore also be a vector.
- Velocity = $\frac{displacement}{time}$. Since displacement is vector, and time is a scalar, velocity must therefore also be a vector.
- Energy = power × time. Power and time are both scalars, so energy must also be a scalar.

In summary:

- vector × scalar = vector
- scalar × scalar = scalar

**Correct answer: D** — the vertical forces on it are balanced

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is ****D**** because:**

- An object falling at terminal velocity is falling at **constant** **velocity**
- This means that it has **no acceleration**

  - This is because acceleration is defined as the rate of change in velocity
  - If velocity doesn't change (i.e. it is constant) then acceleration is 0
- Newton's second law states that:

  - *F* = *ma*
  - Where *F* is the resultant force, *m* is the mass and *a* is the acceleration
- Therefore, an object that has no acceleration also has **no** **resultant force**
- An object falling vertically downwards has no forces in the horizontal direction, only in the vertical

  - If the object has no resultant force, this means the vertical forces on it are **balanced**
