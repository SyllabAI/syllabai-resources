### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- The cash book is both a ledger and a book of prime entry, so consider the three statements carefully
- Statement 1

  - The cash book is a ledger account for both the cash and bank accounts
  - So statement 1 is correct
- Statement 2

  - The cash book is **not** a ledger account for discounts allowed and received
  
    - Entries are made in the cash book for discounts allowed and received
    - But the ledger accounts for those discounts are in the nominal (general) ledger
  - So statement 2 is incorrect
- Statement 3

  - When serving as a book of prime entry, the cash book does record transactions before they are recorded in the ledgers
  
    - For example discounts allowed and received are recorded there before the amounts are transferred to the ledger for those accounts
  - So statement 3 is correct
- Therefore option C is the correct answer

**A** is incorrect; statement 3 is also correct

**B** and **D** are incorrect; the cash book does *not* contain ledger accounts for discounts allowed and discounts received

### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- First consider what the cash book entry represents

  - Tae is a credit supplier
  - So the \$152 credit entry in the bank column represents the payment made to Tae to settle the account
  
    - It is a credit because the payment means that the bank (an asset) is decreasing
  - The \$8 credit entry in the discount column represents a cash discount that Hwan received from Tae
  
    - It is a credit because it needs to balance the debit that will appear in Tae's account
- Now consider what needs to happen in Tae's account

  - The \$152 payment and \$8 discount both reduce the amount that Hwan owes to Tae
  
    - Tae's account is a liability
    - So amounts that reduce the liability need to be entered as debits
  - The two amounts need to be entered separately, however
  
    - A \$152 bank debit entry balances the \$152 bank credit entry in the cash book
    - An \$8 discount received debit entry balances the \$8 discount credit entry in the cash book

**A** is incorrect; the entry for the discount received has not been made in Tae's account

**B** is incorrect; the \$160 has been made as a single bank entry, rather than split into bank and discount received amounts

**C** is incorrect; the discount received has instead been entered as a discount allowed

### Q4
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- The petty cash book is used to record transactions involving small amounts of cash

  - The business will decide what they classify as small amounts of cash
  - \$100 or \$75 are common thresholds for petty cash transactions

**A** is incorrect; only cash transactions below a certain amount are recorded in the petty cash book

**B** is incorrect; only small *cash* transactions are recorded in the petty cash book (plus it is the amount of the *transaction* that matters, not the cost of individual items)

**C** is incorrect; cash purchases and expenses may be recorded in the petty cash book, but only for transactions that are below a certain amount

### Q5
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Focus on the effects that the transactions mentioned in the question have on the cash  and petty cash accounts
- The chief cashier took \$90 from the cash account to put into the petty cash account

  - The amount in petty cash has increased by \$90 + \$15 = \$105
  
    - Petty cash is an asset
    - So that increase should be recorded as a debit
    - This already tells you that C is the correct answer!
  - The amount in the cash account has decreased by \$90
  
    - The cash account is an asset
    - So that decrease should be recorded as a credit
    - This confirms that C is the correct answer

**A** and **B** are incorrect; both transactions increased the amount in petty cash, so the full amount (\$90 + \$15 = \$105) should be debited to the petty cash account

**D** is incorrect; debits and credits are the wrong way round

### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- The cash book includes columns specifically for discounts (discount allowed on the debit side, and discount received on the credit side)

  - So cash discounts received are recorded in the cash book
- Cash discounts also increase profit for the year

  - When calculating the profit for the year on the income statement
  
    - discounts received count as 'other income'
  - Profit for the year is
  
    - gross profit + other income - other expenses
  - So increasing other income increases profit for the year

**A**, **B** and **C** are incorrect; both statements in the table are true for cash discounts
