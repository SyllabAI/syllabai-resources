### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- The invoices sent by Eira to Gwilym are all amounts that Gwilym owes to Eira

  - \$205 + \$120 + \$170 = \$495
- The amount owed will be reduced by two things

  - the credit note sent by Eira to Gwilym (which is issued by a supplier to a customer when an invoice is reduced)
  - the cheque sent by Gwilym to Eira (which is a payment by Gwilym against amounts owed to Eira)
  - \$72 + \$198 = \$270
- The debit note sent by Gwilym to Eira does not change the amount owed

  - A credit customer issues a debit note to a supplier to *request* a reduction in the balance of an invoice

- So at the start of November, the amount owed to Eira by Gwilym is

  - \$495 - \$270 = \$225
- An amount owed to Eira is a liability for Gwilym's business

  - So this balance will appear as a credit on Eira's account in Gwilym's books on 1 November

**A** and **B** are incorrect; \$145 is the amount owed by Gwilym to Eira that you would get if you also subtracted the amount of the debit note (\$80) from the invoices

**D** is incorrect; the amount (\$225) is correct, but an amount owed by Gwilym to Eira should be a credit in Eira's account in Gwilym's books, not a debit

### Q9
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- A trade discount is a reduction in the selling price of goods or services, and is applied before a transaction takes place

  - Therefore on 1 November the amount credited will be the list price minus the trade discount
  - \$500 - \$50 = \$450
- If Inaya pays by the cash discount deadline, then the discount will be recorded at that time in the cash book as a separate entry

  - A cash discount is  not recorded on the same date as part of the same entry for the original credit purchase

**A** is incorrect; this is what you would get if you subtracted the trade and cash discounts from the list price

**C** is incorrect; this is what you would get if you subtracted the cash discount (instead of the trade discount) from the list price

**D** is incorrect; this is simply the list price, without any discount applied

### Q11
