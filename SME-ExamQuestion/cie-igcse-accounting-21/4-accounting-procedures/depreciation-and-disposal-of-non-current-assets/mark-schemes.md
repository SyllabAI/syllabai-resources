### Q11
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- By the accounting principle of matching, capital expenditure for non-current assets should be matched against the income that it has contributed to

  - The full cost of the equipment should not be charged as an expense straightaway
  - Instead, the expense should be spread out over the useful life of the equipment

**A** is incorrect; considering depreciation may allow a business owner to plan ahead for the replacement of non-current assets, but this is not the reason why depreciation is charged

**B** is incorrect; a depreciated value may allow a business owner to set an appropriate resale value for non-current assets, but calculating a depreciated value is part of the *process* of charging depreciation, it is not the reason *why* depreciation is charged

### Q12
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- With straight-line depreciation the same amount of depreciation is charged each year

  - With reducing balance depreciation, the percentage is only applied to the *remaining* balance (i.e. the net book value)
  
    - So each year less depreciation is charged than for the preceding year
  - If the percentages and initial costs are the same, a straight-line depreciation asset will fully depreciate faster than a reducing balance depreciation asset
  - So 'asset X will be fully depreciated before asset Y' is false
- Assets X and Y had the same cost, and the depreciation percentage is the same for each

  - So in the **first year** that depreciation is charged (2024), the depreciation charge will be **the same** for both of them
  
    - \$5000 ✕ 20% = \$1000
  - (In **later years**, the reducing balance depreciation charge for asset X will be **lower** than for asset Y
  
    - \$800 in the second year, \$640 in the third year, etc.)
  - So 'in 2024 the depreciation charge is lower for asset X than for asset Y' is false

**B** is incorrect; 'in 2024 the depreciation charge is lower for asset X than for asset Y' is false

**C** is incorrect; 'asset X will be fully depreciated before asset Y' is false

**D** is incorrect; both statements are false
