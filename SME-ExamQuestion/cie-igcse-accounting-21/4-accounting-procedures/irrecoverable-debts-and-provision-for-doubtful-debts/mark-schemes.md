### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- On 31 December 2023 the provision for doubtful debts should have been lowered to \$4100

  - Instead it was kept at \$5400
  - That means that the provision on 31 December 2023 was too high by
  
    - \$5400 - \$4100 = \$1300
- Consider the effect of that on the profit for the year

  - Decreasing the provision increases the profit
  - Therefore, overstating the provision for doubtful debts will understate the profit for the year
- Now consider the effect on retained earnings

  - Retained earnings = opening balance + profit for the year - dividends paid - transfer to general reserve
  - So understating the profit for the year will also understate the retained earnings
  
    - Because you are adding less to the opening balance

**A** is incorrect; the amount is correct, but the retained earnings were understated not overstated

**B** and **D** are incorrect; \$4100 is the correct provision amount for 31 December 2023, but it is not the amount by which the retained earnings were affected by the error

### Q3

- You can figure the change in the provision for doubtful debts directly

  - Trade receivables owed \$4000 less on 31 March 2024 than they did on 31 March 2023
  - That means the provision for doubtful debts should decrease by 6% of \$4000
  
    - \$4000 ✕ 6% = \$240 decrease
- You could also figure that out 'the long way'

  - \$648 is 6% of the trade receivables balance for 1 April 2023
  - So the trade receivables balance on that date was
  
    - \$648 ÷ 6% = \$10 800

    - \$10 800 - \$4000 = \$6800
  - And 6% of that balance is
  
    - \$6800 ✕ 6% = \$408
  - Which is a decrease of
  
    - \$648 - \$408 = \$240
- Next figure out how a \$240 decrease in provision for doubtful debts would affect the profit for the year

  - Decreasing the provision increases the profit because fewer funds are being set aside to cover doubtful debts
  - So decreasing the provision for doubtful debts will increase the profit for the year

**A** is incorrect; this is the correct amount, but it is identified as a decrease instead of an increase

**C** and **D** are incorrect; \$408 is the new balance on the provision for doubtful debts account, but this is not the amount by which the profit for the year changes

### Q7
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- An irrecoverable debt occurs when a business is unable to receive payment from a credit customer for the amount they owe

  - The relevant trade receivables account is credited in the sales ledger
  
    - The amount they owe is decreasing
    - So that asset is worth less to the company
  - The irrecoverable debts account is debited in the nominal ledger

**A** is incorrect; failing to pay by a particular date does not necessarily mean that a debt is irrecoverable

**B** is incorrect; a recovered debt that had been written off would never be recorded by a credit entry in the trade payable account

**D** is incorrect; irrecoverable debt has to do with a business and its customers, not a business and its suppliers
