### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- Consider the effects of the different errors
- Treating capital receipts as revenue receipts means

  - The receipts will incorrectly appear as income on the income statement
  
    - The income will therefore be overstated
    - This means the profit for the year will be overstated
  - So the first error overstates the profit for the year by \$2000
- Incorrectly treating revenue expenditure as capital expenditure means

  - The expenditure will not appear on the income statement
  
    - The expenses will therefore be understated
    - This means the profit for the year will be overstated
  - So the second error overstates the profit for the year by \$700
- Treating revenue receipts as capital receipts means

  - The receipts will not appear on the income statement
  
    - The income will therefore be understated
    - This means the profit for the year will be understated
  - So the third error understates the profit for the year by \$1800
- Two of the errors cause the profit for the year to be overstated

  - \$2000 + \$700 = \$2700
- Subtract the effect of the third error, which causes the profit for the year to be understated

  - \$2700 - \$1800 = \$900
- The overall effect is an overstatement by \$900

**A** is incorrect; you would get this answer if you had the effects of errors 1 and 3 the wrong way round

**C** is incorrect; you would get this answer if you had the effect of error 2 the wrong way round

**D** is incorrect; you would get this answer if you had the effects of all three errors the wrong way round
