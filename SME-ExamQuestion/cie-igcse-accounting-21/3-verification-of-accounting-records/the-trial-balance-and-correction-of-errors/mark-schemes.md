### Q3
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- An error of complete reversal occurs when a transaction is entered into the wrong sides of both of the correct ledger accounts

  - The account that was debited should have been credited
  - The account that was credited should have been debited
- Here the rent paid should have been credited to the cash book

  - Paying an expense reduces money (an asset)
  
    - And transactions that reduce an asset are entered as credit transactions
  - So the entry was in the right account, but on the wrong side
- It should also have been debited to the rent payable account

  - Paying an expense reduces the amount owed (a liability)
  
    - And transactions that reduce a liability are entered as debit transactions
  - So this entry was also in the right account, but on the wrong side
- So the error is an error of complete reversal

**A** is incorrect; an error of commission occurs when one entry for a transaction is entered into an incorrect account but the type of account is correct

**D** is incorrect; an error of principle occurs when one entry for a transaction is entered into an incorrect account and the type of account is also incorrect

### Q11
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- First consider the effect of closing inventory on gross profit

  - Gross profit = net revenue - cost of sales
  
    - So anything that overstates cost of sales will cause gross profit to be understated
    - Because you are taking more away from net revenue
  - Cost of sales = opening inventory + net purchases - closing inventory
  
    - So anything that understates closing inventory will cause  cost of sales to be overstated
    - Because you are taking less away from opening inventory + net purchases
  - Therefore closing inventory must have been understated by \$850
- Now consider the profit for the year

  - Profit for the year = gross profit + other incomes - other expenses
  
    - So gross profit being understated by \$850 would cause profit for the year to be understated by \$850
  - But profit for the year was *over*stated by \$100
  
    - This means that the second mistake had to cause profit for the year to be overstated by
    
      - \$850, to take profit for the year back to the correct value
      - plus another \$100, to cause profit for the year to be overstated by \$100
  - So the total effect of the second mistake was to overstate profit for the year by \$850 + \$100 = \$950
- At this point you already know the correct answer must be 'D'
- But to check, you can consider the effect of depreciation charges on profit for the year

  - Depreciation charges are included under 'other expenses' when calculating profit for the year
  - Profit for the year = gross profit + other incomes - other expenses
  
    - So anything that understates other expenses will cause  profit for the year to be overstated
    - Because you are taking less away from gross profit + other incomes
  - So depreciation charges must have been understated by \$950
