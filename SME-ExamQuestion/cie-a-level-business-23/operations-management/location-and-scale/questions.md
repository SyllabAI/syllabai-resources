### Q1 — hard — 12 marks

**Q1** [12 marks]

> **[case-study]**
> **Fenwick Growers**
> 
> Fenwick Growers operates greenhouses in country R, growing tomatoes for supermarkets and wholesalers. A large supermarket chain has offered it a three-year contract requiring roughly triple its current volume.
> 
> **Table 1: Fenwick Growers, current and after proposed expansion**
> 
> | &nbsp; | **Current** | **After expansion** |
> |---|---|---|
> | Greenhouse area (hectares) | 12 | 38 |
> | Annual output (tonnes) | 4 200 | 13 400 |
> | Employees | 55 | 160 |
> | Average cost per tonne (\$) | 610 | 540 (forecast) |
> | Number of customers | 9 | 4 |
> 
> The expansion would cost \$14m, financed by a bank loan. The supermarket contract would account for 68% of Fenwick Growers' output and runs for three years, after which it is subject to renegotiation.
> 
> The Managing Director, Ines Duarte, points to the forecast fall in average cost per tonne from \$610 to \$540 and to the security of guaranteed volume.
> 
> The Operations Director, Kwame Boateng, is worried. Fenwick Growers would go from nine customers to four, and the loan repayments would continue for far longer than three years.

Evaluate whether Fenwick Growers should expand its scale of operations to take the supermarket contract.

### Q2 — hard — 12 marks

**Q2** [12 marks]

> **[case-study]**
> **Ockley**
> 
> Ockley manufactures laboratory equipment in country B and needs a second production site. Two options are under consideration: a site 60 km from its existing factory in country B, or a site in country N.
> 
> **Table 1: Comparison of the two sites**
> 
> | &nbsp; | **Country B site** | **Country N site** |
> |---|---|---|
> | Construction and set-up cost (\$m) | 8.4 | 5.1 |
> | Average annual wage per employee (\$) | 34 000 | 11 500 |
> | Distance from Ockley's main factory (km) | 60 | 2 100 |
> | Corporation tax rate (%) | 24 | 12 |
> | Time to obtain regulatory approval for medical-grade equipment (months) | 4 | 19 |
> 
> Ockley's equipment must be certified before it can be sold in its main markets. Around 70% of its customers are in country B and neighbouring countries.
> 
> The Finance Director, Marta Olsen, favours country N. Set-up costs are \$3.3m lower, wages are around a third, and the corporation tax rate is half.
> 
> The Technical Director, Sunil Raman, disagrees. Certification in country N would take 19 months against four, and engineers from the main factory can reach the country B site within an hour to resolve production problems.
> 
> A decision is needed within two months.

Evaluate whether Ockley should locate its second production site in country B or in country N.

**Q3** [8 marks]

> **[case-study]**
> **Tarnwell**
> 
> Tarnwell manufactures water pumps in country G for farms and small industrial users. Five years ago it operated from one site with 90 employees. After rapid growth it now runs four sites employing 620 people.
> 
> **Table 1: Tarnwell selected data**
> 
> |   | **2020** | **2025** |
> |---|---|---|
