### Q1
A joint venture is an agreement in which two businesses set up a separate business together and share its costs, risks and profits **[K]**. Entering a new international market is expensive and risky, because a business may not understand local tastes, laws or distribution systems. A food manufacturer moving into a country where religious rules affect which ingredients are acceptable **[Ap]** could take years to learn this on its own. Working with a partner already established there gives the business that knowledge immediately, and the set-up costs are shared, so expansion becomes possible for a business that could not afford to enter the market by itself **[An]**

However, a joint venture also means giving up control **[K]**. Every major decision must be agreed with the partner, and the two businesses may want different things - one may want rapid growth while the other prefers steady profits **[Ap]**. Disagreements can slow decisions down at exactly the moment a new market needs a quick response to competitors, and any profit made has to be shared rather than kept in full **[An]**

Whether a joint venture is the best method depends on the circumstances **[E]**. Where the market is very different from the home market, or where a government requires foreign firms to work with a local partner, a joint venture may be the only realistic way in **[E]**. Where the business already understands the market and has enough finance, setting up its own operation would let it keep full control and all of the profit, which is likely to be better over time **[E]**. Much also depends on how well matched the partners are, since a joint venture between firms with similar aims is far more likely to work than one formed simply because a partner was available **[E]**. Overall, a joint venture is the best choice when risk and unfamiliarity are high, rather than in every case **[E]**

> **[exam-tip]**

Try to include at least two distinct features in your definition to secure both marks.

### Q36
One disadvantage of changing to a partnership is that the sole trader loses sole control of decision-making **[K]**. Previously, they could make all decisions independently and act quickly when opportunities or problems arose. Once partners join the business, agreement must be reached before decisions can be made **[An]**. If the partners have different views on how the business should be run, this can cause conflict and slow down the decision-making process considerably **[An]**. As a result, the business may miss time-sensitive opportunities that it would previously have been able to act on quickly, weakening its competitive position **[An]**

Also, each partner is legally liable for the actions of all other partners **[K]**. This means that if one partner takes on a large debt or signs an expensive contract without the others' consent, all partners are equally responsible for that obligation **[An]**. For the original sole trader, this represents a significant new risk - previously they were only answerable for their own decisions, but now a partner's poor judgement could expose them to unlimited liability for debts they played no part in creating **[An]**. In the worst case, this could put their personal assets, such as their home or savings, at risk **[An]**

> **Indicative content**
