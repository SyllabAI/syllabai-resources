### Q1
One reason is that FD's largest customers now treat sustainability as a condition of doing business **[1]**. Three customers providing 44% of FD's revenue will buy only from suppliers using entirely recycled material from 2028, and FD currently uses 61% **[1]**. If FD does not invest the \$2.8m needed to reach 100%, it risks losing nearly half its sales at once, which would cost far more than the equipment, so sustainability has become necessary for FD's survival rather than a choice about its image **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Explain requires a clear statement of meaning (AO1) supported by contextual development (AO2)
> 
> - Valid AO1 is awarded 1 mark
> - Valid AO2 is awarded up to 2 marks
> 
> **Indicative content**
> 
> AO1
> 
> - Sustainability matters to a business because of customer requirements, legal and tax changes, cost savings, reputation, and access to finance
> 
> AO2
> 
> - Three customers worth 44% of revenue will require fully recycled packaging from 2028, so failing to invest puts almost half of FD's sales at risk
> - Country M's tax on packaging below 70% recycled content begins in 2027, and FD's current 61% falls below that threshold, adding a cost it does not face today
> - The \$2.8m investment is large, but it answers the customer requirement and the tax at the same time, so the two pressures reinforce one another

### Q2
One benefit is that a trade agreement usually reduces or removes tariffs between the countries involved **[1]**. A tariff is a tax on imports, so removing it lowers the price at which a business can sell its products in the other country **[1]**. Cheaper products are likely to sell in greater numbers, so the business can raise its sales abroad without cutting its own profit margin, and producing those extra units may also lower its average cost **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Explain requires a clear statement of meaning (AO1) supported by contextual development (AO2)
> 
> - Valid AO1 is awarded 1 mark
> - Valid AO2 is awarded up to 2 marks
> 
> **Indicative content**
> 
> AO1
> 
> - Trade agreements can reduce tariffs and quotas, simplify customs procedures, standardise regulations, and give businesses more secure access to a partner country's market
> 
> AO2
> 
> - Removing tariffs lowers the selling price in the partner country without reducing the exporter's margin, so sales volumes are likely to rise
> - Simpler customs procedures cut delays and paperwork costs, which matters most for goods that are perishable or time-sensitive
> - Common product standards allow a business to sell the same version of a product in several countries, avoiding the cost of redesigning it for each market
> - More secure access reduces the risk of sudden trade barriers, making it safer to invest in production capacity aimed at export markets
> 
> Accept any other valid response.

> **[exam-tip]**
> A benefit question needs you to identify who gains and by what route, not simply to assert that something is good for business. Here the route runs from a lower tariff, to a lower selling price, to higher sales volume. Making that path explicit is what separates a three-mark answer from a one-mark one.
