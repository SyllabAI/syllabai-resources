>

### Q10
Delegation is when a manager passes authority to carry out a task to a subordinate **[1]**, while remaining accountable for whether the task is completed properly **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Define requires a clear statement of the meaning of the term
> 
> An example alone is not sufficient for full marks but can support a partial definition for 1 mark
> 
> **Indicative content**
> 
> - Passing authority for a task down to a more junior employee, with the manager still answerable for the outcome
> - Giving a subordinate the power to make decisions about a piece of work, without transferring ultimate responsibility for it
> - Entrusting work to someone lower in the hierarchy, together with the authority needed to complete it

> The examiner is looking for what does and does not transfer. Authority passes to the subordinate; accountability stays with the manager. Getting that asymmetry into your definition secures the second mark, and it is the point students most often get wrong on this topic.

> - A product divisional structure - organising the business around its distinct product lines such as Hydrocamel, recycled bricks, metal recycling, and electricity generation - would allow each division to develop its own strategy suited to its specific market and customer base; this would reduce the problem Amandi has identified of managers focusing too narrowly on their functional responsibilities rather than on the overall strategic direction of their product area
> 
> Evaluation
> 
> - The most appropriate structure depends on WTZ's primary growth priority: if international expansion is the immediate focus, a geographic divisional structure offers the most direct benefits; if product innovation is the priority, a matrix or product divisional structure is more relevant
> - Changing structure is unlikely to be straightforward — it requires a cultural shift within WTZ, and employees and managers accustomed to the current hierarchical approach may resist change; Amandi's attachment to control may also limit how much authority he is willing to delegate in practice
> - A hybrid approach - for example, a product divisional structure with matrix elements for research and development teams - may be the most effective way to support both growth and innovation simultaneously
> - It is worth noting that WTZ has been highly successful under its current structure, so changes should be introduced carefully and incrementally to avoid disrupting the operational efficiency and market position that have driven its growth
