### Q1
The number of atoms in one mole of:

(i)

- Sodium = $6.02\times10^{23}$ **[1 mark]**

(ii)

- Lithium = $6.02\times10^{23}$ **[1 mark]**

(iii)

- Xenon = $6.02\times10^{23}$ **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 3 marks]**

> *Sodium and lithium are metals and xenon is a monatomic element, so you do not need to consider molecular arrangements for these elements. If you do need to consider the molecular formulae these will be given to you in the question.*
In one mole of O<sub>2</sub>:

- There are $6.02\times10^{23}$ O<sub>2</sub> molecules
- In each O<sub>2</sub> molecule there are 2 oxygen atoms, *N*
- Therefore, in one mole of oxygen gas there are

  - $N = 2 \times ( 6.02 \times 10^{23} )$**[1 mark]**
  - $N=12.04\times10^{23}$ **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**
To calculate the molar mass of oxygen gas:

- The atomic mass of oxygen is 16
- Therefore, one mole of oxygen atoms = 16 g
- Therefore, one mole of oxygen gas, O<sub>2</sub> = 2 × 16 = 32 g
- So the molar mass, $M_{r}$ = 32 **[1 mark]**
- Unit = g mol<sup>−1</sup> **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**
To calculate the number of moles in 0.5 kg of O<sub>2</sub> gas:

List the known values:

- Molar mass of O<sub>2</sub>, $M_{r}$ = 32 g mol<sup>−1</sup> (from part (c))
- Mass of O<sub>2</sub> = 0.5 kg = 500 g

Recall the molar mass equation:

- $n=\frac{m}{M_{r}}$
- $n=\frac{500}{32}$ **[1 mark]**
- $n=15.625\mathrm{mol}$ **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**

> *When dealing with molar mass (units grams per mole) it is easier to keep the units in grams.*

### Q3

- $\frac{\mathrm{molar} \mathrm{mass} \mathrm{methane}}{\mathrm{molar} \mathrm{mass} \mathrm{sulphur} \mathrm{dioxide}}=\frac{16}{64}=0.25$** [1 mark]**

- 4 moles of methane are required;** [1 mark]**

### Q6
To determine the molar mass of glucose, C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub>:

- *M*<sub>*r*</sub>* *carbon = 12 g mol<sup>−1</sup>

  - There are 6 atoms of carbon in 1 molecule of glucose
- *M*<sub>*r*</sub> hydrogen = 1 g mol<sup>−1</sup>

  - There are 12 atoms of hydrogen in 1 molecule of glucose
- *M*<sub>*r*</sub> oxygen = 16 g mol<sup>−1</sup>

  - There are 6 atoms of oxygen in 1 molecule of glucose
- Therefore, *M*<sub>*r*</sub> C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> = $( 6 \times 12 ) + ( 12 \times 1 ) + ( 6 \times 16 ) = 180$ g mol<sup>−1</sup> **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 1 mark]**
To calculate the number of moles in 600g C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub>:

- Recall the molar mass equation

  - $n=\frac{m}{M_{r}}$
- $n=\frac{600}{180}$** [1 mark]**
- $n=3.33\mathrm{mol}$** [1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**
To determine how many molecules of glucose there are in 600 g of glucose:

List the known quantities:

- Number of moles of glucose, *n* = 3.33 (from part (b))
- Avogadro's constant, *N*<sub>*A*</sub> = 6.02×10<sup>23</sup> (from the data page)

To determine the number of molecules, *N*:

- Recall the equation for Avogadro's constant and rearrange to make *N* the subject

  - $n=\frac{N}{N_{A}}\rightarrowN=nN_{A}$
- $N = 3.33 \times ( 6.02 \times 10^{23} )$ **[1 mark]**
- $N=2.01\times10^{24}=2\times10^{24}$** [1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**
To determine the mass of carbon in 600 g of glucose:

List the known quantities:

- Atomic mass of carbon = 12
- Number of glucose molecules, *N* = 2.01 ×10<sup>24</sup> (from part (c))
- Unified atomic mass unit, 1 u = 1.66×10<sup>−27</sup> kg (from the data page)

Determine the number of carbon atoms in 600 g of glucose:

- There are 6 carbon atoms per C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>12</sub>O<sub>6</sub> molecule
- Therefore, there are 6*N* carbon atoms in 600 g of glucose

  - Number of C atoms = $6\times2.01\times10^{24}$** [1 mark]**
  - Number of C atoms = $1.206\times10^{25}$ **[1 mark]**

Determine the total mass of carbon atoms:

- Mass of 1 carbon atom = 12 u = $12\times1.66\times10^{-27}=1.992\times10^{-26}\mathrm{kg}$ **[1 mark]**
- Total mass of carbon atoms = mass of 1 carbon atom × number of carbon atoms

  - Total mass of carbon atoms = $( 1.992 \times 10^{- 26} ) \times ( 1.206 \times 10^{25} )$ **[1 mark]**
  - Total mass of carbon atoms = $0.240\mathrm{kg}$  **OR** 240 g** [1 mark]**

**[Total: 5 marks]**
