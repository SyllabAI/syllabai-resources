### Q1
An ideal gas is:

- A gas that obeys (the relationship) *pV *∝ *T  ***[1 mark]**
- Where *p *= pressure of the gas, *V *= volume of the gas and *T *= temperature of the gas <u>in kelvin</u>; **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**
Calculate the magnitude of the work done in kJ during the change XY

List the known quantities:

- For X and Y pressure, *p *= 0.6 × 10<sup>5</sup> Pa
- For X, volume, *V*<sub>*X*</sub> = 0.12 m<sup>3</sup>
- For Y, volume, *V*<sub>*Y*</sub> = 0.04 m<sup>3</sup>

Use the work done on a gas equation:

- $W=p\DeltaV$ **[1 mark]**
- $W = p ( V_{X} - V_{Y} )$
- $W = ( 0.6 \times 10^{5} ) \times ( 0.12 - 0.04 )$
- $W=4800J=4.8\mathrm{kJ}$ **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 2 marks]**
**During XY**

- Work done on gas for XY = +4.8 kJ (from (b))
- Increase in internal energy Δ*U* = − 24 + 4.8 = −19.2 kJ calculated from the equation $\DeltaU=q+W$

**During YZ**

- Work done for YZ = 0 kJ (as there is no change in volume)
- Increase in internal energy Δ*U* =  +19.2 kJ because the total increase in internal energy of the system (XYZX) = 0
- Hence, thermal energy gained = 19.2 kJ

**During ZX**

- Work done for ZX = −15.8 kJ (given in table)
- Increase in internal energy = 0 kJ (temperature and state do not change along ZX so the system is not heated . For the isothermal expansion ZX, the internal energy does not change (Δ*U* = 0), so by $\DeltaU=q+W$, $q=-W=+15.8$ kJ — thermal energy is supplied to maintain constant temperature)
- Hence, thermal energy = 15.8 kJ

A correctly completed table should look like this:

| change | increase in internal energy of gas / kJ | thermal energy supplied to gas / kJ | work done on gas / kJ |
|---|---|---|---|
| XY | **Final answer:** **−19.2** | −24.0 | **Final answer:** **4.8** |
| YZ | **Final answer:** **19.2** | **Final answer:** **19.2** | **Final answer:** **0** |
| ZX | **Final answer:** **0** | **Final answer:** **15.8** | −15.8 |

- **1 mark for each correct row**

**[Total: 3 marks]**

> *The curved line ZX is called an isotherm. It is common for you to be asked to recognise that, if temperature and state of an **ideal gas **do not change, the internal energy does not change. The particles of an ideal gas do not interact so the only internal energy is the sum of kinetic energies (i.e. temperature).*

### Q3
Calculating the work done by the gas:

List the known quantities:

- Change in internal energy, Δ*U* = +43 kJ

- $ΔU=q+W$

**[Total: 3 marks]**

> *In thermodynamics, the sign in front of an energy implies a direction of transfer. In CIE, the convention is:*

- *Work done **on** the system (gas compressed) is positive*
- *Work done **by** the system (gas expands) is negative*

> *Not every website or exam board uses CIE's sign convention for thermodynamics, so be aware of this if attempting practice questions from a range of exam boards or websites.*

### Q6
To find the work done by the gas:

List the known quantities:

- Heat supplied to gas, *q*  = 32.4 kJ
- Upwards acceleration, *a*  = 3.5 m s<sup>−2</sup>
- Mass of piston, *m*  = 0.75 kg
- Distance moved, *s*  = 12 cm = 0.12 m
- Acceleration of free fall, *g*  = 9.81 m s<sup>−2 </sup>(from the data page)

Apply Newton's second law:

- The resultant force, *F*<sub>*res*</sub> , on the piston is:

  - $F_{res}=ma$
- The gas exerts an upward force, *F*<sub>*res*</sub> , and the piston's weight, *mg* , acts downwards

  - $F_{res}=F_{gas}-mg$
- Combine the two equations

  - $F_{gas}-mg=ma$ **[1 mark]**

Calculate the force from the gas:

- $F_{g a s} = m ( a + g ) = 0.75 ( 3.5 + 9.81 )$
- $F_{gas}=9.983N$ **[1 mark]**

Determine the work done, *W *:

- Recall that distance moved parallel to the force was 12 cm
- $W=F_{gas}s=9.983\times0.12$
- $W=1.2J$ **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 3 marks]**

> *Work done pops up in a lot of different areas of physics - you may have gone to the first law of thermodynamics initially. This question, however, makes use of Newton's second law to find a resultant force and then calculate mechanical work.  Annotate the diagram, list your known quantities, and the method will become much clearer.*
