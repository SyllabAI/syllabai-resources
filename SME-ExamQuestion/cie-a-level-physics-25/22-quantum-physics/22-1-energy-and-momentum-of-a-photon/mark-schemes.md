To calculate the kinetic energy in eV:

List the known quantities:

- Elementary charge, *e*  = 1.60 × 10<sup>−19</sup> C

Calculate the kinetic energy, *E*<sub>*k*</sub> , in joules:

- $E_{k}=\frac{1}{2}m_{e}v^{2}$
- $E_{k} = 1 \frac{1}{2} \times 9.11 \times 10^{- 31} \times ( 1.5 \times 10^{6} )^{2}$ **[1 mark]**
- $E_{k}=1.02\times10^{-18}J$

Convert this to eV:

- $1 \mathrm{J} = ( \frac{1}{1.60 \times 10^{- 19} } ) \text{eV}$
- $E_{k}=1.02\times10^{-18}J=\frac{1.02\times10^{-18}}{1.60\times10^{-19}}\mathrm{eV}$ **[1 mark]**
- $E_{k}=6.4\mathrm{eV}$ **[1 mark]**

**[Total: 3 marks]**

> *It can be easy to become confused when converting between eV and J. Just remember, you can only either divide or multiply by a factor of 1.60 × 10*<sup>*−19*</sup>*.*

> *Going from J to eV, your value should increase a lot (so divide by the very small number, 1.60 × 10*<sup>*−19*</sup>*).*

> *Going from eV to J, your value should decrease a lot (so multiply by the very small number, 1.60 × 10*<sup>*−19*</sup>*).*
