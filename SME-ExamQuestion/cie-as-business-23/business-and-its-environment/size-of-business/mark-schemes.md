> - CS opened in 1975
> - Currently targeting a mass market (professional men and women)

> - Shoes sold in own shop
> - Range of sizes and colours

> - Abir would like to design and produce unique shoes for niche markets using price skimming
> - Use of Table 2.1
> - Abir's suggested adoption of JIT

> **AO3 Analysis** (max 2 marks)
> 
> - Lifestyle / personal satisfaction — Yazeem (and Abir) can choose their own hours — likely sole-trader objective
> - Independence — Yazeem does not need anyone's permission to take CS niche
> - Opportunities — small businesses can pivot to niche markets faster than large competitors
> - Creative freedom — both Yazeem and Abir can design unique shoes
> - Expertise — both are highly skilled and can focus on a small customer base
> - USP — expert owners allow CS to charge a higher price (supports Abir's price-skimming idea)
> - Personal service for customers — customers deal directly with the owners — repeat business
> - May struggle obtaining finance — limits CS's ability to compete with mass-market manufacturers
> - Cannot achieve economies of scale — unit costs higher than low-priced mass-market competitors
> 
> **AO4 Evaluation** (6 marks)
> 
> A judgement about the importance to CS of being a small business. Possible evaluative points:
> 
> - Evaluation of the relative importance of different advantages / factors
> - The judgement might depend upon: Yazeem and Abir's personal objectives, whether Yazeem wants to retire, the relative size of competitors, the location of CS, changes in shoe manufacture and retail, changes in technology, price elasticity of demand for CS's shoes

> |---|---|
> | **L1** | Being a small business is important for CS. |
> | **L2** | Being a small business is important for CS because it means Yazeem can retain control of his business. |
> | **L3** | Being a small business is important for CS because it means Yazeem can retain control of his sole-trader operation and Abir can pursue the niche, price-skimmed shoe strategy without needing investor approval. |

> **[exam-tip]**
> The central tension: staying small protects CS's expert / niche / personal-service advantages — BUT staying small means CS can't achieve the economies of scale that the low-priced mass-market competitors enjoy, which is exactly why Yazeem's sales are falling. Balance both sides and anchor your judgement on the niche pivot Abir is proposing. Naming Yazeem, Abir, sole-trader status, or price skimming locks in Eval 5–6.

> **Indicative content** — *AO1 Knowledge and understanding*
> 
> - There are many ways to measure the size of a business and most are more reliable than using the number of employees
> - A business that relies on machines rather than people will be undervalued — fails to take account of capital investment
> - Does not take account of the assets of a business
> - Does not take account of the cash flow of a business
> - No reference to revenue, output, sales, profit
> - Workers can be full-time or part-time
> - Workers can be outsourced
> - Workers can be multi-skilled so fewer needed
> 
> **AO2 Application**
> 
> - Large business may have more employees than a small business — a franchise vs a local shop
> - Large capital-intensive business may have few employees — car production done by robots
> - Large IT / internet businesses often have few employees
> - Small business may have many employees — labour-intensive clothing production factory
> 
> **AO3 Analysis**
> 
> - Number of employees can be misleading depending on whether business is labour or capital intensive
> - One measure used on its own can give a misleading measurement of size
> - Number of employees may be less important than other measures such as market share, turnover
> 
> **Marking guidance** — 4 analysis marks total: 2 per limitation
> 
> *Limitation 1 example* — 'capital-intensive businesses are undervalued by an employee count'
> 
> - *Developed (2): a car manufacturer using robots may have only a few hundred employees but huge revenue; using employee count alone classes it as small, missing its true scale*
> 
> *Limitation 2 example* — 'full-time vs part-time workers are not distinguished'
> 
> - *Developed (2): counting 100 part-time workers and 100 full-time workers as the same overstates the size of a business reliant on part-time staff*
> 
> Accept all valid responses

> **[exam-tip]**
> The 4 analysis marks split 2 + 2 across two limitations. Pick CONTRASTING limitations — e.g. one about capital intensity (Amazon has fewer employees per dollar revenue than a clothing factory) and one about labour structure (part-time vs full-time). Don't list 4 different things and skim each.
