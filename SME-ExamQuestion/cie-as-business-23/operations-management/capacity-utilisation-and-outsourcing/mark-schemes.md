# Mark Schemes — Capacity Utilisation & Outsourcing
**4. Operations Management** · Cambridge (CIE) AS business (9609)


## Exam Questions (3 questions)


### Q1
> **[mark-scheme]**
> | Level | AO1 Knowledge and understanding (1 mark) | AO2 Application (2 marks) | AO3 Analysis (2 marks) |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **2** |   | Developed application of **one** relevant point to a business context | Developed analysis that identifies connections between causes, impacts and / or consequences of **one** relevant point |
> | **1** | Knowledge of **one** relevant point is used to answer the question | Limited application of **one** relevant point to a business context | Limited analysis that identifies connections between causes, impacts and / or consequences of **one** relevant point |
> | **0** | No creditable response | No creditable response | No creditable response |
> 
> **Indicative content** — *AO1 Knowledge and understanding*
> 
> - Stress on employees
> - Impact quality
> - Business inflexibility
> - Unable to maintain machinery
> - Strain on business resources — inventory
> - Sell more
> - Economies of scale
> 
> **AO2 Application**
> 
> - The impact could affect staff — who are likely to be put under excessive stress
> - Could impact on quality — if staff rush work and make mistakes / increase waste
> - Business becomes inflexible — as has no room to increase output for new orders / opportunity cost of not responding to new product demand
> - Might not be able to maintain machinery — which could break down
> - Can put a strain on business resources — staff may need extra training
> - More products produced and available to sell
> - Costs fall due to increased output
> 
> **AO3 Analysis**
> 
> - Overworked staff could make more mistakes — a reduction in quality could impact brand image and lost sales
> - Could lead to high absenteeism and high staff turnover — increase recruitment costs — don't have enough staff to meet orders
> - May have to increase amount of overtime to meet orders — raise business costs
> - If operating over maximum capacity then new orders might not be possible — lose potential customers and sales
> - If demand reduces stock will remain unsold therefore wastage / opportunity cost
> - If machines break down due to lack of maintenance — production stops — lose or delay orders — gives a bad reputation for being unreliable
> - Average costs can rise — due to falling levels of efficiency such as machines breaking down
> - *Developed analysis — overworked staff rush work and make mistakes, lowering product quality; this leads to customer complaints, returns and reputational damage that ultimately reduces future sales* **[2 marks]**
> - *Limited analysis — product quality may fall* **[1 mark]**
> 
> Accept all valid responses

> **[exam-tip]**
> 'Operating OVER maximum capacity' means stretching beyond designed limits — different from being at full capacity. Pick ONE impact (staff stress, quality decline, machine breakdown) and chain it through TWO consequences. Note: some impacts are positive (more sales, lower unit cost) — you can pick either side as long as you analyse it properly.
