# Mark Schemes — Business Finance
**5. Finance & Accounting** · Cambridge (CIE) AS business (9609)


## Exam Questions (5 questions)


### Q1
> **[mark-scheme]**
> | Marks | Knowledge & Application |
> |---|---|
> | **3** | Knowledge of working capital + explanation + applied to a business context |
> | **2** | Knowledge + explanation, OR knowledge + applied to a business context |
> | **1** | Knowledge of working capital only |
> | **0** | No creditable response |
> 
> **Indicative content** — *AO1 Knowledge and understanding*
> 
> - Finance used in day-to-day / trading operations / to enable the business to produce its goods or services / to operate
> - Current assets − current liabilities
> 
> **AO2 Application** — *explanation of working capital*
> 
> - To pay for the running costs — bills / expenses such as staff wages
> - Finance trade credit periods offered to customers
> - Reference to short-term (less than 12 months)
> - Sufficient working capital is needed to ensure the business has enough liquid assets / avoid liquidity problems
> 
> **Application to a business context** (SW or any other scenario)
> 
> - Examples of current assets, e.g. stock, inventory, debtors; or current liabilities, e.g. bank overdraft, short-term loans, creditors
> - Examples of expenses / costs, e.g. staff wages
> - Reference to SW's high production costs eroding working capital
> 
> Accept all valid responses

> **[exam-tip]**
> Three marks split as K (knowledge of the term) + EXP (explanation of purpose) + CTX (apply to a business). For SW, link to the stem: 'production costs are high, so SW has little working capital to pay for raw materials or staff wages'. Don't confuse working capital with cash flow — working capital is a balance-sheet snapshot (current assets − current liabilities).

### Q2
> **[mark-scheme]**
> | Level | AO1 Knowledge and understanding (2 marks) | AO2 Application (2 marks) | AO3 Analysis (2 marks) | AO4 Evaluation (6 marks) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | **3** |   |   |   | **5–6 marks — Developed evaluation in context**: developed judgement / conclusion in the business context; developed evaluative comments which balance some key arguments in context |
> | **2** | **2 marks**: developed knowledge of relevant key term(s) / factor(s) | **2 marks**: developed application of relevant point(s) to the business context | **2 marks**: developed analysis identifying connections between causes, impacts and / or consequences | **3–4 marks — Developed evaluation**: developed judgement / conclusion; developed evaluative comments which balance some key arguments |
> | **1** | **1 mark**: limited knowledge | **1 mark**: limited application | **1 mark**: limited analysis | **1–2 marks — Limited evaluation**: judgement with limited supporting evidence; an attempt is made to balance the arguments |
> | **0** | No creditable response | No creditable response | No creditable response | No creditable response |
> 
> **Indicative content** — *AO1 Knowledge and understanding*
> 
> - Working capital is the capital needed to pay for raw materials, day-to-day running costs and credit offered to customers
> - Working capital = current assets less current liabilities
> - Sources of finance — internal (retained profits, reduce working capital, sale of assets) and external (overdrafts, trade credit, factoring, HP, leasing, loans, equity finance, shares, debentures, venture capital)
> - A start-up business is a company in the first stages of operations, often being financed by its entrepreneurial founders during the initial starting period
> 
> **AO2 Application** (start-up furniture manufacturer)
> 
> - Needs finance to pay suppliers of wood, tools
> - Needs finance to hire craftspersons / machines to make furniture
> - Needs finance for rent to hire premises / warehouses to store furniture pending sale to retailers
> 
> **AO3 Analysis**
> 
> - If wages cannot be paid due to low working capital, could lead to employees leaving the business — thus high labour turnover
> - If unable to meet a customer order due to poor working capital — business may lose important customers
> - Insufficient working capital to pay suppliers — could lead to loss of trade terms or supplier refusing to do business with them altogether
> - If business doesn't have enough working capital to purchase materials — may not be able to produce enough goods to satisfy demand — lose sales
> - Adequate working capital enables materials to be bought in advance of busy periods — such as the lead up to Christmas — so business doesn't miss out on high sales
> - Inadequate working capital may prevent the business from raising additional funds — so will limit or prevent further growth
> - Might be possible to arrange an overdraft — though may only be limited initially — high interest and potential charges
> - As a new starter will not have traded long enough to have retained profits — so would not have 'free' source of finance
> - May not have built up trade relationships — so not trustworthy enough yet to get credit
> - HP and leasing would allow the business to obtain assets without a large initial cash payment — thus reducing cash outflow
> - Venture capital might be an option — but may have to give up a share of the business as well as a percentage of future profits
> 
> **AO4 Evaluation**
> 
> A judgement / conclusion is made as to whether working capital is the most important source of finance for a start-up furniture manufacturer. Possible evaluative points:
> 
> - A conclusion about what the new furniture manufacturer hopes to achieve in the long and short term and how much finance may be required
> - A conclusion as to what the business hopes to achieve once established and what elements of its working capital need improving
> - A judgement that a new business will find it harder to raise finance than a well-established business — so it's imperative to effectively manage working capital
> - A conclusion that working capital is only one source of finance and is unlikely to be sufficient for a start-up furniture manufacturer; however, other options may not be available due to lack of credit history or length of time that the business has been operating
> 
> **Evaluation mark band guidance**
> 
> - **Eval 6** — developed judgement / conclusion made in context (start-up furniture manufacturer) **[6 marks]**
> - **Eval 5** — developed evaluative comments given in context **[5 marks]**
> - **Eval 4** — developed judgement / conclusion made, not in context **[4 marks]**
> - **Eval 3** — developed evaluative comments, not in context **[3 marks]**
> - **Eval 2** — judgement / conclusion with little supporting evidence, not in context **[2 marks]**
> - **Eval 1** — an attempt is made to balance arguments **[1 mark]**
> - **Eval 0** — no evaluation
> 
> Accept all valid responses

> **[exam-tip]**
> The 'start-up' framing is critical — a new business has no retained profits, no credit history and limited collateral, which rules out many external sources. Argue working capital is important because it keeps the business operating day-to-day, but balance against the start-up's need for large initial capital (machinery, premises) that working capital alone can't fund — so a loan or owner's investment may be MORE important at start-up. Always anchor the judgement on a furniture-specific cost (wood, tools, warehouse, craftsmen).

### Q3
> **[mark-scheme]**
> | Rationale | Marks |   |
> |---|---|---|
> | C | Explanation of the difference between cash and profit | 1 |
> | B | Understanding of profit | 1 |
> | A | Understanding of cash | 1 |
> 
> **Content **
> 
> - Cash is the liquidity of a firm – the money available to pay debts/bills/everyday costs
> - Profit is the difference between cost and revenue.
> - Cash is required and will come from revenue, but not all cash is profit as costs need to be paid.
> - Cash is an asset of the business.
> - Cash is required to run a business, whereas profit is something to aim for.
> - A business is unlikely to operate without cash but can operate without profit.
> 
> | **Exemplar** | **Mark** | **Rationale** |
> |---|---|---|
> | A business is unlikely to operate without cash but can operate without profit (C). | 3 | A difference shown, so all three marks gained – the candidate does not need to show understanding of cash or profit if a valid difference has been explained. |
> | Cash can be used to pay the business costs (A) whereas profit is the difference between costs and revenue (B). | 3 | Clear understanding of both cash and profit, the word ‘whereas’ shows a difference. |
> | Cash can be used to pay the business costs (A). Profit is the difference between costs and revenue (B). | 2 | Two statements but no attempt to compare them, so only two marks. |

### Q5
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Answers could include:
> 
> - Capital expenditure is money spent on the purchase of fixed assets such as premises and machinery. Items that will be in use for a long period (more than 1 year) which managers must plan for
> - Revenue expenditure is money used to pay for everyday running costs of a business, e.g., raw materials or components, tea or coffee for the canteen or paper for the offices. These items will be used in the short term and, even though lower value, must still be planned for
> - They should normally be funded differently; working capital / retained capital
> - Revenue expenditure recorded correctly allows a manager to accurately measure the cost of the day-to-day running of a business which is vital information in the early days of a business.
> - They are recorded on different financial documents: revenue expenditure is recorded in the Income Statement; capital expenditure is recorded in the Statement of Financial Position – managers use these documents to assess the financial situation of a business
> 
> Accept any other valid response.
> 
> Effective explanation of why a business manager needs to understand the difference between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure (4–5 marks) 
> Explanation of why a business manager needs to understand the difference between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure (2–3 marks) 
> Descriptive information about capital expenditure/revenue expenditure (1 mark) 
> No creditable content (0 marks)
