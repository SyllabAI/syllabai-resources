> - Assumes all costs follow a straight line (constant)
> - Assumes price is constant

> **Indicative content** — *AO1 Knowledge and understanding*
> 
> - Working capital is the capital needed to pay for raw materials, day-to-day running costs and credit offered to customers
> - Working capital = current assets less current liabilities
> - Sources of finance — internal (retained profits, reduce working capital, sale of assets) and external (overdrafts, trade credit, factoring, HP, leasing, loans, equity finance, shares, debentures, venture capital)
> - A start-up business is a company in the first stages of operations, often being financed by its entrepreneurial founders during the initial starting period
> 
> **AO2 Application** (start-up furniture manufacturer)
> 
> - Needs finance to pay suppliers of wood, tools
> - Needs finance to hire craftspersons / machines to make furniture
> - Needs finance for rent to hire premises / warehouses to store furniture pending sale to retailers
> 
> **AO3 Analysis**
> 
> - If wages cannot be paid due to low working capital, could lead to employees leaving the business — thus high labour turnover
> - If unable to meet a customer order due to poor working capital — business may lose important customers
> - Insufficient working capital to pay suppliers — could lead to loss of trade terms or supplier refusing to do business with them altogether
> - If business doesn't have enough working capital to purchase materials — may not be able to produce enough goods to satisfy demand — lose sales
> - Adequate working capital enables materials to be bought in advance of busy periods — such as the lead up to Christmas — so business doesn't miss out on high sales
> - Inadequate working capital may prevent the business from raising additional funds — so will limit or prevent further growth
> - Might be possible to arrange an overdraft — though may only be limited initially — high interest and potential charges
> - As a new starter will not have traded long enough to have retained profits — so would not have 'free' source of finance
> - May not have built up trade relationships — so not trustworthy enough yet to get credit
> - HP and leasing would allow the business to obtain assets without a large initial cash payment — thus reducing cash outflow
> - Venture capital might be an option — but may have to give up a share of the business as well as a percentage of future profits
> 
> **AO4 Evaluation**
> 
> A judgement / conclusion is made as to whether working capital is the most important source of finance for a start-up furniture manufacturer. Possible evaluative points:
> 
> - A conclusion about what the new furniture manufacturer hopes to achieve in the long and short term and how much finance may be required
> - A conclusion as to what the business hopes to achieve once established and what elements of its working capital need improving
> - A judgement that a new business will find it harder to raise finance than a well-established business — so it's imperative to effectively manage working capital
> - A conclusion that working capital is only one source of finance and is unlikely to be sufficient for a start-up furniture manufacturer; however, other options may not be available due to lack of credit history or length of time that the business has been operating
> 
> **Evaluation mark band guidance**
> 
> - **Eval 6** — developed judgement / conclusion made in context (start-up furniture manufacturer) **[6 marks]**
> - **Eval 5** — developed evaluative comments given in context **[5 marks]**
> - **Eval 4** — developed judgement / conclusion made, not in context **[4 marks]**
> - **Eval 3** — developed evaluative comments, not in context **[3 marks]**
> - **Eval 2** — judgement / conclusion with little supporting evidence, not in context **[2 marks]**
> - **Eval 1** — an attempt is made to balance arguments **[1 mark]**
> - **Eval 0** — no evaluation
> 
> Accept all valid responses

> **[exam-tip]**
> The 'start-up' framing is critical — a new business has no retained profits, no credit history and limited collateral, which rules out many external sources. Argue working capital is important because it keeps the business operating day-to-day, but balance against the start-up's need for large initial capital (machinery, premises) that working capital alone can't fund — so a loan or owner's investment may be MORE important at start-up. Always anchor the judgement on a furniture-specific cost (wood, tools, warehouse, craftsmen).

> - To help increase the odds of success for a new product a business needs to ask a number of questions such as:

>   - how shall the product be priced?
>   - where is the break-even point?

> - However, there are limitations as to the use of break-even analysis, as:
> 
>   - it is a simplistic, static model
>   - the effectiveness depends on the quality and accuracy of the data used
>   - it assumes linearity
>   - it takes no account of possible changes in costs over time
>   - it does not allow for changes in market conditions in the time period, etc.
> - So some general limitations plus some specifi c to new product decisions – non-economic factors may infl uence such a decision – e.g. subjective personal owner preference decisions – political interference, social/cultural infl uences, etc.
