## Exam Questions (30 questions)


### Q1 — hard — 4 marks

**Q1** [4 marks]

A geometric progression has first term $a$, and its terms are connected by the relationship $u_{n+4} = 9 u_{n}$ for all $n \geq 1$.

Given that all the terms of the progression are positive, show that the sum of the first twelve terms of the progression may be written in the form

`S_{12} = ka\left(\sqrt{n} + 1\right)`

where $k$ and $n$ are positive integers and $\sqrt{n}$ is a surd.

### Q2 — very_hard — 5 marks

**Q2** [5 marks]

The $k$th term of a geometric progression is given by $u_{k} = ( - 2 )^{k-1}$.

Calculate the sum of the eleventh through twenty-third terms of the sequence whose $k$th term is given by $v_{k} = u_{k} + \sqrt{13}$, where $u_{k}$ is defined as above. Give your answer as an exact value.

### Q6 — very_hard — 13 marks

**Q6** [4 marks]

The geometric progression $S$ is defined by $S = u_{1} + u_{2} + u_{3} + \dots + u_{n} + \dots$, where $u_{n}$ denotes the $n$th term of the progression. The sum to infinity of the progression exists and is denoted by $S_{\infty}$. The first term of the progression, $u_{1}$, is equal to $a$, and the common ratio of the progression is $r$.

A different progression $T = u_{1}^{2} + u_{2}^{2} + u_{3}^{2} + \dots + u_{n}^{2} + \dots$ is formed by squaring all the terms of the progression $S$ above.

Show that $T$ is also a geometric progression, and that its sum to infinity also exists.

**Q6** [3 marks]

The sum to infinity of the progression $T$ is $T_{\infty}$.

Express the ratio $\frac{T_{\infty}}{S_{\infty}}$ in terms of $a$ and $r$, simplifying your answer as far as possible.

**Q6** [6 marks]

Show that if $T_{\infty} = S_{\infty}$, then $u_{k}^{2} = u_{2k-1} + u_{2k}$ for all $k \geq 1$. Comment on what this shows about the relationship between the terms of the two progressions.
