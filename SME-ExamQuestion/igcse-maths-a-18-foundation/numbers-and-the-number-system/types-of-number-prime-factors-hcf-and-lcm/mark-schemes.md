## Q1 — hard — 5 marks · exam-questions

### 17((a)) — 2 marks
> *Write 140 and 245 as a product of prime factors (using a 'factor tree' for example)*

![Factor trees for 140 and 245. Number 140 has factors 2, 2, 5, 7; 245 includes 5, 7, 7. Trees visually split into prime factors.](assets/001-factor-trees-for-140-and-245-number-140-has-fact.png)

$140=2\times2\times5\times7\,245=5\times7\times7$

**[M1]**

> *Multiply together the common factors that appear in both lists*

$5\times7=35$

**Final answer:** **35**

**[A1]**

### 17((b)) — 3 marks
> **[exam-tip]**
> Look out for these 'when is the next time two things will happen together' types of questions. Although the question doesn't say so explicitly, it is a Lowest Common Multiple question.
> 
> The fact that this is part (b) to a Highest Common Factor part (a) might also be a hint in that direction.

> *List the multiples of 50 and 80*

$50,100,150,200,250,300,350,400,450,....\,80,160,240,320,400,...$

**[M1]**

> *Identify the lowest common multiple*

*400*

**[M1]**

> *Convert to hours and minutes*

*400 minutes = 6 hours and 40 minutes*

> *Calculate the time by adding that on to 10am*

**Final answer:** **16:40**

**[A1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> 4:40 pm will also get the final mark here.

## Q4 — hard — 4 marks · exam-questions

### 23((a)) — 2 marks
> *Use a factor tree, or another method, to break 300 down to prime numbers*

> *First think of two numbers that multiply to make 300*

`table row 300 equals cell 3 cross times 100 end cell end table`

> *Check if either of these are prime (3 is, 100 isn't)*

> *Leave prime numbers and break down non-prime numbers*

$300=3\times2\times50$

**[M1]**

> *Keep doing this until you only have prime numbers*

$300=3\times2\times5\times10\,300=3\times2\times5\times2\times5$

> **[exam-tip]**
> You can then put these in ascending order, or group them using powers, but the question doesn't require that you do.

$300=2\times2\times3\times5\times5\mathrm{or}2^{2}\times3\times5^{2}$

**[A1]**

### 23((b)) — 2 marks
> *Multiply A and B by their coefficients in the question leaving them as products of their primes*

$5A=2\times2\times2\times3\times3\times5\times5=2^{3}\times3^{2}\times5^{2}$

$7B=2\times2\times3\times3\times3\times5\times7=2^{2}\times3^{3}\times5\times7$

**[M1]**

> *To calculate the LCM*

- *compare both lists and identify the highest power of each prime number*
- *multiply them together*

$2^{3}\times3^{3}\times5^{2}\times7$

**Final answer:** **37800**

**[A1]**
