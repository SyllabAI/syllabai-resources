# Mark Schemes — Factorising
**2. Equations, Formulae & Identities** · Edexcel IGCSE Maths A (4MA1) — 18/Foundation


## Q1 — medium — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 18((a)) — 2 marks
> **[exam-tip]**
> Factorise is an algebraic command word that means to put the expression in a bracketed form by dividing by the highest common factor of all terms in the expression.

> *Factorise fully by identifying the highest common factor of both terms*

> *Put that factor outside the brackets, and put everything else that's left inside the brackets*

`3 y open parentheses 2 y space plus space 5 close parentheses`

**[B1 B1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **B1**: For *any* correct factorisation by identifying a common factor, but not necessarily the highest common factor. E.g. 3(2y<sup>2</sup> + 5y) or y(6y + 15) would earn 1 of the 2 marks.
> 
> **B1**: For a fully correct factorisation using the highest common factor.

## Q2 — hard — 5 marks · exam-questions

### 21((c)) — 2 marks
> *Find the highest common factor (HCF) of each term to put outside the brackets*

*The HCF of 16 and 20 is 4*

*The HCF of *$a^{2}$* and *$a^{3}$* is *$a^{2}$

*The HCF of *$b^{3}$* and *$b$* is *$b$

> *So *$4a^{2}b$* is the factor outside the bracket*

> *Divide the terms by that to find what goes inside the brackets (or think about what you multiply *$4a^{2}b$* by to get back to the original expression)*

`4 a squared b open parentheses 4 b squared plus 5 a close parentheses`

**[B1 B1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **B1**: For any correct partial factorisation with at least two factors or the correct common factor  with no more than one error inside the bracket.
> 
> **B1**: For all correct.

### 21((d)) — 3 marks
(i)

> *Find two numbers with a product of -22 and sum of 9*

`table row cell 11 cross times open parentheses negative 2 close parentheses end cell equals cell negative 22 end cell row cell 11 plus open parentheses negative 2 close parentheses end cell equals 9 end table`

> *Put these in the brackets*

`open parentheses x plus 11 close parentheses open parentheses x minus 2 close parentheses`

**[M1 A1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **M1**: You could get this mark with incorrect signs in front of 11 and 2. You would also get this mark for finding any pair of numbers  whose product is -22, or whose sum is 9.
> 
> **A1**: Correct answer.

(ii)

> *Set each bracket from your answer to (i) equal to zero and solve*

`table row cell x plus 11 end cell equals 0 row x equals cell negative 11 end cell end table`

`table row cell x minus 2 end cell equals 0 row x equals 2 end table`

$x=-11,x=2$

**[B1]**

## Q3 — medium — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 13((b)) — 2 marks
> *Find the highest common factor of 12 and 18 (the largest number that goes into 12 and 18 exactly)*

*6*

> *This is the factor we put outside the brackets and divide the terms inside by 6 to get what goes inside the brackets*

$12a\div6=2a\,-18b\div6=-3b$

> *Write it all together*

`6 open parentheses 2 a minus 3 b close parentheses`

**[B1 B1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **B1:** If you took out a factor correctly that wasn't the highest common factor, for example `3 open parentheses 4 a minus 6 b close parentheses` or `2 open parentheses 6 a minus 9 b close parentheses`, or if you took out a factor of 6 but made a mistake inside the brackets, you get this mark.
> 
> **B1:** For the fully factorised answer.
