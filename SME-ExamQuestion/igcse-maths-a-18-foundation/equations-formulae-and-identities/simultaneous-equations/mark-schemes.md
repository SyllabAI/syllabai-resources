## Q1 — medium — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 24() — 3 marks
> *Multiply the second equation by 3 to make the coefficients of y the same*

$21x+9y=24$

> *Subtract the original first equation from your new second equation to eliminate y*

$21 x + 9 y = 24
- ( 2 x + 9 y = 14.5 )
- - - - - - - - - -
 19 x = 9.5$

**[M1]**

> *Divide both sides by 19 to get **x*

$x=\frac{9.5}{19}=0.5$

> *Substitute **x** back into one of the original equations and solve to get y*

$\begin{gathered} 2 ( 0.5 ) + 9 y & = 14.5 \\ 1 + 9 y & = 14.5 \end{gathered}$

> *Subtract 1 from both sides*

$\begin{gathered} 9 y & = 13.5 \end{gathered}$

> *Divide both sides by 9*

$\begin{gathered} y = \frac{13.5}{9} \\ y = 1.5 \end{gathered}$

**[M1]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> It is a good idea to check your answers, by substituting them into the original equation you didn't use to find $y$:
> 
> $\begin{gathered} 7 ( 0.5 ) + 3 ( 1.5 ) & = 8 \\ 3.5 + 4.5 & = 8 \\ 8 = 8 ✔ \end{gathered}$

$x=0.5,y=1.5$

**[A1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **A1**: Equivalent answers, e.g. $x=\frac{1}{2}$ and $y=\frac{3}{2}$, will also get the mark.

## Q2 — hard — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 21() — 3 marks
> *Use the elimination method to solve the simultaneous equations (the substitution method will also work)*

> *To use the elimination method, the coefficient of one variable must be the same in both equations*

> *Make the coefficient of *$y$* the same by multiplying the second equation by 4*

$\begin{gathered} 5 x + y & = 3 ( \times 4 ) \end{gathered}$* *

*gives:*

$\begin{gathered} 20 x + 4 y & = 12 \end{gathered}$

> *Write the first equation in the question underneath this equation*

$\begin{gathered} 20 x + 4 y & = 12 \\ 6 x + 4 y & = 19 \end{gathered}$

> *Subtract the second equation from the first equation to eliminate the *$y$* variable*

$\begin{gathered} 20 x + 4 y & = 12 - \\ 6 x + 4 y & = 19 \end{gathered}$

*___________*

$14x=-7$

**[M1]**

> *Solve for *$x$* by dividing both sides by 14*

$\begin{gathered} \frac{14 x}{14} & = \frac{- 7}{14} \\ x = - 0.5 \end{gathered}$

**[M1]**

> *Substitute the value of *$x$* into *$5x+y=3$*, using either of the original equations will work*

$\begin{gathered} 5 ( - 0.5 ) + y & = 3 \\ - 2.5 + y & = 3 \end{gathered}$

> *Solve for *$y$* by adding 2.5 to both sides*

$y=3+2.5\,y=5.5$

$x=-0.5,y=5.5$

**[A1]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Always substitute both the values into the other original equation to check that both the solutions work together.
> 
> You will not get marks awarded for this but it is always worth checking in case a mistake has been made.

> *Substitute the values for *$x$* and *$y$* into *$6x+4y=19$

$6 ( - 0.5 ) + 4 ( 5.5 ) = 19$

> *Calculate the left hand side to check if it equals 19*

$-3+22=19$

> *The substituted values equate to 19 so they work together and are therefore correct*

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **M1**: Is awarded for correctly eliminating one variable (could by eliminating $y$ or $x$)
> 
> **M1**: For correctly working out the value of one variable ($x=-0.5\mathrm{or}y=5.5$). This mark is only awarded if the first mark is gained.
> 
> **A1**: For both solutions correct. Any equivalent answers (e.g. in fraction form) will gain the mark. This mark is only awarded if the first mark is gained.
