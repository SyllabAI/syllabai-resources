> *Use the elimination method to solve the simultaneous equations (the substitution method will also work)*

> *To use the elimination method, the coefficient of one variable must be the same in both equations*

> *Make the coefficient of *$y$* the same by multiplying the second equation by 4*

$\begin{gathered} 5 x + y & = 3 ( \times 4 ) \end{gathered}$* *

$\begin{gathered} 20 x + 4 y & = 12 \\ 6 x + 4 y & = 19 \end{gathered}$

> *Subtract the second equation from the first equation to eliminate the *$y$* variable*

$\begin{gathered} 20 x + 4 y & = 12 - \\ 6 x + 4 y & = 19 \end{gathered}$

> *Solve for *$x$* by dividing both sides by 14*

$\begin{gathered} \frac{14 x}{14} & = \frac{- 7}{14} \\ x = - 0.5 \end{gathered}$

**[M1]**

> *Substitute the value of *$x$* into *$5x+y=3$*, using either of the original equations will work*

$\begin{gathered} 5 ( - 0.5 ) + y & = 3 \\ - 2.5 + y & = 3 \end{gathered}$

> *Solve for *$y$* by adding 2.5 to both sides*

$y=3+2.5\,y=5.5$

$x=-0.5,y=5.5$

**[A1]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Always substitute both the values into the other original equation to check that both the solutions work together.
> 
> You will not get marks awarded for this but it is always worth checking in case a mistake has been made.

> *Substitute the values for *$x$* and *$y$* into *$6x+4y=19$

$6 ( - 0.5 ) + 4 ( 5.5 ) = 19$

> *Calculate the left hand side to check if it equals 19*

$-3+22=19$

> *The substituted values equate to 19 so they work together and are therefore correct*

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **M1**: Is awarded for correctly eliminating one variable (could by eliminating $y$ or $x$)
>
