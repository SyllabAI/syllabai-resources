### 5() — 6 marks
Both the Fe<sup>2+</sup>-catalysed reaction between I<sup>-</sup> and S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>8</sub><sup>2-</sup> and the autocatalysed reaction between MnO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup> and C<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> involve homogeneous catalysis: the catalyst and reactants are in the same aqueous phase. In both cases, the catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy, and this involves the transition metal ion cycling between two oxidation states (Fe<sup>2+</sup>/Fe<sup>3+</sup> and Mn<sup>7+</sup>/Mn<sup>2+</sup> respectively).

However, the two systems differ in how the catalyst enters the reaction and how the rate changes over time. Fe<sup>2+</sup> is added to the mixture at the start, so the catalyst is present from the beginning and the rate is high initially, then decreases steadily as reactants are consumed. In contrast, Mn<sup>2+</sup> is a product of the MnO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>/C<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> reaction itself. At the start, no Mn<sup>2+</sup> is present and the rate is very low. As Mn<sup>2+</sup> accumulates, the rate increases through a maximum before declining as reactants are used up.
