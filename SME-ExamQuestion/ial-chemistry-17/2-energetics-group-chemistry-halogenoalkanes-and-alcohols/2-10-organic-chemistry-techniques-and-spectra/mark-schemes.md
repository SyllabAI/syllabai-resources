### 1() — 1 marks
**Correct answer: C** — 46

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- The **molecular ion peak** (M<sup>+</sup>) in a mass spectrum corresponds to the mass of the whole intact molecule (less one electron, which has negligible mass)
- M<sup>+</sup> is always the peak at the **highest m/z**

  - Ignoring small isotope peaks at M+1, M+2
- M<sup>+</sup> = 46

  - So, *M*<sub>r</sub>(Z) = 46
- Both ethanol (CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH) and methoxymethane (CH<sub>3</sub>OCH<sub>3</sub>) have *M*<sub>r</sub> = 46, they are **structural isomers**, so the mass spectrum alone cannot distinguish them

**A** is incorrect as 30 is *M*<sub>r</sub> of methanal (HCHO)

**B** is incorrect as 44 is *M*<sub>r</sub> of CO<sub>2</sub>, propane, or ethanal (CH<sub>3</sub>CHO), not Z

**D** is incorrect as 60 is *M*<sub>r</sub> of propan-1-ol or ethanoic acid

> **[exam-tip]**
> The molecular ion peak (M<sup>+</sup>) is always the highest-m/z peak, NOT the tallest peak. The tallest peak is the base peak, which represents the most stable fragment; these are usually different peaks.
> 
> Ethanol (an alcohol, with C–O–H bonds) and methoxymethane (an ether, with C–O–C bonds) are structural isomers, identical *M*<sub>r</sub> but different functional groups, so IR is needed to tell them apart (see parts b and c).
> 
> When writing fragment ions, always include the positive charge: CH<sub>3</sub><sup>+</sup>, not CH<sub>3</sub> or CH<sub>3</sub>•. The mass spectrometer only detects positive ions, so every fragment ion must carry a + charge in your answer.

- Key IR absorption bands to memorise for paper 2:

  - O–H (alcohol): broad, 3230–3550 cm<sup>–¹</sup>
