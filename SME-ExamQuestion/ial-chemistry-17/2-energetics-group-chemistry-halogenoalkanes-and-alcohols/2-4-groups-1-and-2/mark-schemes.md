> 
> Always work through the steps in order:
> 
> - mass
> - → ÷ *M*<sub>r</sub>
> - → moles
> - → x ratio
> - → moles
> - → x 24
> - → volume
> 
> Two common slips to avoid:
> 
> - Treating a given mass as if it were moles (skipping the m → n conversion), the January 2022 examiner report specifically flagged this as a recurring error
> - Multiplying by 22.4 instead of 24 even when the question states RTP
>

## Q6 — easy — 1 marks · multiple-choice-questions

### 1() — 1 marks
**Correct answer: C** — Reactivity increases down the group because first ionisation energy decreases

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Reactivity with water:

  - Mg slow with cold water (readily with steam)
  - Ca steady
  - Sr vigorous
  - Ba very vigorous
  - Reactivity **increases** down Group 2
- Going down the group, atomic radius increases.

  - This means that there are more inner shells leading to **increased shielding** of the outer electrons
  - So, the **first ionisation energy decreases**
  - The two outer electrons are lost more readily resulting in greater / increased reactivity

**A** is incorrect as ionic radius **increases** (not decreases) down the group, and reactivity increases (not decreases), both factual errors

**B** is incorrect as while nuclear charge does increase, the effect is outweighed by the increased shielding; net effect is IE falls, not rises

**D** is incorrect as reactivity changes dramatically down the group (Mg vs Ba behaviour is qualitatively different), forming the same ion does not mean forming it at the same rate or energy

> **[exam-tip]**
> For full marks on structured questions, you need the full reactivity chain:
> 
> - Atomic radius increases
> - → shielding increases
> - → first IE decreases
> - → reactivity increases
> 
> Two common slips to avoid:
> 
> - Saying "more electrons = more reactive" as your whole explanation, this doesn't give the mechanism
> - Claiming that Mg reacts with cold water in the same way as Ca. It doesn't (Mg is slow with cold water and forms MgO + H<sub>2</sub> with steam, which is a different product from Ca(OH)<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>)
> 
> Contrast this with Group 7, where reactivity decreases down the group because oxidising power falls.
