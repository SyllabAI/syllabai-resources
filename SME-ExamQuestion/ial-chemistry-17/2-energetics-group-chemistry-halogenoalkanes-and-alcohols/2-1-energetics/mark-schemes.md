### 1() — 1 marks

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- For an endothermic reaction, the products sit Δ*H* **above** the reactants on the enthalpy profile, so:

  - Forward *E*<sub>a</sub> = reactants → transition state peak
  - Reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> = products → transition state peak
- Since products are higher than reactants, the reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> is **smaller** than the forward *E*<sub>a</sub> by exactly Δ*H*

reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> = forward *E*<sub>a</sub> − Δ*H* = 150 − 80 = +70 kJ mol<sup>–¹</sup>

**A** is incorrect as activation energies are always **positive**. A negative *E*<sub>a</sub> is physically meaningless. This answer comes from doing Δ*H* − forward *E*<sub>a</sub> the wrong way round.

**C** is incorrect as +80 kJ mol<sup>–¹</sup> is just Δ*H*, this isn't the activation energy. Δ*H* and *E*<sub>a</sub> are two different quantities.

**D** is incorrect as +230 kJ mol<sup>–¹</sup> comes from adding Δ*H* instead of subtracting, this would be correct for an exothermic reaction (where Δ*H* is negative), but here Δ*H* is positive so subtraction is needed

> **[exam-tip]**
> For any reaction, the forward and reverse activation energies are linked by Δ*H* through a single formula:
> 
> reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> = forward *E*<sub>a</sub> − Δ*H*
> 
> This one formula works for both endothermic and exothermic reactions, as long as you use the signed value of Δ*H*:
> 
> - **Endothermic** (Δ*H* positive): subtracting a positive Δ*H* gives a smaller result → reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> < forward *E*<sub>a</sub>
> - **Exothermic** (Δ*H* negative): subtracting a negative Δ*H* is the same as adding → reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> > forward *E*<sub>a</sub>
> 
> Edexcel past papers usually test this visually using an enthalpy profile with lettered arrows (e.g. W, X, Y, Z), and ask you to identify the algebraic relationship between the arrows. The mark schemes express it as forward ***E***<sub>a</sub> - reverse ***E***<sub>**a**</sub> = Δ***H***, which is the same formula rearranged.
> 
> Two common slips to watch out for:
> 
> - Confusing Δ*H* with *E*<sub>a</sub>, they are different quantities. Δ*H* is the overall enthalpy change (positive for endothermic, negative for exothermic); *E*<sub>a</sub> is the energy barrier a collision must overcome (**always positive**).
> - Getting a negative answer for *E*<sub>a</sub>, this is physically impossible. If your working gives you a negative activation energy, you've made a sign error (usually adding instead of subtracting Δ*H*).
> 
> Check your answer by picturing the enthalpy profile:
> 
> - For endothermic reactions:
> 
>   - The products sit higher than reactants
>   - So, the reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> must be smaller than forward *E*<sub>a</sub>.
> - For exothermic reactions
> 
>   - The products sit lower than the reactants
>   - So, the reverse *E*<sub>a</sub> must be larger than forward *E*<sub>a</sub>.

>

> Three common slips to avoid in Hess calculations:
