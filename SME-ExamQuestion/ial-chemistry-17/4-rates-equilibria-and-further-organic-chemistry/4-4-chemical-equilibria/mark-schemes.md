### 3() — 3 marks
> **[mark-scheme]**
> To calculate the total entropy change and determine feasibility at 298 K:
> 
> - Substitute *K*<sub>p</sub> = 0.10 atm:
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = *R* ln *K*<sub>p</sub>
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = 8.31 × ln(0.10) = 8.31 × (−2.303)
> 
> Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = **−19.1 J K**<sup>**-1**</sup>** mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>** ****[1 mark]**
> 
> - The forward reaction is **not feasible** at 298 K **[1 mark]**
> - Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> is negative (< 0), so the reaction is not thermodynamically spontaneous / is thermodynamically unfavourable **[1 mark]**
> 
> Allow error carried forward from an incorrect *K*<sub>p</sub> in part (b)
> 
> Allow error carried forward from an incorrect Δ*S*<sub>total</sub>, provided the conclusion follows correctly from the sign of the candidate's value
> 
> Allow error carried forward from an incorrect Δ*S*<sub>total</sub>, provided the stated reason is consistent with the candidate's sign

> **[exam-tip]**
> The relationship Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = *R* ln *K* directly links equilibrium constants to total entropy changes and is examinable at WCH14. It works in both directions:
> 
> - *K* > 1
> 
>   - → ln *K* > 0
>   - → Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> > 0
>   - The reaction is feasible
> - *K* < 1
> 
>   - → ln *K* < 0
>   - → Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> < 0
>   - The reaction is not feasible
> - *K* = 1
> 
>   - → ln *K* = 0
>   - → Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = 0
>   - This is the exact feasibility threshold
> 
> Make sure to use the correct units as *R* is in J K<sup>−1</sup> mol<sup>−1</sup>, so Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> calculated this way is in J K<sup>−1</sup> mol<sup>−1</sup>, not kJ. Examiners note this as a common mistake.
> 
> Even though this reaction has a positive Δ*S*<sub>system</sub> (1 mol of gas → 2 mol of gas increases disorder in the system), it is endothermic, so Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> is strongly negative at 298 K and outweighs the positive Δ*S*<sub>system</sub>, giving a negative overall Δ*S*<sub>total</sub>. The forward reaction only becomes feasible at higher temperatures, where the magnitude of Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> shrinks and Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> eventually flips positive.

### 4() — 2 marks
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The value of *K*<sub>p</sub> for this equilibrium **increases** because:
> 
> - Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> / −$\frac{ΔH}{T}$ becomes less negative as temperature increases
> **OR**
> −$\frac{ΔH}{T}$ increases **[1 mark]**
> - Therefore Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> becomes more positive, increasing the value of ln*K*<sub>*p*</sub> and *K*<sub>*p*</sub>
> **OR**
> Therefore Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> increases, and since Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = *R*ln*K*<sub>*p*</sub>, *K*<sub>*p*</sub> must increase **[1 mark]**
> 
> Answers using Le Chatelier-based explanations are not accepted, and gain zero marks

> **[exam-tip]**
> When an examiner asks for an **entropy-based** explanation of how temperature affects *K*, the mark scheme does not accept Le Chatelier reasoning. You must use Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> = −Δ*H*/*T* and the relationship Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = *R* ln *K*.
> 
> For an endothermic forward reaction (Δ*H* > 0):
> 
> - Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> is always negative (heat taken from surroundings reduces their disorder)
> - As *T* increases, Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> becomes less negative
> - Hence Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> increases → ln *K* increases → *K* increases
> 
> For an exothermic forward reaction, the opposite applies: raising *T* makes Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> less positive, so *K* decreases.
> 
> At IAL, Δ*S*<sub>system</sub> is treated as temperature-independent. Do not suggest it changes significantly with temperature.
> 
> Examiner reports stress: stick to entropy language, name Δ*S*<sub>surroundings</sub> explicitly, and reference Δ*S*<sub>total</sub> = *R* ln *K*.
> 
> Le Chatelier-based answers lose full marks on this part.
