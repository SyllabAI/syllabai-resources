# Mark Schemes — Identification of Ions & Gases
**12. Experimental Techniques & Chemical Analysis** · Cambridge (CIE) IGCSE science (0653)


## Exam Questions (3 questions)


### Q1
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - (i) Hydrogen, H<sub>2</sub> **[1 mark]**
> - (ii) Fizzes more / solid disappears more quickly **[1 mark]**
> - (iii) Zinc (Zn<sup>2+</sup>) or calcium (Ca<sup>2+</sup>) **[1 mark]**
> 
> Accept a quicker rate of bubbles / more effervescence

> **[exam-tip]**
> A white precipitate with sodium hydroxide doesn't distinguish between Zn<sup>2+</sup> and Ca<sup>2+</sup> on its own — you'd need an extra test (excess NaOH dissolves the zinc precipitate but not the calcium one) to tell them apart, which is why both are accepted here.

### Q2
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - Test: lighted splint; observation: squeaky pop **[1 mark]**
> - Gas: hydrogen **[1 mark]**
> 
> Accept H<sub>2</sub>

> **[exam-tip]**
> The "squeaky pop" test is unique to hydrogen — no other common gas gives this exact result with a lighted splint.

### Q3
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - (i) The atom loses two electrons **[1 mark]**
> - (ii) Green **[1 mark]**
> - (ii) A precipitate is formed **[1 mark]**
> 
> Ignore any reference to turning brown

> **[exam-tip]**
> Iron(II) hydroxide precipitate is green and can slowly turn brown on standing in air — but this question only asks about the immediate observation.
