# Mark Schemes — Chemical Change & Rate of Reaction
**6. Chemical Reactions** · Cambridge (CIE) IGCSE science (0653)


## Exam Questions (5 questions)


### Q1
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - (i) 10 (s) and 57 (s) **[1 mark each]**
> - (ii) Independent variable: volume of gas collected; dependent variable: time taken **[1 mark]**
> - (iii) Axes correct way round, labelled with quantity and unit; suitable linear scales; points plotted correctly **[3 marks]**
> - (iv) Line of best fit drawn **[1 mark]**
> - (v) As the volume of gas collected increases, the time taken increases (or reverse) **[1 mark]**
> - (vi) A source of error, e.g. difficulty watching both volume and stop-watch at once, or delay starting timing **[1 mark]**
> - (vii) A way to improve accuracy, e.g. use a gas syringe **[1 mark]**
> 
> Accept quicker rate of bubbles/more effervescence; x-axis time/s, y-axis volume of gas/cm<sup>3</sup>; ecf from (b)(i); accept ± ½ small square; ecf from (b)(iii)

> **[exam-tip]**
> Reading stop-watch displays to the nearest second is a common source of small errors — always double-check whether the display shows minutes and seconds separately before recording your value.

### Q2

> **[exam-tip]**

### Q3

> - (i) Water **[1 mark]**
> - (ii) Frequency of collisions increases **[1 mark]**

### Q4
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - (i) A new substance is made **[1 mark]**
> - (ii) Test: lighted splint; result: burns with a squeaky pop **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> "A new substance formed" is the key phrase examiners look for when justifying a chemical (not physical) change — a colour or state change alone isn't enough.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - A substance that increases the rate of a reaction **[1 mark]**
> - Is unchanged at the end of the reaction **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Both parts of the definition are needed for full marks — stating only "speeds up a reaction" without "unchanged at the end" will lose a mark.

### Q5
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

- A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy
- This means more colliding particles have sufficient energy to react, increasing the rate of reaction

**A** is incorrect because a catalyst does not change the energy of the particles in the reaction mixture

**B** is incorrect because a catalyst reduces, not increases, the minimum energy particles need to react

**C** is incorrect because a catalyst does not change the energy of the particles in the reaction mixture
