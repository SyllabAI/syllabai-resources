# Mark Schemes — Electrochemistry
**4. Electrochemistry** · Cambridge (CIE) IGCSE science (0653)


## Exam Questions (2 questions)


### Q1
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - The positive electrode is called the anode **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> A quick way to remember which electrode is which: the anode is the positive electrode, while the cathode is negative.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> - Positive electrode: chlorine, C*l*<sub>2</sub> **[1 mark]**
> - Negative electrode: hydrogen, H<sub>2</sub> **[1 mark]**
> 
> Ignore chloride

> **[exam-tip]**
> Don't confuse the chlorine gas (C*l*<sub>2</sub>) produced at the anode with chloride ions (C*l*<sup>-</sup>) still in solution — only the gas product should be named here.

### Q2
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- At the anode (positive electrode), Br<sup>-</sup> ions are oxidised to form bromine, Br<sub>2</sub>
- At the cathode (negative electrode), K<sup>+</sup> ions are reduced to form potassium metal

**A** is incorrect because it shows ions, not the elements, being produced at each electrode

**C** is incorrect because it reverses the products - potassium forms at the cathode, not the anode, and bromine forms at the anode, not the cathode

**D** is incorrect because it shows ions, not the elements, being produced at each electrode
