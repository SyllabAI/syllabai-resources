# Mark Schemes — Section A: Directed Writing
**Paper 2: Writing** · Cambridge (CIE) IGCSE english-language (0500 & 0990)


## November 2023 (0500/21) Section A (1 questions)


### Q1
### Model answer

Good evening, and thank you for listening.

We are here to decide whether our school’s old building should be demolished or renewed. At first, the case for demolition seems strong: repairs are costly; the wiring and plumbing are unreliable; the foundations are weakening. Surely, a brand-new, environmentally friendly building would serve us better?

Yet we must question these claims. How “green” is demolition when the act of tearing down and rebuilding releases huge amounts of carbon? A new building might take decades to balance the environmental cost of its construction. We are told that modern designs improve productivity and health, but can bricks and glass alone transform our education? Is it not teachers, resources and relationships that shape learning?

Equally, the arguments for reuse should not go unchallenged. Old buildings connect us to our past and give character to our community, but restoration can be expensive. Adaptive reuse sometimes keeps only the façade, hollowing out the inside until little of the original remains. Is that really preservation, or a kind of deception?

So where does that leave us? If we look at cost, environment and identity together, a more balanced answer appears. Total demolition is wasteful, yet blind preservation may be unrealistic. The best choice is to adapt with imagination: strengthen the structure where needed, integrate green technology, and keep what makes the building distinctive. This approach preserves continuity, protects the environment and provides us with the modern facilities we require.

If we recycle bottles, clothes and even food waste, why not our buildings too? Why destroy a piece of our history when it could be reshaped into a foundation for the future? Progress is not about sweeping aside the past: it is about transforming it into something better.

Thank you.

> ### Commentary
> 
> The speech demonstrates a mature and sophisticated understanding of the debate. It evaluates the case for demolition, acknowledging the appeal of modern, “green” buildings that promise efficiency and healthier environments, but also challenges the assumption that new construction is automatically sustainable. Similarly, it addresses the arguments for preservation, recognising the emotional and historical value of older buildings, while questioning whether adaptive reuse is always authentic or sometimes a superficial gesture. This balance reveals a thoughtful engagement with both explicit and implicit ideas.
