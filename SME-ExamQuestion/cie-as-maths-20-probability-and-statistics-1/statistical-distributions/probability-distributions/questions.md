### Q1 — hard — 6 marks

**Q1** [2 marks]

The random variable $X$ takes the values −3, −1, 1 and 3 only.

Show that

$\text{P} ( X = x ) = \frac{1}{3x^{2}}$ for $x = - 3 ,  - 1$

$\text{P} ( X = x ) = \frac{1}{3x^{3}}$ for $x = 1 ,  3$

cannot be the probability distribution of $X$.

**Q1** [2 marks]

It is given instead that

$\text{P} ( X = x ) = \frac{k}{x^{2}}$ for $x = - 3 ,  - 1$

$\text{P} ( X = x ) = \frac{k}{x^{3}}$ for $x = 1 ,  3$

where $k$ is a constant.

Find the exact value of $k$.

**Q1** [2 marks]

Find $\text{P} ( X < 2 )$.

### Q2 — hard — 9 marks

**Q2** [4 marks]

Leofranc plays a game at a school fair. It costs 2 dollars to play. He chooses a "lucky number" from 1 to 6, then rolls a fair six-sided dice until either his lucky number appears or he has rolled the dice four times.

If his lucky number appears on the first roll, he receives 5 dollars. If it first appears on the second, third or fourth roll, he receives 3 dollars, 2 dollars or 1 dollar respectively. If it has not appeared by the fourth roll, he receives nothing.

The random variable $W$ is Leofranc's profit, in dollars, from one game (the amount he receives minus the cost of playing). A negative profit means that he loses money.

Draw up the probability distribution table for $W$.

**Q2** [3 marks]

Find the probability that, in one game, Leofranc

(i) wins money,

(ii) loses money,

(iii) breaks even (neither wins nor loses money).

**Q2** [2 marks]

Find Leofranc's expected profit from one game.
