## Exam Questions (22 questions)


### Q1 — hard — 10 marks

**Q1** [2 marks]

In a game, Ravi throws five fair coins at the same time. He wins a point if he obtains at least 4 heads.

Show that the probability that Ravi wins a point on any throw of the five coins is $\frac{3}{16}$.

**Q1** [2 marks]

Find the probability that Ravi wins a point for the first time on his 5th throw of the five coins.

**Q1** [5 marks]

Ravi throws the five coins on 120 occasions.

Use an approximation to find the probability that he wins a point on more than 25 of these occasions.

**Q1** [1 mark]

Justify the use of your approximation in part (c).

### Q2 — hard — 12 marks

**Q2** [4 marks]

A farm grows two varieties of cucumber, A and B.

The lengths of cucumbers of variety A are normally distributed with mean 30.0 cm and standard deviation 2.5 cm.

In a random sample of 400 cucumbers of variety A, how many would you expect to have a length between 27.0 cm and 33.5 cm?

**Q2** [3 marks]

The lengths of cucumbers of variety B are normally distributed with mean 28.0 cm and standard deviation $σ$ cm. It is found that 15% of cucumbers of variety B have a length less than 25.0 cm.

Find the value of $σ$.

**Q2** [5 marks]

A random sample of 90 cucumbers of variety B is chosen.

Use a suitable approximation to find the probability that fewer than 10 of these 90 cucumbers have a length less than 25.0 cm.

### Q3 — easy — 5 marks

**Q3** [4 marks]

For each of the following random variables, state whether it can be modelled by a binomial distribution, a normal distribution or neither.

(i) A farmer has many hens. The random variable $A$ is the mass of a randomly selected hen.

(ii) A fair coin is flipped 100 times. The random variable $B$ is the number of times it lands on tails.

(iii) A teacher has a 30-minute break for lunch. The random variable $C$ is the number of emails he receives during his lunch break.

(iv) In a class of 30 students, each student rolls a fair six-sided dice with sides labelled 1 to 6. The random variable $D$ is the number of students who roll a number less than 5.

**Q3** [1 mark]

The digits 1 to 9 are written on individual counters and placed in a bag. A child randomly selects one of the nine counters at a time and replaces it after reading the number. The random variable $X$ is the number of times the child selects a counter until the '5' is selected.

Write down the name of the probability distribution of $X$.

### Q4 — easy — 6 marks

**Q4** [2 marks]

In an experiment there are a fixed number of trials and each trial results in a success or failure. Let $X$ be the number of successful trials.

Write down the two other conditions that would need to be present to make $X$ follow a binomial distribution.

**Q4** [3 marks]

A fair spinner has 8 sectors labelled with the numbers 1 through 8. For each of the following cases, give a reason to explain why a binomial distribution would **not** be appropriate for modelling the specified random variable.

(i) The random variable $A$ is the number of times the spinner is spun until it lands on '1' for the first time.

(ii) When the spinner is spun it rotates exactly 115°. The random variable $B$ is the number of times the spinner lands on '1' when the spinner is spun 20 times.

(iii) The random variable $C$ is the sector number that the spinner lands on when it is spun once.

**Q4** [1 mark]

State which one of the random variables defined in part (b) follows a geometric distribution.

### Q7 — easy — 6 marks

**Q7** [1 mark]

State the conditions which $n$ and $p$ must satisfy for this approximation to be used.

**Q7** [5 marks]

(i) `X \sim \text{B}(6, \; 0.45)`

(ii) `X \sim \text{B}(60, \; 0.05)`

(iii) `X \sim \text{B}(60, \; 0.45)`

### Q8 — easy — 6 marks
