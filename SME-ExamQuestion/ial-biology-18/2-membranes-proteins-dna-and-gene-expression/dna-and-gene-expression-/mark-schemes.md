### 5() — 1 marks
**Correct answer: B** — Half the DNA molecules contain one <sup>15</sup>N strand and one <sup>14</sup>N strand, and half contain only <sup>14</sup>N

**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B.**

- Round 1: the original <sup>15</sup>N<sup>15</sup>N molecule separates and each strand acts as a template, producing two hybrid <sup>15</sup>N<sup>14</sup>N molecules
- Round 2: each of the two hybrid molecules separates

  - The <sup>15</sup>N strand from each acts as a template, producing two hybrid <sup>15</sup>N<sup>14</sup>N molecules
  - The <sup>14</sup>N strand from each acts as a template, producing two <sup>14</sup>N<sup>14</sup>N molecules
- Therefore two of the resulting four molecules are hybrid <sup>15</sup>N<sup>14</sup>N and two contain only <sup>14</sup>N

**A** is incorrect because after two rounds of semi-conservative replication, the <sup>14</sup>N strands produced in round 1 act as templates in round 2, producing molecules made entirely from <sup>14</sup>N; so not all molecules can be hybrid.

**C** is incorrect because the original <sup>15</sup>N strands are retained throughout replication; each acts as a template in round 1, producing hybrid molecules, and these <sup>15</sup>N strands are still present in two of the four molecules after round 2, so not all molecules can contain only <sup>14</sup>N.

**D** is incorrect because after two rounds of semi-conservative replication, the original <sup>15</sup>N strands are not preserved intact in separate molecules; they each act as templates, meaning no molecule contains only <sup>15</sup>N after transfer to the <sup>14</sup>N medium.
