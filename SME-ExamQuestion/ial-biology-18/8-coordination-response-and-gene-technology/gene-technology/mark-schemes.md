### 3() — 4 marks
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Any **three** from:
> 
> Similarities:
> 
> - Both use endonucleases to cut DNA
> - Both target specific DNA sequences
> 
> Differences (maximum of **three**):
> 
> - Traditional GM uses restriction endonucleases **WHILE** CRISPR-Cas9 uses the Cas9 endonuclease
> - In traditional GM restriction endonucleases recognise specific base sequences directly **WHILE** in CRISPR-Cas9 the guide RNA directs Cas9 to the target sequence by complementary base pairing

> **[4 marks]**

> When asked to compare, you must make explicit comparisons, not just describe each technique separately. Use linking words such as "while", "whereas" or "both" to make it clear you are comparing. You can gain marks for similarities as well as differences.

### 6() — 1 marks
To calculate the mean change in haemoglobin concentration for the treatment group between baseline and 12 months:

- Calculate the change for each patient:

  - Patient 1: 94 − 72 = +22
  - Patient 2: 89 − 68 = +21
  - Patient 3: 102 − 81 = +21
  - Patient 4: 98 − 75 = +23
  - Patient 5: 91 − 69 = +22
  - Patient 6: 105 − 83 = +22
  - Patient 7: 87 − 71 = +16
  - Patient 8: 73 − 78 = −5
- Sum the changes and divide by the number of patients:

= (22 + 21 + 21 + 23 + 22 + 22 + 16 − 5) ÷ 8

= 142 ÷ 8

= 17.75

- Express to 2 significant figures:

**=**** ****18**** ****g/L** **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> Take care to include all eight patients in your calculation, including any patients whose haemoglobin concentration decreased over the period. Ignoring a decrease will give an incorrect mean.
> 
> Make sure you round your final answer to 2 significant figures — 17.75 rounded to 2 significant figures is 18, not 17.8 or 17.75.

### 7() — 4 marks
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Arguments supporting the use of CRISPR (maximum of **three**):
> 
> - Table 1 shows that 7 out of 8 treatment group patients showed an increase in haemoglobin concentration at 12 months / mean increase of 18 g/L
> - CRISPR gene therapy could provide a lasting cure **WHILE** current treatments only manage symptoms and require lifelong medical care
> - No off-target editing events / no unintended edits to other genes were detected in the study

> Arguments against the use of CRISPR (maximum of **three**):
> 
> - The sample size is small (n=8 treatment, n=10 control), limiting the reliability of conclusions
> - The follow-up period of 12 months is relatively short **SO** long-term safety data are not yet available
> - Patient 8 showed no improvement / a decrease in haemoglobin concentration, suggesting the treatment is not effective for all patients
> - Off-target editing could introduce mutations that disrupt the function of important proteins / enzymes / receptors / proteins involved in cell cycle regulation
