Comments on the effect of red and far-red light on the germination of these seeds and the processes that occur in their cells could include...

*Indicative content relating to description of results*

- Red light exposure has little effect on germination when exposure to far-red light lasts for 1 second
- Increased red light exposure decreases the percentage germination as far-red light exposure increases
- As far-red exposure increases from 1 to 30/60 seconds the percentage germination shows a downward trend (at all levels of red light exposure)
- As far-red light exposure increases from 30/60 seconds to 960 seconds there is a slight increase in percentage germination (at most levels of red light exposure)

*Indicative content relating to description of named responses*

- Light can affect growth and development in plants
- The effect of light on growth and development is known as photomorphogenesis
- The response to day/night length is known as photoperiodism

*Indicative content relating to the chemical nature of responses*

- Responses to light are regulated by phytochromes
- Red light initiates seed germination / metabolic activity in embryos
- Far-red light prevents seed germination / inhibits metabolic activity in embryos
- Phytochrome exists in two forms; inactive P<sub>R</sub> and active P<sub>FR</sub>
- P<sub>R</sub> absorbs red light / light with wavelength 660 nm
- P<sub>FR</sub> absorbs far-red light / light with wavelength 730 nm
- When P<sub>R</sub> absorbs red light it is converted into P<sub>FR</sub>
- When P<sub>FR</sub> absorbs far-red light it is converted back into P<sub>R</sub>

*Indicative content relating to the detailed mechanism of responses*

- Phytochrome acts as a transcription factor
- P<sub>FR</sub> can enter the nucleus
- P<sub>FR</sub> binds to PIF3 proteins
- P<sub>FR</sub> (together with bound PIF3) binds to DNA and initiates transcription/gene expression
- Transcription leads to production of enzymes that lead to production of gibberellins
- Gibberellins stimulate germination / cause production of amylase in seeds

> *This is a broad question that asks for comments on the effect of red and far-red light on **these seeds** and on **seed germination in general**, as well as references to the **details of processes taking place at the cellular level**. As ever in level-marked questions you should aim to cover as **many different areas as possible **in your answer.*

## Q3 — medium — 8 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
> **[mark-scheme]**
> Any **two** from:
> 
> - Auxin accumulates on the shaded side / is transported down the dark side and less on the bright side **[1 mark]**
> - Auxin stimulates cell elongation on the shaded side **[1 mark]**
> - The shoot grows towards the light / positive phototropism occurs **[1 mark]**
> 
> Accept "IAA" for auxin
> 
> Accept detail of mechanism, e.g. auxin stimulates transport of H<sup>+</sup> ions into cell wall and decreases pH **OR** decreased pH activates expansins / disrupts hydrogen cross-links in the cell wall
> 
> Reject "bend towards light"

> **[exam-tip]**
> Make sure that you describe the full sequence of events rather than jumping straight to the conclusion. Begin with the redistribution of auxin to the shaded side, then explain the effect this has on cell elongation, before stating that the shoot grows towards the light. Note that you must state that the shoot "grows towards" the light — "bends towards" will not be credited.
> 
> For a higher-level answer, you can include the molecular mechanism by which auxin causes cell elongation, such as the activation of proton pumps, the lowering of cell wall pH, and the activation of expansins.

### 3() — 2 marks
To calculate the mean rate of root curvature:

rate = change in curvature ÷ time interval

- Read values from the graph:

  - curvature at 2 hours = 15°
  - curvature at 6 hours = 70°
- Calculate the change in curvature:

70 − 15 = 55

- Calculate the time interval:

6 − 2 = 4 hours

- Calculate the rate:

= 55 ÷ 4 **[1 mark]**

= **13.75 **degrees per hour **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Award full marks for the correct answer in the absence of working
> 
> Accept values within a reasonable range of the graph readings for curvature
> 
> If no marks are awarded for the calculations, then award 1 mark for evidence of the correct rate equation

> **[exam-tip]**
> When calculating a rate of change from a graph, read the values at the two specified time points carefully, calculate the change in the y-axis variable, and divide by the change in time.

> **[exam-tip]**
> A common mistake in gibberellin questions is to state that gibberellin itself breaks down starch — gibberellin is a hormone, not an enzyme. To access full marks, you must make clear that gibberellin initiates a sequence of gene expression events that leads to the production of amylase, and that it is the amylase enzyme that then carries out the hydrolysis of starch.
