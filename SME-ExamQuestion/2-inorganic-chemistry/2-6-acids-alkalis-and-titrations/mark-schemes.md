### 2 — 2 marks
> **[mark-scheme]**
> i) The apparatus the student should use to measure 25.0 cm<sup>3</sup> is:
> 
> - **C** / volumetric pipette** [1 mark]**
> 
> ii) The best indicator to use is:
> 
> - **B** / methyl orange** [1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> For a titration, you need to measure one volume precisely and deliver another volume precisely.
> 
> - A **(volumetric) pipette (C)** is used to accurately measure a fixed volume of one of the solutions to put into the conical flask.
> - A **burette** (not pictured) is used to accurately measure the variable volume of the other solution that is added.
> 
> A measuring cylinder (**D**) or beaker (**B**) are not accurate enough for titration measurements.
> 
> You should be familiar with two titration indicators:
> 
> 1. Methyl orange
> 2. Phenolphthalein
> 
> Litmus is a two-colour indicator, but it is not suitable for a titration as it does not give a sharp endpoint.
> 
> Universal indicator gives a range of colours, but it is not suitable for titrations.
> 
> Iodine solution is an indicator for starch, not for acids and bases.

### 2 — 4 marks
i) The balanced equation for the reaction between H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> and NaOH is:

**H**<sub>**2**</sub>**SO**<sub>**4**</sub>** + 2NaOH → Na**<sub>**2**</sub>**SO**<sub>**4**</sub>** + 2H**<sub>**2**</sub>**O** **[1 mark]**

ii) To calculate the amount of sodium hydroxide in 25.0 cm<sup>3</sup>:

moles= conc x vol in dm<sup>3</sup>

moles of NaOH = 0.80 x 25.0/1000

moles of NaOH = **0.02 mol** **[1 mark]**

iii) To calculate the amount of sulfuric acid in 13.50 cm<sup>3</sup>:

From the equation, the reacting ratio = H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>: 2NaOH

moles of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> = $\frac{\mathrm{moles} \mathrm{of} \mathrm{NaOH}}{2}$

moles of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> = $\frac{0.02}{2}$

moles of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> = **0.01 mol** **[1 mark]**

iv) To calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol/dm<sup>3</sup>:

concentration = $\frac{\mathrm{moles}}{\mathrm{vol} \mathrm{in} \mathrm{dm}^{3}}$

concentration of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> = $\frac{0.01}{0.0135}$

concentration of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> = **0.74 mol/dm**<sup>**3**</sup> **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Allow error carried forward throughout all steps.
> 
> For example, if you didn't get the equation correct in part i), you can still pick up all the other marks as long as you used your ratio correctly.

> **[exam-tip]**
> This is a standard titration calculation. Examiner reports consistently show that the most common mistake is forgetting to use the **mole ratio** from the balanced equation.
> 
> In this case, the ratio is 1:2. This means you need **half** the number of moles of H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> as you have of NaOH.
> 
> A frequent error is to multiply by 2 instead of dividing. Always write down the ratio and double-check which way you need to use it.
