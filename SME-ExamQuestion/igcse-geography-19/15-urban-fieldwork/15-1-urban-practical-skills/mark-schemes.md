## Q7 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
The students could have used data from the local council's deprivation index for Millbrook **[1]** to compare official measures of environmental and social deprivation with the EQS scores they recorded at each site, helping them to assess whether their primary data reflected wider patterns of urban inequality across the town. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means a named secondary source alone is not sufficient. The answer must show how that source would support the enquiry.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a specific and plausible secondary data source
> - **One mark **for explaining how it would support the enquiry into environmental quality change
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Historic maps or aerial photographs showing urban change over time
> - Census data on housing conditions or population density by area
> - Local authority planning documents on regeneration schemes
> - Newspaper articles or local reports on environmental improvement schemes in Millbrook
> 
> No credit for vague sources such as 'the internet' or 'a website' without further specification.
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Naming a secondary source without explaining how it links to the enquiry focus on environmental quality
> - Describing primary data collection instead of secondary data
> - Using Figure 1 as the secondary source (Figure 1 represents primary fieldwork data)

## Q8 — easy — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 3 marks
One risk was personal safety when collecting data in the town centre and inner city areas of Millbrook, where students may have attracted unwanted attention while completing surveys on busy streets. **[1]**

The students could have managed this by working in small groups rather than individually at all times. **[1]** They could also have avoided recording data in isolated or poorly lit locations and ensured that all group members knew the planned route and the contact details of a supervising adult before setting out. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means the answer needs more than naming a risk. The answer must show how it was or could have been managed.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a valid risk linked to urban fieldwork context
> - **One mark** for explaining one way of managing that risk
> - **One further mark** for developing the management strategy
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> **Risks**
> 
> - Traffic when crossing roads between sites along the transect
> - Uneven or slippery pavements in the inner city or CBD
> - Adverse weather conditions during outdoor data collection
> - Conflict or confrontation when approaching members of the public
> 
> **Management strategies**
> 
> - Completing a risk assessment before the visit
> - Wearing high-visibility clothing near roads
> - Informing a supervising adult of the route and timing
> - Avoiding isolated or poorly lit locations
> - Carrying a charged mobile phone at all times
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Naming a risk without explaining how it was managed
> - Giving a rural risk such as uneven moorland that has no clear link to an urban fieldwork context
> - Describing the risk at length without developing the management strategy
