## Q7 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
Figure 1 shows a clear negative correlation between the two variables: as the environmental quality score increases, the percentage of buildings in poor repair decreases. **[1]** For example, the town centre (Site 1) has the lowest EQS score of 7 and the highest percentage of buildings in poor repair at 38%, while the rural fringe (Site 5) has the highest EQS score of 19 and only 2% of buildings in poor repair. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**describe**' means the answer should state what the data shows. No explanation of why the relationship exists is required.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for correctly identifying the overall relationship (negative correlation: as EQS score increases, percentage of buildings in poor repair decreases)
> - **One mark** for supporting this with specific data from Figure 1
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Attempting to explain why the relationship exists rather than describing what it shows
> - Describing only one site rather than the overall pattern across all five sites
> - Stating there is no relationship when a clear negative correlation is visible in the data

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Name **the type of relationship first (positive, negative, or no correlation), then back it up immediately with data from the table.
> 
> **Always **quote both variables together, for example EQS score and building condition, rather than describing each column separately. That paired approach is what earns the second mark.

## Q9 — medium — 4 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 4 marks
One advantage of a line graph is that it clearly shows the trend in environmental quality scores as distance from the town centre increases. **[1]** For example, the steady rise from a score of 7 at Site 1 to 19 at Site 5 would appear as a clear upward line, making the spatial pattern across Millbrook immediately visible to the reader. **[1]**

However, one disadvantage is that a line graph implies continuous change between the five sites, suggesting that environmental quality increases smoothly and evenly across the town. **[1]** In reality, the data was only collected at five specific points, so the line between those points is an assumption rather than a measured fact, which could be misleading. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means each advantage and disadvantage must be developed with a reason rather than simply stated.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for a valid advantage of a line graph
> - **One mark** for development of that advantage with reference to Figure 1
> - **One mark** for a valid disadvantage of a line graph
> - **One mark **for development of that disadvantage
> 
> **[4 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> **Advantages:**
> 
> - Clearly shows trends and patterns across the transect
> - Allows the reader to see the direction and rate of change between sites
> - Effective for showing data collected at points along a continuous transect
> - Easy to read and interpret at a glance
> 
> **Disadvantages **
> 
> - Implies continuous change between data points that were not actually measured
> - Cannot show two variables (EQS score and building condition) on the same axis without a dual axis, which can be confusing
> - Does not show the location type of each site without additional labelling
> - A scatter graph might be more appropriate for showing the relationship between EQS score and building condition
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Stating an advantage or disadvantage without any development
> - Discussing a different graph type rather than focusing on line graphs, as the question requires
> - Writing that a line graph 'looks good' or 'is easy to draw' without any geographical justification

## Q10 — hard — 8 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 8 marks
The primary data I collected provided a useful starting point for my enquiry, but its reliability had clear limitations. **[U]**

I used an environmental quality survey (EQS) to score each site along my transect out of 20 across a range of criteria, including air quality, noise levels, and the condition of buildings. **[K]** This method produced quantitative data that was straightforward to compare between sites and to present as a line graph. **[U]**

However, the EQS relied heavily on personal judgement, particularly for criteria such as air quality, which is difficult to assess accurately by observation alone. **[Ap]** This subjectivity reduced the reliability of the data because different students in the group may have scored the same site differently, making direct comparisons between sites less consistent. **[U]**

I also used a building condition survey as a second primary method, recording the percentage of buildings in a poor state of repair at each site.** [K]** This was more reliable than the EQS because it used a clearer, more objective criterion that was easier to apply consistently across all sites. **[Ap]**

My secondary data came from the local council's environmental quality report for the study area.** [K] **This was a reliable source because it was produced using systematic data collection methods across the whole town rather than at just five points. **[Ap]** However, the report was two years old at the time of my enquiry, which meant it may not have reflected the most recent changes to environmental quality in the area. **[Ap]**

On balance, the secondary data was more reliable than my primary EQS data because it was based on a larger sample and used objective measurement criteria rather than personal judgement. **[Ap]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**evaluate**' means the answer must make judgements about the reliability of the data, not simply describe what was collected. A strong answer will weigh up strengths and limitations and reach a supported conclusion.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a '**level of response**' approach. Each point made does not equal one mark.
> 
> A top-level 3 answer **[7 to 8 marks]** will:
> 
> - **AO1 ****[K]** demonstrate accurate knowledge of primary and secondary data collection methods used in an urban environments enquiry
> - **AO2 ****[U]** show clear understanding of what makes data reliable or unreliable, including sample size, subjectivity, source credibility, and relevance
> - **AO3 ****[Ap]** apply evaluative judgement to their specific enquiry context, reaching a clear and supported conclusion about overall reliability
> 
> **No credit for:**
> 
> - Simply listing data collection methods without any evaluation of their reliability
> - A conclusion that only repeats earlier points without making a judgement
> - Responses that do not refer to both primary and secondary data as the question requires
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question and will depend on the fieldwork. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Structured questionnaires with members of the public as primary data
> - Annotated photographs or field sketches as qualitative primary data
> - Land use surveys along the transect
> - Census data on housing conditions or deprivation as secondary data
> - Local authority regeneration reports or planning documents
> - Historic maps or aerial photographs showing urban change over time
> 
> **Reliability:**
> 
> - Subjectivity of EQS scoring criteria
> - Sample size and representativeness of the transect
> - Observer bias in visual surveys
> - Time of day or weather conditions affecting primary data collection
> - Consistency of data collection between different group members
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Describing the fieldwork process rather than evaluating the reliability of the data collected
> - Focusing entirely on one type of data and neglecting the other
> - Using evaluative language only in the conclusion rather than spreading judgements throughout
> - Writing a conclusion that does not make a clear comparative judgement between primary and secondary data

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Be specific** about your methods. 'I collected data' earns no marks. 'I used an EQS scored out of 20 across six criteria' earns marks because it shows precise knowledge of your own enquiry.
