## Q5 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
They could have chosen sites based on systematic sampling. **[1]** Each site would be placed at equal intervals along the beach to reduce bias and ensure the data covered the full study area. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a** point-marked** approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a valid factor
> - **One mark** for how that factor influenced site selection
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Safety or accessibility: sites with unstable cliffs or restricted access were avoided so that students could reach and leave each location safely
> - Representative: sites are spread at regular intervals along the beach so that any changes in sediment size from west to east could be detected and compared
> - Proximity to a reference point: sites were measured at fixed distances from a groyne or cliff base so that readings could be compared fairly between visits
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Describing data collection methods rather than site selection decisions
> - Discussing a risk and how it was managed

> **[exam-tip]**
> The question asks about **site choice,** not what happened on arrival.
> 
> **Think **about how you would choose a particular spot on the beach and not somewhere else.
> 
> **Your **second sentence should begin with 'because' or 'so that' to earn the development mark.

## Q6 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
One risk is that waves could wash unexpectedly onto the lower beach where students are collecting data. **[1] **The impact could knock a student off their feet, causing them to be dragged into the sea by the backwash and seriously injured. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command words '**identify**' and '**explain**' together mean you must name the risk and then state what harm it could cause. Do not describe how the risk was managed.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a** point-marked** approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a relevant health and safety risk
> - **One mark** for explaining the impact of that risk, not the management of it
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Unstable or crumbling cliff face: falling rocks could strike a student working at the base of the cliff and cause serious injury
> - Slippery seaweed or wet rocks on the beach: a student could slip and fall, causing cuts or broken bones
> - Strong offshore wind: a student could lose their balance, fall and injure themselves on the beach surface
> - Sun exposure during prolonged fieldwork: a student could develop heatstroke or severe sunburn, making them unwell during the fieldwork day
> - Any other appropriate response

> **[exam-tip]**
> **A risk** is something that could go wrong. **Management **is how you tried to prevent it.
> 
> **This** question only wants the risk and its impact. Imagine the worst realistic outcome and describe it clearly.
> 
> **Do not **describe how you stayed safe.
