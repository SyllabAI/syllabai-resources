## Q1 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
Arrange the six values in order from smallest to largest:

*27, 38, 49, 57, 61, 68*

There are 6 values, so the median is the mean of the 3rd and 4th values (49 and 57).

(49 + 57) ÷ 2 = 106 ÷ 2 **[1]**

= **53 mm** **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**calculate**' means you must show your working and give a numerical answer.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a **point-marked** approach.
> 
> - One mark for correct working: ordering the data and showing the calculation (49 + 57) ÷ 2 using the correct middle values
> - One mark for the correct final answer of 53 mm
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> - The answer 53 without the unit mm is acceptable but the unit should be encouraged
> - The working mark requires the **correct method** (identifying the two middle values **and** averaging them). Ordering or selecting the values alone, or using the wrong calculation, does **not** gain the working mark.
> 
> **Do not accept:**
> 
> - The mean of the six values, which gives 50
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Calculating the mean instead of the median. Students must read the command word carefully.
> - Forgetting to reorder the data before selecting the middle values, which leads to the incorrect 3rd and 4th values being used.
> - Selecting only the 3rd value rather than averaging the 3rd and 4th values in an even-numbered data set.

## Q6 — medium — 4 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 1 marks
Site 4 with a value of 61 mm, which is higher than Site 3. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**identify**' means you only need to state the value. No explanation is required here.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a** point-marked** approach.
> 
> - **One mark** correctly identifying 61 mm at Site 4 as the anomalous result
> 
> **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> **An anomaly** is a result that does not fit the pattern of the other values. The general trend in this data is that pebble size decreases from west to east. Look for the value that breaks that trend. One sentence is all you need here.

### 2() — 3 marks
A groyne near Site 4 could have contributed to the anomalous result. **[1]** Groynes are built perpendicular to the beach to interrupt longshore drift, and they trap sediment on their updrift side. **[1]** This means larger pebbles that would normally have been transported eastward by longshore drift were held back at Site 4, producing a higher median pebble length than expected at that point along the beach. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**suggest**' means you must apply your knowledge to give a possible reason, even if it cannot be proved with certainty.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a** point-marked** approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a valid reason for the anomaly
> - **One mark** for development of that reason
> - **One mark** for further development that links back to why the pebble length at Site 4 is larger than expected
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Human error in pebble selection
> - Recording error
> - A recent storm depositing coarser material at Site 4

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Don't **be tempted to explain longshore drift rather than explaining why one specific site has a higher value than expected.

## Q7 — hard — 8 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 8 marks
'How does sediment size change along Chesil Beach from west to east?'

I concluded that sediment size decreases from west to east because longshore drift transports and sorts material, with smaller pebbles carried furthest by south-westerly waves. **[K]** Generally, my data supports this conclusion, but some sites' accuracy and reliability issues limit my confidence. **[Ap]**

A ruler was used to measure the long axis of ten pebbles at each site for my main data collection. **[K]** This simple method is error-prone. Different students may have measured the pebble differently, picking the longest or widest axis. **[Ap]** Inconsistent technique can distort pebble length measurements. **[Ap]**

Instead of picking pebbles by hand, we used systematic sampling to improve reliability. **[K] **This reduced the risk of bias, where students unconsciously chose larger or more interesting pebbles. **[Ap]** However, ten pebbles per site is a small sample, and using twenty would have produced a more reliable median. **[Ap]**

Site 4's anomaly slightly influenced my conclusion. **[K]** I identified it as possibly caused by a nearby groyne trapping sediment, confirming the trend rather than poor data collection. **[Ap]**

My conclusion was strengthened by secondary data from the British Geological Survey that confirmed the eastern end of the beach has much finer sediment than the western end. **[Ap]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**evaluate**' means the answer must consider the strengths and limitations of the enquiry across more than one stage of the enquiry process and must reach a clear overall judgement.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a level of response approach. Each point made in the answer does not equal a mark.
> 
> A top-level 3 answer **[8 marks]** will include specific examples that show:
> 
> - **AO1**: **[K]**  knowledge about coastal processes, fieldwork methods and the enquiry context
> - **AO3**: **[Ap]** application of knowledge to evaluate the accuracy and reliability of data and to assess the validity of the conclusion
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question and will depend on the example used. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Assessing whether the sampling strategy produced a representative sediment sample at each site
> - Evaluation if enough sites were available to draw reliable conclusions about the beach
> - Use of OS maps, BGS records, and Environment Agency coastal monitoring data to support or challenge primary data
> - Decisions on data presentation and whether graphs showed sediment size trends
> - How recent storm activity or unusually calm weather may have affected sediment distribution on the day

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Know **the difference between accuracy and reliability.
> 
> - **Accuracy** asks: are my results close to the true value?
> - **Reliability **asks: if I repeated this, would I get the same results?
> - Use both words correctly and link them to specific parts of your own enquiry.
> 
> **A clear** overall judgement at the end is essential for Level 3. It does not need to be long. One sentence that directly answers whether and why your conclusion was accurate and reliable.
