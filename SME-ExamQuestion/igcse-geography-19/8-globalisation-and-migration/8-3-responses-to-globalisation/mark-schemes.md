## Q7 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
Countries that have strong diplomatic relationships, such as those within trade blocs like the European Union, often remove tariffs and trade barriers between member states.** [1]** This makes it cheaper and easier to import and export goods between member countries, which increases the volume of trade and strengthens economic ties between them. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**describe**' means the answer should state what the relationship looks like and how it affects trade. A full explanation of causes is not required, but a supporting detail is needed for the second mark.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a valid way that geopolitical relationships affect trade
> - **One mark** for developing that point with a supporting detail or example
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Historical colonial relationships creating continued patterns of trade between former colonies and colonial powers
> - Political conflict or sanctions restricting or blocking trade between countries
> - Shared language and cultural ties making trade negotiations easier
> - Diplomatic agreements such as free trade deals opening up new markets
> - Geopolitical tension leading to trade wars and the imposition of tariffs
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Describing the impact of trade on geopolitical relationships rather than the other way around
> - Writing about migration or tourism rather than focusing on trade as the question requires
> - Naming a trade bloc without explaining how it affects trade

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Geopolitical **relationships are about how countries get along politically and diplomatically.
> 
> **Consider** what happens to trade when countries are friendly (trade blocs, free trade deals) versus when they are in conflict (sanctions, tariffs, trade wars).
> 
> **Either **direction gives you a valid answer, as long as you link the political relationship directly to its effect on trade.

## Q8 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
One strategy used by many developed countries is a points-based immigration system, which awards points to applicants based on factors such as qualifications, language skills, and work experience. **[1]**

This allows the government to control the number and type of migrants entering the country, prioritising those who are most likely to contribute to the economy while limiting overall migration numbers. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**

> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark **for identifying a valid migration management strategy linked to a named or implied country context
> - **One mark** for explaining how that strategy manages migration
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Border controls and visa requirements restricting entry
> - Amnesty programmes regularising the status of undocumented migrants already present
> - Integration programmes helping migrants learn the host language and find employment
> - Bilateral agreements between source and destination countries managing migration flows
> - Refugee resettlement programmes offering legal pathways for forced migrants

## Q14 — hard — 12 marks · exam-questions

### 8((f)) — 12 marks
'Globalisation' is the process of the world becoming more interconnected, and the 'global economy' refers to how countries are linked through the production and consumption of goods and services. **[U]** While trade is a key driver of this growth, evidence suggests it relies heavily on other factors to work. **[Ap]**

There is strong evidence that trade is a major driver of the global economy. **[Ap]** This is supported by Figure 8c, which shows the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) free trade deal. **[E]** The figure shows that this agreement involves countries with a combined population of 2.2 billion people and accounts for 28% of global trade and 30% of global economic output. **[E]** This suggests that trade agreements greatly increase economic connections between countries, supporting the view that trade is a major driver. **[Ap]**

However, trade alone cannot explain global economic growth. **[Ap]** Advances in technology, particularly the internet, have increased productivity and reduced costs, letting businesses of all sizes take part in the global economy. **[U]** The internet has reduced the need for physical transport and lowered barriers to trade. **[U]**

In addition, development aid has been important for some countries. **[U]** Figure 8b shows that development aid received by selected African countries has increased since 1999. **[E]** For example, in South Sudan aid per capita rose from \$40 to \$160 by 2019. **[E]** This suggests that for some countries aid, rather than trade, has been more important in supporting economic growth and stability. **[Ap]**

Overall, trade has been a key driver of the global economy, particularly through large trade agreements like the RCEP shown in Figure 8c. **[Ap]** However, technology and aid have also played significant roles, so trade is important but not always the greatest driver for every country. **[Ap]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word is 'discuss'. This means you should argue both sides of the view and reach a supported judgement.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a 'level of response' approach across three assessment objectives, each worth 4 marks.
> 
> A top-level Level 3 answer [9-12 marks] will:
> 
> - **AO2 [U] (4 marks):** explain the relevant geographical ideas and how they link together
> - **AO3 [Ap] (4 marks):** apply this understanding to weigh the arguments and reach a balanced, supported judgement
> - **AO4 [E] (4 marks):** use Figures 8b and 8c accurately to support the points, integrating the data rather than describing it
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Forgetting to refer to both figures
> - Using the resources but not linking them to the question
> - Describing the figures without explaining what they show
> - Not reaching a final judgement

> **[exam-tip]**
> - 'Discuss' means argue both sides, so weigh trade against technology and aid before you decide.
> - Always name the figures (Figure 8b and Figure 8c) and use their data to support a point, not just describe it.
> - End by answering 'to what extent', giving a clear overall judgement.
