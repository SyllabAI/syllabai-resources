## Q16 — easy — 1 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 1 marks
**Correct answer: D** — The increasing connection and integration of the world's economies, cultures, and populations

**The correct answer is ****D:**** **the increasing connection and integration of the world's economies, cultures, and populations.

The alternative answers are incorrect because:

**'A'** describes the opposite of globalisation. Closing borders to trade is associated with protectionism or economic isolationism, which works against the process of global integration.

**'B'** describes a scenario of declining international trade, which is again the opposite of globalisation. Globalisation is characterised by the growth of international trade, not its decline.

**'C'** describes rural-to-urban migration, which is a form of internal migration within a single country. This is related to urbanisation rather than globalisation.

### 8((f)) — 12 marks
Trade has played a major role in driving the global economy, but it is not the only factor, and its value must be considered alongside technology, aid, and global institutions. **[AO2]**

One reason trade can be seen as the biggest driver is the significant growth in global trade over time. Increased imports and exports have built strong economic links between countries, increasing interdependence. **[AO2]** This is supported by Figure 8c, which shows that the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) trade agreement involves a population of 2.2 billion people and accounts for around 28% of global trade and 30% of global economic output. **[AO4]** This suggests that large-scale trade deals have had a major effect on the global economy, supporting the view that trade is a key driver. **[AO3]**

However, this idea can be challenged. Advances in technology, especially the internet, have enabled globalisation by reducing costs and increasing productivity. Businesses can now communicate instantly, handle global supply chains and reach international markets more easily. **[AO2]** This suggests that trade itself depends on technology to function efficiently, weakening the argument that trade alone is the biggest driver. **[AO3]**

In addition, development aid has added to the global economy, especially in countries with weaker trade links. Figure 8b shows that development aid received by some African countries has grown since 1999. For example, South Sudan’s aid per capita grew from around \$40 to \$160 by 2019. **[AO4]** This shows that for some countries, aid has been more important than trade in supporting economic growth and stability. **[AO3]**

Overall, while trade has been a major driver of the global economy, especially through large trade agreements shown in Figure 8c, it is not always the most important factor. Technology and aid have also played important roles, especially in countries that are less integrated into global trade. Therefore, trade is a key driver, but not generally the greatest one. **[AO3]**
