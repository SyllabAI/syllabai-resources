**Rural Fieldwork** · Edexcel IGCSE Geography (4GE1)


## Q1 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 10((a)) — 2 marks
For full marks expect a fully stated more specific aim (objective-like) e.g. to investigate the relative importance** (1) **of farming inputs/factors ** (1)**  in different types of farming** (1)**;  to investigate how various types of farming** (1)** have different inputs ** (1)**; to investigate how various types of farming (1) work as different systems ** (1)**.  Award 1 mark for outlines and broad intentions e.g. to investigate how farms work as a system** (1)**; to investigate ways of raising farm output ** (1)**.

## Q5 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
One risk was walking across uneven moorland and hillside terrain, where students could trip or fall. **[1] **The students could have managed these risks by wearing appropriate footwear such as walking boots and by carrying a map and mobile phone in case of an emergency. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means the answer must go beyond simply naming a risk. The answer must show how the risk was or could have been managed.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a valid risk linked to the fieldwork context
> - **One mark** for explaining a way of managing that risk
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> **Risks**
> 
> - Uneven or boggy terrain on hillsides and moorland
> - Adverse or rapidly changing weather conditions
> - Remote locations with limited mobile phone signal
> - Livestock in fields presenting a hazard
> - Road safety when crossing rural lanes between sites
> 
> **Management strategies**
> 
> - Carrying a fully charged mobile phone and emergency contact detail
> - Completing a risk assessment before the visit
> - Informing a responsible adult of the route and expected return time
> - Working in pairs or groups at all times
> - Checking the weather forecast before setting out
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Naming a risk without explaining how it was managed
> - Giving a generic risk such as 'getting lost' without linking it to the Ashdale fieldwork context
> - Describing what went wrong rather than how a risk was managed

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Link **your risk directly to the Ashdale context.
> 
> **A rural** moorland setting has specific hazards that differ from a town centre.
> 
> **Mentioning** the terrain or the remote location signals to the examiner that you have read the scenario carefully.

## Q10 — hard — 8 marks · exam-questions

### 10((a)) — 8 marks
Relevant equipment and techniques obviously depends to some extent on the precise aims of the investigation and so the data that needs to be collected. There are many possibilities so some examiner discretion will be called for. However, for farm system inputs to be investigated the following are generally most likely to be creditable : Equipment** (4 marks)** – clipboard ** (1)**; base map ** (1)**; camera (1); recording sheets ** (1)**; questionnaire ** (1)** .. Max 1 for stationary Field techniques **(4 marks) **– sketching** (1)**; interviewing ** (1)**; recording ** (1)**individual and/or groupwork ** (1)** ... Accept sampling techniques in this section. In each case, max of 3 marks where equipment/technique not described/developed but merely identified. 4 marks are available for each aspect of the question and max marks in each case requires some description of use e.g. max marks can be awarded where 2 pieces of equipment and 2 field techniques are identified then described as to how they can be used in the field to collect farm system input data/information. Do not double credit the same response between the two sections.
