A brownfield site is land that has been built on before, such as a former factory or industrial site. **[1]**

One advantage is that building on brownfield land does not use up the countryside or green belt, which protects natural habitats and farmland. **[1]**

However, brownfield sites can be expensive to develop because the land may be contaminated from previous industrial use and require cleaning before building can begin. **[1]**

Despite this, using brownfield land helps regenerate derelict urban areas, improving the local environment and attracting investment back into the city. **[1]**

> The command word '**explain**' requires reasons, not just a list of advantages and disadvantages.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for each valid point [max 2 marks]
> - **One mark **for each valid development of a point [max 2 marks]
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> **Advantages:**
> 
> - Located within existing urban areas, so infrastructure (roads, utilities) may already be in place
> - Regenerates derelict or abandoned land, improving urban environments
> - May be closer to existing jobs and services

> 
> - Sites may be irregularly shaped, making development more complex
> - Often more expensive per plot than greenfield development

Redeveloping brownfield sites in Southampton has also helped to regenerate run-down inner-city areas. **[AO3] **For example, redevelopment around the Waterfront and Ocean Village has provided new flats, jobs and services. **[AO3] **This improves the local economy and encourages people to live closer to where they work, reducing car use and traffic congestion. **[AO4]**
