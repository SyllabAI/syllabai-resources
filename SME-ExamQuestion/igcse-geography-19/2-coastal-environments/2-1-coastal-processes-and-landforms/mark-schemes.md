## Q28 — hard — 8 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 8 marks
The shape of coastal land is determined by how marine erosion and sub-aerial processes work together.** [AO3] **Marine erosion is usually easier to see because it removes material directly from the shore. **[AO2] **But sub-aerial processes are also important for weakening structures and making it easier for the coast to retreat from the land on a large scale. **[AO2]**

Marine erosion involves waves directly attacking the base of cliffs using hydraulic action, abrasion, and attrition.** [AO2]** This action creates the initial weakness, like a wave-cut notch, at the base of cliffs.** [AO2]** Marine erosion is responsible for wave-cut platforms, caves, arches, stacks, and stumps.** [AO3]**

Sub-aerial processes above the high tide line include freeze-thaw, salt crystal growth (physical weathering), and chemical weathering (carbonation), plus mass movement (rockfalls, slumps).** [AO2]** These actions weaken the rock above the wave-cut notch, making it unstable and causing it to collapse.** [AO3]** As a result, rockfalls widen cracks into caves, help arches collapse into stacks, and create scree slopes at cliff bases.** [AO2]**

Together they form a cycle where marine erosion attacks the bottom, sub-aerial processes attack the top, and the cliff retreats, leaving a platform.** [AO3]**

With the formation of cliffs, marine erosion forms the notch,** [AO2]** but sub-aerial processes (weathering/mass movement) cause the actual collapse that retreats the cliff.** [AO3]** On the other hand, waves exploit weaknesses that are often created by weathering to form caves;** [AO2] **marine action attacks both sides of a headland;** [AO2] **and weathering weakens arches until they collapse into stacks.** [AO2]**

Overall, although the two processes work together,** [AO2] **marine erosion is the most important because it provides the initial energy and creates the space (notches or cavities) needed to shape the coastline.** [AO3]** Sub-aerial processes mainly assist by weakening rock, but without wave erosion, most coastal landforms would not form. ** [AO2] [AO3]**
