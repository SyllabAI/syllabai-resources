## Q12 — easy — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 3 marks
Many people live in areas at risk because they cannot afford to move elsewhere. **[1] **Housing in safer locations is often expensive. **[1] **People on low incomes have no financial choice but to stay, even when they know the risk is high. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means that the answer should provide **one **reason only, but that reason must be developed through **three **connected steps to earn all 3 marks
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a **point-marked** approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a valid reason
> - **One mark** for developing that reason with a geographical explanation
> - **One mark** for a further development or consequence that shows clear understanding of why this keeps people in a hazardous area
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - People may have lived in the area for generations and have strong emotional ties to their community and family, meaning the social cost of leaving feels greater than the physical risk of staying
> - People may depend on the local land for farming and fertile soils which can support good harvests so leaving would mean losing their only source of income
> - Some people believe the hazard will not affect them personally because previous events were minor or did not cause them direct harm so they underestimate the likelihood of a serious event occurring
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Giving two separate undeveloped reasons rather than one fully developed reason: this limits the response to 2 marks
> - Describing the hazard rather than explaining why people stay

> **[exam-tip]**
> **This type** of question is where students typically lose the third mark.
> 
> **Only **one reason is needed, but you must push it as far as it will go. Ask yourself, 'Why does that matter?' after each sentence. If your answer to that question adds new information, write it down. That is your third mark.
