## Q12 — easy — 2 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 2 marks
One risk is that the riverbank could be wet and slippery after rainfall. **[1]** This could cause a student to lose their footing and fall into the river, resulting in injury or being swept downstream. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command words '**identify**' and '**explain**' together mean you must name the risk and then state what harm it could cause. Do not describe how the risk was managed.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a** point-marked** approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a relevant health and safety risk
> - **One mark** for explaining the impact of that risk, not the management of it
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Fast-flowing or deep water: a student who entered the water could be swept away
> - Uneven or submerged rocks: a student wading in the river could cut their feet or trip and fall
> - Overhanging or unstable vegetation: a falling branch could strike and injure a student working below
> - Any other appropriate response
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Describing data collection methods rather than site selection decisions
> - Discussing a risk and how it was managed

> **[exam-tip]**
> **There **is an important difference between a risk and a risk management strategy.
> 
> - The risk is what could go wrong.
> - The management is how you tried to stop it.
> 
> **This** question only wants the risk and its impact. Describe the worst realistic outcome, and you will get both marks.

## Q15 — medium — 6 marks · exam-questions

### 7() — 6 marks
Accept for up to max marks answers defining each of the three methods.

Systematic sampling = sample taken in regular way **(1) **e.g. every tenth person; every 100 metres **(1)**...

Random sampling = sample taken based on equal chance of inclusion for all **(1) **e.g. using random numbers table; drawn from a hat** (1) **….

Stratified sampling = samples are selected according to a known characteristic** (1)** e.g. age-distribution of population in an opinions survey; sites reflecting rock type in vegetation survey **(1)**….
