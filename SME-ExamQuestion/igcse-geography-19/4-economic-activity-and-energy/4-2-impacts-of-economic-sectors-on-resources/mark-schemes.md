## Q10 — easy — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 3 marks
Malthus argued that population grows faster than food supply, meaning that eventually resources will run out and population will crash through famine, disease, or war. **[1] **Boserup disagreed, arguing that human ingenuity means that when population pressure increases, people find new ways to produce more food through technological innovation. **[1]** The key difference is that Malthus saw population growth as a crisis, whereas Boserup saw it as a driver of progress. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means that the answer needs to develop what each theory means and how they differ from each other.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This question is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for a valid description of the Malthusian view
> - **One mark** for a valid description of the Boserupian view
> - **One mark** for a clear explanation of the difference between the two views
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Reference to Malthusian 'checks' (famine, disease, war) as population control mechanisms
> - Reference to Boserup's examples of agricultural intensification, irrigation, or new crop varieties
> - Contrast of views: Malthus as pessimistic and Boserup as optimistic or problem-focused versus solution-focused
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Describing only one theory
> - Confusing the two theorists. Remember: Malthus came first (1798) and was pessimistic; Boserup responded later and was optimistic

> **[exam-tip]**
> A simple memory trick: Malthus said, 'we will run out'; Boserup said, 'we will find a way.' Hold those two ideas clearly, and the contrast writes itself.

## Q17 — hard — 8 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 8 marks
Malthus argued that population grows faster than food supply, so eventually resources will be unable to support the population.** [K]** He predicted that 'positive checks', such as famine, disease, and war, would control population growth when resources ran out. **[K]** This was a pessimistic view that saw population growth as a problem with no human solution.** [U]**

Boserup offered a different view, arguing that necessity is the mother of invention. **[K]** When population grows and food becomes scarce, people develop new technologies and farming methods to increase food production. **[K] **Examples include the development of irrigation, the Green Revolution, and high-yield crop varieties, all of which have allowed food production to keep pace with population growth. **[U]** This suggests that human ingenuity can overcome resource limits, making Boserup's theory more optimistic and arguably more consistent with what has actually happened in the world since Malthus wrote in 1798. **[Ap]**

However, Malthus's theory does have some merit. **[Ap]** Food insecurity and famine in sub-Saharan Africa persist despite technological advances **[K]**, suggesting that resources do not always keep pace with population. **[U]** This supports the Malthusian idea that population growth can exceed supply. **[Ap]**

Since global food production has grown with population, Boserup's theory does seem more plausible. **[U] **But there is no single theory that explains the population-resource relationship, because it varies by location and level of development. **[Ap]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**analyse**' means that the answer needs to examine the evidence for each theory, make connections between ideas, and reach a reasoned judgement. The examiner expects the answer to weigh up the two theories against each other.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a level of response approach. Each point made in the answer does not equal a mark.
> 
> A top-level Level 3** ****[7–8 marks]**** **answer will**:**
> 
> - **AO1 ****[K]**: show knowledge of the Malthusian and Boserupian theories and relevant examples
> - **AO2** **[U]**: show understanding of how the theories explain the relationship between population and resources, and how they compare
> - **AO3 ****[Ap]**: apply knowledge and understanding to reach a reasoned judgement about which theory is more convincing
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question, as it will depend on the examples used. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> - Wealthier countries tend to reflect Boserupian outcomes; poorer regions can reflect Malthusian pressures
> - Malthus's theory is relevant where development levels are low and technology has not reached all communities
> - Any other appropriate response
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Writing about one theory makes a balanced analysis impossible
