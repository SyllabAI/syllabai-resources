## Q5 — easy — 3 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 3 marks
One risk was traffic when recording data near roadsides and on busy streets in the town centre. **[1]** The students could have managed this by carrying out a risk assessment before the visit, identifying the most dangerous locations in advance. **[1]** They could also have worked in pairs and stood on the pavement away from the road at all times during data collection. **[1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' means the answer must go beyond simply naming a risk. The answer must show how the risk was or could have been managed.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a plausible risk linked to the fieldwork context
> - **One mark** for explaining one way of managing that risk
> - **One further mark** for developing the management strategy
> 
> **[3 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Alternative information that could be provided in the answer includes:
> 
> **Risks**
> 
> - Uneven or slippery surfaces at the rural fringe or industrial site
> - Adverse weather conditions during outdoor data collection
> - Working near an industrial area with potential hazards
> - Personal safety when working in pairs in unfamiliar locations
> 
> **Management strategies**
> 
> - Wearing high-visibility clothing near roads or industrial areas
> - Informing a responsible adult of the route and timing
> - Checking the weather forecast and dressing appropriately
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Naming a risk without explaining how it was managed
> - Giving a very generic risk such as 'getting lost' without linking it to the Thornfield fieldwork context
> - Describing the risk in detail but neglecting the management strategy entirely

> **[exam-tip]**
> **Structure **your answer in two clear parts:
> 
> - the risk
> - the management
> 
> **The** third mark is always for development of the management strategy, so push your answer one step further than the obvious.
> 
> **Instead **of just saying 'do a risk assessment', say what the risk assessment would identify or what action it would lead to.

The students could have used local council planning records **[1]** to find out how many planning applications for solar panel installation had been approved in Thornfield over the past ten years, allowing them to compare the trend in applications with the pattern of solar panels they recorded in Figure 1. **[1]**

> 
> - **One mark** for identifying a specific and plausible secondary data source
> - **One mark** for explaining how it would support the enquiry into changing energy use
> 
> **[2 marks]**
> 
> **Alternative content**

> 
> - Government energy statistics on renewable energy adoption by region
> - Satellite images or historic Google Maps images to track changes in solar panel numbers over time
> - Local newspaper articles reporting on energy schemes in Thornfield

Figure 9c should show all Figure 9b’s data. Conclusions should relate to this data and data-supported conclusions must receive good credit.
