### Q10
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is B**

- The **unified atomic mass unit (u)** is defined as exactly $\frac{1}{12}$ of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

- This is distinct from **relative atomic mass (*****A***<sub>**r**</sub>**)**, which is the weighted average mass of naturally occurring atoms of an element on a scale where an atom of carbon-12 has a mass of exactly 12 units.

**A** is incorrect as the mass of an electron is approximately $\frac{1}{1836}$ u, which is negligible compared to 1 u.

**C** is incorrect as an atom of hydrogen-1 has a mass of approximately 1.0078 u, not exactly 1 u. Hydrogen was the original standard before being replaced by carbon-12 in 1961.

**D** is incorrect as the mass of a proton is approximately 1.0073 u. The formal definition uses the entire carbon-12 atom, which accounts for protons, neutrons, electrons, and nuclear binding energy.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Do not confuse the unified atomic mass unit with relative atomic mass.
> 
> - The atomic mass unit is a fixed quantity, defined as exactly 1/12 the mass of one carbon-12 atom.
> - Relative atomic mass is a dimensionless ratio that uses this unit as its reference scale and takes into account the natural isotopic abundances of an element.

> Two characteristic differences in the mass spectrum of <sup>14</sup>N and <sup>15</sup>N:
> 
> - <sup>14</sup>N and <sup>15</sup>N have different mass/charge (m/z) ratios
> **AND**
> Different relative abundances (peak heights) **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> In a mass spectrum of nitrogen with two isotopes, each isotope produces a separate peak: <sup>14</sup>N at m/z = 14 and <sup>15</sup>N at m/z = 15. The height of each peak reflects the relative abundance of that isotope.
> 
> Mass spectrometry has two main applications at A Level:
> 
> - Determining relative atomic mass from isotope data
> - Identifying organic molecules from fragmentation patterns, which is covered in later topics.
To calculate the relative molecular mass of the enriched nitrous oxide:

- Calculate the relative atomic mass of N in the enriched sample:

*A*<sub>r</sub>(N) = $\frac{(4\times15.00)+(96\times14.00)}{100}$

*A*<sub>r</sub>(N) = $\frac{1404}{100}$ = 14.04 **[1 mark]**

- Calculate *M*<sub>r</sub> of N<sub>2</sub>O using this value of *A*<sub>r</sub>(N):

*M*<sub>r</sub> = (2 × 14.04) + 16.00

*M*<sub>r</sub> = **44.08** **[1 mark]**

> **[exam-tip]**
> The enrichment percentage (4% <sup>15</sup>N by mass) can be treated directly as the percentage abundance of <sup>15</sup>N. The remaining 96% is <sup>14</sup>N.
> 
> Apply the weighted average formula to find Ar(N) first, then use this value in the Mr calculation for N<sub>2</sub>O. Do not use the standard Ar(N) = 14.01 from the data booklet, as this sample has been deliberately enriched in <sup>15</sup>N.
> **[mark-scheme]**
> The atom with twice the protons and neutrons of <sup>15</sup>N:
> 
> - Chemical symbol including mass number: <sup>30</sup>Si **[1 mark]**
> - Number of electrons in the neutral atom: 14 **[1 mark]**
> 
> <sup>15</sup>N has 7 protons and 8 neutrons; doubling gives 14 protons and 16 neutrons; atomic number 14 is silicon; mass number = 14 + 16 = 30.

> **[exam-tip]**
> From the periodic table, nitrogen has atomic number 7, so <sup>15</sup>N has 7 protons and 15 - 7 = 8 neutrons.
> 
> Doubling both gives 14 protons and 16 neutrons. Atomic number 14 is silicon (Si), and mass number = 14 + 16 = 30, giving the symbol <sup>30</sup>Si.
> 
> In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons: 14 electrons. Do not confuse mass number with electron count.
