### Q8
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

**Melting point:**

- Silicon has the highest melting point because it has a **giant covalent structure**.
- Many strong covalent bonds must be broken to melt it, requiring a large amount of energy.
- The melting points of the **metals** (Na, Mg, Al) increase across the period as more electrons are delocalised per atom (1 from Na, 2 from Mg, 3 from Al), strengthening the metallic bonding.
- Phosphorus (P<sub>4</sub>) and sulfur (S<sub>8</sub>) are **simple covalent molecules** with only id-id forces between molecules.
- S<sub>8</sub> has a higher melting point than P<sub>4</sub> because its larger molecular mass (256 vs 124 g mol<sup>-1</sup>) means stronger id-id forces.

![Graph showing relative melting points across Period 3 elements: low for Na, increasing for Mg and Al, peaking at Si, then lower for P and S with S above P, then falling for Cl and Ar; alongside steadily increasing electronegativity from Na to Cl](assets/002-graph-showing-relative-melting-points-across-per.jpg)

**Electronegativity:**

- Electronegativity **increases steadily across Period 3** because nuclear charge increases while the shielding remains approximately constant, so the attraction on bonding electrons increases.

### Q13
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is C**

- Magnesium chloride has the highest melting point because it forms a **giant ionic lattice**.

  - Strong electrostatic attractions between Mg<sup>2+</sup> and Cl<sup>-</sup> ions extend throughout the lattice, so a large amount of energy is required to overcome these forces when the solid melts.
- CCl<sub>4</sub>, S<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub>, and HCl are **simple covalent molecules**.

  - So, the forces between molecules are much weaker than ionic bonds.
- CCl<sub>4</sub> is **non-polar** because of its symmetrical tetrahedral shape.

  - So, the only intermolecular forces present are **instantaneous dipole-induced dipole (id-id) forces**.
- S<sub>2</sub>Cl<sub>2</sub> is **polar**.

  - So, it experiences **permanent dipole-permanent dipole (pd-pd) interactions** as well as id-id forces.
- However, CCl<sub>4</sub> has a **larger molecular mass** (154 vs 135 g mol<sup>-1</sup>)

  - This gives it stronger id-id forces, so CCl<sub>4</sub> has the higher melting point of the two.
- HCl is also a **simple covalent molecule** with pd-pd interactions and id-id forces, but these intermolecular forces are weak.

  - As a result, HCl is a **gas at room temperature** and has a very low melting point.

### Q15
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is D**

**Sulfur**

- The electron configuration of sulfur is 1s<sup>2</sup> 2s<sup>2</sup> 2p<sup>6</sup> 3s<sup>2</sup> 3p<sup>4</sup>.
- The 3p<sup>4</sup> configuration has **one pair** of 3p electrons, so sulfur does **not** satisfy Property 1.
- However, sulfur trioxide reacts with water to produce **sulfuric acid**, a **strong acid** that fully dissociates in aqueous solution:

SO<sub>3</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O $\rightarrow$ H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>

- Therefore, sulfur satisfies **Property 2 only**.

**Phosphorus**

- The electron configuration of phosphorus is 1s<sup>2</sup> 2s<sup>2</sup> 2p<sup>6</sup> 3s<sup>2</sup> 3p<sup>3</sup>.
- By Hund's rule, the three 3p electrons each occupy a **separate 3p orbital**, so phosphorus has **no paired 3p electrons** and satisfies Property 1. ![Electron configuration diagram for phosphorus showing three unpaired electrons each in a separate 3p orbital (3px, 3py, 3pz), with configuration 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p3](assets/003-electron-configuration-diagram-for-phosphorus-sh.jpg)
- Phosphorus oxides (P<sub>4</sub>O<sub>6</sub> and P<sub>4</sub>O<sub>10</sub>) produce only **weak acids** (H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>3</sub> and H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>) that do not fully dissociate, so phosphorus does **not** satisfy Property 2.
- Therefore, phosphorus satisfies **Property 1 only**.

**A** is incorrect as phosphorus oxides produce only weak acids, so phosphorus does not have Property 2.

**B** is incorrect as the electron configuration of sulfur is 3p<sup>4</sup>. Sulfur has one paired 3p electron and therefore does not satisfy Property 1.

**C** is incorrect as neither element has both properties. Sulfur lacks Property 1 and phosphorus lacks Property 2.

> **[exam-tip]**
> Sulfur dioxide (SO<sub>2</sub>) also produces an acid when added to water:
> 
> SO<sub>2</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O $\rightarrow$ H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>3</sub>.
> 
> However, H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>3</sub> is a **weak acid** and does not fully dissociate, so it does not satisfy Property 2. Only SO<sub>3</sub>, which forms the strong acid H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>, satisfies Property 2.

### Q17
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Electronegativity increases continuously from Na to Si as nuclear charge increases while the number of electron shells remains constant:

Na < Mg < Al < Si (continuous increase on y-axis).

- **First ionisation energy** is the energy to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms:

X (g) $\rightarrow$ X<sup>+</sup> (g) + e<sup>-</sup>

- Na has the lowest first ionisation energy (lowest nuclear charge; outer electron is 3s<sup>1</sup>).
- Al has a *lower* first ionisation energy than Mg despite having one more proton.

  - Al's outer electron is in the 3p subshell, which is at a higher energy level and is shielded by the 3s<sup>2</sup> electrons, outweighing the extra nuclear charge.
- Si has the highest first ionisation energy of the four (3p<sup>2</sup>, highest nuclear charge).
- The correct graph shows electronegativity increasing continuously on the y-axis, and first ionisation energy in the order Na < Al < Mg < Si on the x-axis (dip at Al).

**B**, **C**, and **D** are incorrect as they show the wrong trend for electronegativity, miss the dip in first ionisation energy at aluminium, or invert the relative positions of Mg and Al.

### Q18
**Final answer:** **The correct answer is A**

- Silicon has **4 valence electrons** and forms 4 covalent bonds; chlorine has **7 valence electrons** and forms 1 covalent bond.

- One silicon atom bonds with 4 chlorine atoms to form SiCl<sub>4</sub>.
- Since chlorine exists as diatomic Cl<sub>2</sub>, the balanced equation is:

Si + 2Cl<sub>2</sub> $\rightarrow$ SiCl<sub>4</sub>

- Therefore, **2 moles of Cl**<sub>**2**</sub> are required per mole of silicon.

![Dot-and-cross diagram showing silicon bonded to four chlorine atoms in SiCl4](assets/004-dot-and-cross-diagram-showing-silicon-bonded-to-.jpg)

**B** is incorrect as 3 moles of Cl<sub>2</sub> would give SiCl<sub>6</sub>, which silicon cannot form (Period 3 elements beyond the 3s and 3p cannot expand their octet at this level).

**C** is incorrect as 4 refers to the number of chlorine *atoms* in SiCl<sub>4</sub>, not the number of Cl<sub>2</sub> molecules. Since Cl<sub>2</sub> is diatomic, only 2 moles are needed.

**D** is incorrect as 5 moles of Cl<sub>2</sub> would give SiCl<sub>10</sub>, which is not a known compound.
