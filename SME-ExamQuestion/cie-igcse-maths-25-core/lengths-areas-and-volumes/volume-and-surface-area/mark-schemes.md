### Q2
> **[exam-tip]**
> The formula for the volume, $V$, of a sphere is given on the list of formulas in the exam paper:
> 
> $V=\frac{4}{3}πr^{3}$
> 
> - where $r$ is the radius

Here you know the volume, and you want to know the radius

Start by putting the given volume into the formula in place of $V$

$381=\frac{4}{3}πr^{3}$

Now you need to solve that for $r$

- First multiply by 3, to get rid of the fraction on the right-hand side

$\begin{gathered} 3 \times 381 & = 3 \times \frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3} \\ 1143 = 4 \pi r^{3} \end{gathered}$

- Then divide both sides by $4π$, to get rid of the $4π$ on the right-hand side

$\frac{1143}{4π}=r^{3}$

**[M1]**

- You can use your calculator to work out the value of that

$r^{3}=90.957049...$

- To find the value of $r$, take the cube root of that

  - Use your calculator's memory, or the Ans key, to make sure you are taking the cube root of that exact value

$r = \sqrt[3]{90.957049...} = 4.497233...$

Round to 3 significant figures

- The fourth significant figure is a '7'

  - which tells you to round the third significant figure '9' up to a 10
  - That means put a zero where the '9' is
  
    - and increase the '4' before it by 1

**Final answer:** **4.50 cm (3 s.f.)**

**[A1]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **M1**: For $r^{3}=\frac{1143}{4π}$ or equivalent.
> 
> **A1**: For 4.497..., correctly rounded to 4.50.
