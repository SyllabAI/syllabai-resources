### Q10
Development indicators are used to measure progress, but different indicators can present different pictures. **[KU]** For example, GDP per capita shows average wealth, but it hides inequality. **[KU]** A country may seem rich, but many people might still live in poverty. **[E]**

Social indicators, like literacy rate or life expectancy, reveal people’s quality of life. **[KU**] In Cuba, literacy and life expectancy are high, despite a low GDP, showing that wealth alone is not enough to measure development. **[KU]** This highlights hidden strengths that GDP misses. **[E]**

Composite measures, like the Human Development Index (HDI), combine health, education, and income, giving a broader view. [**KU]** For instance, Norway scores 0.95 (2024), while Niger scores 0.39, showing the extremes of the gap. **[KU]** However, HDI still uses averages, hiding inequality within countries, such as differences between urban and rural areas. **[E]**

Overall, the choice of indicators strongly affects how the development gap is understood. **[E] **Using only economic measures exaggerates wealth differences, while social indicators highlight human challenges. **[E]** A mix, like HDI, provides the clearest picture, but no single measure can capture the full gap. **[E]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**assess'** means the answer needs to include a judgement about how the choice of development indicator changes understanding.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a **'level of response'** approach. Each point made in the answer does not equal a mark.
> 
> AO focus = AO1 **[KU]** + AO3 **[E]**
> 
> - A top-level 3 answer [7 marks] will be a clear, detailed explanation with named examples and a balanced assessment.
> - It should show knowledge and understanding **[KU]** of GDP, literacy, life expectancy, and HDI to evaluate (judge) **[E]** its strengths/limits and impact on understanding the gap.
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The above answer is one way to respond to this question. Additional information that could be referenced includes:
> 
> - People per doctor vs infant mortality to contrast health outcomes.
> - How PPP adjustments make economic comparisons fairer.

> **[exam-tip]**
> In synoptic essays, always **compare indicators** rather than describing them separately.
> 
> Show how each indicator affects the interpretation of the gap, not just its definition.
> 
> Use a mix of LIC (e.g. Niger) and HIC (e.g. Norway) examples.
