### Q1
In HICs, growth in tertiary and quaternary sectors has transformed economies and societies. **[KU]** Tertiary services such as healthcare, education, finance, and tourism now dominate employment. **[KU]** Quaternary industries, including IT, biotechnology, and research, provide high-skilled jobs. **[KU]**

Economically, these sectors generate high GDP and export earnings. **[KU]** For example, the UK’s finance sector in London and the IT cluster in Silicon Valley contribute billions of dollars. **[KU]** The multiplier effect occurs as higher wages increase spending in local economies. **[E]** Investment in research and development drives innovation, maintaining global competitiveness. **[KU]**

Socially, growth in these sectors improves living standards.** [KU] **Access to better healthcare and education increases life expectancy and literacy. **[KU] **New jobs attract migrants, creating diverse, multicultural societies. **[E]** Services such as leisure and retail improve quality of life for many. **[KU]**

However, not all impacts are positive. **[E]** Regional inequality increases as wealth and jobs concentrate in certain areas, such as the southeast of England or coastal USA. **[KU]** Low-skilled workers may struggle to find work in high-tech economies, leading to unemployment or low-paid service jobs. **[E]** Rising house prices in successful regions can create social exclusion. **[E]**

Overall, tertiary and quaternary growth benefits economies and societies in HICs, but it creates new inequalities that governments must address to ensure balanced development. **[E]**

### Q2

### Q3

### Q9

### Q13
The employment structure shows the number of people working in primary, secondary and tertiary jobs. **[1 mark]**

Low-income countries have higher numbers of primary sector jobs, such as farming, because of lower levels of development in education and industrialisation. **[1 mark]**

As a country develops, the demand for manufactured goods increases, and this leads to more secondary jobs. **[1 mark] **But as a country grows richer, it manufactures less of its own goods and imports them from other countries instead. **[1 mark]**

HICs need more tertiary and quaternary service employees because they have higher levels of education, economic growth, and investment. **[1 mark]**

> **[mark-scheme]**
> The command word '**explain**' requires answers to provide a developed, reasoned response.
> 
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> - **1 mark **for stating what an employment structure is.
> - Up to** 3 marks **for linking the employment structure to a development level.
> - **1 mark** for explaining how the structure reflects development.
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> - Mechanisation reduces the need for manpower in agriculture.
> - Resources are exhausted.
> - HICs have a higher HDI and a greater proportion of tertiary employment.
> - HICs have a greater demand for services.
> - HICs have high levels of quaternary sector jobs because the population have developed advanced skills.

> **[exam-tip]**
> If you just list sectors or describe a country's job pattern without explaining **how **it links to development, you'll only receive 3–4 marks. To reach 5 marks, explain the **causes and consequences** — think education, technology, and economic development.
