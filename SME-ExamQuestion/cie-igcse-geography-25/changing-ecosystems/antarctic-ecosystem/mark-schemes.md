### Q1
Abiotic factors such as temperature, water availability, light, wind, and soil conditions affect where organisms can live in Antarctica **[KU]**. Temperatures are extremely cold, especially inland, so only specially adapted organisms like mosses and lichens can survive there. Animals such as penguins and seals tend to stay closer to the coast where it is slightly warmer **[KU]**.

Water availability is limited because most water is frozen. In ice-free areas like arid valleys, liquid water is scarce, which means few species can survive there **[KU]**. Winds in Antarctica are fierce and can erode soil or blow snow around, making it harder for plants and animals to survive in exposed areas **[KU]**.

Light is another important factor because the months of darkness in winter limit photosynthesis for plants such as algae and mosses **[KU]. ** Finally, soils in Antarctica are cold, dry and nutrient-poor, so only the hardiest plants can grow, which influences where animals can find food **[KU]**.

All these factors combined mean that most life is found around the coast and in the sea rather than inland **[KU].**
