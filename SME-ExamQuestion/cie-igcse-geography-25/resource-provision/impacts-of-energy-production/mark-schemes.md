### Q1
Norway is a HIC (High Income Country) in northern Europe where 90% of electricity comes from hydropower** [KU]**. This is because Norway has many mountains and rivers, which are perfect for generating hydroelectric power **[KU].** Hydropower also makes up 65% of the total energy use** [KU].**

Norway also uses some fossil fuels, like oil and gas, but most of this is exported instead of used in the country** [KU].** Norway has 1% of the world’s gas reserves and 0.3% of oil reserves. Other renewables like wind and solar are growing. There are now 53 wind farms, with 36 more planned by 2030, including the world’s first floating wind farm** [KU]**. People in Oslo even get a 30% subsidy for installing solar panels at home.

Norway uses little coal and tries to reduce oil use by encouraging electric cars—66% of new car sales were electric in 2021 [**KU].** The energy mix is very sustainable, but hydropower can be affected by drought, like in 2022 when energy supply dropped **[KU].**

Overall, Norway’s energy mix is mainly renewable, reliable, and environmentally friendly
