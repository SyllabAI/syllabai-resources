### Q4

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Mark allocation**
>

> **Alternative content**
>

### Q7
Put a grid over a map of Florida  **[1 mark]** and use a random number generator to choose grid squares to sample  **[1 mark]**. At each site, place a 1 m² quadrat and count the snails inside it  **[1 mark]**, recording each count in a tally table  **[1 mark]**. Repeat this for at least 50 quadrats, then multiply the mean number of snails per m² by the area of Florida  **[1 mark]**.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for each valid point, to a maximum of 5 marks
> - You must cover all three bullet points to gain 5 marks
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Other information that could be used includes:
> 
> - Use GPS coordinates to find the sampling sites
> - Repeat the sampling on several days and use the mean
> - Any other suitable response
> 
> **Common errors**
> 
> - Describing systematic sampling, for example sampling at regular intervals, when the question asks for random sampling
> - Forgetting to repeat the sampling

> **[exam-tip]**
> - Use the three bullets as headings in your plan and give at least one point under each.
> - Do not copy the question into your answer. Start straight away with what you would do.

### Q9
Limitation 1: The data come from one farm, so they may not represent the whole of Finland  **[1 mark]**.

Explanation 1: Other parts of Finland have different temperatures and precipitation, so the pattern could be different there  **[1 mark]**.

Limitation 2: The data are mean values, so they do not show what happens in each year  **[1 mark]**.

Explanation 2: Weather varies from year to year, so in some years November could be warmer than October  **[1 mark]**.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> **Mark allocation**
> 
> This answer is assessed using a point-marked approach.
> 
> - **One mark** for each limitation and **one mark** for its matching explanation, to a maximum of 4 marks
> 
> **Alternative content**
> 
> The answer above is just one example of how to respond to this question. Other information that could be used includes:
> 
> - The climate is changing, so past data may not show future patterns
> - The conclusions say 'always', which one set of data cannot prove
> - Figure 1.1 shows August, not February, as the wettest month, so the first conclusion is not supported by the data
> - Any other suitable response

> **[exam-tip]**
> - Each explanation must match its limitation. Write the explanation straight after the limitation so the examiner can see they belong together.
