## Q6 — very_hard — 20 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 20 marks — command: explore — structured
**The Yard**

Back home the yard

remember me —

the mango tree

still leaning

where I leave it,

heavy with its own gold.

** **

I close my eyes

on this grey street

and the corrugate roof

sing again with rain,

the standpipe drip

its small cool music.

** **

Grandmother chair

still rocking

in the shade of noon,

her voice a low river

running under everything,

calling, calling

till the chickens come.

** **

City can build

its walls around me —

but it cannot fence

the green that grow inside.

That yard is mine.

I carry it like seed.

** **

— Marcia Edmund

**Slack**

He'd let the line out finger by finger,

the kite a scrap of red above the rec,

and tell me, Keep it tight, lad, keep it tight,

his hand a brake behind my smaller hand.

** **

I felt the wind take hold and pull —

that live weight tugging like a fish,

the string a nerve run all the way

from my cold knuckles to the sky.

** **

He's slower now. The garden's mostly his chair.

But Sunday last he passed the reel across,

nodded at the gusting field, and said,

your turn. Don't lose it. So I won't.

** **

I keep it tight. I keep him tight.

The slack we never let between us, held.

** **

— Sean Holroyd

**Marks**

Every birthday, back against the door,

my dad would press a pencil to my head

(mind you stand up straight, no cheating now)

and draw a line, and write the year beside.

** **

I watched the ladder of myself go up,

rung by rung,

year by year,

past the light switch,

past the coat hooks,

nearly to the handle of the loft.

** **

And then I stopped.

Not growing — leaving.

The marks stayed where they were,

a little museum on the paintwork.

** **

Last week I went back home.

The door's been painted over,

twice.

But run your thumb along the gloss

and you can still feel them, just —

small ridges,

the years,

holding on under the white.

** **

— Gerry Flynn

**The Sea Uncaged**

All afternoon the sea lay flat,

a sheet of beaten silver

dozing in its bay.

** **

Then the wind came with its key

and turned the lock,

and the water rose up on its haunches —

** **

grey-flanked, white-clawed,

shaking the spray from its back like a wet dog,

** **

bigger than the harbour wall,

bigger than the church,

opening a green throat in the cliffs.

** **

We stood at the window, small,

and watched it take the beach

in three long strides,

** **

watched it lift a fishing boat

the way a cat lifts a mouse —

almost gently, almost kind —

** **

and we understood, then,

whose house this really was,

who only let us stay.

** **

— Yvette Caron

## Q10 — very_hard — 20 marks · exam-questions

### 1() — 20 marks — command: explore — structured
**What I Keep**

They told me loss would empty me,

that grief would hollow out a room

and leave it bare.

** **

But loss is not an empty house.

Loss is a river that I carry —

it does not stop because you're gone,

it only changes course.

** **

I carry your laugh in the morning.

I carry your voice in the rain.

I carry the way you said my name

like it was worth the saying.

** **

This is not a wound.

This is a window.

Not a closing —

an opening.

** **

And when the grief comes, as it comes,

grey and wide and cold,

I stand inside it like a stone inside a stream

and let it move around me,

and stay.

** **

I am still here.

I am still yours.

I am still becoming what you loved.

** **

— Selam Tesfaye
