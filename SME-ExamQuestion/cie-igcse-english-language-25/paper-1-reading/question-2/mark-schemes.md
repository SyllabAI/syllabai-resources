### Q1
### Model answer

Flying causes enormous environmental harm because aeroplanes produce more carbon dioxide than any other transport. Many people feel compelled to fly - to visit family abroad or for work - and there are few genuinely green alternatives. As for individuals, travellers should prioritise direct flights over cheaper routes with stopovers, since extra legs release more carbon. Travellers should also stay longer at their destination to justify the environmental cost, and pack light, since every kilogram adds to emissions. Airlines bear responsibilities too. A globally connected data network could allocate seats more efficiently. Airlines are experimenting with artificial intelligence to find fuel savings, but they must collaborate on genuinely innovative solutions.

> **[mark-scheme]**
> Responses *might* use the following:
> 
> Problems caused by air travel:
> 
> - air travel produces more CO2 than any other form of transport
> - frequent flying is expensive in both financial and environmental terms
> - there are few accessible greener alternatives for many travellers
> - carbon offsetting is likely ineffective / may inadvertently encourage more flying
> - average plane occupancy is only 80%, meaning resources and fuel are wasted
> - routes with multiple stopovers release more carbon than direct flights
> 
> What individuals can do:
> 
> - choose direct flights with modern, efficient airlines
> - be sceptical of carbon offsetting schemes
> - stay longer at destinations to justify the flight's emissions
> - combine shorter visits into one trip
> - travel on airlines that tend to fill their planes
> - pack light / reduce luggage weight
> 
> What airlines and systems can do:
> 
> - think innovatively and work together / effective global regulation needed
> - use big data and AI to find fuel efficiencies
> - develop and invest in alternative biofuels
> - create a globally connected data network to maximise seat allocation
> 
> **Why this answer would get full marks**
> 
> It covers all three required strands (problems, individual actions, airline/systemic actions) with a well-balanced spread of points. It consistently paraphrases rather than lifting, uses accurate vocabulary, and is organised into a logical progression. It shows understanding of the writer's tone - scepticism about offsetting, urgency about airline action - rather than simply listing facts. This places the response at Level 5 for Reading (9-10) and Level 3 for Writing (4-5).
