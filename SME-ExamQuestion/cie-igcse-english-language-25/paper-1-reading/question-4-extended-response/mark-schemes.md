# Mark Schemes — Question 4: Extended Response
**Paper 1: Reading** · Cambridge (CIE) IGCSE english-language (0500 & 0990)


## Exam Questions: Question 4: Letter (2 questions)


### Q1
### Model answer

Dear Paulo,

I am writing from Ilhabela, where we have just survived the most dramatic night of the voyage. Let me tell you about the past few weeks.

When Andy and Abi asked me to help sail Josephine from Cape Town to Rio, I had my reservations. Andy himself had confessed to being “terrified,” and the boat - an old, second-hand yacht with no autopilot and no radio - was hardly reassuring. Still, they were determined, and sometimes that counts for more than experience.

The highlights were extraordinary. Leaving Cape Town in a stiff breeze, we were joined by a pod of dolphins dancing on the bow wave - a sight I have rarely seen in all my crossings. On night watch under a full moon, Andy laughed aloud at the helm as Josephine thundered over the swells, absorbed despite his nerves. It was the kind of night that reminds you why you sail.

Were they suited to it? Honestly, it is complicated. Josephine is sturdy but old, and without an autopilot someone had to be at the wheel constantly, day and night - exhausting over such a passage. Andy and Abi are brave but inexperienced, and enthusiasm has its limits when you are sleep-deprived and seasick. Yet they never once complained about the hard watches or the monotony of open ocean.

The storm at Ilhabela tested everything. When Josephine snapped her mooring line at two in the morning, I doubted the engine would start; when it did, we all aged a year in relief. Next morning we found another yacht wrecked on the beach - a sobering reminder of what we had escaped.

What will they take away? That the sea does not negotiate. They came looking for adventure, and found it - and a great deal more.

Write soon,

Dael

### Q2
### Model answer

Dear Mr and Mrs [Sylvia's parents],

I hope this letter finds you well. Your daughter Sylvia has kindly given me permission to write to you, and I am delighted to do so.

I came across the violin quite by chance. Our orchestra had arrived in your town for a performance, and I had a few hours free beforehand - time I usually spend exploring the local area. I wandered past a small music shop and decided to look inside. Towards the back, partly hidden, sat the most unusual violin I had ever seen. The maker had carved a remarkably expressive face into the scroll, with winking, cheerful eyes that seemed almost to challenge me. I asked to try it, and from the first chord I knew it was exceptional. I bought it there and then.

I cannot quite put into words how the violin made me feel when I played it that evening during my solo. It was as if the instrument already knew the hall, as if it belonged there. The sound was magnificent - richer and more resonant than my own trusted violin. When Sylvia approached me afterwards, and I understood whose family this instrument had come from, everything suddenly made sense.

As for what should happen to it now, I have thought carefully. The violin bears your husband's face on the scroll and clearly holds enormous sentimental value for your family. I would very much like to return it to you. I only ask that, should you ever wish for it to be played publicly again, you might consider me.

Yours sincerely,

Seth
