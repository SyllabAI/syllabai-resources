### Model answer

The writer uses the image of the “perfect funnel for the strong winds” to convey how Andy's relaxed attitude gives way to a sense of being trapped by the approaching danger. The word “funnel” suggests that the hills on either side of the channel squeeze and concentrate the wind, channelling its full force directly towards the boat, so that there is no way to avoid it. The adjective “perfect” is quietly ominous: it implies that the landscape is ideally shaped to intensify the storm, as if nature itself is conspiring against the sailors. This precise, almost technical description makes the threat feel measured and inevitable rather than dramatic, which is somehow more frightening, and it shows how Andy's earlier hopes of “snorkelling and generally lounging about” have been replaced by an uneasy awareness of risk.
