# ASM-01 — Assessment Overview

## Purpose

This artifact provides the assessment overview for Pearson Edexcel International GCSE
Chemistry (4CH1): how the qualification is examined, how the two papers divide the
content and the marks, and how the assessment objectives are weighted. It is the first
artifact of the Assessment Intelligence (ASM) family and the opening artifact of
BL-008 pass 5. Every structural claim is carried on the specification of record
 and on the official exam corpus of record . The artifact is research evidence for teaching and
revision design; it does not promote itself into canonical assessment identity, and it
rewrites nothing on the canonical planes (Subject Intelligence Build v1, §5).

## Scope

**Covers:** the two-paper qualification structure; paper codes, availability and first
assessment; per-paper weightings and totals; assessment-objective weightings; the
content-scope rule that separates Paper 1 from Paper 2; and the observed session
cadence of the of-record exam corpus.

**Grounding:** SRC-SPEC-4CH1 (specification PDF, Issue 3, September 2024 — pages on
qualification structure and assessment objectives) and SRC-QPMS-4CH1 (16 4CH1-1C
question papers across sessions 2019-06 → 2026-06, each carrying its official paper
identity header).

**Out of scope:** per-question difficulty and archetype analysis (ASM-03..ASM-09),
mark-scheme internals (MS family), examiner sentiment (EXM family), legacy 4CH0
assessment (HIS family).

## Executive Summary

4CH1 is examined through two externally-assessed written papers. Chemistry Paper 1
(4CH1/1C) is a 2-hour, 110-mark paper worth 61.1% of the total International GCSE and
assesses the core (non-bold, non-'C') content; Chemistry Paper 2 (4CH1/2C) is a
1-hour-15-minute, 70-mark paper worth 38.9% and assesses all content including the
bold 'C' extension content. All three assessment objectives carry weightings fixed in
band form (AO1 38–42%, AO2 38–42%, AO3 19–21%), with a per-paper split recorded in the
specification. The exam corpus of record holds 1C papers across sixteen sessions from
2019-06 to 2026-06, confirming the availability pattern and giving a live observation
base for the assessment's opening-question structure. Questions may come from any
topic area on either paper, and both papers mix question styles including
multiple-choice items.

## Main Analysis

### ASM-001 — Two-paper structure: what the qualification consists of

**Claim:** 4CH1 is assessed through exactly two externally-set written papers, defined
in the specification's own "Qualification at a glance" section: Chemistry Paper 1 and
Chemistry Paper 2.
**Evidence:** the specification states the qualification "comprises two externally-assessed
papers" and names them "Chemistry Paper 1" and "Chemistry Paper 2" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p13). Both papers are set and marked by Pearson (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p13, p14).
**Why it matters for assessment design:** there is no coursework or controlled-
assessment component in the examined model; every mark a candidate earns sits in one
of two written sittings, which is the frame all later ASM analysis works inside.

### ASM-002 — Paper codes, availability and first assessment

**Claim:** the two examined papers carry codes 4CH1/1C and 4CH1/2C; both have been
available in November and June sessions since first assessment in June 2019.
**Evidence:** Paper 1's overview row records the paper code listing with "Availability:
November and June" and "First assessment: June 2019" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p13); Paper 2 records its own code with the same availability and first assessment
(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p14). The corpus of record confirms live use of the 1C code across sessions — its
extraction headers carry official paper identity such as "4CH1/1C — 2019-06 (official
extraction)" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13) and "4CH1/1C —
2026-06 (official extraction)" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2026-06/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13).
**Note:** the 4SD0/1C co-listing on Paper 1 is the Double Award intake route named in
the same overview row; this artifact records it without interpretation.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### ASM-003 — Paper weightings and mark totals

**Claim:** Paper 1 contributes 61.1% of the qualification through a 2-hour, 110-mark
paper; Paper 2 contributes 38.9% through a 1-hour-15-minute, 70-mark paper.
**Evidence:** the Paper 1 overview records "61.1% of the total" with a "2-hour written
examination paper" and "The total number of marks is 110" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p13); the Paper 2 overview records "38.9% of the total" with a "1-hour and 15-minute
written examination paper" and "The total number of marks is 70" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p14).
**Assessment-design consequence:** per-minute mark pressure is near-identical across
the two papers (110 marks over 120 minutes vs 70 over 75), so pacing strategy does not
switch between sittings even though paper lengths do.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### ASM-004 — Assessment objectives and their weightings

**Claim:** the qualification weights three assessment objectives in band form — AO1
knowledge and understanding, AO2 application/analysis/evaluation, AO3 experimental
skills — at 38–42%, 38–42% and 19–21% respectively, with a per-paper split fixed in
the specification.
**Evidence:** the assessment-objectives page lists AO1 "Knowledge and understanding of
chemistry" at "38–42%", AO2 "Application of knowledge and understanding, analysis and
evaluation of chemistry" at "38 42%" as printed and AO3 "Experimental skills, analysis
and evaluation of data and methods in chemistry" at "19–21%" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p35). The same page fixes the per-unit split: Paper 1 AO1 23.2–25.7%, AO2 23.2–25.7%,
AO3 11.6–12.8%; Paper 2 AO1 14.8–16.3%, AO2 14.8–16.3%, AO3 7.4–8.2% (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p35).
**Reading for revision design:** AO1 and AO2 are co-primary at roughly double AO3's
weight, so knowledge-recall and application skills dominate the mark tariff, but a
fifth of every paper is experimental-skills work — the practicals lane cannot be
skipped.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### ASM-005 — The content-scope rule that separates the papers

**Claim:** Paper 1 assesses only core content — specification statements not in bold
and without a 'C' reference — while Paper 2 assesses all content including the bold
'C'-referenced extension, with both papers drawing questions from any topic area.
**Evidence:** Paper 1 "Assesses core content that is not in bold and does not have a
'C' reference" with "Questions may come from any topic area across the specification"
(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p13); Paper 2 "Assesses all the content, including content that is in bold and has a
'C' reference" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p14). The live corpus shows the any-topic rule in action on Paper 1: session-opening
Question 1s range from particles and states of matter ("This question is about states of matter" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2021-01/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13)to separation
methods ("This question is about mixtures and compounds" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2022-01/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13)and to atmospheric gases ("This question is about gases in the atmosphere" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2020-01/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13)
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### ASM-061 — The printed question-style mix and calculator permission

**Claim:** each paper's specification entry commits to a style mix in the same words: a mixture of different question styles, enumerated as multiple-choice, short-answer, calculations and extended open-response, with a calculator permission printed beside it. The mix is therefore a printed qualification-level commitment, not an observed regularity — both papers state it identically while differing in duration and total marks.
**Evidence:** Paper 1's entry prints "A mixture of different question styles, including multiple-choice questions, short-answer questions, calculations and extended open-response questions" with "A calculator may be used in the examinations" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p13); Paper 2's entry prints the same style-mix sentence and the same calculator permission (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p14).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### ASM-062 — The aims layer behind the assessment objectives

**Claim:** the specification states qualification-level aims that sit above the assessment objectives: learning unifying patterns and using them in new and changing situations, presenting information clearly and logically with appropriate vocabulary, and developing a logical approach to problem solving in a wider context. The aims are the purpose statement the AO weightings operationalise, and their language anticipates the context-switching the papers later require.
**Evidence:** the aims list prints "learn about unifying patterns and themes in chemistry and use them in new and changing" "situations", "select, organise and present relevant information clearly and logically using appropriate" "vocabulary, definitions and conventions" and "develop a logical approach to problem solving in a wider context" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1, Official-Specifications/igcse-chemistry/international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p9).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### ASM-063 — The Question-1 tariff band across the observed sessions

**Claim:** the opening question's tariff is not fixed: across the observed 1C sessions the Question-1 header oscillates inside a four-to-seven mark band, with consecutive sessions moving between the extremes. The paper therefore opens at a variable depth, and the opener's weight is a session-level design choice rather than a fixed structural constant.
**Evidence:** the 2019-06 paper opens "## Question 1 (4 marks)" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13) while the 2020-11 paper opens "## Question 1 (7 marks)" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2020-11/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13); the 2021-11 and 2022-06 openers print "## Question 1 (5 marks)" each (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2021-11/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13; SRC-QPMS-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-1C/questions.md lines 1-13).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

## Limitations / Coverage Gaps

This artifact is grounded on the 1C papers of the corpus only; the 2C and CR variants
are analysed by the sibling ASM artifacts whose packs hold them. Examiner-report
evidence is deliberately absent here (EXM family ground). The January-session
availability discrepancy (ASM-006) is disclosed, not reconciled: no inference is made
about which administrative route offered January sittings. Question-count and mark-
distribution claims at paper level are deferred to ASM-05/ASM-06, whose packs carry
full question-header grids. Nothing here rewrites the canonical assessment identity
planes; all statements are research observations pinned to the cited sources.
