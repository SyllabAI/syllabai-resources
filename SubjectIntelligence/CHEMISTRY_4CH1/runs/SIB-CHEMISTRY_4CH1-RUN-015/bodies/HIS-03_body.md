
# HIS-03 — Assessment Evolution

## Purpose

This artifact tracks how the qualification's assessment model evolved across the
documentary lineage, as the documents and the examiner-report record state it: the
legacy paper model and assessment-objective weightings, the current paper model and
weightings, the session-availability change, the sample-assessment-material
pipeline, and the live legacy assessment era as witnessed by the archived examiner
reports. Legacy claims quote only LEGACY — HISTORICAL sources; current-model claims
ground on SRC-SPEC-4CH1. Canonical planes are untouched.

## Scope

**Covers:** the two-paper architecture across generations; mark totals and
weightings; assessment-objective weightings; series availability; the SAMs
booklets' contents pages as milestones of the sample-material pipeline; and the
legacy live-era span attested by the examiner reports of June 2011 and January
2019.

**Out of scope:** question presentation style inside papers (HIS-05) and
terminology change (HIS-04).

## Executive Summary

The assessment model is the lineage's most conservative layer and its most
recalibrated one at once. Two externally assessed papers persist from the Issue 3
specification through the current document; the papers' mark totals move from 120
and 60 to 110 and 70, and the weighting split moves from two-thirds/one-third to
61.1%/38.9%. Assessment-objective weightings widen from a fixed 20% investigative
skills block inside a 45–55% knowledge band to three bands of 38–42%, 38–42% and
19–21%. Series availability changes from January and June to November and June in
the current issue's own change log. The sample-assessment-material pipeline is
continuous — a 2008 booklet, a 2011 booklet inside the Issue 5 combination, and the
current specification's pointer to its SAMs document. The archived examiner reports
bracket a live legacy era that ran from June 2011 into January 2019.

## Main Analysis

### HIS-013 — The legacy two-paper model at 120 and 60 marks

**Claim:** the legacy Issue 3 specification assessed the qualification through two
externally assessed papers — Paper 1 (4CH0/1C) at 120 marks worth 66.6% of the
total IGCSE marks and Paper 2 (4CH0/2C) at 60 marks worth 33.3% — both first
assessed June 2011.
**Evidence:** the specification-at-a-glance page states: "This Edexcel IGCSE in
Chemistry qualification comprises TWO externally assessed papers. ... Chemistry
Paper 1 Paper code: 4CH0/1C ... First assessment: June 2011 66.6% of the total
IGCSE marks ... The total number of marks is 120. ... Chemistry Paper 2 Paper
code: 4CH0/2C ... The total number of marks is 60." (SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, Issue
3.pdf p7).
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.

### HIS-014 — The current two-paper model at 110 and 70 marks

**Claim:** the current specification assesses the qualification through Paper 1C at
110 marks (2 hours, 61.1%) and Paper 2C at 70 marks (1 hour 15 minutes, 38.9%),
both at level 1/2 and set and marked by Pearson.
**Evidence:** the assessment table states: "Paper number Level Assessment
information Number of marks allocated in the paper Paper 1C 1/2 Assessed through a
2-hour written examination set and marked by Pearson. The paper is weighted at
61.1% of the qualification. ... 110" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p33) and for Paper 2C: "The
paper is weighted at 38.9% of the qualification" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p33).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-015 — Assessment-objective weightings widen from a fixed 20% AO3 to three near-equal bands

**Claim:** the legacy weighting table gave AO1 45–55%, AO2 25–35% and a fixed AO3
"Investigative skills" 20%; the current table gives AO1 38–42%, AO2 38–42% and AO3
"Experimental skills, analysis and evaluation of data and methods in chemistry"
19–21% — application gains a full band at the expense of the knowledge band's
ceiling.
**Evidence:** the legacy table reads: "% in IGCSE AO1: Knowledge and understanding
45–55% AO2: Application of knowledge and understanding, analysis and evaluation
25–35% AO3: Investigative skills 20% TOTAL 100%"
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, Issue 3.pdf p23); the current table reads: "International
GCSE AO1 Knowledge and understanding of chemistry 38–42% AO2 Application of
knowledge and understanding, analysis and evaluation of chemistry 38 42% AO3
Experimental skills, analysis and evaluation of data and methods in chemistry
19–21%" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p35). The legacy table is
LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the current table is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-016 — Series availability moves from January and June to November and June

**Claim:** the legacy papers were available in January and June series; the current
specification's change summary records that availability of series was amended to
November and June.
**Evidence:** the legacy page states: "Availability: January and June series"
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, Issue 3.pdf p7); the current change summary states:
"Availability of series amended to November and June" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p3). The legacy availability is
LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the amendment note is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

**Claim:** every generation of the qualification ships sample assessment
material — the 2008 booklet with sample question papers and sample mark schemes,
the 2011 booklet bound into the Issue 5 combination, and the current
specification's pointer to its own SAMs document.
**Evidence:** the 2008 booklet's contents read: "Contents Introduction 3 Sample

General Marking Guidance 53 Chemistry Paper 1 55 Chemistry Paper 2 63"

Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-018 — The live legacy assessment era ran from June 2011 into January 2019

**Claim:** the archived legacy examiner reports bracket a live assessment era — the
earliest packed report is the June 2011 Principal Examiner Feedback for Paper 1C,
and the latest packed report carries per-question commentary from January 2019 —
so the legacy model was live and examined for nearly eight years of documented
sessions.
**Evidence:** the June 2011 report cover reads: "Examiners' Report/ Principal
Examiner Feedback June 2011 International GCSE Chemistry (4CH0) Paper 1C Science
Double Award (4SC0) Paper 1C" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2011-06/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p1); the
January 2019 report's opening commentary reads: "Question 1 Part (a) was very well
answered with most candidates scoring all three marks." (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2019-01/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p3). Both are LEGACY — HISTORICAL witnesses.
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.

### HIS-051 — Embedded multiple-choice items are examiner-documented across papers and years

**Claim:** the legacy ER documents embedded multiple-choice items inside the
structured papers across at least three sessions and both paper numbers — Paper 2C
carrying three such items in one question and Paper 1C carrying a multi-part
multiple-choice question late in the paper. **Evidence:** the Summer 2018 Paper 2C
report opens its Question 4 note with "The three multiple choice questions were
answered correctly by the majority of candidates" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2018-06/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p4); the Summer 2016 Paper 2CR report records "The two
multiple choice questions in parts (a) and (b) were answered correctly by the vast
majority of the candidates" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2016-06/4CH0-2CR/er.pdf p3); the
January 2018 Paper 1C report notes "The multiple choice parts were usually well
answered with parts (c) an (d) proving the most difficult" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2018-01/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p8). Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. The mixed question
style the current specification declares (HIS-029) therefore has a documented legacy
antecedent in live marking commentary, not merely in sample materials.
### HIS-052 — The mark allocation is examiner-stated as a detail signal

**Claim:** the legacy examiners explicitly teach the mark allocation as a signal of
required answer detail — two recorded statements across five years make this a
documented assessment-behaviour pattern, not a one-off remark. **Evidence:** the
January 2019 Paper 2C report states "It would perhaps be helpful to remind candidates
to look at the mark allocation when considering the detail required in their
answers" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2019-01/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p5); the January 2015 Paper 2C
report records candidates who "gave either the observation that solid would appear,
or the reason that the solubility of the solid decreases as the solution is cooled,
but very few gave both despite the mark allocation of two, which indicated that two
points were required to gain full marks" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2015-01/4CH0-2C/er.pdf
p4). Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. Both statements ground the reading-the-
question craft that persists unchanged into current assessment practice.
### HIS-053 — The examiner report names the assessment objectives as marking doctrine

**Claim:** at least one legacy report states the specification's assessment
objectives as the direct reason a graphical answer was not credited — the AOs are
thereby documented as live marking doctrine, not front-matter decoration.
**Evidence:** the June 2011 Paper 1C report, discussing best-fit lines, states "The
examiners are aware, from seeing candidates' answers, that some centres train their
candidates to draw a line of best fit as a straight line through the centre of a
group of data points (a scatterplot). This approach was not credited in the mark
scheme. The assessment objectives for this specification emphasise the
identification of anomalies and candidates need to make allowances for those
anomalies at all times" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2011-06/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p4). Temporal
scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. This is the assessment-objective layer that HIS-015
tracked across the specification documents, here witnessed in marking behaviour; the
anomaly-handling doctrine it states is exactly the AO3 skill the current weighting
bands continue to reward.
### HIS-054 — Live mark-scheme adaptation is documented inside a session

**Claim:** the legacy ER documents a mark scheme being adapted during marking when
responses showed knowledge beyond the specification's definitions — a documented
instance of the mark scheme yielding to evidenced candidate knowledge. **Evidence:**
the Summer 2016 Paper 1CR report states "It was felt that part (d) required some
knowledge slightly beyond the specification (although from many of the responses it
was evident that many centres do teach definitions of oxidation and reduction in
addition to those in the specification, and appropriate ones were credited where
correct). Hence the Mark Scheme was adapted to take this into account and a large
majority of candidates were able to score at least one mark" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2016-06/4CH0-1CR/er.pdf p8). Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. For assessment
lineage this documents that the legacy mark schemes were not immutable
instruments within a session — a governance fact about the legacy assessment
apparatus that any archival reconstruction should carry.
