
# HIS-06 — Persistent Patterns

## Purpose

This artifact identifies examination-behaviour patterns that the legacy examiner
reports document, and checks each against the current specification's content and
the packed current-era question material: bonding difficulty, dot-and-cross
diagram craft, equation balancing, measurement precision, ion and gas tests, and
the opening-question convention. Each record pairs a LEGACY — HISTORICAL witness
with the current-side evidence in this artifact's pack, so persistence is asserted
only where both sides are inside the pack. Current-truth statements ground on
SRC-SPEC-4CH1; the packed current-paper window is SUPPLEMENTARY texture. Canonical
planes are untouched.

## Scope

**Covers:** six pattern pairs — bonding as a standing difficulty; dot-and-cross
diagram execution; balancing errors; precision in readings; tests for ions and
gases; and foundational opening questions — each attested by a legacy report
excerpt and located in the current material where the pack holds it.

**Out of scope:** patterns whose current side is not in this pack (no claim either
way), and assessment-model persistence (HIS-03).

## Executive Summary

Six patterns persist legibly across the era boundary. Bonding was already named a
minefield in January 2012 — with electrostatic-attraction wording the specific
casualty — and bonding sub-topics remain the spine of the current Principles topic.
Dot-and-cross diagrams drew execution advice in June 2018 and remain a named current
statement requirement. Equation balancing errors and ambiguity discipline recur in
both eras' reports. Precision in readings — trailing zeros carried from burette to
thermometer — was marked strictly in June 2016 and experimental skills remain
written-examination-assessed now. Tests for ions and gases confused candidates in
2012 and survive as a named current sub-topic with numbered test statements. And
the opening question was already a familiar, high-scoring item in 2011 and 2013,
while the packed June 2019 paper opens with a four-option dissolution question on
particle-level behaviour.

## Main Analysis

### HIS-031 — Bonding difficulty is documented in the legacy era and the sub-topics persist in the current spine

**Claim:** the legacy reports named bonding a standing difficulty — candidates
describing covalent-bond formation where electrostatic attraction was required —
and bonding remains a named current sub-topic trio (ionic, covalent, metallic) with
electrostatic-attraction wording inside the current statements.
**Evidence:** the January 2012 report states: "Questions on bonding continue to
prove a minefield for many candidates, but it was pleasing to see several parts of
this question well answered. In (a)(i), the drawing of dot and cross diagrams was
generally done well, but few correct answers were seen in (a)(ii) – most
candidates described the formation of covalent bonds instead of the electrostatic
attraction between the bonding pair of electrons and the nuclei of the two atoms."
(SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2012-01/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p4); the current topic list carries
"(f) Ionic bonding (g) Covalent bonding (h) Metallic bonding"
(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p17) and the
current statements include "1.45 understand covalent bonds in terms of
electrostatic attractions" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p20). The report is
LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the current statements are CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-032 — Dot-and-cross diagram craft is advised in the legacy era and named in the current statements

**Claim:** the legacy reports gave execution advice on dot-and-cross diagrams —
clarity, crossing out and restarting — and the current specification names
dot-and-cross diagrams as an assessed representation across diatomic, inorganic
and small organic molecules.
**Evidence:** the June 2018 report states: "Candidates should be encouraged to draw
clear dot and cross diagrams as some diagrams were very untidy and difficult to
decipher, causing some to lose marks unnecessarily." (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2018-06/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p6); the current statement 1.46 reads: "understand how to
use dot-and-cross diagrams to represent covalent bonds in: • diatomic molecules,
including hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, halogens and hydrogen halides • inorganic
molecules including water, ammonia and carbon dioxide"
(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p20). The
report is LEGACY — HISTORICAL; statement 1.46 is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-033 — Balancing and state-symbol errors recur across both eras' reports

**Claim:** the legacy reports document recurring balancing errors — omitted
coefficients leaving equations unbalanced — and unnecessary-coefficient ambiguity,
while equations and calculations remain a named current sub-topic.
**Evidence:** the June 2018 report states: "The main error here was to omit the '2'
in front of the HCl therefore leaving the equation unbalanced."
(SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2018-06/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p6); the June 2013 report adds an
ambiguity discipline: "The equation in (c) was usually correctly balanced (often
with an unnecessary coefficient of 1 before O2), but some students wrote 1 very
much like a 2 (with a diagonal line at the top and a horizontal line at the base)
- students should be aware that with ambiguities of this type, the benefit of
doubt is not given." (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2013-06/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p3); the current
topic list carries "(e) Chemical formulae, equations and calculations"
(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p17). The
reports are LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the sub-topic list is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-034 — Precision in readings is marked strictly in the legacy era and experimental skills remain written-assessed

**Claim:** the legacy reports enforced instrument precision across instruments —
candidates who gave burette-reading precision but dropped the trailing zero on
thermometer readings lost the mark — and the current specification keeps
experimental skills assessed through written examinations.
**Evidence:** the June 2016 report states: "Although candidates are used to
providing appropriate precision for burette readings, many did not carry this idea
forward to the thermometer readings in part (a), and hence lost one mark out of
two for failing to include the trailing '0' for the first three readings."
(SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2016-06/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p4); the current specification states:
"The skills developed through these and other practicals will be assessed through
written examinations." (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p34). The report is
LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the current statement is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-035 — Tests for ions and gases confused candidates in the legacy era and survive as a named current sub-topic

**Claim:** the legacy reports document standing confusion in ion and gas tests —
ammonium ions confused with ammonia gas among them — and the current specification
carries a named "Chemical tests" sub-topic with numbered statements for gas tests
including ammonia and cation tests including ammonium.
**Evidence:** the January 2012 report states: "Candidates often score poorly in
tests for ions, and in this part, some were confused between ammonium ions and
ammonia gas or att" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2012-01/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p3); the current
sub-topic list carries "(h) Chemical tests" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p26) and the current
statements read: "2.44 describe tests for these gases: • hydrogen • oxygen •
carbon dioxide • ammonia • chlorine." and "2.47 describe tests for these cations:
• NH4+ using sodium hydroxide solution and identifying the gas evolved"
(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p26). The
report is LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the current statements are CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-063 — The half-equation failure mode persists across the era (2013 to 2019)

**Claim:** the same ionic half-equation failure mode is examiner-documented six
years apart: in 2013 candidates repeated a given half-equation or mis-managed the
oxygen equation; in 2019 the recorded wrong answers show electron-coefficient and
charge errors on magnesium half-equations. **Evidence:** the January 2013 Paper 1C
report records "A surprising number repeated the given half-equation for Al3+ in
(a)(ii), while several attempts at the oxygen equation were unbalanced or began with
O2 – instead of O2–" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2013-01/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p5); the January
2019 Paper 2C report records "A wide range of incorrect answers were seen with common
examples being Mg2+ + 2e- 2Mg and Mg+ + e- Mg" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2019-01/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p5). Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. Both witnesses sit
inside this pack, so the persistence claim is pack-internal: the demand and its
characteristic failure mode were stable across the era's whole span.
### HIS-064 — The bond-energy sign and direction error persists across the era (2014 to 2018)

**Claim:** the bond-energy calculation's characteristic error is examiner-documented
twice: in 2014 the missing negative sign cost the last mark; in 2018 the
subtraction-direction error produced 103 instead of -103, with coefficient errors on
the bond energies also recorded. **Evidence:** the Summer 2014 Paper 2CR report
records candidates who "failed to obtain the last mark by not including the negative
sign" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2014-06/4CH0-2CR/er.pdf p5); the Summer 2018 Paper 2C
report records candidates "usually by doing the subtraction the wrong way round and
hence giving an answer of 103 instead of -103" and "multiplying the H-H and Br-Br
bond energies by two or failing to multiply the H-Br bond energy by two"
(SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2018-06/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p4). Temporal scope: LEGACY —
HISTORICAL. The two witnesses name the same trap from both sides (sign and
direction), giving the persistence claim its precision.
### HIS-065 — The distillation-choice confusion persists across the era (2013 to 2016)

**Claim:** the simple-versus-fractional distillation choice confusion is
examiner-documented three years apart: in 2013 candidates offered simple distillation
for ethanol-water where the question demanded the best method; in 2016 the report
names the confusion directly as a live observation. **Evidence:** the Summer 2013
Paper 2C report records "Simple distillation was often given as a method of
separating ethanol and water. Although this method would produce a separation, it is
not as good as fractional distillation, and the question called for the best method
of separation to be given" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2013-06/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p3); the
Summer 2016 Paper 1CR report records "it was evident there is some confusion about
when to use simple and fractional distillation" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2016-06/4CH0-1CR/er.pdf p3). Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. The persistence is
documented inside the era; the techniques themselves map forward per HIS-007.
### HIS-066 — The mark-allocation misreading persists across the era (2015 to 2019)

**Claim:** candidates under-reading the mark allocation as a detail signal is
examiner-documented four years apart with nearly identical framing: in 2015 few gave
both required points despite an explicit two-mark allocation; in 2019 the examiners
recorded candidates missing the solid-state ion-immobility point that the mark
allocation signaled. **Evidence:** the January 2015 Paper 2C report records
"very few gave both despite the mark allocation of two, which indicated that two
points were required to gain full marks" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2015-01/4CH0-2C/er.pdf
p4); the January 2019 Paper 2C report records candidates who "often scored only one
mark because they failed to mention that ions do not move/flow when solid" and
draws the general lesson "It would perhaps be helpful to remind candidates to look at
the mark allocation when considering the detail required in their answers"
(SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2019-01/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p5). Temporal scope: LEGACY —
HISTORICAL. The pattern pair is fully pack-internal and both halves carry the
same craft lesson.
