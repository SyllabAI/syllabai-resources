# EXM-02 — Recurring Examiner Warnings

## Purpose

This artifact catalogues the recurring warnings the official 4CH1 examiner reports
issue, as held in the corpus of record (SRC-ER-4CH1): the failure modes the examiners
name repeatedly across sessions and papers — uncreditworthy answer forms, misread
demands, observation-vs-explanation confusions — each anchored to its report and
page. Every claim is carried on a bounded, page-located excerpt from the run's
evidence pack, with paper/session identity preserved. Research artifact; canonical
planes untouched.

## Scope

**Covers:** the warning classes visible in the pack's report excerpts — demand-misread
warnings, answer-form warnings, and the discrimination/balance framing that locates
where candidates struggle.

**Grounding:** SRC-ER-4CH1 (the 2019-06 and 2022-06 report excerpts of the pack, plus the appended 2025-06 1C/2C introductions).

**Out of scope:** what examiners reward (EXM-03) and penalise (EXM-04) as their own
catalogues; question-specific mechanics (EXM-05).

## Executive Summary

The held reports warn in consistent classes: answers that merely repeat the question
stem are ruled not creditworthy; answers that give the right observation for the
wrong reason fail the linked-explanation contract; and test-based items are warned
over when candidates use the wrong reagent. The 2022 introductions add the papers'
own discrimination framing — where demand concentrates and which questions separated
grades — which tells revision designers where the warned-for skills actually sit.

## Main Analysis

### EXM-021 — Uncreditworthy answer forms: repeating the stem

**Claim:** examiners warn that restating the question's own wording earns nothing.
**Evidence:** "Those who did not score here often just stated that the elements were
in the same group, which was just a repeat of the stem of the question and is,
therefore, not creditworthy." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p4).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-022 — Demand-misread warnings on description items

**Claim:** examiners warn when candidates answer a different demand than the one
printed (naming dyes instead of describing spot counts).
**Evidence:** "repeated the question and said that V and W only contained one dye,
instead of referring to one spot or equivalent term" (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-1CR/er.pdf p4).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-023 — Wrong-reagent warnings on test items

**Claim:** test items attract warnings for wrong or near-miss reagent choices.
**Evidence:** "some lost the mark in (b) for using a lighted or unlit splint to test
for oxygen. A very small minority gave an incorrect test, e.g. the test for hydrogen
or carbon dioxide." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-2CR/er.pdf p3).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-024 — Half-explanation warnings on displacement reasoning

**Claim:** examiners warn when the observation is present but the causal link is
missing.
**Evidence:** "Some knew bromine was formed but failed to explain that this was
because chlorine is more reactive than bromine." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-2CR/er.pdf p4).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-025 — The discrimination framing that locates demand

**Claim:** the 2022 introductions state where papers discriminated and which
questions carried the demand.
**Evidence:** the 2C introduction frames the paper's spread: "This paper discriminated
well and gave a range of marks for all grades" with "The first question was the
highest scoring question" (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-2C/er.pdf p3); the 1C
introduction states "Candidates found questions difficult" where application demand
concentrates (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-026 — Preparation-dependent performance warnings

**Claim:** examiners warn that specific preparation (the Advance Notice) separated
cohorts.
**Evidence:** "Candidates had clearly read the Advance Notice and were well prepared
for most questions." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3); the 1CR
introduction's demand-range statement — "ranging from some designed to be accessible
for all candidates, to others meant to be challenging for even the most able"
(SRC-ER-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-1CR/er.pdf p3).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-037 — The AO3 methodology warning

**Claim:** the 2025 report converts practical-qualitative weakness into named AO3 deficits — missing specific detail, error-direction reasoning, methodological-improvement proposals and control-variable identification. Where earlier warnings named answer types, the 2025 report names skill deficits inside AO3 — the demand has moved from knowing the test to designing and critiquing the method. That is a specification-level expectation, not a one-session anomaly.
**Evidence:** "Responses to Q07(b), Q07(e), and Q10(a) often lacked specific detail or failed to consider the direction of error." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2025-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3); "This highlights qualitative analysis as a key area for improvement within AO3, including proposing methodological improvements to increase accuracy and identifying appropriate control variables." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2025-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-038 — Terminology precision as the named long-response discriminator

**Claim:** the report's long-response warning cites intermolecular-forces misuse as its instance of a firm-grasp failure — terminology, not effort, is the named cause. The instance matters because the same vocabulary failure recurs across the corpus's warning record: the 2019 2CR report had already flagged candidates over-explaining description parts with intermolecular forces. Terminology discipline is thus the through-line of the long-response warnings.
**Evidence:** "Long-response questions proved challenging for those candidates who lacked a firm grasp of key terminology (eg incorrect use of intermolecular forces)" (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2025-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3).
; "Quite a few candidates felt the need to explain the differences in terms of intermolecular forces." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2019-06/4CH1-2CR/er.pdf p3).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### EXM-039 — Answer the set question: the draft-first reminder

**Claim:** the extended-response warning is explicit that answers drifted off the set question, and states the remedy in technique terms. The remedy is procedural rather than chemical — draft, then tick each printed demand off against the draft — and the same introduction names the questions where application difficulty bit hardest, giving the reminder a concrete revision target.
**Evidence:** "In extended response questions, many candidates did not answer the question given and should be encouraged to draft their ideas first or tick parts of the question off when they appear in the answer." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3).
; "Candidates found questions difficult where they needed to apply their knowledge, most notably Q3(b)(ii), Q3(c) and Q9(a)(iii)." (SRC-ER-4CH1, 2022-06/4CH1-1C/er.pdf p3).
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
