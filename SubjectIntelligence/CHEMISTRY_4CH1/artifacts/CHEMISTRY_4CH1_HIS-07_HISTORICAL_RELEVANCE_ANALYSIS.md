# HIS-07 — Historical Relevance Analysis

## Purpose

This artifact renders the relevance verdict the packed lineage supports: which
parts of the legacy corpus remain load-bearing for a current-era learner, which
parts are superseded as content authority, and which parts persist in reorganized
form. It reads the legacy specification documents and examiner reports only as
LEGACY — HISTORICAL evidence, and takes every statement about what the current
qualification requires from SRC-SPEC-4CH1. The verdict is deliberately bounded to
the pack: persistence is asserted where both sides are inside the windows, and the
rest is left disclosed rather than filled. Canonical planes are untouched.

## Scope

**Covers:** the Principles-spine persistence verdict; the industrial-manufacture
restructuring verdict; the practical-assessment philosophy's evolution into
embedded italicized statements; the terminal externally-examined model's
constancy; the standing instructiveness of the legacy reports' craft guidance; and
the overall dual-use verdict for the legacy archive.

**Out of scope:** the mapping detail itself (HIS-02), assessment numbers
(HIS-03), and the pattern pairs (HIS-06).

## Executive Summary

The relevance verdict splits the legacy corpus into three tiers. The Principles
spine — states of matter through electrolysis — persists structurally into the
current topic 1, so legacy material there remains conceptually aligned even where
its wording is superseded. The legacy industrial-manufacture territory is the
opposite case: its named subsection and numbered Haber conditions have no named
current home, so its content authority is superseded and only its craft lesson —
learn the conditions that are often asked — retains classroom value. Between the
poles sit the practical philosophy and the examination model: investigative skills
were always assessed through examination papers, and the current regime embeds
practicals as italicized specification points while keeping the assessment
written. Finally, the legacy reports' craft guidance — clear diagrams, reading the
question, precision — is era-independent instruction that remains instructive as
technique, never as content authority.

### HIS-041 — The legacy reports' craft guidance remains instructive as technique

**Claim:** the legacy examiner reports' guidance on examination craft — drawing
clear diagrams and restarting rather than patching, and answering exactly what was
asked (a name where a name is asked, a formula where a formula is asked) — is
era-independent instruction that retains classroom value under the current

**Evidence:** the June 2018 report advises: "Candidates should be encouraged to

question asked for a name, a surprising number of candidates chose to give a
formula. Correct formulae (usually Fe2O3) were accepted, but incorrect ones

witnesses are LEGACY — HISTORICAL.
