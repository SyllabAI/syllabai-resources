# HIS-05 — Question Evolution

## Purpose

This artifact traces the evolution of question presentation and marking convention
across the lineage, as the sample materials, the live examiner reports and the
current specification present them: the standing candidate instructions inside
legacy sample papers, the answer-space presentation of legacy question pages, the
legacy sample mark schemes' ACCEPT/NOT conventions, the current specification's
declared question-style mix, a live instance of that mix in the packed 2019-06
current paper, and the paper-handling conventions the legacy reports policed.
Legacy claims quote only LEGACY — HISTORICAL sources; the current mix grounds on
SRC-SPEC-4CH1, with the packed current-paper instance carried as SUPPLEMENTARY
texture. Canonical planes are untouched.

## Scope

**Covers:** the 2008 and 2011 sample papers' cover instructions; dotted-line answer
presentation and per-part mark tagging; sample mark-scheme notes conventions;
multiple-choice questions inside the current declared mix; and ruler/annotation
conventions documented by the legacy reports.

**Out of scope:** mark totals and weightings (HIS-03) and statement-stem wording
(HIS-04).

## Executive Summary

Question craft changes less than content does. The 2008 sample paper and the 2011
sample paper carry the same operative instructions — answer all questions, show all
the steps in any calculations and state the units — verbatim across eight years of
sample-material generations. The legacy question pages present answers on dotted
lines with per-part mark tags in brackets; the legacy sample mark schemes already
carry the ACCEPT/NOT note vocabulary that later live marking is judged by. The
current specification declares a mixture of question styles including
multiple-choice questions, and the packed opening question of the June 2019 Paper
1C is exactly such a four-option multiple-choice item on particle dissolution. The
legacy reports' convention record — rulers stipulated on front covers and enforced,
annotations crossed out and redrawn — completes the picture of stable craft
expectations underneath the changing content.

## Main Analysis

### HIS-025 — The 2008 sample paper fixes the standing candidate instructions

**Claim:** the legacy 2008 sample Paper 1 instructs candidates to answer all
questions, write answers in the spaces provided, and show all calculation steps
with units — under a two-hour, 120-mark cover.
**Evidence:** the sample paper's cover reads: "Answer ALL the questions. Write your
answers in the spaces provided in this question paper. Show all the steps in any
calculations and state the u" (SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, IGCSE2009_Chemistry_SAMs.pdf
p9).
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.

### HIS-026 — The 2011 sample paper repeats the same operative instructions verbatim

**Claim:** the 2011 sample Paper 1C carries the same instruction pair — answer all
questions in the spaces provided, and show all the steps in any calculations and
state the units — under its own two-hour cover, demonstrating instruction
continuity across the legacy sample-material generations.
**Evidence:** the 2011 sample paper's instructions read: "t Answer all questions.
t Answer the questions in the spaces provided – there may be more space than you
need. t Show all the steps in any calculations and state the units."
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, INT_GCSE_CHEM_SPECSAMS_ISS5_comb.PDF p53). The 't' bullets
are the document's own instruction-list markers as extracted.
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.

### HIS-027 — Legacy question pages present answers on dotted lines with per-part mark tags

**Claim:** the legacy sample question pages lay out each answer on dotted response
lines with the mark allocation tagged per part in brackets, as the packed
electrolysis-style question page shows.
**Evidence:** the sample page reads: "(c) By the end of the experiment the water in
the trough is acidic. A sample is tested with universal indicator. (i) State the
colour of universal indicator at the end of the test." — the response then sits on
a dotted line tagged "(1)", and part (ii) repeats the pattern: "Name the acid
formed in the water and give its formula." with the answer dotted after "Name"
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, IGCSE2009_Chemistry_SAMs.pdf p41).
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.

### HIS-028 — The legacy sample mark schemes already carry the ACCEPT/NOT note vocabulary

**Claim:** the 2011 sample mark schemes use the marking-note conventions — ACCEPT
for permitted alternatives, NOT for ruled-out answers, and explicit credit rules
such as both-parts-required — that the lineage's marking culture is built on.
**Evidence:** the sample mark scheme reads: "H+ / H3O+ Cl ACCEPT in either order 2
... (ii) hydrochloric acid ... NOTE candidates can only score full marks by giving
observations for BOTH tubes NOT 'hydrogen produced' NOT 'magnesium dissolves'"
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, INT_GCSE_CHEM_SPECSAMS_ISS5_comb.PDF p112); the next page's
scheme adds: "monomer = propene / propylene AND polymer = poly(propene) /
polypropylene Both must be correct for 1 mark"
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, INT_GCSE_CHEM_SPECSAMS_ISS5_comb.PDF p113).
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.

### HIS-029 — The current specification declares a mixed question style and the packed current paper instantiates it

**Claim:** the current specification declares Paper 1C to be a mixture of different
question styles including multiple-choice questions, short-answer questions,
calculations and extended open-response questions; and the packed opening question
of the June 2019 Paper 1C is a four-option multiple-choice item asking which
statement explains a potassium permanganate crystal's dissolution.
**Evidence:** the current assessment table states: "A mixture of different question
styles, including multiple-choice questions, short-answer questions, calculations
and extended open-response questions." (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p33); the packed current paper
reads: "## Question 1 (4 marks) Potassium permanganate is a purple solid that is
soluble in water. A crystal of potassium permanganate is placed in a beaker
containing water. ... After a short time, the crystal becomes smaller and the
liquid at the bottom of the beaker becomes purple. Which statement explains this
observation?" with options "(A) the crystal condenses in the water (B) the crystal
dissolves in the water (C) the crystal evaporates in the water (D) the crystal
melts in the water" (SRC-QPMS-4CH1, questions.md lines 5-19). The specification
claim is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE; the paper instance is SUPPLEMENTARY texture.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-030 — The legacy reports police paper-handling conventions the covers stipulate

**Claim:** the legacy examiner reports enforce front-cover stipulations and
annotation discipline — rulers required by the cover but not used, curves drawn
over straight lines, and instrument precision in reading scales — documenting a
convention layer around the questions themselves.
**Evidence:** the June 2016 report states: "candidates did not have or use rulers
despite the need for a ruler being stipulated on the front cover of the paper.
There were a few instances of a curve being drawn to join two previously drawn
straight lines. Some candidates would have benefited in (ii) from using a sharp
pencil to draw their lines." (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2016-06/4CH0-1CR/er.pdf p6).
Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL.
