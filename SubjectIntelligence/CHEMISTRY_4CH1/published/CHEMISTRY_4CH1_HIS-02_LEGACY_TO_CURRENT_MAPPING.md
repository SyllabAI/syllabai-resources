# HIS-02 — Legacy → Current Mapping

## Purpose

This artifact maps the legacy 4CH0 specification's content architecture onto the
current 4CH1 specification's architecture as the two documents themselves present
it, using only the bounded excerpts in this artifact's evidence pack. It records
where the legacy spine survives verbatim, where sub-topic names were rewritten, and
where legacy content has no named current home. Legacy wording is quoted only as
LEGACY — HISTORICAL evidence about the legacy document; every statement about what
the current specification requires grounds on SRC-SPEC-4CH1. Canonical planes are
untouched — anchors are deliberately not written.

## Scope

**Covers:** the Principles-of-chemistry sub-topic spine across generations; the
statement-numbering and wording migration at the opening of the qualification; the
fate of the legacy industrial-manufacture subsection; the board's own
legacy-to-current mapping commitment; the reorganization of inorganic content; and
the extension of the bold-content paper-split law with the C-suffix convention.

**Out of scope:** assessment-objective and paper-model change (HIS-03), naming
evolution as such (HIS-04), and which legacy patterns persist in live assessment
(HIS-06).

## Executive Summary

The mapping is structural before it is textual. The legacy Principles section's
nine sub-topics (a)–(i) reappear in the current topic 1 as nine sub-topics (a)–(i)
under rewritten names — Atoms and Atomic structure consolidate around a retained
Atomic structure heading, and the ionic/covalent/metallic trio moves from
compound-type names to bond-type names. Opening statements renumber and reword:
legacy 1.1/1.2 speak of describing interconversions and recalling names, the
current 1.1/1.2 speak of understanding states and interconversions in terms of
particle arrangement, movement and energy. The legacy Section 5's industrial
manufacture subsection — the Haber process with its numbered conditions — has no
named counterpart in the current four-topic architecture, whose ammonia mentions
surface in covalent-bonding and acid-base contexts instead. The board itself
publishes mapping documents highlighting key differences between the new and the
2011 legacy qualifications, and the paper-split law extends: bold content remains
Paper-2-only, now joined by the C-suffix convention.

### HIS-008 — Opening statements renumber and reword around understanding

**Claim:** the legacy statements 1.1–1.3 ask students to understand arrangement,
movement and energy of particles and to describe interconversions and recall their
names; the current 1.1–1.2 consolidate the same territory into "understand" forms
that name the interconversions and their particle-level terms inside the statement.
**Evidence:** the legacy text reads: "1.1 understand the arrangement, movement and
energy of the particles in each of the three states of matter: solid, liquid and
gas 1.2 describe how the interconversion of solids, liquids and gases are achieved
and recall the names used for these interconversions" (SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, Issue
3.pdf p9); the current text reads: "1.1 understand the three states of matter in
terms of the arrangement, movement and energy of the particles 1.2 understand the
interconversions between the three states of matter in terms of: • the names of
the interconversions • how they are" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p17). The legacy wording is
LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the current wording is CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-009 — The legacy industrial-manufacture subsection has no named current home

covalent-bonding and acid-base statement contexts instead.
**Evidence:** the legacy text reads: "d) The industrial manufacture of chemicals

process, including the essential conditions: i a temperature of about 450°C ii a
pressure of about 200 atmospheres iii an iron catalyst"
(SRC-LEGACY-SPEC-4CH0, INT_GCSE_CHEM_SPECSAMS_ISS5_comb.PDF p28); the current
topic map reads: "1 Principles of chemistry 11 2 Inorganic chemistry 16 3
Physical chemistry 21 4 Organic chemistry 23" (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,

(SRC-SPEC-4CH1, international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p20) and in
statement 2.38 ("know that metal oxides, metal hydroxides and ammonia can act as
bases") (SRC-SPEC-4CH1,
international-gcse-chemistry-2017-specification.pdf p25). The legacy subsection is
LEGACY — HISTORICAL; the current statements are CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.
Temporal scope: CURRENT — AUTHORITATIVE.

### HIS-047 — The separation-techniques demand grammar is examiner-documented at mid-era

**Claim:** the legacy ER documents exactly the separation-techniques demand grammar

produce a separation, it is not as good as fractional distillation, and the question
called for the best method of separation to be given" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY,
2013-06/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p3). Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. The mapping of these
techniques into the current qualification is the finding of record of HIS-007 and
HIS-011; this record adds the examiner-side witness that the demand was
method-selection under best-method wording, not merely technique recognition.
### HIS-048 — The ionic half-equation demand is examiner-documented at the era's end

**Claim:** the final legacy session in the pack documents the ionic half-equation

(SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2019-01/4CH0-2C/er.pdf p5). Temporal scope: LEGACY —
HISTORICAL. The wrong-answer forms the examiners preserved (electron

own wrong-answer census remains instructive as technique.
### HIS-049 — The bond-energy calculation demand is examiner-documented with sign discipline

**Claim:** the legacy ER documents bond-energy calculations as a scored demand with

Temporal scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. The demand (bond-energy arithmetic with a
signed result) is carried into the current qualification's energetics content per

the sign is not cosmetic but mark-bearing.
### HIS-050 — Precision of answer at paper end is examiner-stated as a marking law

**Claim:** the legacy examiners state a precision law for identification items late
in the paper: near the end, similar names are not interchangeable, with iodine versus
iodide as the recorded example. **Evidence:** the Summer 2012 Paper 1C report states
"Candidates need to be aware that, particularly towards the end of the paper, a high
level of precision of answer is required. Hence 'iodine' is not an acceptable
alternative to 'iodide'" (SRC-ER-4CH0-LEGACY, 2012-06/4CH0-1C/er.pdf p7). Temporal
scope: LEGACY — HISTORICAL. This is examiner-side evidence for the
terminology-precision discipline HIS-024 established from the specification side,
and it locates the discipline in the paper's difficulty gradient (precision
expectations rise with question position), which is itself technique-relevant to
any learner working legacy papers for practice.
