In this reaction the temperature increases at first but then decreases. The
reaction between the acid and the alkali is exothermic. At the beginning the
temperature goes up because the acid reacts with the alkali, giving out heat.
But when all the alkali has been used up, we are just adding cold acid to o ur
warm solution (there is no reaction because there is nothing for the acid to
react with) and the temperature goes down.
Two lines of best fit can be drawn on our graph. The point where the lines
cross represents complete neutralisation. From the graph, we can identify the
maximum temperature reached during the experiment as 31.8° C. At this point ,
28.00cm3 of acid has been used.

To work out how much heat is released in the neutralisation reaction, we can
use the same calculation as before.
The total volume of the solution at the neutralisation point is 25 + 28 = 53cm3.
Heat given out is shown by Q = mcLJ. T = 53 x 4.18 x (31.8 - 19.3) = 2769.3J.
