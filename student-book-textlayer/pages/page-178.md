BASES
Bases are substances that neutralise acids by combining with the hydrogen
ions in them. When we are referring to a base at this level we usually mean a
metal oxide, a metal hydroxide or ammonia (NH:,).

ALKALIS

Some bases dissolve in water to form solutions containing hydroxide ions.
These are alkalis.
Alkalis are a source of hydroxide (OH-) ions in solution.
Examples of alkalis are sodium hydroxide and potassium hydroxide (all the
Group 1 hydroxides). When sodium hydroxide is in water it breaks apart to
form sodium and hydroxide ions:
NaOH(aq) --. Na•(aq) + OH-(aq)
The other alkali you will meet is a solution of ammonia (NH:,). The ammonia
reacts with the water to form ammonium ions and hydroxide ions:
NH3(aq) + HP(l) .= NH4 •(aq) + OH-(aq)
These alkalis all have a pH greater than 7.
