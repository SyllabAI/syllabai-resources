280 ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

SUBSTITUTION

ALKANES

Alkanes react with halogens in the presence of ultraviolet radiation (UV
light), for example from sunlight. A hydrogen atom in the alkane is replaced by
a halogen atom. This is known as a substitution reaction because one atom
has been replaced (or substituted) by a different one.
EXTENSION WORK
UV light is required for substitution reactions to split halogen molecules into atoms.
Energy is needed to break the covalent bonds between halogen atoms. The atoms
formed are called free radicals due to the presence of unpaired electrons on them. These
are very reactive species which then go on to react with the usually unreactive alkanes.

A mixture of methane and bromine gas is orange because of the presence of
the bromine. If it is exposed to sunlight, it loses its colour, and a mixture of
bromomethane and hydrogen bromide gases is formed.
