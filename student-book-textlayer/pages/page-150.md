BURNING MAGNESIUM

Magnesium burns in oxygen with an extremely
bright white flame to give a white, powdery ash
of magnesium oxide:
2Mg(s) + 0 2(g) -+ 2Mg0(s)
The white powder formed is not very soluble in
water but a very small amount does dissolve to
form an alkaline solution:
MgO(s) + H20(1) -+ Mg(OHh(aq)

BURNING HYDROGEN

Hydrogen burns in oxygen with a pale blue flame. The product is water:

S02(9) + H20 (I) -+ H2S03(aq)

2H2(9) + 0 2(9)-+ 2H20(I)
If you ignite a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen it will explode. This is the basis
of the 'squeaky pop' test for hydrogen (see Chapter 18).
