THEORIES OF ACIDS AND BASES

In Chapter 16, we met the idea that an acid is something that produces H*(aq)
ions in solution and an alkali is something that produces OH-(aq) ions in
solution. This is called the Arrhenius theory of acids and bases. For example,
when hydrogen chloride gas is dissolved in water to form hydrochloric acid the
molecules dissociate to form Ht ions:

HCl(aq) — H*(aq) + Cl-(aq)

A neutralisation reaction can be shown as H* from an acid reacting with OH-
from an alkali to form water.

H*(aq) + OH"(aq) > H,0(\)

The problem with this definition of acids and bases is that it only applies to
reactions that occur in aqueous solution and is not more widely applicable.
