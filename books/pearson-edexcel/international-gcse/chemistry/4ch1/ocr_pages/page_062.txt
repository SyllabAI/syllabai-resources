HINT

If you are doing this experiment, how
can you be sure that all the water has
been driven off? The best way to do
this is to heat it to constant mass. You
heat the crucible and weigh it. Keep
doing this until the mass remains
constant. If the mass has gone down
after heating there may still be water
present, but when the mass remains
constant after heating you know that
all the water has been driven off.

Usually, when you heat a salt that contains water of crystallisation, the water
is driven off, leaving the anhydrous salt behind. Hydrated barium chloride
is a commonly used example because the barium chloride itself doesn’t
decompose even on quite strong heating.

If you heated barium chloride crystals in a crucible you might obtain these
results:

Mass of crucible = 30.00g
Mass of crucible + barium chloride crystals, BaCl,-nH,O = 32.44g
Mass of crucible + anhydrous barium chloride, BaCl, = 32.089

To find n, you need to find the ratio of the number of moles of BaCl, to the
number of moles of water. It’s just another empirical formula calculation
(A, of H = 1, A, of O = 16, A, of Cl = 35.5, A, of Ba = 137).

Mass of BaCl, = 32.08 - 30.00 = 2.08g
Mass of water = 32.44 — 32.08 = 0.36g

CALCULATIONS USING MOLES, CHEMICAL EQUATIONS AND
MASSES OF SUBSTANCES

INTERPRETING SYMBOLS IN
EQUATIONS IN TERMS OF MOLES
KEY POINT

The big numbers in front of the
formulae tell us the number of moles
of each substance that react. Although
there does not appear to be a big
number in front of the CH, and CO,,
there should actually be a ‘1’ in front of
each but we just don’t write it.

If we write a chemical equation:

CH, + 20, + CO; + 2H,0
we can say that 1 molecule of CH, combines with 2 molecules of O, to
form 1 molecule of CO, and 2 molecules of water. When doing calculations,

however, it is often more useful to think about this in terms of moles and take
symbols as meaning moles of each substance. So here we can say:

1 mol CH, reacts with 2mol O, to form 1 mol CO, and 2mol H,O

We have not changed the meaning of the equation as the ratios are still the
same.
