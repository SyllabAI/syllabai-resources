HINT

To find the mass of Fe we multiply the
number of moles of Fe by the mass of
1 mole of Fe (56 g). A common mistake
is to multiply the number of moles by
the mass of 2Fe (112 g), but we have
already used the '2' when we worked
out the number of moles of Fe.

The calculation can also be done in a different way using ratios:
CaCO,(s) + CaO(s) + CO,(g)
Interpret the equation in terms of moles:
1mol CaCO, produces 1 mol CaO (and 1 mol CO.)
Substitute masses where relevant:
100g (1 mol) CaCO, produces 56g (1 mol) CaO
Do a proportion calculation:
If 100g of calcium carbonate gives 56g of calcium oxide

1g of calcium carbonate gives 9 of calcium oxide = 0.56g

25g of calcium carbonate gives 25 x 0.56g of calcium oxide = 14g of
calcium oxide

A PROBLEM ABOUT EXTRACTING IRON

The equation below is for a reaction that occurs in the extraction of iron:
FeO, + 3C — 2Fe + 3CO
Calculate the mass of iron which can be formed from 1000qg of iron oxide.

From the chemical equation we can see that 1 mol FeO, produces 2mol Fe.
We know this because of the big numbers in front of the formulae.

So we know that, if we start with a certain number of moles of Fe,O;, we will
obtain twice as many moles of Fe.

So 6.25 mol Fe,O, produces 2 x 6.25 = 12.5mol Fe:
mass = number of moles x mass of 1 mole
The mass of 12.5mol Fe is 12.5 x 56 = 700g.

ALTERNATIVE METHOD IN TERMS
OF RATIOS

First interpret the equation in terms of moles:
1 mol FeO, reacts with C to form 2mol Fe (and CO,).

We are only looking at how much iron is produced, so let’s introduce just these
masses.

160g (1 mol) of Fe,O; produces 2 x 56g (2 mol) of Fe.
That is, 160g of Fe,O; produces 112g of Fe.

From this we can calculate that 1g of FeO, will produce 112/160g of Fe, and
therefore 1000g of Fe will produce 1000 x 112/160 = 700g.
