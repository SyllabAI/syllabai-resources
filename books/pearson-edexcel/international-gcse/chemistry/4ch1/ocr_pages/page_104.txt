Graphite has high melting and boiling points. To melt/boil graphite, you don’t
just have to separate the layers, you have to break up the whole structure,
including the covalent bonds. That needs very large amounts of energy
because the covalent bonds are so strong.

Graphite conducts electricity. If you look back at Figure 8.30, you will see that
each carbon atom is joined to only three others. Each carbon atom uses three
of its outer shell electrons to form three single covalent bonds. The fourth
electron in the outer shell of each atom is free to move around throughout

the whole of the layer. The electrons that are free to move throughout the
layers are called delocalised electrons. The movement of these delocalised
electrons allows graphite to conduct electricity.
