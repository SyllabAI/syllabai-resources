PRINCIPLES OF CHEMISTRY

You may have come across diagrams
of the atom in which the electrons are
drawn orbiting the nucleus rather like
planets around the sun. This can be
misleading.

Electrons are constantly moving in

the atom and it is impossible to know
exactly where they are at any moment
in time. You can only identify that they
have a particular energy and that they
are likely to be found in a certain region
of space at some particular distance
from the nucleus. Electrons with
different energies are found at different
distances from the nucleus.

ATOMIC NUMBER AND MASS NUMBER

The number of protons in an atom’s nucleus is called its atomic number or
proton number. Each of the 118 different elements has a different number of
protons. For example, if an atom has 8 protons it must be an oxygen atom:

atomic number = number of protons
The mass number (sometimes known as the nucleon number) counts the
total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of the atom:

mass number = number of protons + number of neutrons
