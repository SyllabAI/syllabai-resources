EXOTHERMIC REACTION

Some chemical reactions give out energy in the form of heat. A reaction that
gives out heat to the surroundings is said to be exothermic. If you are holding
a test-tube in which an exothermic reaction is occurring, you will notice that
the test-tube gets warmer.

An example of an exothermic reaction is adding water to calcium oxide. If you
add water to solid calcium oxide, the heat produced is enough to boil the water
and produce steam. Calcium hydroxide is produced.

CaO(s) + H2O(I) + Ca(OH),(s)

In an exothermic reaction, the
products of the reaction have

less (chemical) energy than the
reactants. In the reaction, chemical
energy (stored in the bonds of
chemicals) is converted to heat
energy, which is released to the
surroundings. The temperature

of the reaction mixture and its
surroundings goes up.
