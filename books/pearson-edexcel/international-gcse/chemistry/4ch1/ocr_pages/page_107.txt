METALLIC BONDING

Sodium is a metal. When sodium atoms bond together to form the solid metal,
the outer electron on each sodium atom becomes free to move throughout
the whole structure. The electrons are said to be delocalised. These electrons
are no longer attached to particular atoms or pairs of atoms, instead you can
think of them as flowing around through the whole metal. When a sodium
atom loses its outer electron a sodium ion (Na*) is left behind. A metallic
structure consists of a /attice (regular arrangement) of positive ions in a sea of
delocalised electrons (Figure 9.3).
