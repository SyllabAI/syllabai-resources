A carbon atom has 4 electrons in its outer shell. By sharing 1 electron with
each of 4 hydrogen atoms the C will have 8 electrons in its outer shell and

each H will have 2 electrons in its outer shell. Therefore C forms 4 covalent
bonds, 1 with each H atom. Methane has the formula CH,.

A nitrogen atom has 5 electrons in its outer shell. It will therefore share 3 other
electrons to have 8 electrons in its outer shell. In ammonia, a nitrogen atom
forms 3 covalent bonds, 1 with each H atom.

The formula of ammonia is NH3.

By understanding the bonding in a covalent molecule it is possible to
work out the shape of the molecule. Pairs of electrons in the outer shell
of the central atom repel each other and will therefore tend to get as far
apart as possible. For example, in a methane molecule there are four
pairs of electrons around the central C atom and for these to be as far
away from each other as possible, they must be arranged in a tetrahedral
shape. A tetrahedron (adjective: tetrahedral) is a triangular pyramid.
