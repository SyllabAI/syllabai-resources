# The Cosine Rule

Learn how to solve non-right-angled triangles when Pythagoras and basic trigonometry are not enough.

**Practice questions**

1. Two sides of a triangle are 6 cm and 8 cm, and the angle between them is $50^\circ$. Which side can be found directly using the Cosine Rule?

   - The 6 cm side

   - The side adjacent to the $50^\circ$ angle

   - The side opposite the $50^\circ$ angle ✔

   - The 8 cm side

   _Explanation:_ When two sides and their included angle are known, the Cosine Rule finds the side opposite that angle.

2. Two sides of a triangle are 8 cm and 11 cm, with an included angle of $60^\circ$. Find the third side to 1 decimal place.

   - $9.8$ cm ✔

   - $97.0$ cm

   - $13.6$ cm

   - $16.5$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Using $c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$ gives $c^2=8^2+11^2-2(8)(11)\cos 60^\circ=97$. Therefore, $c=\sqrt{97}=9.8$ cm to 1 decimal place.

3. A triangle has sides 5 cm, 7 cm and 9 cm. Find the angle between the 5 cm and 7 cm sides to 1 decimal place.

   - $50.7^\circ$

   - $84.3^\circ$

   - $33.6^\circ$

   - $95.7^\circ$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The required angle is opposite the 9 cm side. Therefore, $\cos C=\frac{5^2+7^2-9^2}{2(5)(7)}=-0.1$, so $C=\cos^{-1}(-0.1)=95.7^\circ$.

4. A triangle has sides $x+2$ cm and $2x$ cm with an included angle of $60^\circ$. The opposite side is $\sqrt{52}$ cm. What is the valid value of $x$?

   - $x=-4$

   - $x=4$ ✔

   - $x=\pm4$

   - $x=16$

   _Explanation:_ The Cosine Rule gives $52=(x+2)^2+(2x)^2-2(x+2)(2x)\cos60^\circ=3x^2+4$. Hence $x^2=16$, but $x=-4$ would produce negative side lengths, so only $x=4$ is valid.

5. Two sides of a triangle are 10 m and 8 m. Their included angle is obtuse and the area is $24\text{ m}^2$. Find the perimeter to 3 significant figures.

   - $24.0$ m

   - $30.8$ m

   - $35.1$ m ✔

   - $35.8$ m

   _Explanation:_ From the area formula, $24=\frac12(10)(8)\sin C$, so $\sin C=0.6$. As $C$ is obtuse, $\cos C=-0.8$. The third side is $\sqrt{10^2+8^2-2(10)(8)(-0.8)}=\sqrt{292}$ m, giving a perimeter of $10+8+\sqrt{292}=35.1$ m.
