# Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Learn how gradients reveal which straight lines are parallel or perpendicular, then build their equations from points.

**Practice questions**

1. Rearrange $3x+6y=12$ into $y=mx+c$ form. What is the gradient?

   - $-\frac{1}{2}$ ✔

   - $\frac{1}{2}$

   - $-2$

   - $3$

   _Explanation:_ Subtracting $3x$ and dividing by 6 gives $y=-\frac{1}{2}x+2$, so the gradient is $-\frac{1}{2}$.

2. A line is parallel to $y=-3x+4$ and passes through $(0,-2)$. What is its equation?

   - $y=3x-2$

   - $y=-3x+4$

   - $y=-3x-2$ ✔

   - $y=-\frac{1}{3}x-2$

   _Explanation:_ Parallel lines have the same gradient, so the gradient is $-3$. Since the line passes through $(0,-2)$, its $y$-intercept is $-2$, giving $y=-3x-2$.

3. What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to $y=\frac{2}{5}x-7$?

   - $\frac{5}{2}$

   - $-\frac{5}{2}$ ✔

   - $-\frac{2}{5}$

   - $\frac{2}{5}$

   _Explanation:_ Perpendicular gradients have a product of $-1$. The negative reciprocal of $\frac{2}{5}$ is $-\frac{5}{2}$.

4. A line passes through $(-2,1)$ and $(4,10)$. A parallel line passes through $(2,-1)$. What is the equation of the parallel line?

   - $y=\frac{2}{3}x-\frac{7}{3}$

   - $y=-\frac{3}{2}x+2$

   - $y=\frac{3}{2}x+4$

   - $y=\frac{3}{2}x-4$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The first line has gradient $\frac{10-1}{4-(-2)}=\frac{9}{6}=\frac{3}{2}$. Substituting $(2,-1)$ into $y=\frac{3}{2}x+c$ gives $-1=3+c$, so $c=-4$.

5. Line A passes through $(0,2)$ and $(6,5)$. Line B passes through $(1,7)$ and $(k,1)$ and is perpendicular to Line A. What is $k$?

   - $-2$

   - 3

   - 4 ✔

   - 7

   _Explanation:_ Line A has gradient $\frac{5-2}{6-0}=\frac{1}{2}$, so Line B has gradient $-2$. Therefore $\frac{1-7}{k-1}=-2$, and solving $\frac{-6}{k-1}=-2$ gives $k=4$.
