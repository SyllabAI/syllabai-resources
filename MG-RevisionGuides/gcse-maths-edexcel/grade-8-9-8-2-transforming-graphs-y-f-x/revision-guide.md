# Transforming Graphs y=f(x)

Transform graphs by tracking key points through shifts, stretches and reflections, including the tricky inside-the-bracket changes.

**Practice questions**

1. A point $(3,-1)$ lies on $y=f(x)$. Where does it move on $y=f(x)+5$?

   - $(-2,-1)$

   - $(3,-6)$

   - $(3,4)$ ✔

   - $(8,-1)$

   _Explanation:_ The $+5$ is outside $f(x)$, so the graph moves up 5 units. The $x$-coordinate stays 3 and the new $y$-coordinate is $-1+5=4$.

2. The turning point of $y=f(x)$ is $(-2,4)$. What is its new position on $y=f(x-6)$?

   - $(4,4)$ ✔

   - $(-8,4)$

   - $(-2,-2)$

   - $(-2,10)$

   _Explanation:_ Replacing $x$ with $x-6$ translates the graph 6 units to the right, so the turning point moves from $(-2,4)$ to $(4,4)$.

3. A point $(8,-3)$ lies on $y=f(x)$. Where does it move on $y=f(-2x)$?

   - $(16,-3)$

   - $(4,-3)$

   - $(-4,3)$

   - $(-4,-3)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ For the corresponding point, solve $-2x=8$, giving $x=-4$. An inside transformation changes the $x$-coordinate, while the $y$-coordinate remains $-3$.

4. A point $(-1,4)$ lies on $y=f(x)$. Where does it move on $y=-3f(x)+2$?

   - $(-1,14)$

   - $(-1,-10)$ ✔

   - $(3,6)$

   - $(-1,-6)$

   _Explanation:_ The $x$-coordinate is unchanged. The new $y$-coordinate is $-3(4)+2=-12+2=-10$, so the point becomes $(-1,-10)$.

5. The graph of $y=f(x)$ is translated 4 units to the left and then stretched vertically by scale factor 2. Which is its new equation?

   - $y=2f(x-4)$

   - $y=f(2x+4)$

   - $y=2f(x+4)$ ✔

   - $y=2f(x)+4$

   _Explanation:_ A translation 4 units left replaces $x$ with $x+4$. A vertical stretch by scale factor 2 multiplies all outputs by 2, giving $y=2f(x+4)$.
