# Recurring Decimals to Fractions

Turn repeating decimals into exact fractions using place value, powers of 10, and a neat subtraction trick.

**Practice questions**

1. Write $0.777...$ as a fraction in its simplest form.

   - $\frac{7}{10}$

   - $\frac{7}{9}$ ✔

   - $\frac{7}{99}$

   - $\frac{70}{99}$

   _Explanation:_ Let $x=0.777...$. Then $10x=7.777...$, so $10x-x=7$ and therefore $9x=7$. Hence $x=\frac{7}{9}$.

2. Write $0.2666...$ as a fraction in its simplest form.

   - $\frac{13}{50}$

   - $\frac{26}{99}$

   - $\frac{4}{15}$ ✔

   - $\frac{13}{45}$

   _Explanation:_ Let $x=0.2666...$. Subtracting $10x$ from $100x$ cancels the recurring digits: $90x=24$. Therefore $x=\frac{24}{90}=\frac{4}{15}$.

3. Write $1.5272727...$ as a fraction in its simplest form, where the $5$ occurs once and $27$ repeats.

   - $\frac{1527}{1000}$

   - $\frac{1526}{999}$

   - $\frac{509}{330}$

   - $\frac{84}{55}$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Let $x=1.5272727...$. The recurring tails align in $1000x=1527.2727...$ and $10x=15.2727...$. Subtracting gives $990x=1512$, so $x=\frac{1512}{990}=\frac{84}{55}$.

4. Let $x=0.3121212...$, where the $3$ occurs once and $12$ repeats. Which two multiples should be subtracted to cancel the recurring tails?

   - $1000x \text{ and } 10x$ ✔

   - $100x \text{ and } x$

   - $100x \text{ and } 10x$

   - $1000x \text{ and } x$

   _Explanation:_ Multiplying by $10$ moves past the one non-recurring digit. Multiplying by a further $100$ accounts for the two-digit recurring block, so the matching tails occur in $1000x$ and $10x$.

5. Work out $0.1666... \div 0.2222...$.

   - $\frac{1}{27}$

   - $\frac{4}{3}$

   - $\frac{3}{4}$ ✔

   - $\frac{2}{3}$

   _Explanation:_ First convert the decimals: $0.1666...=\frac{1}{6}$ and $0.2222...=\frac{2}{9}$. Then $\frac{1}{6}\div\frac{2}{9}=\frac{1}{6}\times\frac{9}{2}=\frac{3}{4}$.
