# Transformations

Learn how shapes move on grids using slides, flips, turns, and enlargements.

**There are 4 types of transformations we need to know:  
• Translations  
• Rotations  
• Reflections  
• Enlargements**

A translation moves a shape. All points of the shape must be moved by the same amount.

A translation can be up or down and left or right.

## Example: Describe the transformation that maps shape A onto shape B

![transformation 1](../assets/001-transformation-1.jpg)

**Practice questions**

1. A point $(-2,4)$ is translated by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}5\\-3\end{pmatrix}$. Where does it land?

   - $(3,1)$ ✔

   - $(-7,7)$

   - $(7,1)$

   - $(3,7)$

   _Explanation:_ Add the horizontal and vertical components separately: $-2+5=3$ and $4-3=1$. The image is therefore $(3,1)$.

2. A point is translated from $(6,-1)$ to $(2,4)$. What is the translation vector, written as $\begin{pmatrix}\text{horizontal movement}\\\text{vertical movement}\end{pmatrix}$?

   - $\begin{pmatrix}4\\-5\end{pmatrix}$

   - $\begin{pmatrix}-5\\4\end{pmatrix}$

   - $\begin{pmatrix}-4\\5\end{pmatrix}$ ✔

   - $\begin{pmatrix}5\\-4\end{pmatrix}$

   _Explanation:_ Compare the final coordinates with the starting coordinates: $6$ to $2$ is $4$ left, and $-1$ to $4$ is $5$ up. The vector is $\begin{pmatrix}-4\\5\end{pmatrix}$.

**Practice questions**

1. A point $(-3,1)$ is translated $5$ units right and $2$ units up. What is its image?

   - $(2,1)$

   - $(2,3)$ ✔

   - $(-8,3)$

   - $(-5,6)$

   _Explanation:_ Moving $5$ right changes the $x$-coordinate from $-3$ to $2$. Moving $2$ up changes the $y$-coordinate from $1$ to $3$, giving $(2,3)$.

2. A point $(4,-2)$ is translated $3$ units left and $6$ units down. What is its image?

   - $(7,4)$

   - $(1,4)$

   - $(1,-4)$

   - $(1,-8)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Three units left gives $4-3=1$, and six units down gives $-2-6=-8$. The image is $(1,-8)$.

## Example: Describe the transformation that maps shape A onto shape B.

![transformations 11](../assets/020-transformations-11.jpg)

Here the shape has been turned, we have a **rotation**.

To describe the transformation we also need to know the centre of rotation and how far the shape has turned (the angle)  
We can use tracing paper to help work this out.

To do this we get a piece of tracing paper and draw around the shape (shape A).

We then have to work out where to hold the tracing paper (the bit of the paper that is kept in the same position when the paper is rotated).  
You can try different positions

(3,2) is the centre of rotation

![rotation 1](../assets/021-rotation-1.gif)

At (3,2) on the tracing paper we can draw an arrow facing up

If we hold the tracing paper at (3,2) and rotate the paper we will see that the arrow is facing left when the triangle is on shape B.

We have a **rotation, 90° anticlockwise, about the centre (3,2)**

**Practice questions**

1. A point $(2,-5)$ is rotated $90^\circ$ anticlockwise about the origin. Where does it land?

   - $(-5,-2)$

   - $(5,2)$ ✔

   - $(-2,5)$

   - $(-5,2)$

   _Explanation:_ For a $90^\circ$ anticlockwise rotation about the origin, $(x,y)$ becomes $(-y,x)$. Thus $(2,-5)$ becomes $(5,2)$.

2. A point $(5,3)$ is rotated $180^\circ$ about the centre $(1,1)$. What is its image?

   - $(3,-1)$

   - $(-3,1)$

   - $(-3,-1)$ ✔

   - $(7,5)$

   _Explanation:_ Relative to $(1,1)$, the point is $4$ right and $2$ up. A $180^\circ$ rotation makes this $4$ left and $2$ down, giving $(1-4,1-2)=(-3,-1)$.


A reflection is a mirror image of a shape. For a reflection we need a line of reflection (a mirror line). When a shape is reflected it is flipped over the mirror line.

After a shape is reflected each new point is the same distance from the mirror line as the original point.

![transformations 47](../assets/022-transformations-47.jpg)

Here we have a shape reflected over a mirror line. The mirror line is shown by the dotted line.

You can see that each point and its corresponding point on the reflected shape are the same distance away from the mirror line.

## Example: Describe the single transformation that maps shape A onto shape B.

![transformations 52](../assets/027-transformations-52.jpg)

We can see that the shape has been flipped, we have a **reflection**.

We also need to know the mirror line.

The mirror line is half way between each set of corresponding points.

The mirror line is the y axis.

The transformation is **a reflection in the y axis**

**Practice questions**

1. The point $(6,2)$ is reflected in the line $y=5$. What is the image point?

   - $(6,8)$ ✔

   - $(6,3)$

   - $(4,8)$

   - $(-6,2)$

   _Explanation:_ The point is $3$ units below $y=5$, so its image is $3$ units above the line. Its $x$-coordinate stays $6$, giving $(6,8)$.

2. The point $(2,-1)$ is reflected in the line $y=x$. What is the image point?

   - $(2,1)$

   - $(-2,1)$

   - $(-1,2)$ ✔

   - $(1,-2)$

   _Explanation:_ Reflection in $y=x$ swaps the coordinates. Therefore $(2,-1)$ becomes $(-1,2)$.

An enlargement changes the size of a shape. The shape can get bigger or smaller.

To enlarge a shape we need to know the scale factor and the centre of enlargement.  
The scale factor tells us how many times bigger (or smaller) the shape is.  
The centre of enlargement is the starting point, the point from which the enlarged.

## Example: Describe the transformation that maps shape A onto shape B.

![transformations 56](../assets/031-transformations-56.jpg)

We can see that the shape has changed size. It is an **enlargement**.

Triangle A has base of 3 units and triangle B has a base of 6 units  
Triangle A has a height of 2 units and triangle B has a height of 4 units  
The scale factor is 2. (3 × 2 = 6 and 2 × 2 = 4)

To find the centre of enlargement we can extend a line through each set of corresponding points:

![transformations 57](../assets/032-transformations-57.jpg)

We can see that all of the lines meet at (2,2). This is the centre of enlargement.  
The points on B are twice as far as their corresponding points on A from (2,2).

The transformation is an **enlargement, scale factor 2 with centre of enlargement (2,2)**

**Practice questions**

1. A point $(1,-2)$ is enlarged by scale factor $2$ with centre $(0,0)$. Where does it land?

   - $(1,-4)$

   - $(2,-1)$

   - $(2,-4)$ ✔

   - $(-2,4)$

   _Explanation:_ With centre $(0,0)$, multiply both coordinates by the scale factor: $(1\times2,-2\times2)=(2,-4)$.

2. A point $(3,5)$ is enlarged by scale factor $2$ with centre $(2,2)$. What is its image?

   - $(4,8)$ ✔

   - $(6,10)$

   - $(3,6)$

   - $(0,-4)$

   _Explanation:_ From the centre $(2,2)$ to $(3,5)$ is $1$ right and $3$ up. Doubling these movements gives $2$ right and $6$ up, so the image is $(4,8)$.

## Example: Describe the single transformation that maps shape A onto shape B.

![transformations 64](../assets/039-transformations-64.jpg)

We can see that shape B is smaller than shape A. This is an **Enlargement**. (Any change in the size of the shape is called an enlargement.)

Shape A has a base of 4 units and shape B has a base of 2 units. 2 is half of 4. The scale factor is 0.5. The lengths of shape B are half the lengths of shape A.

To find the centre of enlargement we can extend lines through each corner and the corresponding corner on the other shape.

![transformations 65](../assets/040-transformations-65.jpg)

All the lines meet at (8,9). (8,9) is the centre of enlargement.

The transformation is an **enlargement, scale factor 0.5, centre (8,9)**

**Practice questions**

1. A point $(10,13)$ is enlarged by scale factor $0.5$ with centre $(8,9)$. What is its image?

   - $(9,11)$ ✔

   - $(10,11)$

   - $(9,13)$

   - $(12,17)$

   _Explanation:_ From $(8,9)$ to $(10,13)$ is $2$ right and $4$ up. Halving these movements gives $1$ right and $2$ up, so the image is $(9,11)$.

2. A side of a shape is $6$ units long before an enlargement and $9$ units long afterwards. What is the scale factor?

   - $0.5$

   - $1.5$ ✔

   - $2$

   - $3$

   _Explanation:_ The scale factor is the image length divided by the original length: $9\div6=1.5$. The shape has been enlarged by scale factor $1.5$.
