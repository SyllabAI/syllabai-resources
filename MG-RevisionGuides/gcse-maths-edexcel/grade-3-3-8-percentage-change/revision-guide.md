# Percentage Change

Learn a reliable method for comparing old and new amounts, including profits, losses and rounding.

We can calculate a percentage change if we are given an original amount and a new amount.

If the price of a train ticket increased from £50 to £60 we can calculate the percentage change using the formula:

## Percentage change = change⁄original × 100

The original price is £50 and the new price is £60.  
The change (the increase) is £60 − £50 = £10

Percentage change = 10⁄50 × 100 = 20%

**Practice questions**

1. A bicycle originally costs £240. Its price increases to £300. What is the percentage increase?

   - 25% increase ✔

   - 20% increase

   - 60% increase

   - 125% increase

   _Explanation:_ The increase is £300 − £240 = £60. Comparing this with the original £240 gives $60 \div 240 \times 100 = 25\%$.

2. A laptop originally costs £800 and is reduced to £680. What is the percentage decrease?

   - 15% increase

   - 15% decrease ✔

   - 17.6% decrease

   - 120% decrease

   _Explanation:_ The decrease is £800 − £680 = £120. Use the original price as the denominator: $120 \div 800 \times 100 = 15\%$.

**Practice questions**

1. Owen buys 1 kg of apples for £2.40, puts them into 5 equal bags, and sells each bag for 60p. What is his percentage profit?

   - 20% profit

   - 125% profit

   - 25% profit ✔

   - 25% loss

   _Explanation:_ The total selling price is $5 \times £0.60 = £3.00$, so the profit is £0.60. Comparing the profit with the original cost gives $0.60 \div 2.40 \times 100 = 25\%$.

2. A town had 4800 residents and then increased to 5125 residents. Work out the percentage increase to 3 significant figures.

   - 6.34% increase

   - 6.8% increase

   - 6.77% decrease

   - 6.77% increase ✔

   _Explanation:_ The increase is $5125 - 4800 = 325$. Comparing this with the original population gives $325 \div 4800 \times 100 = 6.7708\ldots\%$, which is $6.77\%$ to 3 significant figures.
