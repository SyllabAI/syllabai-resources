# The Nth Term of a Quadratic Sequence

Learn how second differences lead to the formula for any quadratic sequence.

**Practice questions**

1. The nth term of a sequence is $2n^2 + 3n - 1$. What is the 5th term?

   - 24

   - 43

   - 64 ✔

   - 114

   _Explanation:_ Substitute $n=5$, applying the square first: $2(5^2)+3(5)-1=50+15-1=64$.

2. The sequence $6, 14, 26, 42, \ldots$ is quadratic. What is the coefficient of $n^2$ in its nth term?

   - 4

   - 8

   - $-2$

   - 2 ✔

   _Explanation:_ The first differences are $8,12,16$, so the constant second difference is $4$. The coefficient of $n^2$ is half the second difference, so it is $2$.

3. A quadratic sequence begins $4, 9, 16, 25, \ldots$. What are the next two terms?

   - $36, 49$ ✔

   - $34, 45$

   - $35, 48$

   - $36, 47$

   _Explanation:_ The first differences are $5,7,9$, increasing by $2$ each time. The next differences are therefore $11$ and $13$, giving $25+11=36$ and $36+13=49$.

4. Find the nth term of the quadratic sequence $10, 7, 2, -5, \ldots$

   - $n^2+9$

   - $-n^2+11$ ✔

   - $-n^2+9$

   - $-n^2-11$

   _Explanation:_ The first differences are $-3,-5,-7$, so the second difference is $-2$ and the quadratic part is $-n^2$. Comparing $-1,-4,-9,-16$ with the sequence shows that $11$ must be added, giving $-n^2+11$.

5. A sequence has nth term $n^2+4n+3$. Which term has value $63$?

   - The 10th term

   - There is no such term

   - The 6th term ✔

   - The 8th term

   _Explanation:_ Set $n^2+4n+3=63$. This gives $n^2+4n-60=0$, which factorises to $(n+10)(n-6)=0$. Since a term number must be positive, $n=6$.
