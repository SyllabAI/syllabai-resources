# Gradient of a Line

Learn how to measure the steepness of a straight line using grids, coordinates, and missing values.

**Practice questions**

1. From left to right, a line goes 5 squares across and 10 squares down. What is its gradient?

   - 2

   - $\frac{1}{2}$

   - $-2$ ✔

   - $-\frac{1}{2}$

   _Explanation:_ Gradient is rise divided by run. Going down gives a change in $y$ of $-10$, so the gradient is $\frac{-10}{5}=-2$.

2. Find the gradient of the line through $(-3,5)$ and $(5,1)$.

   - $\frac{1}{2}$

   - $-\frac{1}{2}$ ✔

   - $-2$

   - 2

   _Explanation:_ Using $m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$ gives $m=\frac{1-5}{5-(-3)}=\frac{-4}{8}=-\frac{1}{2}$.

3. Between two points, a line goes 4 squares to the right and 12 squares up. What is its gradient?

   - 3 ✔

   - 4

   - $\frac{1}{3}$

   - 12

   _Explanation:_ Gradient is rise over run, so $m=\frac{12}{4}=3$. The fraction $\frac{1}{3}$ comes from incorrectly swapping the rise and run.

4. A line passes through $(1,-3)$ and $(k,9)$. Its gradient is 4. What is $k$?

   - 3

   - 5

   - 12

   - 4 ✔

   _Explanation:_ Set up $4=\frac{9-(-3)}{k-1}=\frac{12}{k-1}$. Therefore $k-1=3$, so $k=4$.

5. A line passes through $(4,-2)$ and $(4,6)$. What is its gradient?

   - 0

   - 8

   - undefined ✔

   - $\frac{1}{8}$

   _Explanation:_ The change in $x$ is $4-4=0$, so calculating the gradient would require division by zero. Therefore, the vertical line has an undefined gradient.
