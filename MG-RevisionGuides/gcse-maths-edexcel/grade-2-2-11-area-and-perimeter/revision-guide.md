# Area and Perimeter

Learn to measure around shapes and calculate the space inside them for common GCSE questions.

## The area of a shape is the space inside the shape. Area is measured in squares.

### Finding the area of a rectangle

Here is a rectangle drawn on a centimetre grid.

![area and perimeter 1](../assets/001-area-and-perimeter-1.jpg)

To find the area of a rectangle multiply the length by the width.  
(We could also count the squares but this will not work from more complex questions)

In this example we have a length of 6cm and a width of 3cm.

Area = length × width  
Area = 6 × 3  
**Area = 18 cm2**

The units are cm2: one cm2 is a square that measures 1cm by 1 cm.

**Example 1: A rectangle is drawn on a centimetre grid Calculate the area of the rectangle**

Area = length × width  
Area = 5 × 4  
**Area = 20cm2**


**Practice questions**

1. A rectangle is $9$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. What is its area?

   - $13\text{ cm}^2$

   - $26\text{ cm}^2$

   - $36\text{ cm}^2$ ✔

   - $45\text{ cm}^2$

   _Explanation:_ The area of a rectangle is length × width, so $9 \times 4 = 36\text{ cm}^2$.

2. A square has an area of $81\text{ cm}^2$. What is its side length?

   - $40.5$ cm

   - $9$ cm ✔

   - $18$ cm

   - $81$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The side length multiplied by itself gives $81$. Since $9 \times 9 = 81$, the side length is $9$ cm.

### Finding the area of a triangle

We can think of a triangle as half of a rectangle

![area and perimeter 9](../assets/003-area-and-perimeter-9.jpg)

To calculate the area of a triangle we can half the area of a triangle

The area of a triangle = 1⁄2 × base × height

**Example 2: Here is a triangle drawn on a centimetre grid. Calculate the area of the triangle.**

Area = 1⁄2 × base × height  
Area = 1⁄2 × 4 × 6  
Area = 2 × 6  
**Area = 12cm2**


**Example 3: Calculate the area of the triangle.**

For the area calculation we use the perpendicular height (it must be straight up, not diagonal).  
The height of this triangle is 12 metres.

Area = 1⁄2 × base × height  
Area = 1⁄2 × 8 × 12  
Area = 4 × 12  
**Area = 48m2**


**Practice questions**

1. A triangle has a base of $12$ cm and a perpendicular height of $9$ cm. What is its area?

   - $108\text{ cm}^2$

   - $21\text{ cm}^2$

   - $42\text{ cm}^2$

   - $54\text{ cm}^2$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Use $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$: $\frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 9 = 54\text{ cm}^2$.

2. A triangle has a base of $15$ m, a perpendicular height of $8$ m and a sloping side of $17$ m. What is its area?

   - $120\text{ m}^2$

   - $60\text{ m}^2$ ✔

   - $68\text{ m}^2$

   - $40\text{ m}^2$

   _Explanation:_ Use the perpendicular height, not the sloping side. The area is $\frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 8 = 60\text{ m}^2$.

### Finding the area of a parallelogram

A parallelogram is a quadrilateral with two sets of parallel sides.

A parallelogram can be made into a rectangle by cutting off and moving a triangle:

![parallelogram to rectangle](../assets/006-parallelogram-to-rectangle.gif)

The area of a parallelogram = base × height

**Example 4: Here is a parallelogram drawn on a centimetre grid. Calculate the area of the parallelogram.**

Area = base × height  
Area = 4 × 3  
**Area = 12cm2**


**Example 5: Calculate the area of the parallelogram.**

For the area calculation we use the perpendicular height (it must be straight up, not diagonal).  
The height of this parallelogram is 11 metres.

Area = base × height  
Area = 8 × 11  
**Area = 88m2**


**Practice questions**

1. A parallelogram has a base of $7$ cm and a perpendicular height of $5$ cm. What is its area?

   - $35\text{ cm}^2$ ✔

   - $24\text{ cm}^2$

   - $12\text{ cm}^2$

   - $70\text{ cm}^2$

   _Explanation:_ The area of a parallelogram is base × perpendicular height, so $7 \times 5 = 35\text{ cm}^2$.

2. A parallelogram has an area of $96\text{ m}^2$ and a base of $12$ m. What is its perpendicular height?

   - $6$ m

   - $84$ m

   - $8$ m ✔

   - $1152$ m

   _Explanation:_ Since area = base × height, divide the area by the base: $96 \div 12 = 8$ m.

### Finding the area of a trapezium

The image below shows a trapezium, a trapezium (trapezoid in American English) is a quadrilateral with two parallel sides.

![area and perimeter 53](../assets/009-area-and-perimeter-53.jpg)

The area of a trapezium = 1⁄2(a + b) × height  
Where a and b are the lengths of the two parallel sides

This formula can be made by splitting the trapezium into a rectangle and a triangle (you don't have to know where the formula comes from, you just have to know the formula).

![trapezium](../assets/010-trapezium.gif)

**Example 6: Find the area of the trapezium**

For the trapezium above:  
Area = 1⁄2(6 + 10) × 7  
Area = 1⁄2(16) × 7  
Area = 8 × 7  
Area = 56 cm2


**Practice questions**

1. A trapezium has parallel sides of $9$ cm and $15$ cm and a perpendicular height of $6$ cm. What is its area?

   - $144\text{ cm}^2$

   - $90\text{ cm}^2$

   - $45\text{ cm}^2$

   - $72\text{ cm}^2$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Use $\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$: $\frac{1}{2}(9+15) \times 6 = 12 \times 6 = 72\text{ cm}^2$.

2. A trapezium has an area of $60\text{ cm}^2$ and parallel sides of $8$ cm and $12$ cm. What is its perpendicular height?

   - $6$ cm ✔

   - $3$ cm

   - $5$ cm

   - $10$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Half the sum of the parallel sides is $\frac{1}{2}(8+12)=10$. Therefore $10 \times h=60$, so $h=6$ cm.

The perimeter of a shape is the distance around the edge of the shape

Here is a rectangle drawn on a centimetre grid.

![area and perimeter 1](../assets/001-area-and-perimeter-1.jpg)

To find the perimeter of a shape we add up the lengths of all of the sides

This rectangle has two sides with a length of 6cm and two sides with a length of 3cm

Perimeter = 6 + 6 + 3 + 3

Perimeter = 18 cm

**Example 1: A rectangle is drawn on a centimetre grid Calculate the perimeter of the rectangle**

Perimeter = 5 + 5 + 4 + 4  
**Perimeter = 18cm**


**Example 2: Calculate the perimeter of the triangle**

Perimeter = 10 + 9 + 7  
**Perimeter = 26m**


**Practice questions**

1. A rectangle is $11$ cm long and $4$ cm wide. What is its perimeter?

   - $44$ cm

   - $30$ cm ✔

   - $15$ cm

   - $22$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Add all four sides: $11+11+4+4=30$ cm. Equivalently, $2(11+4)=30$ cm.

2. A square has an area of $49\text{ cm}^2$. What is its perimeter?

   - $14$ cm

   - $196$ cm

   - $28$ cm ✔

   - $21$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The side length is $7$ cm because $7 \times 7=49$. Its perimeter is $4 \times 7=28$ cm.

3. Two identical rectangles, each $7$ cm by $4$ cm, are joined along one full $4$ cm edge. What is the perimeter of the combined shape?

   - $36$ cm ✔

   - $44$ cm

   - $28$ cm

   - $22$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The two perimeters total $2(7+4)+2(7+4)=44$ cm. The joined $4$ cm edge is inside and was counted twice, so subtract $8$ cm: $44-8=36$ cm.
