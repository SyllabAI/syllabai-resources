# Capture Recapture

Estimate a hidden animal or object population using marked samples, proportional reasoning, and clear assumptions.

**Practice questions**

1. A wildlife worker marks 30 birds and releases them. Later, a sample of 20 birds contains 5 marked birds. Estimate the total bird population.

   - 50

   - 600

   - 120 ✔

   - 30

   _Explanation:_ Use $\dfrac{30 \times 20}{5}=120$. The number marked initially is multiplied by the size of the second sample, then divided by the number of marked animals recaptured.

2. A researcher marks 80 fish. In a second catch of 45 fish, 9 are marked. Which calculation estimates the total fish population?

   - $80+45+9$

   - $80 \times 9 \div 45$

   - $45 \times 9 \div 80$

   - $80 \times 45 \div 9$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The estimate is $\dfrac{\text{number marked initially} \times \text{second sample size}}{\text{number of marked animals recaptured}}$, so the calculation is $80 \times 45 \div 9$.

3. Which assumption helps make a capture-recapture estimate reliable?

   - The marked animals become easier to catch than unmarked animals.

   - The marked animals mix evenly back into the population. ✔

   - Many animals enter or leave the area between samples.

   - The second sample is taken from just one small part of the habitat.

   _Explanation:_ The marked animals must mix evenly so that the second sample is representative of the whole population. Migration, biased sampling or changed catchability can make the estimate unreliable.

4. A biologist marks 55 frogs and releases them. Later, a sample of 32 frogs contains 7 marked frogs. Estimate the total population to the nearest whole frog.

   - 251 ✔

   - 252

   - 94

   - 224

   _Explanation:_ Calculate $\dfrac{55 \times 32}{7}=251.428\ldots$. This rounds to $251$ frogs because the first digit after the decimal point is $4$.

5. A lake contains an estimated 300 fish, of which 60 have been marked. About how many marked fish would you expect in a representative second sample of 40 fish?

   - 5

   - 10

   - 8 ✔

   - 12

   _Explanation:_ The marked proportion is $\dfrac{60}{300}=\dfrac{1}{5}$. Therefore, the expected number marked is $\dfrac{1}{5} \times 40=8$.
