# Coordinates

Learn how to read, plot and use coordinates on a grid, including midpoints and straight lines like x equals 3.

## Writing Coordinates

We use coordinates to describe the position of a point on a grid.

Here we have a grid. On the grid is point A.

![coordinates1](../assets/001-coordinates1.jpg)

On the grid we have an x axis (which runs horizontally) and a y axis (which runs vertically).

We write coordinates in the form (x, y).  
We always write the x coordinate before the y coordinate.

The x coordinate of A is 1. It is 1 along the x axis.  
The y coordinate of A is 3. It is 3 up the y axis.

The coordinates of A are (1, 3).

Here we have another grid. On the grid is point B.

![coordinates2](../assets/002-coordinates2.jpg)

The x coordinate of B is -3. It is -3 along the x axis.  
The y coordinate of B is -2 because it is 2 down the y axis.

The coordinates of B are (-3, -2).

**Practice questions**

1. Point $P$ is at $(4,-1)$. Which movement from the origin plots $P$ correctly?

   - 4 right, 1 down ✔

   - 4 left, 1 down

   - 4 right, 1 up

   - 1 right, 4 down

   _Explanation:_ The positive x-coordinate means move 4 right, and the negative y-coordinate means move 1 down. The order is horizontal movement first, then vertical movement.

2. From the origin, you move 3 squares left and 2 squares up. What are the coordinates of the point?

   - $(3,2)$

   - $(-3,2)$ ✔

   - $(-3,-2)$

   - $(2,-3)$

   _Explanation:_ Moving 3 left gives an x-coordinate of $-3$, while moving 2 up gives a y-coordinate of $2$. Coordinates are written $(x,y)$, so the answer is $(-3,2)$.

3. In which quadrant is the point $(-4,-2)$ located?

   - First quadrant

   - Second quadrant

   - Third quadrant ✔

   - Fourth quadrant

   _Explanation:_ Both coordinates are negative: the point is left of the y-axis and below the x-axis. This places it in the third quadrant.

## Finding the Midpoint of a Line Segment

On the grid we have a line AB (the line runs from A to B).

![coordinates4](../assets/003-coordinates4.jpg)

We can find the midpoint of the line by counting how far along the x axis and how far down the y axis we need to go to get from A to B.

We need to go 6 along the x axis to get from A to B.  
We need to go 4 down the y axis to get from A to B.

From A to B we go 6 along and 4 down.  
We can find the midpoint by halving the distance.

6 ÷ 2 = 3 and 4 ÷ 2 = 2  
The midpoint is 3 along and 2 down from A.

The midpoint is at (-1, 1)

**Practice questions**

1. What is the midpoint of $(2,4)$ and $(6,0)$?

   - $(4,4)$

   - $(2,2)$

   - $(6,2)$

   - $(4,2)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Average the x-coordinates and the y-coordinates separately: $x=(2+6)\div2=4$ and $y=(4+0)\div2=2$. Therefore, the midpoint is $(4,2)$.

2. Find the midpoint of the line segment joining $(-3,7)$ and $(5,-1)$.

   - $(1,3)$ ✔

   - $(2,6)$

   - $(-4,8)$

   - $(1,4)$

   _Explanation:_ The midpoint has coordinates $((-3+5)\div2,(7+(-1))\div2)=(1,3)$. Each coordinate is found by averaging the corresponding coordinates of the endpoints.

3. The midpoint of $AB$ is $M(-2,3)$. If $A=(4,1)$, what are the coordinates of $B$?

   - $(-6,5)$

   - $(-8,5)$ ✔

   - $(0,2)$

   - $(8,-5)$

   _Explanation:_ The midpoint is halfway between the endpoints, so $B=2M-A$. Thus $B=(-4,6)-(4,1)=(-8,5)$.

## Vertical and Horizontal Lines

On the grid we have a line.

![coordinates10](../assets/004-coordinates10.jpg)

The line runs along all of the points where y = 2.

Every point on the line has a y coordinate of 2.

The line is called y = 2

Here is another line

![coordinates17](../assets/005-coordinates17.jpg)

The line runs along all of the points where x = -3

Every point on the line has a x coordinate of -3

The equation of the line is x = -3

**Practice questions**

1. Which point lies on the vertical line $x=-2$?

   - $(2,-5)$

   - $(5,-2)$

   - $(-2,5)$ ✔

   - $(-5,2)$

   _Explanation:_ Every point on the line $x=-2$ must have an x-coordinate of $-2$. The point $(-2,5)$ satisfies this; its y-coordinate can be any value.

2. Which point lies on the horizontal line $y=-4$?

   - $(3,-4)$ ✔

   - $(-4,3)$

   - $(-4,-3)$

   - $(4,3)$

   _Explanation:_ Every point on the line $y=-4$ has a y-coordinate of $-4$. The x-coordinate can be any value, so $(3,-4)$ is on the line.

3. A vertical line passes through the point $(-5,7)$. What is the equation of the line?

   - $y=7$

   - $y=-5$

   - $x=7$

   - $x=-5$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ A vertical line has a constant x-coordinate. Since the point has x-coordinate $-5$, the equation is $x=-5$.
