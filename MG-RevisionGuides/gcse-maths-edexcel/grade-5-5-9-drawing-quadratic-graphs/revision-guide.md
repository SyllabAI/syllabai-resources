# Drawing Quadratic Graphs

Learn to build tables, plot smooth parabolas, and read roots, turning points and equation solutions from quadratic graphs.

**Practice questions**

1. For $y=x^2-x-4$, which coordinate should be plotted when $x=3$?

   - $(3,-2)$

   - $(3,2)$ ✔

   - $(2,3)$

   - $(-3,2)$

   _Explanation:_ Substitute $x=3$: $y=3^2-3-4=9-3-4=2$. Therefore, the coordinate is $(3,2)$.

2. Which statement describes the graph of $y=4x-x^2$?

   - It opens upwards and has a lowest point

   - It opens upwards and has a highest point

   - It opens downwards and has a highest point ✔

   - It is a straight line

   _Explanation:_ The coefficient of $x^2$ is negative because $y=-x^2+4x$. Therefore, the quadratic graph opens downwards and has a highest point.

3. A quadratic graph crosses the $x$-axis at $(-2,0)$ and $(4,0)$. What are its roots?

   - $1$ and $-9$

   - $0$ and $0$

   - $-2$ and $0$

   - $-2$ and $4$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The roots are the $x$-coordinates where the graph crosses the $x$-axis, so the roots are $-2$ and $4$.

4. For $y=x^2-2x-1$, what is the value of $y$ when $x=-2$?

   - $7$ ✔

   - $-1$

   - $3$

   - $-9$

   _Explanation:_ Substitute $x=-2$: $y=(-2)^2-2(-2)-1=4+4-1=7$. Take care that squaring a negative number gives a positive result.

5. What is the turning point of the graph $y=x^2-6x+5$?

   - $(1,0)$

   - $(5,0)$

   - $(3,-4)$ ✔

   - $(-3,32)$

   _Explanation:_ The roots are $x=1$ and $x=5$, so the axis of symmetry is halfway between them at $x=3$. Substituting gives $y=3^2-6(3)+5=-4$, so the turning point is $(3,-4)$.
