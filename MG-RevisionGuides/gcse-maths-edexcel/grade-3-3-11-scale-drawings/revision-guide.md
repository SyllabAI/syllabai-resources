# Scale Drawings

Learn how drawings and models use scale to turn small measurements into real-life distances.

The lengths in a scale drawing are in proportion to the actual lengths of an object.

We often use a ratio to show the scale of a drawing  
If we have a scale of 1:5 all of the lengths in the scale drawing are 5 times smaller than in real life

If a map has a scale of 1:5000 to find the actual distance between two points we would have to multiply the distance on the map by 5000

If two points are 10 cm apart on the map they are 10 cm × 5000 apart in real life

10 cm × 5000 = 50000 cm  
50000 cm = 500 m

**Practice questions**

1. On a map with scale $1:50000$, two towns are $6$ cm apart. What is the real distance between them in kilometres?

   - $300$ km

   - $30$ km

   - $0.3$ km

   - $3$ km ✔

   _Explanation:_ Multiply $6$ cm by $50000$ to get $300000$ cm. Since $100000$ cm is $1$ km, this is $3$ km.

2. A scale drawing uses a scale of $1:200$. The actual length of a wall is $8$ m. What is the length of the wall on the drawing?

   - $4$ cm ✔

   - $40$ cm

   - $1.6$ cm

   - $1600$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Convert $8$ m to $800$ cm, then divide by $200$ because the drawing is smaller: $800\div200=4$ cm.


**Example: A map has a scale 1:20000  
The actual distance between two towns is 12km  
Find the distance between the two towns on the map  
Give your answer in cm**

We can convert 12 km into cm first  
To convert km to metres we times by 1000  
To convert metres into centimetres we times by 100

12 km = 12000 m = 1200000 cm

The real life distance is 20000 times bigger than the distance on the map  
To find the distance on the map we need to divide the real distance by 20000

1200000⁄20000 = 120⁄2 = 60 cm

**Practice questions**

1. A model boat is $9$ cm long and has scale $1:50$. Work out the real length in metres.

   - $45$ m

   - $4.5$ m ✔

   - $450$ m

   - $0.18$ m

   _Explanation:_ Multiply $9$ cm by $50$ to get $450$ cm. Then divide by $100$ to convert to metres, giving $4.5$ m.

2. A rectangular garden is $3$ cm by $2$ cm on a scale drawing. The scale is $1$ cm representing $4$ m. What is the actual area of the garden?

   - $24\ m^2$

   - $48\ m^2$

   - $96\ m^2$ ✔

   - $12\ m^2$

   _Explanation:_ The actual dimensions are $3\times4=12$ m and $2\times4=8$ m. The area is $12\times8=96\ m^2$.

3. An accurate scale drawing shows that a chair is about twice as tall as a box. The real box is $35$ cm tall. Estimate the real height of the chair.

   - $17.5$ cm

   - $37$ cm

   - $140$ cm

   - $70$ cm ✔

   _Explanation:_ The chair is twice the height of the box, so calculate $2\times35=70$ cm. Dividing by $2$ would give the box height from the chair height, not the other way around.
