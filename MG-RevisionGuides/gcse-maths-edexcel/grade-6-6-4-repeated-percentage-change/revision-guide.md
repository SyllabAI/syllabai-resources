# Repeated Percentage Change

Explore how percentage multipliers solve growth, decay, compound interest and shape changes efficiently.

**Practice questions**

1. An investment of £200 grows by 10% each year for 3 years. What is it worth after 3 years?

   - £220

   - £242

   - £260

   - £266.20 ✔

   _Explanation:_ A 10% increase has multiplier $1.10$. Repeating it for 3 years gives $200\times1.10^3=£266.20$.

2. A car worth £500 depreciates by 12% each year. What is it worth after 2 years?

   - £380

   - £387.20 ✔

   - £390

   - £440

   _Explanation:_ A 12% decrease has multiplier $0.88$. After 2 years, its value is $500\times0.88^2=£387.20$.

3. A price of £50 is reduced by 20% and then increased by 20%. What is the final price?

   - £48 ✔

   - £50

   - £52

   - £60

   _Explanation:_ The combined multiplier is $0.80\times1.20=0.96$, so the final price is $50\times0.96=£48$. The changes do not cancel because they are applied to different amounts.

4. An account is worth £1102.50 after earning 5% compound interest for 2 years. How much was invested at the start?

   - £992.25

   - £1050

   - £1000 ✔

   - £1002.27

   _Explanation:_ Work backwards by dividing by the repeated multiplier: $1102.50\div1.05^2=£1000$.

5. An investment increases from £1000 to £1210 over 2 years at a fixed annual compound interest rate. What is the annual interest rate?

   - 21%

   - 11%

   - 10.5%

   - 10% ✔

   _Explanation:_ The two-year multiplier is $1210\div1000=1.21$. The annual multiplier is $\sqrt{1.21}=1.10$, so the annual interest rate is 10%.
