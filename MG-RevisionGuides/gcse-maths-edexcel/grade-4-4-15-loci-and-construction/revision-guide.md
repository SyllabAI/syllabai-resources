# Loci and Construction

**Practice questions**

1. What is the locus of points that are equidistant from two fixed points $A$ and $B$?

   - A circle with diameter $AB$

   - The perpendicular bisector of $AB$ ✔

   - The line segment $AB$

   - A circle centred at $A$ passing through $B$

   _Explanation:_ Every point on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ is the same distance from $A$ as it is from $B$.

2. What is the locus of points exactly $3\text{ cm}$ from a fixed point $P$?

   - A straight line through $P$

   - A disc of radius $3\text{ cm}$

   - A perpendicular bisector through $P$

   - A circle centred at $P$ with radius $3\text{ cm}$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ A circle is the set of points at a fixed distance from its centre, so the locus is the circumference of a circle with radius $3\text{ cm}$.

3. Which method correctly constructs the perpendicular bisector of a line segment $AB$?

   - Draw equal-radius arcs, with radius greater than half of $AB$, centred at $A$ and $B$, then join the two arc intersections ✔

   - Measure the midpoint of $AB$ with a ruler and draw any line through it

   - Draw a circle with $AB$ as its radius

   - Draw equal arcs from the midpoint of $AB$ only

   _Explanation:_ Equal-radius arcs from $A$ and $B$ meet at points equidistant from both endpoints. Joining these intersections forms the perpendicular bisector.

4. The locus of points exactly $2\text{ cm}$ from a straight line $\ell$ consists of:

   - One line parallel to $\ell$, $2\text{ cm}$ away

   - A circle of radius $2\text{ cm}$

   - Two lines parallel to $\ell$, each $2\text{ cm}$ away ✔

   - Two lines perpendicular to $\ell$

   _Explanation:_ Points can lie $2\text{ cm}$ from $\ell$ on either side, giving two parallel lines, each $2\text{ cm}$ from the original line.

5. To construct a triangle with side lengths $5\text{ cm}$, $6\text{ cm}$ and $7\text{ cm}$, what should you do after drawing the $7\text{ cm}$ side?

   - Draw perpendicular lines at both endpoints

   - Draw an arc of radius $5\text{ cm}$ from one endpoint and an arc of radius $6\text{ cm}$ from the other; their intersection is the third vertex ✔

   - Mark the midpoint of the $7\text{ cm}$ side and draw a $6\text{ cm}$ perpendicular

   - Draw a circle of radius $7\text{ cm}$ around each endpoint

   _Explanation:_ The third vertex must be $5\text{ cm}$ from one endpoint and $6\text{ cm}$ from the other, so it lies where those two arcs intersect.
