# Probability Trees

Learn how to build probability trees and use them to find combined chances over two events.

**Practice questions**

1. A player wins a game with probability $0.68$. What probability should go on the “not win” branch of a probability tree?

   - $0.68$

   - $0.32$ ✔

   - $0.42$

   - $1.68$

   _Explanation:_ The probabilities on a pair of branches must add to $1$, so the probability of not winning is $1-0.68=0.32$.

2. A pupil scores a penalty with probability $0.4$ and then serves an ace with probability $0.3$. The events are independent. What is the probability that she does both?

   - $0.7$

   - $0.1$

   - $0.12$ ✔

   - $0.58$

   _Explanation:_ Multiply probabilities along the same route of a probability tree: $0.4\times0.3=0.12$.

3. Ava takes the bus with probability $\frac{2}{5}$ each day and walks otherwise. Her choices on Monday and Tuesday are independent. What is the probability that she travels in different ways on the two days?

   - $\frac{6}{25}$

   - $\frac{4}{25}$

   - $\frac{9}{25}$

   - $\frac{12}{25}$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ There are two suitable routes: bus then walk, or walk then bus. Their total probability is $\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{3}{5}+\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{2}{5}=\frac{12}{25}$.

4. A train is late on any day with probability $0.25$. For two independent days, what is the probability that it is late on at least one day?

   - $0.4375$ ✔

   - $0.0625$

   - $0.5$

   - $0.5625$

   _Explanation:_ Use the complement. The probability of no late days is $0.75\times0.75=0.5625$, so the probability of at least one late day is $1-0.5625=0.4375$.

5. A bag contains $3$ red counters and $2$ blue counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement. What is the probability that exactly one counter is blue?

   - $\frac{3}{10}$

   - $\frac{2}{5}$

   - $\frac{3}{5}$ ✔

   - $\frac{4}{5}$

   _Explanation:_ Add the blue-then-red and red-then-blue routes: $\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{3}{4}+\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{2}{4}=\frac{3}{10}+\frac{3}{10}=\frac{3}{5}$. The second probabilities change because there is no replacement.
