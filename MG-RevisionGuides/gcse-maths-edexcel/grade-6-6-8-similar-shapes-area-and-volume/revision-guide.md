# Similar Shapes (Area and Volume)

Learn how length scale factors affect area and volume in similar 3D shapes.

**Practice questions**

1. Two similar cylinders have radii 3 cm and 7.5 cm. The smaller cylinder has height 8 cm. What is the height of the larger cylinder?

   - 12.5 cm

   - 20 cm ✔

   - 3.2 cm

   - 50 cm

   _Explanation:_ The length scale factor is $7.5 \div 3=2.5$. Corresponding lengths use the same scale factor, so the height is $8\times2.5=20$ cm.

2. A solid is enlarged with length scale factor 3. Its original surface area is 18 cm². What is its enlarged surface area?

   - 54 cm²

   - 486 cm²

   - 1620 cm²

   - 162 cm² ✔

   _Explanation:_ The area scale factor is the square of the length scale factor: $3^2=9$. Therefore, the enlarged surface area is $18\times9=162$ cm².

3. Two similar solids have corresponding lengths of 15 cm and 10 cm. The solid with length 15 cm has a volume of 675 cm³. What is the volume of the solid with length 10 cm?

   - 200 cm³ ✔

   - 300 cm³

   - 450 cm³

   - 1012.5 cm³

   _Explanation:_ The length scale factor is $10\div15=\frac{2}{3}$, so the volume scale factor is $\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^3=\frac{8}{27}$. Thus, the volume is $675\times\frac{8}{27}=200$ cm³.

4. Similar solids P and Q have surface areas 64 cm² and 100 cm² respectively. The volume of P is 256 cm³. What is the volume of Q?

   - 400 cm³

   - 625 cm³

   - 500 cm³ ✔

   - 800 cm³

   _Explanation:_ The area scale factor is $\frac{100}{64}=\frac{25}{16}$, so the length scale factor is $\sqrt{\frac{25}{16}}=\frac{5}{4}$. The volume scale factor is $\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^3=\frac{125}{64}$, giving $256\times\frac{125}{64}=500$ cm³.

5. A frustum is formed by removing a small cone from a similar large cone. The large cone has height 15 cm and diameter 12 cm. The removed cone has height 5 cm and diameter 4 cm. What is the volume of the frustum?

   - $160π$ cm³

   - $520π$ cm³

   - $\frac{160}{3}π$ cm³

   - $\frac{520}{3}π$ cm³ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The radii are 6 cm and 2 cm. The cone volumes are $\frac{1}{3}π(6^2)(15)=180π$ and $\frac{1}{3}π(2^2)(5)=\frac{20}{3}π$. Subtracting gives $180π-\frac{20}{3}π=\frac{520}{3}π$ cm³.
