# Completing the Square

Turn awkward quadratics into a form that reveals graph points and exact equation solutions.

**Practice questions**

1. Rewrite $x^2+6x+2$ in completed square form.

   - $(x+3)^2-7$ ✔

   - $(x+3)^2+2$

   - $(x+6)^2-34$

   - $(x-3)^2-7$

   _Explanation:_ Halve 6 to get 3. Since $(x+3)^2=x^2+6x+9$, subtract 7 to obtain $(x+3)^2-7$.

2. Complete the square for $x^2-5x+1$.

   - $(x-\frac{5}{2})^2-\frac{3}{2}$

   - $(x+\frac{5}{2})^2-\frac{21}{4}$

   - $(x-\frac{5}{2})^2-\frac{21}{4}$ ✔

   - $(x-\frac{5}{2})^2+\frac{21}{4}$

   _Explanation:_ Halve $-5$ to get $-\frac{5}{2}$. Expanding the square produces an extra $\frac{25}{4}$, so the correction gives $(x-\frac{5}{2})^2-\frac{21}{4}$.

3. Rewrite $3x^2-12x+7$ in the form $a(x+b)^2+c$.

   - $3(x-2)^2+7$

   - $3(x+2)^2-5$

   - $3(x-4)^2-41$

   - $3(x-2)^2-5$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Factor out 3 from the quadratic terms: $3(x^2-4x)+7$. Since $x^2-4x=(x-2)^2-4$, the expression becomes $3(x-2)^2-5$.

4. Solve $x^2+8x+10=0$ exactly by completing the square.

   - $x=4\pm\sqrt{6}$

   - $x=-4\pm\sqrt{6}$ ✔

   - $x=-4\pm\sqrt{26}$

   - $x=-4+\sqrt{6}$

   _Explanation:_ Completing the square gives $(x+4)^2-6=0$, so $(x+4)^2=6$. Taking both square roots gives $x=-4\pm\sqrt{6}$.

5. What is the turning point of $y=(x+5)^2-2$?

   - $(5,-2)$

   - $(-5,2)$

   - $(-5,-2)$ ✔

   - $(5,2)$

   _Explanation:_ The squared bracket is smallest when $x+5=0$, so $x=-5$. The corresponding value of $y$ is $-2$, giving the turning point $(-5,-2)$.
