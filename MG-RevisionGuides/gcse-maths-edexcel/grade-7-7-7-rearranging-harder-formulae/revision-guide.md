# Rearranging Harder Formulae

Master the balance method for changing the subject when formulae include powers, brackets, fractions and repeated variables.

**Practice questions**

1. Make $a$ the subject of $S=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$.

   - $a=\frac{2(S-ut)}{t^2}$ ✔

   - $a=\frac{S-ut}{2t^2}$

   - $a=\frac{2S-ut}{t^2}$

   - $a=\frac{S-ut}{t^2}$

   _Explanation:_ Subtract $ut$ to get $S-ut=\frac{1}{2}at^2$. Multiplying by $2$ and dividing by $t^2$ gives $a=\frac{2(S-ut)}{t^2}$.

2. Make $x$ the subject of $x(a+3)=b(x-2)$.

   - $x=\frac{2b}{a+3-b}$

   - $x=\frac{-2b}{a+3+b}$

   - $x=\frac{-2b}{a+3-b}$ ✔

   - $x=\frac{b-2}{a+3-b}$

   _Explanation:_ Expand to get $ax+3x=bx-2b$. Collecting the terms containing $x$ gives $x(a+3-b)=-2b$, so $x=\frac{-2b}{a+3-b}$.

3. Make $p$ the subject of $q^2=p^2+6s$, with no information restricting the sign of $p$.

   - $p=q^2-6s$

   - $p=\sqrt{q^2+6s}$

   - $p=\sqrt{q^2-6s}$

   - $p=\pm\sqrt{q^2-6s}$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ First isolate the squared term: $p^2=q^2-6s$. Taking the square root gives both possible signs, so $p=\pm\sqrt{q^2-6s}$.

4. Make $q$ the subject of $\frac{1}{m}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{s}$.

   - $q=\frac{ms}{s-m}$

   - $q=\frac{ms}{s+m}$ ✔

   - $q=\frac{m+s}{ms}$

   - $q=\frac{ms}{m-s}$

   _Explanation:_ Multiplying by $mqs$ gives $qs=ms-mq$. Collecting the terms containing $q$ gives $q(s+m)=ms$, so $q=\frac{ms}{s+m}$.

5. Make $r$ the subject of $A=\frac{pr}{r+q}$.

   - $r=\frac{Aq}{p-A}$ ✔

   - $r=\frac{Aq}{A-p}$

   - $r=\frac{p-A}{Aq}$

   - $r=\frac{Ap}{q-A}$

   _Explanation:_ Multiply by $r+q$ to get $A(r+q)=pr$. Expanding and collecting the $r$ terms gives $Aq=r(p-A)$, so $r=\frac{Aq}{p-A}$.
