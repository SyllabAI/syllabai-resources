# Probability Equation Questions

Turn two counter picks into equations, solve carefully, and choose the only answer that makes sense.

**Practice questions**

1. A bag contains $x$ blue counters and 5 red counters. Two counters are taken without replacement, and the probability that both are blue is $\frac{3}{10}$. Which equation should you write?

   - $\frac{x}{x+5}\times\frac{x-1}{x+4}=\frac{3}{10}$ ✔

   - $\frac{x}{x+5}\times\frac{x}{x+5}=\frac{3}{10}$

   - $\frac{x}{x+5}\times\frac{x-1}{x+5}=\frac{3}{10}$

   - $\frac{x}{x+5}\times\frac{x+1}{x+4}=\frac{3}{10}$

   _Explanation:_ After one blue counter is removed, there are $x-1$ blue counters and $x+4$ counters in total, so the second probability is $\frac{x-1}{x+4}$.

2. A bag contains 4 red counters and $y$ blue counters. A counter is chosen, replaced, and then another is chosen. The probability of choosing two red counters is $\frac{4}{25}$. Find $y$.

   - 10

   - 4

   - 6 ✔

   - 21

   _Explanation:_ Replacement keeps the probabilities the same, so $\left(\frac{4}{4+y}\right)^2=\frac{4}{25}$. Taking the positive square root gives $\frac{4}{4+y}=\frac{2}{5}$, so $y=6$.

3. A bag contains 4 red counters and $x$ blue counters. Two counters are taken without replacement. The probability that both are blue is $\frac{1}{3}$. Find $x$.

   - 10

   - 6 ✔

   - 4

   - 3

   _Explanation:_ Solve $\frac{x}{x+4}\times\frac{x-1}{x+3}=\frac{1}{3}$. This simplifies to $(x-6)(x+1)=0$, and only $x=6$ is a valid number of counters.

4. There are $n$ counters in a bag. Five are red and the rest are blue. Two counters are taken without replacement, and the probability that both are blue is $\frac{3}{7}$. What is $n$?

   - 3.5

   - 20

   - 10

   - 15 ✔

   _Explanation:_ The equation is $\frac{n-5}{n}\times\frac{n-6}{n-1}=\frac{3}{7}$. Its solutions are $n=15$ and $n=3.5$, but a total number of counters must be a whole number greater than 5, so $n=15$.

5. The ratio of red counters to blue counters in a bag is $3:2$. Two counters are taken without replacement, and the probability that both are blue is $\frac{3}{20}$. How many counters are in the bag?

   - 10

   - 15

   - 25 ✔

   - 5

   _Explanation:_ Let the numbers of red and blue counters be $3k$ and $2k$. Then $\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{2k-1}{5k-1}=\frac{3}{20}$, giving $k=5$. The total is $5k=25$ counters.
