# Estimating

Learn quick rounding methods to simplify awkward numbers and decide whether answers make sense.

## Estimating

To work out an approximate answer to a complex calculation we estimate.

To estimate we round the numbers in the calculation to numbers that make the calculation easier  
This is usually one significant figure (but it does not have to be).

**Example 1: Estimate 4.13 × 28.1⁄0.53**

To estimate the answer we need to round the numbers.

We can round 4.13 to 4 (to one significant figure). Two significant figures would probably make the calculation too complex.

28.1 could be rounded to 30 (one significant figure) or 28 (two significant figures).

0.53 rounds to 0.5 (one significant figure), again two significant figures would make the calculation too complex.

4 × 30⁄0.5

We can now calculate the estimated answer:

4 × 30⁄0.5 = 120⁄0.5 = 240

Note dividing by 0.5 is the same as multiplying by 2 (0.5 goes into 120 240 times).


**Practice questions**

1. To estimate $39.6 \times 0.48$, which rounded calculation is best?

   - $40 \times 0.5$ ✔

   - $30 \times 0.5$

   - $40 \times 0.4$

   - $4 \times 0.5$

   _Explanation:_ To round to one significant figure, $39.6$ becomes $40$ and $0.48$ becomes $0.5$. This gives the easy calculation $40 \times 0.5=20$.

2. Estimate $\frac{28.6+41.2}{0.21}$ by rounding each number to one significant figure.

   - $35$

   - $300$

   - $350$ ✔

   - $700$

   _Explanation:_ Round to get $\frac{30+40}{0.2}=\frac{70}{0.2}=350$. Dividing by $0.2$ is the same as multiplying by $5$.

3. A calculator gives possible answers $1.7$, $17$, $170$ and $0.17$ for $\frac{512.8}{18.6+\sqrt{98.1}}$. Which answer is most sensible?

   - $1.7$

   - $17$ ✔

   - $170$

   - $0.17$

   _Explanation:_ Estimate using $512.8\approx500$, $18.6\approx20$ and $\sqrt{98.1}\approx10$. Then $500\div(20+10)=500\div30\approx17$.


**Example 2: Someone gets paid £18.75 per hour. They work 29 hours in a week. Work out an estimate for how much they get paid in for the week. State whether your answer is an overestimate or an underestimate.**

We can round £18.75 to £20 (one significant figure) and we can round 29 to 30 (one significant figure).

We can estimate the pay to be £20 × 30 = £600  
We rounded both the hourly rate and the number of hours up, so the actual pay will be lower than £600. We call this an overestimate.


**Practice questions**

1. Lina estimates $\frac{78}{0.41}$ using $\frac{80}{0.4}=200$. Is $200$ an underestimate or an overestimate?

   - An underestimate

   - Neither — the estimate is exact

   - It is impossible to tell

   - An overestimate ✔

   _Explanation:_ The numerator was rounded up and the denominator was rounded down. Both changes make the value of the fraction larger, so $200$ is an overestimate.

2. A circular pond has radius $21.8$ m. Estimate its area using $A=\pi r^2$, with $r\approx20$ and $\pi\approx3$. Is the estimate too small or too large?

   - $120\text{ m}^2$, too small

   - $1200\text{ m}^2$, too small ✔

   - $1200\text{ m}^2$, too large

   - $12{,}000\text{ m}^2$, too large

   _Explanation:_ The estimate is $3\times20^2=3\times400=1200\text{ m}^2$. Both $21.8$ and $\pi$ have been rounded down, so the estimated area is too small.
