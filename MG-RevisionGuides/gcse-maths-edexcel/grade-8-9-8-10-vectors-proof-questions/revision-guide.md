# Vectors Proof Questions

Learn how ratios and scalar multiples let you prove points are collinear using vectors.

**Practice questions**

1. The position vectors of A and B are $2a$ and $5b$. What is the directed vector $\text{AB}$?

   - $2a-5b$

   - $5b-2a$ ✔

   - $2a+5b$

   - $5a-2b$

   _Explanation:_ Subtract the position vector of A from the position vector of B: $\text{AB}=5b-2a$. The vector $2a-5b$ points in the opposite direction.

2. Point P lies on AB with $\text{AP}:\text{PB}=3:2$. The position vectors of A and B are $4a$ and $9b$. What is the position vector of P?

   - $\frac{12}{5}a+\frac{18}{5}b$

   - $-\frac{12}{5}a+\frac{27}{5}b$

   - $\frac{32}{5}a-\frac{27}{5}b$

   - $\frac{8}{5}a+\frac{27}{5}b$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ P is $\frac{3}{5}$ of the way from A to B, so its position vector is $4a+\frac{3}{5}(9b-4a)=\frac{8}{5}a+\frac{27}{5}b$.

3. Points A, B and C have position vectors $2a+b$, $5a+7b$ and $3a+3b$ respectively. Which equation proves that A, B and C are collinear?

   - $\text{AB}=3\text{AC}$ ✔

   - $\text{AB}=2\text{AC}$

   - $\text{BC}=2\text{AC}$

   - $\text{AC}=3\text{AB}$

   _Explanation:_ $\text{AB}=(5a+7b)-(2a+b)=3a+6b$, while $\text{AC}=(3a+3b)-(2a+b)=a+2b$. Therefore $\text{AB}=3\text{AC}$, proving the vectors are parallel and share point A.

4. OACB is a parallelogram. A and B are adjacent to O, with position vectors $6a$ and $4b$, and C is the opposite corner. M is the midpoint of AB. Which equation proves that O, M and C are collinear?

   - $\text{OM}=2\text{OC}$

   - $\text{MC}=2\text{OM}$

   - $\text{OC}=2\text{OM}$ ✔

   - $\text{OC}=\text{OM}+\text{AB}$

   _Explanation:_ For the opposite corner, $\text{OC}=6a+4b$. Since M is the midpoint of AB, $\text{OM}=\frac{1}{2}(6a+4b)=3a+2b$. Hence $\text{OC}=2\text{OM}$, so O, M and C are collinear.

5. Vectors $a$ and $b$ are not parallel. In triangle OAB, the position vectors of A and B are $6a$ and $6b$. C lies on OA with $\text{OC}:\text{CA}=5:1$, and D lies on AB with $\text{AD}:\text{DB}=1:2$. The line OB is extended to E, where C, D and E are collinear. What is the directed vector $\text{BE}$?

   - $2b$

   - $4b$ ✔

   - $5b$

   - $10b$

   _Explanation:_ C has position vector $5a$, and D has position vector $6a+\frac{1}{3}(6b-6a)=4a+2b$. Thus $\text{CD}=-a+2b$. To reach OB, the $a$ coefficient must become zero, so $\text{CE}=5\text{CD}=-5a+10b$. E therefore has position vector $10b$, giving $\text{BE}=10b-6b=4b$.
