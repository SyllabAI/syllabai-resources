# Quadratic Inequalities

Learn how factorising and sign diagrams turn quadratic inequalities into clear ranges of values.

**Practice questions**

1. Solve $x^2-6x+8\le 0$.

   - $2\le x\le 4$ ✔

   - $2<x<4$

   - $x\le 2$ or $x\ge 4$

   - $x<2$ or $x>4$

   _Explanation:_ Factorising gives $(x-2)(x-4)\le 0$. The upward-opening quadratic is zero at $2$ and $4$ and negative between them, so $2\le x\le 4$.

2. Find the integer values satisfying $x^2-8x+14<0$.

   - $2,3,4,5$

   - $3,4,5$ ✔

   - $3,4$

   - $3,4,5,6$

   _Explanation:_ The roots are $4-\sqrt{2}\approx2.59$ and $4+\sqrt{2}\approx5.41$. The quadratic is negative between the roots, so the integer values are $3,4,5$.

3. Solve $x^2<49$.

   - $x<7$

   - $x<-7$ or $x>7$

   - $-7<x<7$ ✔

   - $-7\le x\le 7$

   _Explanation:_ Rewrite the inequality as $(x-7)(x+7)<0$. The expression is negative strictly between its roots, giving $-7<x<7$.

4. Solve $5x+3\ge 2x^2$.

   - $x\le-\frac{1}{2}$ or $x\ge3$

   - $-\frac{1}{2}<x<3$

   - $\frac{1}{2}\le x\le3$

   - $-\frac{1}{2}\le x\le3$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Rearranging gives $2x^2-5x-3\le0$, which factorises to $(2x+1)(x-3)\le0$. The expression is non-positive between the roots, including them, so $-\frac{1}{2}\le x\le3$.

5. An upward-opening quadratic has roots at $x=-2$ and $x=5$. For which values of $x$ is the quadratic greater than zero?

   - $-2<x<5$

   - $x\le-2$ or $x\ge5$

   - $x<-2$ or $x>5$ ✔

   - $x>-2$ or $x<5$

   _Explanation:_ An upward-opening quadratic is positive outside its two roots. Because the inequality is strictly greater than zero, the roots are excluded, giving $x<-2$ or $x>5$.
