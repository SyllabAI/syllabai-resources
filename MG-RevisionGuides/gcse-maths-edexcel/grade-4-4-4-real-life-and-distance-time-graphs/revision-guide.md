# Real Life and Distance Time Graphs

**Practice questions**

1. What does a horizontal section on a distance–time graph show?

   - The object is moving at a constant speed.

   - The object is stationary. ✔

   - The object is moving back towards its starting point.

   - The object is accelerating.

   _Explanation:_ A horizontal line means the distance does not change as time passes, so the object is stationary.

2. A cyclist travels $1200$ metres in $10$ minutes. What is the cyclist's average speed in metres per second?

   - $0.5$ m/s

   - $1.2$ m/s

   - $2$ m/s ✔

   - $120$ m/s

   _Explanation:_ Convert $10$ minutes to $600$ seconds, then calculate $\text{speed}=1200\div600=2$ m/s.

3. A journey has two sections. In the first section, a person travels $3$ km in $20$ minutes. In the second section, they travel $2$ km in $10$ minutes. During which section are they travelling faster?

   - The second section ✔

   - The first section

   - They travel at the same speed

   - There is not enough information

   _Explanation:_ The first speed is $3\div\frac{1}{3}=9$ km/h. The second speed is $2\div\frac{1}{6}=12$ km/h, so the second section is faster.

4. On a distance–time graph showing distance from home, what does a line sloping downwards represent?

   - The person is stationary.

   - The person is travelling away from home.

   - The person is increasing their speed.

   - The person is travelling back towards home. ✔

   _Explanation:_ A downward slope shows that the person's distance from home is decreasing, so they are travelling towards home.

5. A walker travels $6$ km in $15$ minutes, rests for $10$ minutes, and then walks $6$ km home in $20$ minutes. What is the walker's average speed for the whole journey, including the rest?

   - $12$ km/h

   - $16$ km/h ✔

   - $18$ km/h

   - $24$ km/h

   _Explanation:_ The total distance is $12$ km and the total time is $45$ minutes, or $0.75$ hours. Therefore, the average speed is $12\div0.75=16$ km/h.
