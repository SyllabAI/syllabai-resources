# The Product Rule for Counting

Multiply, reduce and halve choices confidently when counting menus, cards, teams and handshakes.

**Practice questions**

1. A café offers 5 sandwiches and 4 drinks. You choose one sandwich and one drink. How many different meals are possible?

   - 9

   - 20 ✔

   - 10

   - 25

   _Explanation:_ Each of the 5 sandwiches can be paired with any of the 4 drinks, so the product rule gives $5 \times 4=20$.

2. A password consists of one of 3 letters followed by one of 4 digits and then one of 2 symbols. How many different passwords are possible?

   - 9

   - 12

   - 18

   - 24 ✔

   _Explanation:_ Multiply the number of choices at each stage: $3 \times 4 \times 2=24$.

3. A meal deal has 6 wrap choices and some drink choices. A sign claims that there are exactly 50 possible wrap-and-drink deals. Why must the claim be incorrect?

   - $50 \div 6$ is not a whole number ✔

   - There must be $50-6=44$ drinks

   - There must be $50 \times 6=300$ drinks

   - The number of deals must always be less than 50

   _Explanation:_ The number of drinks would have to be $50 \div 6\approx 8.33$. A number of choices must be a whole number, so exactly 50 deals are impossible.

4. There are 12 different cards. One card is given to Nia and then a different card is given to Omar. How many possible allocations are there?

   - 24

   - 66

   - 132 ✔

   - 144

   _Explanation:_ There are 12 choices for Nia's card and then 11 remaining choices for Omar's card, giving $12 \times 11=132$ allocations.

5. There are 14 teams in a league and each team plays every other team exactly once. How many matches are played?

   - 78

   - 91 ✔

   - 182

   - 196

   _Explanation:_ The product $14 \times 13$ counts each match twice, once from each team's viewpoint. Therefore, the number of matches is $14 \times 13 \div 2=91$.
