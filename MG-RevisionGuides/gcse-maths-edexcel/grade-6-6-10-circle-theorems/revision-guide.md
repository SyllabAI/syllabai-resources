# Circle Theorems

Learn how key circle facts unlock tangent, chord, and centre-angle questions.

**Practice questions**

1. Points $A$, $B$ and $C$ lie on a circle. A tangent touches the circle at $C$. Given that angle $ABC=64^\circ$ and angle $ACB=73^\circ$, find the angle between the tangent and chord $CB$.

   - $64^\circ$

   - $73^\circ$

   - $43^\circ$ ✔

   - $53^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ First, angle $BAC=180^\circ-64^\circ-73^\circ=43^\circ$. By the alternate segment theorem, the angle between the tangent and chord $CB$ equals angle $BAC$, so it is $43^\circ$.

2. $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angles $ADB$ and $ACB$ stand on the same chord $AB$. If angle $ADB=43^\circ$ and angle $DAB=112^\circ$, what are angle $ACB$ and angle $BCD$ respectively?

   - $43^\circ$ and $112^\circ$

   - $43^\circ$ and $68^\circ$ ✔

   - $68^\circ$ and $43^\circ$

   - $137^\circ$ and $68^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Angles in the same segment are equal, so angle $ACB=43^\circ$. Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral total $180^\circ$, so angle $BCD=180^\circ-112^\circ=68^\circ$.

3. A tangent at $A$ meets a point $B$. The circle has centre $O$, with $OA=5$ cm and $AB=12$ cm. Find $OB$.

   - $13$ cm ✔

   - $7$ cm

   - $17$ cm

   - $\sqrt{119}$ cm

   _Explanation:_ A radius is perpendicular to a tangent at the point of contact, so triangle $OAB$ is right-angled at $A$. Therefore, $OB=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=\sqrt{169}=13$ cm.

4. Points $A$ and $B$ lie on a circle with centre $O$. In triangle $AOB$, angle $ABO=38^\circ$. Find angle $AOB$.

   - $76^\circ$

   - $142^\circ$

   - $52^\circ$

   - $104^\circ$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Since $OA$ and $OB$ are radii, triangle $AOB$ is isosceles. The two base angles are both $38^\circ$, so angle $AOB=180^\circ-38^\circ-38^\circ=104^\circ$.

5. The reflex angle $BOC$ at the centre of a circle is $250^\circ$. An angle $BAC$ at the circumference subtends the same reflex arc $BC$. Find angle $BAC$.

   - $55^\circ$

   - $110^\circ$

   - $125^\circ$ ✔

   - $250^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference when both subtend the same arc. Therefore, angle $BAC=250^\circ\div2=125^\circ$.
