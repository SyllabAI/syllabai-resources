# Quadratic Simultaneous Equations

Learn how to combine a straight line with a quadratic equation to find all intersection points algebraically.

**Practice questions**

1. For $x^2 + y^2 = 13$ and $y = 5 - 2x$, which equation correctly substitutes for $y$?

   - $x^2 + 5 - 2x^2 = 13$

   - $x^2 + (5 - 2x) = 13$

   - $x^2 + (5 - 2x)^2 = 13$ ✔

   - $(x^2 + 5 - 2x)^2 = 13$

   _Explanation:_ Substitute the whole expression $5-2x$ for $y$. Since the equation contains $y^2$, brackets are needed: $x^2+(5-2x)^2=13$.

2. Which ordered pair satisfies both $x^2 + y^2 = 10$ and $y = x + 2$?

   - $(1, 3)$ ✔

   - $(3, 1)$

   - $(-1, 3)$

   - $(1, -3)$

   _Explanation:_ For $(1,3)$, the linear equation is satisfied because $3=1+2$, and the quadratic equation is satisfied because $1^2+3^2=10$.

3. Solve $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and $x - y = 1$. Which list gives both solution pairs?

   - $(4, -3)$ and $(-3, -4)$

   - $(3, 4)$ and $(-4, -3)$

   - $(4, 3)$ and $(-4, -3)$

   - $(4, 3)$ and $(-3, -4)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Rearrange the line to $y=x-1$. Substitution gives $x^2+(x-1)^2=25$, with $x=4$ or $x=-3$. The matching $y$-values are $3$ and $-4$.

4. Solve $x^2 + y^2 = 10$ and $x + y = 1$, giving each coordinate to 3 significant figures.

   - No real solution pairs

   - $(2.68, -1.68)$ and $(-1.68, 2.68)$ ✔

   - $(2.68, 1.68)$ and $(-1.68, -2.68)$

   - $(2.67, -1.67)$ and $(-1.67, 2.67)$

   _Explanation:_ Using $y=1-x$ gives $2x^2-2x-9=0$, so $x\approx2.679$ or $x\approx-1.679$. Substituting back and rounding gives the two stated pairs.

5. Solve $x^2 - 2y^2 = -7$ and $x + 2y = 3$. Which answer gives both solution pairs?

   - $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 4)$

   - $(-1, 4)$ and $(-5, 2)$

   - $(-1, 2)$ and $(-5, 4)$ ✔

   - $(1, -2)$ and $(5, -4)$

   _Explanation:_ From the linear equation, $x=3-2y$. Substitution gives $y=2$ or $y=4$, producing the matching pairs $(-1,2)$ and $(-5,4)$.
