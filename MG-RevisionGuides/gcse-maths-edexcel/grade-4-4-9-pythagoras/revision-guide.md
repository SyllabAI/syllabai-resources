# Pythagoras

Use squares and square roots to solve missing-side problems in right-angled triangles, shapes, and real-life contexts.

**Practice questions**

1. Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $A$. Which side is the hypotenuse?

   - $AB$

   - $AC$

   - $BC$ ✔

   - It cannot be identified

   _Explanation:_ The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Since the right angle is at $A$, the hypotenuse is $BC$.

2. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the length of its hypotenuse.

   - 10 cm ✔

   - 14 cm

   - 7 cm

   - 100 cm

   _Explanation:_ Add the squares of the shorter sides: $6^2+8^2=36+64=100$. Therefore, the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{100}=10$ cm.

3. A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm and one shorter side of 5 cm. Find the other shorter side.

   - 18 cm

   - 13.9 cm

   - 8 cm

   - 12 cm ✔

   _Explanation:_ Subtract because the hypotenuse is known: $13^2-5^2=169-25=144$. The missing side is $\sqrt{144}=12$ cm.

4. A rectangle is 12 cm long and has a diagonal of 15 cm. What is its width?

   - 27 cm

   - 9 cm ✔

   - 19.2 cm

   - 3 cm

   _Explanation:_ The diagonal is the hypotenuse, so $15^2-12^2=225-144=81$. The width is $\sqrt{81}=9$ cm.

5. The points $A(-2,1)$ and $B(4,9)$ are joined by a straight line. Find the length $AB$.

   - 14 units

   - $\sqrt{28}$ units

   - 10 units ✔

   - 100 units

   _Explanation:_ The horizontal change is $4-(-2)=6$ and the vertical change is $9-1=8$. Therefore, $AB=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=\sqrt{100}=10$ units.
