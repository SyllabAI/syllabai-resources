# Factorising Harder Quadratics

Learn a reliable method for breaking trickier quadratics into brackets and using them to solve equations.

**Practice questions**

1. To factorise $6x^2+11x+3$ by splitting the middle term, which two numbers should be used?

   - 6 and 5

   - 18 and $-7$

   - 9 and 2 ✔

   - 3 and 6

   _Explanation:_ The numbers must multiply to $6\times3=18$ and add to 11. Since $9\times2=18$ and $9+2=11$, split $11x$ into $9x+2x$.

2. Factorise $6x^2+11x+3$.

   - $(6x+1)(x+3)$

   - $(3x+1)(2x+3)$ ✔

   - $(3x+3)(2x+1)$

   - $(6x+3)(x+1)$

   _Explanation:_ Splitting the middle term gives $6x^2+9x+2x+3$. Factorising by grouping produces $3x(2x+3)+1(2x+3)=(3x+1)(2x+3)$.

3. Factorise $8x^2-2x-3$.

   - $(4x+3)(2x-1)$

   - $(8x+3)(x-1)$

   - $(4x-1)(2x+3)$

   - $(4x-3)(2x+1)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The product $8\times(-3)=-24$, so split $-2x$ as $4x-6x$. Grouping then gives $4x(2x+1)-3(2x+1)=(4x-3)(2x+1)$.

4. Factorise fully $12x^2-75$.

   - $3(2x-5)(2x+5)$ ✔

   - $3(4x^2-25)$

   - $3(4x-5)(x+5)$

   - $(6x-15)(2x+5)$

   _Explanation:_ First take out the common factor 3: $12x^2-75=3(4x^2-25)$. Then use the difference of two squares: $4x^2-25=(2x-5)(2x+5)$.

5. Solve $3x^2+x-2=0$ by factorising.

   - $x=-\frac{2}{3}$ or $x=1$

   - $x=2$ or $x=-1$

   - $x=\frac{2}{3}$ or $x=-1$ ✔

   - $x=-\frac{1}{3}$ or $x=2$

   _Explanation:_ The quadratic factorises as $(3x-2)(x+1)=0$. Therefore $3x-2=0$ gives $x=\frac{2}{3}$, while $x+1=0$ gives $x=-1$.
