# Area of Compound Shapes

Split awkward diagrams into rectangles and triangles, then add or subtract areas to find the part you need.

## Area of Compound Shapes

A compound shape is a shape made up of different shapes.

Here is a compound shape:

![compound shapes 1](../assets/001-compound-shapes-1.jpg)

To work out the area of this shape we can split it into two rectangles.

There are two ways of splitting the shape into two rectangles. We can split the shape like this:

![compound shapes 2](../assets/002-compound-shapes-2.jpg)

To find the area of the whole shape we will work out the area of the two rectangles and add them together.

To find the area of a rectangle we need to multiply the length by the width.

We know the length and the width of the bigger rectangle. We need to find a missing length of the smaller rectangle.

The height of the whole shape is 9 cm and the height of the bottom shape is 5 cm. To find the height of the top shape we need to take 5 away from 9  
9 - 5 = 4  
(The smaller rectangle is a square)

![compound shapes 3](../assets/003-compound-shapes-3.jpg)

We now have all the information we need to calculate the areas.

The area of the large rectangle is 5 × 10 = 50 cm2

The area of the square is 4 × 4 = 16 cm2

The area of the whole shape is 16 + 50  
16 + 50 = 66 cm2

**Practice questions**

1. An L-shaped patio has an overall bottom length of 12 m and an overall height of 8 m. The top section is 5 m wide, and the vertical drop to the lower section is 3 m. What is the area of the patio?

   - 61 m$^2$

   - 75 m$^2$ ✔

   - 96 m$^2$

   - 40 m$^2$

   _Explanation:_ Split the shape into a $5 \times 8$ rectangle and a lower rectangle. The lower rectangle is $(12-5)=7$ m wide and $(8-3)=5$ m high, so the area is $5 \times 8+7 \times 5=40+35=75$ m$^2$.

2. A compound shape is made from a lower rectangle measuring 8 cm wide by 7 cm high and a smaller rectangle on top. The total height is 11 cm, and the top rectangle is 3 cm wide. What is the total area?

   - 80 cm$^2$

   - 65 cm$^2$

   - 89 cm$^2$

   - 68 cm$^2$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The top rectangle has height $11-7=4$ cm. The total area is $8 \times 7+3 \times 4=56+12=68$ cm$^2$.

**Practice questions**

1. A large rectangle measures 14 cm by 9 cm. A rectangular section measuring 6 cm by 4 cm is cut out from one corner. What area remains?

   - 102 cm$^2$ ✔

   - 150 cm$^2$

   - 78 cm$^2$

   - 126 cm$^2$

   _Explanation:_ Find the area of the large rectangle and subtract the cut-out section: $14 \times 9-6 \times 4=126-24=102$ cm$^2$.


Here is another compound shape:

![compound shapes 6](../assets/006-compound-shapes-6.jpg)

To find the area of the compound shape we need to split it into a rectangle and a triangle.

![compound shapes 7](../assets/007-compound-shapes-7.jpg)

We know the length and width of the rectangle.

The area of a triangle = 1⁄2 × base × height

We need to work out the base and the height of the triangle.

The base is 12 - 6 = 6 cm  
The height is 11 - 4 = 7 cm

![compound shapes 8](../assets/008-compound-shapes-8.jpg)

The area of the rectangle = 12 × 4 = 48 cm2

The area of the triangle = 1⁄2 × 6 × 7  
1⁄2 × 6 × 7 = 3 × 7 = 21 cm2

The total area = 48 + 21 = 69 cm2

**Practice questions**

1. A compound shape consists of a 7 cm by 5 cm rectangle joined to a right-angled triangle with base 6 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. What is its total area?

   - 65 cm$^2$

   - 40 cm$^2$

   - 50 cm$^2$ ✔

   - 35 cm$^2$

   _Explanation:_ The rectangle has area $7 \times 5=35$ cm$^2$, and the triangle has area $\frac{1}{2}\times 6\times 5=15$ cm$^2$. The total is $35+15=50$ cm$^2$.

2. A right-angled trapezium-shaped lawn has parallel sides of 10 m and 16 m and a perpendicular height of 6 m. Split it into a rectangle and a triangle. Turf costs £5 per m$^2$. What is the total cost?

   - £300

   - £390 ✔

   - £480

   - £78

   _Explanation:_ The rectangle has area $10 \times 6=60$ m$^2$. The triangle has base $16-10=6$ m, so its area is $\frac{1}{2}\times 6\times 6=18$ m$^2$. The total area is $78$ m$^2$, giving a cost of $78 \times 5=£390$.
