# Proof of the Circle Theorems

Learn how the main circle theorems are proved from basic angle facts, not just memorised.

**Practice questions**

1. In a proof that the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, two angles at the centre are $180^\circ-2x$ and $180^\circ-2y$. What is the remaining angle around the centre?

   - $x+y$

   - $180^\circ-2x-2y$

   - $360^\circ-2x-2y$

   - $2x+2y$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Angles around the centre total $360^\circ$. Subtracting $(180^\circ-2x)+(180^\circ-2y)$ leaves $2x+2y=2(x+y)$, twice the corresponding angle at the circumference.

2. $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral and $\angle ABC=112^\circ$. Which statement must be true?

   - $\angle BCD=68^\circ$

   - $\angle DAB=112^\circ$

   - $\angle ADC=68^\circ$ ✔

   - $\angle ADC=112^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$, so $\angle ADC=180^\circ-112^\circ=68^\circ$.

3. Points $A$ and $B$ lie on a circle with centre $O$. Which statement justifies writing $\angle OAB=\angle ABO$ in a proof?

   - $AB$ is a chord, so its end angles are equal

   - $OA=OB$, so triangle $AOB$ is isosceles ✔

   - A radius is always perpendicular to a chord

   - Angles around $O$ add to $360^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Both $OA$ and $OB$ are radii, so $OA=OB$. Therefore triangle $AOB$ is isosceles and its base angles are equal.

4. A tangent touches a circle at $C$, $BC$ is a chord and $O$ is the centre. If $\angle OCB=28^\circ$, what angle does chord $BC$ make with the tangent at $C$?

   - $62^\circ$ ✔

   - $28^\circ$

   - $56^\circ$

   - $118^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ The radius $OC$ is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact. Therefore the angle between the tangent and $BC$ is $90^\circ-28^\circ=62^\circ$; this is also the angle in the alternate segment.

5. $AB$ is a diameter of a circle, and $C$ is a point on the circumference. Which argument proves that $\angle ACB=90^\circ$?

   - The angle at the circumference equals the angle at the centre

   - Triangle $ACB$ is isosceles because $AB$ is a diameter

   - The angle at the centre is $180^\circ$, and the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc is half of this ✔

   - Opposite angles in every quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ The diameter forms a straight angle of $180^\circ$ at the centre. The angle at the circumference subtended by the same arc is half the angle at the centre, so $\angle ACB=90^\circ$.
