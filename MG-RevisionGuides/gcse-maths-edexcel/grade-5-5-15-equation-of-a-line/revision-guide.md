# Equation of a Line

Learn how gradients and intercepts describe straight lines so you can read, rearrange and write line equations confidently.

**Practice questions**

1. What are the gradient and y-intercept of the line $y = 5x - 3$?

   - Gradient $-3$, y-intercept $5$

   - Gradient $5$, y-intercept $-3$ ✔

   - Gradient $-5$, y-intercept $3$

   - Gradient $3$, y-intercept $-5$

   _Explanation:_ In the form $y = mx + c$, $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the y-intercept. Therefore, the gradient is $5$ and the y-intercept is $-3$.

2. A line has gradient $-2$ and crosses the y-axis at $(0, 6)$. Which is its equation?

   - $y = 6x - 2$

   - $y = 2x + 6$

   - $y = -2x - 6$

   - $y = -2x + 6$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Substitute the gradient $m=-2$ and y-intercept $c=6$ into $y=mx+c$. This gives $y=-2x+6$.

3. A straight line passes through $(2, 7)$ and $(6, -1)$. What is its gradient?

   - $-2$ ✔

   - $2$

   - $-\frac{1}{2}$

   - $-8$

   _Explanation:_ The gradient is change in $y$ divided by change in $x$: $\frac{-1-7}{6-2}=\frac{-8}{4}=-2$.

4. Where does the line $y = 3 - 4x$ cross the y-axis?

   - $(3,0)$

   - $(0,-4)$

   - $(0,3)$ ✔

   - $(-4,0)$

   _Explanation:_ At the y-axis, $x=0$. Substituting gives $y=3-4(0)=3$, so the line crosses at $(0,3)$.

5. A line is parallel to $2x + 5y = 15$ and crosses the y-axis at $(0,-1)$. Which is its equation?

   - $y=\frac{2}{5}x-1$

   - $y=-\frac{2}{5}x-1$ ✔

   - $y=-\frac{5}{2}x-1$

   - $y=-\frac{2}{5}x+1$

   _Explanation:_ Rearranging gives $5y=-2x+15$, so $y=-\frac{2}{5}x+3$ and its gradient is $-\frac{2}{5}$. Parallel lines have the same gradient, and the required y-intercept is $-1$, giving $y=-\frac{2}{5}x-1$.
