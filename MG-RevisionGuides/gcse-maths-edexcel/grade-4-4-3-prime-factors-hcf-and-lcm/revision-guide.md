# Prime Factors, HCF and LCM

Learn to break numbers into primes, then use them to solve HCF, LCM and repeat-timing problems.

**Practice questions**

1. Write $96$ as a product of prime factors using index notation.

   - $2^4 \times 3$

   - $2^3 \times 3^2$

   - $2^5 \times 3$ ✔

   - $2^4 \times 6$

   _Explanation:_ Dividing repeatedly by $2$ gives $96=2\times2\times2\times2\times2\times3$, so $96=2^5\times3$. The expression $2^4\times6$ equals $96$, but $6$ is not prime.

2. Find the HCF of $48$ and $60$.

   - $12$ ✔

   - $240$

   - $6$

   - $24$

   _Explanation:_ The prime factorizations are $48=2^4\times3$ and $60=2^2\times3\times5$. Taking the common primes with their lowest powers gives $2^2\times3=12$.

3. Given $A=2^3\times3$ and $B=2\times3^2\times5$, find the LCM of $A$ and $B$.

   - $2\times3$

   - $2^3\times3^2$

   - $2^4\times3^3\times5$

   - $2^3\times3^2\times5$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ For the LCM, include every prime using its highest power in either number. This gives $2^3\times3^2\times5$.

4. A red light flashes every $45$ seconds and a blue light flashes every $2$ minutes. They flash together at 10:00. When will they next flash together?

   - 10:01:30

   - 10:06 ✔

   - 10:02

   - 11:30

   _Explanation:_ First convert $2$ minutes to $120$ seconds. The LCM of $45$ and $120$ is $360$ seconds, which is $6$ minutes, so they next flash together at 10:06.

5. Two whole numbers greater than $10$ have HCF $8$ and LCM $120$. Which pair could they be?

   - $8$ and $120$

   - $16$ and $60$

   - $24$ and $40$ ✔

   - $32$ and $120$

   _Explanation:_ For $24$ and $40$, the HCF is $8$ and the LCM is $120$. The other pairs either have the wrong HCF or include a number that is not greater than $10$.
