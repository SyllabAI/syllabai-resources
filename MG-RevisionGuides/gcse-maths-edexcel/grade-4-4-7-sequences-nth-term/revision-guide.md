# Sequences (Nth Term)

Spot sequence rules, use nth-term formulae, and decide confidently whether numbers belong in a sequence.

**Practice questions**

1. Find the nth term of the arithmetic sequence $7, 11, 15, 19, \ldots$

   - $7n+4$

   - $3n+4$

   - $4n+3$ ✔

   - $4n+7$

   _Explanation:_ The common difference is $4$, so start with $4n$. Since $4n$ gives $4$ when $n=1$, add $3$ to get the first term $7$. Therefore, the nth term is $4n+3$.

2. The nth term of a sequence is $6n-1$. What is the 20th term?

   - $119$ ✔

   - $114$

   - $120$

   - $125$

   _Explanation:_ Substitute $n=20$: $6(20)-1=120-1=119$.

3. The nth term of a sequence is $5n+2$. Is $42$ a term in the sequence?

   - Yes, it is the 7th term

   - No, because $42$ is not a multiple of $5$

   - No, because $n=8.4$

   - Yes, it is the 8th term ✔

   _Explanation:_ Solve $5n+2=42$. This gives $5n=40$, so $n=8$. Since $8$ is a positive whole number, $42$ is the 8th term.

4. A sequence starts with $3$. The rule is: multiply the previous term by $3$, then subtract $2$. What is the 4th term?

   - $49$

   - $55$ ✔

   - $19$

   - $57$

   _Explanation:_ Apply the rule one term at a time: $3, 7, 19, 55$. Therefore, the 4th term is $55$.

5. The nth term of a sequence is $n^2+3n$. Is $70$ a term in the sequence?

   - No, because $70$ is not a square number

   - Yes, it is the 10th term

   - Yes, it is the 7th term ✔

   - No, because $n$ is negative

   _Explanation:_ Set $n^2+3n=70$, giving $n^2+3n-70=0$. Factorising gives $(n+10)(n-7)=0$, so the positive whole-number solution is $n=7$. Therefore, $70$ is the 7th term.
