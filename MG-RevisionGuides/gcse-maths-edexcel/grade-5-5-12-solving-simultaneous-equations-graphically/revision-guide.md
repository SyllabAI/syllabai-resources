# Solving Simultaneous Equations Graphically

Learn how the crossing point of two straight-line graphs gives the values that satisfy both equations.

**Practice questions**

1. To draw the line $y=2x-3$, which points should you plot for $x=0$, $x=1$ and $x=2$?

   - $(0,3)$, $(1,5)$ and $(2,7)$

   - $(0,-3)$, $(1,-1)$ and $(2,1)$ ✔

   - $(0,-3)$, $(1,-2)$ and $(2,-1)$

   - $(-3,0)$, $(-1,1)$ and $(1,2)$

   _Explanation:_ Substituting $x=0,1,2$ into $y=2x-3$ gives $y=-3,-1,1$, so the points are $(0,-3)$, $(1,-1)$ and $(2,1)$.

2. Which rearrangement of $3y-x=6$ is suitable for drawing its straight-line graph?

   - $y=3x-6$

   - $y=\frac{1}{3}x-2$

   - $y=\frac{1}{3}x+2$ ✔

   - $y=x+2$

   _Explanation:_ Add $x$ to both sides to get $3y=x+6$, then divide every term by $3$: $y=\frac{1}{3}x+2$.

3. The graphs of $y=x-4$ and $2y+x=-2$ are drawn on the same axes. At which point do they intersect?

   - $(-2,-6)$

   - $(2,2)$

   - $(-2,2)$

   - $(2,-2)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ At $(2,-2)$, the first equation gives $-2=2-4$ and the second gives $2(-2)+2=-2$. As the point lies on both lines, it is their intersection.

4. The graphs of $y=2x+1$ and $y=2x-3$ are drawn on the same axes. How many solutions do the simultaneous equations have?

   - No solutions ✔

   - Exactly one solution

   - Exactly two solutions

   - Infinitely many solutions

   _Explanation:_ Both lines have gradient $2$ but different intercepts, so they are parallel and never intersect. Therefore, there is no solution.

5. Two straight-line graphs cross at $(-3,2)$. What is the solution of the corresponding simultaneous equations?

   - $x=3$, $y=-2$

   - $x=2$, $y=-3$

   - $x=-3$, $y=2$ ✔

   - $x=-2$, $y=3$

   _Explanation:_ The solution is the coordinate of the intersection, read as $x$ first and then $y$. Therefore, $(-3,2)$ means $x=-3$ and $y=2$.
