# Midpoint of a Line Segment

**Practice questions**

1. Find the midpoint of the line segment joining $(2,4)$ and $(8,10)$.

   - $(3,3)$

   - $(5,7)$ ✔

   - $(6,6)$

   - $(10,14)$

   _Explanation:_ Average the $x$-coordinates and the $y$-coordinates: $\left(\frac{2+8}{2},\frac{4+10}{2}\right)=(5,7)$.

2. What is the midpoint of the line segment joining $(-6,3)$ and $(4,11)$?

   - $(-2,8)$

   - $(1,7)$

   - $(-5,4)$

   - $(-1,7)$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ The midpoint is $\left(\frac{-6+4}{2},\frac{3+11}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{-2}{2},\frac{14}{2}\right)=(-1,7)$.

3. Find the midpoint of the line segment joining $(1.5,-2)$ and $(4.5,6)$.

   - $(3,2)$ ✔

   - $(3,4)$

   - $(2,3)$

   - $(6,4)$

   _Explanation:_ Average each pair of coordinates: $\frac{1.5+4.5}{2}=3$ and $\frac{-2+6}{2}=2$, so the midpoint is $(3,2)$.

4. One endpoint of a line segment is $(1,-3)$ and its midpoint is $(4,1)$. What is the other endpoint?

   - $(2,-1)$

   - $(5,4)$

   - $(7,5)$ ✔

   - $(8,2)$

   _Explanation:_ From $(1,-3)$ to the midpoint $(4,1)$, the change is $(+3,+4)$. Make the same change again to get the other endpoint: $(4+3,1+4)=(7,5)$.

5. The midpoint of the line segment joining $(2,y)$ and $(8,12)$ is $(5,9)$. Find $y$.

   - $3$

   - $6$ ✔

   - $7$

   - $10$

   _Explanation:_ The midpoint's $y$-coordinate is the average, so $\frac{y+12}{2}=9$. Therefore $y+12=18$, giving $y=6$.
