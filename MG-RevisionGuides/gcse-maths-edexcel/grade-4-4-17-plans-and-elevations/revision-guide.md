# Plans and Elevations

Learn how to turn 3D solids into top, front and side views, and back again using grids and scales.

**Practice questions**

1. A square-based pyramid has a base side of $4$ m and a perpendicular height of $2.5$ m. It is drawn using a scale of $2$ cm to $1$ m. Which front elevation should be drawn?

   - A triangle with base $8$ cm and height $5$ cm ✔

   - A triangle with base $4$ cm and height $2.5$ cm

   - A square measuring $8$ cm by $8$ cm

   - A triangle with base $5$ cm and height $8$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The scale multiplies each real measurement by $2$: $4 \times 2=8$ cm and $2.5 \times 2=5$ cm. The front elevation of the pyramid is therefore a triangle with base $8$ cm and height $5$ cm.

2. A tower of $3$ cubes stands on one grid square. A single cube is placed on the grid square directly to its right. What does the plan show?

   - One square

   - Three squares in a column

   - Two adjacent squares ✔

   - Four squares in a row

   _Explanation:_ A plan is the view from directly above, so it shows only the footprint. The tower occupies one square and the single cube occupies the adjacent square.

3. A cone has radius $3$ cm and perpendicular height $5$ cm. Which side elevation should be drawn?

   - A circle with diameter $6$ cm

   - An isosceles triangle with base $6$ cm and height $5$ cm ✔

   - An isosceles triangle with base $3$ cm and height $5$ cm

   - An isosceles triangle with base $6$ cm and height $3$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The side elevation of a cone is an isosceles triangle. Its base is the cone's diameter, $2 \times 3=6$ cm, and its vertical height is $5$ cm.

4. A cuboid is $6$ cm wide, $4$ cm deep and $3$ cm high. Which dimensions are shown by its plan?

   - $6$ cm wide and $3$ cm high

   - $4$ cm deep and $3$ cm high

   - $3$ cm high and $6$ cm wide

   - $6$ cm wide and $4$ cm deep ✔

   _Explanation:_ The plan is the view from above, so it shows the width and depth: $6$ cm by $4$ cm. The height is not visible in the plan.

5. A solid made from cubes has stack heights of $1$ at the front-left, $3$ at the front-right, $2$ at the back-left and $1$ at the back-right. Looking from the right, what heights appear from front to back?

   - Front $3$ high, back $2$ high ✔

   - Front $1$ high, back $2$ high

   - Front $3$ high, back $1$ high

   - Front $2$ high, back $3$ high

   _Explanation:_ From the right, the stacks at each depth overlap. The maximum front height is $\max(1,3)=3$, and the maximum back height is $\max(2,1)=2$.
