# Surds

Learn why surds are exact roots and how to simplify, expand, and rationalise them confidently.

**Practice questions**

1. Simplify $\sqrt{80}$ fully.

   - $4\sqrt{5}$ ✔

   - $16\sqrt{5}$

   - $8\sqrt{10}$

   - $2\sqrt{20}$

   _Explanation:_ Use the largest square factor: $\sqrt{80}=\sqrt{16\times5}=4\sqrt{5}$. Although $2\sqrt{20}$ is equivalent, it is not fully simplified.

2. Expand and simplify $(2+\sqrt{5})(3-\sqrt{5})$.

   - $1-\sqrt{5}$

   - $1+\sqrt{5}$ ✔

   - $6+\sqrt{5}$

   - $11+\sqrt{5}$

   _Explanation:_ Expanding gives $6-2\sqrt{5}+3\sqrt{5}-5$. Combining like terms produces $1+\sqrt{5}$.

3. Simplify $3\sqrt{48}$ fully.

   - $4\sqrt{3}$

   - $6\sqrt{12}$

   - $12\sqrt{3}$ ✔

   - $48\sqrt{3}$

   _Explanation:_ Since $\sqrt{48}=\sqrt{16\times3}=4\sqrt{3}$, multiplying by 3 gives $3\times4\sqrt{3}=12\sqrt{3}$.

4. Expand and simplify $(4-\sqrt{7})^2$.

   - $9$

   - $23$

   - $23+8\sqrt{7}$

   - $23-8\sqrt{7}$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Using $(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2$, we get $16-8\sqrt{7}+7=23-8\sqrt{7}$.

5. Rationalise the denominator of $\frac{1}{4-\sqrt{7}}$ and simplify.

   - $\frac{4-\sqrt{7}}{9}$

   - $\frac{4+\sqrt{7}}{23}$

   - $4+\sqrt{7}$

   - $\frac{4+\sqrt{7}}{9}$ ✔

   _Explanation:_ Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate $4+\sqrt{7}$. The denominator becomes $(4-\sqrt{7})(4+\sqrt{7})=16-7=9$, giving $\frac{4+\sqrt{7}}{9}$.
