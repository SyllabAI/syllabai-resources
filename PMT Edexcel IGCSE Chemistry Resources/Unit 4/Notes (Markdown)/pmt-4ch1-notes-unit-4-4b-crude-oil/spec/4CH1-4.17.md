# 4CH1-4.17 — describe how long-chain alkanes are converted to alkenes and shorter-chain alkanes by catalytic cracking(using silica or alumina as the catalyst and a temperature in the range of 600-700℃)

- Hydrocarbons can be cracked to produce smaller, more useful molecules. This process involved heating the hydrocarbons to vaporise them. 

- The vapours are: 

   - Either passed over a hot catalyst (silica or alumina) 

   - Mixed with steam and heated to a very high temperature (temperature in the range of 600-700˚C) so that thermal decomposition reactions can occur. 

- The products of cracking include alkanes and unsaturated hydrocarbons called alkenes. Alkenes have the general formula CnH2n
