_<mark>3.17</mark>_   _<mark>know</mark>_   _<mark>that</mark>_   _<mark>some</mark>_   _<mark>reactions</mark>_   _<mark>are</mark>_   _<mark>reversible</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>this</mark>_   _<mark>is</mark>_   _<mark>indicated</mark>_   _<mark>by</mark>_   _<mark>the symbol</mark>_  **** <mark>⇌</mark>  **** _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>equations</mark>_ 

   - In some chemical reactions, the products of the reaction can react to produce the original reactants 

      - These are called reversible reactions 

      - The direction of the reaction can be changed by changing the conditions 

- E.g. The Haber Process 

nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia 

_<mark>3.18</mark>_   _<mark>describe</mark>_   _<mark>reversible</mark>_   _<mark>reactions</mark>_   _<mark>such</mark>_   _<mark>as</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>dehydration</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>hydrated copper(II)</mark>_   _<mark>sulfate</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>effect</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>heat</mark>_   _<mark>on</mark>_   _<mark>ammonium</mark>_   _<mark>chloride</mark>_ 

   - Dehydration of hydrated copper(II) sulfate 

      - Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water ⇌ hydrated copper(II) sulfate 

      - White solid turns blue in presence of water 

      - Forward reaction add water 

      - Reverse reaction heat the hydrated copper(II) sulfate (water evaporates) 

   - Effect of heat on ammonium chloride 

      - Ammonium chloride ⇌ ammonia + hydrogen chloride 

      - The white solid (ammonium chloride) breaks down into ammonia and hydrogen chloride (colourless gases) using heat 

      - These gases can then react to form ammonium chloride 

- _<mark>3.19</mark>_   _<mark>(chemistry</mark>_   _<mark>only)</mark>_   _<mark>know</mark>_   _<mark>that</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>reversible</mark>_   _<mark>reaction</mark>_   _<mark>can</mark>_   _<mark>reach</mark>_   _<mark>dynamic equilibrium</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>sealed</mark>_   _<mark>container</mark>_ 

   - When a reversible reaction occurs in a closed system/sealed container, equilibrium is reached when the reactions occur at exactly the same rate in each direction 

_<mark>3.20</mark>_   _<mark>(chemistry</mark>_   _<mark>only)</mark>_   _<mark>know</mark>_   _<mark>that</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>characteristics</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>reaction</mark>_   _<mark>at</mark>_   _<mark>dynamic equilibrium</mark>_   _<mark>are:</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>forward</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>reverse</mark>_   _<mark>reactions</mark>_   _<mark>occur</mark>_   _<mark>at</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>same</mark>_   _<mark>rate, the</mark>_   _<mark>concentrations</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>reactants</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>products</mark>_   _<mark>remain</mark>_   _<mark>constant</mark>_ 

- A reaction at dynamic equilibrium: one in which the forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant

# _<mark>3.21</mark>_   _<mark>(chemistry</mark>_   _<mark>only)</mark>_   _<mark>understand</mark>_   _<mark>why</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>catalyst</mark>_   _<mark>does</mark>_   _<mark>not</mark>_   _<mark>affect</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>position of</mark>_   _<mark>equilibrium</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>reversible</mark>_   _<mark>reaction</mark>_ {#pmt-4ch1-notes-unit-3-3c-reversible-reactions-and-equilibria-s1}
- A catalyst overall increases the rate of reaction, it increases the rate of a reaction by speeding up the forward AND reverse reactions EQUALLY, therefore it helps a reversible reaction reach equilibrium quicker and DOES NOT have an effect on the position of equilibrium

- The relative amounts of all the reacting substances at equilibrium depend on the conditions of the reaction. 

- If a system is at equilibrium and a change is made to any of the conditions, then the system responds to counteract the change. 

changing the pressure: 

- In gaseous reactions, an increase in pressure will favour the reaction that produces the least number of molecules as shown by the symbol equation for that reaction. 

- If **** pressure is increased: equilibrium shifts to **** side of equation with fewer moles of gas (e.g. N2  + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3,  left side has 4 moles of gas (1+3) and right has 2 moles of gas. If you increase the pressure equilibrium moves right as there are fewer moles of gas on the right hand side, making more product) 

- if **** pressure is decreased: equilibrium will shift to **** side of equation with more moles of gas **** (e.g. for previous example equilibrium would move left, making more reactant.
