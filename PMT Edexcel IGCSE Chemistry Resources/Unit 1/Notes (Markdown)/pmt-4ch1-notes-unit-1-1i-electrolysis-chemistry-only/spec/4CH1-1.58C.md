# 4CH1-1.58C — describe experiments to investigate electrolysis, using inert electrodes, of molten compounds (including lead(II) bromide) and aqueous solutions (including sodium chloride, dilute sulfuric acid and copper(II) sulfate) and to predict the products

- During electrolysis, **** positively charged **** ions move to the **** negative electrode (cathode), and **** negatively charged ions **** move to the **** positive electrode (anode). 

- Ions are discharged at the electrodes producing elements, this process is called electrolysis 

- When you have a ionic solution (NOT a molten ionic compound), your solution will contain: the ions that make up the ionic compound, and the ions in water (OH<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> and H<sup>+</sup> )<sup></sup> 

- at the cathode (-): 

   - hydrogen (from H<sup>+</sup> <sup></sup> in water) is produced UNLESS the + ions in the ionic compound are from a metal less reactive than hydrogen 

   - if the metal is less reactive, it will be produced instead 

- at the anode (+): 

   - oxygen (from OH<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> in water) will be produced UNLESS the ionic compound contains halide ions (Cl<sup>-</sup> ,<sup></sup>  Br<sup>-</sup>  ,<sup></sup>  I<sup>-</sup>  )<sup></sup> 

   - if there are halide ions, the halogen will be produced instead (e.g. Cl2)  

![1i-electrolysis-chemistry-only figure](images/1a7afc41ff0d8b1a.png)

   - Using the logic above… 

   - Electrolysis of: 

      - Sodium chloride solution 

         - H<sup>+</sup> <sup></sup> ions go to cathode, H2 (g) is produced (Na is more reactive than hydrogen) 

         - Cl<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> ions go to anode, Cl2 (g) is produced (Cl<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> are halide ions) 

      - Copper (II) sulfate solution 

         - Cu<sup>+</sup> <sup></sup> ions go to cathode, Cu (s) is produced (Cu is less reactive than hydrogen) 

         - OH<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> ions go to anode, O2  (g) is produced (SO42- ions are not halide ions) 

      - Water acidified with sulfuric acid 

         - H<sup>+</sup> <sup></sup> to cathode, H2 (g) is produced (these are the other ions present in sulfuric acid H2SO 4)  

         - OH<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> to anode, O2  (g) is produced (SO42- ions are not halide ions) 

      - Molten lead (II) bromide (demonstration) 

         - Pb<sup>2+</sup> <sup></sup> to cathode, Pb (s) is produced (not in solution so these are the only + ions present) 

         - Br<sup>-</sup>  <sup></sup> to anode, Br2  (l) is produced **** (not in solution so these are the only - ions present)
