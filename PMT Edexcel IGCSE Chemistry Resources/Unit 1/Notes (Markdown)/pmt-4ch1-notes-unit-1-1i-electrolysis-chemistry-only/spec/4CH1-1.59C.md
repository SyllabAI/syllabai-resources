# 4CH1-1.59C — write ionic half-equations representing the reactions at the electrodes during electrolysis and understand why these reactions are classified as oxidation or reduction

- This is an example of a half equation; the small number is always the same as the 2 larger numbers within the equation. & electrons are represented by the symbol ‘e-‘ 

![1i-electrolysis-chemistry-only figure](images/8360cb2f1aece4ea.png)

- Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons) 

- Reduction Is Gain (of electrons) 

![1i-electrolysis-chemistry-only figure](images/3822f29c1e84b486.png)

- writing half equations for the reactions at each electrode: 

   - negative electrode: X<sup>+</sup> <sup></sup> -> X, so ionic equation must be: 

      - X<sup>+</sup> <sup></sup> + e<sup>-</sup>  <sup></sup> -> X, electrons gained, so positive ions are reduced 

   - positive electrode: X<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> -> X, so ionic equation must be: X<sup>-</sup> <sup></sup> -> e<sup>-</sup>  <sup></sup> + X, electrons are lost, so negative ions are oxidised
