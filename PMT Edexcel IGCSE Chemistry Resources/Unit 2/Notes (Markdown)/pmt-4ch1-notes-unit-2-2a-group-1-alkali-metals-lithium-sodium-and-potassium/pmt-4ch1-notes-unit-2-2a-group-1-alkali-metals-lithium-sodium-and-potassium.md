# _<mark>2.1</mark>_   _<mark>understand</mark>_   _<mark>how</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>similarities</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>reactions</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>these</mark>_   _<mark>elements</mark>_   _<mark>with water</mark>_   _<mark>provide</mark>_   _<mark>evidence</mark>_   _<mark>for</mark>_   _<mark>their</mark>_   _<mark>recognition</mark>_   _<mark>as</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>family</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>elements</mark>_ {#pmt-4ch1-notes-unit-2-2a-group-1-alkali-metals-lithium-sodium-and-potassium-s1}
- They have characteristic properties due to the single electron in their outer shell. 

- ● All of the metals in group one react vigorously with water to create an alkaline solution and hydrogen. 

# _<mark>2.2</mark>_   _<mark>understand</mark>_   _<mark>how</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>differences</mark>_   _<mark>between</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>reactions</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>these elements</mark>_   _<mark>with</mark>_   _<mark>air</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>water</mark>_   _<mark>provide</mark>_   _<mark>evidence</mark>_   _<mark>for</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>trend</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>reactivity</mark>_   _<mark>in Group</mark>_   _<mark>1</mark>_ {#pmt-4ch1-notes-unit-2-2a-group-1-alkali-metals-lithium-sodium-and-potassium-s2}
- They all react with oxygen to create an oxide. 

- More bubbles with reaction with water = more vigorous reaction = more reactive alkali metal 

- Reactivity increases down the group – therefore reactivity increases from lithium to potassium 

|metal|reaction with water|
|---|---|
|lithium|fizzes steadily|
|sodium|melts into a ball then fizzes quickly|
|potassium|gives off sparks and hydrogen burns with a lilac flame|

# _<mark>2.3</mark>_   _<mark>use</mark>_   _<mark>knowledge</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>trends</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>Group</mark>_   _<mark>1</mark>_   _<mark>to</mark>_   _<mark>predict</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>properties</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>other alkali</mark>_   _<mark>metals</mark>_ {#pmt-4ch1-notes-unit-2-2a-group-1-alkali-metals-lithium-sodium-and-potassium-s3}
- see 2.2- increase in reactivity down the group applies for any reaction 

# _<mark>2.4</mark>_   _<mark>(chemistry</mark>_   _<mark>only)</mark>_   _<mark>explain</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>trend</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>reactivity</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>Group</mark>_   _<mark>1</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>terms</mark>_   _<mark>of electronic</mark>_   _<mark>configurations</mark>_ {#pmt-4ch1-notes-unit-2-2a-group-1-alkali-metals-lithium-sodium-and-potassium-s4}
- Down the group – easier to lose electrons and form positive metal ions (cations) (these are formed when metals react) 

- It is easier to lose electrons due to the increase in electron shells as you go down the group. This means there is more electron shielding and so decrease in attraction between the positively charged nucleus and the negatively charged outer shell electrons, which can then be lost more easily
