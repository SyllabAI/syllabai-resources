_<mark>2.10</mark>_   _<mark>understand</mark>_   _<mark>how</mark>_   _<mark>to</mark>_   _<mark>determine</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>percentage</mark>_   _<mark>by</mark>_   _<mark>volume</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>oxygen</mark>_   _<mark>in air</mark>_   _<mark>using</mark>_   _<mark>experiments</mark>_   _<mark>involving</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>reactions</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>metals</mark>_   _<mark>(e.g.</mark>_   _<mark>iron)</mark>_   _<mark>and non-metals</mark>_   _<mark>(e.g.</mark>_   _<mark>phosphorus)</mark>_   _<mark>with</mark>_   _<mark>air</mark>_ 

example using copper: 

- 100 cm<sup>3</sup>  <sup></sup> of air passed from side to side over copper that was being heated with a Bunsen burner 

- All oxygen in air will react with copper 

- It’s a closed system – therefore, no air could get in or out 

- As it is passed, the volume of air will decrease 

- Continued until the volume stops decreasing, then record the volume of remaining air 

- There would be about 79cm<sup>3</sup> <sup></sup> left, showing that 21cm<sup>3</sup> <sup></sup> of the original 100cm<sup>3</sup> <sup></sup> of air was oxygen 

- The reaction happening in this case (with copper) is: 

   -  copper + oxygen -> copper (II) oxide // 2Cu (s) + O2 (g) -> CuO (s) 

      - Would notice black copper oxide forming 

# _<mark>2.11</mark>_   _<mark>describe</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>combustion</mark>_   _<mark>of</mark>_   _<mark>elements</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>oxygen,</mark>_   _<mark>including</mark>_   _<mark>magnesium, hydrogen</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>sulfur</mark>_ {#pmt-4ch1-notes-unit-2-2c-gases-in-the-atmosphere-s2}
- Combustion is an example of oxidation 

   - In an oxidation reaction, a substance gains oxygen 

   - Metals and non-metals can take part in these reactions 

- E.g. magnesium + oxygen -> magnesium oxide 

   - 2Mg + O2  -> 2MgO 

- E.g. sulfur + oxygen -> sulfur dioxide 

   - S + O2 -> SO2 

- E.g. hydrogen + oxygen -> water o 2H2  + O2 -> 2H2O

_<mark>2.13</mark>_   _<mark>know</mark>_   _<mark>that</mark>_   _<mark>carbon</mark>_   _<mark>dioxide</mark>_   _<mark>is</mark>_   _<mark>a</mark>_   _<mark>greenhouse</mark>_   _<mark>gas</mark>_   _<mark>and</mark>_   _<mark>that</mark>_   _<mark>increasing amounts</mark>_   _<mark>in</mark>_   _<mark>the</mark>_   _<mark>atmosphere</mark>_   _<mark>may</mark>_   _<mark>contribute</mark>_   _<mark>to</mark>_   _<mark>climate</mark>_   _<mark>change</mark>_ 

- Greenhouse gas effect maintains temperatures on Earth high enough to support life 

- Greenhouse gases include: water vapour, CO2 & CH4 

- Explanation of the greenhouse gas effect: 

   - Electromagnetic radiation at most wavelengths from the sun passes through the Earth’s atmosphere 

   - The Earth absorbs some radiation and thus warms up (essential for life on Earth). But some heat is radiated from the Earth as infrared radiation. 

   - Some of this IR radiation is absorbed by greenhouse gases in the atmosphere 

   - Atmosphere warms up leading to the greenhouse effect and global warming 

- Global warming is an ‘enhanced greenhouse effect’ 

- An increase in average global temperature is a major cause of climate change
