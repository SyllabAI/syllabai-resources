## What a best buy question is really asking

In a best buy question, you are not looking for the lowest total price. You are looking for the option that gives the lowest cost for the same amount of product.

A useful method is:
1. Read the price and amount for each option.
2. Adjust any offers so you know the total cost and total amount.
3. Choose a fair unit to compare, such as per item, per kg, per 100 g, or per 100 ml.
4. Convert to pence if that makes the arithmetic easier.
5. Calculate the unit price for each option.
6. Compare the unit prices.
7. Pick the option with the smaller unit price.

A fair comparison usually means finding a unit price, such as cost per item, per kg, per 100 g, or per 100 ml. The calculation is done using the following formula:

$$$\text{unit price} = \frac{\text{price}}{\text{amount}}$$

The best value is the option with the smaller unit price. Always compare like with like before you choose.

## Working in pence for small items

For small items, it is often easier to work in pence instead of pounds. That keeps the division simple and gives sensible answers like pence per roll or pence per bar.

For example, £2.64 is 264p. If 12 cereal bars cost 264p, then $264 \div 12 = 22$, so each bar costs 22p.

When the products are countable, compare the cost of one item in each pack. Then choose the smaller cost per item.

## Worked example: cost per item

Two packs of kitchen rolls are on sale: Pack A has 8 rolls for £3.60 and Pack B has 6 rolls for £2.82. Since the total prices and quantities differ, we must compare the cost per individual roll to determine which is the best buy.

Convert the prices to pence first to simplify the division. Pack A costs 360p, so $360 \div 8 = 45$, which is 45p per roll. Pack B costs 282p, so $282 \div 6 = 47$, which is 47p per roll.

Now the comparison is fair because both units are in pence per roll. Pack A is the better value because 45p per roll is less than 47p per roll.

## Choosing a sensible unit for weights and volumes

When products are sold by weight or volume, you must choose one sensible unit for all options. Common choices are per kg, per 100 g, and per 100 ml.

Suppose Jar A is 250 g for £5.95 and Jar B is 400 g for £8.80. Comparing per 100 g works well because both masses scale neatly, and you must use the same unit for both jars.

Jar A is 2.5 lots of 100 g, so $5.95 \div 2.5 = 2.38$, which is £2.38 per 100 g. Jar B is 4 lots of 100 g, so $8.80 \div 4 = 2.20$, which is £2.20 per 100 g. Since £2.20 is smaller than £2.38, Jar B is the better value.

## Scaling to the same amount

Sometimes one amount is a simple multiple of another, so scaling is quicker than finding the cost of one unit. Multiply or divide both amount and price by the same factor to keep the deal equivalent.

Example: Bottle A is 750 ml for £4.20 and Bottle B is 250 ml for £1.50. Since 750 ml is 3 lots of 250 ml, multiply Bottle B's price by 3. We get $3 \times 1.50 = 4.50$, so the matching price is £4.50.

Now the amounts match at 750 ml, so compare the total prices for the same volume. Bottle A is the better value because £4.20 is less than £4.50.

## Offers: extra free and percentage off

Special offers must be adjusted before you compare. 'Extra free' changes the amount, while '% off' changes the price, so do not change both unless the question says to.

Pack A has 200 tea bags plus 25% extra free for £3.60. Since 25% of 200 is 50, Pack A has 250 tea bags. The price stays 360p, and $360 \div 250 = 1.44$, so Pack A costs 1.44p per tea bag.

Pack B has 200 tea bags with 10% off the normal price of £3.60. Ten percent of 360p is 36p, so the new price is 324p. Then $324 \div 200 = 1.62$, so Pack B costs 1.62p per tea bag and Pack A is the better value.

## Check yourself

1. In a best buy question, what should you usually compare?

   - The cost for the same amount of product ✔

   - Only the total price

   - Only the amount of product

   - Only the size of the offer label

   _Explanation:_ Best buy means compare like with like, usually by finding a unit price. The common wrong answer is B, but a smaller pack can have a lower price tag and still cost more per item. Hint: Think about "per item" or "per $100$ g".

2. Which pack of pens is the better value?

   - Pack A: 15 pens for £4.20 ✔

   - Pack B: 10 pens for £2.90

   - They are the same value

   - Not enough information

   _Explanation:_ Convert to pence and divide. Pack A costs $420 \div 15 = 28$ p per pen, while Pack B costs $290 \div 10 = 29$ p per pen, so Pack A is better value. The common wrong answer is B because it has the cheaper total price, but best buy depends on cost per pen.

3. Which drink is the best value?

   - Bottle A: $500$ ml for $£1.80$

   - Bottle B: $600$ ml plus $20\%$ extra free for $£2.10$ ✔

   - Bottle C: $750$ ml for $£2.52$

   _Explanation:_ Adjust the offer first. Bottle B becomes $720$ ml ($600 \times 1.2$), so its cost is about $29.2$p per $100$ ml. Bottle A is $36$p per $100$ ml ($180 \div 5$) and Bottle C is $33.6$p per $100$ ml ($252 \div 7.5$), so Bottle B is the best value. The common wrong answer is C because it is the largest bottle, but the largest volume is not always the cheapest per unit.
