## What fractions mean

A fraction shows equal parts of a whole. In $\frac{3}{5}$, the numerator 3 tells you how many parts you have and the denominator 5 tells you the whole is split into 5 equal parts.

Different fractions can name the same amount, such as $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{2}{4}$. We call these equivalent fractions, and that idea lets us rewrite fractions when we need a common denominator.

## Simplifying fractions

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by a common factor. If you use the highest common factor, you reach simplest form in one step. Here is a full example with $\frac{12}{18}$.

$$$\frac{12}{18}=\frac{12\div6}{18\div6}=\frac{2}{3}$$

After dividing both by 6, the fraction is simplest because 2 and 3 share no factor greater than 1. A quick check can save time: if both numbers are even, try 2 first, and if the digits add to a multiple of 3, try 3.

## Adding and subtracting fractions

![Fraction strips showing one quarter plus one sixth rewritten as three twelfths and two twelfths, then combined to make five twelfths](../assets/001-58914fa5-1abd-4bc2-99e3-16a24efa35bf.jpeg)
To add or subtract fractions, make the denominators the same first. The lowest common multiple is often the best common denominator because it keeps the numbers small.

The strip model shows why $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{6}$ can both be rewritten in twelfths.
$$\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{3}{12}+\frac{2}{12}=\frac{5}{12}$$

We add the numerators but keep the denominator as 12 because the part size has not changed. The same idea works for subtraction. Never add or subtract the denominators, so $\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5}$ is $\frac{3}{5}$, not $\frac{3}{10}$.

## Multiplying fractions

When multiplying fractions, multiply numerator by numerator and denominator by denominator. You do not need a common denominator for multiplication.

Here is a full example: $$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{9}{10}=\frac{18}{30}=\frac{3}{5}$$ Multiply first, then simplify the final fraction.

You can often cancel common factors before multiplying to keep the numbers smaller. In $\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{8}{9}$, divide 3 and 9 by 3, and divide 8 and 4 by 4 first.

$$$\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{8}{9}=\frac{1\times2}{1\times3}=\frac{2}{3}$$ Only cancel when the fractions are being multiplied.

## Dividing fractions

![Flowchart showing keep, change, flip for four sevenths divided by two thirds, changing to four sevenths times three halves, then simplifying to six sevenths](../assets/002-a8ac143a-a6b1-43e0-ad5a-a2d8607f998b.jpeg)
To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. A helpful memory aid is keep, change, flip: keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, and flip the second fraction.

Work through the example step by step:
$$\frac{4}{7}\div\frac{2}{3}=\frac{4}{7}\times\frac{3}{2}=\frac{12}{14}=\frac{6}{7}$$

After flipping the second fraction, it becomes an ordinary multiplication problem. Note that a denominator can never be 0, and you cannot divide by a fraction equal to 0. Division by zero is undefined.

## Mixed numbers and improper fractions

A mixed number has a whole number part and a fraction part, such as $1 \frac{3}{4}$. An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator, such as $\frac{7}{4}$.

To change a mixed number into an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the same denominator.
$$1 \frac{3}{4}=\frac{1\times4+3}{4}=\frac{7}{4}$$
The denominator stays 4 because the size of each part is still one quarter.

To change back, divide the numerator by the denominator to find the whole number and the remainder. This works because the remainder represents the leftover fractional parts.
$$\frac{28}{15}=1 \frac{13}{15}$$

In multiplication and division questions with mixed numbers, convert to improper fractions before you start. At the end, simplify and change back to a mixed number only if the question asks for it.

## Worked example with mixed-number addition

For addition and subtraction, changing mixed numbers to improper fractions is often the safest method. Then you can use the same common-denominator idea as with ordinary fractions.

Work out $2 \frac{1}{4}+1 \frac{2}{3}$ by converting first.
$$2 \frac{1}{4}=\frac{9}{4},\qquad 1 \frac{2}{3}=\frac{5}{3}$$

With denominator 12, the sum becomes
$$\frac{9}{4}+\frac{5}{3}=\frac{27}{12}+\frac{20}{12}=\frac{47}{12}$$

Finally, turn the improper fraction back into a mixed number.
$$\frac{47}{12}=3 \frac{11}{12}$$
So the answer is $3 \frac{11}{12}$.

## Check yourself

1. Which is the simplest form of $\frac{18}{24}$?

   - $$\frac{3}{4}$ ✔

   - $$\frac{9}{12}$

   - $$\frac{6}{8}$

   - $$\frac{2}{3}$

   _Explanation:_ $$\frac{18}{24}$ simplifies by dividing top and bottom by 6, giving $\frac{3}{4}$. A common wrong answer is $\frac{9}{12}$ because it divides by 2 but stops too early, so the fraction is not fully simplified. Hint: Look for the highest common factor of 18 and 24.

2. Which statement is correct?

   - In $\frac{3}{4}+\frac{8}{9}$, you can cancel 4 with 8 before adding.

   - In $\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{8}{9}$, you can cancel common factors before multiplying. ✔

   - To multiply fractions, add the denominators and multiply the numerators.

   - You must find a common denominator before multiplying fractions.

   _Explanation:_ B is correct because a product can be simplified by cancelling common factors across numerators and denominators before multiplying. The most common wrong choice is A, but cancelling across addition changes the value of the sum and is not allowed.

3. Stretch: Work out $1 \frac{1}{2} \div \frac{3}{5}$.

   - $$\frac{9}{10}$

   - $$\frac{5}{2}$ ✔

   - $$\frac{1}{10}$

   - $$2$

   _Explanation:_ First convert $1 \frac{1}{2}$ to $\frac{3}{2}$. Then use keep, change, flip: $\frac{3}{2} \div \frac{3}{5} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{5}{3} = \frac{5}{2}$. The most common wrong answer is A, which comes from multiplying by $\frac{3}{5}$ instead of its reciprocal.
