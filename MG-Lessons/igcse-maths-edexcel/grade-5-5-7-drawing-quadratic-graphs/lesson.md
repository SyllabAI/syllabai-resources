## What is a quadratic graph?

Quadratic graphs come from equations where the highest power of $x$ is $x^2$. Their shape is called a parabola.

A parabola can open upwards or downwards. It is drawn as a smooth curve, not straight line segments.

To draw one accurately, make a table of values, plot the coordinates, and then draw a smooth curve. You can check your graph using intercepts, symmetry, and the turning point.

## Substituting values

When $x=-2$, substitute carefully into $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$. Use brackets because the x-value is negative to ensure the signs are handled correctly.

$$$y = (-2)^2 - 2(-2) - 3$$

Simplify each part of the equation. Since $(-2)^2 = 4$ and $-2(-2)=4$, the expression simplifies to $y = 4 + 4 - 3 = 5$.

The resulting coordinate is $(-2, 5)$. Repeating this step for several x-values builds the complete table of coordinates for the graph.

## Completing the table of values

To draw the full graph, repeat the substitution for several $x$-values from $-2$ to $4$. The table gives all the coordinates you need.

The completed table has $x$-values $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ and $y$-values $5, 0, -3, -4, -3, 0, 5$. Read each matching pair as one coordinate.

The $y$-values $5, 0, -3, -4, -3, 0, 5$ are symmetric. That tells you the curve has its lowest point at $(1, -4)$.

## Plotting and drawing the curve

![Graph of y equals x squared minus 2x minus 3 on labelled axes with plotted points, x-intercepts, y-intercept, and turning point marked](../assets/001-f198f2e4-1fd6-4619-a6a5-edb1cbabd141.jpeg)

These plotted points come directly from the completed table: $(-2,5)$, $(-1,0)$, $(0,-3)$, $(1,-4)$, $(2,-3)$, $(3,0)$, and $(4,5)$.

A smooth parabola passes through them, so do not use a ruler to join point to point. Quadratic graphs must be drawn as a single continuous curve to correctly represent the function.

Check the intercepts before finishing the curve. The graph crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,-3)$ and the $x$-axis at $(-1,0)$ and $(3,0)$. The turning point is $(1,-4)$, which sits halfway between the matching pairs of points, showing the symmetry line $x=1$.

## When the parabola opens downwards

![Graph of y equals minus x squared plus 4 on labelled axes with plotted points, x-intercepts, and maximum turning point marked](../assets/002-08c1df17-3b1d-47e6-90aa-1169b9b574eb.jpeg)

If the coefficient of $x^2$ is negative, the parabola opens downwards instead of upwards. For $y = -x^2 + 4$, the graph has a maximum rather than a minimum.

Substituting a few values gives points such as $(-2,0)$, $(0,4)$ and $(2,0)$. The matching $y$-values on the left and right show symmetry about $x=0$.

The turning point is $(0,4)$, which is the highest point on the graph. You still plot points first and then draw one smooth curve to connect them.

## Common mistakes and exam technique

A very common mistake is forgetting brackets with negative $x$-values. If $x=-2$, then $x^2$ means $(-2)^2=4$, not $-2^2=-4$.

Another mistake is joining the points with straight line segments or using a ruler. A quadratic must be drawn as one smooth curve after all the points are plotted.

In an exam, choose a sensible scale and plot with small neat crosses. Then check $x=0$ for the $y$-intercept and look for symmetry before you finish the curve.

## Check yourself

1. Which equation will produce a quadratic graph?

   - $$y = x^2 - 3x + 1$ ✔

   - $$y = 2x + 5$

   - $$y = \frac{3}{x}$

   - $$y = \sqrt{x}$

   _Explanation:_ A quadratic equation has highest power $2$, so $y = x^2 - 3x + 1$ is quadratic. The common wrong answer B is linear because the highest power of $x$ is only $1$. Look for an $x^2$ term.

2. Which coordinate lies on the graph of $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$?

   - $$(2,-3)$ ✔

   - $$(2,3)$

   - $$(-3,2)$

   - $$(3,-2)$

   _Explanation:_ Substituting $x=2$ gives $y = 2^2 - 2(2) - 3 = 4 - 4 - 3 = -3$, so $(2,-3)$ is correct. The common wrong answer B comes from changing the sign of the final result.

3. For $y = -x^2 + 4$, the point $(-1,3)$ is on the graph. Which point must also be on the graph?

   - $$(1,3)$ ✔

   - $$(1,-3)$

   - $$(-1,-3)$

   - $$(3,1)$

   _Explanation:_ The graph of $y = -x^2 + 4$ is symmetric about the y-axis, so points the same distance left and right have the same y-value. The common wrong answer B changes the sign of y, but symmetry keeps y the same.
