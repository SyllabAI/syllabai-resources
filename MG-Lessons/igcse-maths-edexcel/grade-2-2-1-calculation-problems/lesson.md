## Read, plan, calculate, answer

A calculation problem is a worded question where you must choose the right operations. Good answers follow a routine: read carefully, plan the steps, do the calculation, then answer the question asked. In a "show that" question, your working should make the final result easy to follow.

First identify how many items, hours, packs or lengths there are, and the amount for one item or one group. Then decide whether the question is asking for a total, change, a missing amount, or whether there is enough, and check that the units match.

A unit price is the cost of one item, and a rate is an amount for one unit such as £$9.50$ per hour or $40\text{ g}$ per wash. Matching the numbers to these ideas helps you choose between multiplying, dividing, adding, and subtracting.

## Money, pence, and change

When prices are mixed between pounds and pence, convert to one unit before calculating. Working in pence is often safest because £10 becomes 1000p.

Aisha buys 7 rolls at 68p each. First find the total cost: $$7 \times 68 = 476$$ So the rolls cost 476p.

Now subtract from the amount paid: $$1000 - 476 = 524$$ The change is 524p, which is £5.24. If you mixed £10 and 476p without converting, the subtraction would not be valid.

## Packs and practical rounding

Many practical division questions do not end with a whole number. If the question asks for "as many as possible", round down because you cannot buy or make part of a whole item.

Sam has £6 and each chocolate bar costs 45p, so convert to 600p and divide: 

$$600 \div 45 = 13 \text{ remainder } 15$$

Sam can buy 13 bars, not 14, because 14 bars would cost 630p.

If the question asks for "enough", round up whenever there is a remainder. For 1040 g of washing powder sold in 650 g packs, $$1040 \div 650 = 1.6$$ so 1 pack is not enough and 2 packs are needed.

## Measurements must match

Before dividing measurements, convert them to the same unit. Useful facts for these calculations include $1 \, \text{L} = 1000 \, \text{ml}$, $1 \, \text{kg} = 1000 \, \text{g}$, and $1 \, \text{m} = 100 \, \text{cm}$.

For example, if Priya has 1.8 L of water, she has 1800 ml. If each cup needs 220 ml, we divide the total volume by the amount needed per cup:

$$$\frac{1800 \, \text{ml}}{220 \, \text{ml/cup}} \approx 8.18 \, \text{cups}$$

The $\text{ml}$ units cancel, leaving the result in cups. Since she can only make full cups, the practical answer is 8 cups, as the extra 40 ml is a remainder and is not enough for another full cup.

## Finding hidden unit prices

Sometimes the question gives the price of several items instead of one item. Divide first to reveal the unit price, then continue with the rest of the problem.

If 4 pens cost £1.40, work in pence: 140p for 4 pens. To find the price of one pen, divide the total by the quantity:
$$140 \div 4 = 35$$
One pen costs 35p.

If 3 pens and 5 pencils cost £1.80, then 180p minus the cost of 3 pens is:
$$180 - 3 \times 35 = 75$$
This calculation shows that 5 pencils cost 75p.

Now divide again to find the unit price of a pencil:
$$75 \div 5 = 15$$
One pencil costs 15p, so one pen and 2 pencils cost 65p. Converting both totals to pence keeps the steps consistent.

## Total and difference with a bar model

![Bar model of Ben and Sara's savings showing equal parts, Sara's extra £28, and total £376](../assets/001-6c2a3c79-409c-4040-8df5-bbac71520149.jpeg)
A bar model helps when two amounts have a known total and one amount is larger than the other. Remove the extra part first, because only then are you left with two equal amounts.

Ben and Sara save £376 altogether, and Sara saves £28 more than Ben. To solve this, subtract the extra amount from the total and then halve what is left to find the smaller share.

$$$376 - 28 = 348$$
$$348 \div 2 = 174$$
So Ben saved £174.

You can check your work by finding Sara's amount: $174 + 28 = 202$. Then, verifying the total gives $174 + 202 = 376$, which matches the original information.

## Check yourself

1. A packet of 8 pens costs $£3.20$. To find the cost of 3 pens, what is the best first step?

   - Divide $£3.20$ by 8 to find the cost of 1 pen ✔

   - Multiply $£3.20$ by 3

   - Divide $£3.20$ by 3

   _Explanation:_ Dividing $£3.20$ by 8 gives the unit price of one pen, and then you can multiply by 3. The most common wrong answer is multiplying $£3.20$ by 3, which treats the price of 8 pens as if it were the price of 1 pen.

2. Priya has 1.8 litres of water and each cup needs 220 ml. How many full cups can she make?

   - 8 ✔

   - 9

   - 18

   - 0.8

   _Explanation:_ Convert 1.8 litres to 1800 ml, then divide: $1800 \div 220 = 8$ remainder 40, so only 8 full cups can be made. The most common wrong answer is 9, which rounds up even though there is not enough water for a ninth cup.

3. Stretch: Two cousins save £512 altogether. One cousin saves £36 more than the other. How much does the smaller saver have?

   - £238 ✔

   - £256

   - £274

   - £220

   _Explanation:_ Subtract the extra £36 first to leave two equal amounts: $512 - 36 = 476$, and $476 \div 2 = 238$. The most common wrong answer is £256, which is half the total and ignores the difference between the two cousins.
