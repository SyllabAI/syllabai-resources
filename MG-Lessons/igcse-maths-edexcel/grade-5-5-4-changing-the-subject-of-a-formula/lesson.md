## What does "make a letter the subject" mean?

![Flowchart showing p multiplied by 4 then increased by 13 to make z, with reverse arrows subtract 13 then divide by 4 to get p back](../assets/001-cd6cde21-a688-47a2-9acb-6fbbd996ee4b.jpeg)

A formula links quantities, and the subject is the letter isolated on its own. If a question says "make $x$ the subject", your final line should be $x = \text{something}$.

Think about what has been done to the target letter, then undo those operations in reverse order. Whatever you do to one side of the equation, do to the other side to keep it balanced.

Addition is undone by subtraction, multiplication by division, and squaring by square rooting. This inverse-operation idea is the key to every rearranging question.

## Worked example: reverse the operations

Make $p$ the subject of $z = 4p + 13$. Undo the last operation first by subtracting 13 from both sides. $$z - 13 = 4p$$

Now divide by 4 to undo the multiplication. Write the required subject on the left. $$\frac{z - 13}{4} = p$$ $$p = \frac{z - 13}{4}$$

A common mistake is dividing by 4 first. That would be wrong because only $p$ is multiplied by 4, not the whole expression $4p + 13$.

## Brackets and simple fractions

If the variable is inside a bracket, undo what happens to the whole bracket first. In $x = 5(h - 2)$, divide by 5 before changing anything inside. $$\frac{x}{5} = h - 2$$

Now add 2 to both sides to isolate $h$. Do not write $x = 5h - 2$, because the 5 multiplies every term in the bracket. $$h = \frac{x}{5} + 2$$

For a fraction like $y = \frac{3x - 1}{5}$, clear the denominator first by multiplying both sides by 5. Then add 1 and divide by 3. $$5y = 3x - 1$$ $$x = \frac{5y + 1}{3}$$

## When the variable is in a denominator or has a fractional coefficient

If the target variable is in a denominator, multiply both sides by that denominator to remove the fraction. For $k = \frac{m + 4}{r}$, this gives
$$kr = m + 4$$

Now divide by $k$ to isolate $r$. This gives
$$r = \frac{m + 4}{k}$$

If a coefficient is a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. From $y = \frac{3}{4}x - 8$, first get $y + 8 = \frac{3}{4}x$, then use $\frac{4}{3}$ to obtain
$$x = \frac{4(y + 8)}{3}$$

## Squares, cubes and square roots

When the target variable is squared, isolate the square first and then square root both sides. From $s = n^2 + 10$, subtract 10 to get $$s - 10 = n^2$$

Square rooting gives two possible values unless the context says the variable must be positive. That is why the final answer is $$n = \pm\sqrt{s - 10}$$

If the whole formula starts with a square root, undo that root first by squaring. For $g = \sqrt{\frac{q + 4}{7}}$, this leads to $$q = 7g^2 - 4$$ and similarly $y = x^3 - 6$ gives $x = \sqrt[3]{y + 6}$.

## Science-style formulas use the same algebra

The letters may look like physics, but the rearranging steps are unchanged. In $V = U + aT$, subtract $U$ first to get
$$V - U = aT$$

Now divide by $T$ to isolate $a$. The result is
$$a = \frac{V - U}{T}$$

You can do the same with squared terms in formulas such as $A^2 = B^2 + 6kL$. Subtract $B^2$ and divide by $6L$ to get
$$k = \frac{A^2 - B^2}{6L}$$

## Check yourself

1. To make $p$ the subject of $z = 4p + 13$, what should you do first?

   - Subtract 13 from both sides ✔

   - Divide both sides by 4

   - Add 13 to both sides

   - Multiply both sides by 4

   _Explanation:_ The correct first step is to subtract 13, because adding 13 is the last thing that happened to $p$. The most common wrong answer is dividing by 4 first, but that is wrong because only $4p$ is multiplied by 4, not the whole expression $4p + 13$.

2. Make $r$ the subject of $k = \frac{m + 4}{r}$.

   - $$r = \frac{k}{m + 4}$

   - $$r = \frac{m + 4}{k}$ ✔

   - $$r = k(m + 4)$

   - $$r = m + 4 - k$

   _Explanation:_ Multiply both sides by $r$ first to get $kr = m + 4$, then divide by $k$, so $r = \frac{m + 4}{k}$. The most common wrong answer is $r = \frac{k}{m + 4}$, but that flips the fraction without doing the same balanced steps to both sides.

3. Make $k$ the subject of $A^2 = B^2 + 6kL$.

   - $$k = \frac{A^2 - B^2}{6L}$ ✔

   - $$k = \frac{A^2 + B^2}{6L}$

   - $$k = \frac{(A - B)^2}{6L}$

   - $$k = \frac{A^2 - B^2}{6}L$

   _Explanation:_ Subtract $B^2$ from both sides to get $A^2 - B^2 = 6kL$, then divide everything by $6L$. The most common wrong answer is $k = \frac{A^2 - B^2}{6}L$, but that divides by 6 and then multiplies by $L$, so it does not undo the full coefficient $6L$.
