## Area and perimeter: different measurements

Area measures the flat space inside a 2D shape, while perimeter measures the distance around the outside. The clue is in the units: area uses square units such as $cm^2$, and perimeter uses length units such as $cm$.

On a centimetre grid, each small square has area $1\text{ cm}^2$. You can count squares for simple shapes, but formulas are faster and less likely to lead to mistakes.

## Rectangles and squares

For a rectangle, the area is defined by $A = l \times w$ and the perimeter by $P = 2l + 2w$. A square is a special rectangle with all sides equal, so its specific formulas are $A = s^2$ and $P = 4s$.

For a rectangle measuring $6$ cm by $3$ cm, the calculations are $A = 6 \times 3 = 18$ and $P = 2(6) + 2(3) = 18$. Notice that the area value is $18 \text{ cm}^2$ while the perimeter is $18 \text{ cm}$.

## Triangles, parallelograms and trapezia

![Four shapes showing area formulas: rectangle with length and width, triangle with perpendicular height, parallelogram with perpendicular height, and trapezium with parallel sides and height](../assets/001-78c40137-7b7e-41dc-8966-abff5b620d82.jpeg)
Triangles, parallelograms and trapezia all use a perpendicular height. That height must meet the base at a right angle, so a sloping side is usually not the height.

The key area formulas are $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$ for a triangle, $A = bh$ for a parallelogram, and $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$ for a trapezium. In a trapezium, $a$ and $b$ are the parallel sides, so you add those first.

For example, with parallel sides $6$ m and $12$ m and height $5$ m, the calculation is $A = \frac{1}{2}(6+12) \times 5 = 45$. This gives a total area of $45$ m$^2$.

## Working backwards from perimeter

Sometimes you know a relationship instead of both side lengths. If the length is twice the width, let the width be $w$ cm and the length be $2w$ cm.

With perimeter 30 cm, add all four sides to get $w + w + 2w + 2w = 30$. This simplifies to $6w = 30$, so $w = 5$.

The width is $5$ cm and the length is $10$ cm. Once you have the dimensions, you can draw the rectangle accurately on a grid.

## Perimeter of compound shapes

![Step-shaped compound figure made from two rectangles with outside boundary highlighted and side lengths labelled 6, 6, 3, 3, 3 and 9 cm](../assets/002-466d4dc9-7d05-44db-b8a6-67dbd852fe3c.jpeg)
A compound shape is made from simpler shapes joined together. For perimeter, count only the outside boundary and ignore any edge hidden inside the shape.

For the step shape shown, the outside edges are $6$, $6$, $3$, $3$, $3$ and $9$. Adding them gives $P = 6 + 6 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 9 = 30$, so the perimeter is 30 cm.

Tracing the outline with your finger or pencil is a good checking method. Tick each outside edge once so you do not miss a side or count one twice.

## Working backwards from area

If a triangle's base is twice its height, let the height be $h$ cm and the base be $2h$ cm. Then $A = \frac{1}{2} \times 2h \times h = h^2$, which turns the formula into a simple equation in one variable.

So if the area is 25 square centimetres, then $h^2 = 25$, so $h = 5$. The base is twice the height, so the base is $10$ cm.

The same idea works for other shapes too: substitute the relationship first, then solve. Check at the end that your answer matches the shape and the units.

## Check yourself

1. Which statement is correct for a rectangle that is $7\text{ cm}$ by $4\text{ cm}$?

   - Its area is $28\text{ cm}^2$ and its perimeter is $22\text{ cm}$ ✔

   - Its area is $22\text{ cm}$ and its perimeter is $28\text{ cm}^2$

   - Its area is $28\text{ cm}$ and its perimeter is $22\text{ cm}^2$

   - Its area is $11\text{ cm}^2$ and its perimeter is $14\text{ cm}$

   _Explanation:_ A $7$ by $4$ rectangle has area $7 \times 4 = 28$ and perimeter $7+4+7+4 = 22$. So the correct statement is $28\text{ cm}^2$ for area and $22\text{ cm}$ for perimeter; common wrong B swaps the two ideas and the units. Hint: Multiply for area, add around the edge for perimeter.

2. A parallelogram has base $9\text{ cm}$, slanted side $6\text{ cm}$ and perpendicular height $4\text{ cm}$. What is its area?

   - $$36\text{ cm}^2$ ✔

   - $$18\text{ cm}^2$

   - $$54\text{ cm}^2$

   - $$13\text{ cm}^2$

   _Explanation:_ The area of a parallelogram is base times perpendicular height, so $9 \times 4 = 36$. Common wrong answer C uses the slanted side as the height, but the height must be at right angles to the base. Hint: Ignore the slanted side unless it is perpendicular.

3. Two $6$ cm by $3$ cm rectangles are joined along one $3$ cm edge. What is the perimeter of the new shape?

   - 30 cm ✔

   - 36 cm

   - 33 cm

   - 24 cm

   _Explanation:_ Each rectangle has perimeter 18 cm, so adding them first gives $18+18=36$. The shared 3 cm edge is inside the shape and was counted twice, so subtract 6 to get 30 cm; common wrong B forgets to remove the internal edge. Hint: Hidden edges do not belong to the perimeter.
