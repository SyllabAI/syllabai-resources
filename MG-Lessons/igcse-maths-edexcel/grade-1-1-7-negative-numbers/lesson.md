## Negative numbers on a number line

![Number line from negative 10 to 10 with arrows showing 4 minus 7 ending at negative 3 and negative 6 plus 8 ending at 2](../assets/001-7914d7f9-d6c9-461c-aed9-6016f0a0d5c3.jpeg)
Negative numbers are numbers less than $0$. They are used for temperatures below freezing, money owed, and floors below ground level.

On a number line, numbers get smaller as you move left and bigger as you move right. This means $-8$ is smaller than $-3$, even though $8$ is bigger than $3$.

Zero is the boundary between negative and positive numbers. A quick check is to ask which number is further right, because that number is greater.

## Adding and subtracting negative numbers

Adding a positive number moves you right on the number line, and adding a negative number moves you left. If both numbers are negative, add the sizes and keep the negative sign, so $-5 + (-7) = -12$.

Worked example: $-6 + 8$. Start at $-6$ and move $8$ places right: $-5, -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$. You land on $2$, so $-6 + 8 = 2$.

Subtracting a negative does the opposite, so it is the same as adding a positive. For example, $7 - (-8) = 7 + 8 = 15$, so the answer is bigger than $7$.

## Choosing numbers and why order matters

When you choose numbers to fill boxes, addition can be done in either order but subtraction cannot. That is why you must pay attention to which number comes first.

Worked example: use $-8, -2, 3, 8$ to make one number minus another equal $-10$. Try $-2 - 8$, which gives $-10$, so the numbers are $-2$ and $8$ in that order.

If you reverse the numbers, $8 - (-2) = 10$, not $-10$. Brackets around the negative number help you see the subtraction clearly.

## Multiplying and dividing with negative numbers

For multiplication and division, use a two-stage method. First ignore the signs and work out the size, then use the sign rule: same signs give a positive answer and different signs give a negative answer.

Example 1: $6 \times (-5)$. Ignore the signs to get $6 \times 5 = 30$, then notice the signs are different, so the answer is $-30$. Example 2: $-4 \times (-7)$ gives $4 \times 7 = 28$, and the signs are the same, so the answer is $28$.

Division works the same way, so $-36 \div 6 = -6$ because the signs are different. If you multiply several numbers, an even number of negative signs gives a positive answer and an odd number gives a negative answer.

## Sequences and temperatures

In a sequence, find the change from one term to the next and keep using it, even when you cross $0$. Negative numbers do not stop the pattern.

Example: $14, 8, 2, \ldots$ goes down by $6$ each time, so the next two terms are $-4$ and $-10$. Example: $-18, -10, -2, \ldots$ goes up by $8$ each time, so the next two terms are $6$ and $14$.

Temperature changes work like moves on a number line, and the lowest temperature is the one furthest left. From $-6$ °C to $3$ °C, go $6$ degrees up to $0$ and $3$ more, so the temperature difference is $9$ °C.

## Check yourself

1. Which statement is true?

   - $$-8 > -3$

   - $$-3 > -8$ ✔

   - $$-3 = 3$

   - $$0 < -5$

   _Explanation:_ On a number line, $-3$ is to the right of $-8$, so $-3$ is greater. The common wrong answer A comes from comparing the digits $8$ and $3$ instead of the positions of the numbers.

2. Use two of these numbers: $-8, -2, 3, 8$. Which choice makes one number minus another equal to $-10$?

   - first $-2$, second $8$ ✔

   - first $8$, second $-2$

   - first $-8$, second $3$

   - first $3$, second $-8$

   _Explanation:_ Only A gives $-2 - 8 = -10$. The common wrong answer B gives $8 - (-2) = 10$, which shows why the order matters in subtraction.

3. Stretch: A temperature starts at $-4$ °C. It rises by $7$ °C and then falls by $10$ °C. What is the final temperature?

   - $$-7$ °C ✔

   - $$3$ °C

   - $$-1$ °C

   - $$13$ °C

   _Explanation:_ First rise: $-4 + 7 = 3$. Then fall $10$: $3 - 10 = -7$, so A is correct. The common wrong answer B stops after the first change and forgets the temperature then falls by $10$ °C.
