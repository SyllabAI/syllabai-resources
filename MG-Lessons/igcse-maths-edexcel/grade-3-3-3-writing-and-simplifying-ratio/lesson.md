## What a ratio means

A ratio compares two or more quantities. If the ratio of apples to oranges is $3:5$, that means 3 parts apples for every 5 parts oranges.

Order matters. "Apples to oranges" is $3:5$, but "oranges to apples" would be $5:3$.

Ratios can compare counts, lengths, masses, money, or any other like quantities. The key is to write the quantities in the order the question asks.

## Simplifying a ratio

To simplify a ratio, divide every part by the same whole number. The best number to use is often the highest common factor, or HCF. Example: simplify $84:36$. Both numbers divide by 12, so

$$$84:36 = 7:3$$

Now 7 and 3 have no common factor bigger than 1, so $7:3$ is in simplest form. If you divide only one part, the ratio changes and the answer becomes wrong.

## Matching units first

Before writing a ratio, make sure the units match. You cannot compare $120 \, \text{cm}$ and $2 \, \text{m}$ directly because the numbers use different units. Convert $2 \, \text{m}$ to $200 \, \text{cm}$, then write and simplify the ratio:

$$$120:200 = 3:5$$

So the ratio of $120 \, \text{cm}$ to $2 \, \text{m}$ is $3:5$. If the units already match, you can write the ratio straight away.

## Ratios of values, not counts

Sometimes the question asks for the ratio of values, not just the number of objects. When dealing with coins or items of different worth, you must first calculate the total value of each group separately.

For example, Sam has four $20p$ coins and three $50p$ coins. Their total values are $80p$ and $150p$, resulting in the following ratio calculation: $$80:150 = 8:15$$

The final ratio of values is $8:15$. Note that $4:3$ would only compare the number of coins, which is a common error when the question specifically asks for the ratio of their worth.

## Turning a fraction into a ratio

![Bar model of 9 equal parts with 2 left-handed and 7 right-handed labelled](../assets/001-cbfbe961-19a7-477b-b1de-9c016af77abb.jpeg)
A fraction can turn into a ratio when one group is given as a fraction of the whole and the other group is the rest. The denominator tells you the total number of equal parts, and the numerator tells you the named group.

If $\frac{2}{9}$ of a class are left-handed, then 2 parts are left-handed out of 9 parts altogether. The remaining parts are $9 - 2 = 7$.

Write the ratio in the order asked: left-handed to right-handed, so $2:7$. If the ratio still has a common factor, simplify it.

## Special forms of ratios

Sometimes a question asks for a ratio in the form $n:1$ or $1:n$. To achieve this, divide both parts of the ratio by the value you want to become $1$.

For $10.5:3.5$, divide both numbers by $3.5$ so the second part becomes $1$:
$$10.5:3.5 = 3:1$$
The ratio remains equivalent because both parts were divided by the same number.

For $14:35$, divide both numbers by $14$ so the first part becomes $1$:
$$14:35 = 1:2.5$$
In these specific forms, a decimal value of $n$ is expected and allowed.

## Building three-part ratios

A three-part ratio compares three quantities, such as blue : red : yellow. Start with one group as 1 part, then build the other groups from the words in the question.

If red is three times blue and yellow is half of red, start with blue = 1. Then red = 3 and yellow = 1.5, so the ratio is $1:3:1.5$.

To make every part whole, multiply all parts by 2: $$1:3:1.5 = 2:6:3$$ You must scale every part by the same number.

## Check yourself

1. There are 5 red counters and 8 blue counters. What is the ratio red : blue?

   - $$5:8$ ✔

   - $$8:5$

   - $$13:8$

   - $$3:8$

   _Explanation:_ $$5:8$ is correct because red comes first and blue comes second. The common wrong answer $8:5$ reverses the order, so it answers blue : red instead.

2. A ribbon is $120 \, \text{cm}$ long and a cord is $2 \, \text{m}$ long. What is the ratio ribbon : cord in simplest form?

   - $$3:5$ ✔

   - $$120:2$

   - $$5:3$

   - $$12:20$

   _Explanation:_ Convert $2 \, \text{m}$ to $200 \, \text{cm}$ first, so the ratio is $120:200 = 3:5$. The common wrong answer $120:2$ ignores the different units and compares centimetres with metres.

3. Lena has three £2 coins and four 50p coins. What is the ratio of the value of the £2 coins to the value of the 50p coins?

   - $$3:1$ ✔

   - $$3:4$

   - $$6:2$

   - $$1:3$

   _Explanation:_ Three £2 coins are worth £6 and four 50p coins are worth £2, so the value ratio is $6:2 = 3:1$. The common wrong answer $3:4$ compares the number of coins instead of their values.

4. Write $14:35$ in the form $1:n$.

   - $$1:2.5$ ✔

   - $$2:5$

   - $$0.4:1$

   - $$1:0.4$

   _Explanation:_ To get the form $1:n$, make the first part 1 by dividing both parts by 14, giving $1:2.5$. The common wrong answer $2:5$ is simplified, but it is not in the required form because the first part is not 1.

5. There are 96 people in a hall. Half are Year 11. The number of Year 11 people is three times the number of Year 10 people. The rest are Year 9. What is Year 9 : Year 10 in the form $n:1$?

   - $$2:1$ ✔

   - $$1:2$

   - $$3:1$

   - $$4:1$

   _Explanation:_ Half of 96 is 48, so Year 11 = 48. If Year 11 is three times Year 10, then Year 10 = 16 and Year 9 = $96 - 48 - 16 = 32$, so Year 9 : Year 10 = $32:16 = 2:1$. The common wrong answer $3:1$ comes from using the "three times" fact as the final ratio without working out the missing group.
