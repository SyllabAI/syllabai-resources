## What rounding means

![Place value chart for 7518 and 6.47 beside a number line from 300 to 400 showing 376 past the halfway point 350 and rounding to 400](../assets/001-83736531-76a7-4946-bc5b-7c3e6e2fd964.jpeg)

Rounding changes a number to a nearby, simpler number. You might round to the nearest $10$, $100$, or $1000$, or to a set number of decimal places or significant figures.

Place value tells you which digit you are keeping. In $7518$, the $5$ is in the hundreds place, and in $6.47$, the $4$ is in the tenths place.

The digit immediately to the right decides the outcome. If it is $0$ to $4$, you round down; if it is $5$ to $9$, you round up.

## Rounding whole numbers

To round whole numbers, find the place value first, then check the digit immediately to its right. After you decide up or down, replace all later digits with zeros.

Example: Round 468 to the nearest hundred. Keep the 4, look at the 6, so round up to 5 hundreds.

$$468 \to 500$$

Example: Round 5274 to the nearest thousand. Keep the 5, look at the 2, so round down.

$$5274 \to 5000$$

Do not just chop digits off. Whole-number answers must keep the place value, so 7518 to the nearest hundred is 7500, not 75.

## Rounding decimals to decimal places

Decimal places count digits after the decimal point. If a question asks for $2$ decimal places, the final answer must show exactly two digits after the point.

Example: Round $23.1681$ to $2$ decimal places. Keep $23.16$, look at the next digit $8$, and round the hundredths digit up.

$$$23.1681 \to 23.17$$

Sometimes rounding causes a carry. To round $5.4096$ to $3$ decimal places, the $6$ makes the $9$ round up. The final $0$ stays because the answer must show $3$ decimal places.

$$$5.4096 \to 5.410$$

## Rounding to significant figures

Significant figures start at the first non-zero digit, not at the decimal point. Zeros before that first non-zero digit do not count.

Example: 58.165 correct to 1 significant figure keeps the 5 and looks at the next digit 8. Since 8 is 5 or more, it rounds up:
$$58.165 \to 60$$

For 0.06849 correct to 2 significant figures, ignore the zeros before the 6. Keep 6 and 8, look at the next digit 4, and round down:
$$0.06849 \to 0.068$$

Zeros can be significant if they come after the first non-zero digit. In 90437, the first two significant figures are 9 and 0, so the number rounds to:
$$90437 \to 90000$$

## Exam technique and common traps

The phrase 'correct to' simply means the same as 'rounded to'. Always underline whether the question asks for the nearest 100, 2 decimal places, or 3 significant figures.

Then circle the digit you are keeping and check the digit immediately to its right. This simple visual check stops you from rounding the wrong place value by mistake.

Finally, check the form of your answer. Whole numbers may need zeros as placeholders, like $7518$ becoming $7500$, and decimals may need trailing zeros to show precision, like $5.4096$ becoming $5.410$.

## Check yourself

1. What is $468$ rounded to the nearest hundred?

   - 400

   - 500 ✔

   - 470

   - 4680

   _Explanation:_ $$468$ has $4$ hundreds and the next digit is $6$, so it rounds up to $5$ hundreds, which is $500$. The common wrong answer $400$ happens when you look only at the hundreds digit and forget that the tens digit decides. Hint: Check the tens digit.

2. Which answer is $5.4096$ correct to 3 decimal places?

   - $$5.409$

   - $$5.41$

   - $$5.410$ ✔

   - $$5.400$

   _Explanation:_ The third decimal place is $9$ and the next digit is $6$, so the $9$ rounds up and causes a carry, giving $5.410$. The common wrong answer $5.41$ has the right value but it is not written to 3 decimal places because it only shows 2 digits after the decimal point. Hint: Check both the rounding and the number of digits after the decimal point.

3. What is $0.06849$ correct to $2$ decimal places?

   - $$0.07$ ✔

   - $$0.068$

   - $$0.06$

   - $$0.069$

   _Explanation:_ Two decimal places means keep the tenths and hundredths digits, so $0.06849$ becomes $0.07$ because the third decimal digit is $8$. The common wrong answer $0.068$ is $2$ significant figures, not $2$ decimal places. Hint: Decimal places are counted from the decimal point.

4. What is $9.995$ correct to $3$ significant figures?

   - $$9.99$

   - $$10$

   - $$10.0$ ✔

   - $$10.00$

   _Explanation:_ The first three significant figures are $9$, $9$, and $9$, and the next digit is $5$, so rounding causes a carry to $10.0$. The common wrong answer $10$ does not show $3$ significant figures, and $10.00$ shows $4$ significant figures. Note: Always count significant figures starting from the first non-zero digit.
