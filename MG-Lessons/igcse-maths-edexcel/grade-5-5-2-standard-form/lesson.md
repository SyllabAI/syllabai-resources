## What standard form means

![Decimal point movement diagram showing 6.38 moving 3 places right to make 6380 and 2 places left to make 0.0638, with the rule a x 10^n where 1 <= a < 10](../assets/001-ed81fa29-549c-4cb0-b644-ed32efb1f0c4.jpeg)

Standard form is built from powers of 10. Multiplying by $10^n$ moves the decimal point right if $n$ is positive and left if $n$ is negative.

A number is in standard form when it is written as $a \times 10^n$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $n$ is an integer. The number $a$ is the front number, so it must be at least 1 but less than 10.

This makes very large and very small numbers shorter to write and easier to compare. For example, $4.8 \times 10^6$ is valid, but $12.5 \times 10^3$ and $0.72 \times 10^5$ are not in standard form.

## From standard form to ordinary numbers

To change from standard form to an ordinary number, move the decimal point by the size of the exponent. For $8.52 \times 10^5$, move 5 places right:

$$$8.52 \times 10^5 = 852000$$

If the exponent is negative, move the decimal point left instead. For $3.07 \times 10^{-4}$, move 4 places left:

$$$3.07 \times 10^{-4} = 0.000307$$

A negative exponent does not mean the answer is negative. It usually means the answer is between 0 and 1, and you may need zero placeholders.

## From ordinary numbers to standard form

To write an ordinary number in standard form, place the decimal point after the first non-zero digit. Then count how many places the decimal point moved and use a positive exponent for large numbers and a negative exponent for small decimals.

For $64\,200\,000$, place the decimal point to make $6.42$. The decimal point moved 7 places left, so:

$$$64\,200\,000 = 6.42 \times 10^7$$

For $0.0000739$, place the decimal point to make $7.39$. The decimal point moved 5 places right, so the exponent is negative:

$$$0.0000739 = 7.39 \times 10^{-5}$$

## Multiplying and dividing

When multiplying or dividing in standard form, work with the front numbers and the powers of 10 separately. Use the index laws $10^m \times 10^n = 10^{m+n}$ and $10^m \div 10^n = 10^{m-n}$ to handle the exponents.

For multiplication:
$$
(4.2 \times 10^5)(3.5 \times 10^{-2}) = (4.2 \times 3.5)(10^5 \times 10^{-2}) = 14.7 \times 10^3
$$
This is not yet in standard form because the front number is greater than 10, so rewrite it as $1.47 \times 10^4$.

For division:
$$
(9.6 \times 10^{-4}) \div (3.2 \times 10^{-6}) = (9.6 \div 3.2)(10^{-4} \div 10^{-6}) = 3 \times 10^2
$$
Be careful with negative exponents during division, because subtracting a negative yields a positive result: $-4 - (-6) = 2$.

Always check the final front number. If it is not between 1 and 10, you must adjust the front number and change the exponent accordingly to keep the value the same.

## Adding, subtracting and comparing

For ordering, it is often easiest to convert all the numbers to ordinary numbers or make sure they are all in proper standard form. For adding and subtracting, do not add or subtract the exponents directly.

To calculate $8.4 \times 10^5 - 6.7 \times 10^4$, first rewrite the second number with the same power of 10: $$6.7 \times 10^4 = 0.67 \times 10^5$$ Then subtract the front numbers: $$8.4 \times 10^5 - 0.67 \times 10^5 = 7.73 \times 10^5$$

For example, $5.4 \times 10^2 = 540$ but $5400 \times 10^{-4} = 0.54$. That shows why converting can make the size of each number much clearer.

## Using standard form in harder calculations

If a calculation contains awkward decimals, first convert each number into standard form. This approach makes the place value significantly easier to track and reduces the likelihood of errors.

Consider the following expression: 
$$\frac{0.04 \times 0.03}{0.005}$$
To simplify this, we first write each decimal in standard form: $4 \times 10^{-2}$, $3 \times 10^{-2}$, and $5 \times 10^{-3}$.

Next, substitute these values back into the fraction. This allows you to work with the coefficients and the powers of ten separately:
$$\frac{(4 \times 10^{-2})(3 \times 10^{-2})}{5 \times 10^{-3}}$$

Calculating the constants gives $\frac{4 \times 3}{5} = 2.4$, while the powers of ten give $10^{-2} \times 10^{-2} \div 10^{-3} = 10^{-1}$. Combining these parts, the final result is:
$$2.4 \times 10^{-1}$$

## Check yourself

1. Which number is already in standard form?

   - $$7.41 \times 10^{-3}$ ✔

   - $$12.5 \times 10^3$

   - $$0.72 \times 10^5$

   - $$7.41 \times 10^{3.5}$

   _Explanation:_ $$7.41 \times 10^{-3}$ is correct because the front number 7.41 is between 1 and 10 and the exponent is an integer. A common mistake is choosing $12.5 \times 10^3$, but 12.5 is too large, so it has not been fully written in standard form.

2. Which is the correct standard form of $0.00048$?

   - $$4.8 \times 10^{-4}$ ✔

   - $$48 \times 10^{-5}$

   - $$4.8 \times 10^4$

   - $$0.48 \times 10^{-3}$

   _Explanation:_ $$0.00048 = 4.8 \times 10^{-4}$ because the decimal point moves 4 places right to make 4.8, so the exponent is negative 4. A common mistake is $0.48 \times 10^{-3}$, which has the right value but is still not in standard form because the front number is less than 1.

3. Find $8.4 \times 10^5 - 6.7 \times 10^4$ in standard form.

   - $$7.73 \times 10^5$ ✔

   - $$1.7 \times 10^1$

   - $$7.73 \times 10^4$

   - $$0.773 \times 10^6$

   _Explanation:_ First rewrite $6.7 \times 10^4$ as $0.67 \times 10^5$, then subtract to get $7.73 \times 10^5$. A common mistake is $1.7 \times 10^1$, which comes from subtracting the exponents directly, but that rule is only for division, not subtraction. Hint: Make the powers of 10 match before subtracting.

4. A sample contains $4.5 \times 10^8$ bacteria. Each bacterium has mass $3.2 \times 10^{-12}$ g. What is the total mass of the sample in standard form?

   - $$1.44 \times 10^{-3}$ g ✔

   - $$14.4 \times 10^{-4}$ g

   - $$1.44 \times 10^{20}$ g

   - $$7.7 \times 10^{-4}$ g

   _Explanation:_ Multiply the front numbers to get 14.4 and add the exponents to get $10^{-4}$, giving $14.4 \times 10^{-4}$. The correct standard form is $1.44 \times 10^{-3}$, and a common mistake is to stop at $14.4 \times 10^{-4}$ without rewriting the front number so it lies between 1 and 10.
