## What percentage change measures

![Flow diagram showing original amount, change, new amount, and the percentage change formula with the original amount highlighted in the denominator](../assets/001-62c028b0-7b5a-4d01-9647-85ef641219bb.jpeg)

Percentage change tells you how big a change is compared with what you started with. That is why a £20 change can be tiny in one question but huge in another.

First find the actual change. For an increase, do new minus original; for a decrease, do original minus new.

$$$\text{percentage change}=\frac{\text{change}}{\text{original amount}}\times 100$$

The denominator is always the original amount, because that is the value before the change happened.

## Worked example: percentage increase

A council tax bill was £1840 last year and £1935 this year. Because the new amount is larger, this is a percentage increase.

First find the increase:
$$1935 - 1840 = 95$$

Now compare that increase with the original bill:
$$\frac{95}{1840} \times 100 = 5.163043478\ldots$$

Round only at the final step. To 1 decimal place, $5.163043478\ldots \approx 5.2$, so the percentage increase is **5.2%**.

## Worked example: percentage decrease

Sometimes a question only says "percentage change". Start by comparing the two values, then decide whether the change is an increase or a decrease.

Example: the average price of a flat changes from $£256800$ to $£249900$. The new value is smaller, so first find the decrease:
$$256800 - 249900 = 6900$$

Now divide by the original price, not the new price.
$$\frac{6900}{256800} \times 100 = 2.686915887\dots \approx 2.7$$
So the percentage change is **2.7% decrease**.

## Profit and loss use the same formula

In selling questions, the original amount is the cost price because that is what you paid at the start. Profit means the selling price is bigger, and loss means it is smaller.

Example: a shopkeeper buys 12 bottles of juice for $£4.80$ and sells each bottle for $60\text{p}$. Keep the money in the same units, then find the total selling price. $$60\text{ p}=0.60,\qquad 12\times 0.60=7.20$$

Now find the profit and turn it into a percentage of the cost price. $$7.20-4.80=2.40,\qquad \frac{2.40}{4.80}\times 100=50$$ The percentage profit is **50%**.

## Multi-step example with units

Sometimes you must convert units before you can work out the selling price. Keep mass in one unit and money in one unit before doing percentage calculations.

Example: 1.5 kg of sweets costs £3.60, and each bag holds 125 g. Convert kilograms to grams, then find how many bags can be made.

$$1.5\times 1000=1500 \text{ g},\qquad 1500\div 125=12$$

Each bag sells for 40p, so convert to pounds and find the total sales.

$$40\text{ p}=0.40,\qquad 12\times 0.40=4.80$$

Now find the profit and percentage profit.

$$4.80-3.60=1.20,\qquad \frac{1.20}{3.60}\times 100=33.333\ldots \approx 33.3$$

So the percentage profit is **33.3%**.

## Check yourself

1. A phone price goes from £$500$ to £$560$. Which number should be on the bottom of the fraction when finding the percentage increase?

   - 500 ✔

   - 60

   - 560

   - 1060

   _Explanation:_ The denominator is $500$ because percentage change is always measured against the original amount. The common wrong answer $560$ is the new amount, but that compares the change with what it ended at instead of what it started at. Hint: Ask yourself, 'What was the price before it changed?'

2. A driver's insurance falls from £420 to £386. Which calculation gives the percentage decrease?

   - $$\frac{34}{420}\times 100$ ✔

   - $$\frac{34}{386}\times 100$

   - $$\frac{386}{420}\times 100$

   - $$\frac{420}{34}\times 100$

   _Explanation:_ The decrease is $420-386=34$, and percentage decrease is change divided by original amount, so $\frac{34}{420}\times 100$ is correct. The common wrong answer $\frac{34}{386}\times 100$ is wrong because 386 is the new amount, not the starting value.

3. A trader buys 40 cans for £15. He sells 28 cans at 55p each and the rest at 25p each. What is the percentage profit to 3 significant figures?

   - 18.4%

   - 22.7% ✔

   - 3.40%

   - 81.5%

   _Explanation:_ First find the total selling price: $28\times 0.55 + 12\times 0.25 = 18.40$. Then profit $=18.40-15.00=3.40$, so percentage profit $=\frac{3.40}{15.00}\times 100=22.666\ldots \approx 22.7$. The common wrong answer 18.4% is wrong because £18.40 is the total money taken, not the percentage profit.
