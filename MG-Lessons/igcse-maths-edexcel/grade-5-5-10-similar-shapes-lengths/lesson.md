## Same shape, different size

Similar shapes have equal matching angles and their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. Corresponding sides are sides in matching positions, found from the angle information or the order of the letters.

The key idea is that one multiplier works for every length. If the scale factor from the small shape to the large shape is $k$, then every matching side is multiplied by $k$.

Useful relationships are $\text{scale factor} = \frac{\text{large side}}{\text{small side}}$ and $\frac{\text{small side}}{\text{large side}} = \frac{1}{\text{scale factor}}$. Multiply to go to the larger shape, and divide to go back to the smaller shape.

## Worked example: matching named triangles

![Side-by-side similar triangles with matching angles and corresponding sides labelled AB 10 cm, AC 18 cm, PQ 15 cm, and QR 24 cm](../assets/001-6c45a571-8a27-4161-87db-cefab5350d85.jpeg)
When triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $PQR$, the letter order tells us $A \leftrightarrow P$, $B \leftrightarrow Q$, and $C \leftrightarrow R$. This means the corresponding sides are $AB \leftrightarrow PQ$, $AC \leftrightarrow PR$, and $BC \leftrightarrow QR$.

Use one reliable pair to find the scale factor from triangle $ABC$ to triangle $PQR$. In this case:
$$\frac{PQ}{AB} = \frac{15}{10} = 1.5$$
Since $AC \leftrightarrow PR$, we can determine the length of $PR$ by calculating $18 \times 1.5 = 27$ cm.

Now work back from the larger triangle to the smaller triangle for $BC$. Since $BC \leftrightarrow QR$, we calculate $BC = 24 \div 1.5 = 16$ cm. A common mistake is to match sides by eyesight instead of using the formal letter order provided in the similarity statement.

## Parallel lines inside a triangle

![Triangle ACD with points B and E on two sides, BE parallel to CD, and lengths AB 10 cm, BC 5 cm, AE 8 cm, CD 12 cm](../assets/002-a6995112-26d4-455b-b725-dab961eaab29.jpeg)
If a line inside a triangle is parallel to one side, the small triangle and the large triangle are similar because matching angles are equal. In triangle $ABE$ inside triangle $ACD$, with $BE \parallel CD$, the matching sides are $AB \leftrightarrow AC$, $AE \leftrightarrow AD$, and $BE \leftrightarrow CD$.

Keep the ratios in the same order all the way through. Here $\frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{AE}{AD} = \frac{BE}{CD}$, and first we need the whole side of the large triangle: $AC = 10 + 5 = 15$ cm.

The scale factor from the small triangle to the large triangle is $\frac{AC}{AB} = \frac{15}{10} = 1.5$. So $AD = 8 \times 1.5 = 12$ cm, then $ED = 12 - 8 = 4$ cm, and $BE = 12 \div 1.5 = 8$ cm.

## Bow-tie and back-to-back triangles

Bow-tie diagrams often have two triangles on opposite sides of a crossing point. Vertically opposite angles are equal, and parallel lines create equal corresponding angles, so the triangles are similar.

If $AB \parallel XY$, lines $AY$ and $BX$ meet at $P$, and $AB = 7$ cm, $XY = 21$ cm, $XP = 18$ cm, then triangle $ABP$ is similar to triangle $YXP$. The matching order is $A \leftrightarrow Y$, $B \leftrightarrow X$, $P \leftrightarrow P$, so $BP \leftrightarrow XP$.

The scale factor from triangle $ABP$ to triangle $YXP$ is $\frac{YX}{AB} = \frac{21}{7} = 3$, so $BP = 18 \div 3 = 6$ cm. The same idea works for back-to-back triangles: if $AB \parallel DE$, then triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $EDC$.

## Using a given ratio

Sometimes the question gives a ratio instead of two actual matching lengths. If $AB:AC = 2:5$, then the scale factor from $AB$ to $AC$ is $\frac{5}{2} = 2.5$.

Use the actual lengths after converting the ratio. If $AB = 6$ cm and $BE$ corresponds to $CD$ with $BE = 4$ cm, then $AC = 6 \times 2.5 = 15$ cm and $CD = 4 \times 2.5 = 10$ cm.

If the diagram asks for an extra section, subtract carefully. For example, if $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$, then $BC = 15 - 6 = 9$ cm, and the ratio itself is not a length.

## Showing rectangles are not similar

![Two rectangles labelled 140 mm by 75 mm and 180 mm by 90 mm with arrows comparing longer and shorter sides](../assets/003-a0bdc7ae-d9c7-4e3c-afdd-50fd7081c628.jpeg)

All rectangles have four right angles, so angle matching alone does not prove similarity. Their length scale factor and width scale factor must also be the same.

Compare the longer sides and shorter sides separately. For rectangles $140$ mm by $75$ mm and $180$ mm by $90$ mm, the long-side factor is $\frac{180}{140} = \frac{9}{7} \approx 1.29$ but the short-side factor is $\frac{90}{75} = 1.2$.

Because these scale factors are different, the rectangles are not mathematically similar. A common mistake is to think all rectangles are similar because they all have right angles.

## Check yourself

1. Triangles are similar. A side of $8$ cm in the smaller triangle corresponds to $12$ cm in the larger triangle. What is the scale factor from small to large?

   - $$1.5$ ✔

   - $$\frac{2}{3}$

   - $$4$

   - $$20$

   _Explanation:_ The scale factor from small to large is $\frac{12}{8} = 1.5$. Option B, $\frac{2}{3}$, is the scale factor in the opposite direction, from large to small.

2. In triangle $ACD$, $BE \parallel CD$. If $AB = 6$ cm, $AC = 9$ cm, and $CD = 15$ cm, what is $BE$?

   - $$10$ cm ✔

   - $$22.5$ cm

   - $$6$ cm

   - $$2.5$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Because $BE \parallel CD$, the triangles are similar and $\frac{BE}{CD} = \frac{AB}{AC} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$, so $BE = 15 \times \frac{2}{3} = 10$ cm. Option B, $22.5$ cm, comes from using the ratio the wrong way round and multiplying instead of dividing.

3. In similar triangles, $AB:AC = 2:5$ and $AB = 8$ cm. If $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$, what is $BC$?

   - $$12$ cm ✔

   - $$13$ cm

   - $$5$ cm

   - $$20$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The ratio gives scale factor $\frac{AC}{AB} = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5$, so $AC = 8 \times 2.5 = 20$ cm. Then $BC = 20 - 8 = 12$ cm, while option D, $20$ cm, is the full side $AC$, not the missing section $BC$.

4. Stretch: Which pair of rectangles is mathematically similar?

   - $$6$ cm by $4$ cm and $15$ cm by $10$ cm ✔

   - $$6$ cm by $4$ cm and $12$ cm by $9$ cm

   - $$8$ cm by $5$ cm and $12$ cm by $7$ cm

   - $$10$ cm by $6$ cm and $15$ cm by $8$ cm

   _Explanation:_ In option A, both dimensions scale by $2.5$, so the rectangles are similar. Option B is a common trap: $6 \to 12$ has a scale factor of $2$, but $4 \to 9$ has a scale factor of $2.25$, meaning the rectangles are not similar. To be similar, the ratio of corresponding sides must be equal.
