## Hours, minutes, and duration

Most time questions at this level use one key fact: $1 \text{ hour} = 60 \text{ minutes}$. To change hours to minutes, multiply by 60.

To change minutes to hours, divide by 60. A duration is a length of time, such as $45 \text{ minutes}$ or $2 \text{ hours } 30 \text{ minutes}$. When comparing or adding durations, it is often easiest to change everything into minutes first.

Useful mental facts are half an hour = $30 \text{ minutes}$, quarter of an hour = $15 \text{ minutes}$, and three quarters of an hour = $45 \text{ minutes}$. These help you estimate answers quickly and spot mistakes.

## Mixed times and fractions of an hour

Mixed times such as 3 hours 15 minutes are easiest if you convert the hours part first. Then add the extra minutes.

For example, 3 hours 15 minutes becomes $3 \times 60 = 180$ minutes, then $180 + 15 = 195$ minutes. So 3 hours 15 minutes is 195 minutes.

Fractions of an hour also use 60 minutes. So $1\frac{3}{4}$ hours is $60 + 45 = 105$ minutes because $\frac{3}{4}$ of 60 is 45.

## Finding the difference between times

The difference between two durations tells you how far apart they are. To find it safely, convert both to minutes first and then subtract the smaller number from the larger number.

Example: 2 hours 10 minutes is $2 \times 60 + 10 = 130$ minutes. Also, $1\frac{3}{4}$ hours is $60 + 45 = 105$ minutes.

Now compare the totals: $130 - 105 = 25$. The difference is 25 minutes.

## Adding time by splitting into easy parts

![Timeline from 7:45 pm to 9:23 pm showing jumps of 15 minutes, 60 minutes, and 23 minutes](../assets/001-3c173a0d-9347-4531-a1a4-510c471889a5.jpeg)
When a duration crosses an hour, split it into easy jumps. First go to the next o'clock time, then add the rest.

Example: 98 minutes can be split as $15 + 60 + 23$. So 7:45 pm becomes 8:00 pm, then 9:00 pm, then 9:23 pm.

This method is safer than treating clock times like ordinary decimals. For example, 7:50 pm plus 35 minutes is 8:25 pm, not 7.85 pm.

## 24-hour clock and timetables

Timetables often use the 24-hour clock to specify times clearly. For example, 0715 means 7:15 am and 1632 means 4:32 pm.

In a travel timetable, one vertical column usually follows one bus or train from stop to stop. A dash means that service does not stop at that specific location.

Example: a train leaves at 06:20 and arrives at 09:05. Count up to 09:20 to get 3 hours, then subtract 15 minutes, so the journey is 2 hours 45 minutes. In minutes, that is $2 \times 60 + 45 = 165$.

## Working backwards from a deadline

Sometimes you know the deadline and need the latest time to leave. In that case, work backwards by subtracting each walking or waiting time in reverse order.

Example: Ben must be at work by 9:00 am, and the walk from the bus stop to work takes 10 minutes. So he needs a bus that arrives by 8:50 am; from 08:35, 08:47, 08:56, and 09:04, the latest suitable arrival is 08:47.

If that bus leaves Ben's starting stop at 08:12 and he needs 12 minutes to walk there, subtract 12 minutes again. Ben must leave home at 8:00 am. Writing the full time chain helps you check every stage.

## Check yourself

1. How many minutes are in $2\frac{1}{2}$ hours?

   - 120

   - 150 ✔

   - 180

   - 90

   _Explanation:_ B is correct because 2 hours is 120 minutes and half an hour is 30 minutes, giving 150 minutes altogether. A is the most common wrong answer because it forgets to include the extra half hour.

2. What is the difference, in minutes, between 50 minutes and $1\frac{1}{2}$ hours?

   - 20

   - 40 ✔

   - 90

   - 140

   _Explanation:_ B is correct because $1\frac{1}{2}$ hours is 90 minutes and $90 - 50 = 40$. C is a common mistake because it is the converted value of $1\frac{1}{2}$ hours, not the difference.

3. A traveller must arrive by 10:30 am. Which is the latest possible arrival time they can use?

   - 09:50

   - 10:18 ✔

   - 10:34

   - 11:05

   _Explanation:_ B is correct because it is the latest time that is still at or before 10:30 am. C is the most common wrong answer because it is later, but it misses the deadline by 4 minutes. Remember: The answer must be on time, not just the latest overall.
