## Area, formulae, and the correct height

The key formula for this topic is the trapezium area formula:
$$A_{\text{trapezium}} = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$$
Also remember $A_{\text{rectangle}} = bh$ and $A_{\text{triangle}} = \frac{1}{2}bh$ when you split a compound shape into simpler parts.

In a trapezium, $a$ and $b$ are the parallel sides. The height $h$ must be perpendicular to them, so do not use a sloping side unless it makes a right angle.

![Rectangle, triangle, and trapezium with bases, parallel sides, and perpendicular heights labelled](../assets/001-e41ee303-7821-4048-887a-78c25a35e48d.jpeg)
Area is the flat space inside a 2D shape, measured in square units such as square centimetres or square metres.

## Two main strategies

![L-shaped compound rectilinear shape split into two rectangles with a dashed line and missing width 7 cm labelled](../assets/002-3d5ef90d-5812-4422-8439-478844475251.jpeg)

Most compound shapes can be handled in one of two ways: split the shape into simple parts and add their areas, or find a large outer shape and subtract the missing part.

Right-angled compound shapes often split neatly into rectangles. If a length is missing, work it out before finding any area. In the L-shape shown, the missing width is $13 - 6 = 7$ cm.

Never measure a diagram that is not accurately drawn. Use the labelled lengths only.

## Worked example: L-shape made from two rectangles

Split the L-shape into a left rectangle and a right rectangle. The missing width is $13 - 6 = 7$ cm, so the two rectangles are $6 \times 8$ and $7 \times 5$.

Find each area separately. $$6 \times 8 = 48, \qquad 7 \times 5 = 35$$

Both answers are in square centimetres. Add the two areas to get the total.

$$$48 + 35 = 83$$ So the area of the compound shape is $83$ square centimetres.

## Worked example: rectangle plus triangle

A compound shape can mix different simple shapes, such as a rectangle and a triangle. Find each area separately, using the perpendicular height for the triangle.

For a rectangle $3$ cm by $10$ cm, the area is $$3 \times 10 = 30$$

For a right-angled triangle with base $6$ cm and height $4$ cm, the area is $$\frac{1}{2}\times 6 \times 4 = 12$$ This gives the two parts of the compound shape.

Now add the two parts: $$30 + 12 = 42$$ So the total area is $42$ square centimetres.

## Subtracting unshaded parts

Sometimes the easiest method is whole shape minus hole. This is common in walls with doors, windows, or inner cut-outs.

For a wall $5$ m by $3$ m, the area is
$$5 \times 3 = 15$$
For a door $1$ m by $2$ m, the area is
$$1 \times 2 = 2$$
These are the outer area and the cut-out area.

Subtract the part you do not want.
$$15 - 2 = 13$$
So the painted area is $13$ square metres. If there are several identical cut-outs, find their total area before subtracting.

## Stretch: area then money

In real-life problems, area can be just the first step. Check that the price is given per square metre before you multiply. A field is made from a rectangle $70$ m by $90$ m and a right-angled triangle with base $80$ m and height $65$ m.

$$$70\times90 = 6300,\qquad \frac{1}{2}\times80\times65 = 2600$$

Now add the areas together and then apply the price to find the total value. Calculating the total sum first allows you to determine the final valuation accurately.

$$$6300 + 2600 = 8900$$ $$8900\times4 = 35600$$ So the field sells for £35,600.

## Check yourself

1. In a trapezium, which measurement should be used as the height in $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$?

   - The longest side

   - The sloping side

   - The perpendicular distance between the parallel sides ✔

   - The sum of the parallel sides

   _Explanation:_ The height is the perpendicular distance between the two parallel sides, so that value is $h$. A common mistake is choosing the sloping side, but it is not the height unless it is at $90^\circ$ to the base.

2. Which calculation correctly finds the area of the L-shape with measurements 13 cm, 8 cm, 6 cm, and 5 cm?

   - $$6\times8 + 7\times5$ ✔

   - $$13\times8$

   - $$13\times5 + 6\times8$

   - $$6\times5 + 7\times8$

   _Explanation:_ Splitting the shape gives two rectangles, so you add $6\times8$ and $7\times5$. The common wrong answer $13\times8$ finds the area of the outside rectangle and wrongly includes the missing corner that is not part of the shape.

3. A large rectangle measures 11 cm by 8 cm. Inside it are three identical unshaded rectangles, each 3 cm by 2 cm. What is the shaded area?

   - 70 square centimetres ✔

   - 82 square centimetres

   - 66 square centimetres

   - 106 square centimetres

   _Explanation:_ The large rectangle has area $11\times8 = 88$. Each small rectangle has area $3\times2 = 6$, so three of them have total area $18$, and $88 - 18 = 70$. The common wrong answer 82 comes from subtracting only one cut-out instead of all three.

4. A field is made from a rectangle $70\text{ m}$ by $90\text{ m}$ and a right-angled triangle with base $80\text{ m}$ and height $65\text{ m}$. It is sold for $£4$ per square metre. How much money is received?

   - £8,900

   - £17,800

   - £35,600 ✔

   - £37,400

   _Explanation:_ The rectangle area is $6300$, the triangle area is $2600$, so the total area is $8900$ square metres. Multiplying by $£4$ per square metre gives $£35,600$. The common wrong answer $£8,900$ comes from finding the area but forgetting the cost step.
