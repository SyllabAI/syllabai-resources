## Key ideas

![Area model of one rectangle with height k and widths a and b split into areas ka and kb to show k(a + b) = ka + kb](../assets/001-2d069416-7dce-4fb8-a9a2-e11bf489e125.jpeg)

Expanding removes brackets by multiplying the outside factor by every term inside. Factorising is the reverse process, where you rewrite a sum as a product with brackets.

In algebra, a variable is a letter that represents a number, and a term is one part of an expression. A coefficient is the number multiplying the variable, and like terms have exactly the same variable part, so $6x$ and $2x$ can combine but $6x$ and $2y$ cannot.

Be careful with powers. $x$ and $x^2$ are not like terms, so they cannot be collected together.

## Expanding one bracket

To expand a single bracket, multiply the outside term by every term inside the bracket. The distributive law says $k(a + b) = ka + kb$ and $k(a - b) = ka - kb$.

Start with a numerical example.
$$8(4s - 3) = 8 \times 4s + 8 \times (-3) = 32s - 24$$
The 8 affects both terms, not just the first one.

The same rule works when the outside factor contains a variable.
$$5x(3x - 2) = 5x \times 3x + 5x \times (-2) = 15x^2 - 10x$$
A common mistake is to multiply only the first term, which leaves the answer incomplete.

## Expanding and simplifying

When there are two brackets, expand each one first and then collect like terms. If there is a minus sign before a bracket, treat it as multiplying by a negative number.

For $4(3y - 5) - 2(5y + 1)$, expand both parts: $$4(3y - 5) = 12y - 20,\qquad -2(5y + 1) = -10y - 2$$ The $+1$ became $-2$ because it was multiplied by $-2$.

Now collect like terms: $$12y - 20 - 10y - 2 = 2y - 22$$ If you get $2y - 19$, you have probably forgotten to change the sign of the final 1.

## Factorising fully

Factorising is the reverse of expanding. To factorise fully, find the highest common factor of all the terms and place it outside a bracket.

For $20y + 35$, the HCF is 5.
$$20y + 35 = 5(4y + 7)$$
Each term was divided by 5 to make the bracket.

Variables can also be part of the HCF. In $12m^2n + 18mn^2$, the numerical HCF is 6 and the common variable part is $mn$.
$$12m^2n \div 6mn = 2m,\qquad 18mn^2 \div 6mn = 3n$$
So the fully factorised form is $6mn(2m + 3n)$, which you can check by expanding back.

## Solving equations with brackets

When solving an equation with a bracket, it is often quickest to undo the outside multiplication first. In $5(b + 4) = 45$, divide both sides by 5 to get $b + 4 = 9$. Now undo the addition by subtracting 4 from both sides.

$$$b = 5$$

A quick check gives $5(5 + 4) = 45$, so the solution works. Decimal answers are fine too. For $4(c - 3) = 18$, divide first to get $c - 3 = 4.5$, then add 3 to get $c = 7.5$.

## Check yourself

1. Which pair are like terms?

   - $$6x$ and $2x$ ✔

   - $$6x$ and $2y$

   - $$x$ and $x^2$

   - $$3a$ and $3a^2$

   _Explanation:_ $$6x$ and $2x$ are like terms because the variable part is exactly $x$ in both terms, so they can be combined to make $8x$. A very common wrong choice is C, but $x$ and $x^2$ are different because the powers do not match.

2. Expand and simplify $4(3y - 5) - 2(5y + 1)$.

   - $$2y - 22$ ✔

   - $$2y - 19$

   - $$22y - 22$

   - $$2y + 22$

   _Explanation:_ The correct expansion is $12y - 20 - 10y - 2$, which simplifies to $2y - 22$. A very common wrong answer is B, which happens when you change $5y$ to $-10y$ but forget that the $+1$ must also become $-2$.

3. Solve $5(b + 4) = 45$.

   - $$b = 9$

   - $$b = 5$ ✔

   - $$b = 41$

   - $$b = 1$

   _Explanation:_ Divide both sides by 5 first to get $b + 4 = 9$, then subtract 4 to get $b = 5$. A common wrong answer is A because 9 is only the value of the bracket, not the final value of $b$.
