## What is a sequence?

A sequence is an ordered list of terms. The position matters: in $4, 7, 10, 13$, the first term is 4 and the fourth term is 13.

If the same amount is added or subtracted each time, the sequence is arithmetic. That fixed amount is called the common difference.

## Term-to-term rule and $n$th term

A term-to-term rule tells you how to get from one term to the next. In $6, 10, 14, 18$, the rule is add 4, so the next two terms are 22 and 26.

An $n$th term is different because it lets you jump straight to any position. Here $n$ means the term number, so you can substitute $n = 1, 2, 10$ and so on.

## Using a given $n$th term

If the $n$th term is $3n + 4$, substitute the position into the formula to find specific values. For $n = 1$ and $n = 2$, the first two terms are $3(1) + 4 = 7$ and $3(2) + 4 = 10$.

To check whether 31 is in the sequence, set the formula equal to 31 and solve for $n$. Since $3n + 4 = 31$ gives $n = 9$, 31 is confirmed as the ninth term in the sequence.

## Finding the $n$th term of an arithmetic sequence

![Table showing positions 1 to 4 matched with terms 8, 13, 18, 23, arrows labelled +5, and a comparison with 5n values 5, 10, 15, 20 plus an added 3 to reach the sequence](../assets/001-2f159df1-81d0-4ce3-9aaa-13afc5fc00d0.jpeg)

For $8, 13, 18, 23$, the gaps are all 5, so this is an arithmetic sequence. That means the $n$th term starts with $5n$.

When $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$, the expression $5n$ gives 5, 10, 15, 20. These are each 3 less than the actual terms 8, 13, 18, 23.

So add 3 to fix every term. The $n$th term is $5n + 3$, and a quick check with $n = 1$ gives $5(1) + 3 = 8$.

## Pattern sequences

![Tile patterns 1, 2, and 3 showing a fixed block of 3 orange tiles and a blue growing part that adds 2 tiles each time, with totals 5, 7, and 9 and the rule 2n + 3](../assets/002-3b0f8182-e753-49cc-bc6a-c3d470dd7555.jpeg)
Pattern sequences work the same way as number sequences, except the pattern number is the value of $n$. A good method for solving these is to separate a growing part from a fixed part.

In this example, the blue tiles grow by 2 each time, so the growing part is $2n$. The orange tiles stay at a constant 3, so the expression for the total number of tiles is $2n + 3$.

## Stretch: first negative term

Once you have an $n$th term, you can find later or negative terms without listing everything. For the sequence $30, 26, 22, 18, 14$, the common difference is $-4$, so the formula is $34 - 4n$.

To find the first negative term, solve the inequality $34 - 4n < 0$. This calculation gives $n > 8.5$, which means the first whole-number position is $n = 9$.

Finally, substitute the position into the formula to find the value: $34 - 4(9) = -2$. This method is much more efficient than continuing the list manually.

## Check yourself

1. In the sequence $4, 7, 10, 13$, what is the common difference?

   - $$+3$ ✔

   - $$+4$

   - $$+7$

   - $$-3$

   _Explanation:_ The correct answer is $+3$ because each term increases by 3: $7 - 4 = 3$ and $10 - 7 = 3$. The most common wrong answer is $+4$, which is the first term, not the gap between terms.

2. The $n$th term of a sequence is $3n + 4$. What is the 5th term?

   - 15

   - 19 ✔

   - 20

   - 12

   _Explanation:_ Substitute $n = 5$ into the formula: $3(5) + 4 = 19$. The most common wrong answer is 15, which comes from doing $3 \times 5$ and forgetting to add 4.

3. A tile pattern has $n$th term $2n + 3$. How many tiles are in pattern 6?

   - 12

   - 15 ✔

   - 18

   - 9

   _Explanation:_ Substitute $n = 6$: $2(6) + 3 = 15$. The most common wrong answer is 12, which comes from using only the growing part and forgetting the fixed +3.
