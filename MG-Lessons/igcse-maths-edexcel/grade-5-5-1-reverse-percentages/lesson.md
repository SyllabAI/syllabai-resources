## Working backwards with percentages

![Bar models comparing original 100 percent with final 106 percent after an increase, and original 100 percent with sale price 80 percent after a 20 percent discount](../assets/001-5ddabf37-00dc-4e72-b15f-799948b95aa2.jpeg)

A reverse percentage question asks you to find the original amount before a percentage change happened. The original amount is always $100\%$, and the final amount is after the increase or decrease.

If something increases by $p\%$, the final amount is $100\% + p\%$ of the original, so the multiplier is $1 + \frac{p}{100}$. If something decreases by $p\%$, the final amount is $100\% - p\%$ of the original, so a $20\%$ discount uses a multiplier of $0.8$.

## The key reverse percentage formula

In a forward percentage question, you multiply the original amount by the multiplier to get the final amount. In a reverse percentage question, you do the opposite and divide by the multiplier instead.

The algebra is shown below, and it explains why division is the key step. This is the method to use whenever the final amount is given.

$$$\text{original} \times \text{multiplier} = \text{final}$$

$$\text{original} = \frac{\text{final}}{\text{multiplier}}$$

Do not just subtract the percentage from the final amount. The percentage change was based on the original amount, not on the final one.

## Worked example with a final amount

A flat increases in value by $6\%$ and is then worth £318,000. Because the final value is after the increase, £318,000 represents $106\%$ of the original value.

Convert $106\%$ to a multiplier of $1.06$, then divide the final value by $1.06$ to work backwards. This undoes the increase.

$$$318000 \div 1.06 = 300000$$

So the flat was originally worth £300,000. Check by going forwards with $300000 \times 1.06 = 318000$, which matches the final value.

## When the question gives the amount of change

Sometimes the number in the question is not the final amount. If a bill increases by $5\%$ and the increase is £74, then £74 is $5\%$ of the original bill.

In this case, divide the amount of change by the percentage change as a decimal. This gives you the whole original amount.

$$$\text{original} = \frac{\text{amount of change}}{\frac{\text{percentage change}}{100}}$$

For the bill, $74 \div 0.05 = 1480$, so the original bill was £1,480. A quick check is $1480 \times 0.05 = 74$, which matches the increase.

## Choose the method before you calculate

Ask yourself what the given number represents before touching the calculator. If it is the final amount, divide by the final multiplier such as $1.08$, $0.8$, or $0.75$.

If it is the amount of increase or reduction, divide by the change percentage as a decimal such as $0.04$ or $0.15$. This is the key decision that separates two very similar-looking question types.

If a ticket costs £312 after a $4\%$ increase, use $312 \div 1.04$. If the increase itself is £12, use $12 \div 0.04$.

## Sense checks that catch mistakes

An increase multiplier must be greater than $1$, and a decrease multiplier must be less than $1$. If your multiplier breaks that rule, you have probably chosen the wrong percentage.

After you find the original amount, go forwards to check. Multiply by the same multiplier, or find the stated percentage of your answer, and see if you get the number from the question.

## Check yourself

1. A coat is reduced by $25\%$. Which multiplier takes the normal price to the sale price?

   - $$0.75$ ✔

   - $$0.25$

   - $$1.25$

   - $$75$

   _Explanation:_ A $25\%$ reduction leaves $75\%$ of the original price, so the correct multiplier is $0.75$. The most common wrong answer is $0.25$, but that is the size of the discount, not the part of the price that remains.

2. A car was reduced by $20\%$ and now costs £8,400. Which calculation finds the original price?

   - $$8400 \div 0.8$ ✔

   - $$8400 \div 0.2$

   - $$8400 \times 0.8$

   - $$8400 \times 1.2$

   _Explanation:_ After a $20\%$ reduction, the sale price is $80\%$ of the original, so dividing by $0.8$ finds the original price. The most common wrong answer is dividing by $0.2$, but $0.2$ is the discount part, not the part that remains.

3. A sofa was reduced by $20\%$ in a sale, and the reduction was £84. What was the sale price?

   - £336 ✔

   - £420

   - £504

   - £105

   _Explanation:_ The £84 is the reduction, so it represents $20\%$ of the original price. Divide $84$ by $0.2$ to get the original price $£420$, then find the sale price as $£420 - £84 = £336$ or $£420 \times 0.8 = £336$. The common error is selecting $£420$, which is the original price before the reduction.
