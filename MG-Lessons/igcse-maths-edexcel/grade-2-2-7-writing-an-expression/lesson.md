## From words to algebra

![Flowchart showing the steps for writing an algebraic expression: identify the variable, spot key words, write each part, then combine carefully](../assets/001-c11a0023-35b0-4758-add8-2d5e6285c3e7.jpeg)

A variable is a letter such as $x$ or $n$ that stands for an unknown or changing number. An expression combines numbers, variables, and operations, such as $x+6$ or $5m$.

When a question says "write an expression", you usually do not need an equals sign. Save the equals sign for a formula, such as $C=40m+65j$.

A reliable method is to choose the variable, spot the key words, and combine the parts carefully. Words like "each", "half" and "total" tell you which operation to use.

## Word clues and operation signs

Words like "more than", "older than" and "longer than" usually mean add. Words like "fewer than", "younger than" and "spends" usually mean subtract.

Be careful with the order in subtraction. "$4$ fewer than $m$" means start with $m$ and take away $4$, so write $m-4$, not $4-m$.

Words like "each", "per" and "times" usually mean multiply, while "half" and "shared equally" usually mean divide. If an adult ticket costs $x$, then a junior ticket at half price costs $\frac{x}{2}$.

## Build totals in chunks

A good method is to write one small expression for each type of item, then add the parts together. This stops you mixing different prices or quantities.

Example: a bottle of juice costs $j$ pounds and a sandwich costs $s$ pounds. Then 4 bottles cost $4j$ and 3 sandwiches cost $3s$. Add the two parts for the total cost:

$$$4j+3s$$

The word "and" tells you to add the chunks, not multiply the two different items together. This is an expression because it describes the total cost without naming the total. If the total were called $C$, the formula would be $C=4j+3s$.

## Worked example: ages and a total

Related expressions often start from one variable. In age problems, it is helpful to write each person's age first before you find the total.

Example: Lina is $n$ years old. Her brother is 6 years older ($n+6$), and her cousin is 3 years younger ($n-3$). The total of the three ages is: $$n+(n+6)+(n-3)$$

Now collect like terms. The three $n$ terms make $3n$, and $6-3=3$, so the simplified expression is: $$3n+3$$ This is a shorter but equivalent expression.

## Division, packs and formulae

Division appears when you know a total and want the number of equal items. If tickets cost £4 each and the total money is $y$, the number of tickets sold is $\frac{y}{4}$.

Packs questions need careful reading. If there are $s$ small boxes and $l$ large boxes, then the total number of boxes is just $s+l$.

Multiply only when the question asks for what is inside the packs. If small boxes hold 6 pencils and large boxes hold 10 pencils, the total number of pencils is $6s+10l$.

Remember the difference between expression and formula. $40m+65j$ is an expression, but $C=40m+65j$ is a formula because it names the total as $C$.

## Check yourself

1. If one notebook costs $n$ pounds, which expression represents the cost of 5 notebooks?

   - $$n+5$

   - $$5n$ ✔

   - $$\frac{n}{5}$

   - $$n-5$

   _Explanation:_ $$5n$ means 5 lots of $n$, so it matches 5 notebooks at $n$ pounds each. The common wrong answer $n+5$ adds 5 instead of multiplying, so it does not represent 5 equal costs.

2. Pens cost $p$ pounds each and rulers cost $r$ pounds each. Which expression gives the total cost of 6 pens and 2 rulers?

   - $$6p+2r$ ✔

   - $$8pr$

   - $$6+r+2p$

   - $$6(p+r)$

   _Explanation:_ $$6p+2r$ is right because you find the cost of each type separately and then add them. The common wrong answer $6(p+r)$ gives 6 rulers as well as 6 pens, so it does not match 2 rulers.

3. A shop has $s$ small boxes of pencils and $l$ large boxes. A small box holds 6 pencils and a large box holds 10 pencils. Which expression gives the total number of pencils?

   - $$s+l$

   - $$6s+10l$ ✔

   - $$16sl$

   - $$6+10+s+l$

   _Explanation:_ $$6s+10l$ is correct because you multiply each number of boxes by the pencils in each box, then add the two amounts. The common wrong answer $s+l$ only counts boxes, so it answers a different question.

4. Adult tickets cost $a$ pounds each. Child tickets cost £2 less than an adult ticket. Which expression gives the total cost of 3 adult tickets and 4 child tickets?

   - $$3a+4(a-2)$ ✔

   - $$7a-2$

   - $$3(a-2)+4a$

   - $$12a-8$

   _Explanation:_ Each child ticket costs $a-2$, so 4 child tickets cost $4(a-2)$, and 3 adult tickets cost $3a$. The common wrong answer $7a-2$ subtracts 2 only once, but the £2 reduction applies to each of the 4 child tickets.
