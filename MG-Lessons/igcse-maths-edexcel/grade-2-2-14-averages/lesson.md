## Mean, median, mode and range

Averages help us describe a set of data with a single useful number. In this topic, you need mean, median, mode and range.

Mean, median and mode all describe a typical value in different ways. Range is slightly different because it describes spread, not the centre.

Before finding the median, and sometimes before checking the mode, write the data in ascending order. If a value repeats, keep every copy because repeats affect the mode, median and mean.

## Range and mode

The range tells you how spread out the data is. You calculate it by subtracting the smallest value from the largest value.

Range = largest value - smallest value.

For the set 114, 96, 130, 121, 118, 105, the smallest value is 96 and the largest is 130, so the range is 130 - 96 = 34.

The mode is the value that appears most often in a data set. In the collection 4, 8, 6, 8, 10, 5, 8, 6, the mode is 8 because it appears three times.

## Median: find the middle after ordering

![Ordered number rows showing median for odd and even data sets, with 9 highlighted as the middle of five values and 126 and 129 highlighted for six values where the median is 127.5](../assets/001-2f1b9c0a-ac16-4b27-a305-498017d18072.jpeg)

The median is the middle value after the data has been written in ascending order. This is why ordering is a rule, not just a suggestion.

If there are $5$ values, there is one middle value. For the set $13, 6, 9, 15, 7$, the ordered list is $6, 7, 9, 13, 15$, so the median is $9$.

If there are $6$ values, the median is halfway between the 3rd and 4th values. For the set $142, 118, 135, 126, 110, 129$, the ordered list is $110, 118, 126, 129, 135, 142$, so the median is:
$$\frac{126+129}{2} = 127.5$$

A common mistake is choosing the number written in the middle of the original list. That only works by luck, so always order the data first to ensure you find the correct middle value.

## Mean = total divided by number of values

The mean is what people often call the average. Find it with: $$\text{mean} = \frac{\text{total of values}}{\text{number of values}}$$

For $9, 4, 12, 8, 7$, first add the values to get $9+4+12+8+7 = 40$. There are $5$ numbers, so the mean is $40 \div 5 = 8$.

You can think of the mean as a fair share of the total. In exam questions, write the total clearly before dividing because it helps with checking and later working backwards.

## Working backwards from a mean

When a mean is given and one value is missing, start by finding the total. You can calculate this sum using the following formula:

$$$\text{total} = \text{mean} \times \text{number of values}$$

Suppose $8, 12, 5, 10, 9, 7, ?$ has mean $9$. The full total must be $9 \times 7 = 63$. The known numbers add to $8+12+5+10+9+7 = 51$.

So the missing value is $63 - 51 = 12$. A common mistake is to stop at $63$. That is the total of all seven numbers, not the hidden number.

## Updating and combining means

When a new value is added, or when two groups are combined, the safest method is to go back to totals. Do not average averages unless the group sizes are the same.

If a runner has a mean of $6.4$ km over $5$ days, the total for those days is $32$ km. After an extra day of $11$ km, the new total is $43$ km, so the new mean is $43/6$, which is about $7.17$ km.

If one group of $8$ scores has mean $40$, its total is $320$. If another group of $2$ scores has mean $28$, its total is $56$. Together, the $10$ scores total $376$, so the combined mean is $376/10 = 37.6$.

A common wrong method is to average the two means directly: $(40+28)/2 = 34$. That ignores that the groups have different sizes.

## Check yourself

1. Which statement is correct?

   - The mode is the largest value.

   - The range is found by subtracting the smallest value from the largest value. ✔

   - The median is the first value after ordering.

   - The mean is the value that appears most often.

   _Explanation:_ B is correct because the range measures spread using the largest and smallest values. A is the most common wrong answer because it confuses mode with maximum; the mode is the most frequent value, not the biggest.

2. What is the median of $12, 7, 15, 9, 11, 18$?

   - $$11$

   - $$11.5$ ✔

   - $$12$

   - $$72$

   _Explanation:_ Order the list as $7, 9, 11, 12, 15, 18$ and average the two middle values: $\frac{11+12}{2} = 11.5$. Option C is a common wrong answer because students sometimes choose the 4th value only, but an even number of values needs the mean of the 3rd and 4th values. Hint: There are $6$ numbers, so you need the two middle positions.

3. Seven numbers have mean $9$: $8, 12, 5, 10, 9, 7, ?$. What is the missing number?

   - $$12$ ✔

   - $$9$

   - $$63$

   - $$51$

   _Explanation:_ The total of all $7$ numbers is $9 \times 7 = 63$, and the known numbers add to $51$, so the missing value is $63 - 51 = 12$. $63$ is the total required, but not the specific missing value.

4. Stretch: Six cards show 2, 5, 6, 7, ?, ?. The mode is $2$ and the mean is $5$. What is the larger missing number?

   - $$2$

   - $$8$ ✔

   - $$10$

   - $$5$

   _Explanation:_ Since the mean is $5$, the total of the $6$ cards is $30$. The known cards total $20$, so the two missing numbers add to $10$. To make $2$ the mode, one missing number must be $2$, so the other is $8$.
