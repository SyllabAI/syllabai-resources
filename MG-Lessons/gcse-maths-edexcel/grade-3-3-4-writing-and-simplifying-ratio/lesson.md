## What a ratio tells you

![Counters diagram showing 6 red counters and 4 blue counters, with labels showing red to blue as 6:4 and blue to red as 4:6](../assets/001-93815a1a-66a9-47ab-b7a9-bef03b1aae9b.jpeg)

A ratio compares amounts in a fixed order. Each number in the ratio is called a part.

If there are 6 red counters and 4 blue counters, the ratio of red to blue is $6:4$. If the question asks for the ratio of blue to red, the answer is $4:6$, so the order matters.

## Simplifying ratios

A ratio is in simplest form when no whole number greater than 1 divides every part. To simplify, divide every part by the same common factor.

For $450 \, \text{cm}$ to $30 \, \text{cm}$, write the ratio as $450:30$ and divide both parts by 30. This gives $15:1$.

Always match units before you simplify. For $1 \, \text{m}$ to $50 \, \text{cm}$, change $1 \, \text{m}$ to $100 \, \text{cm}$ first, then $100:50 = 2:1$.

## Comparing values, not just counts

Sometimes a question is about value, not just the number of objects. In money questions, work out what each group is worth before writing the ratio.

Mia has three 20p coins and two 50p coins. Their values are $60 \, \text{p}$ and $100 \, \text{p}$, so the ratio of values is $60:100$.

Divide both parts by 20 to simplify. The simplest ratio is $3:5$.

## From a fraction to a ratio

![Bar model split into 8 equal parts with 3 left-handed parts and 5 right-handed parts, showing the ratio 3:5](../assets/002-07c04673-0fa0-4a32-8f28-55986dcf509c.jpeg)

If $\frac{3}{8}$ of a class are left-handed, then 3 of the 8 equal parts are left-handed. The rest of the class are right-handed.

The number of right-handed parts is $8-3=5$. So the ratio of left-handed to right-handed is $3:5$.

In general, if $\frac{a}{b}$ of a group are one type, then the rest are $b-a$ parts. That turns the fraction into a two-part ratio.

## Writing ratios in special forms

Sometimes a question asks for a ratio in the form $n:1$ or $1:n$. To achieve this, make the part that must be 1 by dividing every part of the ratio by that specific number.

To write $8.4:2.1$ in the form $n:1$, divide both parts by 2.1. This calculation gives the ratio $4:1$, which means $n=4$.

Similarly, to write $16:40$ in the form $1:n$, divide both parts by 16. This results in the ratio $1:2.5$, showing that $n=2.5$.

## Building three-part ratios

A three-part ratio compares three amounts in order. Words like "twice", "three times", and "half as many" tell you how to build the parts.

Let blue be 1 part. If red is three times blue, red is 3 parts, and if yellow is half of red, yellow is 1.5 parts.

So the ratio of blue to red to yellow is $1:3:1.5$. Ratios are usually written with whole numbers, so multiply every part by 2 to get $2:6:3$.

## Check yourself

1. There are 6 red counters and 4 blue counters. What is the ratio of blue counters to red counters?

   - $$6:4$

   - $$4:6$ ✔

   - $$3:2$

   - $$2:3$

   _Explanation:_ The ratio of blue to red must put blue first, then red, so it is $4:6$. A common wrong answer is $6:4$ because it swaps the order asked in the question.

2. Noah has four 10p coins and one £1 coin. What is the ratio of the value of the 10p coins to the value of the £1 coin in simplest form?

   - $$4:1$

   - $$2:5$ ✔

   - $$4:10$

   - $$5:2$

   _Explanation:_ Four 10p coins are worth $40 \, \text{p}$ and one £1 coin is worth $100 \, \text{p}$, so the value ratio is $40:100 = 2:5$. A common wrong answer is $4:1$ because it counts coins instead of comparing their values.

3. Write $12:3$ in the form $n:1$.

   - $$4:1$ ✔

   - $$1:4$

   - $$12:1$

   - $$3:1$

   _Explanation:_ To get the form $n:1$, the second part must become 1, so divide both parts by 3 to get $4:1$. A common wrong answer is $1:4$ because that is the form $1:n$, not $n:1$.

4. Blue counters are twice as many as yellow counters, and red counters are half as many as blue counters. What percentage of the counters are yellow?

   - $$20\%$

   - $$25\%$ ✔

   - $$33\frac{1}{3}\%$

   - $$50\%$

   _Explanation:_ Let yellow be 1 part, so blue is 2 parts and red is 1 part. There are 4 parts altogether, so yellow is $\frac{1}{4}$ of the total, which is $25\%$; a common wrong answer is $50\%$ because it compares yellow only with blue instead of with the whole group.
