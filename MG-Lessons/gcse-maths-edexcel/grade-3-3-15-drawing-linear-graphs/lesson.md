## Axes, coordinates and straight lines

![Coordinate grid with x-axis, y-axis, origin, and the plotted points (2, -1) and (-1, 3)](../assets/001-4693ee6b-7354-4ed5-8dd7-fca476795cf4.jpeg)
The horizontal axis is the $x$-axis and the vertical axis is the $y$-axis. They cross at the origin, written as $O$ or $(0,0)$.

A coordinate point is written as $(x,y)$. Always move across first for $x$, then up or down for $y$, so $(3,-2)$ is not the same as $(-2,3)$.

Many straight-line equations can be written as $y=mx+c$, and the number on its own, $c$, is the value of $y$ when $x=0$. This gives a quick check when you are filling in a table.

## Making a table of values

A table of values matches each chosen $x$-value to its correct $y$-value. To fill it in, substitute the $x$ into the equation and calculate carefully.

For $y=3x-1$, when $x=-2$, substitute into the rule and simplify: $$y=3(-2)-1=-7$$ For $x=1$, repeat the process: $$y=3(1)-1=2$$

The completed table is below.

| $x$ | -2 | -1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | -7 | -4 | -1 | 2 | 5 |

Notice that when $x=0$, the value of $y$ is $-1$, so the line will cross the $y$-axis at $-1$.

## Worked example: drawing the graph

![Straight-line graph of y = 2x + 1 with labelled points (-3, -5), (0, 1), and (3, 7)](../assets/002-806ad38c-e8e0-471b-8b91-21c9e8c8dc04.jpeg)

Draw the graph of $y=2x+1$ for $x$ from $-3$ to $3$. Start by making a table of values, and use the quick check that when $x=0$, the graph should cross the $y$-axis at $1$.

For $x=-3$, substitute into the rule and simplify:
$$y=2(-3)+1=-6+1=-5$$
For $x=3$, substitute again and simplify:
$$y=2(3)+1=6+1=7$$

The full table is below:

| $x$ | -3 | -2 | -1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | -5 | -3 | -1 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |

Plot the points and join them with one straight line using a ruler from $x=-3$ to $x=3$.

## Negative and fractional coefficients

The coefficient is the number multiplying the variable, so the coefficient of $x$ in $-3x$ is $-3$. Straight-line equations still work the same way when the coefficient is negative or fractional.

For $y=\frac{1}{2}x+2$, when $x=-3$, substitute carefully. $$y=\frac{1}{2}(-3)+2=-1.5+2=0.5$$

When $x=3$, repeat the process with the positive value. $$y=\frac{1}{2}(3)+2=1.5+2=3.5$$

Be especially careful with signs in equations like $y=2-3x$, because a negative value of $x$ makes the term $-3x$ positive. Some points sit halfway between grid lines, so always check the axis scale before plotting.

## Reading values from a graph

To find a $y$-value from a graph, start at the given $x$ on the horizontal axis, move to the line, then go across to the $y$-axis. To find an $x$-value, start on the $y$-axis, go across to the line, then go down to the $x$-axis.

For example, on the graph of $y=2-2x$, when $x=0.5$, the line gives $y\approx 1$.

$$$y=2-2(0.5)=1$$

The substitution check agrees with the graph reading. You can also work backwards from a $y$-value. On the graph of $y=\frac{1}{2}x+2$, if $y=3.5$, the corresponding $x$-value is about $3$.

## Rearranging before you draw

Sometimes the equation is not written with $y$ on its own. Rearrange first, because tables are much easier to make from a rule written as $y=$ something.

Start with $x+y=5$ and subtract $x$ from both sides.

$$x+y=5$$
$$y=5-x$$

Be careful, because this is not the same as $y=x-5$.

Now fill a table of values, such as this one.

| $x$ | -1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

Plot the points and join them with a ruler.

## Check yourself

1. Which coordinate means 3 squares right and 2 squares down from the origin?

   - $$(3,-2)$ ✔

   - $$(-2,3)$

   - $$(-3,2)$

   - $$(2,-3)$

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because the first coordinate gives the horizontal move and the second gives the vertical move, so right 3 then down 2 is $(3,-2)$. B is the most common mistake because it swaps the coordinates, but $(-2,3)$ means 2 left and 3 up.

2. Which point lies on the graph of $y=2x+1$?

   - $$(1,3)$ ✔

   - $$(1,2)$

   - $$(2,1)$

   - $$(0,0)$

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because substituting $x=1$ gives $y=2(1)+1=3$. B is the most common wrong answer because it forgets the $+1$ and uses $y=2x$ instead.

3. Stretch: On the graph of $y=\frac{1}{2}x+2$, which $x$-value gives $y=3.5$?

   - $$1.5$

   - $$3$ ✔

   - $$5$

   - $$7$

   _Explanation:_ Hint: Start at $y=3.5$, or solve the equation backwards. B is correct because $3.5=\frac{1}{2}x+2$ gives $1.5=\frac{1}{2}x$, so $x=3$. A is the most common wrong answer because it stops after subtracting $2$ and forgets to divide by $\frac{1}{2}$.
