## What bar charts show

![Simple bar chart of monthly temperatures with Month on the horizontal axis and Temperature in degrees Celsius on the vertical axis; separate bars for March 11, April 14, May 18, and June 21](../assets/001-3d579f81-84e3-40fa-887b-6bdc43511fe7.jpeg)
A bar chart compares amounts in different categories, such as months, colours, or sports. Each category has its own separate bar, and a taller bar means a bigger value.

The horizontal axis usually shows the categories, and the vertical axis usually shows the numbers. In the chart above, the months are along the bottom and the temperatures are up the side.

## Reading the scale carefully

The vertical axis may go up in $1$s, $2$s, $5$s, or $10$s, so always check the scale before reading a bar. A sensible first question is, "What does each labelled line stand for?"

Some bars land exactly on a labelled line, but others sit halfway between two lines. On a scale marked in $2$s, halfway between $10$ and $12$ is $11$, and halfway between $20$ and $22$ is $21$.

## Finding the range

The range tells you how spread out the data is. To find it from a bar chart, read the value of the highest bar and the value of the lowest bar, then subtract.

Example temperatures are January 6°C, February 8°C, March 12°C, April 17°C, and May 20°C. In this data set, the highest value is 20 and the lowest value is 6.

Subtract the smallest value from the biggest value to find the difference: $$20 - 6 = 14$$

So the range is 14°C. Do not use the first and last bars of a chart unless they also happen to be the smallest and biggest values.

## Grouped bar charts

![Grouped bar chart of miles run by Aisha and Ben on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday with a key; Aisha bars are 4, 5, and 3 while Ben bars are 7, 6, and 4](../assets/002-038cbb74-f11e-40d6-8798-d0863d66214c.jpeg)

A grouped bar chart shows two or more bars for each category, so you can compare groups side by side. A key tells you which bar belongs to each person or group.

For Monday in the chart above, Aisha ran 4 miles and Ben ran 7 miles. By looking at the difference between the two bars, we can see that Ben ran 3 miles further.

$$$7 - 4 = 3$$

You can also add bars across several days if you want to compare totals. Read the key carefully first, because mixing up the bars is a common mistake.

## Frequency charts, mode, and totals

On a frequency bar chart, the height of each bar shows how often something happens. The mode is the category with the tallest bar because it has the greatest frequency.

Suppose a team scored 0 goals in 3 matches, 1 goal in 5 matches, 2 goals in 4 matches, and 3 goals in 2 matches. To find total goals, multiply each goals value by its frequency:

$$0 \times 3 = 0$$
$$1 \times 5 = 5$$
$$2 \times 4 = 8$$
$$3 \times 2 = 6$$

Now add the goal totals, not just the frequencies:

$$0 + 5 + 8 + 6 = 19$$

So the team scored 19 goals in total.

## From tally to bar chart

A tally is a quick way to count results, usually grouped in fives. Once you know the frequency for each category, you can draw the matching bars.

For the bike colours Blue, Red, Blue, Black, Blue, Red, White, Black, Blue, White, the frequencies are Black 2, Blue 4, Red 2, and White 2. Draw bars to heights 2, 4, 2, and 2, then label the axes Colour and Frequency.

When checking a bar chart, look for equal bar widths, clear gaps, labelled axes, and an even scale. On a grouped bar chart, there should also be a key so the reader knows which bars belong to which group.

## Check yourself

1. Which axis usually shows the categories on a bar chart?

   - The horizontal axis ✔

   - The vertical axis

   - A diagonal axis

   - The key

   _Explanation:_ The horizontal axis usually lists the categories, while the vertical axis shows the values or frequencies. The common wrong answer is B because students often focus on the numbers up the side first, but that side is the scale, not the category labels.

2. A bar chart has category values 9, 13, 7, and 15. What is the range?

   - 6

   - 8 ✔

   - 15

   - 22

   _Explanation:_ The range is the biggest value minus the smallest value, so $15 - 7 = 8$. The common wrong answer is A because students sometimes subtract the first and last values, but range is not "last minus first" unless those happen to be the smallest and biggest. Hint: Find the biggest and smallest values first.

3. In a grouped bar chart, Aisha runs 5 miles on Tuesday and Ben runs 6 miles on Tuesday. How much farther did Ben run?

   - 1 ✔

   - 5

   - 6

   - 11

   _Explanation:_ To find how much farther one person ran, subtract the smaller value from the larger value: $6 - 5 = 1$. Therefore, Ben ran 1 mile farther. Adding the two values ($6 + 5 = 11$) gives the combined total, which is a common error when looking for a difference.

4. Maya has run 22 miles from Monday to Friday. Liam has run 27 miles and will not run on Saturday. How many miles must Maya run on Saturday so that their totals are equal?

   - 49

   - 5 ✔

   - 22

   - 27

   _Explanation:_ Maya needs to make up the difference between the totals, so calculate $27 - 22 = 5$. The common wrong answer is A because adding the totals together does not tell you how much Maya still needs to catch up.
