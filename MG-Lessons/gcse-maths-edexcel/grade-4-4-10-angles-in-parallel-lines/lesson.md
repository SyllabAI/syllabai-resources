## Build on the angle facts you already know

Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet. We write $AB \parallel CD$ when two lines are parallel.

A transversal is a line that cuts across two or more lines. Before using parallel-line rules, keep basic facts ready: angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$, vertically opposite angles are equal, and angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$.

In a written solution, each new angle needs a reason. Common reasons include angles on a straight line, vertically opposite, corresponding, alternate, co-interior, and angles in a triangle.

## Spot the F, Z and C patterns

![Two horizontal parallel lines cut by a slanted transversal, with F, Z and C shapes highlighting corresponding, alternate and co-interior angle pairs](../assets/001-9f492aff-91a9-46c9-8fd0-4480650dcd39.jpeg)

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, look for shapes instead of trying to memorise a list. The position of the angles tells you the rule.

An F-shape shows corresponding angles, and they are equal. A Z-shape shows alternate angles, and they are equal too.

A C-shape shows co-interior angles. These angles sit in the interior, meaning the space between the parallel lines, and they add to $180^\circ$.

## Worked example: vertically opposite, then corresponding

At the top crossing, the bottom-right angle is $58^\circ$. Angle $x$ is the top-left angle at the same crossing, and angle $y$ is the top-left angle at the lower crossing.

Work one angle at a time. Angle $x$ is vertically opposite the $58^\circ$ angle, so $x = 58^\circ$.

Now compare $x$ and $y$ across the parallel lines. They are in the same relative position, so they are corresponding angles and $y = 58^\circ$.

## Co-interior angles and a common mistake

One common mistake is treating every angle pair in parallel lines as equal. If the angles make a C-shape, they are co-interior and must add to $180^\circ$.

Suppose one co-interior angle is $118^\circ$ and the other is $x$. Then $x = 180^\circ - 118^\circ = 62^\circ$.

This is angle chasing. Write each new value on the diagram and give the reason before moving on.

## Worked example: parallel lines with an isosceles triangle

![Two parallel lines AB and CD with isosceles triangle EFG between them, exterior angle AEG marked 112 degrees and angle FGD marked x](../assets/002-63c9d4da-9ec4-4956-82e1-7e2fa7d1c2d2.jpeg)
In this diagram, $AB \parallel CD$, triangle $EFG$ is isosceles with $EG = FG$, and $\angle AEG = 112^\circ$. We want to find $\angle FGD$.

First use the straight line at $E$. Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$, so $\angle GEF = 180^\circ - 112^\circ = 68^\circ$.

Because $EG = FG$, triangle $EFG$ is isosceles. The base angles are equal, so $\angle EFG = 68^\circ$.

Now use the parallel lines. Since $EF \parallel GD$ and $FG$ is a transversal, $\angle EFG$ and $\angle FGD$ are alternate angles, so $\angle FGD = 68^\circ$.

## Parallelograms hide parallel lines

A parallelogram is a hidden parallel-lines question. Even without a diagram, the letters in parallelogram $ABCD$ go around the shape in order, so $AB$ and $CD$ are opposite sides and $AD$ and $BC$ are opposite sides. That means $AB \parallel CD$ and $AD \parallel BC$.

Example: if $\angle DAB = 126^\circ$, then side $AB$ cuts the parallel lines $AD$ and $BC$. So $\angle DAB$ and $\angle ABC$ are co-interior angles and add to $180^\circ$. Therefore $\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 126^\circ = 54^\circ$.

Keep using the same idea around the shape. Side $BC$ cuts the parallel lines $AB$ and $CD$, so $\angle ABC$ and $\angle BCD$ add to $180^\circ$, giving $\angle BCD = 126^\circ$. Then side $CD$ cuts the parallel lines $AD$ and $BC$, so $\angle BCD$ and $\angle CDA$ add to $180^\circ$. This gives $\angle CDA = 180^\circ - 126^\circ = 54^\circ$.

## Check yourself

1. When a transversal crosses parallel lines, which pair of angles add to $180^\circ$?

   - Corresponding angles

   - Alternate angles

   - Co-interior angles ✔

   - Vertically opposite angles

   _Explanation:_ Co-interior angles sit inside the parallel lines on the same side of the transversal, making a C-shape, so they sum to $180^\circ$. The most common wrong answer is corresponding angles, but corresponding angles are equal, not supplementary.

2. Two co-interior angles are $118^\circ$ and $x$. What is $x$?

   - $$62^\circ$ ✔

   - $$118^\circ$

   - $$72^\circ$

   - $$242^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Co-interior angles add to $180^\circ$, so $x = 180^\circ - 118^\circ = 62^\circ$. The most common wrong answer is $118^\circ$, but that would only be true for corresponding or alternate angles, not co-interior ones.

3. In parallelogram $ABCD$, if $\angle DAB = 126^\circ$, what is $\angle ABC$?

   - $$54^\circ$ ✔

   - $$88^\circ$

   - $$92^\circ$

   - $$126^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Since $AD \parallel BC$, $\angle DAB$ and $\angle ABC$ are co-interior angles on transversal $AB$, so they add to $180^\circ$. Therefore $\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 126^\circ = 54^\circ$.
