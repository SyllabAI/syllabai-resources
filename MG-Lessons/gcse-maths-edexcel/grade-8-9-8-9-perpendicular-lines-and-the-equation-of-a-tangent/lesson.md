## Reading line information

In $y=mx+c$, $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the y-intercept. The line crosses the y-axis at $(0,c)$, so you can often read one point straight from the equation.

A positive gradient rises from left to right, and a negative gradient falls from left to right. Parallel lines have the same gradient, while non-vertical perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to $-1$.

## From two points to an equation

If you know two points on a line, first find the gradient using $$m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$$ Change in $y$ goes on top and change in $x$ goes on the bottom.

For the points $(0,6)$ and $(4,18)$, $$m=\frac{18-6}{4-0}=\frac{12}{4}=3$$ Because one point has $x=0$, it gives the y-intercept straight away, so $c=6$.

Substitute $m=3$ and $c=6$ into $y=mx+c$. $$y=3x+6$$ If neither point had $x=0$, you would substitute one point into $y=mx+c$ to find $c$.

## Negative reciprocals

![Coordinate graph showing two perpendicular lines crossing at a right angle, with gradients 2 and -1/2 labelled](../assets/001-d47ba1f4-4c4c-4ed7-88bc-2f3693e92ce5.jpeg)

Perpendicular lines make a right angle, so their gradients multiply to $-1$. For example, if one gradient is $2$, the perpendicular gradient is $-\frac{1}{2}$.

Always rearrange a line into $y=mx+c$ before reading its gradient. A very common mistake is to flip the fraction but forget the sign. For example, the perpendicular to $-\frac{2}{3}$ is $\frac{3}{2}$.

## Worked example: perpendicular through a point

Find the equation of the line through $(6,7)$ that is perpendicular to $y=2x-4$. The given line has gradient $2$, so the perpendicular gradient is $-\frac{1}{2}$.

Start with the general equation: $$y=-\frac{1}{2}x+c$$ Then substitute the point $(6,7)$ to find the value of the constant $c$: $$7=-\frac{1}{2}(6)+c=-3+c$$

Add $3$ to both sides to solve for the intercept: $$c=10$$ So the final required equation is: $$y=-\frac{1}{2}x+10$$

## Tangents use the radius

![Coordinate graph of a circle centred at the origin with point P(2,4), the radius to P, and the tangent at P marked perpendicular](../assets/002-47625d4d-5457-4395-a7d6-e6fd76da49c9.jpeg)
A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point. The radius to that point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent, so the tangent gradient is the negative reciprocal of the radius gradient.

For circles of the form $x^2+y^2=r^2$, the centre is $(0,0)$. For $x^2+y^2=20$ and $P(2,4)$, the radius gradient is calculated using the change in $y$ over the change in $x$:
$$m=\frac{4-0}{2-0}=2$$

Since the tangent is perpendicular, its gradient is $-\frac{1}{2}$, and its equation starts as $y=-\frac{1}{2}x+c$. Substitute the coordinates of $P(2,4)$ to find the $y$-intercept $c$:
$$4=-\frac{1}{2}(2)+c=-1+c \Rightarrow c=5$$

Therefore, the final equation for the tangent is:
$$y=-\frac{1}{2}x+5$$
If your tangent has the same gradient as the radius, it is not a tangent at all.

## Check yourself

1. In the equation $y=-3x+5$, what are the gradient and y-intercept?

   - gradient $-3$, y-intercept $5$ ✔

   - gradient $5$, y-intercept $-3$

   - gradient $3$, y-intercept $5$

   - gradient $-3$, y-intercept $-5$

   _Explanation:_ In $y=mx+c$, the number multiplying $x$ is the gradient and the constant term is the y-intercept, so $m=-3$ and $c=5$. The most common wrong answer is B, which swaps the two numbers without matching them to their positions in the formula.

2. A line is perpendicular to $2y=3x+8$. What is its gradient?

   - $$\frac{3}{2}$

   - $$-\frac{2}{3}$ ✔

   - $$\frac{2}{3}$

   - $$-\frac{3}{2}$

   _Explanation:_ Rearranging gives $y=\frac{3}{2}x+4$, so the original gradient is $\frac{3}{2}$. A perpendicular line has the negative reciprocal gradient $-\frac{2}{3}$. The most common wrong answer is D, which changes the sign but forgets to flip the fraction. Hint: Get it into $y=mx+c$ first.

3. The circle $x^2+y^2=25$ has centre $(0,0)$ and point $P(3,4)$ on the circle. What is the gradient of the tangent at $P$?

   - $$\frac{4}{3}$

   - $$-\frac{4}{3}$

   - $$-\frac{3}{4}$ ✔

   - $$\frac{3}{4}$

   _Explanation:_ The radius from the centre to $P(3,4)$ has gradient $\frac{4}{3}$. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius, so its gradient is the negative reciprocal $-\frac{3}{4}$. The most common wrong answer is A, which uses the radius gradient instead of the tangent gradient.

4. Stretch: The circle $x^2+y^2=25$ has point $P(-3,4)$ on the circle. Which is the equation of the tangent at $P$?

   - $$y=-\frac{4}{3}x$

   - $$y=\frac{3}{4}x+\frac{25}{4}$ ✔

   - $$y=-\frac{3}{4}x+\frac{25}{4}$

   - $$y=\frac{4}{3}x+8$

   _Explanation:_ The radius gradient is $\frac{4-0}{-3-0}=-\frac{4}{3}$, so the tangent gradient is $\frac{3}{4}$. Using $(-3,4)$ in $y=\frac{3}{4}x+c$ gives $4=\frac{3}{4}(-3)+c$, so $c=\frac{25}{4}$. The most common wrong answer is A, which is the radius line through the origin, not the tangent.
