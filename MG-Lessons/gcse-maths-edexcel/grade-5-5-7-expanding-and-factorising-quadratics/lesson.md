## What is a quadratic?

A quadratic expression has highest power 2 and is often written as:

$$$x^2 + bx + c$$

Expanding removes brackets to create this form, and factorising puts the quadratic back into brackets.

To work accurately, you need to recognise terms, coefficients, and like terms. Like terms can be collected only if the variable and power match, which is why the two middle terms often combine after expansion.

## Expanding with four products

![Box method diagram for expanding (x + 5)(x - 4) using a 2 by 2 grid with headers x and -4 across the top, x and 5 down the side, and cell products x^2, -4x, 5x, and -20, followed by collect like terms to make x^2 + x - 20](../assets/001-df600d22-d80c-4241-8d31-dc442d6887e2.jpeg)

When you expand two brackets, every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second bracket. A grid or box helps you see all four products so no term is missed.

For $(x + 5)(x - 4)$, make four products: $x \times x = x^2$, $x \times -4 = -4x$, $5 \times x = 5x$, and $5 \times -4 = -20$. This gives the expression $x^2 - 4x + 5x - 20$ before simplifying.

Now collect like terms in the middle: $-4x + 5x = x$. By combining these results, we find that $(x + 5)(x - 4) = x^2 + x - 20$.

## Coefficients and negative signs

With coefficients, multiply the numbers and the variables together. Also keep checking the signs, because a negative times a positive is negative: $(-1) \times (+1) = -1$.

Expand $(4x - 1)(2x + 3)$ by making four products: $4x \times 2x = 8x^2$, $4x \times 3 = 12x$, $-1 \times 2x = -2x$, and $-1 \times 3 = -3$. Before collecting, the result is $8x^2 + 12x - 2x - 3$.

Now collect like terms in the middle to get $8x^2 + 10x - 3$. If your middle term has the wrong sign, recheck the negative multiplication.

## Factorising with product and sum

Factorising reverses expanding. For quadratics of the form $x^2 + bx + c$, find two numbers whose product is $c$ and whose sum is $b$.

Example: factorise $n^2 - 4n - 21$. The numbers must multiply to $-21$ and add to $-4$, so they are $-7$ and $3$.

Put those numbers into the brackets to get $(n - 7)(n + 3)$. A negative constant means the bracket signs must be different, because one number is positive and the other is negative.

## Special patterns

A squared bracket means the same bracket is multiplied by itself, so $(b - 6)^2 = (b - 6)(b - 6)$. Expanding gives $b^2 - 6b - 6b + 36 = b^2 - 12b + 36$.

Some quadratics are perfect squares, so they factorise to repeated brackets. For example, $x^2 - 16x + 64 = (x - 8)^2$ because 64 is $8^2$ and the middle term is $-8x - 8x = -16x$.

A difference of two squares has the form $$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$$ So $x^2 - 64 = (x - 8)(x + 8)$, but $x^2 + 64$ does not fit this pattern.

## Check yourself

1. What is the expansion of $(x + 3)(x + 2)$?

   - $$x^2 + 5x + 6$ ✔

   - $$x^2 + 6$

   - $$x^2 + 5$

   - $$x^2 + 6x + 5$

   _Explanation:_ Hint: Think four products, then collect like terms. $x$ multiplies both terms in the second bracket and 3 multiplies both terms as well, so the four products are $x^2$, $2x$, $3x$, and 6, which collect to $x^2 + 5x + 6$. The common wrong answer is B because it multiplies only the first and last terms and misses the two middle products.

2. Which is the correct factorisation of $x^2 - 4x - 21$?

   - $$(x - 7)(x + 3)$ ✔

   - $$(x + 7)(x - 3)$

   - $$(x - 7)(x - 3)$

   - $$(x + 21)(x - 1)$

   _Explanation:_ The correct pair is $-7$ and 3 because $-7 \times 3 = -21$ and $-7 + 3 = -4$, so the factorisation is $(x - 7)(x + 3)$. The common wrong answer is C because two negatives do make a negative middle term, but their product is $+21$, not $-21$.
