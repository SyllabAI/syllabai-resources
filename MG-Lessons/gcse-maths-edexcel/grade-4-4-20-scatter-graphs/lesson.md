## Reading scatter graphs

A scatter graph is used when you have two pieces of information about the same person, object, or event. Each point represents one pair of values, such as hours revised and test score.

The horizontal axis is the $x$-axis and the vertical axis is the $y$-axis. Always read the axis labels first, because swapping them changes the meaning of every point.

For example, the point $(8,24)$ could mean 8 hours of revision and 24 marks. To plot $(12,30)$, go 12 across on the $x$-axis and then 30 up on the $y$-axis.

## Correlation patterns

![Three small scatter graphs labelled positive correlation, negative correlation, and no correlation, each with x-axis and y-axis and blue points showing different patterns](../assets/001-5f186647-bf6b-44ce-9c62-9f36915887b8.jpeg)

Correlation describes the overall relationship between two variables on a scatter graph. To spot it, look at the points from left to right rather than focusing on just one point.

If the points rise as you move right, there is positive correlation. If the points fall as you move right, there is negative correlation.

If the points are scattered with no clear pattern, there is no correlation. Correlation shows a link in the data, but it does not prove that one variable causes the other.

## Drawing a line of best fit

When the points follow a rough straight-line pattern, you can draw a line of best fit. This line shows the overall trend through the middle of the points.

A good line of best fit has roughly the same number of points above it as below it. It does not need to pass through every point.

Do not join the dots one by one, because a scatter graph is not a line graph. The line also does not have to go through $(0,0)$ unless the data suggest that it should.

## Worked example: estimating from a line of best fit

![Scatter graph of hours practised against test score with blue points, a red line of best fit, and green guide lines from 14 hours to an estimated score of about 26](../assets/002-11b6dc9f-8b85-46c6-abc6-8f6dcdedb5bc.jpeg)

This scatter graph already has a line of best fit. Suppose the question asks for the estimated test score when a student practised for 14 hours.

Find 14 on the horizontal axis and draw or imagine a vertical line up to the red line of best fit. Use the best fit line rather than the nearest blue point, because you want the overall trend.

From the place where the guide line meets the best fit line, move horizontally to the vertical axis. The reading is about 26, so a sensible estimate is about 26 marks.

Because 14 is inside the plotted range, this is interpolation. Different students may read 25 or 26 depending on how the line is drawn, so an approximate answer is expected.

## Reliable and unreliable estimates

Estimates are most reliable when they are made within the range of the data already plotted. This is called interpolation.

Estimating outside the plotted range is called extrapolation, and it is less reliable because the trend might not continue. For example, if the plotted times run from about $2$ to $18$ hours, predicting a result at $30$ hours is risky.

If there is no correlation, a line of best fit will not give a meaningful prediction. In an exam, lightly draw your vertical and horizontal guide lines and write "about" before your estimate.

## Check yourself

1. A scatter graph compares age of a phone with selling price. The points slope downwards from left to right. What is the correlation?

   - Positive correlation

   - Negative correlation ✔

   - No correlation

   - The graph must be wrong

   _Explanation:_ Downwards from left to right means that as age increases, selling price tends to decrease, so this is negative correlation. A is the most common wrong answer, but correlation is decided by whether the points rise or fall from left to right.

2. Which statement about a line of best fit is correct?

   - It should join each point to the next point

   - It must go through every single point

   - It should go through the middle of the cluster with roughly equal points on each side ✔

   - It must pass through the origin

   _Explanation:_ A sensible line of best fit represents the overall trend, so it should pass through the middle of the points with a balanced spread above and below. D is a common wrong answer, but a scatter graph only goes through the origin if the data actually suggest that.

3. A scatter graph shows a positive correlation between ice cream sales and sunburn cases. What is the best conclusion?

   - Buying ice cream causes sunburn

   - Sunburn causes people to buy ice cream

   - Warm weather could increase both ice cream sales and sunburn ✔

   - There is actually no correlation

   _Explanation:_ C is best because a third factor such as warm sunny weather could affect both variables at the same time. A is the common wrong answer because correlation does not prove that one variable causes the other.
