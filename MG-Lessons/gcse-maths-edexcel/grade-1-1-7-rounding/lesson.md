## The rounding rule

![Number lines showing 7642 below 7650 so it rounds to 7600, and 8.36 above 8.35 so it rounds to 8.4](../assets/001-ffb023e8-17eb-42ee-8474-dd7d32d93860.jpeg)

Rounding changes a number to a nearby, simpler value. The place value tells you which digit matters, so in $7518$ the $5$ is the hundreds digit. You only change the number at the place value named in the question.

Use the same rule every time: look at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is $0$ to $4$, round down, and if it is $5$ to $9$, round up.

A number line shows the idea clearly. Values below the midpoint round down, while values at or above the midpoint round up.

## Whole numbers: nearest hundred and nearest thousand

For whole numbers, first find the place value you are keeping. For nearest hundred, keep the hundreds digit and check the tens digit. For nearest thousand, keep the thousands digit and check the hundreds digit.

Worked example: round $7642$ to the nearest hundred. The hundreds digit is $6$ and the tens digit is $4$, so the $6$ stays the same. The last two digits become zeros, giving $7642 \to 7600$.

Worked example: round $9732$ to the nearest thousand. The thousands digit is $9$ and the hundreds digit is $7$, so you round up to the next thousand. That gives $9732 \to 10000$.

## Decimal places

Decimal places are counted after the decimal point. In $6.47$, the $4$ is the tenths digit and the $7$ is the hundredths digit. To round to 1 decimal place, keep the tenths digit and look at the hundredths digit.

Worked example: $8.36$ to 1 decimal place keeps the $3$ in the tenths place. The next digit is $6$, so you round the $3$ up to $4$. The answer is $8.4$, not $8.3$.

The same method works for more decimal places. For example, $4.72851$ to 3 decimal places keeps $728$, then the next digit is $5$, so $4.72851 \to 4.729$.

## Significant figures

Significant figures start at the first non-zero digit, not at the decimal point. Zeros before the first non-zero digit do not count, so the first significant figure in $0.5837$ is $5$.

Worked example: $0.5837$ to 2 significant figures keeps $5$ and $8$. The next digit is $3$, so it rounds down to $0.58$.

Worked example: $68542$ to 2 significant figures keeps $6$ and $8$. The next digit is $5$, so the $8$ rounds up to $9$ and the remaining digits become zeros. The answer is $69000$.

## Choosing the right method

Always read the instruction carefully because decimal places and significant figures mean different things. For $82.319$, rounding to 2 decimal places gives $82.32$, but rounding to 2 significant figures gives $82$.

A quick self-check can save marks. Nearest hundred answers should end in two zeros, nearest thousand answers should end in three zeros, and 2 decimal places means writing two digits after the decimal point. For example, write $7.20$ rather than $7.2$ if the question asks for 2 decimal places.

Be careful with zeros in large numbers. $69000$ and $69$ are not the same size, so do not remove zeros that show place value.

## Check yourself

1. When rounding $7642$ to the nearest hundred, which digit tells you whether to round up or down?

   - The tens digit, $4$ ✔

   - The units digit, $2$

   - The hundreds digit, $6$

   - The thousands digit, $7$

   _Explanation:_ The correct digit is the tens digit because it is immediately to the right of the hundreds digit. The common wrong answer is the units digit, but that looks two places to the right and would not tell you how to round to the nearest hundred.

2. Write $82.319$ correct to 2 decimal places.

   - $$82.32$ ✔

   - $$82.31$

   - $$82.3$

   - $$82.319$

   _Explanation:_ To 2 decimal places, you keep $31$ after the decimal point and look at the next digit, which is $9$. Since $9$ means round up, $82.319$ becomes $82.32$. The common wrong answer $82.31$ comes from cutting off the number instead of rounding it.

3. Which pair of rounded answers for $82.319$ is correct?

   - To 2 decimal places: $82.32$; to 2 significant figures: $82$ ✔

   - To 2 decimal places: $82.31$; to 2 significant figures: $82.3$

   - To 2 decimal places: $82.3$; to 2 significant figures: $82.32$

   - To 2 decimal places: $82.32$; to 2 significant figures: $80$

   _Explanation:_ To 2 decimal places, keep $31$ and look at the next digit $9$, so the answer is $82.32$. To 2 significant figures, the first two significant digits are $8$ and $2$, and the next digit is $3$, so the answer stays $82$. The common wrong answer $80$ treats 2 significant figures as if it were 1 significant figure.
