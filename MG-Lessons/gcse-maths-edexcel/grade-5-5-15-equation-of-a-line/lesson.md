## What $y = mx + c$ means

![Graph of the line y = 2x + 3 with y-intercept at (0,3) and a rise of 4 over a run of 2 labelled to show gradient 2](../assets/001-c1e6e90e-c1b1-4662-aba0-174cba440eed.jpeg)

A straight line is usually written as $y = mx + c$. In this form, $m$ is the gradient and $c$ is the y-intercept.

The y-intercept is where the line crosses the y-axis, so it happens when $x = 0$. A positive gradient rises from left to right, a negative gradient falls, and a horizontal line has gradient 0.

The crossing point is $(0, c)$. This means you can read a lot from the equation before you even draw the line.

## Finding the gradient from two points

Gradient tells you how steep the line is. Always compare points from left to right so the sign is correct.

To calculate the gradient, use the formula:

$$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$

Choose two exact points on the line, then count how much $y$ changes and how much $x$ changes.

Between $(1, 7)$ and $(3, 1)$, $x$ increases by 2 while $y$ decreases by 6. So

$$m = \frac{-6}{2} = -3$$

and the line slopes downward.

## Worked example from a graph

![Graph of a downward sloping line through the points (0,5) and (2,-1), with the y-intercept marked and changes +2 in x and -6 in y labelled to show gradient -3 and equation y = -3x + 5](../assets/002-17694495-85fc-405a-a672-79d60bdb5600.jpeg)
To find an equation from a graph, you need the gradient and the y-intercept. This line passes through $(0, 5)$ and $(2, -1)$.

Because $(0, 5)$ is on the y-axis, the intercept is $c = 5$. Now compare the two points from left to right to find the gradient.

From $(0, 5)$ to $(2, -1)$, $x$ increases by 2 and $y$ decreases by 6. So
$$
m = \frac{-6}{2} = -3
$$
and the negative sign matches the downward slope.

Substitute $m = -3$ and $c = 5$ into $y = mx + c$. The equation is
$$
y = -3x + 5
$$

## Rearranging before you read the gradient

Sometimes the line is not written as $y = mx + c$. Rearrange first so $y$ is on its own, then you can spot the gradient and intercept.

Start with
$$
3y - 6x = 12
$$
Add $6x$ to both sides to get
$$
3y = 6x + 12
$$

Now divide every term by 3 to get
$$
y = 2x + 4
$$
Compare with $y = mx + c$, so the gradient is 2 and the y-intercept is 4.

## Parallel and horizontal lines

Parallel lines never meet and have the same gradient. So if two lines are parallel, keep the same $m$ value and change $c$ if needed.

For example, $x + 2y = 10$ rearranges to:
$$
y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 5
$$

A parallel line through $(0, 1)$ must have the same gradient, so its equation is:
$$
y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 1
$$

A horizontal line is a special case with gradient 0. Its equation is just $y = c$, such as $y = 4$.

## Quick graph clues

When matching a line to an equation, first look at whether it goes up, goes down, or stays flat. Then check where it crosses the y-axis.

A positive line through the origin matches something like $y = 2x$ because there is no added constant. A flat line crossing at $4$ is $y = 4$.

A negative line crossing at $4$ could be written as $y = 4 - 2x$, which is the same as $y = -2x + 4$. Watch for hidden coefficients too: in $y = 2 - x$, the gradient is $-1$.

## Check yourself

1. For the line $y = 6 - 2x$, which statement is correct?

   - The gradient is $-2$ and the y-intercept is 6 ✔

   - The gradient is 6 and the y-intercept is $-2$

   - The gradient is 2 and the y-intercept is 6

   - The gradient is $-2$ and the y-intercept is $-6$

   _Explanation:_ Rewrite the equation as $y = -2x + 6$. That shows the gradient is $-2$ and the y-intercept is 6. The common wrong answer is C, which spots the 2 next to $x$ but forgets that it is negative.

2. A line has gradient 4 and passes through $(0, -3)$. What is its equation?

   - $$y = 4x - 3$ ✔

   - $$y = -3x + 4$

   - $$y = 4x + 3$

   - $$y = -4x - 3$

   _Explanation:_ In $y = mx + c$, use $m = 4$ and $c = -3$, giving $y = 4x - 3$. The common wrong answer is C, which keeps the correct gradient but changes the sign of the y-intercept. Hint: A point with $x = 0$ tells you the y-intercept.

3. Which equation is parallel to $x + 2y = 10$ and crosses the y-axis at 1?

   - $$y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 1$ ✔

   - $$y = -2x + 1$

   - $$y = \frac{1}{2}x + 1$

   - $$y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 5$

   _Explanation:_ Rearranging $x + 2y = 10$ gives $y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 5$, so any parallel line must keep gradient $-\frac{1}{2}$. The common wrong answer is D, which keeps the old intercept 5 instead of using the new intercept 1. Hint: Rearrange first, then keep the same gradient.
