## Area rules and the main idea

Area is the amount of space inside a 2D shape. It is measured in square units such as $cm^2$ and $m^2$.

For a rectangle, use $A = l \times w$. For a triangle, use $A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$, where the height meets the base at a right angle.

A compound shape is made from two or more simple shapes. Split it into rectangles and triangles you already know, then add joined parts or subtract cut-out parts.

Use the lengths written on the diagram, not a ruler. Many exam diagrams are not drawn accurately.

## Worked example: split and add

![Compound shape split into a rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm and a right-angled triangle with base 4 cm and height 5 cm](../assets/001-1bebe5c1-e451-426e-8964-300dcd1b20c1.jpeg)

Start by drawing a splitting line so each part has a familiar formula. Here the compound shape becomes a rectangle and a right-angled triangle.

Rectangle area: $8 \times 5 = 40$, so the rectangle is $40 \, \text{cm}^2$. Triangle area: $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 5 = 10$, so the triangle is $10 \, \text{cm}^2$.

Add the areas because the two shapes are joined together. Total area $= 40 + 10 = 50 \, \text{cm}^2$.

## Worked example: finding missing lengths in an L-shape

![L-shaped rectilinear shape with given sides 14 cm, 9 cm, 6 cm, 4 cm and missing sides 8 cm and 5 cm, split into two rectangles](../assets/002-22514018-40eb-40ad-b05a-041f55d8f864.jpeg)

Before finding area, work out the missing lengths. In a closed rectilinear shape, the total width across the top matches the total width across the bottom, and the total height up matches the total height down.

Missing width $= 14 - 6 = 8 \, \text{cm}$. Missing lower height $= 9 - 4 = 5 \, \text{cm}$. Now split the shape into two rectangles. Their areas are $6 \times 9 = 54 \, \text{cm}^2$ and $8 \times 5 = 40 \, \text{cm}^2$.

Add the parts to get the total area: $54 + 40 = 94 \, \text{cm}^2$. Do not multiply every label you see; only use the dimensions that belong to each rectangle.

## Subtracting holes and unshaded parts

If a question asks for a shaded region or a hole has been removed, find the large area first. Then subtract the unwanted inner part.

Example: a triangle has base $14 \, \text{cm}$ and height $8 \, \text{cm}$, and inside it is an unshaded rectangle $5 \, \text{cm}$ by $3 \, \text{cm}$. Triangle area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 14 \times 8 = 56 \, \text{cm}^2$, and rectangle area $= 5 \times 3 = 15 \, \text{cm}^2$.

Shaded area $= 56 - 15 = 41 \, \text{cm}^2$. The common mistake is to add because there are two shapes, but one area is being taken away.

## Area with money

The phrase "per square metre" means for each $1 \, \text{m}^2$. In word problems involving money, you should first calculate the total area of the shape and only then multiply by the unit price.

Example: an L-shaped field is split into rectangles $50 \, \text{m} \times 80 \, \text{m}$ and $70 \, \text{m} \times 30 \, \text{m}$. Their areas are $4000 \, \text{m}^2$ and $2100 \, \text{m}^2$, giving a total area of $6100 \, \text{m}^2$.

At £3 per $\text{m}^2$, multiply the total area by the price: $6100 \times 3 = 18,300$. This calculation shows that the entire field is worth £18,300.

The area unit $\text{m}^2$ matches the "per $\text{m}^2$" unit in the price, so the final result is in pounds. Remember not to multiply by the price until after you have found the whole area.

## Check yourself

1. Which measurement should you use as the height in $A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$ for a triangle?

   - The length that meets the base at a right angle ✔

   - The longest side

   - The sloping side

   - Any side on the diagram

   _Explanation:_ The height in the triangle area formula is the perpendicular distance from the base. The common wrong answer is C, but the sloping side is not usually at $90^\circ$ to the base, so it is not the correct height.

2. An L-shape has bottom length 15 cm, left height 10 cm, top length 9 cm, and a vertical drop of 4 cm at the top right. What is its area?

   - $$126 \, \text{cm}^2$ ✔

   - $$150 \, \text{cm}^2$

   - $$114 \, \text{cm}^2$

   - $$96 \, \text{cm}^2$

   _Explanation:_ The missing width is $15 - 9 = 6$ and the lower height is $10 - 4 = 6$, so the two rectangles have areas $9 \times 10 = 90$ and $6 \times 6 = 36$, giving $126 \, \text{cm}^2$. The common wrong answer is B because $15 \times 10$ finds the area of the full outer rectangle and ignores the missing corner.

3. A floor is made from a rectangle $7 \, \text{m}$ by $5 \, \text{m}$ and a triangle with base $3 \, \text{m}$ and height $5 \, \text{m}$. Tiles cost £12 per m². What is the total cost?

   - £510 ✔

   - £420

   - £85

   - £630

   _Explanation:_ The rectangle area is $35 \, \text{m}^2$ and the triangle area is $\frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 5 = 7.5 \, \text{m}^2$, so the total area is $42.5 \, \text{m}^2$. Then $42.5 \times 12 = 510$, so the cost is £510; the common wrong answer is B because it prices only the rectangle and forgets the triangle.
