## Circle parts and key formulas

![Labelled circle showing centre, radius, diameter, circumference, tangent, arc, and sector](../assets/001-2cfbddc8-2fdc-4a02-8b93-ca08649c4f2c.jpeg)

Circles have special parts that appear in questions and diagrams. The centre is the middle point, a radius goes from the centre to the edge, and a diameter goes all the way across through the centre.

A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point, an arc is part of the circumference, and a sector is a slice made by two radii and an arc. These words help you read the diagram before you choose a formula.

The diameter is twice the radius, so $d = 2r$ and $r = \frac{d}{2}$. Circumference is the distance around the edge, while area is the space inside. Circumference uses length units such as cm or m, while area uses square units such as $cm^2$ or $m^2$. The key formulas are shown below.

$$$C = \pi d = 2\pi r$$
$$A = \pi r^2$$

## Finding circumference

Circumference is the perimeter of a circle, so use it when the question says around the edge, around a field, or around a rim. Use $C = \pi d$ when the diameter is given, or $C = 2\pi r$ when the radius is given.

Worked example: a badge has radius 7.3 cm. Substitute into the formula: $$C = 2\pi r = 2 \times \pi \times 7.3 = 14.6\pi \approx 45.8672$$ Rounded to 2 decimal places, the circumference is 45.87 cm.

Application: a circular field with diameter 30 m has circumference $$C = \pi d = 30\pi \approx 94.2478$$ Fencing at £14.50 per metre costs $94.2478 \times 14.50 \approx 1366.59$, so the total is £1366.59.

## Finding area

Area measures the space inside the circle, so it is written in square units. The formula is $A = \pi r^2$, and it needs the radius for the calculation.

Worked example: a circle has diameter 11 m. First find the radius:
$$r = \frac{11}{2} = 5.5$$

Then substitute into the area formula:
$$A = \pi \times 5.5^2 = 30.25\pi \approx 95.0332$$
So the area is 95.0 $m^2$ to 1 decimal place.

The most common mistake is using 11 as the radius and squaring it. Since the diameter is twice the radius, that wrong method makes the area four times too large.

## Semi-circles and perimeter

![Semicircle with radius, diameter, highlighted curved edge, and full perimeter labelled as curved edge plus diameter](../assets/002-e519fc47-9867-4be0-b413-0c21143e9a34.jpeg)

For a part-circle, first decide what fraction of the full circle you have. A semi-circle is half a circle, so its curved edge is half the full circumference, but its perimeter also includes the straight diameter.

Worked example: a semicircle has radius $6$ cm. The curved edge is half the full circumference, so:

$$\text{curved edge} = \pi r = \pi \times 6 = 6\pi$$

The straight edge is the diameter, so it is $2r = 12$ cm. Add both parts to find the total length:

$$\text{perimeter} = 6\pi + 12 \approx 30.85$$

So the perimeter is about $30.85$ cm.

For the area of a semi-circle, you find the full circle area $\pi r^2$ and then halve it. For perimeter, do not simply halve the total circumference of a full circle, because you must also add the diameter to close the shape.

## Shaded and compound areas

Shaded regions and compound shapes are usually solved by adding joined pieces or subtracting cut-out pieces. Draw or label the radii first so you do not mix up radius and diameter.

Example: a ring has large diameter 18 cm and small radius 7 cm. The large radius is 9 cm, and the two circle areas are: $$A_{\text{large}} = \pi \times 9^2 = 81\pi$$ $$A_{\text{small}} = \pi \times 7^2 = 49\pi$$

Subtract the smaller area from the larger one. $$A_{\text{shaded}} = 81\pi - 49\pi = 32\pi \approx 100.5$$ So the shaded area is 100.5 cm², or $32\pi$ cm² in exact form.

## Check yourself

1. A circle has diameter 12 cm. What is its radius?

   - 6 cm ✔

   - 12 cm

   - 24 cm

   - 3 cm

   _Explanation:_ The radius is half the diameter, so $r = \frac{12}{2} = 6$ cm. The most common mistake is choosing 12 cm, which treats the radius and diameter as the same even though the diameter is two radii.

2. A circle has diameter 14 cm. What is its area in terms of $\pi$?

   - $$49\pi$ cm² ✔

   - $$196\pi$ cm²

   - $$14\pi$ cm²

   - $$28\pi$ cm²

   _Explanation:_ You must halve 14 cm to get radius 7 cm, then use $A = \pi r^2 = \pi \times 7^2 = 49\pi$. The most common wrong answer is $196\pi$, which comes from squaring the diameter instead of the radius. Hint: Area formula uses radius, not diameter.

3. A semicircle has area $18\pi \, \text{cm}^2$. What is its perimeter?

   - $$6\pi + 12$ cm ✔

   - $$6\pi$ cm

   - $$12\pi + 12$ cm

   - $$36\pi$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Since the semicircle area is $18\pi$, the full circle area is $36\pi$, so $r^2 = 36$ and $r = 6$. The perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter, giving $6\pi + 12$ cm; the most common wrong answer is $6\pi$ cm, which forgets the straight edge.
