## Brackets mean multiply

![Two-panel algebra diagram showing 3 multiplied by x and 2 to expand 3(x+2), and 8r+20 rewritten as 4(2r+5) by taking out common factor 4](../assets/001-5c2e2368-bce3-4fc3-9b81-ebf7092fb3f6.jpeg)
Brackets show multiplication. In $3(x+2)$, the 3 multiplies both $x$ and $2$, so expanding means removing brackets by multiplying.

Factorising is the reverse process. You look for something every term shares, called a common factor, and put it outside a bracket.

An expression has no equals sign, while an equation does. Like terms have the same variable part, so $5x$ and $2x$ combine but $5x$ and $2x^2$ do not.

## Worked examples: expanding one bracket

To expand one bracket, multiply the outside factor by every term inside. For the expression $6(3x+4)$, we begin by writing out the individual products:
$$6(3x+4)=6\times 3x + 6\times 4$$

Evaluating these multiplications gives the final expression. A common mistake is $18x+4$, which occurs if you forget to multiply the constant term by 6.
$$6(3x+4)=18x+24$$

If the outside factor includes a letter, it still multiplies every term. For the example $3p(p-5)$, we calculate the products for both the variable and constant:
$$3p \times p = 3p^2,\qquad 3p \times (-5)=-15p$$
This results in the expanded form $3p(p-5)=3p^2-15p$.

## Factorising by taking out the HCF

To factorise, find the highest common factor (HCF) of all the terms and place it outside a bracket. It is the reverse of expanding, so you can check by expanding your answer.

For $8r+20$, the HCF is 4. Rewrite the expression as
$$8r+20=4(2r)+4(5)=4(2r+5)$$

For $14a^2b+21ab^2$, the number HCF is 7 and both terms contain $ab$. Since
$$14a^2b \div 7ab = 2a,\qquad 21ab^2 \div 7ab = 3b$$

The fully factorised form is
$$7ab(2a+3b)$$
This is fully factorised because nothing else is common inside the bracket.

## Expand, then simplify

When there is more than one bracket, expand each bracket first and then collect like terms. A minus sign before a bracket changes the sign of every term inside that bracket.

For example, consider an expression with subtraction:
$$4(2y-3)-3(y+5)=8y-12-3y-15$$
Then collect like terms to find the final result:
$$8y-3y-12-15=5y-27$$

You can apply the same method to expressions with addition:
$$5(s+3)+2(s-4)=5s+15+2s-8=7s+7$$
Always follow the order: expand first, simplify second.

## Solving equations with brackets

When solving an equation with brackets, think about inverse operations. If the whole bracket is multiplied by a number, dividing both sides first is usually quicker than expanding the brackets.

For $6(d+2)=48$, divide both sides by 6 to get
$$d+2=8$$
Then subtract 2 from both sides to find the solution:
$$d=6$$

For $2(v-6)=15$, divide by 2 to get
$$v-6=7.5$$
Then add 6 to both sides to get $v=13.5$. You should always check your final answer by substituting it back into the original equation.

## Check yourself

1. Which expansion is correct for $4(x+3)$?

   - $$4x+3$

   - $$4x+12$ ✔

   - $$7x$

   - $$12x$

   _Explanation:_ $$4$ multiplies both terms, so $4\times x=4x$ and $4\times 3=12$. The common wrong answer $4x+3$ forgets to multiply the constant by 4.

2. Which is the fully factorised form of $15x^2y - 20xy^2$?

   - $$5xy(3x-4y)$ ✔

   - $$5x(3xy-4y^2)$

   - $$5y(3x^2-4xy)$

   - $$5(3x^2y-4xy^2)$

   _Explanation:_ Both terms share $5xy$, so taking out the full common factor leaves $3x-4y$. The common wrong answer $5x(3xy-4y^2)$ only takes out $5x$, so it is not fully factorised because a $y$ is still common inside the bracket.

3. What is the best first step to solve $3(k-4)=21$?

   - Subtract 4 from both sides

   - Divide both sides by 3 ✔

   - Expand to $3k-12=21$

   - Add 4 to both sides

   _Explanation:_ The whole bracket is multiplied by 3, so dividing both sides by 3 isolates the bracket straight away. The common wrong answer "subtract 4" is wrong because the 4 is inside the bracket and has not been separated from the multiplication yet.
