## What the question is asking

A calculator is only as good as the input you type. In GCSE questions, you need to enter the expression exactly as written, then decide whether to copy the display or round.

If a question says "write down all the figures on your calculator display", do not round or drop zeros. If your calculator shows a fraction but the question wants display figures, use the decimal-change key such as S-D or S⇔D.

For example, $4.7+2.35=7.05$, so you must write $7.05$ exactly. The zero matters because it shows the hundredths digit.

## Roots, powers and brackets

![Comparison showing a squared bracket, a grouped fraction with numerator and denominator labelled, and a square root covering a whole fraction](../assets/001-89fbc3de-7739-49f9-bb2f-42bd3da7d5ee.jpeg)

Square roots, powers and brackets tell the calculator what belongs together. If a whole bracket is being squared, the brackets must be typed before the square.

Work out $(3.20-0.45)^2-5.18$ by entering it exactly as written. The calculator does brackets first, then powers, then the final subtraction. The steps are:

$$$3.20-0.45=2.75$$
$$2.75^2=7.5625$$
$$7.5625-5.18=2.3825$$

A common mistake is typing $3.20-0.45^2$, which squares only $0.45$.

## Fractions and the whole root

For longer fractions, use the fraction template if your calculator has one. Without it, put the whole numerator in brackets before dividing by the denominator.

For $$\frac{3.18+2.6^2}{2.07}$$ work out the top first: $3.18+2.6^2=9.94$. Then divide by $2.07$ to get $$\frac{9.94}{2.07}=4.801932367\ldots$$

If the square root covers a whole fraction, do the division before pressing square root. In $$\sqrt{\frac{18.6-2.4}{4.1+2.2}}$$ the root applies to both the numerator and the denominator, not just the top line.

## Rounding correctly

Only round at the end unless the question says to copy all the figures. Decimal places count digits after the decimal point, while significant figures start at the first non-zero digit.

If the display is $1.6043$, then to 2 decimal places you keep $1.60$ because the third decimal digit is 4. To 2 significant figures you keep 1 and 6, then look at the next digit 0, so the answer is $1.6$.

Keep a final zero when it shows the required accuracy. Writing $1.60$ tells the examiner you rounded to 2 decimal places, but $1.6$ does not.

## Check DEG for trigonometry

Before using $\sin$, $\cos$ or $\tan$ with degree angles, check the calculator is in DEG mode. If it shows RAD, the value will be wrong for a GCSE degrees question.

For $$\frac{\sin 30^\circ+\cos 60^\circ}{\cos 30^\circ-\sin 20^\circ}$$ a typical display is $1.908377034$. Rounded to 2 decimal places, the answer is $1.91$.

Type the whole fraction carefully, using a fraction template or brackets for the top and bottom. Round only after you have the full display.

## Check yourself

1. Your display shows 7.05 and the question says "write down all the figures on your calculator display". What should you write?

   - 7.05 ✔

   - 7.5

   - 7.1

   - 7.050

   _Explanation:_ $$7.05$ is correct because you copy the display exactly without rounding or changing digits. A common error is $7.5$, but that drops the zero and changes the value. Keep every digit shown on the screen, but do not add extra ones.

2. Which entry matches $\sqrt{\frac{18.6-2.4}{4.1+2.2}}$ on a calculator without a fraction key?

   - $$\sqrt{((18.6-2.4)/(4.1+2.2))}$ ✔

   - $$\frac{\sqrt{18.6-2.4}}{4.1+2.2}$

   - $$\sqrt{18.6-2.4} \div (4.1+2.2)$

   - $$(18.6-2.4) \div \sqrt{4.1+2.2}$

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because the brackets keep the whole fraction inside the square root. A common wrong answer is C, but there the calculator square-roots only $18.6-2.4$ and then divides by the denominator. Hint: Ask yourself what the root bar is covering.

3. Before calculating $\tan 35^\circ$, which calculator mode should you check?

   - DEG ✔

   - RAD

   - SCI

   - FIX

   _Explanation:_ DEG is correct because the angle is given in degrees, which is the usual GCSE unit. A common wrong choice is RAD, but radians mode makes $\sin$, $\cos$ and $\tan$ use a different angle scale and gives the wrong answer.
