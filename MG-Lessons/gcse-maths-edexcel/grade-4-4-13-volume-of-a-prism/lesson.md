## Prism basics

![Prism with one repeated end face labelled cross-section and an arrow labelled length of prism.](../assets/001-27927a05-707d-4df8-82da-768cbf857941.jpeg)

A prism has the same 2D shape all the way through, so the repeated face is called the cross-section. The distance it extends is the length or depth of the prism.

To find the volume, first work out the area of the cross-section, then multiply by the prism length. Use $V = A \times L$, and write the answer in cubic units such as $\text{cm}^{3}$ or $\text{m}^{3}$.

## Worked example: rectangular prism

A prism has a rectangular cross-section measuring $6\,\text{cm}$ by $5\,\text{cm}$ and length $8\,\text{cm}$. First, find the cross-section area of the rectangular face.

$$$A = 6 \times 5 = 30\,\text{cm}^{2}$$

The repeated face has an area of $30\,\text{cm}^{2}$. Now, multiply this area by the prism's length to calculate the total capacity.

$$$V = 30 \times 8 = 240\,\text{cm}^{3}$$

The final volume is $240\,\text{cm}^{3}$. Multiplying area by length turns square units into cubic units.

## Different cross-sections

![Three prisms side by side: a triangular prism with base, perpendicular height and length labelled; a trapezium prism with both parallel sides, perpendicular height and length labelled; and an L-shaped prism split into rectangles with dimensions labelled.](../assets/002-09a74123-4f93-440d-9e20-4e0b59ccb4d7.jpeg)

Different prisms all use the same volume rule, but the area formula changes with the cross-section. Triangle, trapezium and composite cross-sections are all solved by finding area first.

For a triangle, use $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$, where $h$ is the perpendicular height. Do not use the sloping side unless it is perpendicular to the base.

For a trapezium, use $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$, where $a$ and $b$ are the parallel sides. For an L-shaped cross-section, split the shape into rectangles, add their areas, then multiply by the prism length.

## Worked example: L-shaped prism

An L-shaped cross-section can be split into two rectangles measuring $5 \, \text{cm}$ by $2 \, \text{cm}$ and $3 \, \text{cm}$ by $4 \, \text{cm}$. The prism is $10 \, \text{cm}$ deep. Find each rectangle area first.

$$$A_1 = 5 \times 2 = 10 \, \text{cm}^{2},\qquad A_2 = 3 \times 4 = 12 \, \text{cm}^{2}$$

Add them to get the full cross-section area: $A = 10 + 12 = 22 \, \text{cm}^{2}$. Now multiply by the prism depth.

$$$V = 22 \times 10 = 220 \, \text{cm}^{3}$$

The volume is $220 \, \text{cm}^{3}$. Splitting the cross-section first prevents double-counting.

## Working backwards to find a missing length

Sometimes the volume is given and one dimension is missing. Write the full volume formula first so you do not forget a factor such as $\frac{1}{2}$.

A triangular prism has base $7 \, \text{cm}$, height $x \, \text{cm}$, length $12 \, \text{cm}$ and volume $252 \, \text{cm}^{3}$. The unknown is part of the triangle area.

$$$\frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times x \times 12 = 252$$

Simplify and solve: $$42x = 252$$ $$x = \frac{252}{42} = 6 \, \text{cm}$$ The missing height is $6 \, \text{cm}$.

## Capacity, litres and rounding

For containers, volume often becomes capacity. Remember $1 \, \text{ml} = 1 \, \text{cm}^{3}$ and $1 \, \text{L} = 1000 \, \text{cm}^{3}$.

A rectangular container measures $70 \, \text{cm}$ by $50 \, \text{cm}$ by $40 \, \text{cm}$. First find the volume in cubic centimetres, then convert it to litres.

$$$V = 70 \times 50 \times 40 = 140000 \, \text{cm}^{3} = 140 \, \text{L}$$

If each bucket holds $12 \, \text{L}$, then $140 \div 12 = 11.66\ldots$. You need 12 buckets because filling questions round up, while "how many full boxes fit" questions round down.

## Check yourself

1. A prism has cross-section area $12 \, \text{cm}^{2}$ and length $9 \, \text{cm}$. Which calculation gives its volume?

   - $$12 \times 9$ ✔

   - $$12 + 9$

   - $$12 \div 9$

   - $$2(12+9)$

   _Explanation:_ Volume of a prism is area of cross-section multiplied by length, so the correct calculation is $12 \times 9$. A common mistake is $12+9$, but area and length are different kinds of measurement, so adding them does not give a volume.

2. A triangular prism has a right-angled triangular cross-section with perpendicular sides $6 \, \text{cm}$ and $8 \, \text{cm}$. The prism is $9 \, \text{cm}$ long. What is its volume?

   - $$216 \, \text{cm}^{3}$ ✔

   - $$432 \, \text{cm}^{3}$

   - $$126 \, \text{cm}^{3}$

   - $$270 \, \text{cm}^{3}$

   _Explanation:_ The triangle area is $A = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = 24 \, \text{cm}^{2}$, then $24 \times 9 = 216 \, \text{cm}^{3}$. A common mistake is $432 \, \text{cm}^{3}$, which comes from forgetting the $\frac{1}{2}$ in the triangle area formula.

3. A triangular prism has a triangular cross-section with base $9$ cm and height $x$ cm, and the prism is $8$ cm long. Its volume is $180$ cubic centimeters. What is $x$?

   - $$2.5$ cm

   - $$4$ cm

   - $$5$ cm ✔

   - $$10$ cm

   _Explanation:_ The triangular cross-section area is half of $9x$, so it is $4.5x$. Multiplying by the prism length $8$ gives a volume of $36x$. Since $36x = 180$, $x = 5$ cm. A common mistake is $2.5$ cm, which comes from forgetting to halve the triangle's area and using $72x = 180$ instead.

4. A tank has volume $54000 \, \text{cm}^{3}$. Each jug holds $8 \, \text{L}$. How many jugs are needed to fill the tank?

   - 6

   - 6.75

   - 7 ✔

   - 8

   _Explanation:_ First convert $54000 \, \text{cm}^{3}$ to litres: $54000 \div 1000 = 54 \, \text{L}$. Then $54 \div 8 = 6.75$, so you need 7 whole jugs; a common mistake is 6, but rounding down would not fill the tank.
