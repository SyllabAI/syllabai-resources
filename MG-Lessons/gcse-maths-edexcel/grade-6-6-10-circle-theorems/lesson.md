## Spot the clues

Circle theorem questions are really about spotting structure in a diagram. Look for radii, chords, tangents, and shapes with all vertices on the circle.

The main angle facts come from three clues: equal radii, a tangent touching the circle, and angles standing on the same chord or arc. Start by marking these clues on the diagram before you calculate anything.

## Equal radii make isosceles triangles

All radii in the same circle are equal. So if $OA$ and $OB$ are radii, triangle $AOB$ is isosceles and its base angles are equal.

Worked example: if $\angle ABO = 42^\circ$, then $\angle OAB = 42^\circ$ because $OA = OB$. Angles in triangle $AOB$ must add to $180^\circ$.

$$$\angle AOB = 180^\circ - 42^\circ - 42^\circ = 96^\circ$$

Hidden isosceles triangles are often the first step in a longer circle theorem question. Identifying these equal sides is key to unlocking more complex geometry problems.

## Tangent and radius

A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point. The radius to that point is perpendicular to the tangent, so the angle there is $90^\circ$.

Worked example: $AB$ and $CB$ are tangents to a circle with centre $O$, and $\angle ABC = 50^\circ$. In quadrilateral $AOCB$, the two right angles give $\angle AOC = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 50^\circ = 130^\circ$.

Now $OA = OC$ because both are radii, so triangle $AOC$ is isosceles. Each base angle is $\frac{180^\circ - 130^\circ}{2} = 25^\circ$, so $\angle OAC = 25^\circ$.

## Centre angle and circumference angle

![Circle with centre O, chord BC, central angle BOC of 74° and angle BAC of 37° at the circumference standing on the same chord](../assets/001-94174955-181c-453a-9ea1-4eaa9f7fdb28.jpeg)

When two angles stand on the same chord, the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. In the diagram, both angles stand on chord $BC$.

If $\angle BOC = 74^\circ$, then $\angle BAC = \frac{74^\circ}{2} = 37^\circ$. If you know the circumference angle instead, double it to get the centre angle.

Check carefully whether the diagram shows the smaller centre angle or a reflex angle. Use the angle on the correct arc before you halve or double.

## Same segment and cyclic quadrilaterals

Two angles in the same segment stand on the same chord, so they are equal. A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle, and its opposite angles add to $180^\circ$.

Worked example: if $\angle ADB = 47^\circ$ and points $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ lie on the same circle, then $\angle ACB = 47^\circ$ because both angles stand on chord $AB$.

If $\angle ADC = 78^\circ$, then the opposite angle in cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$ is $\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 78^\circ = 102^\circ$. These two facts often appear together in multi-step questions.

## Alternate segment theorem and tangent lengths

![Circle with tangent at C, radius OC at right angles to the tangent, chord CB, angle x between radius OC and chord CB, and angle BAC in the opposite segment](../assets/002-8c3e1f40-7ffc-46f6-a5c0-7eecfe2bb0dd.jpeg)
The alternate segment theorem says that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the opposite segment. Match the chord carefully: if the tangent angle uses chord $CB$, look for the angle on the circumference in the opposite segment made by chord $CB$. In this diagram, the labelled angle $x$ is between $OC$ and $CB$, so the tangent-chord angle and $x$ add to $90^\circ$.

Example: in triangle $ABC$, $\angle ABC = 58^\circ$ and $\angle ACB = 71^\circ$, so $\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 58^\circ - 71^\circ = 51^\circ$. By the alternate segment theorem, the angle between the tangent at $C$ and chord $CB$ is also $51^\circ$. Since $OC$ is perpendicular to the tangent, the labelled angle is $x = 90^\circ - 51^\circ = 39^\circ$.

Tangents can also lead to length questions because a radius and tangent make a right-angled triangle. If $OA = 6$ cm, $AC = 8$ cm, and $O$, $B$, $C$ are on a straight line, then triangle $OAC$ is right-angled and
$$OC^2 = 6^2 + 8^2 = 100$$
so $OC = 10$ cm.

Since $OB = 6$ cm is also a radius, $BC = 10 - 6 = 4$ cm. Always check whether the question wants the whole sloping side or just the leftover section.

## Check yourself

1. A and B lie on a circle with centre O. If OA and OB are radii and $\angle ABO = 35^\circ$, what is $\angle AOB$?

   - $$110^\circ$ ✔

   - $$70^\circ$

   - $$145^\circ$

   - $$35^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ $$OA = OB$, so triangle $AOB$ is isosceles and $\angle OAB = 35^\circ$ too. Then $\angle AOB = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 35^\circ = 110^\circ$; the common wrong answer $70^\circ$ just doubles the base angle and forgets that all three angles in the triangle must add to $180^\circ$.

2. Angles $BOC$ and $BAC$ stand on the same chord $BC$. If $\angle BOC = 124^\circ$, what is $\angle BAC$?

   - $$62^\circ$ ✔

   - $$124^\circ$

   - $$248^\circ$

   - $$56^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same chord, so $\angle BAC = \frac{124^\circ}{2} = 62^\circ$. The common wrong answer $124^\circ$ treats the two angles as equal, but the centre angle should be the larger one.

3. A tangent touches the circle at C. In triangle ABC, $\angle ABC = 53^\circ$ and $\angle ACB = 64^\circ$. What is the angle between the tangent at C and chord CB?

   - $$63^\circ$ ✔

   - $$53^\circ$

   - $$64^\circ$

   - $$117^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ First find the third angle: $\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 53^\circ - 64^\circ = 63^\circ$. By the alternate segment theorem, the angle between the tangent and chord $CB$ equals the angle in the opposite segment on chord $CB$, so $63^\circ$ is correct; $53^\circ$ is the common wrong choice because it picks a known angle without matching the chord.

4. AC is a tangent at A. O is the centre, and O, B, C lie on a straight line. OA = 5 cm and AC = 12 cm. What is BC?

   - 8 cm ✔

   - 13 cm

   - 17 cm

   - 7 cm

   _Explanation:_ Triangle $OAC$ is right-angled at A, so $OC = 13$ cm by Pythagoras because $5^2 + 12^2 = 13^2$. Then $OB = 5$ cm as a radius, so $BC = 13 - 5 = 8$ cm; 13 cm is the common wrong answer because it is $OC$, not the required segment $BC$.
