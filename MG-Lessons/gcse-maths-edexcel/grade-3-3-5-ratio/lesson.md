## What a ratio means

![Bar model showing girls:boys = 3:2 as five equal boxes, three labelled girls and two labelled boys](../assets/001-86dc39df-c8a5-4f1a-b4c5-1903c4e7ea88.jpeg)

A ratio compares quantities using equal parts. The ratio $3:2$ means 3 equal parts of the first amount for every 2 equal parts of the second amount.

Order matters. If girls : boys = $3:2$, the girls get the 3 parts and the boys get the 2 parts, so there are $3+2=5$ parts altogether.

You will use the same idea for lengths, money, sweets and mixtures. Find one part first, then multiply to get the amounts you need.

## Sharing a total in a ratio

To share a total, add the ratio parts first. In $4:3:2$, there are $4+3+2=9$ equal parts altogether, rather than just three individual people.

Ari, Beth and Cleo share 63 sweets in the ratio $4:3:2$. To find the value of one single part, divide the total amount by the sum of the ratio numbers:
$$63 \div 9 = 7$$

Now multiply by each ratio number to find each individual share. This keeps the proportions correct:
$$\text{Ari}=4\times 7=28,\quad \text{Beth}=3\times 7=21,\quad \text{Cleo}=2\times 7=14$$

A common mistake is dividing by the number of people involved, which would be 3 in this example. The correct divisor is always the total number of parts, which is 9.

## When you are given a share or a difference

![Stacked bars showing Harper has 3 equal parts and Imani has 8 equal parts, with the extra 5 parts on Imani's bar labelled 30 more badges](../assets/002-491cab3b-b6a1-4787-881a-aee61c2b1934.jpeg)

Sometimes the total is not given. Match the known amount to the correct ratio part, or use the difference between parts if the question says "more than".

If Ellen and Faisal share money in the ratio $2:5$ and Faisal gets £85, then 5 parts are worth £85. One part is $85 \div 5 = 17$, so Ellen gets $2 \times 17 = 34$ pounds.

If Harper and Imani have badges in the ratio $3:8$ and Imani has 30 more, then the extra 30 badges come from the difference in parts, not from the 8 parts. Since $8-3=5$, one part is $30 \div 5 = 6$ and Imani has $8 \times 6 = 48$ badges.

## Mixtures and remaining amounts

Ratios also appear in recipes and mixtures. If paint is mixed red : yellow : white = $5:4:1$ and the total needed is 600 ml, then there are 10 parts so one part is $600 \div 10 = 60$ ml.

The amounts needed are $5\times 60=300$ ml red, $4\times 60=240$ ml yellow and $1\times 60=60$ ml white. This lets you check quickly whether the ingredients you have are enough.

Sometimes you only split what is left. If 160 counters have 25% red and $\frac{1}{4}$ blue, then 80 counters remain, and those 80 are split in the ratio $3:1$.

There are 4 parts in $3:1$, so one part is $80 \div 4 = 20$. Yellow gets 3 parts, so yellow has $3\times 20=60$ counters.

## Stretch: greatest amount you can make

For 'greatest amount' questions, find the one-part value each ingredient could provide. The smallest one-part value tells you which ingredient will run out first.

Example: red : yellow : white = $5:4:1$ with 310 ml red, 250 ml yellow and 70 ml white. The one-part values are:

$$$310 \div 5 = 62,\quad 250 \div 4 = 62.5,\quad 70 \div 1 = 70$$

The smallest one-part value is 62, so red is the limiting ingredient. A full batch has 10 parts, so the greatest total mixture is $10 \times 62 = 620$ ml.

## Check yourself

1. In the ratio girls : boys = $3:2$, which statement is correct?

   - There are exactly 3 girls and 2 boys

   - For every 3 boys, there are 2 girls

   - For every 3 equal parts of girls, there are 2 equal parts of boys ✔

   - There are 5 girls altogether

   _Explanation:_ C is correct because a ratio compares equal parts and keeps the order shown. The common wrong answer B reverses the order, so it describes boys : girls = $3:2$ instead.

2. Harper and Imani have badges in the ratio $3:8$. Imani has 30 more badges than Harper. How many badges does Imani have?

   - 48 ✔

   - 30

   - 38

   - 240

   _Explanation:_ The difference in the ratio is $8-3=5$ parts, so 5 parts represent 30 and one part is 6. Imani has 8 parts, so $8\times 6=48$. The common wrong answer D comes from multiplying 30 by 8 without first finding one part.

3. Red : yellow : white = $5:4:1$. You have 150 ml red, 132 ml yellow and 40 ml white. What is the greatest total amount of paint you can make?

   - 300 ml ✔

   - 330 ml

   - 400 ml

   - 132 ml

   _Explanation:_ Red gives $150 \div 5 = 30$, yellow gives $132 \div 4 = 33$, and white gives $40 \div 1 = 40$. The smallest one-part value is 30, so the largest full batch is $10 \times 30 = 300$ ml. The common wrong answer B uses yellow's value of 33, but that would need $5 \times 33 = 165$ ml of red, which you do not have.
