## What an exchange rate tells you

![Flowchart showing when to multiply or divide in exchange-rate questions, with branches for rates written as £1 = foreign currency and foreign currency 1 = pounds](../assets/001-97406039-bde7-42be-8314-a886de7c0b7b.jpeg)

A currency is the money used in a country, such as pounds, euros, or yen. An exchange rate tells you how much one currency is worth in another, for example £1 = €1.18.

The key question is always: what currency do you have, and what currency do you want? If £1 buys $r$ units of a foreign currency, then

$$$\text{foreign amount} = \text{pounds} \times r \qquad \text{and} \qquad \text{pounds} = \text{foreign amount} \div r$$

If you move in the same direction as the rate, multiply. If you are working backwards, divide.

## Worked example: pounds into euros

The rate $£1 = €1.18$ means each pound gives 1.18 euros. So to change $£265$ into euros, multiply 265 by 1.18.

Think of 1.18 as "euros per pound", so the pounds cancel when you multiply. That gives the amount in euros.

$$$265 \times 1.18 = 312.70$$

The traveller receives $€312.70$. A good check is that the answer should be bigger than 265, because each pound is worth more than 1 euro here.

## Worked example: euros back into pounds

A tourist has €80 left and the rate is $£1 = €1.18$. Because the rate tells us euros for 1 pound, changing euros back into pounds means working backwards, so divide by 1.18.

$$$80 \div 1.18 = 67.796\dots$$

Round to the nearest penny because pounds are written to two decimal places. The tourist receives $£67.80$. A good check is that the pound amount should be less than 80, because 1 pound is worth more than 1 euro at this rate.

## When the rate is written the other way round

Sometimes the exchange rate starts with the foreign currency, for example $€1 = £0.87$. This means 1 euro is worth 87 pence, so if you have euros and want pounds, you multiply by 0.87.

For a meal costing €32, you can use this rate directly. This step converts the currency into pounds by applying the multiplier to the euro amount.

$$$32 \times 0.87 = 27.84$$

The meal costs £27.84. If you started with pounds and wanted euros from this rate, you would divide by 0.87 instead.

## Multi-step questions: find the total first

Some questions hide the exchange inside another calculation. If a hotel costs ¥9000 per night for 5 nights, first find the total in yen because "per" means "for each".

First calculate the total cost.
$$9000 \times 5 = 45000$$
This is the number of yen for all 5 nights.

Now use the exchange rate £1 = ¥150 and work backwards into pounds.
$$45000 \div 150 = 300$$
This gives the price in pounds.

The hotel costs £300. Always do the total-first step before converting.

## Comparing prices in different countries

To compare two prices, put them in the same currency first. Usually it is easiest to convert the foreign price into pounds and then compare with the UK price.

Trainers cost 3200 rupees in India and £45 in the UK, with rate £1 = 80 rupees. Convert the Indian price first: $$3200 \div 80 = 40$$

So the trainers cost £40 in India. Now compare £40 and £45, then find the difference: $$45 - 40 = 5$$

The trainers are £5 cheaper in India. You must compare like with like, not rupees directly with pounds.

## Choosing the better deal

Sometimes two exchange rates are written in different directions. If you want to change pounds into euros, compare how many euros you get for £1.

City A gives £1 = €1.12. City B gives €1 = £0.88, so convert that to euros per pound:

$$$1 \div 0.88 = 1.136\ldots$$

City B gives about €1.14 for each pound, so it is better than City A. In greatest-number-of-notes questions, round down because you cannot have part of a note.

## Check yourself

1. The exchange rate is $£1 = €1.20$. What do you do to change $£80$ into euros?

   - Multiply by $1.20$ ✔

   - Divide by $1.20$

   - Multiply by $80$

   - Divide by $80$

   _Explanation:_ The rate gives euros for each pound, so changing pounds into euros means multiply by $1.20$. The most common wrong answer is B, but division is for changing euros back into pounds when the rate is written as $£1 = €1.20$.

2. A tourist has €80 left. The exchange rate is £1 = €1.18. How many pounds do they receive, to the nearest penny?

   - £67.80 ✔

   - £94.40

   - £67.79

   - £68.80

   _Explanation:_ You divide by 1.18 because you are changing euros back into pounds, and $80 \div 1.18 = 67.796\dots$, which rounds to £67.80. The most common wrong answer is B, which comes from multiplying instead of dividing.

3. A museum ticket costs 2400 yen per person for 3 people. The exchange rate is £1 = ¥150. What is the total cost in pounds?

   - £16

   - £48 ✔

   - £7200

   - £50

   _Explanation:_ First find the total yen: $2400 \times 3 = 7200$. Then divide by 150 to change yen into pounds, giving £48. The most common wrong answer is A, which forgets to include all 3 people.

4. In City A the rate is $£1 = €1.12$. In City B the rate is $€1 = £0.88$. A traveller wants to change pounds into euros. Which city is better?

   - City A, because $1.12$ is bigger than $0.88$

   - City B, because it gives about $€1.14$ for each $£1$ ✔

   - They are the same

   - Not enough information

   _Explanation:_ For pounds into euros, both rates must be compared as euros per $£1$. City A gives $€1.12$ per $£1$, while City B gives about $€1.14$ per $£1$ (calculated as $1 \div 0.88 \approx 1.136$), so City B is better. The most common wrong answer is A, which compares $1.12$ and $0.88$ without noticing they are in different directions.
