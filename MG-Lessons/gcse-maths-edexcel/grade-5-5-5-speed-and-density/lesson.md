## Per means divide

![Two formula triangles showing distance-speed-time and mass-density-volume with units](../assets/001-3cfa86fc-8045-44f2-8776-682cea51a8f7.jpeg)
The word "per" means divide. So average speed is distance per time, and density is mass per volume.

You must also match the units. Speed is often in $\text{m/s}$ or mph, while density might be in $\text{g/cm}^3$ or $\text{kg/m}^3$.

The key formulas are below. Pressure has the same divide pattern, even though today we will focus on speed and density.
$$\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$
$$\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$$
$$\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$$

## Rearranging the formulas

If a formula looks like top divided by bottom, the top quantity comes from multiplying and a bottom quantity comes from dividing. This is the same idea as the cover-up method: cover the missing quantity and see whether you need multiplication or division.

For speed, this gives $\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}$ and $\text{time} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$. For density, it gives $\text{mass} = \text{density} \times \text{volume}$ and $\text{volume} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{density}}$.

Pressure follows the same pattern with $\text{force} = \text{pressure} \times \text{area}$ and $\text{area} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{pressure}}$. A quick check helps: if the answer is volume, time or area, it is usually a bottom quantity, so divide, but if the answer is distance, mass or force, it is usually the top quantity, so multiply.

## Worked example with time conversion

For speed in mph, use miles and hours. For speed in m/s, use metres and seconds. Suppose a car travels $310$ miles in $5$ hours $40$ minutes; to find the speed, you must convert the time first.

$$$5 + \frac{40}{60} = 5.666\ldots$$ Then calculate the speed by dividing the distance by the decimal time: $$\frac{310}{5.666\ldots} = 54.705\ldots$$

So the average speed is $54.7$ mph to 1 decimal place. Do not write $5$ hours $40$ minutes as $5.40$ hours, because minutes are parts of $60$, not parts of $100$. Miles divided by hours gives mph, so the units make sense.

## Whole journeys use totals

For a whole journey, average speed is total distance divided by total time. Do not usually add the speeds and divide by $2$, because the parts of the journey may last different lengths of time.

A van travels $150$ miles at $60$ mph, then $90$ miles in $2$ hours $15$ minutes. The first part takes $$\frac{150}{60} = 2.5 \, \text{h}$$ and the second part is $$2 + \frac{15}{60} = 2.25 \, \text{h}$$.

Now add the totals: distance $= 150 + 90 = 240$ miles and time $= 2.5 + 2.25 = 4.75$ hours. So $$\text{average speed} = \frac{240}{4.75} = 50.526\ldots$$ which is about $50.5 \, \text{mph}$.

## Density of a mixture

In mixture questions, keep mass and volume separate. Do not add densities directly, because density is only found after you know the total mass and total volume.

Start by finding any missing total mass or total volume. Suppose Liquid A has volume $120 \, \text{cm}^3$ and density $1.25 \, \text{g/cm}^3$, while Liquid C has total mass $240 \, \text{g}$ and density $1.2 \, \text{g/cm}^3$.

$$$\text{volume of C} = \frac{240}{1.2} = 200 \, \text{cm}^3$$ $$\text{mass of A} = 1.25 \times 120 = 150 \, \text{g}$$

That leaves Liquid B with volume $200 - 120 = 80 \, \text{cm}^3$ and mass $240 - 150 = 90 \, \text{g}$. Its density is: $$\text{density of B} = \frac{90}{80} = 1.125 \, \text{g/cm}^3$$

## Check yourself

1. A metal object has mass $630 \, \text{g}$ and volume $35 \, \text{cm}^3$. What is its density?

   - $$18 \, \text{g/cm}^3$ ✔

   - $$0.0556 \, \text{g/cm}^3$

   - $$18 \, \text{cm}^3$

   - $$665 \, \text{g/cm}^3$

   _Explanation:_ Density is mass divided by volume, so $630 \div 35 = 18$, giving $18 \, \text{g/cm}^3$. The common wrong answer B comes from reversing the division, and C shows the unit mistake of giving a volume unit instead of a density unit.

2. Which is the correct conversion of $4$ hours $15$ minutes into hours?

   - $$4.15$

   - $$4.25$ ✔

   - $$4.50$

   - $$4.75$

   _Explanation:_ $$15$ minutes is $\frac{15}{60} = 0.25$ hours, so the total is $4.25$ hours. The common wrong answer A treats minutes like hundredths of an hour, but $1$ hour is $60$ minutes, not $100$.

3. Liquid A has volume $120 \, \text{cm}^3$ and density $1.25 \, \text{g/cm}^3$. It is mixed to make Liquid C with total mass $240 \, \text{g}$ and density $1.2 \, \text{g/cm}^3$. What is the density of Liquid B?

   - $$1.125 \, \text{g/cm}^3$ ✔

   - $$1.2 \, \text{g/cm}^3$

   - $$2.45 \, \text{g/cm}^3$

   - $$0.75 \, \text{g/cm}^3$

   _Explanation:_ First find the total volume of C: $240 \div 1.2 = 200 \, \text{cm}^3$. Then A has mass $1.25 \times 120 = 150 \, \text{g}$, so B has volume $80 \, \text{cm}^3$ and mass $90 \, \text{g}$, giving $90 \div 80 = 1.125 \, \text{g/cm}^3$. The common wrong answer B just copies the mixture density, and C comes from adding densities, which is not valid.
