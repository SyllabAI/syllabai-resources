## Reading the question and choosing the operation

![Flowchart of the steps for solving calculation word problems: underline numbers and units, make units match, choose the operation, then give the answer with the correct unit](../assets/001-b9f3bf66-1aa0-4f24-b99a-ebaaa00470e7.jpeg)

Calculation problems are word questions where you must decide which operation fits the story. Clues such as total, change, each, groups, left over, and enough help you choose between add, subtract, multiply, and divide.

A good routine is to underline the important numbers and units, make the units match, then calculate. Finally, write a sentence with the correct unit and check that your answer makes sense in the real situation.

In money questions, converting to pence first often avoids mistakes. In measure questions, you may need to change litres to millilitres or kilograms to grams before you start.

## Money problems, total cost, and change

Money questions are usually easier if you work in pence from the start. This stops you mixing pounds and pence in the same calculation.

Suppose Aisha buys 7 cakes at 62p each and pays with £10. Convert £10 to 1000p, then find the total cost:
$$7 \times 62 = 434$$
So the cakes cost 434p.

Now subtract this total from the amount paid:
$$1000 - 434 = 566$$
Since 566p = £5.66, her change is £5.66.

This answer is sensible because 7 cakes at about 60p each would cost a little over £4. So the change from £10 should be a little under £6.

## As many as possible and enough for

When a question says "as many as possible", divide the amount you have by the amount needed for one item. The whole number answer tells you how many full items you can buy, make, or cut.

For 1600 ml of water and cups of 180 ml, calculate: $$1600 \div 180 = 8 \text{ remainder } 160$$ Because both amounts are in ml, the division gives a number of cups, so only 8 full cups can be made.

If the question asks for enough packs, boxes, or buses, round up instead. Needing 900 g from packs of 600 g gives $900 \div 600 = 1.5$, so 2 packs are needed.

## Group prices and unit cost

A group price tells you the cost of several identical items together, such as "5 pens for 80p". First work out how many groups are needed, then multiply by the group price.

For 20 pens sold 5 for 80p, there are $20 \div 5 = 4$ groups, so the pens cost $4 \times 80 = 320$ pence. For 30 rulers sold 10 for £2.40, convert £2.40 to 240p, then use $30 \div 10 = 3$ and $3 \times 240 = 720$ pence.

For 5 calculators at £4.50 each, convert £4.50 to 450p and calculate $5 \times 450 = 2250$. The total is $320 + 720 + 2250 = 3290$, so the bill is £32.90.

To find the cost of one item, divide instead of multiply. For example, if 4 pens cost 120p, then 1 pen costs $120 \div 4 = 30$ pence.

## Deposits, instalments, and budgets

A deposit is money paid at the start, and an instalment is one of the equal payments made later. For a bike costing £540 with a £120 deposit, subtract the deposit first to find the remaining balance:
$$540 - 120 = 420$$

Now divide the remaining amount by the number of payments to find the value of each instalment. In this example, if there are 12 monthly payments:
$$420 \div 12 = 35$$
Each monthly payment is £35.

For budget-related questions, always calculate the total cost first and compare it with the available budget. If the total is more than the budget, subtract to find the shortage; if it is less, subtract to find how much money is left over.

## Check yourself

1. Eva buys 6 apples at 35p each and pays with £5. After converting £5 to 500p, what should she do next?

   - Work out $6 \times 35$ to find the total cost ✔

   - Work out $500 \div 35$ to find the change

   - Work out $500 - 35$ because she buys apples

   - Work out $500 + 35$ to include the payment

   _Explanation:_ She needs the total cost of 6 apples first, so multiply the number of apples by the cost of each apple. A common wrong choice is $500 - 35$, but that only subtracts the price of one apple, not all 6. Hint: Ask yourself, "What is the cost of all the apples together?"

2. Ben has £4 and snack bars cost 35p each. How many bars can he buy if he buys as many as possible?

   - 10

   - 11 ✔

   - 12

   - 15

   _Explanation:_ Convert £4 to 400p and divide: $400 \div 35 = 11 \text{ remainder } 15$, so Ben can buy 11 full bars. A common wrong answer is 12 because $11.4$ rounds up, but he does not have enough money for a 12th bar. The phrase "as many as possible" means you cannot go over the amount of money you have.

3. Owen wants 20 calculators and 20 folders. Calculators are only sold in packs of 2 for £9.80 per pack, and folders are only sold in packs of 3 for £2.40 per pack. He has £110. How much is he short by?

   - £4.80 ✔

   - £4.00

   - £1.40

   - He has enough money

   _Explanation:_ 20 calculators means $20 \div 2 = 10$ packs, so the calculators cost $10 \times 9.80 = 98$, or £98. To get 20 folders when they are only sold in packs of 3, Owen must buy 7 packs, giving $7 \times 3 = 21$ folders. The folders cost $7 \times 2.40 = 16.80$, or £16.80. The total cost is $98 + 16.80 = 114.80$, so he is short by $114.80 - 110 = 4.80$, which is £4.80.
