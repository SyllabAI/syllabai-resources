## Reading a pictogram

![Pictogram titled Fruit sold with a key showing 1 apple symbol equals 4 fruits, and rows for Apples, Bananas, and Pears using full and part apple symbols](../assets/001-09db9527-0901-471a-925c-a4637eb42934.jpeg)
A pictogram uses symbols or pictures to show how many of something there are. The key tells you what one full symbol is worth, so never assume one picture means 1 item.

A tally chart records counts with tally marks, and the frequency is the final count for each category. A pictogram shows the same data more visually, but the numbers still come from counting correctly.

In the diagram, the key says 1 apple symbol represents 4 fruits. Apples has $2$ full symbols so it shows $2 \times 4 = 8$, and Bananas has $1$ full symbol plus a half symbol so it shows $4 + 2 = 6$.

## Full, half, and quarter symbols

A full symbol does not always fit exactly, so pictograms often use half or quarter symbols. If 1 full symbol represents 8 pizzas, then half represents 4, quarter represents 2, and three-quarters represents 6.

When a row shows 1 full symbol and a half symbol, first turn each part into pizzas. Then add the values:

$$8 + 4 = 12$$

If a row shows 2 full symbols and a half symbol, do not count "2.5 pizzas". Use the key to count items:

$$8 + 8 + 4 = 20$$

## Worked example: reading a row

Suppose the key says 1 full star represents 8 stickers. Friday shows 2 full stars and a quarter star, so we need the value of the part symbol first.

A quarter means divide the key by 4. $$8 \div 4 = 2$$ So the quarter star is worth 2 stickers.

Now add the values of all the symbols. $$8 + 8 + 2 = 18$$ So Friday represents 18 stickers.

## Completing a pictogram from numbers

![Instructional diagram with key 1 circle equals 2 ice creams, showing 6 as 3 full circles, 9 as 4 full circles and a half circle, and 11 as 5 full circles and a half circle](../assets/002-d7a50c33-3ba6-4708-b898-860d630cac6d.jpeg)

To draw a pictogram row, divide the number by the key to find how many full symbols you can use. Then use any leftover amount to decide whether you need a half or quarter symbol.

For 9 ice creams, $9 \div 2 = 4$ with $1$ left over. Draw 4 full circles for 8 ice creams, then a half circle for the extra 1.

For 11 ice creams, $11 \div 2 = 5$ with $1$ left over. Draw 5 full circles and a half circle, because odd totals often need a half symbol when the key is 2.

## Spotting mistakes

A correct pictogram should have a key, symbols of the same size and style, and clear part-symbols. If the data came from a tally chart, the frequencies shown must match the counts.

For example, if the key shows a vertically split half symbol, every half symbol in the pictogram should look the same. Changing to a different shape or a horizontal split makes the chart unclear and can lose marks.

## Stretch: finding a missing key

Sometimes the key is missing, but you are told the total. Suppose Monday shows $2\frac{1}{4}$ symbols, Tuesday shows $1\frac{1}{4}$ symbols, and Wednesday shows $1\frac{1}{2}$ symbols. Together they represent 40 stickers.

First add the symbol amounts to find the total number of full-symbol equivalents.
$$2\frac{1}{4} + 1\frac{1}{4} + 1\frac{1}{2} = 5$$
So the three rows together are worth the same as 5 full symbols.

Now divide the total number of stickers by 5.
$$40 \div 5 = 8$$
So one full symbol represents 8 stickers, and 18 stickers would be shown by 2 full symbols and a quarter symbol.

## Check yourself

1. What is the first thing to check when reading a pictogram?

   - The key ✔

   - The tally marks

   - The colour of the symbols

   - The order of the rows

   _Explanation:_ The key tells you what each full symbol represents, so you must read it before converting symbols into amounts. The common wrong answer B mixes up tally charts with pictograms, but tally marks do not tell you the value of a symbol in a pictogram. Hint: Do not assume one picture means one item.

2. The key says 1 symbol = 8 stickers. A row shows 1 full symbol and a three-quarter symbol. How many stickers does it represent?

   - 11

   - 14 ✔

   - 9

   - 1.75

   _Explanation:_ Three-quarters of 8 is 6, so the total is $8 + 6 = 14$. The common wrong answer 11 comes from adding 8 and 3, but the three-quarter symbol is worth 6 items, not 3.

3. A pictogram key shows a circle split vertically to mean half a symbol. One row uses a circle split horizontally instead. What is one thing wrong?

   - Nothing is wrong because both pictures show half

   - The symbol is inconsistent with the key ✔

   - The row should count as two quarter symbols

   - The key must change to match that row

   _Explanation:_ The symbols in the chart should match the key exactly, so a different split makes the pictogram inconsistent. The common wrong answer A ignores that exam markers need the same symbol style all the way through the chart.
