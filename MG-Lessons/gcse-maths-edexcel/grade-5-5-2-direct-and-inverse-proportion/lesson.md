## Scaling and the two main patterns

Proportion describes how two quantities change together. A unit rate is the amount for 1 unit, such as cost per apple or cartons per hour, and it often helps you spot the relationship.

In direct proportion, both quantities scale by the same factor, so if apples double, the cost doubles. The algebra model is $y=kx$, where $k$ is the constant of proportionality.

In inverse proportion, increasing one quantity makes the other decrease by the same factor, so if workers double, the time halves. The algebra model is $y=\frac{k}{x}$.

## Direct proportion by finding a unit rate

If cost is directly proportional to the number of apples $a$, you can write $C=ka$. From 8 apples costing £1.20, the constant is $k=1.20 \div 8 = 0.15$, so each apple costs 15p.

Now substitute $a=11$ into the formula $C=0.15a$. Then $C=0.15 \times 11 = 1.65$, so 11 apples cost £1.65.

This unit rate method works whenever the rate stays steady. Find the amount for 1 unit first, then scale up to the new amount.

## Mixed cost questions

Some cost questions mix two different items in one total. First find the known item's unit rate, then remove its total cost before dividing what is left.

Here, 5 packets of rice cost £2.25, so one packet costs $2.25 \div 5 = 0.45$. That means 4 packets of rice cost $4 \times 0.45 = 1.80$.

The full total for 4 packets of rice and 3 tins of soup is £5.10, so the 3 tins must cost $5.10 - 1.80 = 3.30$. Divide by 3 to get one tin: $3.30 \div 3 = 1.10$, so each tin costs £1.10.

A common mistake is to divide the full £5.10 by 3 straight away. That ignores the rice, so it overestimates the price of one tin.

## Inverse proportion and total work

In inverse proportion, multiplying one quantity by a factor divides the other by the same factor. For fixed jobs, the useful idea is that the product workers $\times$ time stays constant.

If 3 painters take 8 days, the total work is $3 \times 8 = 24$ painter-days. With 4 painters, the time is $24 \div 4 = 6$ days.

Always do a sanity check at the end. More painters should mean less time, so 6 days makes sense and 10 days would not.

## Square, inverse square, and graph shapes

![Four coordinate graphs showing a straight line through the origin, an inverse graph with opposite branches, a U-shaped parabola through the origin, and an inverse square graph with both branches above the x-axis](../assets/001-d58715df-e740-4418-9600-6a63b63a564b.jpeg)

If a quantity is directly proportional to $x^2$, write $y=kx^2$. If it is inversely proportional to $x^2$, write $y=\frac{k}{x^2}$, so you must square before multiplying or dividing.

For example, if $F=\frac{48}{d^2}$ and $d=4$, then $F=\frac{48}{4^2}=\frac{48}{16}=3$. The common mistake is using $48 \div 4$, but the formula says square the distance first.

Use the graph shapes to recognise the rule quickly, and remember that $\propto$ means "is proportional to". $y \propto x$ is a straight line through the origin, $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ has two opposite branches, $y \propto x^2$ is a U-shaped curve through the origin, and $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$ has two branches above the $x$-axis when the constant is positive.

## Check yourself

1. For a fixed painting job, more identical workers means fewer days. What type of proportion links the number of workers and the number of days?

   - Direct proportion

   - Inverse proportion ✔

   - Direct proportion to $x^2$

   - No proportion at all

   _Explanation:_ It is inverse proportion because the total work stays constant, so workers $\times$ days stays the same. The most common wrong answer is direct proportion, but direct proportion would make both quantities increase together, which is not what happens here.

2. 3 sandwiches and 2 drinks cost £8.40. The 3 sandwiches alone cost £5.40. What is the cost of 1 drink?

   - £1.50 ✔

   - £2.70

   - £4.20

   - £1.80

   _Explanation:_ Subtract the sandwich cost first: $£8.40 - £5.40 = £3.00$ for 2 drinks, then divide by 2 to get $£1.50$ each. The most common wrong answer is £4.20, which comes from dividing the whole total by 2 and forgetting that sandwiches are included. Hint: Remove the sandwich cost before dividing.

3. If $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$, what happens to $y$ when $x$ doubles?

   - $$y$ doubles

   - $$y$ halves

   - $$y$ becomes one quarter of its original value ✔

   - $$y$ stays the same

   _Explanation:_ In inverse square proportion, doubling $x$ multiplies the denominator by $2^2=4$, so the value becomes a quarter. The most common wrong answer is $y$ halves, which would be correct for $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ but not for $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$.
