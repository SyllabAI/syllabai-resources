## Sectors as fractions of circles

![Sector of a circle with centre O, two radii, central angle theta, and arc length L labelled](../assets/001-5ffbb45c-4558-4c1b-ac1e-946b77b89e53.jpeg)

A sector is a slice of a circle made from two radii and the arc between them. The diagram shows the central angle $\theta$ and the curved edge, which is the arc length $L$.

A full circle is $360^{\circ}$, so a sector with angle $\theta$ is the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of the whole circle. This same fraction tells you how much of the full area and full circumference you are using.

## Formulae and units

For a full circle, the area is $\pi r^2$ and the circumference is $2\pi r$. Here $r$ is the radius. A sector takes the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of each full-circle formula.

Use the same fraction for both formulae. The first finds area inside the sector, and the second finds curved distance around it.

$$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{sector area} &= \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2 \\
\text{arc length} &= \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r
\end{aligned}
$$

Area answers use square units such as $\text{cm}^2$, while arc length answers use length units such as $\text{cm}$. If the question says "in terms of $\pi$", keep $\pi$ in the final answer instead of changing it to a decimal.

## Worked example: sector area

To find the area of a sector, multiply the full circle area by the sector fraction. For radius $8$ cm and angle $135^\circ$, start by substituting into the formula.

$$$A = \frac{135}{360} \times \pi \times 8^2$$

The fraction $\frac{135}{360}$ simplifies to $\frac{3}{8}$, so $A = \frac{3}{8} \times 64\pi = 24\pi$. As a decimal, $24\pi \approx 75.4$, so the area is $75.4 \, \text{cm}^2$ to 3 significant figures.

## Worked example: arc length

Arc length is calculated as a fraction of the full circumference, not a fraction of the area. For a circle with a radius of $10$ cm and a central angle of $108^\circ$, we substitute these values into the arc length formula.

$$$L = \frac{108}{360} \times 2\pi \times 10$$

Since the fraction $\frac{108}{360}$ reduces to $\frac{3}{10}$, the expression simplifies to $L = \frac{3}{10} \times 20\pi = 6\pi$. The final arc length is $6\pi$ cm, which is approximately $18.8$ cm.

## Perimeter of a sector

The perimeter of a sector is the curved arc plus the two straight radii. If you only find the arc length, the answer is incomplete.

For radius $4.8$ cm and angle $75^\circ$, first find the arc length. Then add both radii to get the whole outside distance.
$$L = \frac{75}{360} \times 2\pi \times 4.8 = 2\pi$$

$$$P = 2\pi + 4.8 + 4.8 = 2\pi + 9.6 \approx 15.9$$
So the perimeter is $15.9$ cm to 3 significant figures. In exact form, it is $2\pi + 9.6$ cm.

## When the arc length is given

Sometimes the angle is missing, but you are given the radius and the arc length. Then the sector area can be found with the shortcut $A = \frac{1}{2}rL$.

For $r = 8$ cm and $L = 5\pi$ cm, substitute straight into the shortcut. Multiply the radius by the arc length, then halve the result.

$$$A = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 5\pi$$

This simplifies to $A = 20\pi$, so the area is $20\pi \, \text{cm}^2$. The shortcut works because area and arc length both use the same sector fraction, so the angle cancels out.

## Check yourself

1. A sector has central angle $120^{\circ}$. What fraction of the full circle is it?

   - $$\frac{1}{3}$ ✔

   - $$\frac{2}{3}$

   - $$\frac{1}{4}$

   - $$\frac{3}{4}$

   _Explanation:_ The fraction is $\frac{120}{360} = \frac{1}{3}$. The most common wrong answer is $\frac{2}{3}$, which comes from dividing by $180$ as if a full turn were a straight line instead of a full circle.

2. A sector has radius $5$ cm and angle $72^\circ$. What is its arc length?

   - $$2\pi$ cm ✔

   - $$5\pi$ cm

   - $$10\pi$ cm

   - $$\pi$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Since $\frac{72}{360} = \frac{1}{5}$, the arc length is $\frac{1}{5} \times 2\pi \times 5 = 2\pi$ cm. The most common wrong answer is $5\pi$ cm, which comes from using the area formula $\pi r^2$ instead of the circumference formula $2\pi r$.

3. A sector has radius $9$ cm and arc length $4\pi$ cm. What is its area?

   - $$18\pi \, \text{cm}^2$ ✔

   - $$36\pi \, \text{cm}^2$

   - $$13\pi \, \text{cm}^2$

   - $$2\pi \, \text{cm}^2$

   _Explanation:_ Use $A = \frac{1}{2}rL$, so $A = \frac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 4\pi = 18\pi \, \text{cm}^2$. The most common wrong answer is $36\pi \, \text{cm}^2$, which comes from forgetting the $\frac{1}{2}$.
