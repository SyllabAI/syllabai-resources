## What a quadratic graph looks like

A quadratic graph comes from a formula with an $x^2$ term, such as $y=x^2-x-4$. Its shape is a smooth curve called a parabola, not a straight line. To draw it, you usually make a table of values first.

If the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, the parabola opens upwards and has a lowest point. If the coefficient of $x^2$ is negative, it opens downwards and has a highest point.

## Substituting to make coordinates

To draw the graph, choose some $x$-values and substitute them into the formula to find matching $y$-values. Each answer becomes a coordinate $(x,y)$ that you can plot, with $x$ first and $y$ second.

For $y=x^2-2x-1$, use brackets for negative values. This avoids the common mistake of saying $(-1)^2=-1$.

$$x=-1 \Rightarrow y=(-1)^2-2(-1)-1=1+2-1=2$$

$$x=0 \Rightarrow y=0^2-2(0)-1=-1$$

One more value gives

$$x=3 \Rightarrow y=3^2-2(3)-1=9-6-1=2$$

so three points are $(-1,2)$, $(0,-1)$ and $(3,2)$. Notice that the first and third points have the same $y$-value, which hints at the graph's symmetry.

## Completing a table of values

A table of values organises your work so you can spot the pattern in the graph. Good choices are usually whole-number $x$-values spaced evenly, such as from $-2$ to $4$.

For $y=x^2-x-4$, start carefully with the negative inputs. Both calculations use brackets because the input is negative.

$$$x=-2 \Rightarrow y=(-2)^2-(-2)-4=4+2-4=2$$ 

 $$x=-1 \Rightarrow y=(-1)^2-(-1)-4=1+1-4=-2$$

Continuing in the same way gives the completed table below. The values reach a lowest part then rise again, which is characteristic of an upward-opening parabola.

$x$: $-2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$
$y$: $2, -2, -4, -4, -2, 2, 8$

## Plotting the curve

![Graph of y equals x squared minus x minus 4 with plotted points, a marked turning point near 0.5 comma minus 4.25, and a dashed horizontal line y equals minus 2 crossing at x equals minus 1 and x equals 2](../assets/001-4bba2fb9-f591-4ae3-be46-1dd9c535e3c3.jpeg)

Plot each coordinate from the table as a small cross, then draw one smooth curve through the pattern of points. Do not join them with straight ruler lines, because a quadratic graph bends continuously.

For $y=x^2-x-4$, the lowest part of the curve is between $x=0$ and $x=1$, so the turning point is about $(0.5,-4.25)$. The dashed line $y=-2$ meets the curve twice, so one graph can give two solutions for the same $y$-value.

## Reading roots and the turning point

![Graph of y equals x squared minus 4x plus 3 with roots at x equals 1 and x equals 3 and turning point at 2 comma minus 1](../assets/002-d2ebf953-d054-4beb-b8a2-5997d39dfd17.jpeg)

The roots are the $x$-values where the curve crosses the $x$-axis, because every point on the $x$-axis has $y=0$. The turning point is the lowest point here because the graph opens upwards.

On this graph of $y=x^2-4x+3$, the roots are $x=1$ and $x=3$. The turning point is $(2,-1)$, which sits halfway between the roots. This halfway idea is a useful symmetry check.

## Solving equations from the graph

To solve $x^2-x-4=-2$, match the right-hand side to a $y$-value. Draw or imagine the horizontal line $y=-2$ and read the intersection points, which give $x=-1$ and $x=2$.

If the equation does not already match the graph, rearrange first. For example, $x^2=3x+4$ becomes $x^2-3x-4=0$, so the solutions are the roots of the graph $y=x^2-3x-4$.

## Check yourself

1. For $y=5x-x^2$, what shape will the graph have?

   - It opens downwards and has a highest point. ✔

   - It opens upwards and has a lowest point.

   - It is a straight line because the $5x$ term is positive.

   - It has no turning point.

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because the coefficient of $x^2$ is negative, so the parabola opens downwards. B is the common wrong answer because the positive $5x$ term can distract you, but the sign of the $x^2$ term decides the opening direction.

2. On the graph of $y=x^2-x-4$, which is the best estimate of the turning point?

   - $$(0.5,-4.25)$ ✔

   - $$(0,-4)$

   - $$(1,-4)$

   - $$(0.5,4.25)$

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because the lowest point lies halfway between $x=0$ and $x=1$ and slightly below $y=-4$. B and C are common wrong answers because they are plotted points, but the smooth curve dips a little lower between them.

3. The graph of $y=x^2-4x+3$ crosses the x-axis at $x=1$ and $x=3$. What are the solutions of $x^2-4x+3=0$?

   - $$x=1$ and $x=3$ ✔

   - $$x=2$ and $x=-1$

   - $$x=-1$ and $x=4$

   - $$x=0$ and $x=3$

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because roots are the $x$-values where the graph has $y=0$, so they are read from the x-axis crossings. B is a common wrong answer because $x=2$ is the x-coordinate of the turning point, not a root.

4. A student looks at the graph of $y=x^2-x-4$ and says the roots are $x=-1$ and $x=2$. What has the student actually read from the graph?

   - The solutions of $x^2-x-4=-2$ ✔

   - The turning point of the graph

   - The solutions of $x^2-x-4=0$

   - The y-intercept of the graph

   _Explanation:_ A is correct because $x=-1$ and $x=2$ are where the curve meets the horizontal line $y=-2$. C is the common wrong answer because roots must come from x-axis crossings, not just any convenient pair of x-values on the curve.
