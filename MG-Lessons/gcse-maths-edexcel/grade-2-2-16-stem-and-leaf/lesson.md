## Reading stems, leaves and the key

Here is a simple ordered stem and leaf diagram:

```text
Stem | Leaf
 1   | 2 5 8
 2   | 1 4 7
 3   | 0 6
```

This represents the numbers 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30 and 36. The stem is the first part of each number, and the leaf is the last digit.

A key is essential because it tells you exactly what one stem and one leaf mean, including the units. For example, if the key is $4 \mid 7 = 47$ seconds, then stem 4 and leaf 7 make 47 seconds. If the units were centimetres instead, the same stem and leaf would mean 47 cm.

In an ordered diagram, the leaves in each row go from smallest to largest. This makes it quick to read the data in order and find the smallest, largest and middle values.

## Splitting numbers into stems and leaves

When numbers have 3 digits, the stem can be all digits except the last one. That means 153 is split into stem 15 and leaf 3.

Using the same rule, 168 becomes $16 \mid 8$ and 190 becomes $19 \mid 0$. The 0 leaf matters, because without it the value 190 would be recorded incorrectly.

A suitable key for these heights is $15 \mid 3 = 153$ cm. Always choose a key that makes the place value clear to the reader.

## Worked example: drawing an ordered diagram

To draw an ordered diagram, write the stems first. For the heights $28, 33, 36, 41, 44, 39, 52, 50, 47, 34, 42, 46, 55, 31, 38$, the stems are 2, 3, 4 and 5.

Place each units digit in the correct row. Then order the leaves from smallest to largest, giving:

$$$\begin{array}{rcl}
2 & \mid & 8 \\
3 & \mid & 1 \; 3 \; 4 \; 6 \; 8 \; 9 \\
4 & \mid & 1 \; 2 \; 4 \; 6 \; 7 \\
5 & \mid & 0 \; 2 \; 5
\end{array}$$

Now add a key such as $3 \mid 1 = 31$ cm. Finally, count the leaves: there are 15 leaves, so every one of the 15 heights has been used once.

## Reading the median and range

Suppose an ordered ages diagram has rows $1 \mid 8 \; 9$, $2 \mid 1 \; 2 \; 5 \; 6 \; 8$ and $3 \mid 0 \; 3 \; 4$. Reading across gives the 10 ages 18, 19, 21, 22, 25, 26, 28, 30, 33 and 34.

The smallest age is 18 and the largest is 34, so the range is $34 - 18 = 16$ years. Because the diagram is ordered, you can read these values straight off without sorting again.

There are 10 values, so the median is halfway between the 5th and 6th values. Those are 25 and 26, so the median is $\frac{25 + 26}{2} = 25.5$ years.

## Decimal data needs a clear key

Stem and leaf diagrams can also show decimals with one decimal place. If the key is $2 \mid 6 = 2.6$ kg, then the stem is the whole number part and the leaf is the tenths digit.

For the masses $1.4, 3.0, 2.6, 3.4, 1.7, 2.2, 4.1, 3.4, 2.9$ kg, the ordered diagram is:
- $1 \mid 4 \; 7$
- $2 \mid 2 \; 6 \; 9$
- $3 \mid 0 \; 4 \; 4$
- $4 \mid 1$

Repeated values stay repeated, so $3 \mid 4 \; 4$ shows two masses of 3.4 kg. There are 9 masses, so the median is the 5th value. Reading from the ordered diagram, the 5th value is 2.9 kg.

## Probability from a stem and leaf diagram

If one value is chosen at random, use $P = \frac{\text{number wanted}}{\text{total number}}$. This works when each value in the diagram is equally likely to be chosen.

Suppose the ordered test scores are shown by $5 \mid 8$, $6 \mid 1 \; 4 \; 9$, $7 \mid 0 \; 2 \; 7 \; 8$, $8 \mid 1 \; 5 \; 6$ and $9 \mid 0 \; 3$. There are 13 scores altogether.

Scores more than 80 are 81, 85, 86, 90 and 93, so there are 5 scores wanted. The probability is therefore $\frac{5}{13}$, and 80 itself does not count because "more than 80" does not include 80.

## Check yourself

1. If the key is $4 \mid 7 = 47$ seconds, what does $6 \mid 2$ mean?

   - 62 seconds ✔

   - 6.2 seconds

   - 602 seconds

   - 26 seconds

   _Explanation:_ 6 is the tens part and 2 is the units part, so the value is 62 seconds. The common wrong answer is 6.2 seconds, but the key shows the bar separates tens and units here, not whole numbers and tenths.

2. Which row is correctly ordered and includes every value for the scores 41, 47, 42, 46 and 44?

   - $$4 \mid 7 \; 1 \; 2 \; 6 \; 4$

   - $$4 \mid 1 \; 2 \; 4 \; 6 \; 7$ ✔

   - $$4 \mid 1 \; 2 \; 6 \; 7$

   - $$4 \mid 4 \; 1 \; 2 \; 6 \; 7$

   _Explanation:_ The leaves must be in ascending order and there must be one leaf for each value, so $4 \mid 1 \; 2 \; 4 \; 6 \; 7$ is correct. The common wrong answer is C because it looks ordered, but it misses the leaf 4 for the score 44.

3. In a diagram with key $2 \mid 6 = 2.6$ kg, what does $4 \mid 1$ represent?

   - 41 kg

   - 4.1 kg ✔

   - 1.4 kg

   - 4.01 kg

   _Explanation:_ The key shows the stem is the whole number part and the leaf is the tenths digit, so $4 \mid 1$ means 4.1 kg. The common wrong answer is 41 kg because it ignores the decimal meaning given by the key.

4. The ordered values read from a stem and leaf diagram are $10, 12, 12$ and $15$. A new value of $99$ is added. What is the new median?

   - $$12$ ✔

   - $$13.5$

   - $$15$

   - $$99$

   _Explanation:_ After adding $99$, the ordered list is $10, 12, 12, 15, 99$ and the middle value is still $12$. The common wrong answer is $15$ because some students assume a new high value must push the median up, but you must reorder the data and find the middle again.
