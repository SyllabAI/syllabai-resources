## Angle basics

![Four labelled angles P, Q, R and S showing right, reflex, obtuse and acute examples with degree measures and a marked vertex](../assets/001-4d368883-921d-4df0-bd64-f04a0a3247fe.jpeg)

An angle measures the amount of turn between two straight lines that meet. The meeting point is called the vertex, and angles are measured in degrees, written $^\circ$.

A right angle is $90^\circ$, a straight line angle is $180^\circ$, and a full turn is $360^\circ$. These benchmark angles help you estimate and classify other angles quickly.

Acute angles are less than $90^\circ$, obtuse angles are more than $90^\circ$ but less than $180^\circ$, and reflex angles are more than $180^\circ$ but less than $360^\circ$. Use the number or angle fact given, not just what the picture seems to show.

## Measuring and drawing with a protractor

![Protractor measuring an obtuse angle of 118 degrees from the right-hand zero line, with vertex and correct scale labelled](../assets/002-89d280b5-d070-4775-b634-c840dc78886b.jpeg)
A protractor is used to measure or draw an angle accurately. Put the centre on the vertex and line one arm of the angle up with $0^\circ$.

Most protractors have two scales, so always read from the $0^\circ$ that lies on the arm of your angle. In the diagram, the correct reading is $118^\circ$, so the angle is obtuse.

To draw $110^\circ$, start with one line, place the centre of the protractor on the vertex, and make a mark at $110^\circ$ on the correct scale. Then draw the second arm through the mark.

## Angle facts on lines and around points

Three facts come up all the time: angles on a straight line add up to $180^{\circ}$, a right angle is $90^{\circ}$, and angles around a point add up to $360^{\circ}$. When you see a missing angle, start by naming the fact you are using.

Example: one angle on a straight line is $127^{\circ}$ and the other is $x$. To find the missing value, use the straight line fact and subtract the known angle from the total.

$$$x = 180 - 127 = 53$$

So the missing angle is $53^{\circ}$. If a right angle is split into two smaller angles, those parts add to $90^{\circ}$. Unless the question says the diagram is accurate, trust the angle facts rather than the picture.

## Triangles and isosceles triangles

![Isosceles triangle with equal sides marked, base angles labelled 68 degrees and top angle labelled x](../assets/003-08a379a9-7cf3-471a-9bc9-13586541616d.jpeg)

The three interior angles of any triangle add to $180^\circ$. In an isosceles triangle, the base angles are equal because two sides are equal.

In the diagram, the two base angles are $68^\circ$. Use the triangle sum to find the top angle $x$: 

$$x = 180 - 68 - 68 = 44$$

So the top angle is $44^\circ$. When two angles in a triangle are known, add them first and subtract from $180^\circ$. Matching tick marks on the sides usually tell you the triangle is isosceles.

## Squares and equilateral triangles

A square has four right angles, so each corner is $90^\circ$. An equilateral triangle has three equal angles, so each angle is $60^\circ$.

If a square and an equilateral triangle share a side, one shared corner has a $90^\circ$ angle from the square and a $60^\circ$ angle from the triangle. Add them to find the combined angle.

$$$90 + 60 = 150$$

So that angle is $150^\circ$. In an exam, write the reason as well as the answer, such as 'angles in a triangle add to $180^\circ$' or 'angles around a point add to $360^\circ$'. Clear reasons make it easier to earn full marks.

## Check yourself

1. Which type of angle is $245^{\circ}$?

   - Acute angle

   - Obtuse angle

   - Reflex angle ✔

   - Right angle

   _Explanation:_ $$245^{\circ}$ is greater than $180^{\circ}$ but less than $360^{\circ}$, so it is a reflex angle. The common wrong answer is obtuse, but obtuse angles stop before $180^{\circ}$.

2. Four angles around a point are $90^\circ$, $105^\circ$, $118^\circ$ and $x$. What is $x$?

   - $$47^\circ$ ✔

   - $$57^\circ$

   - $$313^\circ$

   - $$242^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Add the known angles first: $90^\circ + 105^\circ + 118^\circ = 313^\circ$. Then subtract from $360^\circ$ to get $47^\circ$; the common wrong answer $313^\circ$ is just the total of the known angles, not the missing angle.

3. A square and an equilateral triangle share a side. At one shared corner, the inside angles of the two shapes are $90^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. The rest of the turn around that point is $x$. What is $x$?

   - $$150^\circ$

   - $$210^\circ$ ✔

   - $$270^\circ$

   - $$300^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ The two known angles make $150^\circ$ together. Angles around a point total $360^\circ$, so the remaining angle is $210^\circ$; the common wrong answer $150^\circ$ is just the known part, not the missing reflex angle.
