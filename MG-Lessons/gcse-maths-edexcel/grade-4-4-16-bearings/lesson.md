## What a bearing is

![alt="Point A with a north line labelled N, compass directions E S W, and a ray to B making a clockwise 67 degree angle from north"](../assets/001-11351349-ef73-4c8c-8583-b29c69fc5aaf.jpeg)
A bearing describes direction using an angle. It is always measured from North, going clockwise, and written with three figures.

A North line is the starting line for every bearing, and clockwise means the same direction as the hands of a clock. So $7^\circ$ becomes $007^\circ$, $42^\circ$ becomes $042^\circ$, and $115^\circ$ stays $115^\circ$.

When you read "the bearing of B from A", the word from tells you where to start. You measure the angle at A, not at B.

## Reading a bearing from a diagram

To find the bearing of B from A, start at A and look at the North line drawn there. Turn clockwise until you are pointing along the line from A to B.

If that angle is $67^\circ$, write the bearing as $067^\circ$. The zero is added because a bearing must have three figures.

This question is "the bearing of B from A", so measuring at B would give the wrong direction. The word "from" tells you where the angle is measured.

## Drawing a bearing

![alt="Point P with a north line, a protractor centred at P, and a ray to Q 5 cm away at 120 degrees clockwise from north"](../assets/002-1e8e2394-0d0a-4dc1-bc60-9c000e8c308e.jpeg)
To draw a bearing, begin with the starting point and draw a North line straight up from it. Then place the centre of the protractor on the point and line up $0^\circ$ with North.

Suppose Q is 5 cm from P on a bearing of $120^\circ$. Mark $120^\circ$ clockwise, draw a ray from P through the mark, then measure 5 cm along the ray.

Finally, label the end of the ray Q. Since $120^\circ$ is between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, the line should point down and to the right, so that is a good sense check.

## When the marked angle is anticlockwise

Sometimes a diagram shows a small angle on the left side of the North line. That small angle is not the bearing because bearings must be measured clockwise.

If the line to D is $35^\circ$ anticlockwise from North at C, use a full turn to find the clockwise angle:

$$$360^\circ - 35^\circ = 325^\circ$$

So the bearing of D from C is $325^\circ$. A common mistake is to write $035^\circ$. That only gives the small angle, but it is measured the wrong way round.

## Reverse bearings

A reverse bearing is the bearing for going back the other way. Opposite directions differ by $180^{\circ}$, so add or subtract $180^{\circ}$ to find it.

Example 1: the bearing of T from V is $072^{\circ}$. This is less than $180^{\circ}$, so add $180^{\circ}$:
$$072^{\circ} + 180^{\circ} = 252^{\circ}$$
So the bearing of V from T is $252^{\circ}$.

Example 2: the bearing of B from A is $230^{\circ}$. This is greater than $180^{\circ}$, so subtract $180^{\circ}$:
$$230^{\circ} - 180^{\circ} = 050^{\circ}$$
The reverse bearing is $050^{\circ}$, and the leading zero is still needed.

## Check yourself

1. Which statement about bearings is correct?

   - A bearing is measured clockwise from North and written with three figures. ✔

   - A bearing is measured anticlockwise from North and written with three figures.

   - A bearing is measured clockwise from East and written with three figures.

   - A bearing is measured from North, but it can be written with one or two figures.

   _Explanation:_ Bearings are always measured from North, always clockwise, and always written with three figures. The most common wrong answer is B because students remember North but forget that the turn must be clockwise, not anticlockwise.

2. A question asks for "the bearing of R from S". Where should the angle be measured?

   - At S ✔

   - At R

   - Halfway between S and R

   - At whichever point is closer to North

   _Explanation:_ The word from tells you the starting point, so the angle is measured at S. The most common wrong answer is B because students swap the points and measure at the destination instead. Hint: Read the phrase carefully and circle the word "from".

3. The line from X to Y is $25^\circ$ anticlockwise from North at X. What is the bearing of X from Y?

   - $$155^\circ$ ✔

   - $$335^\circ$

   - $$205^\circ$

   - $$025^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ First find the bearing of Y from X: $360^\circ - 25^\circ = 335^\circ$. Then reverse it by subtracting $180^\circ$, giving $155^\circ$. The most common wrong answer is B because it finds the first bearing correctly but forgets the question asks for the bearing back from Y to X.
