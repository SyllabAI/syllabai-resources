## Factors, multiples and primes

A factor divides a number exactly, and a multiple is a number in its times table. HCF is about common factors, while LCM is about common multiples.

A prime number is greater than 1 and has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. For example, 7 is a factor of 42 because $42 \div 7 = 6$ exactly, while 1 is not prime.

## Writing a number as prime factors

![Factor tree for 84 showing branches to 2, 2, 3 and 7, with the prime factorisation written underneath](../assets/001-18fb559f-ad88-4174-ae65-9e28aeff6fbe.jpeg)
A product of prime factors means writing a number as a multiplication of primes only. Use a factor tree or repeated division, starting with small primes like 2, 3 and 5.

In this example, $84 = 2 \times 42 = 2 \times 2 \times 21 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7$. Index notation makes repeated factors shorter and easier to compare later for HCF and LCM.

## Finding the HCF

The highest common factor, or HCF, is the largest factor shared by all the numbers. Using prime factors, keep only the primes that appear in every number and use the smallest power of each.

For 54 and 126, write $54 = 2 \times 3^3$ and $126 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 7$. The common primes are 2 and 3. Use the smallest powers, $2^1$ and $3^2$, then multiply.

$$$\text{HCF} = 2 \times 3^2 = 18$$

A quick check is that 18 divides both numbers exactly. This confirmation ensures that the result is correctly identified as a common factor.

## Finding the LCM

The lowest common multiple, or LCM, is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of all the numbers. Using prime factors, include every prime that appears and use the largest power of each.

For 45 and 60, write $45 = 3^2 \times 5$ and $60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5$. To determine the LCM, identify the highest power of every prime factor present: $2^2$, $3^2$, and $5$.

$$$\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 180$$
If your answer is smaller than the largest original number (60), you have likely mixed up the LCM with the HCF.

Repeated events use the LCM because you want to find the first time both patterns meet again. Always change your values to the same units, such as minutes, before starting the calculation.

## Stretch: working backwards

Harder questions sometimes give the HCF and LCM and ask for the original numbers. If the HCF is $h$, then both numbers are multiples of $h$, and the leftover parts must be coprime.

Suppose the HCF is 6 and the LCM is 72. Divide $72$ by $6$ to get $12$, and the leftover pair must be a coprime factor pair of 12.

The pairs are $1$ and $12$, $2$ and $6$, and $3$ and $4$. Only $3$ and $4$ are coprime and give numbers greater than 8, so the numbers are 18 and 24. You can check that their HCF is 6 and their LCM is 72.

## Check yourself

1. Which statement is correct?

   - 1 is a prime number

   - 6 is a multiple of 42

   - 7 is a factor of 42 ✔

   - 9 is a factor of 42

   _Explanation:_ $$42 \div 7 = 6$ exactly, so 7 is a factor of 42. Option B is the common mix-up: 42 is a multiple of 6, not the other way round.

2. What is the HCF of 72 and 90?

   - 6

   - 18 ✔

   - 30

   - 360

   _Explanation:_ $$72 = 2^3 \times 3^2$ and $90 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 5$, so the shared primes are 2 and $3^2$. That gives $2 \times 3^2 = 18$. Option D is the common wrong answer because it uses all primes with the highest powers, which finds the LCM instead.

3. Two whole numbers greater than 8 have HCF 6 and LCM 72. Which pair could they be?

   - 12 and 36

   - 18 and 24 ✔

   - 6 and 72

   - 30 and 42

   _Explanation:_ Hint: Divide the LCM by the HCF first. If the HCF is 6, both numbers are $6 \times a$ and $6 \times b$, where $a$ and $b$ are coprime. Since $72 \div 6 = 12$, we need a coprime factor pair of 12, which is 3 and 4, so the numbers are 18 and 24. Option A is the common wrong answer because 12 and 36 are multiples of 6, but their HCF is 12, not 6.
