## Exact means no rounding

![Right-angled triangle with angle theta and sides labelled opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse](../assets/001-830405f9-c1b7-44a4-a70c-de2647ac296e.jpeg)
In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Relative to the chosen angle $\theta$, the other sides are called opposite and adjacent.

SOHCAHTOA tells you that $\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$, $\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ and $\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$. In triangle questions, label the sides first and then choose the ratio.

An exact value is written with no rounding. So $\sin(45^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ is exact, while $0.707$ is only an approximation.

## The special triangles

The two triangles worth memorising are:

- $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$: side lengths $1$, $1$ and $\sqrt{2}$
- $30^\circ$-$60^\circ$-$90^\circ$: side lengths $1$, $\sqrt{3}$ and $2$

In the $45^\circ$ triangle, the two shorter sides are equal, so $\sin(45^\circ)=\cos(45^\circ)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\tan(45^\circ)=1$.

For the $30^\circ$-$60^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle, the shorter side $1$ is opposite the smaller angle $30^\circ$, the side $\sqrt{3}$ is opposite $60^\circ$, and the hypotenuse is $2$.

- For $30^\circ$: opposite $=1$, adjacent $=\sqrt{3}$, hypotenuse $=2$
- For $60^\circ$: opposite $=\sqrt{3}$, adjacent $=1$, hypotenuse $=2$

So the exact values are:

- $\sin(30^\circ)=\frac{1}{2}$, $\cos(30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\tan(30^\circ)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$
- $\sin(60^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\cos(60^\circ)=\frac{1}{2}$, $\tan(60^\circ)=\sqrt{3}$

## A pattern for the exact value table

You need the values at $0^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ ready to write down. This table shows the sine and cosine rows together.

$$$\begin{array}{c|ccccc} \theta & 0^\circ & 30^\circ & 45^\circ & 60^\circ & 90^\circ \\ \hline \sin\theta & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & 1 \\ \cos\theta & 1 & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{1}{2} & 0 \end{array}$$

The sine row follows the pattern $\frac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2}$. Cosine is the same list backwards.

For tangent, use $\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$. So $\tan(30^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, $\tan(45^\circ)=1$, $\tan(60^\circ)=\sqrt{3}$, and $\tan(90^\circ)$ is undefined.

## Worked example 1: finding a hypotenuse

A right-angled triangle has a $30^\circ$ angle, opposite side $18$ cm and hypotenuse $h$. Since we have the opposite side and want the hypotenuse, we use the sine ratio:
$$\sin(30^\circ)=\frac{18}{h}$$

Now substitute the exact value $\sin(30^\circ)=\frac{1}{2}$. It is helpful to keep the fraction form rather than turning it into a decimal to simplify the algebra:
$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{18}{h}$$

Solve for $h$ by doubling $18$, or by dividing by $\frac{1}{2}$. The final result for the hypotenuse is calculated as:
$$h=18 \div \frac{1}{2}=36$$

## Worked example 2: finding an adjacent side

Take a triangle with a $60^\circ$ angle, an opposite side of $18$ cm, and an adjacent side $a$. Since we are working with the opposite and adjacent sides, we use the tangent ratio: $$\tan(60^\circ)=\frac{18}{a}$$

Substitute the exact value $\tan(60^\circ)=\sqrt{3}$ to keep the answer in surd form. This gives us an equation that can be rearranged to solve for $a$: $$\sqrt{3}=\frac{18}{a}$$

Rearrange the equation and rationalise the denominator to put the answer in standard exact form. The calculated length of the adjacent side is: $$a=\frac{18}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{18\sqrt{3}}{3}=6\sqrt{3}$$

## Check yourself

1. Which of these is the exact value of $\sin(45^{\circ})$?

   - $$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ ✔

   - 0.707

   - $$\frac{1}{2}$

   - $$\sqrt{2}$

   _Explanation:_ $$\sin(45^{\circ})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ comes from the $45^{\circ}$ special triangle. The common wrong answer 0.707 is only a rounded decimal, so it is not exact. Hint: Exact answers are usually fractions, whole numbers or surds.

2. Which statement explains why $\tan(45^\circ)=1$?

   - The hypotenuse and adjacent side are equal

   - In a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle, the opposite and adjacent sides are equal ✔

   - The opposite side is $0$

   - Tangent is always equal to sine

   _Explanation:_ $$\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$. For a $45^\circ$ angle in a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle, the opposite and adjacent sides are equal, so $\tan(45^\circ)=\frac{x}{x}=1$.

3. A right-angled triangle has a $45^\circ$ angle and adjacent side $7$ cm. What is the hypotenuse exactly?

   - $$7\sqrt{2}$ cm ✔

   - $$\frac{7\sqrt{2}}{2}$ cm

   - $$14$ cm

   - $$7$ cm

   _Explanation:_ Use $\cos(45^\circ)=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}=\frac{7}{h}$. Rearranging gives $h=7\sqrt{2}$ cm. The common wrong answer $\frac{7\sqrt{2}}{2}$ comes from multiplying by $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ instead of dividing by it.
