## What standard form means

![Worked diagram showing 64800000 changing to 6.48 times 10^7 and 0.000731 changing to 7.31 times 10^{-4}, with decimal moves labelled](../assets/001-4475652a-c940-47f4-89b6-b91d0bfae536.jpeg)

Standard form writes a number as $a \times 10^n$. The coefficient $a$ must satisfy $1 \le a < 10$, and $n$ must be an integer, so it can be positive, negative, or zero.

Positive exponents make numbers larger and negative exponents make numbers smaller. The exponent tells you how many places the decimal point has moved.

$$4.6 \times 10^8$ is in standard form, but $46 \times 10^7$ is not because the coefficient is too big. $0.48 \times 10^5$ is not finished either, and it should be rewritten as $4.8 \times 10^4$.

## From standard form to ordinary numbers

To turn standard form into an ordinary number, move the decimal point. Move it right for a positive exponent and left for a negative exponent.

Example: $8.17 \times 10^4 = 81,700$. The exponent 4 means the decimal point moves four places to the right.

Example: $5.09 \times 10^{-3} = 0.00509$. Count the places carefully and add zeros only as placeholders when you need them.

## From ordinary numbers to standard form

To convert an ordinary number into standard form, put the decimal point after the first non-zero digit. Then count how many places it moved and decide whether the exponent is positive or negative.

For $64\,800\,000$, the coefficient is $6.48$ because the decimal point moves 7 places left. So $64\,800\,000 = 6.48 \times 10^7$.

For $0.000731$, the coefficient is $7.31$ because the decimal point moves 4 places right. Because the original number is between 0 and 1, the exponent is negative, so $0.000731 = 7.31 \times 10^{-4}$.

## Multiplying and dividing in standard form

When multiplying in standard form, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents. When dividing, divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents.

$$$(a \times 10^m)(b \times 10^n) = ab \times 10^{m+n}$$

$$(a \times 10^m) \div (b \times 10^n) = \frac{a}{b} \times 10^{m-n}$$

Worked multiplication: $(7 \times 10^5)(3 \times 10^{-3}) = 21 \times 10^2$ because $7 \times 3 = 21$ and $5 + (-3) = 2$. The coefficient 21 is too large, so rewrite the answer as $2.1 \times 10^3$.

Worked division: $(8.4 \times 10^{-5}) \div (2.1 \times 10^{-7}) = 4 \times 10^2$ because $8.4 \div 2.1 = 4$ and $-5 - (-7) = 2$. Be careful with two minus signs, since subtracting a negative changes to addition.

## Adding, subtracting, and comparing

For addition and subtraction, do not just add or subtract the exponents unless the powers of 10 are already the same. It is usually safest to convert to ordinary numbers first.

Example: $6.0 \times 10^4 = 60,000$ and $7.5 \times 10^3 = 7,500$. So $60,000 - 7,500 = 52,500 = 5.25 \times 10^4$.

For comparison or ordering, work out each value before deciding. A number with a small coefficient can still be larger once the power of 10 is applied.

## Final checks for exam questions

After every answer, check the coefficient. If it is 10 or more, or less than 1, shift the decimal point and change the exponent to keep the value the same.

Some calculators display standard form as $1.2\text{E}5$. That means $1.2 \times 10^5$, and you should rewrite it clearly in your answer.

In worded questions, choose the operation first. Total means add, difference means subtract, times larger means divide, and repeated equal amounts mean multiply.

## Check yourself

1. Which number is in standard form?

   - $$4.6 \times 10^8$ ✔

   - $$46 \times 10^7$

   - $$0.48 \times 10^5$

   - $$7.2 \times 10^{1.5}$

   _Explanation:_ $$4.6 \times 10^8$ is correct because the coefficient 4.6 is at least 1 and less than 10, and the exponent 8 is an integer. The common wrong answer is B, but 46 is too large for the coefficient, so it should be rewritten as $4.6 \times 10^8$. Hint: Check the front number first, then check whether the power of 10 is a whole-number exponent.

2. Work out $(7 \times 10^5)(3 \times 10^{-3})$.

   - $$2.1 \times 10^3$ ✔

   - $$21 \times 10^2$

   - $$2.1 \times 10^2$

   - $$21 \times 10^3$

   _Explanation:_ Multiply the coefficients to get 21 and add the exponents to get $10^2$, so the unadjusted answer is $21 \times 10^2$. The common wrong answer is B because it forgets the final check that the coefficient must be between 1 and 10, so the correct standard form is $2.1 \times 10^3$. Hint: Get the unadjusted answer first, then check the coefficient.

3. Which number is smallest?

   - $$4.8 \times 10^2$

   - $$0.048 \times 10^2$

   - $$4800 \times 10^{-4}$ ✔

   - $$48$

   _Explanation:_ Convert each number first: $4.8 \times 10^2 = 480$, $0.048 \times 10^2 = 4.8$, $4800 \times 10^{-4} = 0.48$, and $48 = 48$. The common wrong answer is B because 0.048 looks tiny, but multiplying by $10^2$ makes it 4.8.
