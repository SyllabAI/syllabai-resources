## Area and perimeter basics

![Rectangle on a centimetre grid, 4 cm by 3 cm, with the inside shaded for area and arrows around the outside for perimeter](../assets/001-fccf722f-8e6a-460e-9b4a-0c412d64618e.jpeg)

Area is the amount of space inside a shape. Perimeter is the total distance around the outside.

On a $1 \, \text{cm}$ grid, each small square has area $1 \, \text{cm}^2$. In the rectangle shown, $4 \times 3 = 12$, so the area is $12 \, \text{cm}^2$.

For perimeter, add the outside edges: $4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14$. Perimeter uses length units like $\text{cm}$ or $\text{m}$, but area uses square units like $\text{cm}^2$ or $\text{m}^2$.

## Rectangle and square formulae

A rectangle has four right angles. If its length is $l$ and width is $w$, use these rules.
$$A = l \times w$$
$$P = 2l + 2w$$

The area multiplies the side lengths, while the perimeter adds the outside edges. A square is a special rectangle with all four sides equal. If the side length is $s$, use these rules.
$$A = s^2$$
$$P = 4s$$

Because all four sides match, the perimeter is four times one side. Before calculating, underline whether the question asks for area or perimeter. That helps you choose the right formula and the right units.

## Worked example: rectangle from perimeter clues

A rectangle has perimeter $30 \, \text{cm}$ and its length is twice its width. Let the width be 1 part, so the length is 2 parts.

Around the shape you have width + length + width + length, so that is $1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6$ equal parts. Each part is $30 \div 6 = 5$, so the width is $5 \, \text{cm}$ and the length is $10 \, \text{cm}$.

Check by substituting back: $2 \times 10 + 2 \times 5 = 20 + 10 = 30$. If you were drawing it on a grid, you would draw a rectangle 10 squares by 5 squares and label the sides.

## Triangles and parallelograms

![Triangle with base 6 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm, and a parallelogram with base 4 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm, both showing dashed heights and right angle markers](../assets/002-57454bb6-d781-4b38-a7da-9a45261ff60e.jpeg)

For triangles and parallelograms, the height must be perpendicular to the base. A sloping side is not the height unless it meets the base at $90^\circ$.

For a triangle, use $$A = \frac{b \times h}{2}$$ where $b$ is the base and $h$ is the perpendicular height. For a parallelogram, use $$A = b \times h$$ because it behaves like a slanted rectangle.

With base $6 \, \text{cm}$ and height $5 \, \text{cm}$, a triangle has area $$A = \frac{6 \times 5}{2} = 15$$ so the area is $15 \, \text{cm}^2$. With base $4 \, \text{cm}$ and height $7 \, \text{cm}$, a parallelogram has area $$A = 4 \times 7 = 28$$ so the area is $28 \, \text{cm}^2$.

## Trapeziums and missing heights

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides. To find its area, use the following formula where $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the parallel sides and $h$ is the perpendicular height:

$$$A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$$

If the parallel sides are $7 \, \text{m}$ and $11 \, \text{m}$, and the height is $5 \, \text{m}$, then the calculation is $A = \frac{1}{2}(7+11) \times 5 = \frac{1}{2} \times 18 \times 5 = 45$. This means the total area is $45 \, \text{m}^2$.

If the area is $48 \, \text{cm}^2$ and the parallel sides are $4 \, \text{cm}$ and $12 \, \text{cm}$, then half the sum of the parallel sides is $8$. Solving the equation $48 = 8h$ tells us the height is $h = 6 \, \text{cm}$.

## Compound shapes and perimeter

![L-shaped compound shape made from two 6 cm by 3 cm rectangles, with the outside perimeter highlighted and the internal joined edge shown faintly and not counted](../assets/003-34ae38ee-2d36-4cf6-80e3-8c11d842325f.jpeg)
A compound shape is made from two or more simpler shapes joined together. For perimeter, count only the outside edges and ignore any internal joined edge.

Two separate $6$ cm by $3$ cm rectangles have total perimeter $18 + 18 = 36$ cm. When they are joined along one $3$ cm edge, that shared edge was counted twice, so subtract $3 + 3 = 6$.

The perimeter of the L-shape is therefore $36 - 6 = 30$ cm. A common mistake is to include the internal edge, but it is inside the shape, not on the boundary.

## Check yourself

1. Which answer is written in the correct units for an area?

   - $$18 \, \text{cm}$

   - $$18 \, \text{cm}^2$ ✔

   - $$18 \, \text{cm}^3$

   - $$18^\circ$

   _Explanation:_ Area is measured in square units because it counts the squares covering a surface. The most common wrong answer is $18 \, \text{cm}$, but $\text{cm}$ is a length unit, so it would suit a perimeter, not an area.

2. A rectangle has perimeter $32 \, \text{cm}$ and its length is three times its width. What is the width?

   - $$4 \, \text{cm}$ ✔

   - $$8 \, \text{cm}$

   - $$6 \, \text{cm}$

   - $$12 \, \text{cm}$

   _Explanation:_ Let the width be 1 part and the length be 3 parts, so the total around is $1 + 3 + 1 + 3 = 8$ parts. Each part is $32 \div 8 = 4 \, \text{cm}$, while the common wrong answer $8 \, \text{cm}$ comes from dividing by $4$ as if all four sides were equal like a square.

3. Stretch: A trapezium has area $60 \, \text{cm}^2$ and parallel sides $8 \, \text{cm}$ and $12 \, \text{cm}$. What is its height?

   - $$3 \, \text{cm}$

   - $$6 \, \text{cm}$ ✔

   - $$10 \, \text{cm}$

   - $$30 \, \text{cm}$

   _Explanation:_ The area of a trapezium is $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$. Here, $\frac{8+12}{2} = 10$, so $60 = 10h$. Dividing by $10$ gives $h = 6 \, \text{cm}$. The wrong answer $3 \, \text{cm}$ comes from dividing by $20$ and forgetting that the halving happens before solving for $h$.
