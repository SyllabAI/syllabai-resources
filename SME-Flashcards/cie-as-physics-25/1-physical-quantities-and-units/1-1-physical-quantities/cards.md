## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_6CtMjSyDj63hvjys`)

**FRONT**

Define **physical quantity**.


**BACK**

A **physical quantity** is a property that can be **measured**, consisting of a **numerical magnitude** and a **unit**.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4s4cXGz9nHh5hTG3`)

**FRONT**

Why must a physical quantity always be stated with a unit?


**BACK**

The same **symbol** can represent different quantities, so the **unit** gives it context. For example, *v* could mean velocity (**m s**<sup>**-1**</sup>), volume (**m**<sup>**3**</sup>) or *V* potential difference (**V**).

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_2DVXZwdRXQ9dPHf4`)

**FRONT**

Estimate the energy required for an adult to walk up a flight of stairs.


**BACK**

Using $Δ E_{p} = m g Δ h$ with mass *m* ≈ 70 kg, *g* ≈ 10 N kg<sup>-1</sup> and height Δ*h* ≈ 3 m:

$Δ E_{p} = 70 \times 10 \times 3 = 2100 \text{J}$

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_hFNmKPCvn68k98Bp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A physical quantity is fully described by its numerical value alone.


**BACK**

**False.**

A physical quantity must also include a **unit** — without it, the numerical value is meaningless.
