## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_PwJJBXrVnxwqpHYb`)

**FRONT**

Why are **synthetic indicators** used in acid-alkali titrations instead of litmus?


**BACK**

Synthetic indicators are used because they produce a **sharp colour change** at the endpoint, making it easy to identify exactly when neutralisation has occurred. Litmus changes colour gradually and passes through a purple stage, so the endpoint is hard to pinpoint.


Spec links: `spcpt_cpBnmWZwd6vvbzFb`

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_6vFv7wvF4GmVZXfg`)

**FRONT**

What is **universal indicator**?


**BACK**

**Universal indicator** is a wide-range indicator made from a mixture of different plant **indicators**. It gives an approximate pH value by changing colour across a broad pH range, and the colour is matched to a **colour chart** to estimate the pH of an unknown solution.


Spec links: `spcpt_VTXRmNZhfK7NZmBR`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_q7NdmxpqGjkdBtYG`)

**FRONT**

Why is **universal indicator** not suitable for acid-alkali titrations?


**BACK**

Universal indicator is not suitable for titrations because it cannot produce a **sharp colour change** at the endpoint. A titration requires an exact endpoint, so an indicator that changes colour gradually cannot reliably show when neutralisation is complete.


Spec links: `spcpt_VTXRmNZhfK7NZmBR`


## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_zY8Gk88QjqhvDNpt`)

**FRONT**

What is an **acid**?


**BACK**

An **acid** is a substance that, when added to water, releases positively charged **hydrogen ions** (H<sup>+</sup>). The presence of H<sup>+</sup> ions is what makes a solution acidic.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_Xq99jKXR5NwkQBvm`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

All reactions of acids count as neutralisation reactions.


**BACK**

**False.**

Neutralisation specifically requires an acid to react with an **alkali**, producing water. When a metal reacts with an acid, a salt is formed but no water is produced, so it is not a **neutralisation** reaction.


Spec links: `spcpt_wjk7Td2wsMdggt6D`


## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_VhxkbYTH7pZjmzcG`)

**FRONT**

Define **neutralisation**.


**BACK**

**Neutralisation** is the reaction between an acid and an alkali in which H<sup>+</sup> ions and OH<sup>–</sup> ions combine to produce **water** (H<sub>2</sub>O). The reaction results in a neutral solution.


Spec links: `spcpt_wjk7Td2wsMdggt6D`

How is **neutralisation** used in soil treatment?


**BACK**

Neutralisation is used to raise the pH of acidic soils that are too acidic for some crops. Bases such as **limestone** or **quicklime** are added to the soil to neutralise the acid and increase the pH.

## Card 18 — true_or_false (`fl_GR3YVDYswsfccsXC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The equation H<sup>+</sup> (aq) + OH<sup>–</sup> (aq) → H<sub>2</sub>O (l) represents all acid-base neutralisations.


**BACK**

**True.**

This **net ionic equation** applies to every acid-base neutralisation. The H<sup>+</sup> and OH<sup>–</sup> ions always combine to produce **water**, regardless of which specific acid and alkali are used.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_vfCsZjMqPcVNWcXp`)

**FRONT**

What is a **titration**?


**BACK**

A **titration** is a technique used to determine the **concentration** of a solution. In an acid-base titration, it measures exactly how much of one solution is needed to neutralise another.


Spec links: `spcpt_5ZkBPGYbZ4RfFFt3`

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_bbCbskZqPgPTCvy5`)

**FRONT**

Why is a **rough titration** performed before the accurate titrations?


**BACK**

The rough titration gives an approximate **endpoint** volume. This allows the accurate titrations to be performed by adding the solution drop by drop as the endpoint is approached, making the result more **precise**.


Spec links: `spcpt_5ZkBPGYbZ4RfFFt3`


## Card 24 — keyword_definition (`fl_wkn2b2nThHw9XGSH`)

**FRONT**

What are **concordant results**?


**BACK**

**Concordant results** are two or more titration readings that are within 0.1 cm<sup>3</sup> of each other. They are used to confirm the **reliability** of the titration and are averaged to give a **mean titre**.


Spec links: `spcpt_5ZkBPGYbZ4RfFFt3`
