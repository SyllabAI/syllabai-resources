## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Xm4TTkgC2kxsKV5C`)

**FRONT**

Define **atom**.


**BACK**

An **atom** is the smallest particle of an element, containing **electrons** surrounding a **nucleus** that holds **protons** and **neutrons**.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_CWTggcg4Sh65XqZr`)

**FRONT**

Define **molecule**.


**BACK**

A **molecule** is a group of two or more **atoms** chemically combined to form an identifiable unit that retains the properties and composition of the substance.

**BACK**

Electrons have a relative mass of 1, the same as protons.


**BACK**

**False.**

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_4wSsgcnpZ4PCgckx`)

**FRONT**

What is the **atomic number** of an element?


**BACK**

**Atomic number** is the number of **protons** in the nucleus of an atom. In a neutral atom, it also equals the number of **electrons**.


Spec links: `spcpt_K5SR7p9cSGxhrBCP`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_Hsd6MJPmbyspg84y`)

**FRONT**

How do you calculate the number of neutrons in an atom?


**BACK**

The number of **neutrons** is calculated by subtracting the **atomic number** from the **mass number**: neutrons = mass number − atomic number.


Spec links: `spcpt_K5SR7p9cSGxhrBCP`


## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_9xRStBTk5M3jnfdh`)

**FRONT**

What is the **mass number** of an atom?


**BACK**

**Mass number** is the total number of **protons** and **neutrons** in the nucleus of an atom.

**FRONT**

Define **isotope**.


**BACK**

An **isotope** is one of two or more atoms of the same element that have the same number of **protons** but a different number of **neutrons**.

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_PVfzrDCq27h5YVQS`)

**FRONT**

What is **relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>)?


**BACK**

**Relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>) is the average mass of one atom of an element, taking into account the **abundance** of all its **isotopes**. It is relative to 1/12 the mass of a carbon-12 atom.


Spec links: `spcpt_y4PqM2VKGx82tP5h`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_fCFFJxXkDScMFtQ5`)

**FRONT**

Why is the relative atomic mass of an element often not a whole number?


**BACK**

The **relative atomic mass** is an average that accounts for all the **isotopes** of an element and their **relative abundances**. If an element has isotopes with different masses, the average may fall between whole numbers.


Spec links: `spcpt_y4PqM2VKGx82tP5h`

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_WQ5f7pH9z96wFtjP`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Isotopes of the same element have different numbers of protons.


**BACK**

**False.**

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same **atomic number** (same proton count) but different **mass numbers** (different numbers of neutrons).

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_NbhrZ8GcShQXGRnN`)

**FRONT**

A sample of rubidium contains 72% <sup>85</sup>Rb and 28% <sup>87</sup>Rb. What is the relative atomic mass of this sample?


**BACK**

The **relative atomic mass** is calculated as:

*A*<sub>r</sub> = [(72 × 85) + (28 × 87)] ÷ 100 = (6120 + 2436) ÷ 100 = **85.6**


Spec links: `spcpt_y4PqM2VKGx82tP5h`


## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_yRrnzM6kpCSKZSQV`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The relative atomic mass of an element can be calculated using only one isotope.


**BACK**

**False.**

The relative atomic mass must account for **all isotopes** and their **percentage abundances**. Using only one isotope would give just the mass of that isotope, not a true average.


Spec links: `spcpt_y4PqM2VKGx82tP5h`


## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_yKBDprWM4gBpP96B`)

**FRONT**

How do you recognise isotopes from their nuclear notation?


**BACK**

You can recognise isotopes by looking at their notation: they share the same chemical **symbol** but carry different **mass numbers** as superscripts. For example, <sup>63</sup>Cu and <sup>65</sup>Cu are both isotopes of copper.


Spec links: `spcpt_y4PqM2VKGx82tP5h`
