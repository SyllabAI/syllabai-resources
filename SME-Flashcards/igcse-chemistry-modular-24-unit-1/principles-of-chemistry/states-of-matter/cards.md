## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_rqfRvVc548wRx4Q2`)

**FRONT**

How does particle arrangement differ between **solids**, **liquids** and **gases**?


**BACK**

Particle arrangement differs across the three states. In a **solid**, particles are in a regular arrangement. In a **liquid** and a **gas**, particles are randomly arranged, but in a liquid they are close together and in a gas they are far apart.


Spec links: `spcpt_VPj7CKzRzPpJfZcP`

Freezing occurs at a different temperature from melting for a pure substance.


**BACK**

For a pure substance, **freezing** and **melting** occur at the **same temperature**, known as the melting point. For example, water melts and freezes at 0 °C.

**BACK**

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_wkChc9PWTg34jpP8`)

**FRONT**

Why do substances with stronger forces between particles have higher **melting points** and **boiling points**?


**BACK**

Stronger forces between particles require **more energy** to overcome. This means a higher temperature is needed to change state, so the **melting point** and **boiling point** of the substance are higher.

**BACK**

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_MhxHSNzG3266Hfbr`)

**FRONT**

Define **diffusion**.


**BACK**

**Diffusion** is the spreading of particles through a substance due to their **random movement**. Diffusion results in particles mixing together evenly over time.


Spec links: `spcpt_Ws4J3j9XHXQrRvWt`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_KKq2n299pmZmt5Nw`)

**FRONT**

Why does diffusion occur more slowly in **liquids** than in **gases**?


**BACK**

Diffusion is slower in **liquids** because the particles are more closely packed together and move more slowly than in gases. In **gases**, particles have large gaps between them and move freely, allowing faster mixing.


Spec links: `spcpt_Ws4J3j9XHXQrRvWt`

**BACK**

**False.**

Diffusion in **gases** is faster than in liquids. In gases, particles are more spread out and move more quickly, so they mix together more rapidly.
