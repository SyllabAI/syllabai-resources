## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_d7sdt9CK7DVVMCnN`)

**FRONT**

Define **unsaturated** (as used for hydrocarbons).


**BACK**

An **unsaturated** hydrocarbon contains at least one **carbon-carbon double bond** (C=C). **Alkenes** are unsaturated because they contain a C=C bond.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_28qknkZ3NTsQW4kR`)

**FRONT**

What is the general formula of the alkenes?


**BACK**

The general formula of the alkenes is C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n</sub>. This means for every n carbon atoms, there are 2n hydrogen atoms.


Spec links: `spcpt_VwybskmpnnBhx5sh`


## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_Krzph5Y87sMR9yT8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Alkenes are saturated hydrocarbons.


**BACK**

**False.**

Alkenes are **unsaturated** hydrocarbons. They contain a **carbon-carbon double bond** (C=C), which means they are not fully saturated with hydrogen.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_BTw9T53q9x8wGypJ`)

**FRONT**

Why are alkenes more reactive than alkanes?


**BACK**

Alkenes are more reactive because they contain a **carbon-carbon double bond** (C=C). This double bond can break open, allowing other atoms to bond to the carbon atoms.

**FRONT**

What do the numbers in alkene names (such as but-1-ene or pent-2-ene) indicate?


**BACK**

The numbers indicate the **position of the C=C double bond** — specifically, which carbon atom the double bond begins on, counting from the left of the carbon chain.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_jwYsx6d4fQ3khhvp`)

**FRONT**

What is an **addition reaction**?


**BACK**

An **addition reaction** is one in which atoms of a simple molecule **add across** a double bond, converting it to a single bond. No atoms are lost in the process.

**FRONT**

What is formed when bromine reacts with ethene?


**BACK**

When bromine reacts with ethene, **dibromoethane** is formed. This is an example of an **addition reaction** in which bromine atoms add across the C=C double bond.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_3GK9NRPnjZBXFTkr`)

**FRONT**

Why does bromine water decolourise when added to an alkene?


**BACK**

Bromine water decolourises because the **bromine** atoms add across the **C=C double bond** in an addition reaction. The bromine is no longer free in solution, so the orange colour disappears.

**FRONT**

What happens when bromine water is added to an alkane?


**BACK**

When bromine water is added to an **alkane**, the solution remains **orange**. Alkanes are saturated and cannot react with bromine, so the bromine stays in solution.
