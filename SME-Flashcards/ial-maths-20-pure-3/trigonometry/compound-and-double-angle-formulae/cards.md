## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_8J5RMbpBWZbv5gTr`)

**FRONT**

How do the signs work in the $\mathrm{tan}$ compound angle formula?


**BACK**

The sign on the left matches the one in the **numerator**, and is opposite to the one in the **denominator**.

So `\tan\left(A + B\right) \equiv \frac{\tan A + \tan B}{1 - \tan A \tan B}`.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_WdgYM29HSYxj75TV`)

**FRONT**

How is the $\mathrm{tan}$ compound angle formula derived?


**BACK**

Write `\tan\left(A + B\right)` as `\frac{\sin\left(A + B\right)}{\cos\left(A + B\right)}` and expand both parts.

Dividing the numerator and denominator by $\mathrm{cos} A \mathrm{cos} B$ then turns every term into a tangent.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_zsJPwYCpP4PFKvFh`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\cos\left(A - B\right) \equiv \cos A \cos B + \sin A \sin B`


**BACK**

**True.**

For cosine the sign on the right is **opposite** to the one on the left, so a minus outside gives a plus inside.

Sine and tangent behave the other way round, which is why the cosine pair is the one that catches people out.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_5x5gbVpxYWNpnNQF`)

**FRONT**

Why is `\sin \left(A + B\right)` not simply $\mathrm{sin} A + \mathrm{sin} B$?


**BACK**

Because sine is not additive: adding the angles does not add the values.

Arithmetic settles it at once. $\mathrm{sin} 90 ^{\circ} = 1$, but $\mathrm{sin} 45 ^{\circ} + \mathrm{sin} 45 ^{\circ} \approx 1 . 41$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_mJvr3JdCmyCfv84z`)

**FRONT**

How can a compound angle formula give the exact value of $\mathrm{sin} 75 \circ$?


**BACK**

Write $75 ^{\circ}$ as $45 ^{\circ} + 30 ^{\circ}$, both of which have exact values.

Expanding `\sin\left(45^{\circ} + 30^{\circ}\right)` then gives an exact answer in surds.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_9ZvmGrWpKqJMD2yV`)

**FRONT**

What are the three forms of $\mathrm{cos} 2 A$?


**BACK**

$\mathrm{cos}^{2} A - \mathrm{sin}^{2} A$, $2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} A - 1$, and $1 - 2 \mathrm{sin}^{2} A$.

All three are equal, so any of them may be used.

**FRONT**

**BACK**

Pick the one that leaves you with the function you actually want.

Use $2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} A - 1$ when the rest of the expression is in cosines, and $1 - 2 \mathrm{sin}^{2} A$ when it is in sines.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_MHWyHZ2bC6dtJRGg`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\mathrm{sin} 2 A$ is twice $\mathrm{sin} A$.


**BACK**

**False.**

Doubling the angle is not the same as doubling the value.

$\mathrm{sin} 60 ^{\circ} \approx 0 . 87$, whereas twice $\mathrm{sin} 30 ^{\circ}$ is exactly $1$.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_W4w6NN6DJb3kjfzT`)

**FRONT**

Why are there three forms of $\mathrm{cos} 2 A$ but only one of $\mathrm{sin} 2 A$?


**BACK**

Because $\mathrm{cos}^{2} A - \mathrm{sin}^{2} A$ contains two squares, and $\mathrm{sin}^{2} A + \mathrm{cos}^{2} A \equiv 1$ can be used to eliminate either one.

$2 \mathrm{sin} A \mathrm{cos} A$ has no square in it, so there is nothing to eliminate.

## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_rgPrjfZxhpb6Y3p9`)

**FRONT**

Define **harmonic form**.


**BACK**

Writing an expression such as $a \mathrm{sin} x + b \mathrm{cos} x$ as a single trigonometric function, `R \sin\left(x + \alpha\right)`.

It is the reverse of expanding with a compound angle formula, and can be thought of as factorising.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_PcQhh6HjZVy7bMKZ`)

**FRONT**

How do you rewrite an expression in harmonic form?


**BACK**

Expand the target form with the appropriate **compound angle formula**, then **equate coefficients** of $\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$.

That gives $R \mathrm{cos} α = a$ and $R \mathrm{sin} α = b$, which between them fix both unknowns.


Spec links: `spcpt_hXbbmBmmgzKY7p5f`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_3MDh3tfVQPBHXDrT`)

**FRONT**

Why does dividing the two coefficient equations give you $\mathrm{tan} α$?


**BACK**

Because the $R$s cancel, leaving $\frac{\mathrm{sin}α}{\mathrm{cos}α} = \frac{b}{a}$.

That quotient is $\mathrm{tan} α$, so $α = \mathrm{tan}^{-1} \frac{b}{a}$.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_fDnn6c3FYmVSSF4T`)

**FRONT**

Why is harmonic form useful?


**BACK**

It turns two trigonometric terms into **one**, which can then be solved or analysed like any single function.

The maximum of `R \sin\left(x + \alpha\right)` is $R$ and its minimum is $- R$, which is immediate once it is written that way.

## Card 18 — true_or_false (`fl_M24jRtVVy2FZVbT2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

There is only one correct harmonic form for a given expression.


**BACK**

**False.**

The same expression can be written with $\mathrm{sin}$ or with $\mathrm{cos}$, and with $+ α$ or $- α$, giving four equivalent forms.

A question will say which of them it wants.

**FRONT**

What does the period of a model tell you about the situation?


**BACK**

How long it takes for the behaviour to repeat exactly once.

For a tide model with $t$ in hours, a period of $12$ means the pattern comes round every $12$ hours.

**FRONT**

Why might you simplify a trigonometric model before interpreting it?


**BACK**

Because the behaviour is far easier to read from a single term than from two.

Writing $a \mathrm{sin} t + b \mathrm{cos} t$ in harmonic form, for instance, makes both the maximum and the period immediate.
