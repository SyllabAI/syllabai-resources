## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_m9m5SvqZQhMr4gff`)

**FRONT**

How do you prove a trigonometric identity?


**BACK**

Start on **one side** and work step by step until you reach the other.

Every step must itself be an identity, so that the chain holds for every value of the angle.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_XjRDK7mycTWH6MXP`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A trigonometric proof must start from the left-hand side.


**BACK**

**False.**

Either end is a legitimate starting point, and the right-hand side is sometimes the easier one.

What matters is starting from the **more complicated** side, since that is the one with something to simplify.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_qFZmDZrVDWn5V6Zq`)

**FRONT**

How does the target expression help you choose your next step in a proof?


**BACK**

It tells you which functions you have to end up with, so any identity moving you towards them is worth trying.

If the target is in $\mathrm{tan}$ alone, for instance, every $\mathrm{sin}$ and $\mathrm{cos}$ has to go.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_yNkrwbhQHwXPRdTn`)

**FRONT**

How can a compound angle formula help prove an identity that contains no sum of angles?


**BACK**

By substituting cleverly: $x$ can be written as `\left(x + y\right) - y`, or $2 x$ as $x + x$.

That creates a compound angle where none was visible, which makes the formula available.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_D42zSp45cTRZGZbf`)

**FRONT**

What makes trigonometric proofs involving fractions harder?


**BACK**

They often produce fractions within fractions, which have to be handled confidently.

Multiplying numerator and denominator by the same expression usually clears them in a single step.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_2F96KkjXVBgmVxVd`)

**FRONT**

You cannot see how to start a trigonometric proof. What is usually worth trying?


**BACK**

Write everything in terms of $\mathrm{sin}$ and $\mathrm{cos}$.

That clears $\mathrm{tan}$, $\mathrm{sec}$, $\text{cosec}$ and $\mathrm{cot}$ in one move, and usually makes the structure obvious.
