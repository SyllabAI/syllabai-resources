Exponential **growth** is modelled by `y = A \text{e}^{\_\_\_\_\_\_}` and exponential **decay** by `y = A \text{e}^{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`, where $t$ is time and $k > 0$.


**BACK**

Exponential **growth** is modelled by $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$ and exponential **decay** by $y = A \text{e}^{-kt}$, where $t$ is time and $k > 0$.

Because $k$ is taken positive, the sign in the power alone tells you which of the two it is.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_TzbsPTKr6q2zCfZB`)

**FRONT**

In $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$, what does $A$ represent?


**BACK**

The **initial value**, that is the value of $y$ when $t = 0$.

Substituting $t = 0$ gives $\text{e}^{0} = 1$, so $y = A$.

**FRONT**

Why is $t$ used rather than $x$ in these models?


**BACK**

Because most of the situations being modelled are **time-dependent**.

The mathematics is identical; only the label changes, to match what the variable actually stands for.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_HzvS8PzMns5rn8T2`)

**FRONT**

Why can any exponential $a^{x}$ be rewritten as $\text{e}^{kx}$?


**BACK**

Because a suitable $k$ can always be found, namely $k = \mathrm{ln} a$, since $a = \text{e}^{\mathrm{ln}a}$.

Working in base $\text{e}$ throughout makes the calculus much simpler, which is why models are written that way.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_fk7jyCNgyKkKDmyy`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The graph of $y = \mathrm{ln} x$ is the reflection of $y = \text{e}^{x}$ in the line $y = x$.


**BACK**

**True.**

They are inverse functions, and the graph of any inverse is that reflection.

So $y = \mathrm{ln} x$ passes through `\left(1 , 0\right)` where $y = \text{e}^{x}$ passes through `\left(0 , 1\right)`.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_jmjnmQtrYb3yGqQY`)

**FRONT**

What does $k$ control in $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$?


**BACK**

The **rate** of the growth or decay: a larger $k$ means a faster change.

So $A$ fixes where the model starts and $k$ fixes how quickly it moves.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_RzddrJyC8Q4Vmf2b`)

**FRONT**

What kinds of situation are modelled by exponential growth and decay?


**BACK**

**Growth**: populations of animals or humans, and investments under compound interest.

**Decay**: levels of radioactivity, the amount of a drug in the bloodstream, and depreciation in value.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_RgGvkVW8WbZMWJw5`)

**FRONT**

Why does an exponential model usually apply only for a limited time?


**BACK**

Because unlimited growth or decay is not realistic: a population cannot increase for ever, and an old car does not become worth nothing at all.

The model describes a phase of the situation rather than the whole of it.

## Card 9 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_6NYtgVtT7Y585Py5`)

**FRONT**

If $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$ then:

`\frac{\text{d} y}{\text{d} t} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

$\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}t} = A k \text{e}^{kt}$

That is $k$ times $y$ itself, so the rate of change is **proportional to the quantity**, which is the defining feature of exponential behaviour.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_mkck9Rr3yD5XR4qc`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An exponential decay model eventually reaches zero.


**BACK**

**False.**

The curve approaches zero but never gets there, because $\text{e}^{-kt}$ stays positive for every $t$.

In a real context that is one of the places the model stops being realistic.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_PqcpXsMRZtxP8Tht`)

**FRONT**

How would you find how long a sample takes to halve, given $N = 2000 \text{e}^{-kt}$?


**BACK**

Set $N = 1000$ and solve $1000 = 2000 \text{e}^{-kt}$ for $t$.

Dividing gives $\text{e}^{-kt} = 0 . 5$, and taking natural logarithms brings $t$ down out of the power.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_TgWJqpxScTcWC5fK`)

**FRONT**

Why plot $\mathrm{ln} y$ against $t$ instead of $y$ against $t$?


**BACK**

Because it turns an exponential curve into a **straight line**, which is far easier to fit and to read values from.

It also compresses a wide range of values onto a manageable scale.

## Card 14 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_H5vZNHZ6BdjXx6Fk`)

**FRONT**

Taking $\mathrm{ln}$ of both sides of $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$ gives:

`\ln y = \_\_\_\_\_\_ + \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

$\mathrm{ln} y = k t + \mathrm{ln} A$

That is $y = m x + c$ with $\mathrm{ln} y$ as the vertical variable, so plotting it against $t$ gives a straight line.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_zjZPc2RCRG2ByNct`)

**FRONT**

On a graph of $\mathrm{ln} y$ against $t$ for $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$, what do the gradient and intercept give you?


**BACK**

The gradient is $k$, and the vertical intercept is $\mathrm{ln} A$.

So $A$ is found by raising $\text{e}$ to the power of that intercept, **not** by reading it off directly.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_Zfm6BykbzkQ559cj`)

**FRONT**

How do you straighten a model of the form $y = A b^{kx}$?


**BACK**

Take logarithms of both sides, which brings the power down: $\mathrm{ln} y = k x \mathrm{ln} b + \mathrm{ln} A$.

Plotting $\mathrm{ln} y$ against $x$ then gives a straight line, this time of gradient $k \mathrm{ln} b$.

## Card 17 — true_or_false (`fl_wWs4j27Qzbc76ZG2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Plotting $\mathrm{ln} y$ against $\mathrm{ln} t$ would also straighten $y = A \text{e}^{kt}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

An exponential model straightens when $\mathrm{ln} y$ is plotted against $t$ itself.

Plotting $\mathrm{ln} y$ against $\mathrm{ln} t$ straightens a different kind of model, one in which $t$ is raised to a power.
