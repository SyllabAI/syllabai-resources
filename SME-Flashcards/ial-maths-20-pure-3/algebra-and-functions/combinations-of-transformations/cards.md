## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_rrhBDVsFpBnDP78v`)

**FRONT**

In `y = k \text{f} \left(a x + b\right) + c`, which transformations act horizontally?


**BACK**

The ones **inside** the brackets, $a$ and $b$.

The $k$ and the $c$, which sit outside the brackets, act vertically instead.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_mBb543t8CBYqNMrq`)

**FRONT**

Which do you apply first, the transformations inside the brackets or those outside?


**BACK**

**Inside first**, then outside.

Every transformation is applied to the graph **one at a time**, so the sequence has to be worked through rather than done all at once.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_XDWx3PPMzW4Nxppb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two transformations applied in either order always give the same final graph.


**BACK**

**False.**

Applying them in the wrong order generally produces a different graph, which is exactly why the rules about inside and outside exist.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_fqVtrTJHfnXWvRTY`)

**FRONT**

In what order do you apply transformations when a modulus is involved?


**BACK**

Everything **inside** the modulus sign first, then the effect of the **modulus** itself, then anything **outside** it.

The modulus behaves like a bracket: whatever it contains is dealt with before it acts.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_dzjpQQ4Cxb3tGgzr`)

**FRONT**

What happens to a graph's asymptotes under a combination of transformations?


**BACK**

They move too, and are transformed **one at a time in the same order** as the graph itself.

An asymptote is defined by the graph's own behaviour, so it follows the graph wherever the transformation sends it.
