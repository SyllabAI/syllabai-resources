## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_WprWJTYh7FxHH8tg`)

**FRONT**

What are the two sections of a statement of profit or loss, and what does each find?


**BACK**

The **trading section** finds the **gross profit**.

The **profit and loss section** finds the **profit for the year**, and the gross profit is what links the two.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_5DB4gVB6CFsN8ZJS`)

**FRONT**

Why does a service business's statement of profit or loss have no gross profit?


**BACK**

Because it does not buy or sell goods, so there is no **trading section** and no cost of sales.

The statement simply starts with the income received and subtracts the expenses.

**FRONT**

Which entries to the statement of profit or loss increase the profit?


**BACK**

**Credit** entries increase the profit, and these are usually the **incomes**.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_FtWjj5krzhfrT6Xw`)

**FRONT**

Define **operating profit**.


**BACK**

**Operating profit**, also called **profit from operations**, is the profit for the year **before the loan interest is subtracted**.

Where a business has no finance costs, the two figures are the same.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_6QkYJM2xsXcZ7BFq`)

**FRONT**

What does a statement of financial position show, and for what moment in time?


**BACK**

It shows the business's **assets, liabilities and capital** at a **particular date**.

It is a snapshot of the position on that one day, rather than a record of what happened over a period.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_fdqkmfgWnYxcYhgx`)

**FRONT**

How is the capital figure built up in the statement of financial position?


**BACK**

Finally subtract the **drawings** to give the capital at that date.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_Z7MRwMbfTtFDkdgs`)

**FRONT**

Why must the two halves of a statement of financial position have equal totals?


**BACK**

Because everything the business owns has been funded either by its **owner** or by someone it **owes**.

The total assets therefore equal the capital plus the liabilities.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_gjmNXxmx59ZYPDXp`)

**FRONT**

On a trial balance, how can you tell whether "bank" is an asset or a liability?


**BACK**

By the side the balance is on.

A **debit** balance means there is money in the bank, which is an asset; a **credit** balance means the account is overdrawn, which is a liability.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_Q8MBHtJrQZx5qSyn`)

**FRONT**

Why is the statement of financial position prepared after the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

Because the **profit or loss for the year** is needed before the capital section can be completed.

The profit increases the capital and a loss reduces it, so it has to be known first.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_W8rbR5Pby6tNcF5s`)

**FRONT**

Furniture costing \$15 000 is depreciated at 20% per annum reducing balance. What are the accumulated depreciation and net book value after two years?


**BACK**

The accumulated depreciation is **\$5400** and the net book value is **\$9600**.

The charges are 20% × \$15 000 = \$3000 and then 20% × \$12 000 = \$2400, and \$15 000 − \$5400 = \$9600.

**FRONT**

Why is the allowance for irrecoverable debts recalculated every year?


**BACK**

Because it is normally a **percentage of the trade receivables**, and that balance changes from one year to the next.

A fresh estimate is therefore needed at each year end.
