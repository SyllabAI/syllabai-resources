# Incomplete Records

## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_bPcgZthvg2RYCTkM`)

**FRONT**

Define **incomplete records**.


**BACK**

**Incomplete records** are the accounting records of a business that does **not keep a full set** of them.

Some transactions have been entered only once, or not at all.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_mS3ctyjb5qqNchZd`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Keeping incomplete records breaks the accounting principle of duality.


**BACK**

**True.**

Duality requires every transaction to have **two** entries, and incomplete records are kept with single entries instead.


Spec links: `spcpt_yShHpBdf2hd86Gn2`


## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_jPr8QzWxKcVSPc6h`)

**FRONT**

Why might a business end up with incomplete records?


**BACK**

The owner may **lack the technical skills** to keep a full set, or it may simply be the business's practice to make **single entries**.

Records can also be **lost, damaged or stolen**.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_5kzSmckkn38tRtyq`)

**FRONT**

What does an owner lose by not keeping a full set of records?


**BACK**

They cannot judge the **financial position** of the business or compare this year's performance with previous years.

**Fraud** becomes difficult to detect, and banks and lenders cannot readily be given the information they want.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_GkYGCCTzNVx9KG3V`)

**FRONT**

Which four methods recover missing figures from incomplete records?


**BACK**

The four methods, and what each is for:

- a **statement of affairs**, for the capital
- the **ledger accounts**, for cash, bank, expenses and income
- the **sales and purchases ledger control accounts**, for credit sales, credit purchases and the trade receivables and payables
- **mark-up, margin and inventory turnover**, for sales, purchases, gross profit and inventory

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_3VDnRhdntzh9yWRK`)

**FRONT**

Define **statement of affairs**.


**BACK**

A **statement of affairs** lists a business's assets and liabilities in order to find a **missing capital figure**.

It is used where there is no full set of records, so a statement of financial position cannot simply be drawn up.


Spec links: `spcpt_8khhZjs7Fs5JgjyS`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_7mbhVCwx4FJdBTGh`)

**FRONT**

Why can a statement of affairs find the capital when the records cannot?


**BACK**

Because the capital is simply what is left when the liabilities are taken away from the assets.

Those two are things a business still knows even without a full set of accounting records.


Spec links: `spcpt_8khhZjs7Fs5JgjyS`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_BVMxdfCtpt3ZxtTB`)

**FRONT**

A business's assets total \$69 800 and its liabilities \$21 100. What capital does the statement of affairs give?


**BACK**

**\$48 700.**

The capital is the assets less the liabilities, so \$69 800 − \$21 100 = \$48 700.

## Card 10 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_6QwBKsHCDfpSS925`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for finding the profit from the movement in capital:

Profit = closing capital − opening capital + `\_\_\_\_\_\_` − `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

Profit = closing capital − opening capital + **drawings** − **capital introduced**.

A positive result is a profit and a negative one a loss.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_Wjxfx27674X2s9Rc`)

**FRONT**

Why are drawings added when finding the profit from the capital movement?


**BACK**

Because the drawings have already reduced the closing capital.

Adding them back restores the profit the business actually made before the owner took anything out of it.


Spec links: `spcpt_8khhZjs7Fs5JgjyS`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_Ps7gw3dxCVdw9kJJ`)

**FRONT**

Why is capital introduced subtracted when finding the profit?


**BACK**

Because it raised the closing capital without the business having earned it.

Taking it out again leaves only the increase that the trading itself produced.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_z35bPWvdV9tjx6Kp`)

**FRONT**

Capital was \$48 700 at the start and \$80 500 at the end, with drawings of \$5000 and capital introduced of \$10 000. What is the profit?

**\$26 800.**

The capital rose by \$80 500 − \$48 700 = \$31 800, and then \$31 800 + \$5000 − \$10 000 = \$26 800.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_FGJ3q6qvzpYhG9qy`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

When a capital account is used to find the result, a profit is the balancing figure on the credit side.


**BACK**

**True.**

A profit balances on the **credit** side and a loss on the **debit** side, because a profit increases what the business owes its owner.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_jpT6Z6hMPpVzZxtv`)

**FRONT**

How can a ledger account be used to find a value you have not been given?


**BACK**

Draw up the account, enter every figure you **do** know on its correct side, and then find the **balancing figure**.

That balancing figure is the missing value, and the method works whenever only one figure is unknown.

Which values can a sales ledger control account be used to find?

The **credit sales**, and the trade receivables at the start or the end of the year.

It also gives the **receipts** from customers, the **discount allowed**, the **sales returns** and the **irrecoverable debts** written off.

## Card 23 — true_or_false (`fl_7BV2JSZZxKZRWVgj`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A 25% mark-up and a 25% margin give the same gross profit on the same goods.


**BACK**

**False.**

A mark-up is a percentage of the **cost of sales**, while a margin is a percentage of the **revenue**. The same percentage applied to different figures gives different results.


Spec links: `spcpt_8jcx7rw6BbBt8hb5`


## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_CTvMzcPprfC7p8zj`)

**FRONT**

Revenue is \$50 000 and the gross profit margin is 60%. What is the cost of sales?


**BACK**

**\$20 000.**

The margin is a percentage of revenue, so the gross profit is 60% × \$50 000 = \$30 000, and \$50 000 − \$30 000 = \$20 000.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_BjJ6pkBJ5N4TZbvq`)

**FRONT**

You know the revenue and the mark-up. How do you find the cost of sales?


**BACK**

Add the mark-up to 100% to get the revenue as a percentage of the cost of sales, then **divide** the revenue by that percentage.

A 20% mark-up makes the revenue 120% of the cost of sales.

## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_VKh3gwZYGXQZzw7s`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the cost of sales from the rate of inventory turnover?


**BACK**

**Multiply the average inventory by the rate.**

Dividing the cost of sales by the rate gives the average inventory instead, since the two are the other way round.

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_CYtwZmNwkYcnqXSF`)

**FRONT**

Average inventory is \$67 500, the rate of inventory turnover is 10 times and the mark-up is 33⅓%. What is the revenue?


**BACK**

**\$900 000.**

The cost of sales is \$67 500 × 10 = \$675 000, and the gross profit is 33⅓% × \$675 000 = \$225 000.

Adding the two gives \$675 000 + \$225 000 = \$900 000.

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_Gys6TmjdqkvWKsMT`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the purchases once the cost of sales is known?


**BACK**

Rearrange the cost of sales formula to give **purchases = cost of sales + closing inventory − opening inventory**.

That is the last step in recovering a trading section from incomplete records.
