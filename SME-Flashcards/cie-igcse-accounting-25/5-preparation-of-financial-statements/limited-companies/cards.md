**FRONT**

What do ordinary shareholders have that preference shareholders do not?


**BACK**

**Voting rights**, because the ordinary shareholders are the owners of the company.

Preference shareholders have no vote at shareholders' meetings.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_yQ4FZSCHz6t9KKyG`)

**FRONT**

How do preference dividends differ from ordinary dividends?


**BACK**

Preference shareholders receive a **fixed rate** based on the face value of their shares, and they are paid **before** ordinary shareholders.

An ordinary dividend is not fixed and may be withheld altogether in a poor year.


Spec links: `spcpt_pM2vgz5VQQ2CqPZt`


## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_8ZvVB5RFHTggSK4n`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A company can buy back its redeemable preference shares.


**BACK**

**True.**

That is what "redeemable" means: the company can buy them back at an agreed date and price. Non-redeemable preference shares cannot be bought back at all.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_cT2PwD4b8XzbQXtM`)

**FRONT**

Where does the dividend on a redeemable preference share appear?


**BACK**

As a **finance cost** in the statement of profit or loss.

Any part of it still unpaid at the year end is shown as a **current liability**.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_P9dpZTvJ87sZVr6n`)

**FRONT**

How does paying an ordinary dividend affect the financial statements?


**BACK**

It reduces the **retained earnings** and the **bank** balance in the statement of financial position.

Ordinary dividends are not accrued either, so they affect the statements only when they are actually paid.


Spec links: `spcpt_pM2vgz5VQQ2CqPZt`


## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_kRKm3YktXZqqNzqh`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Redeemable preference shares are part of a company's equity.


**BACK**

**False.**

They are treated as a **long-term loan** and appear under non-current liabilities. Only ordinary shares and **non-redeemable** preference shares count as equity.


Spec links: `spcpt_pM2vgz5VQQ2CqPZt`


## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_xmhXPbph8SQK793m`)

**FRONT**

Define **debenture**.


**BACK**

A **debenture** is a **long-term loan** to a company, repaid in full on a fixed future date.

It carries a **fixed rate of interest**, and debenture holders are repaid before shareholders if the company fails.


Spec links: `spcpt_pM2vgz5VQQ2CqPZt`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_k6ypS529rs9KMpt9`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between share capital and loan capital?


**BACK**

**Share capital** is raised by issuing shares, and those shareholders become part-owners of the company.

**Loan capital** is borrowed from outside the company and has to be repaid, debentures being the main example.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_GpRhrchRVkHyPgsq`)

**FRONT**

What is the only difference between a limited company's statement of profit or loss and a sole trader's?


**BACK**

The **finance costs**.

**Debenture interest** and **redeemable preference share dividends** are included there, and nothing of that kind arises for a sole trader.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_TYwMd4Xp6V45MSXT`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

All preference share dividends are finance costs in the statement of profit or loss.


**BACK**

**False.**

Only the dividends on **redeemable** preference shares are. Those on **non-redeemable** preference shares are distributions of profit and appear in the statement of changes in equity.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_7nTQC5bpVzQNtnNM`)

**FRONT**

Why is a redeemable preference share dividend treated as a finance cost?


**BACK**

Because redeemable preference shares are treated as a **long-term loan**, so their dividend behaves like loan interest.

It is a cost of borrowing rather than a share of the profit.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_vwQ4HWd6wdwmY3ph`)

**FRONT**

A company has 4% debentures of \$46 000 and none of the interest has been paid. What is the finance cost for the year?


**BACK**

**\$1840**, since 4% × \$46 000 = \$1840.

The whole year's interest is charged whether or not any of it has actually been paid.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_XzWgzMqwnXDxyB3V`)

**FRONT**

Define the **statement of changes in equity**.


**BACK**

The **statement of changes in equity** shows how a limited company's **equity has changed over the year**.

It sets out the movements in the share capital, the general reserve and the retained earnings.


Spec links: `spcpt_28Nh56fBJ9nS3pcT`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_hXbf8W22HKpftW3q`)

**FRONT**

What are the three things that change the retained earnings during the year?


**BACK**

The **profit for the year** is added.

The **dividends paid** are subtracted, and so is any **transfer to the general reserve**.

An **interim** dividend is paid partway through the year.

## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_sfPj4SKHpXyt9xFQ`)

**FRONT**

In what order are a limited company's three financial statements prepared?


**BACK**

Statement of **profit or loss**, then statement of **changes in equity**, then statement of **financial position**.

Each one needs a figure that the statement before it produces.
