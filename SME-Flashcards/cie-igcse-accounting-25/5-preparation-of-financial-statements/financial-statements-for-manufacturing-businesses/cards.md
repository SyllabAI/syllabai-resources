## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_v6ZGNXFjvtrj45rd`)

**FRONT**

Which three financial statements does a manufacturing business prepare?


**BACK**

A **manufacturing account**, which works out the total cost of making the goods.

Then a **statement of profit or loss** and a **statement of financial position**, as any other business would.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_PSHCfRQvbHgXnVPy`)

**FRONT**

Why might a manufacturer buy in finished goods as well as making its own?


**BACK**

It gains more goods to sell without having to take on more workers.

It also frees the existing workforce to produce other products.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_NDcY9WhdcHqwpTPP`)

**FRONT**

What are the drawbacks of a manufacturer buying in finished goods?


**BACK**

Buying them may cost more than making them.

The business also has **no control over their quality**, which it does have over its own production.

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_FjfjWTdyqtQVMcCW`)

**FRONT**

Define **direct costs**.


**BACK**

**Direct costs** are the costs that can be **directly linked to producing the goods**.

A cost that cannot be traced to the goods themselves is an indirect cost instead.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_8tqkT2rN9rMKPjdy`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for the prime cost:

Prime cost = direct material + direct `\_\_\_\_\_\_` + direct `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

Prime cost = direct material + direct **labour** + direct **expenses**.

Direct expenses are production costs that are neither materials nor labour, such as royalties or the hire of machinery.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_XR79GnZbyvwNGXjb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The wages of a factory supervisor are a direct cost.


**BACK**

**False.**

A supervisor does not make the goods, so those wages are an **indirect** cost and part of the factory overheads. Only the wages of people who make, assemble or package the goods count as direct labour.


Spec links: `spcpt_DNxT2swm9RFMtbCp`


## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_h2Cp6Wr7Wyt6mwqp`)

**FRONT**

Define **factory overheads**.


**BACK**

**Factory overheads** are the **indirect costs** of production, the costs of keeping the factory running from day to day.

Factory rent, heating and lighting, depreciation of machinery and the wages of cleaners are all examples.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_nKDFQJkWPKYRGGv9`)

**FRONT**

What three kinds of inventory does a manufacturing business hold?


**BACK**

**Raw materials**, **work in progress** and **finished goods**.

Each of the three has its own opening and closing balance.


Spec links: `spcpt_3B426PVHNmmjmKXy`


## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_kxKNtTJYbPvm73vn`)

**FRONT**

Define **work in progress**.


**BACK**

**Work in progress** is the inventory of goods that are **partly made** and not yet finished.

Like the other two inventories, it is a current asset in the statement of financial position.


Spec links: `spcpt_3B426PVHNmmjmKXy`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_jXYPWXhR2bF8JcXk`)

**FRONT**

Which of a manufacturer's inventories appear in the manufacturing account?


**BACK**

The **raw materials** and the **work in progress**.

The finished goods appear in the statement of profit or loss instead, since they are what the business actually sells.


Spec links: `spcpt_3B426PVHNmmjmKXy`


## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_4F27cfxtshjZDj5c`)

**FRONT**

Define the **cost of production**.


**BACK**

The **cost of production** is the total cost of making the goods during the year.

It is the final figure that the manufacturing account is prepared to work out.


Spec links: `spcpt_3BNrpRWHyHjRWpYj`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_F5gjNsp7brDR74DB`)

**FRONT**

How do you work out the cost of materials consumed?


**BACK**

Start with the **opening inventory of raw materials** and add the purchases, less any purchases returns, plus the carriage inwards.

Then subtract the **closing inventory of raw materials**.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_CBtnYKVHXrR6yYyD`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The closing work in progress is subtracted in the manufacturing account.


**BACK**

**True.**

The opening work in progress is added and the closing work in progress subtracted, so that only what was actually finished during the year reaches the cost of production.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_P7SyYgm64wcY9T4D`)

**FRONT**

A business runs both a factory and an office. Which of its expenses go into the cost of production?


**BACK**

Only the **factory** expenses.

The office expenses are not part of making the goods, so they are left for the profit and loss section instead.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_Hsh9SbMKcNkNh33f`)

**FRONT**

Opening raw materials were \$45 000, purchases \$160 000 and closing raw materials \$53 750. What is the cost of materials consumed?


**BACK**

**\$151 250.**

The opening inventory plus purchases give \$45 000 + \$160 000 = \$205 000, and \$205 000 − \$53 750 = \$151 250.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_kth4DyJc2gQmGFrG`)

**FRONT**

The prime cost is \$170 690, the factory overheads \$48 120, and the work in progress moved from \$6000 to \$8000. What is the cost of production?


**BACK**

**\$216 810.**

The prime cost plus the overheads give \$218 810, and then \$218 810 + \$6000 − \$8000 = \$216 810.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_Hkkyt8hzzQ9gDsQr`)

**FRONT**

What replaces purchases in a manufacturer's trading section, and where does it come from?


**BACK**

The **cost of production**, which is the final figure of the **manufacturing account**.

That is why the manufacturing account has to be prepared before the statement of profit or loss.


Spec links: `spcpt_x8VkGcBZX3h4R48T`


## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_m2r3Q62n43NsMgBy`)

**FRONT**

A manufacturer buys in some finished goods as well as making its own. How does that appear in the trading section?


**BACK**

Those purchases are **added to the cost of production**.

Together they make up the cost of everything the business had available to sell during the year.

Opening finished goods were \$12 450, the cost of production \$197 655 and closing finished goods \$14 650. What is the cost of sales?
