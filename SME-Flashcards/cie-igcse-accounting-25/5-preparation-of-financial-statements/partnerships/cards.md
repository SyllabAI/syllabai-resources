## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_67BpQHVjXszsrB6r`)

**FRONT**

Define **partnership agreement**.


**BACK**

A **partnership agreement** is a document setting out the **terms on which the partnership operates**.

Its purpose is to help the partners avoid disagreements later on.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_2GccsvzbchCggKmF`)

**FRONT**

Why are partners given interest on their capital?


**BACK**

To **reward** them for investing their own money in the business.

A partner who has put in more capital therefore earns more interest.


Spec links: `spcpt_WxSGP6KgDjM2QbtJ`


## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_9XWH8P4zqYgfKNkg`)

**FRONT**

Why is interest charged on partners' drawings?


**BACK**

To **discourage** partners from taking money out of the business.

It makes withdrawing money cost the partner something, which is the opposite purpose to interest on capital.


Spec links: `spcpt_WxSGP6KgDjM2QbtJ`


## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_djqfZZwGJVr7HhMq`)

**FRONT**

Why might a partner lend money to the business rather than invest more capital?


**BACK**

Because a **loan is repayable**, so the money is not tied up in the business, and it gives the partner a guaranteed income from the repayments.

It also avoids having to alter the partnership agreement.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_4rt3XfHxz86dmFrY`)

**FRONT**

How are losses shared between partners?


**BACK**

In the **same agreed ratio** as profits.

The agreement covers the distribution of profits **and** losses, so one ratio serves for both.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_GXTcCzTw5zNPZfxW`)

**FRONT**

Which partnership items are kept out of the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

**Interest on partners' capital**, **interest on partners' drawings**, and **partners' salaries**.

All three are dealt with in the appropriation account instead.


Spec links: `spcpt_p4chhSwqYdzSphkM`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_zTNp76vyqrqcDVFS`)

**FRONT**

Why is a partner's salary not an expense of the business?


**BACK**

Because it is not a payment for work in the way an employee's wage is: it is an agreed way of **dividing the profit** between the partners.

An employee's salary is a genuine expense and does appear in the statement of profit or loss.


Spec links: `spcpt_p4chhSwqYdzSphkM`


## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_rrfnHFWK9hg4Fs4G`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Interest on a partner's loan is treated differently from interest on a partner's capital.


**BACK**

**True.**

Loan interest is a **finance cost**, an expense in the statement of profit or loss. Interest on capital is a **share of the profit** and is dealt with in the appropriation account.


Spec links: `spcpt_p4chhSwqYdzSphkM`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_TF2V3hkGKP6sZ37r`)

**FRONT**

A partner lends the partnership \$5000 with loan interest at 5% per annum. How does that appear in the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

As a finance cost of **\$250**, since \$5000 × 5% = \$250.

It is subtracted after the profit from operations to give the profit for the year.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_tW2nrdX4VmnGYJyX`)

**FRONT**

Do the adjustments after a trial balance work differently for a partnership?


**BACK**

No, they work exactly as they do for a sole trader.

Depreciation, accruals, prepayments and irrecoverable debts are all dealt with in the same way.


Spec links: `spcpt_p4chhSwqYdzSphkM`


## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_NGfh5kcX8dycWP6k`)

**FRONT**

Define the **appropriation account**.


**BACK**

The **appropriation account** shows how a partnership's profit or loss for the year is **divided between the partners**.

It is prepared after the statement of profit or loss, following the terms of the partnership agreement.


Spec links: `spcpt_xmbRNPjWg6ZZ4fmp`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_5BnVThdqN9jhtPDc`)

**FRONT**

Why is the appropriation account prepared after the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

Because it starts with the **profit for the year**, which is what the statement of profit or loss produces.

There is nothing to divide between the partners until that figure is known.


Spec links: `spcpt_xmbRNPjWg6ZZ4fmp`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_JJhksJj8BCxq87gH`)

**FRONT**

Is the appropriation account part of the double entry system?


**BACK**

**Yes.**

The profit or loss for the year is transferred into it from the statement of profit or loss by a double entry, rather than simply being copied across.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_gp2QZDWc3bdDDsRC`)

**FRONT**

Why is the interest on drawings added rather than subtracted?


**BACK**

Because the account is written from the **business's point of view**, and that interest is money the partners **owe the business**.

It therefore increases the amount available to share out between them.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_Fz5Y28ZCt6xMWzKC`)

**FRONT**

What is subtracted from the profit before the residual profit is found?


**BACK**

The **interest on partners' capital** and the **partners' salaries**.

What remains is the residual profit, which is then shared in the agreed ratio.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_nctDgTgsFB9nnPNq`)

**FRONT**

The profit is \$30 000, interest on drawings totals \$850, interest on capital \$4250 and salaries \$6000. What is the residual profit?


**BACK**

Add the interest on drawings, then subtract the interest on capital and the salaries: \$30 000 + \$850 − \$4250 − \$6000 = \$20 600.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_dC86Zy49s4b8pjxq`)

**FRONT**

What is recorded in a partner's capital account?


**BACK**

Only the capital the partner has **invested**: the original amount, plus anything put in later, less any capital withdrawn.

Drawings and loans from the partner are never entered there.


Spec links: `spcpt_zCBgZPRGHk2HVrZs`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_k2FTP2DNtw8TzTqF`)

**FRONT**

Why do partnerships keep a capital account and a current account separately?


**BACK**

To keep **permanent** changes apart from **temporary** ones.

The capital account then shows plainly what each partner has invested, which also makes the interest on capital straightforward to calculate.


Spec links: `spcpt_zCBgZPRGHk2HVrZs` `spcpt_hD6NGkpTwYPjXJ2S`


## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_JY7m6ntZzNMm8Dyw`)

**FRONT**

Which entries go on the credit side of a partner's current account?


**BACK**

Anything that **increases** what the business owes the partner.

That means the **profit share**, the **interest on capital**, the **interest on a loan**, and a **partner's salary**.


Spec links: `spcpt_hD6NGkpTwYPjXJ2S`


## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_t7fN6kJwghv4BwZd`)

**FRONT**

Which entries go on the debit side of a partner's current account?


**BACK**

Anything that **decreases** what the business owes the partner.

That means the **loss** for the year, the partner's **drawings**, and the **interest on drawings**.


Spec links: `spcpt_hD6NGkpTwYPjXJ2S`


## Card 23 — true_or_false (`fl_sZDSJqTt5SB3rdY4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A partner receives cash when they are awarded a salary or interest on capital.


**BACK**

**False.**

No money moves at that point. The amount is credited to the partner's **current account**, and they may then take it out as drawings, which is when the bank account is used.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_kphWCCYMyxTSc3pg`)

**FRONT**

What does a debit balance on a partner's current account mean?


**BACK**

That the partner has **taken more out of the business than their share of the profits**.

A credit balance means the opposite: they have left part of their share in the business.


Spec links: `spcpt_hD6NGkpTwYPjXJ2S`


## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_b4TpFzPpWDX9B6nR`)

**FRONT**

What is the double entry for awarding interest on a partner's capital?


**BACK**

**Debit** the appropriation account and **credit** the partner's current account.

The appropriation account is where the profit is being divided, and the current account is where the partner's share builds up.

## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_Qb5zWGY5zstsNgwy`)

**FRONT**

How does a partnership's statement of financial position differ from a sole trader's?


**BACK**

Only in the **capital section**.

The assets and liabilities are set out in exactly the same way, but each partner's **capital account** and **current account** balances are shown separately.

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_PcWdszmmWR9nGJK2`)

**FRONT**

In the capital section, how is a debit balance on a partner's current account shown?


**BACK**

In **brackets**, and treated as a **negative** figure when the section is totalled.

It is subtracted rather than added, because that partner owes money back to the business.

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_MT5qg4D7YcW8xntP`)

**FRONT**

Which columns are used in the capital section of a partnership's statement of financial position?


**BACK**

One column for **each partner**, and one for the **total**.

Each partner's capital and current account balances go in their own column, and the combined figures go in the total column.

**FRONT**

A partner's capital account stands at \$12 000 and their current account works out at a debit balance of \$1000. What is their total?


**BACK**

**\$11 000.**

The current account balance is written as (1000) and subtracted, so \$12 000 − \$1000 = \$11 000.
