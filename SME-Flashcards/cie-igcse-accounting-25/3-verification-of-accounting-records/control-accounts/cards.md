## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_244WNBHxBxZb5yX6`)

**FRONT**

Define a **control account**.


**BACK**

A **control account** is a **summary** of all the balances and transactions for either trade receivables or trade payables. A **sales ledger** control account covers the credit customers, and a **purchases ledger** control account covers the credit suppliers.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_McjJDdcDhNcqK7vJ`)

**FRONT**

Where do the figures for a control account come from, and why does that matter?


**BACK**

From the **books of prime entry**, never from the ledger accounts. Using an independent source is what allows **errors in the ledger** to show up when the two are compared.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_PSz38JN2Y9W7jbyv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A control account that agrees with the ledger total proves the ledger is free of errors.


**BACK**

**False.**

It proves there are no **arithmetic** errors, but not that every entry is right. Errors of commission, omission, original entry and compensating errors all survive the check.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_PD926RFkVDvmdsWZ`)

**FRONT**

How does a control account reveal that an error has been made?


**BACK**

Its closing balance is compared with the **total of the individual ledger accounts**. If the two figures do not agree, there is an error somewhere in that ledger.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_TQrJk83W7mSWMpbb`)

**FRONT**

Give two advantages of using control accounts.


**BACK**

Control accounts:

- help **locate errors** in the sales or purchases ledger
- give the **total** for trade receivables or trade payables, so the statement of financial position can be prepared quickly
- help **reduce and prevent fraud**
- prove the **arithmetical accuracy** of the ledger

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_6Y22yc7WVyj6vGMW`)

**FRONT**

What is a contra entry between the sales and purchases ledgers?


**BACK**

It is where the same person is **both a credit customer and a credit supplier**, and the two balances are **offset** against each other. No money changes hands; the smaller balance is simply cancelled against the larger.


Spec links: `spcpt_Cwn6fzh7Dj855d2d`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_MnnRGrwht6vdssBy`)

**FRONT**

How is a contra entry between the sales and purchases ledgers recorded?


**BACK**

**Debit** the trade payables account and **credit** the trade receivables account. The **general journal** is the book of prime entry, and the entry carries a narrative explaining the offset.


Spec links: `spcpt_Cwn6fzh7Dj855d2d`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_992CS7J4t5DF797S`)

**FRONT**

Aadam owes Brie \$500 and Brie owes Aadam \$300. What happens if they agree a contra entry?


**BACK**

Each balance is reduced by **\$300**, the smaller of the two. Aadam still owes Brie **\$200**, and Brie now owes Aadam nothing.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_dN8W4djgqVYn7Sqp`)

**FRONT**

Which books of prime entry supply the figures for a sales ledger control account?


**BACK**

The **sales journal** and **sales returns journal**, the **cash book**, and the **general journal**. Between them they give credit sales, returns, money received, discounts allowed, dishonoured cheques, interest, contras and irrecoverable debts.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_vYn7vshVfGF6kv8V`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Cash sales appear in the sales ledger control account.


**BACK**

**False.**

Cash sales never enter the sales ledger at all, because no customer is left owing anything. Only **credit** sales, and money received against invoices, appear.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_hgqhPfW4GTJ2zxzW`)

**FRONT**

Name three items on the debit side of a sales ledger control account.


**BACK**

The debit side carries:

- the **opening balance** for customers who owe money
- **sales**, credit sales only
- **interest** charged on overdue accounts
- **bank**, for dishonoured cheques and refunds paid to customers

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_2QMxFbGQhSpTvWB8`)

**FRONT**

Why might a sales ledger control account have a credit balance?


**BACK**

Because the business **owes a customer** money. That happens when a customer pays in advance, overpays, or is owed a refund for goods already paid for and returned.


Spec links: `spcpt_Ps7XCDhrS3ytGqYR`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_g7rbMfgjtRFJWnr9`)

**FRONT**

Where do irrecoverable debts written off appear in a sales ledger control account, and why?


**BACK**

They appear on the **credit** side. Writing the debt off means the customer no longer owes the money, so the total owed to the business falls.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_FzTqrF4vXWSBZZzV`)

**FRONT**

Where does a dishonoured cheque appear in a sales ledger control account, and why?


**BACK**

It appears on the **debit** side, entered as **bank**. The payment has been reversed, so the customer owes the money once again.

## Card 20 — true_or_false (`fl_b4SSGKXVqmmspDVj`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A contra entry appears on the debit side of the purchases ledger control account.


**BACK**

**True.**

A contra **debits** trade payables and credits trade receivables, so it reduces what the business owes. It therefore sits on the debit side here, and on the credit side of the sales ledger control account.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_gXyKrfCZn34jTXKv`)

**FRONT**

Why are cash purchases left out of a purchases ledger control account?


**BACK**

They never enter the **purchases ledger** at all. Paying at the time of purchase means no supplier is ever owed anything, so there is no balance to control.

**FRONT**

Where does interest charged by a credit supplier appear, and why?


**BACK**

Interest charged by a supplier appears on the **credit** side. It increases what the business owes, just as a further credit purchase would.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_sNRXvSPsssnwJcTH`)

**FRONT**

A purchases ledger control account opens with a credit balance of \$17 150 and a debit balance of \$1350. What does that mean?


**BACK**

The business **owes \$17 150** to most of its suppliers, while other suppliers **owe it \$1350**. The two are shown separately rather than netted off against each other.
