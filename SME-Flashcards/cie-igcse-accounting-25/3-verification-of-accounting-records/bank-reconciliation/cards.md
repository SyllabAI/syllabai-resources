## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_h3PYwj6B4zKxpkgY`)

**FRONT**

From whose point of view is a bank statement written, and what does that mean for its entries?


**BACK**

From the **bank's** point of view. A **debit** entry means money has **left** the account, and a **credit** entry means money has **entered** it.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_79R9RB4CgdxN822x`)

**FRONT**

Define an **unpresented cheque**.


**BACK**

An **unpresented cheque** is one the business has **written** but which the recipient has **not yet paid into their bank**. It is in the cash book but has not yet reached the bank statement.


Spec links: `spcpt_R5phbk5HTB9HpSdC`


## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_8Gd9YhffGZq49x7J`)

**FRONT**

Which items appear on the bank statement but not yet in the cash book?


**BACK**

Bank transfers the business has not recorded, such as direct debits, standing orders, credit transfers and dividends. Also amounts applied by the bank itself, such as **bank charges** and **interest**, and any **dishonoured cheques**.


Spec links: `spcpt_R5phbk5HTB9HpSdC`


## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_d6SkpHYF8PGHzznW`)

**FRONT**

Define an **uncredited deposit**.


**BACK**

An **uncredited deposit** is money the business has **paid in** but which the bank has **not yet cleared**. It is in the cash book but has not yet reached the bank statement.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_j4WMVnctW9jHfBFd`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A business can correct an error that it finds on its bank statement.


**BACK**

**False.**

Only the **bank** can correct its own error, so the business must tell them and ask for it to be put right. Errors in the **cash book** are the business's own to fix.


Spec links: `spcpt_R5phbk5HTB9HpSdC`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_DFxWysSVWpZ6djJS`)

**FRONT**

How do you enter a bank statement transaction into the cash book?


**BACK**

**Debit** the cash book if the transaction **increased** the bank balance, and **credit** it if the transaction **decreased** it. The entries are the **opposite** way round from how they appear on the bank statement.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_bFK4kwncDjgMmvHp`)

**FRONT**

Give two reasons why a bank might dishonour a cheque.


**BACK**

A cheque may be dishonoured because:

- it is **not dated or signed**
- the amount written in **words** does not agree with the amount in **figures**
- the person who wrote it has **insufficient funds** to cover it

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_St9qypGgrrMGdbJ2`)

**FRONT**

After updating the cash book, why might the two balances still not match?


**BACK**

Because of **timing**: cheques written or paid in may not have cleared the bank yet. Those differences cannot be entered anywhere, so a **bank reconciliation statement** is prepared to explain them.


Spec links: `spcpt_yBccpvmcrXJtFHdQ`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_cgc8fjFvz97NHfBN`)

**FRONT**

What is the purpose of a bank reconciliation statement?


**BACK**

It **explains why** the balance on the bank statement differs from the bank balance in the cash book. It sets out the unpresented cheques, uncredited deposits and errors that account for the gap.


Spec links: `spcpt_yPXt72qncMcVHNFw`


## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_Nz9RDVhnfycY6pp6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Uncredited deposits are debit entries in the cash book that are missing from the bank statement.


**BACK**

**True.**

They are money the business has **paid in** that the bank has not yet cleared, so they sit on the **debit** side of the cash book with nothing to match on the statement. Unpresented cheques are the mirror image, on the credit side.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_6T9zJ5K28SS4Nm8z`)

**FRONT**

Why is a reconciliation statement needed rather than simply updating the records?


**BACK**

The **cash book** can be updated with anything missing from it, but the business **cannot alter the bank statement**. The differences that remain have nowhere to be entered, so a statement is prepared to show them.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_4Y9gkWKNFD9Bm7wP`)

**FRONT**

What must you do before preparing a bank reconciliation statement?


**BACK**

**Update the cash book** using the bank statement, and work out the new balance. The reconciliation then explains only what is left over after that.


Spec links: `spcpt_ShDsTtvdYVYd3xtQ`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_pd8KhGFdcwZjZZz9`)

**FRONT**

Starting from the bank statement balance, what is added and what is subtracted?


**BACK**

**Add** the uncredited deposits and **subtract** the unpresented cheques. Bank errors are added if they left the statement balance too low, and subtracted if they left it too high.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_jJQw9P7m38WjKTpj`)

**FRONT**

What does a negative figure at the end of a bank reconciliation statement mean?


**BACK**

It means the **cash book** should show a **credit balance**, so the business is overdrawn on its own records too. A positive figure means a debit balance, with money in the bank.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_WnKnXcZdS3MnY7H6`)

**FRONT**

A bank statement shows an account \$345 overdrawn, with uncredited deposits of \$900 and unpresented cheques of \$950. What is the cash book balance?


**BACK**

The cash book shows a **credit balance of \$395**, so it is overdrawn as well. Starting at −\$345, adding \$900 gives \$555, and subtracting \$950 gives **−\$395**.
