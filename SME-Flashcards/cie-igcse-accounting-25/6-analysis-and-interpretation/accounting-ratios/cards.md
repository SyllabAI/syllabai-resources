## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_8dP9VQBFVyJBJNBv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A mark-up of more than 100% is possible.


**BACK**

**True.**

The mark-up is measured against the **cost of sales**, so it can easily exceed 100%. A gross profit **margin** cannot, because the gross profit is only ever part of the revenue.

How can a business improve its gross profit margin?


**BACK**

By **increasing the selling price** of its goods, or by buying them from a **cheaper supplier**.

Both widen the gap between the revenue and the cost of sales.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_9w8DFYCBj7vWHcTX`)

**FRONT**

What does the profit margin tell you, and how does it differ from the gross profit margin?


**BACK**

It is the proportion of the revenue turned into **profit for the year**, after other income and all the expenses.

The gross profit margin looks only at the gross profit, before any expenses are taken off.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_ZbY5fM4KkByP33dM`)

**FRONT**

Define **capital employed**.


**BACK**

**Capital employed** is the equity, or the owner's capital, plus the **non-current liabilities**.

It is the total long-term finance the business has to work with.


Spec links: `spcpt_hK6CKYPjPBpwgBZb`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_Y4CftQ3BSYxKCcgR`)

**FRONT**

Which profit figure does the return on capital employed use, and why?


**BACK**

The profit **before finance costs**, so any loan or debenture interest has to be added back on.

The ratio measures the return on all the long-term finance, including the part that was borrowed.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_FnwJvQc3v8q8chmG`)

**FRONT**

How does the acid test ratio differ from the current ratio?


**BACK**

It leaves the **inventory** out of the current assets.

Inventory is the current asset that is hardest to turn into cash quickly, so removing it gives a stricter test.

**BACK**

They are the same measurement in different units, so dividing 365 by either one gives the other.

## Card 52 — question_and_answer (`fl_HjmNPpm5x8nfYSpj`)

**FRONT**

How does the historic cost principle limit a set of financial statements?


**BACK**

Every transaction is recorded at its **actual cost at the time it happened**.

Transactions from different times are therefore hard to compare fairly, because prices will have moved in between.
