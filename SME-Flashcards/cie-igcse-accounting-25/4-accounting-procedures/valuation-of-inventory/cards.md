## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Y7nV8YCTfSHSdY7y`)

**FRONT**

Define the **net realisable value** of inventory.


**BACK**

The **net realisable value** is the amount a business expects to receive from selling the goods, after the costs of selling them.

It is the **selling price minus any selling expenses**, such as repairs to damaged goods.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_2dVJ626dsYDP2Yhb`)

**FRONT**

At what value is inventory shown in the financial statements?


**BACK**

At the **lower** of its cost and its net realisable value.

This follows the principle of **prudence**, so that neither the asset nor the profit is overstated.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_h7ckhZSv2nzTGzCj`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Inventory is normally valued at its net realisable value.


**BACK**

**False.**

For most inventory the **cost** is the lower of the two figures, so the cost is what is normally used. Net realisable value takes over only in particular cases.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_kTMqD9bxjFYH6dss`)

**FRONT**

When is the net realisable value lower than the cost?


**BACK**

When goods have been **damaged** and need repair before they can be sold, when they are **close to their expiry date**, or when they are being sold cheaply to **promote a new product**.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_nXGQptTddm48Jz3F`)

**FRONT**

An item cost \$15 plus \$3 delivery, and after \$8 of repairs it could be sold for \$24. What is its inventory value?


**BACK**

**\$16.**

The cost is \$15 + \$3 = \$18 and the net realisable value is \$24 − \$8 = \$16, and inventory takes the lower of the two.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_zZgDyyYgsHVp8S6x`)

**FRONT**

How do you value an inventory made up of several different items?


**BACK**

Compare the cost and the net realisable value **for each item separately**, take the lower figure, and multiply it by the number of units.

Adding those item totals together gives the valuation of the whole inventory.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_4FZCPyRrGq9p4XGV`)

**FRONT**

How does the value of the closing inventory affect the profit?


**BACK**

A higher closing inventory gives a **higher** profit.

The closing inventory is **subtracted** from the cost of sales, so a larger figure makes the cost of sales smaller.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_yvGXD7vfZMGtBX2m`)

**FRONT**

How does the value of the opening inventory affect the profit?


**BACK**

A higher opening inventory gives a **lower** profit.

The opening inventory is **added** to the cost of sales, so a larger figure makes the cost of sales bigger.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_XRRBtHG7rsrGBDrq`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An error in the opening inventory affects the assets in the statement of financial position.


**BACK**

**False.**

The opening inventory does not appear in the statement of financial position at all. Only the **closing** inventory does, under current assets.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_KyBxsHCnD3J6grjk`)

**FRONT**

The opening inventory has been undervalued. What is the effect on the profit for the year?


**BACK**

Both the gross profit and the profit for the year are **overstated**.

Too little has been added to the cost of sales, so the cost of sales comes out too low.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_S9C8V87pcj7QkccC`)

**FRONT**

The closing inventory has been overvalued. What is the effect on the statement of financial position?


**BACK**

Both the **assets** and the **capital** are **overstated**.

The closing inventory is a current asset, and the profit it has overstated is carried into the capital.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_c6hCHCSTG26CDzMX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An inventory error changes the gross profit and the profit for the year by the same amount.


**BACK**

**True.**

The error sits in the **cost of sales**, which feeds into both figures, so the two move in the same direction and by the same amount.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_SHHmw3PPpKsrszKN`)

**FRONT**

Why does an error in the opening inventory leave the capital at the end of the year unaffected?


**BACK**

Because the same figure was the previous year's closing inventory, so it distorted that year's profit in the **opposite** direction.

The two errors cancel, and the closing capital is correct even though each year's profit was wrong.
