## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_vwtzSG5WvH6twY6V`)

**FRONT**

Define the **matching** principle.


**BACK**

The **matching** principle states that costs and revenues should be recorded in the financial period in which they are **incurred**.

That is not necessarily the period in which the money is paid or received.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_6vPWSnN3mB9FXvBz`)

**FRONT**

Why might the rent expense in the statement of profit or loss differ from the rent actually paid?


**BACK**

Because the statement shows the amount **due for that financial period**, not the amount that happened to be paid.

The two differ whenever the period covered by the invoice does not line up with the financial year.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_DPTkp8HkHSMnNrNN`)

**FRONT**

An invoice covers January 2023 to July 2024, and the financial period is March 2023 to February 2024. How is the invoice split?


**BACK**

The January and February 2023 part belongs to **last year's** statement of profit or loss.

March 2023 to February 2024 belongs to **this year's**, and March to July 2024 belongs to **next year's**.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_PqMZx5M3SFWhvBmB`)

**FRONT**

Define **accrued expense**.


**BACK**

An **accrued expense** is an expense that is still **owed** at the end of a financial period.

It is also called an **accrual**, and the expense is said to be **in arrears**.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_hxdwmd9kW9tKq5vx`)

**FRONT**

Why does an accrued expense arise?


**BACK**

Because the business has **paid less than the amount due** for the period.

This usually happens when the supplier's invoice period does not line up with the business's financial year.

**FRONT**

It appears in the statement of financial position as a **current liability**.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_Zp7qMwbJXj2JGmqb`)

**FRONT**

A customer pays in advance for a service to be provided next year. Why is that money a liability for the business?


**BACK**

Until it does, it owes the customer either the service or the money back, so the amount is a **current liability**.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_f9zpftXc6zBYq6mk`)

**FRONT**

Rent receivable of \$6300 is due for the year, but only \$5800 has been received. What is the accrued income?


**BACK**

The accrued income is **\$500**, since \$6300 − \$5800 = \$500.

It is shown as a **current asset**, and the full \$6300 still goes to the statement of profit or loss.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_pRJCRqPtRcxVF5hG`)

**FRONT**

How does finding the amount paid differ from finding the amount due?


**BACK**

Every adjustment is **reversed**.

An amount that would be added when finding the amount due is subtracted when finding the amount paid, and the other way round.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_68dM8D3CtvBdWfRt`)

**FRONT**

Commission of \$4200 a year is owed, with \$700 in arrears at the start and three months owed at the end. How much was paid during the year?


**BACK**

**\$3850.**

The opening arrears were paid during the year, so they are added, while the closing arrears of 3 × \$350 = \$1050 were not, so they are subtracted.

That gives \$4200 + \$700 − \$1050 = \$3850.

Where do the opening and closing balances go when an expense account has an accrual?

They sit on opposite sides, because this year's closing balance becomes next year's opening balance.

**FRONT**

Why are an income account's entries on the opposite sides from an expense account's?


**BACK**

Because money received is **credited** to the account while money paid is **debited** to it.

Every other entry follows from that, which makes the two accounts mirror images of one another.

## Card 36 — question_and_answer (`fl_9DzTs94mnsDvgVCd`)

**FRONT**

A tenant pays \$8400 covering 16 months' rent. What is the monthly rent, and how much goes to the statement of profit or loss for the 12-month financial year?


**BACK**

The monthly rent is \$8400 ÷ 16 = **\$525**.

The amount for the financial year is therefore 12 × \$525 = **\$6300**.

## Card 37 — question_and_answer (`fl_VDnX3fkyqCH6Zq7H`)

**FRONT**

A single rent payment covers 16 months when the financial year is only 12 months long. What might the other four months represent?


**BACK**

Some of them clear **arrears** owed from before the year began, and the rest are **paid in advance** for next year.

Only the twelve months of the financial year itself go to the statement of profit or loss.
