## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_BDkcFVQPPFQmQgx3`)

**FRONT**

Define **capital expenditure**.


**BACK**

**Capital expenditure** is money spent on **non-current assets** for the long-term benefit of the business. It appears in the **statement of financial position**, never in the statement of profit or loss.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_QPBQdMw9VNcjDDFg`)

**FRONT**

Give three costs that are capital expenditure besides the purchase price.


**BACK**

Capital expenditure also covers the cost of:

- **delivering** the asset
- **installing** it
- **legal costs** on the purchase
- **decorating** it when new, and **extending** it later

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_Qpj6vgBGDsSCW6Y7`)

**FRONT**

Define **revenue expenditure**.


**BACK**

**Revenue expenditure** is money spent on the **day-to-day running costs** of the business. It appears in the **statement of profit or loss**, never as an asset in the statement of financial position.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_65F7RKHjCnrswqTY`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Extending an existing warehouse is capital expenditure.


**BACK**

**True.**

An **extension** makes the asset bigger and gives the business long-term benefit, so it is capital expenditure. A **repair** would not be, because it only keeps the asset working as before.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_QSM2hmgjrF6CPrRN`)

**FRONT**

Why is decorating a new asset treated differently from redecorating an old one?


**BACK**

Decorating a new asset is part of **getting it ready to use**, so it is capital expenditure. Redecorating an existing one only **maintains** what is already there, which is a running cost and so revenue expenditure.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_vpGVPNJCJzNKm54J`)

**FRONT**

A vehicle costs \$27 500, with delivery \$300, insurance \$800 and fuel \$400. What is the capital expenditure?


**BACK**

The capital expenditure is **\$27 800**. Only the vehicle and its **delivery** are capital, so \$27 500 + \$300 = **\$27 800**, while the insurance and fuel are running costs.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_3mSGJPTZQyx9d6tR`)

**FRONT**

Define a **capital receipt**.


**BACK**

A **capital receipt** is money received from something **outside** the business's day-to-day trading. Capital receipts are **one-off**, and they never appear in the statement of profit or loss.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_MnsjWN5HWRXr4WFb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Only the profit or loss on selling a non-current asset reaches the statement of profit or loss, not the sale proceeds.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **proceeds** are a capital receipt and stay out of the statement of profit or loss altogether. Only the **profit or loss** on the sale goes there, and it is worked out against the asset's **net book value** rather than its original cost.


Spec links: `spcpt_4sjmFTwJfSpRcc9K`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_RHHQtMtnYW7Zcx7G`)

**FRONT**

Which parts of the statement of financial position can a capital receipt affect?


**BACK**

It can change the **non-current assets**, the **current assets**, the **non-current liabilities** or the **capital**. Selling an asset reduces non-current assets, taking a loan increases non-current liabilities, and money coming in raises the bank balance.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_BtHPMWpPzYyDcQ9R`)

**FRONT**

Capital expenditure is wrongly treated as revenue expenditure. What are the effects?


**BACK**

**Expenses** are overstated, so the **profit** is understated. In the statement of financial position the **non-current assets** are understated, and the **capital** is understated too because of the lower profit.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_38VqdTy8Psbfk8wZ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Some non-current assets should be treated as expenses rather than as assets.


**BACK**

**True.**

Low-value items such as calculators, staplers and waste bins are treated as **expenses** instead. Recording and tracking them as assets would cost the business more effort than the information is worth.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_wf9Xx8CTKrYk7JK2`)

**FRONT**

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_gFNjyhD5j83GMD5f`)

**FRONT**

Why does misclassifying expenditure affect the capital as well as the profit?


**BACK**

The **profit for the year** is transferred to the **capital** account at the end of the period. Anything that misstates the profit therefore misstates the capital by the same amount.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_Qs3Q7VZVF2pfhRh3`)

**FRONT**

A draft profit of \$45 700 includes a \$10 000 bank loan treated as a revenue receipt. What is the correct profit?


**BACK**

The correct profit is **\$35 700**. A loan is a **capital receipt** and should never have been counted as income, so \$45 700 − \$10 000 = **\$35 700**.
