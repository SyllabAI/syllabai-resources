# Irrecoverable Debts & Allowance for Irrecoverable Debts

## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_kwntVhcPRCfvMHkH`)

**FRONT**

Define **irrecoverable debt**.


**BACK**

An **irrecoverable debt** is an amount owed by a credit customer that the business is **unable to collect**.

It used to be called a **bad debt**.


Spec links: `spcpt_PWVJkYFbw3BGJ3DP`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZdsxGgFpWxXhFrdG`)

**FRONT**

Why are irrecoverable debts written off?


**BACK**

To follow the principle of **prudence**.

Leaving the debt in the accounts would **overstate the assets**, because the money is not going to arrive.


Spec links: `spcpt_PWVJkYFbw3BGJ3DP`


## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_jvXczGjv36n2Gwwh`)

**FRONT**

What is the double entry to write off an irrecoverable debt?


**BACK**

**Credit** the customer's account in the sales ledger, because the amount owed is falling.

**Debit** the irrecoverable debts account, because the write-off is an **expense**.

The book of prime entry is the **journal**.


Spec links: `spcpt_PWVJkYFbw3BGJ3DP`


## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_DYgRFXzTn8FZmvKX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A debt should be written off as soon as a customer misses a payment.


**BACK**

**False.**

The business should first try everything it reasonably can to collect the money. A debt is written off once collection has become genuinely unlikely, such as when the customer is bankrupt or cannot be traced.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_4XTfKjK7nG5r2Qw3`)

**FRONT**

Julius owes \$1200 and pays \$500 before being declared bankrupt. How much is written off, and on which side of his account?


**BACK**

**\$700**, entered on the **credit** side of his account in the sales ledger.

That entry closes the account, because \$500 + \$700 = \$1200.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_F2q2RVzJP7QMvw7P`)

**FRONT**

How can a business reduce its irrecoverable debts?


**BACK**

By managing credit carefully:

- setting a **credit limit** for each customer
- running **credit checks** on new customers
- **communicating regularly** about balances owed
- taking **legal action** as a last resort

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_hnpXHnx96gw67BMc`)

**FRONT**

How can a debt that has already been written off come back?


**BACK**

The customer pays after all, either in full or in part.

This can follow renewed contact with the customer, an unexpected payment, or the use of a debt collection service.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_FRJDVPX6DszDdKtK`)

**FRONT**

What is the book of prime entry for the recovery of a debt written off?


**BACK**

The **cash book**.

A recovery begins with money actually being received, and that is what the cash book records.


Spec links: `spcpt_FHrND9BzXnbywTxR`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_R3pKq6Gb2ZKkJ6kb`)

**FRONT**

How is a recovery recorded when the debt was written off in the same year?


**BACK**

The customer's account is used again: the debt is **put back** into the sales ledger, and the payment is then recorded in the normal way.

So the account is debited to restore the debt, and the debts recovered account is credited.


Spec links: `spcpt_FHrND9BzXnbywTxR`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_yMyjwJKWJxzgGyH2`)

**FRONT**

How is a recovery recorded when the debt was written off in an earlier financial year?


**BACK**

The sales ledger is not used at all.

**Debit** the cash or bank account with the money received, and **credit** the debts recovered account.

**True or False?**

A debt recovered can be set against the irrecoverable debts account instead of being shown separately.


**BACK**

The balance on the debts recovered account can be transferred to the irrecoverable debts account, so that only the net expense reaches the statement of profit or loss.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_TkQv5bWqT6GHMHyC`)

**FRONT**

\$750 was written off and \$300 of it was recovered in the same year. What single figure could go to the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

**\$450** as an expense, since \$750 − \$300 = \$450.

The alternative is to show the \$750 expense and the \$300 income separately, and both presentations are acceptable.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_C2N7hBr3QNrDHb2w`)

**FRONT**

Define **allowance for irrecoverable debts**.


**BACK**

An **allowance for irrecoverable debts** is an **estimate** of the amount of a period's sales that will turn out to be irrecoverable.

It is an estimate of future losses, not a debt that has actually gone bad.


Spec links: `spcpt_SdFbzMpmsf3hNVwY`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_TR54NV9kH2n6kWN6`)

**FRONT**

Which two accounting principles does an allowance for irrecoverable debts follow?


**BACK**

**Prudence**, because neither the assets nor the profit are overstated.

**Matching**, because the estimated loss is charged as an expense in the same period as the sales that gave rise to it.


Spec links: `spcpt_SdFbzMpmsf3hNVwY`


## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_fxjH5BXctYn97ks8`)

**FRONT**

How is an allowance for irrecoverable debts first set up?


**BACK**

**Debit** the statement of profit or loss with the estimated amount and **credit** the allowance for irrecoverable debts account.

No entry is made in any customer's account in the sales ledger, because no particular debt has gone bad.


Spec links: `spcpt_SdFbzMpmsf3hNVwY`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZWxQmwCVc4kHppMg`)

**FRONT**

Why does an allowance for irrecoverable debts account have a credit balance?


**BACK**

Because it represents a **reduction in an asset**, namely the amount owed by trade receivables.

It is kept in a separate account, just as a provision for depreciation is kept apart from the asset account it relates to.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZpxywJs6gsMsqpzj`)

**FRONT**

The allowance for irrecoverable debts increases this year. How does that affect the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

Only the **increase** is charged, as an **expense**.

The whole allowance is not charged again, because the rest of it was already charged in earlier years.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_4bvWJZ77H9PNTB8B`)

**FRONT**

The allowance for irrecoverable debts decreases this year. How does that affect the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

The **decrease** is treated as an **income**, so it increases the profit for the year.

Part of the expense charged in earlier years is being given back, because the estimate has turned out to be too cautious.

**True or False?**

When an allowance for irrecoverable debts is first created, the whole amount is charged as an expense.


**BACK**

**True.**

In the first year the allowance has risen from **zero**, so the increase and the whole allowance are the same figure.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_XQ3T9Vj6QGRv6n6Y`)

**FRONT**

An allowance of \$1530 is to be changed to 3% of trade receivables of \$48 500. What goes to the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

The new allowance is 3% × \$48 500 = **\$1455**, a decrease of \$75.

That \$75 is treated as an **income**, so it increases the profit for the year by \$75.
