## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_HVQv6pm4JJqkXhgV`)

**FRONT**

Define **depreciation**.


**BACK**

**Depreciation** is the financial measure of how a **non-current asset** loses value over time. It spreads the cost of the asset across its expected useful life.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_WJR3yHTH4WPsHb9J`)

**FRONT**

Give three reasons why a non-current asset loses value.


**BACK**

A non-current asset loses value through:

- **wear and tear**
- becoming **outdated or obsolete**
- the **reduction in its remaining useful life**
- being **used up or depleted**

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Depreciation is an expense even though no money is spent.


**BACK**

**True.**

Nothing leaves the bank when depreciation is charged. It records the **loss in value** the asset has already suffered, which is a real cost to the business even though no payment is made.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_Yvpjj4rk9hTWZGJx`)

**FRONT**

Which accounting principle explains why an asset's cost is spread over its useful life?


**BACK**

The principle is **matching**. The cost of the asset is set against the **income it helps to earn**, so a \$30 000 vehicle expected to last eight years is charged over those eight years rather than all at once.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_6Hr9XZFHFJNKNmJf`)

**FRONT**

When is the straight-line method appropriate?


**BACK**

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_QyDRZWFm6HwdGMSt`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An asset depreciated by the straight-line method can reach a net book value of zero.


**BACK**

**True.**

Once the total depreciation charged equals the cost, the asset is **fully depreciated** and its net book value is zero. That cannot happen under the reducing balance method, where each charge is only a fraction of what is left.


Spec links: `spcpt_WRzvRkrvngbg2f2k`

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_vwX5GtKjY4ZTsnp4`)

**FRONT**

Machinery cost \$18 000 and is depreciated at 15% per annum straight-line. What is its net book value after 3 years?


**BACK**

The net book value is **\$9900**. Each year's charge is 15% × \$18 000 = \$2700, so three years come to \$8100, and \$18 000 − \$8100 = **\$9900**.


Spec links: `spcpt_WRzvRkrvngbg2f2k`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_gr78XfrmXQ4WfTSv`)

**FRONT**

A vehicle cost \$30 000 and is expected to be worth \$12 000 after 3 years. What is the yearly depreciation?


**BACK**

The yearly charge is **\$6000**. The loss in value is \$30 000 − \$12 000 = \$18 000, and \$18 000 ÷ 3 = **\$6000**.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_GPWm2Qx78HXBVbqY`)

**FRONT**

What does the reducing balance method of depreciation assume?


**BACK**

It assumes the asset loses value **in proportion to its current value**. The charge is therefore a fixed percentage of the **net book value**, not of the original cost.


Spec links: `spcpt_5J94bqwbBp362JMW`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_FKKyVY5njTmGW9SG`)

**FRONT**

When is the reducing balance method appropriate?


**BACK**

When an asset loses value **quickly at first** and more slowly later, such as a vehicle. The pattern of the charge then matches the pattern of the real loss in value.


Spec links: `spcpt_5J94bqwbBp362JMW`


## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZJy6yz4fv7Y5fs8R`)

**FRONT**

How do you calculate reducing balance depreciation over several years?


**BACK**

Work **one year at a time**. Take the percentage of the net book value at the start of the year, subtract it to get the new net book value, then repeat for the next year.


Spec links: `spcpt_5J94bqwbBp362JMW`

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_nk3VDj7299vCMSWK`)

**FRONT**

Why does a business keep two separate accounts for each type of non-current asset?


**BACK**

So that it always has a record of both the **original cost** and the **depreciation charged so far**.

Keeping the two figures apart means neither one is lost, and the statement of financial position can show both.

Depreciation is recorded in the non-current asset account.


**BACK**

## Card 31 — question_and_answer (`fl_QwRzgrwQrTDDNX3w`)

**FRONT**

What is the double entry to record the year's depreciation charge?


**BACK**

**Debit** the statement of profit or loss and **credit** the provision for depreciation account.

The debit records the year's depreciation as an **expense**, and the credit reduces the value of the asset.

The book of prime entry is the **journal**.

## Card 33 — question_and_answer (`fl_tNWthr6JHxQY7Ks2`)

**FRONT**

A provision for depreciation account opens with a balance of \$4000 and this year's charge is \$4200. What is the closing balance, and what amount goes to the statement of profit or loss?


**BACK**

The closing balance is **\$8200** and the amount in the statement of profit or loss is **\$4200**.

The account collects every year's charge, so \$4000 + \$4200 = \$8200, but only the current year's charge is an expense.

## Card 34 — question_and_answer (`fl_mpQbczvkSj87wJqV`)

**FRONT**

A business buys extra equipment part-way through the year and charges a full year's depreciation on it. Why can the new and the old equipment be depreciated together?


**BACK**

Because both are being charged for a **full year**, so there is nothing to be gained by working them out separately.

The new purchase is simply added to the existing equipment before the year's charge is calculated.

## Card 37 — question_and_answer (`fl_ScRmftvyJVjKMP2p`)

**FRONT**

How do you work out the profit or loss when a non-current asset is sold?


**BACK**

Compare the **proceeds of the sale** with the **net book value** of the asset.

Proceeds above the net book value give a **profit**, and proceeds below it give a **loss**.
