## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Y76jGk5D4hDYFF9b`)

**FRONT**

Define **book-keeping**.


**BACK**

**Book-keeping** is the process of keeping detailed records of a business's financial transactions. It is carried out by **book-keepers**, who record every transaction the business makes, no matter how small.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_G9TTVH2RHbDgVJHW`)

**FRONT**

Where does a book-keeper get the information needed to record a business's transactions?


**BACK**

A book-keeper works from the business's **business documents**. These include bank statements, receipts, invoices and cheques.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_mDvrySvXFpvV4tZm`)

**FRONT**

Define **accounting**.


**BACK**

**Accounting** is the use of the records kept by book-keepers to prepare a business's **financial statements**. It is carried out by **accountants**, who are responsible for managing, updating, correcting and reporting the business's accounts.


Spec links: `spcpt_mKbPctgrnyN5kQhX`


## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_GjZhnVMKyWnxm9nh`)

**FRONT**

What do accountants provide to a business's owners and managers, and what do the owners and managers use it for?


**BACK**

Accountants provide **financial information**, drawn from the book-keeper's records. Owners and managers use it to **monitor the progress** of the business and to make **decisions** about how to improve profit or reduce a loss.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_s7WHTGmNKnFzP3c6`)

**FRONT**

Book-keeping and accounting are carried out at different points in the financial cycle. How often is each one carried out?


**BACK**

**Book-keeping** is carried out **day-to-day**, as transactions happen. **Accounting** uses those records to provide information at **regular intervals**, such as at the end of a financial year.

A business owner can carry out the book-keeping for their own business.


**BACK**

**True.**

Book-keeping is a **role**, not a separate person, so an owner can do it themselves. Many small business owners keep their own records, and then pass them to an **accountant**.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_KtMpsfG4M9qTsJ7S`)

**FRONT**

Define a **statement of profit or loss**.


**BACK**

A **statement of profit or loss** is a financial statement that measures the **profit or loss** a business has made over a period of time. The profit or loss is the difference between the business's **total income** and its **total expenses**.


Spec links: `spcpt_ZZjTcQmMYdMV2PXq`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_b3pvJSKcMNRggnbN`)

**FRONT**

What is **gross profit**, and how is it calculated?


**BACK**

**Gross profit** is the profit a business makes from its **trading** activities. It is calculated as **revenue − cost of sales**, and appears in the statements of profit or loss of trading and manufacturing businesses.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_4rkkH2PvXKhhgqSk`)

**FRONT**

Give two reasons why a business owner needs a statement of profit or loss.


**BACK**

A statement of profit or loss shows the owner:

- how the business has **performed** over a period of time, usually a year
- whether the business is **profitable**, so the owner can see whether they are making money on their investment
- whether **changes** need to be made, if a loss has been made

## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_JcyYZyh7g86z9rQT`)

**FRONT**

Define **assets**.


**BACK**

**Assets** are the things a business **owns**, such as premises, inventory, motor vehicles and money in the bank. They also include amounts **owed to** the business by others, which are called **trade receivables**.


Spec links: `spcpt_sJCf4JTbRHCFdk7D`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_RPq2DJ935xC4wCMZ`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a **current** item and a **non-current** item in a statement of financial position?


**BACK**

The dividing line is **one year**. **Current** assets and liabilities are expected to be turned into cash or paid off **within a year**, while **non-current** ones are held or repaid over a **longer** period.


Spec links: `spcpt_sJCf4JTbRHCFdk7D`


## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_23g3c7PTV8n8Ggzm`)

**FRONT**

Define **liabilities**.


**BACK**

**Liabilities** are the amounts a business **owes** to other people or businesses, such as bank loans and a bank overdraft. Amounts owed to credit suppliers are called **trade payables**.


Spec links: `spcpt_sJCf4JTbRHCFdk7D`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_8C9zGwfTsF49NxSX`)

**FRONT**

What is **working capital**, and how is it calculated?


**BACK**

**Working capital** is the money a business would have left if it turned all of its **current assets** into cash and paid off all of its **current liabilities**. It is calculated as **current assets − current liabilities**, and is the capital available for **day-to-day** trading.


Spec links: `spcpt_sJCf4JTbRHCFdk7D`


## Card 16 — keyword_definition (`fl_CWVH3mBTNJcJmF7t`)

**FRONT**

Define **capital**, also known as **owner's equity**.


**BACK**

**Capital** is any resource the **owner** provides to start the business or to keep it going, usually money but sometimes other assets such as buildings or vehicles. The business technically **owes** these resources back to the owner.


Spec links: `spcpt_sJCf4JTbRHCFdk7D`


## Card 17 — true_or_false (`fl_pF4n6kTfpC4Z72SX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A business's capital can only change when the owner puts money in or takes it out.


**BACK**

**False.**

Capital also changes with the business's **results**: a **profit** increases capital and a **loss** decreases it. Capital introduced by the owner, and **drawings** taken out for personal use, are only two of the ways capital moves.

## Card 22 — true_or_false (`fl_qRDD5gJKxDj57Szj`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Every transaction changes the total value of a business's assets.


**BACK**

**False.**

A transaction can swap one asset for another and leave the total unchanged. When a credit customer settles their account by cheque, the money in the bank rises and the amount owed by that customer falls by the same amount.


Spec links: `spcpt_GFCZzW9fjGR377Vy`


## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_D5wzcnKCc9YsBSSg`)

**FRONT**

Why does the accounting equation still balance after every transaction?


**BACK**

Every transaction causes **at least two changes** that cancel each other out. Both sides may **increase** by the same amount, both may **decrease** by the same amount, or the changes may happen **within one side** so that the totals stay the same.


Spec links: `spcpt_GFCZzW9fjGR377Vy`


## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_5pnJk6vtqMpsvys5`)

**FRONT**

A business pays one of its trade payables by cheque. What is the effect on its assets and on its liabilities?


**BACK**

**Assets decrease**, because the money in the bank falls. **Liabilities decrease** by the same amount, because the business now owes less to that supplier.
