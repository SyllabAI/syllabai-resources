## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_4HhtFbrSnssfkH9k`)

**FRONT**

How do you decide whether a transaction goes in the cash account or the bank account?


**BACK**

Use the **cash** account when **physical money** changes hands. Use the **bank** account when no physical money is involved, such as a bank transfer, a cheque, a standing order or a direct debit.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_D9RN5msHtpzNNPGQ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Paying a supplier by cheque is entered in the cash account.


**BACK**

**False.**

A cheque involves **no physical money**, so it is entered in the **bank** account. Only transactions where notes and coins change hands go in the cash account.

Which two transactions are entered in both the cash and the bank accounts?

**Depositing** cash from the business into the business bank account, and **withdrawing** cash from the bank for business use. Both move money between the two accounts rather than in or out of the business.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_zYFw8CV5p5hnfhT4`)

**FRONT**

Give two benefits of a business keeping cash on the premises.


**BACK**

Keeping cash lets the business:

- pay for purchases or expenses **immediately**
- deal with suppliers who **require cash payment**
- keep trading through **power cuts** and internet outages

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_M6Td6vf63Z83hNqB`)

**FRONT**

Give two limitations of a business keeping cash on the premises.


**BACK**

Cash held on the premises:

- can be **stolen or lost**
- earns no **interest**, unlike money left in the bank
- needs someone to **manage** it and the cash columns of the cash book

In the cash book, when do you debit an account and when do you credit it?

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_WKBvtVZn3ByfYr3D`)

**FRONT**

Define a **contra entry**.


**BACK**

A **contra entry** is a transaction entered on **both sides** of the cash book, in the cash column on one side and the bank column on the other. It arises when money moves between the till and the bank.


Spec links: `spcpt_XmgbPMNWMDJRzxP7`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_zV4BvGMFh54XGQBq`)

**FRONT**

Why can the bank balance sit on either side of the cash book when the cash balance cannot?


**BACK**

A bank account can be **overdrawn**, which makes it a liability and gives it a credit balance. Cash in hand cannot fall below zero, so it stays an **asset** with a debit balance.


Spec links: `spcpt_XmgbPMNWMDJRzxP7`

In a contra entry, the details column names the same account as the column the amount sits in.


**BACK**

**FRONT**

Which two transactions produce a contra entry in the cash book?


**BACK**

**Withdrawing** cash from the bank for business use, which debits cash and credits bank. **Depositing** cash into the bank, which debits bank and credits cash.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_7hcKyKhFMvkrNkp9`)

**FRONT**

How do you balance the cash and bank columns of the cash book?


**BACK**

Balance each pair of columns **separately**, exactly as you would any other ledger account, ignoring the other columns. The cash balance always comes down on the **debit** side, while the bank balance can come down on either.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_PZmM2xSFpSRYvwXh`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The discount columns of the cash book are balanced in the same way as the cash and bank columns.


**BACK**

**False.**

The discount columns are only **totalled**, never balanced. The two totals will usually be different, because discount allowed and discount received have nothing to do with each other.

**FRONT**

Is the cash book part of the double entry system for discount?


**BACK**

For **discount** the cash book acts only as a **book of prime entry**, which is why the totals must be posted out to the discount accounts separately. For its cash and bank columns it is part of the double entry system itself.

## Card 22 — keyword_definition (`fl_m7kncXjQ5SSyPbjp`)

**FRONT**

Define the **petty cash book**.


**BACK**

The **petty cash book** records transactions involving **small amounts of cash**. Each business decides what counts as a small amount, commonly anything up to \$75 or \$100.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_Yn6CVb4nbCgHJKfK`)

**FRONT**

What is recorded on the debit side of the petty cash book, and what on the credit side?


**BACK**

The **debit** side shows cash **received**, including the opening balance and any top-up from the cash or bank account. The **credit** side shows cash **paid out**, for small expenses and small amounts to trade payables.


Spec links: `spcpt_KGJGHCSn8px5mmjB`

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The balance brought down in the petty cash book is always on the debit side.


**BACK**

**True.**

Petty cash is an **asset**, so its balance is always a **debit**. The balance c/d therefore goes on the credit side, which normally has the smaller total, and comes down on the debit side.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_pJqy8ZRp47spxgKf`)

**FRONT**

What are the analysis columns of the petty cash book for?


**BACK**

They split the payments on the **credit** side into categories, such as travel, office expenses and ledger accounts. That lets the **total** of each column be posted to its ledger account in a single entry.


Spec links: `spcpt_KGJGHCSn8px5mmjB`


## Card 26 — keyword_definition (`fl_g9y65SVPr8B7yDRM`)

**FRONT**

Define the **imprest system**.


**BACK**

The **imprest system** restores the petty cash float to a fixed **imprest amount** at the start of each period. If the balance has fallen below it the float is topped up, and if it is above the excess is paid back into the cash or bank account.


Spec links: `spcpt_rTV5rK5fWBR32rKZ`


## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_tzdCG2ksh6wDCY4c`)

**FRONT**

How are the analysis column totals dealt with at the end of the period?


**BACK**

Each **expense** column total is transferred to its ledger account as a single entry. The **ledger accounts** column is the exception: those amounts must be posted to the **individual** accounts separately.

**BACK**
