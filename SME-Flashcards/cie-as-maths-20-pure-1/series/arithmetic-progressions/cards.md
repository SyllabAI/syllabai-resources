## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Vcz47msGYryPq9QR`)

**FRONT**

Define **arithmetic progression**.


**BACK**

An **arithmetic progression** is a progression in which the difference between consecutive terms is always the same.

That constant difference is called the **common difference**, written $d$.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4jVjghwHgG2N5M2t`)

**FRONT**

In an arithmetic progression with first term $a$ and common difference $d$, what is the $n$th term?


**BACK**

The $n$th term is:

`u_{n} = a + \left(n - 1\right)d`

Here $a$ is the first term and $d$ is the common difference.

## Card 3 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_3SJkQmtGCcYzmH6V`)

**FRONT**

The 15th term of an arithmetic progression with first term $a$ and common difference $d$ is $- 19$. Complete the equation this gives:

`a + \_\_\_\_\_\_d = -19`


**BACK**

The completed equation is:

$a + 14 d = - 19$

The first term is already $a$, so by the time you reach the 15th term the common difference has been added only 14 times.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_b2HNcCbVGvsWVgqp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In an arithmetic progression, the common difference must be positive.


**BACK**

**False.**

The common difference can be negative, and then every term is smaller than the one before: $2 ,  0 . 5 ,  - 1 ,  - 2 . 5 ,  - 4 ,  \dots$ has $d = - 1 . 5$.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_K4rWS28C3NDSZnHJ`)

**FRONT**

The first three terms of an arithmetic progression are $12$, $k^{2}$ and $5 k$. What equation does being arithmetic give you?


**BACK**

There is only one common difference, so the second term minus the first must equal the third minus the second:

$k^{2} - 12 = 5 k - k^{2}$

Rearranging gives the quadratic $2 k^{2} - 5 k - 12 = 0$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_rmZ4Cx3hrty7pbB6`)

**FRONT**

You know the 9th term and the 15th term of an arithmetic progression. How do you find the first term and the common difference?


**BACK**

Substitute each of them into the $n$th term formula, which gives two simultaneous equations in $a$ and $d$.

Subtracting one equation from the other removes $a$ and leaves $d$, which you then substitute back to find $a$.

## Card 7 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_TCdjZDgCnfF547sr`)

**FRONT**

In an arithmetic progression with first term $a$ and common difference $d$, complete the formula for the sum of the first $n$ terms:

`S_{n} = \frac{n}{2}\left(\_\_\_\_\_\_ + \left(n - 1\right)d\right)`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

`S_{n} = \frac{n}{2}\left(2a + \left(n - 1\right)d\right)`

Note that the first term inside the bracket is $2 a$, not $a$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_6zBJ42jPTHtRjmSX`)

**FRONT**

In the proof of the arithmetic series formula, why divide by 2 at the end?


**BACK**

Because the sum has been written out twice, once forwards and once in reverse, and the two copies added together.

That total is $2 S_{n}$, so halving it recovers the sum of the first $n$ terms.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_cWwyVHTZ3fSFD7HT`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic progression is $n$ times the average of its first and last terms.


**BACK**

**True.**

That is exactly the formula `S_{n} = \frac{n}{2}\left(a + l\right)`, where $l$ is the last term, since $\frac{a+l}{2}$ is the average of the first and last terms.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_qTsRqFFQgkKrfgdw`)

**FRONT**

Why does setting the sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic progression equal to a given total produce a quadratic equation in $n$?


**BACK**

Because the sum formula multiplies $n$ by a bracket that itself contains $n$, so expanding it produces a term in $n^{2}$.

Setting that equal to a number and rearranging therefore leaves a quadratic to solve.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_S7fYsfjydcWKm2s7`)

**FRONT**

Solving a problem about an arithmetic series gives $n = 40$ or $n = - \frac{125}{3}$. Which do you take, and why?


**BACK**

Take $n = 40$.

Since $n$ counts how many terms are being added, it must be a positive whole number, which rules the other root out.
