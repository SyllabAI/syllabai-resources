## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_pg3JzVxpTCxTWWXP`)

**FRONT**

In `y = k \text{f}\left(a x + b\right) + c`, which transformation is applied first?


**BACK**

The **horizontal** one, inside the brackets.

The **vertical** transformations, which sit outside the brackets, are applied afterwards.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_YcJ6vhcSpD6kypzs`)

**FRONT**

When two vertical transformations act outside the brackets, which goes first?


**BACK**

The **stretch or reflection** goes first, and the **translation** afterwards.

In `y = 3 \text{f}\left(x\right) - 2` that means stretching by scale factor $3$ and only then translating down by $2$.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_NKStZfdYw5BhdRZQ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`y = 3 \text{f}\left(x\right) - 2` and `y = 3 \left(\text{f}\left(x\right) - 2\right)` describe the same graph.


**BACK**

**False.**

The second expands to `y = 3 \text{f}\left(x\right) - 6`, which is a different graph.

Translating before stretching means the stretch acts on the shift as well, which is exactly why the order is fixed.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_YfMr6g6XrdtZ4fzy`)

**FRONT**

The point `\left(- 2, 6\right)` lies on `y = \text{f}\left(x\right)`. Where does it move to on `y = 3 \text{f}\left(x\right) - 2`?


**BACK**

It moves to `\left(- 2, 16\right)`.

The stretch takes the $y$-coordinate to $3 \times 6 = 18$ and the translation then brings it down to $16$, while the $x$-coordinate is untouched because both transformations are vertical.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_RwsygZ7dshkDy4fQ`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the asymptotes after a combination of transformations?


**BACK**

Apply the transformations to the asymptotes **one at a time**, in the same order as you apply them to the graph.

An asymptote is just a line, so each stage moves or stretches it in exactly the way that stage moves or stretches the curve.
