## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_7ykTGcG87Wv4f2Ty`)

**FRONT**

A wheel's height is modelled by `H = 50 - 48 \cos \left(\frac{\pi}{15} t + 0.3\right)` metres. What do the $50$ and the $48$ tell you?


**BACK**

The $50$ is the height of the **centre** of the wheel, and the $48$ is its **radius**.

The cosine runs between $- 1$ and $1$, so the height swings $48$ metres either side of $50$, between $2$ and $98$ metres.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_GyHcbW3yZttDjNgf`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The maximum of $y = 15 - 23 \mathrm{sin} 3 x$ occurs when $\mathrm{sin} 3 x = 1$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The coefficient is **negative**, so the largest $y$ comes from the smallest sine, at $\mathrm{sin} 3 x = - 1$, giving $y = 38$.

Putting $\mathrm{sin} 3 x = 1$ gives the minimum instead, $y = - 8$.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_qqF6rjvrkpyhymfN`)

**FRONT**

For `H = 50 - 48 \cos \left(\frac{\pi}{15} t + 0.3\right)`, with $t$ in minutes, how do you find when the passenger is first at the top?


**BACK**

Set the cosine to the value that makes $H$ largest, then solve for $t$.

Here $\frac{π}{15} t + 0 . 3 = π$, giving `t = \frac{15}{\pi} \left(\pi - 0.3\right) = 13.6` minutes, and the working must be done in **radians** because the model is written in them.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_SD4Jx27tnT7V2f3S`)

**FRONT**

What does the period of a trigonometric model tell you?


**BACK**

How long the situation takes to return to where it started and begin repeating.

For a wheel modelled against time in minutes it is the time for one complete revolution, so knowing a single cycle fixes the whole model.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_82ptJfBW3ZN3XHzy`)

**FRONT**

Complete the period $T$ of `\cos \left(q x + r\right)` in each measure:

`T = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{q} \text{ in degrees, and } T = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{q} \text{ in radians}`


**BACK**

The completed formulae are:

$T = \frac{360^{\circ}}{q} \text{in degrees, and} T = \frac{2π}{q} \text{in radians}$

In both, $T$ comes out in the same units as $x$, so a model in minutes gives a period in minutes.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_NpngGBj5P8wFPNQW`)

**FRONT**

What is the period of `y = \cos \left(\frac{7}{8} x - 1.5\right)`, with $x$ in radians?


**BACK**

The period is $\frac{2π}{\frac{7}{8}} = \frac{16π}{7}$.

The $- 1 . 5$ shifts the curve sideways but leaves the length of one cycle completely untouched.
