## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_8sDTCXTqqCHBbZXs`)

**FRONT**

What is a **conversion graph**?


**BACK**

A conversion graph shows how to **convert** from **one quantity to another quantity**.

Examples of quantities you could convert using a graph include:

- US dollars (\$) and GB Pounds (£)
- ºC and ºF
- Number of miles travelled and litres of petrol used



## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_CXGCFqRfvVdcHmgy`)

**FRONT**

How do you use a **conversion graph**?


**BACK**

To **convert **one quantity to another quantity using a **conversion graph**:

1. Find the value on the axis for the given quantity.
2. Draw a straight line (horizontal or vertical) from the value to the graph.
3. Draw a straight line from this point on the graph to the other axis.
4. Read off the value on this axis.

**FRONT**

What does a **distance-time graph** show?


**BACK**

A **distance-time graph** shows how the** distance** of an object from a point changes over** time**.



## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_cdsWYjH43DRTYqNM`)

**FRONT**

What does a **horizontal line** on a **distance-time graph** represent?


**BACK**

A **horizontal line** on a **distance-time graph** represents an object that is not moving (it is **stationary**).

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_rWK8zxG5wycR6HqP`)

**FRONT**

On a **speed-time graph**, what do a positive gradient and a negative gradient each tell you?


**BACK**

A **positive** gradient means the object is **speeding up**, or accelerating.

A **negative** gradient means the object is **slowing down**, or decelerating.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_mKcNGsSTGm24wVZC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A horizontal line on a **speed-time graph** means the object is not moving.


**BACK**

**False.**

A horizontal line on a speed-time graph means the object is travelling at a **constant speed**.

The speed is not changing, so the object is neither speeding up nor slowing down.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_my2MFNczwMtKS3tm`)

**FRONT**

On a **speed-time graph**, how can you tell that the object is at rest?


**BACK**

Its speed is **zero**, so the line lies along the horizontal axis.

Being at rest is the special case of a constant speed where that speed happens to be zero.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_gDj7MfRdcfVVTKF5`)

**FRONT**

On a **speed-time graph**, an object travels at a constant speed between 6 seconds and 15 seconds. For how long is it at that constant speed?


**BACK**

For **9 seconds**.

Read the two times off the horizontal axis and subtract one from the other: $15 - 6 = 9$.
