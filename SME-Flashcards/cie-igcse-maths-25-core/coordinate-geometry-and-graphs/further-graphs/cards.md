## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_fXNmfbPpjjTswqWh`)

**FRONT**

Define a **parabola**.


**BACK**

A **parabola** is the smooth symmetrical curve of a quadratic graph, $y = a x^{2} + b x + c$.

It is the shape you get whether the curve opens upwards or downwards.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_pCTGY7FjwndBrW7z`)

**FRONT**

Which part of an equation tells you whether its graph is a straight line, a parabola or a reciprocal curve?


**BACK**

Look at how $x$ appears: on its own it gives a straight line, squared it gives a quadratic curve, and in the **denominator** it gives a reciprocal graph.

So $y = 3 x + 2$, $y = 2 x^{2} + 3 x + 4$ and $y = \frac{4}{x}$ are one of each.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_PhKbV4Y7gykT2xqC`)

**FRONT**

How can you tell from its equation whether a quadratic graph is a u-shape or an n-shape?


**BACK**

Look at the number in front of $x^{2}$: **positive** gives a u-shape and **negative** gives an n-shape.

So $y = 2 x^{2} + 3 x + 4$ is a u-shape, while $y = - 3 x^{2} + 2 x + 4$ is an n-shape.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_sNJYTzT7GrRGzZQX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Every quadratic graph crosses the $x$-axis.


**BACK**

**False.**

A quadratic graph may cross the $x$-axis twice, just **touch** it once, or miss it altogether, so it can have two roots, one root or none at all.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZpSYJD9bfNX6NcHm`)

**FRONT**

What kind of symmetry does every quadratic graph have?


**BACK**

A **vertical line of symmetry** down its middle, with an equation of the form $x = k$.

The value of $k$ is the $x$-coordinate of the point where the curve turns, and the roots sit symmetrically either side of it.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_NMXfdZkqMP6VqbwT`)

**FRONT**

How many times does a quadratic graph cross the $y$-axis?


**BACK**

Exactly once, so a quadratic always has one $y$-intercept.

Substituting $x = 0$ gives exactly one value of $y$, so there is only ever one crossing point.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_mmwjdMzncQQ7FFvS`)

**FRONT**

What does the graph of $y = \frac{a}{x}$ look like?


**BACK**

It has **two separate L-shaped branches**, one in each of two opposite quadrants, and they never join up.

The L becomes more **rectangular** as $a$ gets smaller.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Changing the sign of $a$ in $y = \frac{a}{x}$ moves both branches into the other pair of quadrants.


**BACK**

**True.**

For a positive $a$ both branches sit where $x$ and $y$ have the **same** sign, and for a negative $a$ they sit where the two signs are **opposite**.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_3nyWdnS99FbnXcJS`)

**FRONT**

How do you complete a table of values for the graph of $y = 10 - 8 x^{2}$?


**BACK**

**Substitute** each $x$ value from the table into the equation to work out the matching $y$ value.

For $x = - 1 . 5$ this gives $y = 10 - 8 \times 2 . 25 = - 8$.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_KW4TNFNXs772Wg6N`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A **curved** graph drawn from a table of values should be joined up using a ruler.


**BACK**

**False.**

A curve must be drawn as a **single smooth freehand curve** passing through all the plotted points.

A ruler is only for straight-line graphs, and using one on a curve produces a series of straight segments instead.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_hYvHSf4JfKbqjfv3`)

**FRONT**

How accurately should each point be plotted on the grid?


**BACK**

Mark each point with a **cross**, positioned to within **half of the smallest square** on the grid.

A point plotted more roughly than that can pull the whole curve out of shape.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_9Pp3tXXTDWywCHwF`)

**FRONT**

When making a table of values for $y = \frac{1}{x}$, why is there no $y$ value to write in the $x = 0$ column?


**BACK**

Because $1$ divided by $0$ has no value: division by zero is not allowed, and a calculator returns an error.

Leave that column blank and plot no point there.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_D9Xr2rsBVCHTdVDM`)

**FRONT**

Define a **root** of an equation.


**BACK**

A **root** is a value of the unknown that makes both sides of the equation equal.

It is another word for a **solution**.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_n2cQJqKpGQt9HdcV`)

**FRONT**

Why must both graphs be drawn on the same axes before you can use their intersection?


**BACK**

Because an intersection is a point lying on **both** graphs, and that only means anything if the two share the same scales and origin.

On separate axes the same pair of coordinates would refer to two different positions.


Spec links: `spcpt_nJvPgFGmPTS4vH5M`


## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_pzNRCY3zq2xT5x92`)

**FRONT**

How do you use the graph of $y = x^{2} - 4 x - 2$ to solve $x^{2} - 4 x - 2 = 0$?


**BACK**

Read off the $x$-coordinates where the curve **crosses** the horizontal axis.

Setting the expression equal to zero is the same as asking where $y = 0$, which is exactly that axis.


Spec links: `spcpt_nJvPgFGmPTS4vH5M`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_xtTNrHvgThJ3V32q`)

**FRONT**

Using the graph of $y = x^{2} - 4 x - 2$, how do you solve $x^{2} - 4 x - 2 = 5$?


**BACK**

Draw the **horizontal line** $y = 5$ and read off the $x$-coordinates where it crosses the curve.

Whatever number sits on the right-hand side, draw the horizontal line at that height.

## Card 19 — true_or_false (`fl_MFT8spRk29rTYrbC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The solutions of $10 - 8 x^{2} = 8$ are the two points where the line $y = 8$ meets the curve.


**BACK**

**False.**

The solutions are the two $x$-**coordinates** of those points rather than the points themselves, because the equation asks only for values of $x$.

Here they are $x = - 0 . 5$ and $x = 0 . 5$.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_CVqtQc7hDbnFYNfW`)

**FRONT**

Using the graph of $y = x^{2} - 4 x - 2$, how do you solve $x^{2} - 4 x - 2 = x + 1$?


**BACK**

Plot the straight line $y = x + 1$ on the same axes, then read off the $x$-coordinates where it crosses the curve.

The right-hand side is no longer a constant, so the line you draw slopes instead of being horizontal.

## Card 22 — true_or_false (`fl_wWKWcndGJyTs2QJX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A curve drawn from one equation can be used to solve other equations as well.


**BACK**

**True.**

The curve shows every value that its expression takes, so any equation asking when that expression equals something can be answered by drawing the matching line.

Only the line changes, never the curve.

**FRONT**

Why can a solution read off a graph only ever be an estimate?


**BACK**

Because the answer is judged by eye against a printed scale rather than calculated exactly.

Round it to an accuracy that suits the scale, typically no more than two decimal places.
