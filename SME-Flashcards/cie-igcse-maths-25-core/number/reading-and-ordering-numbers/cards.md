## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_jsKH7HbFPRrsDZfx`)

**FRONT**

Define the **place value** of a digit.


**BACK**

The **place value** of a digit is the value it stands for because of its **position** in the number.

In 9876 the 8 sits in the **hundreds** place, so its value is 800.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_qMwNzyGkthzKbPtK`)

**FRONT**

What are the first three **decimal places** called, reading from the decimal point?


**BACK**

The first three decimal places are the **tenths**, **hundredths** and **thousandths**.

In 36.952 the 9 is worth 0.9, the 5 is worth 0.05 and the 2 is worth 0.002.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_XJP4YrvZtFybsTDD`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$23 . 45678$ is read as "twenty three point four five six seven eight".


**BACK**

**True.**

The whole number part is read as usual, and the digits after the decimal point are then read **one at a time**.

You would not read the decimal part as a single number, such as "point forty five thousand six hundred and seventy eight".

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_f63fjCd4HZwDwvm9`)

**FRONT**

Why is a large number such as 54 687 321 written with spaces, and where do the spaces go?


**BACK**

The spaces split the digits into **groups of three**, counted from the ones digit and working **left**, which makes the place values much easier to read.

So 54687321 is normally written as 54 687 321.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_yh2VM2rXd3nsJS5V`)

**FRONT**

Define a **negative number**.


**BACK**

A **negative number** is any number less than zero, written with a minus sign in front of it.

Negative and positive numbers together are often called **directed** numbers, because the sign gives a direction as well as a size.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_Cs3JDRJDctj5CnfH`)

**FRONT**

Why is `5 - \left(- 3\right)` equal to $8$?


**BACK**

Because **subtracting** a negative number is the same as **adding** the positive one, so the calculation becomes $5 + 3$.

In the same way, adding a negative is the same as subtracting the positive, so `7 + \left(- 3\right) = 7 - 3 = 4`.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_rMW4JsykT5tBWBY5`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$- 3^{2}$ and `\left(- 3\right)^{2}` mean the same thing.


**BACK**

**False.**

Only the bracketed form squares the **negative** number, giving `\left(- 3\right) \times \left(- 3\right) = 9`.

Without brackets just the $3$ is squared and the minus is applied afterwards, giving $- 9$.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_mkHn8pg9fpgM9SXQ`)

**FRONT**

The temperature is $- 4 ^{\circ} \text{C}$ and it warms up by $6 ^{\circ} \text{C}$. What is the new temperature?


**BACK**

It is $2 ^{\circ} \text{C}$, because warming up means adding, giving `\left(- 4\right) + 6 = 2`.

Counting upwards from $- 4$ crosses zero after four steps, leaving two more to go.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_jTT58Dkz64qpzsRy`)

**FRONT**

Someone is $300$ in debt and pays off $200$. Why is their position now $- 100$ rather than $- 500$?


**BACK**

Because paying money off **reduces** a debt, so the $200$ is added to $- 300$ to give $- 100$.

Borrowing more would instead add another negative, as in `\left(- 200\right) + \left(- 400\right) = - 600`.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_FCnX2RFyXK4753fx`)

**FRONT**

How do you use place value to decide which of two numbers is bigger?


**BACK**

Write them underneath each other with their place-value columns lined up, then compare digits starting from the **highest** place value on the left.

The first column where the two digits differ is the one that decides it.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_YjCnf5TDykyvf9kf`)

**FRONT**

Why is $- 14$ smaller than $- 8$, even though $14$ is bigger than $8$?


**BACK**

Because for negative numbers, the larger the digits the **further below zero** the number sits.

On a number line $- 14$ lies further to the left than $- 8$, and further left always means smaller.

## Card 18 — true_or_false (`fl_HCR2Hw8jTp3gFyqJ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In a list containing both positive and negative numbers, every negative number comes before every positive one.


**BACK**

**True.**

Every negative number sits below zero and every positive number above it, so the two groups never interleave.

That is why you can order each group separately and then simply join the two lists.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_Dy6n4MyqDXBvZFM2`)

**FRONT**

Define **ascending** and **descending** order.


**BACK**

**Ascending** order means increasing, so you start with the smallest value, which is the most negative one if there is any.

**Descending** order means decreasing, so you start with the largest value instead.
