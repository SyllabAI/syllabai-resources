## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZsW54VT6JjcX7rsv`)

**FRONT**

What is a **square root **of a number?


**BACK**

A **square root** of a number is a value that, when **squared**, gives the **original number**.

E.g. 3 is the square root of 9 because 3<sup>2</sup> = 3 x 3 = 9.



## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_gtHPBF6KX9kH4gwp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False? **

Every **positive **number has **two square roots**.


**BACK**

**True. **

Every positive number has two square roots, **one positive and one negative**.

E.g. $\sqrt{64}=8$ and $-8$

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_TQTMj22dkk77ZPSx`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **reciprocal**.


**BACK**

The **reciprocal **of a number is the number that you **multiply it by to get 1**.

For example, $\frac{2}{3}$ is the reciprocal of $\frac{3}{2}$.



## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_G6fqnVPxJPq3ydsg`)

**FRONT**

How do I **estimate a root**, e.g. the square root of 27?


**BACK**

You can estimate the root of a number by finding the closest integer roots either side of it.

E.g. To find $\sqrt{27}$

$\sqrt{25}=5$ and $\sqrt{36}=6$

So $\sqrt{27}$ is between $5$ and $6$

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_RCNMbj6pDjKZ3YKS`)

**FRONT**

How do you simplify a power that is raised to another power, such as `\left(14^{3}\right)^{2}`?


**BACK**

**Multiply** the two indices: `\left(a^{m}\right)^{n} = a^{m n}`.

So `\left(14^{3}\right)^{2} = 14^{3 \times 2} = 14^{6}`.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_nW3jXfwTKjmBrHfc`)

**FRONT**

What is the value of a number raised to the power of $0$?


**BACK**

Any non-zero number raised to the power of $0$ is $1$.

The base makes no difference: $8^{0} = 1$, $100^{0} = 1$ and `\left(- 3\right)^{0} = 1`.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_rPv5f6xPcj2pX9Hh`)

**FRONT**

What does a **negative index** tell you to do, for example in $2^{-4}$?


**BACK**

A negative index means the **reciprocal** of the positive power: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^{n}}$.

So $2^{-4} = \frac{1}{2^{4}} = \frac{1}{16}$.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_FNSG3prfD82KRMFf`)

**FRONT**

How do you expand a **product** or a **fraction** that is raised to a power?


**BACK**

Apply the power to **each** number separately: `\left(a b\right)^{n} = a^{n} b^{n}` and `\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{n} = \frac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}`.

For example, `\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2} = \frac{3^{2}}{4^{2}} = \frac{9}{16}`.


Spec links: `spcpt_YJXJpK2M5xGWQmkZ`


## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_Nz3cVXCN9BrgK686`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The index laws can be used to simplify $2^{5} \times 4^{3}$.


**BACK**

**True.**

Although the two bases look different, $4 = 2^{2}$, so the whole expression can be written with base $2$:

`2^{5} \times \left(2^{2}\right)^{3} = 2^{5} \times 2^{6} = 2^{11}`

The index laws only work once the **bases are the same**.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_bHDKFqxQGh2WNzy3`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the value of $x$ in the equation $6^{10+x} = 6^{2}$?


**BACK**

Since both sides have the **same base**, their indices must be equal, so $10 + x = 2$.

Subtracting $10$ from both sides gives $x = - 8$.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_s9yPTzr6SQgdVh32`)

**FRONT**

What does a number written in **standard form** look like?


**BACK**

A number in **standard form** looks like $a\times10^{n}$, e.g. $5.2\times10^{3}$.

It is a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of ten.



## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Gv3BKfCzZfpTCG2P`)

**FRONT**

When a number is written in **standard form** $a\times10^{n}$, which **values** can $a$ be?


**BACK**

For $a\times10^{n}$, $a$ is **at least 1 and less than 10**, $1\leqa<10$.

This means there is one non-zero digit before the decimal point.



## Card 15 — true_or_false (`fl_GsD8YFVymGPFPMWG`)

**FRONT**

**True or False? **

When a number is written in **standard form** $a\times10^{n}$, $n$ can be a fraction.


**BACK**

**False.**

For a number written in standard form, $a\times10^{n}$, the value $n$ must be an **integer**.

It can be zero, positive or negative.



## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_xy9z6h9hYbyJGSKg`)

**FRONT**

When a value **between 0 and 1**, is written in standard form $a\times10^{n}$, what **type of number** will $n$ be?


**BACK**

If a value is between 0 and 1 then when it is written in standard form, $n$ will be a **negative integer**.

E.g. 0.083 would be written as 8.3 x 10<sup>-2</sup>.



## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_XkDjcH3Ynnx2bm7n`)

**FRONT**

How do you work out `\left(3 \times 10^{8}\right) \times \left(2 \times 10^{- 3}\right)`?


**BACK**

Multiply the number parts, then **add** the powers of ten: $3 \times 2 = 6$ and $10^{8} \times 10^{-3} = 10^{5}$.

This gives $6 \times 10^{5}$.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_6dy3PcdMpTRjQd6n`)

**FRONT**

A calculation gives the answer $243 \times 10^{20}$. How do you rewrite this in standard form?


**BACK**

Write $243$ itself in standard form, then combine the two powers of ten:

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_Bpcx3gXdYsX8XXWY`)

**FRONT**

Why should each number in standard form be put in **brackets** when you type a calculation into a calculator?


**BACK**

A number such as $3 \times 10^{8}$ is a **product**, not a single value, so without brackets the calculator applies the next operation to only part of it.

For example, typing $6 \times 10^{5} \div 2 \times 10^{3}$ divides by $2$ and then multiplies by $10^{3}$, instead of dividing by the whole of $2 \times 10^{3}$.
