## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Gs3whkzky4pzjjBF`)

**FRONT**

Define a **ratio**.


**BACK**

A **ratio** is a way of comparing one **part** of a whole to another **part**.

It is written using a colon, such as $2 : 5$ or $4 : 2 : 3$.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_ftmQgRPxrPSJ5TY4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The ratio $1 : 4$ means the same as the fraction $\frac{1}{4}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

$1 : 4$ means 1 part to 4 parts, so there are 5 parts altogether.

The first quantity is therefore $\frac{1}{5}$ of the whole, not $\frac{1}{4}$.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_vc2CgVsvcNTbJsYZ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Writing a ratio as $5 : 3$ instead of $3 : 5$ changes what it means.


**BACK**

**True.**

Whatever is mentioned **first** in the question is matched to the **first** number in the ratio, and so on in order.

So a mixture of flour to butter in the ratio $3 : 5$ is quite different from one in the ratio $5 : 3$.

The first is made up of $2$ parts, the second of $5$ parts and the third of $3$ parts.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_Bh4yPsVNFm98cfk2`)

**FRONT**

Define **equivalent ratios**.


**BACK**

**Equivalent ratios** are ratios that represent the **same proportion** of a whole.

For example $5 : 10$ and $20 : 40$ are equivalent, because each part keeps the same size relative to the others.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_b4XRPvPrSvKhNsWK`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

To find a ratio equivalent to $3 : 4$, you can add $2$ to each part to get $5 : 6$.


**BACK**

**False.**

Equivalent ratios come from **multiplying or dividing** each part by the same value, never from adding.

$3 : 4$ and $5 : 6$ are not equivalent, because $3$ is three quarters of $4$ but $5$ is not three quarters of $6$.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_8KZ6gJStwcKHwp7x`)

**FRONT**

When is a ratio in its **simplest form**?


**BACK**

When **all** of its values are whole numbers **and** they share no common factor other than $1$.

Both conditions have to hold, not just one of them.


Spec links: `spcpt_Mgf9PGRQsNn5jFp3`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_sKtdBFSsqGf9DwpN`)

**FRONT**

How do you simplify the ratio $30 : 66 : 12$?


**BACK**

Divide **every** part by a common factor, ideally the **highest** common factor so that it is done in one step.

Here the highest common factor is $6$, and dividing gives $5 : 11 : 2$.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_yW3nMTJm2jKmJB59`)

**FRONT**

How do you share an amount in a given ratio?


**BACK**

Add the parts to find the **total number of parts**, then divide the amount by that total to find what **one part** is worth.

Finally, multiply that value by each part of the ratio in turn.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_RwvZ6F39Js4xZP2d`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If an amount has been shared correctly in a ratio, adding the shares together gives back the original amount.


**BACK**

**True.**

The shares are simply the whole amount split up, so nothing is created or lost along the way.

Sharing £60 in the ratio $1 : 2$ gives £20 and £40, and $20 + 40 = 60$.

**FRONT**

**BACK**

The $10$ parts altogether then give $10 \times 3 = 30$ leaves.

## Card 25 — keyword_definition (`fl_nW7qDgVNtS3rmRnv`)

**FRONT**

Define **direct proportion**.


**BACK**

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when increasing or decreasing one by a certain factor changes the other by the **same factor**.

The ratio of the two quantities therefore stays constant.

$8$ boxes weigh $60$ kg. Use the **unitary method** to find the weight of $7$ boxes.


**BACK**

## Card 28 — keyword_definition (`fl_G4YwWr4K4Zw26jZr`)

**FRONT**

Define **inverse proportion**.


**BACK**

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when increasing one by a certain factor **decreases** the other by that same factor.

It works the other way round too: decreasing one increases the other.

**BACK**

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_3cXBzx6HMtC3XQqN`)

**FRONT**

$5$ workers take $20$ hours to finish a job. How long would $8$ workers take?


**BACK**

$12 . 5$ hours.

One worker would take $5 \times 20 = 100$ hours, because fewer workers means more time, and then $100 \div 8 = 12 . 5$ hours for eight of them.

## Card 31 — true_or_false (`fl_PCcTW6HprTmRmzy8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The unitary method can be used for inverse proportion as well as for direct proportion.


**BACK**

**True.**

You still find the value for **one** unit first, but you use the **opposite** operation at each step.

Where direct proportion divides to reach one unit, inverse proportion multiplies.
