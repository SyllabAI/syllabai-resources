## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_PFGVRXVFPdVKmh9d`)

**FRONT**

Without a calculator, how do you find `50 \%`, `25 \%`, `10 \%` and `1 \%` of an amount?


**BACK**

Halve it for `50 \%`, halve it **twice** for `25 \%`, divide by $10$ for `10 \%` and divide by $100$ for `1 \%`.

Each one is easy because that percentage is a simple fraction of the whole.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_RyF2N5SrmCZg7bcT`)

**FRONT**

Without a calculator, how would you find `12 \%` of an amount?


**BACK**

Find `10 \%` and `1 \%`, then add the `10 \%` to **two** lots of the `1 \%`.

The same building-block idea gives `20 \%` by doubling the `10 \%`, and `5 \%` by halving it.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_55k4xD72f4cZP9JZ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`10 \%` of $40$ is the same as `40 \%` of $10$.


**BACK**

**True.**

Both are the same multiplication, since $0 . 1 \times 40$ and $0 . 4 \times 10$ each give $4$.

Swapping the percentage and the amount round like this can sometimes make a calculation much easier.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_vgNSs9qnRK6vvXKS`)

**FRONT**

With a calculator, how do you find `12 \%` of $650$?


**BACK**

Write `12 \%` as the decimal $0 . 12$ and multiply, giving $0 . 12 \times 650 = 78$.

A percentage over $100$ works in exactly the same way and simply gives a multiplier bigger than $1$, so `126 \%` becomes $1 . 26$.

**BACK**

The amount you are comparing **against**, here the full price, always goes on the bottom.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_CSFd7jKkhP8DSJBB`)

**FRONT**

Without a calculator, how do you increase $200$ by `21\%`?


**BACK**

Find `21\%` of $200$, which is $42$, and then **add** it to the original amount.

That gives $200 + 42 = 242$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_y5MjSYMNBHcW7vTY`)

**FRONT**

An item costing £$500$ is **discounted** by `35\%`. Without a calculator, how do you find the new price?


**BACK**

A discount is a decrease, so find `35\%` of $500$, which is $175$, and then **subtract** it from the original price.

That gives $500 - 175 = 325$, so the new price is £$325$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_BzkqYVt688NhtnTN`)

**FRONT**

Why does decreasing an amount by `35\%` mean finding `65\%` of it?


**BACK**

Taking `35\%` away leaves the rest of the amount behind, and the whole amount is `100\%`.

So what remains is `100\% - 35\% = 65\%`.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_CQ7dVrkMdjytT4TW`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the **multiplier** that was used for a percentage change?


**BACK**

Divide the amount **after** the change by the amount **before** it:

$m = \frac{\text{amount after}}{\text{amount before}}$

Going from $250$ to $310$ gives $\frac{310}{250} = 1 . 24$.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_gxVvpBQW7BFTqp4F`)

**FRONT**

What percentage changes do the multipliers $1 . 05$ and $0 . 75$ represent?


**BACK**

$1 . 05$ is an increase of `5\%`, and $0 . 75$ is a decrease of `25\%`.

Compare the multiplier with $1$: how far above $1$ it sits gives the increase, and how far below gives the decrease.

## Card 14 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_mM5N6JPGT3jDWYY3`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for a percentage change:

`\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{after} - \_\_\_\_\_\_}{\_\_\_\_\_\_} \times 100`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{after}-\text{before}}{\text{before}} \times 100$

The **before** value goes underneath, because a change is always measured against what you started with.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_j4HRryDJ3qrfN6mW`)

**FRONT**

When a percentage change works out **negative**, what does that tell you?


**BACK**

A negative answer is a percentage **decrease**, so an answer of $- 28$ means a decrease of `28\%`.

A positive answer is a percentage increase.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_JT6CTTkbc2kwCPYv`)

**FRONT**

When finding a **percentage profit or loss**, which price plays the part of the "before" value?


**BACK**

The **cost price**, which is what the shop paid, is the "before" value, and the **selling price** is the "after".

A car bought for £$8000$ and sold for £$5600$ gives $\frac{5600}{8000} = 0 . 7$, which is a loss of `30\%`.
