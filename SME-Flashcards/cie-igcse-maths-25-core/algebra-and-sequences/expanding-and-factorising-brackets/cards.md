## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_s8gVrjRR5zrJY3Vm`)

**FRONT**

Explain how to **expand** a single bracket.

E.g. $2 ( x + 3 )$.


**BACK**

Expanding a bracket means multiplying the term outside the bracket by **each term** inside the bracket.

So $2 ( x + 3 )$ becomes $2x+6$ from $2\timesx+2\times3$.



## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_WWsFgr2djpznKR6k`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$- 2 ( 1 - x )$ expands to $-2-2x$.


**BACK**

**False.**

$- 2 ( 1 - x )$ can be thought of as $( - 2 ) \\times 1 + ( - 2 ) \\times ( - x )$.

Multiplying **two negatives** gives a **positive**, so the answer is $-2+2x$.



## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_YwzQmVHSMCjDZz9M`)

**FRONT**

How can you** simplify** $3 ( x + 5 + x )$ **before** expanding the brackets?


**BACK**

You can simplify $3 ( x + 5 + x )$ before expanding by first **collecting like terms** inside the brackets to give $3 ( 2 x + 5 )$.

This can also be written as $3 ( 5 + 2 x )$.



## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_p7k4gCTJH8zb8Zfp`)

**FRONT**

Explain how to** simplify **an expression with two sets of brackets

E.g. $2 ( x + 1 ) + 3 ( x - 1 )$.


**BACK**

To simplify expressions like $2 ( x + 1 ) + 3 ( x - 1 )$ you **expand the brackets** then **collect like terms**.

So $2 ( x + 1 ) + 3 ( x - 1 )$ expands to give $2x+2+3x-3$.
 The terms are then collected to give $5x-1$.



## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_q7BhnmpkS3nZXCH6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$3 ( x + 2 )$ expands to $3x+6$, then the 3's cancel to give $x+2$.


**BACK**

**False.**

You **cannot cancel** the 3's because $3 ( x + 2 )$ is an **expression**, not an equation.

You can only cancel both sides by 3 if you had an equation, like $3 ( x + 2 ) = 9$.



## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_kSygB9dytkR2YVRK`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the **highest power** of $x$ when expanding expressions?

E.g. $2 x^{2} ( x + x^{2} )$.


**BACK**

The **highest power** of $x$ will come from multiplying the outside term by the inside term with the highest power.

The  highest power of $x$ in $2 x^{2} ( x + x^{2} )$ is $x^{4}$.

This can be found by multiplying $x^{2}$ outside the bracket by the highest power, $x^{2}$, from inside the bracket.



## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_rzNChFsKXN7n93mW`)

**FRONT**

How many multiplications are needed to expand `\left(x + 1\right) \left(x + 3\right)`, and why?


**BACK**

**Four**, because every term in the first bracket must be multiplied by every term in the second bracket.

The four products here are $x \times x$, $x \times 3$, $1 \times x$ and $1 \times 3$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_kdF3gRJG2fMQ235y`)

**FRONT**

When expanding `\left(x - 3\right) \left(3 x - 5\right)` using **FOIL**, which two products are the **Outside** and **Inside** ones?


**BACK**

**Outside** is `x \times \left(- 5\right)`, the first term of the first bracket multiplied by the last term of the second bracket.

**Inside** is `\left(- 3\right) \times 3 x`, the last term of the first bracket multiplied by the first term of the second bracket.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_3XP5sgX5kn7442Yr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\left(x + y\right)^{2}` expands to $x^{2} + y^{2}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The expansion of `\left(x + y\right)^{2}` is $x^{2} + x y + x y + y^{2}$, which simplifies to $x^{2} + 2 x y + y^{2}$.

The two $x y$ **cross terms** are exactly what $x^{2} + y^{2}$ leaves out.

**FRONT**

What is the first thing to do when expanding `\left(x + 3\right)^{2}`?


**BACK**

Write the square as **two brackets multiplied together**, `\left(x + 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)`.

It can then be expanded in exactly the same way as any other pair of brackets.

## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_r3n9BgBM9WMgh5XB`)

**FRONT**

Describe how to **factorise** simple expressions such as $6x+8$.


**BACK**

To factorise simple expressions like $6x+8$:

1. Find the **highest common factor** of 6 and 8 (which is 2)
2. Write this factor **outside** a set of brackets
3. Write inside the brackets what you must multiply the factor by to get the **original **expression

So $6x+8$ becomes $2 ( 3 x + 4 )$.
