## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_bQhbXcSMp9cf3Xd9`)

**FRONT**

How can you deduce the order of reactivity of metals from experimental results?


**BACK**

The order of reactivity can be deduced by comparing how **vigorously** metals react with water or dilute acid, or by measuring **temperature changes**. A more vigorous reaction or larger temperature change indicates a more reactive metal.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_R4RTj3gY7WX35Nh5`)

**FRONT**

Why are carbon and hydrogen included in the reactivity series even though they are non-metals?


**BACK**

Carbon and hydrogen are included in the reactivity series because they are used as **reference points**. Carbon is used to extract metals below it by **reduction**, and hydrogen allows comparison of metal reactivity through acid reactions.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_QgmTJG4VqM4XMbQy`)

**FRONT**

Define the **reactivity series**.


**BACK**

The **reactivity series** is a list of metals arranged in order of decreasing reactivity, based on observations of their reactions with water and dilute acids.

Carbon and hydrogen are also included as reference points for extraction and acid reactions.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_qGjQGw4GpD7SnnKk`)

**FRONT**

Why is a more reactive metal more likely to undergo displacement reactions than a less reactive metal?


**BACK**

A more reactive metal **loses electrons more easily** and forms positive ions more readily. This means it can displace less reactive metals from solutions of their salts by transferring electrons to the metal ions in solution.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_KwJrdrzFTWkDPXHb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In a displacement reaction, the more reactive metal is reduced.


**BACK**

**False.**

In a displacement reaction, the more reactive metal is **oxidised** (it loses electrons to form positive ions). The less reactive metal ion is **reduced** (it gains electrons to form the metal).

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_g36Y6g6fr9fDsdQT`)

**FRONT**

What observation would you see when magnesium reacts with copper sulfate solution? Explain what causes this change.


**BACK**

The **blue colour** of the copper sulfate solution fades as colourless magnesium sulfate forms, and **copper metal deposits** on the magnesium surface.

This occurs because magnesium is more reactive than copper: Mg atoms lose electrons to Cu<sup>2+</sup> ions in solution, reducing them to copper metal.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_rTSP6fhNWK6K8Ct2`)

**FRONT**

Define **displacement reaction**.


**BACK**

A **displacement reaction** is a reaction in which a more reactive metal displaces (pushes out) a less reactive metal from a solution of one of its salts.

For example, magnesium displaces copper from copper sulfate solution.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_4kPY3J5ybydC3QCd`)

**FRONT**

How do barrier methods such as painting and greasing prevent iron from rusting?


**BACK**

Barrier methods prevent rusting by **excluding oxygen and water** from reaching the surface of the iron. As long as the barrier is intact, the iron cannot come into contact with both conditions needed for rust to form.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_t6wQRy7p4fRk5Nr2`)

**FRONT**

In an experiment investigating rusting, a nail in a tube with boiled water and an oil layer does not rust. Why?


**BACK**

The nail does not rust because **oxygen is absent**. Boiling the water removes dissolved air, and the oil layer prevents oxygen from the atmosphere entering. Without oxygen, rusting cannot occur even though water is present.

**FRONT**

What is galvanising?


**BACK**

Galvanising is the process of **coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc** to protect it from rusting. The zinc acts as both a physical barrier and a source of sacrificial protection.

In sacrificial protection, a less reactive metal is attached to iron to prevent rusting.


**BACK**
