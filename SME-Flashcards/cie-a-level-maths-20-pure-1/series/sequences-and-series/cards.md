## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_7GQ3JyYqtt25H6Kg`)

**FRONT**

Define **progression**.


**BACK**

A **progression**, also called a **sequence**, is an ordered list of numbers together with a rule for finding them all.

The individual numbers in it are called **terms**.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_KdcGNhkbdpgnCwDT`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a progression and a series?


**BACK**

A **progression** is the list of terms itself, while a **series** is what you get by adding those terms together.

So $1 , 4 , 7 , 10 , 13$ is a progression and $1 + 4 + 7 + 10 + 13$ is the series that goes with it.

## Card 3 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_pb3rMkpKWMcMTwjY`)

**FRONT**

In the progression $1 , 4 , 7 , 10 , 13 , \dots$ complete both values:

`u_{4} = \_\_\_\_\_\_ \text{ and } S_{4} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed values are:

$u_{4} = 10 \text{and} S_{4} = 22$

The notation $u_{n}$ means the $n$th **term** on its own, while $S_{n}$ means the **sum** of the first $n$ terms, here $1 + 4 + 7 + 10$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_8vsQgtRBC9GDHWBT`)

**FRONT**

You are given a list of numbers. How do you decide whether it is arithmetic or geometric?


**BACK**

Test the **differences** between consecutive terms, then test the **ratios** of consecutive terms.

If every difference is the same it is arithmetic, if every ratio is the same it is geometric, and if neither is constant it is neither.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_FtFwr4vzrkn4sv7D`)

**FRONT**

Complete the two conditions on three numbers $a$, $b$ and $c$, filling in the missing expression in $b$ each time:

`\_\_\_\_\_\_ = a + c \text{ (arithmetic progression)}`

`\_\_\_\_\_\_ = a c \text{ (geometric progression)}`


**BACK**

The completed conditions are:

$2 b = a + c \text{(arithmetic progression)}$

$b^{2} = a c \text{(geometric progression)}$

Both come from there being a single common difference or a single common ratio, so the middle term is pinned down by the two outside it.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_PJvsmRGvk3Q682tW`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In the notation $u_{5}$, the $5$ tells you the value of that term.


**BACK**

**False.**

The subscript gives the **position** of the term, not its value.

In the progression $1 , 4 , 7 , 10 , 13 , \dots$ the fifth term is $u_{5} = 13$, so the subscript and the value are different numbers.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_C9Q8VsSkGw8M2pJs`)

**FRONT**

A quantity changes repeatedly. How can you tell whether to model it with an arithmetic progression or a geometric one?


**BACK**

Use an **arithmetic** progression when a fixed amount is added or subtracted each time.

Use a **geometric** progression when the quantity is multiplied by a fixed amount each time, which includes changing by a fixed percentage.

## Card 8 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_JwdvsFfdmDHssqCh`)

**FRONT**

Complete the common ratio for each of these geometric models:

A quantity that increases by 10% each year has `r = \_\_\_\_\_\_`.

A quantity that decreases by 5% each year has `r = \_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

The completed ratios are:

A quantity that increases by 10% each year has $r = 1 . 1$.

A quantity that decreases by 5% each year has $r = 0 . 95$.

Each term is the whole of the previous one after the change, not just the change itself.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_S9nW8kytmWytbt9h`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If a quantity increases by the same percentage every year, it increases by the same amount every year.


**BACK**

**False.**

The percentage stays the same, but it is applied to a larger amount each time, so the actual increase grows.

A profit of £20 000 rising by 10% gains £2 000 in the first year and £2 200 in the second.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_YRbQpcQdh4xdHyZK`)

**FRONT**

A grandfather gives £10 on a first birthday and doubles the gift each year. Which formula gives the gift **on** the 18th birthday, and which gives the **total** given by then?


**BACK**

The gift on the 18th birthday is a single term, so use the $n$th term formula for a geometric progression with $n = 18$.

The total given by then adds up all 18 gifts, so use the sum formula instead.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_xMcQvbkYRPMQVM3Q`)

**FRONT**

Someone pays money into an account every month from January to December. When you model the year's total as an arithmetic series, what is $n$?


**BACK**

There are $12$ terms.

There is one payment in each of the twelve months and the January payment is the first term, so twelve payments are added in all.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_WwHMrpxFcjgVn4sH`)

**FRONT**

In a savings plan modelled as an arithmetic progression, what does the common difference represent?


**BACK**

The amount by which each payment is bigger than the one before.

It is not the size of a payment itself, which starts at the first term $a$ and grows from there.
