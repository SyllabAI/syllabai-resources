## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_3tDprwptyTbZ3RnW`)

**FRONT**

What is meant by the **gradient of a curve** at a particular point?


**BACK**

It is the gradient of the **tangent** to the curve at that point.

The gradient of a curve is not fixed: it changes from point to point, which is why a gradient can only be given **at a particular point**.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_n9B5R7vYxGpMJmTP`)

**FRONT**

Define **tangent** to a curve.


**BACK**

A straight line that **touches** a curve at a given point, running in the same direction as the curve at that point.

At any given point of a **smooth** curve there is exactly **one** tangent.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_bZgyf392nBsdnfGT`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A line that is a tangent to a curve at one point cannot meet the curve anywhere else.


**BACK**

**False.**

A tangent only has to **touch** the curve at the point where it is a tangent. Further along it may well **cut** the curve at another point.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_dVq4zCybBBzp27Wd`)

**FRONT**

A tangent has been drawn to a curve at the point $A$ on an accurately drawn graph. Which two points should you use to work out the gradient of the curve at $A$?


**BACK**

Any two points that lie **on the tangent line**, chosen where it passes through easily read coordinates; points on the **curve** itself must not be used.

The gradient is then the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$ between those two points. So, **for example**, a tangent through `\left(1 , - 2\right)` and `\left(3 , 4\right)` has gradient:

`\frac{4 - \left(- 2\right)}{3 - 1} = \frac{6}{2} = 3`

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_Rjj3zqKd3JVsYthJ`)

**FRONT**

Are there points on a graph where a curve has no gradient?


**BACK**

Yes. A tangent can only be drawn where the curve is **smooth**.

At a sharp corner, such as the vertex of the graph of `y = |\text{f}(x)|`, no tangent can be drawn, so the gradient there is **undefined**.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_3kGRsNMXTZP8rHX5`)

**FRONT**

Define **differentiation**.


**BACK**

**Differentiation** is the operation that finds the **rate of change** of a function with respect to a variable.

The result of differentiating a function is called its **derivative**.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_rsSGpN44zBTgP44P`)

**FRONT**

A point $Q$ on a curve is moved closer and closer to a fixed point $P$ on the same curve. What happens to the chord $P Q$?


**BACK**

It turns towards the **tangent** to the curve at $P$.

So the gradient of the chord closes in on the gradient of the curve at $P$, and that limiting value is the derivative there.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_7R6Ycx7jZbq4vnHH`)

**FRONT**

How does the gradient of $y = x^{2}$ behave as $x$ increases from $0$?


**BACK**

It starts close to zero near the origin and gets steadily **larger**.

The curve is almost flat at the bottom and rises more and more steeply, so its rate of change grows as $x$ grows.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_qNcVWpV5sGw6rqky`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The derivative of a function is a single number.


**BACK**

**False.**

For most functions the rate of change differs at different values of $x$, so the derivative is an expression in $x$ rather than one value.

It is a single number only in the special case of a straight line, whose gradient never changes.

## Card 12 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_G25mWHXfhq5RxMWm`)

**FRONT**

Complete the rule for differentiating a power of $x$, filling in the missing **coefficient** and **index**:

`\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}\left(x^{n}\right) = \_\_\_\_\_\_ x^{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

`\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}\left(x^{n}\right) = n x^{n-1}`

Multiply by the **index**, then reduce the index by 1.

This works for any constant $n$, including negative and fractional values.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_5yD6JsRBwjQBkcyj`)

**FRONT**

How do you differentiate a term such as $- 4 x^{5}$, where a power of $x$ has a constant multiplier?


**BACK**

Differentiate the power of $x$ and keep the multiplier:

`\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x}\left(ax^{n}\right) = anx^{n-1}`

So, **for example**, $- 4 x^{5}$ differentiates to $- 4 \times 5 x^{4} = - 20 x^{4}$.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_7VCT7XrDnnkmcMnV`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Differentiating $x^{-4}$ gives $- 4 x^{-3}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The index is always **reduced** by 1, even when it is already negative: $- 4 - 1 = - 5$, so the derivative is $- 4 x^{-5}$.

Negative and fractional indices are a common place to lose marks for exactly this reason.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_y88kTBDGg9GQnKRM`)

**FRONT**

What must you do to terms such as $\sqrt{x}$ and $\frac{4}{x}$ before you can differentiate them?


**BACK**

Rewrite each one as a **power** of $x$, so that the rule for differentiating $x^{n}$ can be applied:

$\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$

$\frac{4}{x} = 4 x^{-1}$

The derivative is often converted back into root or fraction form at the end.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_WSPDgxnDsGDvh9Tz`)

**FRONT**

How do you differentiate an expression that is a sum or difference of several terms?


**BACK**

Differentiate the terms **one at a time**, keeping them in the same order.

So, **for example**, $y = x^{4} - \sqrt{x} + \frac{4}{x}$ gives:

$\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = 4 x^{3} - \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} - \frac{4}{x^{2}}$

Notice that the last term changes sign, because differentiating $4 x^{-1}$ gives $- 4 x^{-2}$.

## Card 17 — true_or_false (`fl_7xsFTmPvHhkDwCxS`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The derivative of $y=\sqrt{7}$ is $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{7}}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

$\sqrt{7}$ is a **constant**: there is no $x$ in it. The derivative of any constant is **zero**, so $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = 0$.

The graph of a constant is a horizontal line, which has gradient zero everywhere.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_4P33gndN64Mcnqp3`)

**FRONT**

What is the derivative of a term such as $- 5 x$, where $x$ has no visible index?


**BACK**

The **coefficient** on its own, so $- 5$. In general:

$\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}x} ( a x ) = a$

This makes sense because $y = a x$ is a **straight line** with gradient $a$, the same at every point.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_TVr8726fHkcRcTxJ`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between $\text{f} ' ( x )$ and $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}$?


**BACK**

There is no difference in meaning: both stand for the **derivative**.

$\text{f} ' ( x )$ is used when a function has been defined as $\text{f} ( x )$, and $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}$ when the curve is written as $y$ in terms of $x$.

## Card 20 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_7PCfYzbjwB4ndDkv`)

**FRONT**

If $y$ is a function of $u$, and $u$ is a function of $x$, complete the chain rule:

`\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = \frac{\text{d}y}{\_\_\_\_\_\_} \times \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{\text{d}x}`


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

$\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}u} \times \frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}$

The $\text{d} u$ sits on the bottom of one derivative and the top of the other, which is what links the two together.

**FRONT**

Why use the chain rule on `\left(3x + 2\right)^{7}` rather than expanding the brackets?

The chain rule also works when the index is negative or fractional, where expanding is not possible at all.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_2pBDS8VKB58sngBT`)

**FRONT**

What is the quick pattern for differentiating `\left(\text{f}\left(x\right)\right)^{n}` without introducing $u$?


**BACK**

Differentiate the outer power as usual, then multiply by the derivative of the inside:

`\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = n\,\text{f}'\left(x\right)\left(\text{f}\left(x\right)\right)^{n - 1}`

So, **for example**, `\left(2x^{2} - 4\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}` differentiates to `-\frac{1}{2}\left(2x^{2} - 4\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}} \times 4x`.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_qxX9xXvBswhxG7sW`)

**FRONT**

After using a substitution, why must $u$ be replaced at the end?


**BACK**

Because $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x}$ has to be a function of $x$, and $u$ was only a temporary label for part of the expression.

An answer left in terms of $u$ cannot have an $x$-value substituted into it.
