**BACK**

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_n6DWHQncM9MsfJ4h`)

**FRONT**

Why is a polynomial graph always drawn as one smooth curve?


**BACK**

Because a polynomial is defined for **every** value of $x$, with no gaps and no sharp corners.

That is what separates it from a graph such as $y = \frac{1}{x}$, which breaks at $x = 0$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_vY8D6w5JXjQh5V7h`)

**FRONT**

What are the asymptotes of $y = \frac{a}{x}$?


**BACK**

The two axes: $x = 0$ vertically and $y = 0$ horizontally.

They are drawn with **dotted** lines, because the curve approaches them without ever reaching them.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_9zWYFTpvSbHVYJmz`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The graph of $y = \frac{a}{x}$ never crosses either axis.


**BACK**

**True.**

No value of $x$ makes $y$ equal to zero, and $x = 0$ gives no value of $y$ at all.

A transformation of the graph can move it so that it does cross an axis.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_mJw4v7bdHWBJ6yJ3`)

**FRONT**

How does the **sign** of $a$ affect the graph of $y = \frac{a}{x}$?


**BACK**

With $a > 0$ the two branches sit in the **top-right** and **bottom-left**.

With $a < 0$ they sit in the **top-left** and **bottom-right** instead.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_QvFjVmQW5VWwYk9k`)

**FRONT**

How does the **size** of $a$ affect the graph of $y = \frac{a}{x}$?


**BACK**

It controls how **steep** the curves are.

The closer $a$ is to zero, the more tightly the curve hugs the axes and the more **L-shaped** it looks.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A sketch reliably tells you how many times two graphs intersect.


**BACK**

**False.**

Only working algebraically settles the number for certain.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_Wjpr4gs57dDTNqNr`)

**FRONT**

Why use graphs and algebra together rather than either alone?


**BACK**

The algebra gives the exact values, while the graph shows what the situation looks like and whether an answer is plausible.

A sketch on its own is not precise enough, and algebra on its own gives no picture at all.

## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_hvhrsgGVMbx7rqFH`)

**FRONT**

Define the **constant of proportionality**.


**BACK**

The **constant of proportionality** is the fixed number $k$ that turns a statement of proportion into an equation you can work with.

Its value is the same for every pair of values the two variables take.

## Card 19 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_MtqwvPndHNNCRsGK`)

**FRONT**

Complete the equation that each statement of proportion gives:

`y \propto x \Rightarrow y = \_\_\_\_\_\_ \text{ and } y \propto \frac{1}{x} \Rightarrow y = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed pair is:

$y \propto x \Rightarrow y = k x \text{and} y \propto \frac{1}{x} \Rightarrow y = \frac{k}{x}$

The first is **direct** proportion and the second is **inverse** proportion.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_WtDvWzmgwPPk2qzd`)

**FRONT**

$y$ is inversely proportional to the cube of $x$. What equation does this give?


**BACK**

Writing it as an equation gives $y = \frac{k}{x^{3}}$.

The words "the cube of $x$" say what sits underneath, so it is $x^{3}$ and not $x$ that $y$ is inversely proportional to.

## Card 21 — true_or_false (`fl_8qhf9szyRvfvWKpw`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If $y$ is directly proportional to $x$, the graph of $y$ against $x$ must pass through the origin.


**BACK**

**True.**

Putting $x = 0$ into $y = k x$ always gives $y = 0$, so the line goes through `\left(0, 0\right)`.

A line such as $y = 2 x + 5$ is therefore **not** a direct proportion, although $y - 5$ is directly proportional to $x$.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_Wny9D5CJ5hN9YPrv`)

**FRONT**

$y$ is proportional to $x + 4$. What is the equation, and why are the brackets needed?


**BACK**

The equation is `y = k \left(x + 4\right)`.

The whole of $x + 4$ is what $y$ is proportional to, so writing $y = k x + 4$ would multiply only the $x$ by $k$ and give a completely different relationship.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_5b5Zy9CCPg4cDDDr`)

**FRONT**

Given that $y \propto x$ and that $y = 12$ when $x = 3$, find the equation.


**BACK**

Write $y = k x$, then substitute the pair of values: $12 = 3 k$, so $k = 4$.

The equation is therefore $y = 4 x$, which now gives $y$ for any value of $x$.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_wNnWywmhdRr39pK5`)

**FRONT**

What stays constant in a direct proportion, and what stays constant in an inverse one?


**BACK**

In a **direct** proportion the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ is constant and equal to $k$.

In an **inverse** proportion it is the product $x y$ that is constant and equal to $k$.
