## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_snpK4wq6F9RKvd7w`)

**FRONT**

Define **diffraction**.


**BACK**

**Diffraction** is the spreading out of waves as they pass through a gap (aperture) or around the edge of an obstacle.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_GHHphW9hCqtmmssc`)

**FRONT**

Diffraction is most pronounced when the width of the gap and the **..........** of the wave are similar in size.


**BACK**

Diffraction is most pronounced when the width of the gap and the **wavelength** of the wave are similar in size.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_k8Bm95BSnrRnQV8s`)

**FRONT**

What is the only property of a wave that changes when it is diffracted, and why?


**BACK**

Its **amplitude** decreases, because some energy is dissipated as the wave passes through the gap. The wavelength (and frequency) stay constant.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_wrRbPHXqqMJh8qR9`)

**FRONT**

Besides passing through a narrow gap, in what other situation can diffraction occur?


**BACK**

Waves can also diffract by curving around the edge of a barrier or obstacle.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_vsHtc9xNbpcSRY3h`)

**FRONT**

Compare the diffraction of a wave through a gap that is much bigger than its wavelength with one much smaller than its wavelength.


**BACK**

- Gap much **bigger** than the wavelength: little diffraction, the wave passes through largely undeviated
- Gap much **smaller** than the wavelength: the wave spreads out significantly after passing through

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_QvZhg4KRmHVpy29r`)

**FRONT**

True or False?

Only water waves and sound waves can be diffracted; light waves cannot.


**BACK**

**False.**

Any type of wave can be diffracted, including light.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_2NFM6cXWXWDDhjq6`)

**FRONT**

Name a piece of apparatus used to demonstrate diffraction of water waves, and describe how the amount of diffraction can be varied in this experiment.


**BACK**

A **ripple tank**. The amount of diffraction can be varied by changing the size of the gap relative to the wavelength of the waves.
