## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_y9xHtfnfJGtzgBbp`)

**FRONT**

Define **radiant flux intensity**.


**BACK**

**Radiant flux intensity** is the radiant power transmitted normally through a surface per unit area, measured on Earth.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_vkG69gVMgvktswWp`)

**FRONT**

State the inverse square law of flux equation, and define each symbol.


**BACK**

$F = \frac{L}{4πd^{2}}$

- *F* = radiant flux intensity (W m<sup>-2</sup>)
- *L* = luminosity of the source (W)
- *d* = distance between the star and Earth (m)


Spec links: `spcpt_QFVt366tdYxp46tS`


## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_xRkrsFyYYBXCCkpd`)

**FRONT**

Explain, in terms of surface area, why radiant flux intensity decreases with distance from a star according to an inverse square law.


**BACK**

Radiation spreads out uniformly over the surface of an expanding sphere of area $4 π d^{2}$, so the same total power (**luminosity**) is spread over an increasingly large area as distance *d* increases.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_wr9jkYB3tVhJQTZq`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A star's luminosity decreases as it moves further away from Earth.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Luminosity** is a fixed, intrinsic property of the star; it is the **radiant flux intensity** (observed brightness) that decreases with distance, not the luminosity.

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_9bSkF6k7BK4zV8Dk`)

**FRONT**

Define a **Cepheid variable** star.


**BACK**

A **Cepheid variable** is a type of pulsating star whose brightness increases and decreases over a set time period, with this period related to its luminosity.


Spec links: `spcpt_BhZGFTbw6Pv5bCsr`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_hxnR5dywpXtr6vrV`)

**FRONT**

What is a type 1a supernova, and why is it useful as a standard candle?


**BACK**

A **type 1a supernova** is an explosion involving a white dwarf. Its peak luminosity always reaches the same value, so it can be used to estimate distance.


Spec links: `spcpt_BhZGFTbw6Pv5bCsr`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_HCQc6KYQwhPxYMp4`)

**FRONT**

Outline how the distance to a standard candle is determined.


**BACK**

Its **radiant flux intensity** is measured from Earth. Since its **luminosity** is known (as it is a standard candle), the distance is calculated using the inverse square law of flux.


Spec links: `spcpt_9fCqBXrtwsqDJ4YK`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_ryZ3CkdSSRchfG7B`)

**FRONT**

Why are type 1a supernovae more suitable than Cepheid variable stars for measuring distances to the most distant galaxies?


**BACK**

Type 1a supernovae have a much greater **luminosity**, so their **radiant flux intensity** is much greater and they are more likely to be observed at very large distances.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Standard candles all have the same radiant flux intensity as observed from Earth.


**BACK**

**False.**

Standard candles have a known **luminosity**; their observed **radiant flux intensity** varies with distance, which is what allows distance to be calculated.
