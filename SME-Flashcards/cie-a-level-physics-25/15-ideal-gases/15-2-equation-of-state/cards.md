## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_DSwr4Tt8w5TNjDwp`)

**FRONT**

Define an **ideal gas**.


**BACK**

An **ideal gas** is one which obeys the relation $p V \propto T$, where *p* is pressure, *V* is volume and *T* is thermodynamic temperature.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4kfKSCR5xFHzVcPZ`)

**FRONT**

For a fixed volume of gas, how does the pressure change as temperature increases, and why?


**BACK**

**Pressure increases**.

The molecules have higher **kinetic energy**, so they move about more and **collide more** with the container walls, creating more pressure.


Spec links: `spcpt_JBz9h7zKcyqSCKrc`


## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_7pVsnV2h5T53XJBb`)

**FRONT**

For a gas at constant pressure, what happens to its volume as temperature increases, and why?


**BACK**

The **volume increases**.

The molecules have higher **kinetic energy**, so they move further apart, **expanding** to create a bigger volume.


Spec links: `spcpt_JBz9h7zKcyqSCKrc`


## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_GQQ22XyVW8gKHFVj`)

**FRONT**

At constant temperature, how are the pressure and volume of an ideal gas related?


**BACK**

**Pressure** and **volume** are **inversely proportional**.

A smaller volume gives a smaller container surface area, so there are more collisions, creating more pressure.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_FmP8b9rxjjzsM2qY`)

**FRONT**

State the ideal gas equation in terms of the number of moles *n*.


**BACK**

$p V = n R T$

where *R* is the molar gas constant (8.31 J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>)

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_4VYZtPySPxcqsBhs`)

**FRONT**

Define the **Boltzmann constant**.


**BACK**

The **Boltzmann constant**, *k*, relates the microscopic properties of particles (e.g. kinetic energy) to their macroscopic properties (e.g. temperature): $k = \frac{R}{N_{A}}$, equal to 1.38 × 10<sup>-23</sup> J K<sup>-1</sup>.
