## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_qTJdVHRsQDdpdwRZ`)

**FRONT**

What is the **reactivity series**?


**BACK**

The **reactivity series** is a list of metals (and some non-metals) arranged in order of reactivity, from most reactive to least reactive, based on their reactions with water and acids.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_zmWW8NxQkyQZZZrf`)

**FRONT**

How can you deduce the order of reactivity of metals from experimental results?


**BACK**

The more vigorous the reaction with water or dilute acid, the more reactive the metal. A greater **temperature change** can also indicate a more reactive metal — but only when comparing metals in the **same type of reaction** (e.g. displacement reactions with the same acid or salt solution).

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_z34GVgQt26v7qBNN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Carbon and hydrogen are included in the reactivity series.


**BACK**

**True.**

**Carbon** and **hydrogen** are non-metals included in the reactivity series. Carbon sits between aluminium and zinc; hydrogen sits between iron and copper. They are included because they are used to extract metals from their oxides.

**BACK**

**Potassium** is so reactive that it reacts **explosively** with acids. This makes the reaction extremely dangerous and unsuitable for laboratory use.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_NrBHVSgMv52zYVcB`)

**FRONT**

What is **rust**?


**BACK**

**Rust** is a reddish-brown compound called **hydrated iron(III) oxide**. It forms when iron reacts with both water and oxygen.


Spec links: `spcpt_DCVGP2B8XHRfvn7g`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_bFWwNDMMm3qd6YB5`)

**FRONT**

What two substances must be present for iron to rust?


**BACK**

Both **water** and **oxygen** must be present for iron to rust. If either is absent, rusting does not occur.

**True or False?**

An iron nail kept in dry air will rust.


**BACK**

**False.**

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Pzgb2RrFRMGqzNXS`)

**FRONT**

How do barrier methods prevent rusting?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

Barrier methods work by **excluding oxygen or water** (or both) from reaching the iron surface. If either substance cannot contact the iron, the rusting reaction cannot take place.


Spec links: `spcpt_DCVGP2B8XHRfvn7g`

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_BDn5FPKP2CHwp7nd`)

**FRONT**

Why does painting an iron gate prevent rusting, and what happens if the paint is scratched?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

Paint acts as a **barrier** that keeps oxygen and water away from the iron. If the paint is **scratched**, iron is exposed to both substances and rusting begins at the damaged area.
