## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_55ZdGFCbxZWQKnv3`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **malleable**.


**BACK**

A **malleable** material can be hammered or pressed into different shapes without breaking. Metals are malleable because layers of positive metal ions can slide over each other.

**BACK**

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_nsQVfCwdpKBksmhH`)

**FRONT**

What products form when a metal reacts with cold water?


**BACK**

When a metal reacts with cold water, the products are a **metal hydroxide** and **hydrogen** gas.

For example: Ca (s) + 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l) → Ca(OH)<sub>2</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g)

**BACK**

**False.**

When a metal reacts with **steam**, the products are a **metal oxide** and **hydrogen**. It is not a metal hydroxide. A metal hydroxide forms only when the metal reacts with cold water.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_4Wf2hXD7xPTxVFkZ`)

**FRONT**

Why are non-metals described as brittle when solid?


**BACK**

Non-metals are **brittle** because, unlike metals, their electrons are all involved in covalent bonding. The layers cannot slide over each other, so the structure shatters when force is applied.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_dvtX6zfNt62qSVmJ`)

**FRONT**

Why is aluminium resistant to corrosion despite being a reactive metal?


**BACK**

Aluminium forms a thin, protective layer of **aluminium oxide** on its surface. This layer prevents oxygen and water from reaching the metal underneath, stopping further corrosion.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_mz9vB7xzDQb3mpYz`)

**FRONT**

How can you identify an alloy from a diagram of its structure?


**BACK**

An alloy is shown by a diagram with **different-sized particles** arranged in **irregular, distorted layers**. A pure metal has uniform-sized particles in regular rows.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_PNsnBVj2Ym9Qzsfz`)

**FRONT**

Why are alloys often harder and stronger than pure metals?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

Alloys contain atoms of **different sizes**, which distort the regular arrangement of layers. This makes it harder for the layers to **slide over each other**, so the alloy is harder and stronger.
