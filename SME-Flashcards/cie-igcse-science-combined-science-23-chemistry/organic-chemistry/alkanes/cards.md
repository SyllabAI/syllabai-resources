## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_DMNPjBN23VnJVjzC`)

**FRONT**

Define **alkane**.


**BACK**

An **alkane** is a saturated hydrocarbon with the general formula C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub>. All carbon-carbon bonds in alkanes are single bonds (C-C).

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_ybF55KWbCnv7KmRT`)

**FRONT**

Why are alkanes described as generally unreactive compounds?


**BACK**

Alkanes are generally unreactive because they contain only **strong single covalent bonds** (C-C and C-H). These bonds require a lot of energy to break, making alkanes resistant to most chemical reactions.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZN8WwQ5VswWCFhZy`)

**FRONT**

What are the products of the complete combustion of an alkane?


**BACK**

Complete combustion of an alkane produces **carbon dioxide** and **water**. For example: CH<sub>4</sub> (g) + 2O<sub>2</sub> (g) → CO<sub>2</sub> (g) + 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_Z5dsdQCy73F6Nbmr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Alkanes contain covalent bonds because they are made of non-metal atoms.


**BACK**

**True.**

Alkanes are made entirely of carbon and hydrogen atoms, which are both **non-metals**. Non-metal atoms form **covalent bonds** by sharing electrons.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_cbH2998mTB4xrvj7`)

**FRONT**

How does the boiling point change as alkane chain length increases, and why?


**BACK**

Boiling point **increases** with chain length. Longer alkane molecules have stronger intermolecular forces between them, so more energy is needed to separate the molecules.
