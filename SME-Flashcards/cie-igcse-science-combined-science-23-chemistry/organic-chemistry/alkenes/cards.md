## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_zkX75Bx2hgyS2hb8`)

**FRONT**

Define **alkene**.


**BACK**

An **alkene** is an unsaturated hydrocarbon containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond (C=C). The general formula of the alkenes is C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n</sub>.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4QwDygQdHxPJBsQ2`)

**FRONT**

How is bromine water used to distinguish an alkene from an alkane?


**BACK**

A few drops of **bromine water** (orange) are added to the sample and shaken. With an **alkene**, the bromine water is **decolourised** (turns colourless) as bromine reacts with the C=C double bond. With an **alkane**, there is **no colour change** and the solution stays orange.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_HFHccChBkvGqwMnr`)

**FRONT**

Why are alkenes more reactive than alkanes?


**BACK**

Alkenes are more reactive because they contain a **carbon-carbon double bond** (C=C) that can open up and form new bonds. Alkanes only contain strong single bonds, which are harder to break.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_7GbFnsFBJyv2HBDF`)

**FRONT**

Why is cracking important in the petroleum industry?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

Cracking converts excess **long-chain** hydrocarbons into more useful **short-chain** molecules such as petrol (alkanes) and **alkenes**. Alkenes are used to make polymers and hydrogen is used in ammonia manufacture.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_tSwy3DH84qgzjj8c`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition reaction**.

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

An **addition reaction** is a reaction in which atoms from a small molecule add across the C=C double bond of an alkene, forming only **one product**.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_CtTVWHxx5TzRGY3r`)

**FRONT**

What type of compound is formed when bromine reacts with an alkene in an addition reaction?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

A **saturated compound** is formed. The bromine atoms add across the C=C double bond, breaking the double bond and converting the alkene into a compound with only single carbon-carbon bonds.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Z9rytgqWvxXH3jRz`)

**FRONT**

What product is formed when an alkene reacts with steam in an addition reaction, and what catalyst is required?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

When an alkene reacts with steam, an **alcohol** is formed. This is also called a **hydration reaction**. An **acid catalyst** is required. For example, ethene + steam → ethanol.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_k8q3HWDqjF4ntzcz`)

**FRONT**

Why do alkenes undergo addition reactions, whereas alkanes do not?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

Alkenes contain a **C=C double bond** that can break open to form new bonds with other molecules. Alkanes contain only strong single covalent bonds, which do not open up to add other atoms in this way.
