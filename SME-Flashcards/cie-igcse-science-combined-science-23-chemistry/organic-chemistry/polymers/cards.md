## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_vBv7d6xrGG7Dh5FY`)

**FRONT**

Define **polymer**.


**BACK**

A **polymer** is a large molecule built up from many smaller repeating units called **monomers** joined together by covalent bonds.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_YWGPJP8hFsn4Qqsm`)

**FRONT**

Define **monomer**.


**BACK**

A **monomer** is a small molecule that can join with many other monomers to form a long-chain **polymer**. For example, ethene is the monomer used to make poly(ethene).

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_pX3ZSKd8KmyZDGGc`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In addition polymerisation, two products are formed.


**BACK**

**False.**

In addition polymerisation, **only one product** is formed. The monomers join together as the C=C double bond breaks, and no other molecules are produced as byproducts.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_7HZhWNCNkDnyZ9kG`)

**FRONT**

Why must the monomer used in addition polymerisation be an **alkene**?


**BACK**

The monomer must be an **alkene** because it contains a **C=C double bond**. During addition polymerisation, this double bond breaks and the monomers link together. Saturated compounds without a double bond cannot undergo this reaction.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_34Y2dBrBBwRQNkhx`)

**FRONT**

Give an example of **addition polymerisation**, naming the monomer and the polymer.


**BACK**

**Ethene** monomers add together to form **poly(ethene)**.

The **C=C double bond** in each ethene molecule opens up, allowing many monomers to link into one long chain.

(Poly(propene) and PVC form the same way, from propene and chloroethene.)

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_cF7hfWHVT58h2S6g`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Monomers are joined together in polymers by covalent bonds.


**BACK**

**True.**

Each monomer in a polymer chain is joined to the next by **covalent bonds** formed when the C=C double bond breaks and new C-C single bonds are created.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_Nxttg3cDKh9hwbmt`)

**FRONT**

What happens to the C=C double bond in an alkene monomer during addition polymerisation?


**BACK**

The **C=C double bond breaks** during addition polymerisation. One bond of the double bond breaks in each monomer, allowing the carbon atoms to form new single bonds with the adjacent monomers in the growing polymer chain.
