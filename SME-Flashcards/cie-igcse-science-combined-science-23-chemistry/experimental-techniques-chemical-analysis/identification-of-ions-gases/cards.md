## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_vnH4Q5Drd4zynXbM`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **anion**.


**BACK**

An **anion** is a negatively charged ion. In qualitative analysis, common anions tested for include carbonate (CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sup>), halide (Cl<sup>-</sup>, Br<sup>-</sup>, I<sup>-</sup>) and sulfate (SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>) ions.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_FwSvDTMbQSbCJgm4`)

**FRONT**

What are the **reagent** and **result** for the test for **carbonate ions**?


**BACK**

Add dilute acid to the sample — effervescence occurs. Bubble the gas produced through **limewater**. A positive result is limewater turning **cloudy white**, indicating carbon dioxide is present.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_Gwmxww9sMK6hQMN8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Dilute hydrochloric acid should be used to acidify a sample before testing for halide ions.

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

**False.**

**Dilute nitric acid** must be used. Hydrochloric acid contains chloride ions (Cl<sup>-</sup>) which would react with silver nitrate and give a false positive result for chloride.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_tWk9tngJ7VDk47GG`)

**FRONT**

Why is **nitric acid** added before **barium nitrate** in the test for sulfate ions?


**BACK**

**Nitric acid** is added first to remove any **carbonate** ions present. Carbonate ions would also produce a white precipitate with barium nitrate, which could give a false positive result for sulfate.

**FRONT**

Why does **chlorine** gas turn damp blue litmus paper red before bleaching it white?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

When chlorine dissolves in water, it forms acidic products (including **hydrochloric acid** and hypochlorous acid). These acids turn the litmus red. The chlorine then **bleaches** the litmus white by oxidising the dye.

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_gmM2CJgPVmcY6vBm`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between **ammonium ions** (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>) and **ammonia gas** (NH<sub>3</sub>) in the context of gas tests?


**BACK**

**Ammonium ions** (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>) are aqueous cations present in solution. **Ammonia gas** (NH<sub>3</sub>) is the gas released when ammonium ions are warmed with sodium hydroxide. The gas test uses damp red litmus paper, which turns blue in the presence of NH<sub>3</sub>.
