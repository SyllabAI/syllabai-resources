## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_CwTCsj6ZpPFTZWtx`)

**FRONT**

Define **paper chromatography**.


**BACK**

**Paper chromatography** is a technique used to separate mixtures of soluble substances based on their different solubilities in a solvent. More soluble components travel further up the paper.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_MgCCFGV5VHPHxR7t`)

**FRONT**

Why is a **pencil** used to draw the baseline on chromatography paper rather than a pen?


**BACK**

A **pencil** is used because the graphite does not dissolve in the solvent. Ink from a pen would dissolve and run up the paper with the solvent, contaminating the chromatogram.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_wqy4RCZpn4fpsjxY`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A pure substance will produce only one spot on a chromatogram.


**BACK**

**True.**

A **pure substance** contains only one component, so it travels a single fixed distance up the paper, producing just one spot. An impure substance or mixture produces multiple spots.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_CkdjJP8s97VfTCsS`)

**FRONT**

Define ***R***<sub>**f**</sub>** value** in paper chromatography.

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

The ***R***<sub>**f**</sub>** value** (retention factor) is the ratio of the distance moved by a substance to the distance moved by the solvent front. It has no units and is always less than 1.


Spec links: `spcpt_KJMXNxfmMb7Tr2Rx`


## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_zmQ3Q7Rtv5BHTbzP`)

**FRONT**

How is the Rf value calculated in a chromatography experiment?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

*R*<sub>f</sub> = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent. For example, if a substance moves 3 cm and the solvent moves 6 cm, the *R*<sub>f</sub> value is 0.5.


Spec links: `spcpt_KJMXNxfmMb7Tr2Rx`


## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_bRkYWfcwMBx7kPBy`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The *R*<sub>f</sub> value of a substance stays the same regardless of which solvent is used.

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

**False.**

The *R*<sub>f</sub> value depends on the **solvent** used. Changing the solvent changes the relative solubilities of the components and therefore changes their *R*<sub>f</sub> values.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_BS4KNyTPTmbKRCM4`)

**FRONT**

How can chromatography be used to tell if two substances are the **same compound**?


**BACK**

If two substances are the same compound, their spots will travel to the same height on the chromatogram (i.e. they will be in line horizontally). They will also have identical *R*<sub>f</sub> values under the same conditions.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_zYHh4HX4FHRFJ96F`)

**FRONT**

Why must the pencil baseline be placed **above** the solvent level in a chromatography experiment?


**BACK**

The baseline must be above the solvent so that the sample spots are not submerged. If the spots touched the solvent directly, the substances would **dissolve** into it rather than being carried up the paper by **capillary action**.


Spec links: `spcpt_MVWqykYw7gj7Pwnb`


## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_JKgRzJ4SrHKR7C4z`)

**FRONT**

Define **filtration** as a separation technique.


**BACK**

**Filtration** is a technique used to separate an undissolved solid from a liquid or solution. The mixture is poured through filter paper in a funnel; the solid remains as a **residue** and the liquid passes through as the **filtrate**.


Spec links: `spcpt_gpJvWd4Dv97PHj3h`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_bCg8fb5bSyvwcBFM`)

**FRONT**

Why is it important to heat a solution for **no more than half** of the liquid off during crystallisation?


**BACK**

If more than half the liquid is removed by direct heating, the crystals may **decompose** or form too quickly and be impure. Removing only half ensures a concentrated, **saturated solution** remains, from which good-quality crystals form slowly on cooling.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_RG6kYCywkSRQmh7T`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Fractional distillation is used to separate liquids with very different boiling points.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Fractional distillation** is used when the boiling points of liquids are **close together**. It gives a higher degree of purity than simple distillation and is used for mixtures such as ethanol and water.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_xsPbztQGbKBdgdQz`)

**FRONT**

How is a **suitable solvent** used to separate a mixture of two solids?


**BACK**

A solvent is chosen in which only the desired solid dissolves. The mixture is stirred in the solvent, the undissolved solid is removed by filtration, and the dissolved solid is recovered by evaporation or crystallisation.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_9589xJh6VgwbRxJw`)

**FRONT**

How do you test whether a solution is **saturated** during crystallisation?


**BACK**

Dip a clean, dry, cold glass rod into the hot solution. If crystals form on the rod when it is removed and cools, the solution is saturated and ready to be set aside to crystallise.

Simple distillation can be used to separate two miscible liquids with similar boiling points.


**BACK**

**False.**

**FRONT**

Define **crystallisation** as a separation technique.


**BACK**

**Crystallisation** is a method of separating a dissolved solid from a solution by evaporating the solvent to form a saturated solution, which is then cooled slowly so that pure crystals of the solute form.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_JQBQGXQZ5V3fTczJ`)

**FRONT**

What property difference does **distillation** exploit to separate the components of a mixture?


**BACK**

Distillation exploits differences in **boiling point**. The liquid with the lower boiling point vaporises first, passes through a condenser where it cools back to a liquid, and is collected separately.
