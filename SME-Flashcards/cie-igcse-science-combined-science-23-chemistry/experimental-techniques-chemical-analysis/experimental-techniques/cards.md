## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_RC3WM5SNv8KFxJqn`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **burette**.


**BACK**

A **burette** is a piece of apparatus used to measure a variable volume of liquid between 0 cm<sup>3</sup> and 50 cm<sup>3</sup>. It is the most accurate way to measure variable volumes and is read from top to bottom (0.00 cm<sup>3</sup> at the top).

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_9wg2thysQMjzFjzJ`)

**FRONT**

Why is a gas syringe preferred over downward displacement of water for collecting gas?


**BACK**

A **gas syringe** is preferred because it is more precise and accurate. Downward displacement of water cannot be used if the gas is **soluble** in water, which would give an inaccurate volume reading.

**BACK**

Temperature is measured in **degrees Celsius (°C)**. Laboratory thermometers typically have a precision of 0.5 °C or 1 °C, while digital temperature probes can read to 0.1 °C.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_QJ2BXpBFM7QTqfP3`)

**FRONT**

Why is the **pencil line** kept above the solvent level at the start of a chromatography experiment involving liquid volume measurement?


**BACK**

The pencil line must be above the solvent level so that the sample spots do not dissolve directly into the solvent. This ensures the solvent carries the substances up the paper by **capillary action** rather than washing them away.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_wGshnjrR7btcQwgg`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between the **filtrate** and the **residue** in a filtration?


**BACK**

The **filtrate** is the liquid or solution that passes through the filter paper. The **residue** is the solid that remains on the filter paper and does not pass through.

## Card 16 — keyword_definition (`fl_tj5N6cyQ7HbzP2HS`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **saturated solution**.


**BACK**

A **saturated solution** is a solution containing the maximum concentration of solute dissolved in the solvent at a specified temperature. Adding more solute will not cause further dissolution.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_WkKWrpQ7NbK5rNbV`)

**FRONT**

Why does a substance described as **insoluble** not form a solution?


**BACK**

An **insoluble** substance will not dissolve in a given solvent. Its particles do not break apart and mix evenly with the solvent particles, so no solution is formed. For example, sand is insoluble in water.
