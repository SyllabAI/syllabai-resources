**FRONT**

What is an **element**?


**BACK**

An **element** is a pure substance made of atoms that all contain the same number of protons and cannot be broken down into anything simpler by chemical means. There are around 118 elements listed in the Periodic Table.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_5WXsgtftJqQ6mxj3`)

**FRONT**

What is a **compound**?


**BACK**

A **compound** is a pure substance made up of two or more elements chemically combined. Compounds cannot be separated into their elements by physical means and there is an unlimited number of possible compounds.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_XtVZMCCKwTPfncgG`)

**FRONT**

What is the key difference between a **compound** and a **mixture**?


**BACK**

In a **compound**, elements are chemically bonded together and cannot be separated by physical means. In a **mixture**, substances are not chemically combined and can be separated by physical methods such as filtration. Compounds have fixed compositions; mixtures do not.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_mkn6crRXnBmK9TdW`)

**FRONT**

What is the **nucleus** of an atom?


**BACK**

The **nucleus** is the central region of an atom containing **protons** and **neutrons**. It carries almost all of the atom's mass. Electrons move rapidly around the nucleus in shells.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_kN6jkrQ2sjXB4R78`)

**FRONT**

What are the relative mass and relative charge of a **proton**, a **neutron** and an **electron**?


**BACK**

A **proton** has relative mass 1 and charge 1+. A **neutron** has relative mass 1 and charge 0. An **electron** has negligible mass (approximately 1/1840) and charge 1-.

**BACK**

The **proton number** (or atomic number, symbol *Z*) is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It is also equal to the number of electrons in a neutral atom and determines the element's position on the Periodic Table.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_zfVDzryqFCyrHRCY`)

**FRONT**

Why is the mass of an electron described as negligible when calculating the mass of an atom?


**BACK**

An electron has a relative mass of approximately 1/1840, which is tiny compared with the relative mass of 1 for each proton and neutron. Because electrons contribute so little to the total, their mass is considered negligible and the mass of the atom is taken as the number of **protons plus neutrons**.

**FRONT**

What is **relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>)?


**BACK**

**Relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>) is the average mass of one atom of an element compared with 1/12th the mass of a **carbon-12** atom. Because it is a ratio, *A*<sub>r</sub> has **no units**.

## Card 23 — true_or_false (`fl_FMFnp4KhNvWWkkhR`)

**FRONT**

All elements in the same **group** of the Periodic Table have the same number of electrons in their outer shell.


**BACK**

**True.**

Elements in the same **group** share the same number of outer-shell electrons. This is why elements in the same group have similar chemical properties, as chemical reactivity is determined by the outer electrons.

**FRONT**

How does the electronic configuration of an element indicate which **period** and **group** it belongs to?


**BACK**

The number of occupied shells gives the **period** number. The number of electrons in the outermost shell gives the **group** number (for Groups I to VII). For example, chlorine (2,8,7) has 3 shells so is in Period 3 and 7 outer electrons so is in Group VII.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_pdGJr94ShhmJTRFG`)

**FRONT**

Write the electronic configuration of a sodium ion Na<sup>+</sup> and explain why it differs from the sodium atom.


**BACK**

A sodium atom has 11 electrons with configuration 2,8,1. A sodium **ion** (Na<sup>+</sup>) has lost one electron, leaving 10 electrons, giving the configuration **2,8**. This is the same as the noble gas neon, giving Na<sup>+</sup> a stable full outer shell.

## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_N7rxMHKqpQWsFWCn`)

**FRONT**

What is the **valence shell** and why is it chemically important?


**BACK**

The **valence shell** is the outermost occupied electron shell of an atom. It is chemically important because atoms react with other atoms to fill or empty this shell, achieving a more stable full outer-shell configuration like the noble gases.
