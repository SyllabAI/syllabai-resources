## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_CqfPp232NhMcQs2V`)

**FRONT**

What is an **ion**?


**BACK**

An **ion** is an electrically charged atom or group of atoms formed by the loss or gain of electrons. Ions form so that the atom can achieve a full outer shell of electrons, like the electronic configuration of a noble gas.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_nmTdPPwb4Xr5SHxq`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a **cation** and an **anion**?


**BACK**

A **cation** is a positively charged ion formed when an atom **loses** electrons, leaving it with more protons than electrons. An **anion** is a negatively charged ion formed when an atom **gains** electrons, giving it more electrons than protons.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_sfMCy8H5fhcMRDMk`)

**FRONT**

Why does sodium form an Na<sup>+</sup> ion rather than Na<sup>2+</sup> or Na<sup>-</sup>?


**BACK**

Sodium is in Group I and has one outer-shell electron. Losing this single electron gives sodium a full outer shell (2,8), the configuration of neon. Removing two electrons would require much more energy and would not give a stable noble-gas configuration, so Na<sup>+</sup> is strongly preferred.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_8BMD3t3tWhtxxSMg`)

**FRONT**

Describe the formation of ionic bonding between sodium and chlorine.


**BACK**

Sodium (Group I) **loses** one outer electron to form Na<sup>+</sup> with configuration 2,8. Chlorine (Group VII) **gains** that electron to form Cl<sup>-</sup> with configuration 2,8,8. The oppositely charged ions are held together by strong **electrostatic forces**, forming the ionic compound NaCl.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_Ys4mzwN6FdJb5v2p`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The overall charge of an ionic compound is always zero.


**BACK**

**True.**

In an ionic compound, the positive and negative charges of the ions balance each other exactly so the compound has **no overall charge**. For example, in NaCl one Na<sup>+</sup> balances one Cl<sup>-</sup>.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_jWfQpwhssVpDGYJb`)

**FRONT**

Why do ionic compounds form a giant lattice rather than small separate molecules?


**BACK**

Each ion in an ionic compound attracts all the oppositely charged ions around it, not just one. This means that a vast, extended network of alternating positive and negative ions builds up, forming a **giant lattice** rather than discrete molecules.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_9VYSX54kD9rmqqT9`)

**FRONT**

How does the charge on the ions in MgO compare with the charge on the ions in NaCl, and what does this mean for the ratio of ions in each compound?

**(Extended Tier Only)**


**BACK**

In NaCl, the ions are Na<sup>+</sup> and Cl<sup>-</sup> (charges of 1+ and 1-), so the ratio is 1:1. In MgO, the ions are Mg<sup>2+</sup> and O<sup>2-</sup> (charges of 2+ and 2-), which also cancel in a 1:1 ratio. Both compounds have no overall charge because the total positive charge equals the total negative charge.

**FRONT**

What is the typical physical state of an ionic compound at room temperature?


**BACK**

Ionic compounds are typically **solid** at room temperature. They have high melting and boiling points because a large amount of energy is needed to break the many strong electrostatic forces of attraction throughout the giant lattice.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_HJTSFJTGKKvF4JwJ`)

**FRONT**

Why does an ionic compound not conduct electricity in the solid state?


**BACK**

In the solid state, the ions are locked in fixed positions within the giant **lattice** and cannot move. Electrical conduction requires charged particles that are free to move, so solid ionic compounds are poor conductors.

How does the giant lattice structure explain why ionic compounds have high melting points?


**BACK**
