## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_nZ2DbbxR6Xcyqg4Z`)

**FRONT**

What is **electrolysis**?


**BACK**

**Electrolysis** is the decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.

**FRONT**

What is an **electrolyte**?


**BACK**

An **electrolyte** is the ionic compound in a molten or dissolved solution that conducts electricity during electrolysis.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_GFGJzvczCtqdfzHG`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Solid ionic compounds can undergo electrolysis.


**BACK**

**False.**

In the solid state, ions are fixed in place and cannot move, so the compound cannot conduct electricity. Electrolysis requires the compound to be **molten** or **dissolved** in solution.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_2B4hmBwSD4DC26r4`)

**FRONT**

At which electrode are metals or hydrogen gas produced during electrolysis, and why?


**BACK**

Metals or hydrogen are produced at the **cathode** (negative electrode). Positive metal or hydrogen ions are attracted to the cathode, where they gain electrons (**reduction**) to form neutral atoms or molecules.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_6YYgjpnG8NNYtFSz`)

**FRONT**

During electrolysis of an aqueous solution, why is hydrogen produced at the cathode rather than the metal?


**BACK**

Hydrogen is produced instead of the metal when the metal is more reactive than hydrogen. The less reactive species is preferentially discharged at the cathode. In aqueous solution, water dissociates to provide H<sup>+</sup> ions, which are discharged in preference to ions of reactive metals.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_X8QWxpNp3SQ7zPnW`)

**FRONT**

How does charge transfer occur in the external circuit during electrolysis?


**BACK**

In the external circuit, **electrons** carry the charge. They flow from the negative terminal of the power supply to the cathode, and from the anode back to the positive terminal of the power supply.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_zpbqGYSWj8d7xxnR`)

**FRONT**

What happens to anions at the anode during electrolysis?


**BACK**

**Anions** (negative ions) move towards the anode and lose electrons there. This process is **oxidation**. The atoms or molecules produced then leave the electrode as a product.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_cRhNtJvjPNhJBsbX`)

**FRONT**

What general rule predicts the electrode products when a binary ionic compound is electrolysed in the molten state?


**BACK**

The positive ion migrates to the **cathode** and forms the metal product; the negative ion migrates to the **anode** and forms the non-metal product. A binary molten compound always produces its two constituent elements.

**BACK**

**Potassium** metal forms at the **cathode** and **chlorine** gas forms at the **anode**. K<sup>+</sup> ions are reduced at the cathode and Cl<sup>–</sup> ions are oxidised at the anode.
