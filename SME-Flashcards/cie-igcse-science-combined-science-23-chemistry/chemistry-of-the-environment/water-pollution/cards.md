## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_2S9fpKSfm5MP8MQ2`)

**FRONT**

Define **anhydrous**.


**BACK**

**Anhydrous** describes a substance that contains no water molecules. Anhydrous salts are used as chemical tests for water because they react with water to form hydrated versions.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_89xXzRz2xK8SJCSC`)

**FRONT**

What colour change is seen when water is added to anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride?


**BACK**

Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride changes from **blue** to **pink** in the presence of water. This colour change shows that water is present.

Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is blue.


**BACK**

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_cTccNxGm8NrcnzPQ`)

**FRONT**

How does the boiling point of impure water compare to that of pure water?


**BACK**

Impure water boils at a temperature **above 100 °C**, because dissolved impurities increase the boiling point. Pure water boils at exactly 100 °C.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_NyvZwTw7sQh6CtY7`)

**FRONT**

Define **distilled water**.


**BACK**

**Distilled water** is water that has been boiled to vapour and condensed back to a liquid, removing most dissolved impurities. It is used in practical chemistry to avoid interference from dissolved ions.


Spec links: `spcpt_xFJ4W77byXTyX9g2`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_3ZCP9yFcn2gYQR4j`)

**FRONT**

Why is distilled water used in practical chemistry rather than tap water?


**BACK**

Distilled water is used because it contains **fewer chemical impurities** than tap water. Dissolved ions in tap water could interfere with chemical reactions and affect results.


Spec links: `spcpt_xFJ4W77byXTyX9g2`


## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_NSPFPYFXxgv3d2J4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Impurities in water decrease its boiling point and increase its melting point.


**BACK**

**False.**

Impurities **increase** the boiling point and **decrease** the melting point of water. This is why impure water boils above 100 °C and melts below 0 °C.


Spec links: `spcpt_xFJ4W77byXTyX9g2`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_Bjj4Q5QhMBGRXz9W`)

**FRONT**

Why do pure substances have sharp melting and boiling points, while mixtures do not?


**BACK**

**Pure substances** contain only one type of particle, so they all change state at the same temperature. **Mixtures** contain different substances that melt or boil at different temperatures, giving a range rather than a single point.


Spec links: `spcpt_xFJ4W77byXTyX9g2`


## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_KRhtB3rvmZrkSHg7`)

**FRONT**

Define **sedimentation** in the context of water treatment.


**BACK**

**Sedimentation** is the process where water is allowed to stand in tanks so that mud, sand, and other solid particles settle to the bottom due to gravity. It is the first stage of water treatment.


Spec links: `spcpt_xpst8cvWjRVN4p6X`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_VZgBMVpVKBjMzRxj`)

**FRONT**

Why is chlorine added to water during water treatment?


**BACK**

Chlorine is added to **kill microbes** (pathogens) such as bacteria present in the water. This prevents water-borne diseases such as **cholera** and **typhoid**.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_5rrmmBsgXZdzDMFw`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Sedimentation and filtration remove soluble impurities from water.


**BACK**

**False.**

Sedimentation and filtration remove **solid (insoluble)** impurities such as mud and sand. Soluble impurities require other treatments such as carbon filtration.


Spec links: `spcpt_xpst8cvWjRVN4p6X`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_X9Sws9j4KkC6pYJm`)

**FRONT**

What are the three main stages of domestic water treatment, in order?


**BACK**

1. **Sedimentation and filtration** to remove solid particles
2. Filtration through **carbon** to remove tastes and odours
3. **Chlorination** to kill microbes


Spec links: `spcpt_xpst8cvWjRVN4p6X`


## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_rGCTxHzZPN32tX3x`)

**FRONT**

Define **filtration** in the context of water treatment.


**BACK**

**Filtration** is passing water through a filter to trap small **suspended solids** that did not settle out during sedimentation.


Spec links: `spcpt_xpst8cvWjRVN4p6X`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_M54fNkMKDwqS8X39`)

**FRONT**

Why can't filtration alone make water safe to drink?


**BACK**

Filtration can only remove solid particles, but **bacteria and other microorganisms** are too small to be trapped by filters. Chlorination is needed to kill these pathogens and make the water safe.


Spec links: `spcpt_xpst8cvWjRVN4p6X`
