## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_pXMtbpFHTDpk3prj`)

**FRONT**

What is an **acid**?


**BACK**

An **acid** is a substance that produces hydrogen ions (H<sup>+</sup>) when dissolved in water. Acids have a pH below 7 and can neutralise bases to form a salt and water.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Acids turn litmus red and methyl orange red.


**BACK**

**True.**

Both litmus and methyl orange turn **red** in acidic conditions. Litmus turns blue in alkalis, and methyl orange turns **yellow** in alkalis.

**BACK**

What is an **alkali**?


**BACK**

An **alkali** is a soluble base that produces hydroxide ions (OH<sup>-</sup>) when dissolved in water. Alkalis have a pH above 7.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_zHfTdYXnsDmHDxcr`)

**FRONT**

Why do bases have a pH above 7?


**BACK**

Bases release **hydroxide ions** (OH<sup>-</sup>) in aqueous solution. The presence of OH<sup>-</sup> ions raises the pH above 7, making the solution alkaline.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_9bw8Xhqsknx63gZv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

All bases are alkalis.


**BACK**

**False.**

An **alkali** is a *soluble* base. Many bases are insoluble in water (e.g. copper oxide) and are therefore not alkalis. All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_bN2wS6fzyQWcw5Tt`)

**FRONT**

What does the **pH scale** measure?


**BACK**

The **pH scale** measures the relative acidity or alkalinity of a solution. It runs from 0 to 14: pH below 7 is acidic, pH 7 is neutral and pH above 7 is alkaline.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_YwMVBs9ppVDXCwzv`)

**FRONT**

Why is universal indicator more useful than litmus for comparing the acidity of two solutions?


**BACK**

**Universal indicator** gives a range of colours across the pH scale, allowing the pH to be estimated and solutions to be compared numerically. **Litmus** only distinguishes between acid and alkali, with no information on strength.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_JzCKwMy6XgXfSgVH`)

**FRONT**

Why do metals form basic oxides while non-metals form acidic oxides?


**BACK**

The acid-base character of an oxide is determined by the **metallic character** of the element. Metals tend to lose electrons and form oxides that react with acids, making them **basic**. Non-metals tend to share or gain electrons and form oxides that react with bases, making them **acidic**.
