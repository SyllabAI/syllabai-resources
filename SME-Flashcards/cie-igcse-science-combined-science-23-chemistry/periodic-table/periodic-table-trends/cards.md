## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_fggsQQWt6qW6c8nc`)

**FRONT**

What information does the **group number** of an element give you about its atoms?


**BACK**

The **group number** shows how many electrons are in the outermost shell of the atom. For example, Group 2 elements have 2 electrons in their outer shell. This rule applies to Groups I-VII but not to Group 0 (noble gases), which have full outer shells.

**BACK**

**True.**

Elements in the same **group** have the same number of outer shell electrons, which gives them similar chemical properties and reactivity patterns.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_BmVTz6WvPJxkyGjC`)

**FRONT**

Why do elements in the same group have similar chemical properties?


**BACK**

Elements in the same **group** have the same number of electrons in their outer shell. Chemical reactions involve outer shell electrons, so elements with the same outer electron count behave similarly. This pattern holds for Groups I-VII but not Group 0, whose full outer shells make them unreactive.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_mWy7nMSPdKKNV2Kp`)

**FRONT**

How does metallic character change as you move across a period from left to right?


**BACK**

**Metallic character decreases** across a period from left to right. Atoms become less likely to lose electrons and more likely to gain or share them, making elements progressively less metallic.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Metallic character increases as you move down a group.


**BACK**

**True.**

Going down a group, atoms have more electron shells, making it easier to lose outer electrons. This increases **metallic character** down a group.

## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_FyPdgCvtw7mf8QsP`)

**FRONT**

What are **metalloids** (semi-metals)?


**BACK**

**Metalloids** are elements that display properties of both metals and non-metals. They are found along the zig-zag boundary between metals and non-metals in the Periodic Table.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_sDvtg4bfCQcBvr2V`)

**FRONT**

The reactivity of Group I metals increases down the group. Predict whether rubidium or caesium reacts more vigorously with water.


**BACK**

**Caesium** reacts more vigorously. Caesium is lower in Group I than rubidium, so it is more reactive. It can explode on contact with water due to the rapid release of heat and hydrogen gas.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_PzqrYH6mnXqPGNrY`)

**FRONT**

Describe the trend in reactivity of Group I metals going down the group, using the reactions of lithium, sodium and potassium with water as evidence.


**BACK**

**Reactivity increases** down Group I. Lithium reacts slowly with gentle fizzing, sodium reacts more vigorously and moves rapidly on water, and potassium reacts even more vigorously, burning with a **lilac flame**.
