**BACK**

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_6HjNNK7wqNVK33Nx`)

**FRONT**

Why do all Group I metals have similar chemical properties?


**BACK**

All Group I metals have **one electron** in their outer shell. This single outer electron is readily lost during reactions, which gives all Group I metals similar reactivity and means they form similar compounds.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_xstrxDWbVFVDjgvw`)

**FRONT**

How does reactivity change going down Group VII, and why?


**BACK**

**Reactivity decreases** going down Group VII. As the number of electron shells increases, the outer shell is further from the nucleus and there is more **electron shielding**, reducing the force of attraction for an incoming electron.

## Card 20 — keyword_definition (`fl_cnM3QQ3ZZqKPYFmP`)

**FRONT**

What is a **halogen displacement reaction**?


**BACK**

A **halogen displacement reaction** occurs when a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from an aqueous solution of its halide salt. The more reactive halogen gains electrons (is reduced) while the halide ions lose electrons (are oxidised).


Spec links: `spcpt_GqnJQcwyVd8tF3Y9`


## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_Pmx43SwKKTPp352D`)

**FRONT**

Why can chlorine displace bromine from potassium bromide solution, but bromine cannot displace chlorine from potassium chloride solution?


**BACK**

Chlorine is **more reactive** than bromine, so it can oxidise bromide ions to bromine. Bromine is **less reactive** than chlorine, so it cannot oxidise chloride ions. A halogen can only displace a **less reactive** halide.

## Card 34 — question_and_answer (`fl_JpkVy4JxZXz2QNzx`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

Transition elements have partially filled inner electron shells that allow electrons to absorb and emit specific wavelengths of visible light, producing **colour**. Group I metals do not have this electronic arrangement, so their compounds are generally **colourless** (white).
