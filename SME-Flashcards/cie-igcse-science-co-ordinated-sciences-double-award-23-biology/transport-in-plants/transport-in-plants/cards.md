## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_sVJ5BgkvstTwHHfT`)

**FRONT**

Describe the pathway taken by water through a plant.


**BACK**

Absorbed from the soil by root hair cells (by osmosis).

Moves across the root to the xylem.

Transported up the stem in the xylem.

Moves into the leaf, evaporates from the cells and leaves through the stomata (transpiration).

**BACK**

Record the starting position of the air bubble.

Measure how far the bubble moves in a set time.

Repeat under different conditions (e.g. higher temperature or wind speed).

This maintains a steep concentration gradient, so water diffuses out faster.

**FRONT**

Explain why high humidity decreases the rate of transpiration (Extended).

This reduces the concentration gradient between the leaf and the air, so less water diffuses out.

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_8ttJfWrBTf3wZ4Cq`)

**FRONT**

Explain why a plant wilts when it loses too much water (Extended).


**BACK**

The cells lose water faster than it is replaced, so they become flaccid.

The flaccid cells can no longer support the plant, so it wilts.

What is meant by a 'source' and a 'sink' in translocation (Extended)?

Sink: a part that uses or stores sucrose (e.g. roots, growing tips, storage organs).
