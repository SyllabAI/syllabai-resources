## Card 17 — keyword_definition (`fl_4WVbp8YWJtfzb373`)

**FRONT**

Antigen


**BACK**

An **antigen** is a molecule (often on the surface of a **pathogen**) that the body recognises as **foreign** and that triggers an **immune response**.

## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_M8QJZjHbB7j3kmmQ`)

**FRONT**

Antibody


**BACK**

An **antibody** is a protein produced by **lymphocytes** that binds to a specific **antigen** to help destroy the **pathogen**.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_TRMFCJ79t7pt3Qt4`)

**FRONT**

Describe how vaccination makes a person immune (Extended).


**BACK**

A vaccine containing weakened or dead pathogens (or their antigens) is given.

The antigens trigger lymphocytes to produce antibodies.

Memory cells are made.

If the real pathogen is met later, antibodies are produced quickly, so the person does not become ill.
