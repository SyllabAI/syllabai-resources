## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_4G9dPkFGstbwpBt9`)

**FRONT**

Describe the 'lock and key' model of enzyme action.


**BACK**

The substrate (key) fits exactly into the complementary active site of the enzyme (lock), forming an enzyme-substrate complex.

The reaction occurs and the products are released.

The enzyme is left unchanged and can be reused.


Spec links: `spcpt_63nvfjHzNwNXtM7v`

## Card 21 — keyword_definition (`fl_f5rsRvg5G7HgShxF`)

**FRONT**

Denatured


**BACK**

When an enzyme's active site changes shape (e.g. due to high temperature or extreme pH) so the substrate can no longer fit and the enzyme stops working.


Spec links: `spcpt_GKzqChJ4dZpB6vhr`


## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_d9C42xnQQNXr3tYP`)

**FRONT**

Describe how increasing temperature affects enzyme activity up to the optimum.


**BACK**

As temperature increases, enzyme and substrate molecules gain kinetic energy, move faster and collide more often, so the rate of reaction increases.


Spec links: `spcpt_GKzqChJ4dZpB6vhr`


## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_q952hb3xTx9SGfkZ`)

**FRONT**

What happens to enzyme activity above the optimum temperature?


**BACK**

The high temperature makes the active site change shape (the enzyme denatures), so the substrate no longer fits and the rate of reaction falls sharply.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_gDRsGgfjCnQwC6Vz`)

**FRONT**

How does pH affect the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction?


**BACK**

Each enzyme works fastest at its optimum pH.

Moving away from the optimum (more acidic or alkaline) slows the reaction.

An extreme pH denatures the enzyme.


Spec links: `spcpt_sGTvKmZpTSd9qKGK`
