**FRONT**

**Tone**


**BACK**

The sound of the narrator's **'voice'**, which should suit the **mood**, e.g. short sentences can create tension and unease.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_48R4JD6MxgKcbNB7`)

**FRONT**

What are the six steps of the descriptive writing structure?


**BACK**

**Panoramic** (whole scene) → **zoom** (one detail) → **single line** (key feeling) → **shift** (another part) → **shift** (a final part) → **panoramic** (scene changed)


Spec links: `spcpt_V7JF95jqB4WPKZBy`

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_vr6JPrr972nBWVFt`)

**FRONT**

**Freytag’s Pyramid**


**BACK**

A **five-part story structure**: exposition (set the scene) → rising action (build tension) → climax (turning point) → falling action (consequences) → resolution (ending)


Spec links: `spcpt_Hc6BxFrVrrSxZgvC`

**BACK**

**Showing** what a character is like through their actions, speech and thoughts rather than telling the reader, e.g. 'His head dropped'.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_F2XmQNxFd4cBFgkz`)

**FRONT**

What happens at the climax of your story?


**BACK**

The **turning point**: everything changes and your character can't go back; **vary your sentences** here for dramatic effect.


Spec links: `spcpt_Hc6BxFrVrrSxZgvC`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_6tyBWxn7TGVtYYff`)

**FRONT**

How do you keep your story plot manageable in the exam?


**BACK**

Plan **one main event** and **one main setting**, with no more than **two characters**; avoid long timescales and multiple plot twists.


Spec links: `spcpt_Hc6BxFrVrrSxZgvC`
