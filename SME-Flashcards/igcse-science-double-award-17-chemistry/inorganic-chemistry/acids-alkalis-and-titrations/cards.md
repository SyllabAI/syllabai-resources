## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_XJBHvFBJwD7SBXCm`)

**FRONT**

Why is universal indicator not suitable for titrations?


**BACK**

A **sharp colour change** is required to identify the titration **endpoint**. Universal indicator changes colour gradually across a range of pH values and cannot give a precise endpoint.

Litmus is a suitable indicator for acid-alkali titrations.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Litmus** is not suitable for titrations because its colour change is not sharp and it passes through a purple transition colour near neutral pH, making it difficult to identify the **endpoint** accurately.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_WyWbRDjMGYRt8HdM`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

All reactions of acids with other substances are neutralisation reactions.


**BACK**

**False.**

Neutralisation requires both a **salt** and **water** to be produced. When a **metal** reacts with an **acid**, a salt and **hydrogen gas** are formed — but no water — so it is not a neutralisation reaction.


Spec links: `spcpt_yFHP4rTvHdVcJXKp`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_zqkV8rp7v7DXvFyP`)

**FRONT**

What is the net ionic equation for all acid-base neutralisation reactions?


**BACK**

H<sup>+</sup> (aq) + OH<sup>-</sup> (aq) → H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

The **H**<sup>**+**</sup> ions from the acid react with the **OH**<sup>**-**</sup> ions from the alkali to produce **water**.

**FRONT**

Why are bases added to soil to treat acidity?


**BACK**

Bases such as **limestone** and **quicklime** neutralise excess **acid** in the soil, raising the **pH** to a level at which crops can grow.
