## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_CV8ywf9f7HFwMDH3`)

**FRONT**

Define **alkali metals**.


**BACK**

The **alkali metals** are the Group 1 elements: **lithium**, **sodium**, **potassium**, **rubidium**, **caesium** and **francium**. They are soft, low-density metals that form **alkaline solutions** when they react with water.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_NGHJsxDSk57ttYjc`)

**FRONT**

Why do all Group 1 elements share similar chemical properties?


**BACK**

All Group 1 elements share similar chemical properties because they each have **one electron** in their outermost shell. This means they all need to lose just one electron to become highly stable.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_NqbVbrRbvSYWNXds`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Reactivity increases as you go down Group 1.


**BACK**

**True.**

As you descend Group 1, each element has more electron shells, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more easily lost. This makes each successive element **more reactive**.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_Qd89dgWxW8gDxbmh`)

**FRONT**

Why do alkali metals tarnish when exposed to air?


**BACK**

Alkali metals **tarnish** because they react with **oxygen** in the air to form a **metal oxide**, which appears as a dull coating on the surface. The rate of tarnishing increases going down the group.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_NKR8ZnjKzXSRZtkC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The melting point of Group 1 metals increases going down the group.


**BACK**

**False.**

The **melting point decreases** going down Group 1. This is because the attractive forces between the outer electrons and the positive ions become weaker as the atoms get larger.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_6jznZDF9WhMN9ctM`)

**FRONT**

What evidence from their reactions with water places the Group 1 elements in the same chemical family?


**BACK**

All Group 1 metals react with water to produce a **metal hydroxide** and **hydrogen** gas, following the same general pattern: 2M (s) + 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l) → 2MOH (aq) + H<sub>2</sub> (g). This consistent pattern of behaviour confirms they belong to the same **chemical family**.
