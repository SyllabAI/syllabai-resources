## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_8WCkPMRZvcMvJqbQ`)

**FRONT**

Why does a substance with **stronger forces between its particles** have a higher **melting point**?


**BACK**

A stronger force between particles requires **more energy** to overcome. This means a higher temperature is needed to give the particles enough energy to move around each other, so the **melting point** is higher.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_53KYxyYqNnhNrgKt`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between **boiling** and **evaporation**?


**BACK**

**Boiling** occurs at a specific temperature (the boiling point) and involves bubbles of gas forming inside the liquid. **Evaporation** occurs over a range of temperatures and only at the surface of the liquid, where high-energy particles escape.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_yPY4sP5VqYy5fCHw`)

**FRONT**

Why is a change of state described as a **physical change**?


**BACK**

A change of state is a **physical change** because the particles themselves remain unchanged. Only the **forces between particles** change. They weaken on heating and strengthen on cooling.

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_QPfwgCX2MkSkqkDM`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Particles in a liquid are randomly arranged and move around each other.


**BACK**

**True.**

Unlike **solids**, where particles **vibrate about fixed positions** in a **regular arrangement**, liquid particles are **randomly arranged** and **move around each other**, which is why liquids can flow.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_wfyWGHJVkxPdfkcy`)

**FRONT**

What happens to the particles of a solid when it **melts**?


**BACK**

When a solid melts, particles absorb **heat energy** which is used to overcome the **forces between particles**. The particles break free from their fixed positions and begin to **move around each other** as a liquid. During melting, the temperature stays constant because the energy is used to break forces, not to increase the speed of particles.

The freezing point of a pure substance is the same temperature as its melting point.


**BACK**

Freezing is the reverse of melting and occurs at the same temperature. For example, water freezes and melts at 0 °C.

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_chvMFmvh6BwShgds`)

**FRONT**

What is **diffusion**?


**BACK**

**Diffusion** is the movement of particles from a region of **high concentration** to a region of **low concentration**, due to the random motion of particles. It requires no energy input.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_279TRyKBQdDr6czk`)

**FRONT**

Why does diffusion occur **faster in gases** than in **liquids**?


**BACK**

In a gas, particles are **far apart** and move **quickly in all directions**, so they mix more easily. In a liquid, particles are **closer together** and move more slowly, so diffusion is slower.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_RVJx4Vhz8n3whnxg`)

**FRONT**

Why does a coloured solution become **paler** when diluted?


**BACK**

Adding more solvent **spreads out** the solute particles, so fewer are present in any given volume. The colour appears **paler** because the coloured particles are less concentrated, but they are not destroyed.

## Card 20 — keyword_definition (`fl_kCmrCdJyfBCw8dSs`)

**FRONT**

What is a **saturated solution**?


**BACK**

A **saturated solution** is a solution that contains the **maximum** amount of **solute** that can be dissolved in the **solvent** at a given temperature. No more solute can dissolve.
