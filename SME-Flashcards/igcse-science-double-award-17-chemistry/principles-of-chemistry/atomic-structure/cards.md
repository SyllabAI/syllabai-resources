## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_dbqtyPyYBpKQByGN`)

**FRONT**

What is an **atom**?


**BACK**

An **atom** is the smallest particle of an element, containing **electrons** surrounding a **nucleus** that contains **protons** and **neutrons**.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_pQZhhF8M4vvdCNGf`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Protons and neutrons are found in the nucleus of an atom.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **nucleus** is at the centre of the atom and contains **protons** and **neutrons**. **Electrons** orbit the nucleus in **shells**.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_CsZQhvmNk2N9fkZQ`)

**FRONT**

What is a **molecule**?


**BACK**

A **molecule** is a group of two or more atoms **covalently bonded** together. Molecules can be made of atoms of the same element (e.g. O<sub>2</sub>) or different elements (e.g. H<sub>2</sub>O).

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_wtJR5qVpQ8MmcHzc`)

**FRONT**

What are the relative masses and charges of a proton, neutron and electron?


**BACK**

A **proton** has relative mass 1 and charge +1. A **neutron** has relative mass 1 and charge 0. An **electron** has negligible mass and charge −1.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_VddPScnB8qsbDy7f`)

**FRONT**

How do you calculate the number of neutrons in an atom?


**BACK**

The number of **neutrons** is calculated by subtracting the atomic number from the mass number. For example, lithium (atomic number 3, mass number 7) has 7 − 3 = 4 neutrons.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_RYYgx8hFfQyQ2t8J`)

**FRONT**

What is the **relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>) of an element?


**BACK**

**Relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>) is the **average** mass of one atom of an element, taking into account the abundance of all its isotopes, measured **relative to** 1/12th the mass of a **carbon-12** atom.

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_Gp67K7tmXZPXMnqy`)

**FRONT**

What is **relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>)?


**BACK**

**Relative atomic mass** (*A*<sub>r</sub>) is the average mass of one atom of an element, calculated from the mass numbers and percentage abundances of all its isotopes.


Spec links: `spcpt_hxKk9zVSYMcGqNkR`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_xzhWskMsGQBw3Bw6`)

**FRONT**

What is the formula for calculating relative atomic mass from two isotopes?


**BACK**

The **relative atomic mass** is calculated as: *A*<sub>r</sub> = [(% of isotope A × mass of isotope A) + (% of isotope B × mass of isotope B)] ÷ 100. Additional isotopes extend the numerator in the same way.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_rWWR7mDBKmf2szKM`)

**FRONT**

A sample of rubidium contains 72% <sup>85</sup>Rb and 28% <sup>87</sup>Rb. What is the relative atomic mass of this sample?


**BACK**

The **relative atomic mass** = [(72 × 85) + (28 × 87)] ÷ 100 = (6120 + 2436) ÷ 100 = **85.6** (to 1 d.p.).
