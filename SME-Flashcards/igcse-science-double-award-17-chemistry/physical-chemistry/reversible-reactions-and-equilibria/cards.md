## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_nCZgP38JH37zpQ75`)

**FRONT**

Define **reversible reaction**.


**BACK**

A **reversible reaction** is one in which the products can react to re-form the original **reactants**, so the reaction can proceed in both the forward and the reverse direction.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_3bRRVWvrzz9SCh7j`)

**FRONT**

What symbol is used in equations to show a reversible reaction?


**BACK**

The symbol **⇌** is used, with two half-arrowheads pointing in opposite directions to show the **forward** and **reverse** reactions occurring simultaneously.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_CKZzwDWwC2kdGfnt`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The decomposition of ammonium chloride is a reversible reaction.


**BACK**

**True.**

Heating **ammonium chloride** produces **ammonia** and **hydrogen chloride** gases, and when these cool they recombine to form solid **ammonium chloride** again, so the reaction is reversible (NH<sub>4</sub>Cl ⇌ NH<sub>3</sub> + HCl).

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_Dxp9mY3jV6nxm4Qc`)

**FRONT**

What colour change occurs when hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated?


**BACK**

**Hydrated copper(II) sulfate** is blue and turns **white** when heated as it loses its water of crystallisation to form anhydrous **copper(II) sulfate**.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_RGh5Q35q3CFBNzt2`)

**FRONT**

Define **water of crystallisation**.


**BACK**

**Water of crystallisation** is water that is chemically incorporated into the crystal structure of a hydrated salt during the crystallisation process, affecting the salt's shape and colour.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_9f76wJsNB3wBYFMf`)

**FRONT**

How can the direction of a reversible reaction be changed?


**BACK**

A **reversible reaction** can be made to go in the **forward** or **reverse** direction by changing the reaction **conditions**, such as temperature. Heating hydrated copper(II) sulfate drives the reaction forward (dehydration); adding water reverses it.
