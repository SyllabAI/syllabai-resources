## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_RsFQKM3Khymn5knq`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition polymerisation**.


**BACK**

**Addition polymerisation** is a reaction in which many small **monomer** molecules containing a **C=C double bond** join together to form a large **polymer** molecule, with no by-products.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_r6h5HF73FsJGs9C3`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Addition polymerisation produces by-products alongside the polymer.


**BACK**

**False.**

In **addition polymerisation**, only one product is formed. All the atoms in the **monomers** are incorporated into the **polymer** chain, with no by-products.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_vJTKSrQpSz4R77Dc`)

**FRONT**

Define **repeat unit** (in polymer chemistry).


**BACK**

A **repeat unit** is the smallest group of atoms that is repeated throughout a **polymer** chain. It is drawn inside brackets with a subscript n and bonds extending outside the brackets.


Spec links: `spcpt_mMzNyNHHDpWHRJvy`


## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_PHVXjDPswRHDNV6Z`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

To draw a repeat unit from a monomer, replace the C=C double bond with a single bond and add extension bonds at each end.


**BACK**

**True.**

The double bond in the **monomer** becomes a single bond in the **repeat unit**. Bonds are added to each end of the unit, extending outside the brackets, with a subscript n to indicate the large number of repeating units.


Spec links: `spcpt_mMzNyNHHDpWHRJvy`

How do you deduce the monomer from a polymer repeat unit?


**BACK**

To deduce the **monomer** from a polymer repeat unit, change the single bond in the repeat unit back to a C=C **double bond**, then remove the extension bonds at each end and the subscript n.

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_8vHPpHCVX6G5FW7T`)

**FRONT**

Define **non-biodegradable**.


**BACK**

A **non-biodegradable** material is one that cannot be broken down by **microorganisms** such as **decomposers**. Addition polymers are non-biodegradable due to their strong **C-C bonds**.

Addition polymers biodegrade quickly in landfill sites.


**BACK**

**Addition polymers** are **non-biodegradable** because their strong C-C bonds cannot be broken down by microorganisms. They persist in **landfill** sites, causing sites to fill up rapidly.
