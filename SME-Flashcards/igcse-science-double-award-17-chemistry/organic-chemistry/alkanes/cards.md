**FRONT**

Define **saturated hydrocarbon**.


**BACK**

A **saturated hydrocarbon** is a compound of carbon and hydrogen that contains only carbon-carbon **single bonds** and no **double bonds**.

**FRONT**

**BACK**

**Alkanes** are described as **saturated** because they contain only carbon-carbon **single bonds** — there are no double bonds in their structure.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_MzbSxW7vvmdx9kBx`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The molecular formula of methane is CH<sub>4</sub>.


**BACK**

**True.**

**Methane** is the first member of the alkane homologous series and has the molecular formula CH<sub>4</sub>, fitting the general formula C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub> with n = 1.

**FRONT**

Define **homologous series**.


**BACK**

A **homologous series** is a family of compounds that share the same **general formula**, differ by CH<sub>2</sub> between successive members and show a gradual trend in physical properties.

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_spq2kFhmSxyC5X7q`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Alkanes are generally very reactive compounds.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Alkanes** are generally **unreactive** compounds, though they do undergo combustion, can be cracked into smaller molecules and can react with **halogens** in the presence of **ultraviolet (UV) light**.


Spec links: `spcpt_NGfdnB4Z2q4QS4jm`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_YDJRPKbsnX57t6Xn`)

**FRONT**

What condition is required for alkanes to react with halogens?


**BACK**

Alkanes react with halogens only in the presence of **ultraviolet (UV) radiation**, which provides the energy needed to initiate the **substitution reaction**.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_dNnWRgtrZqFv7N63`)

**FRONT**

What type of organic compound forms when an alkane reacts with a halogen?


**BACK**

A **halogenoalkane** (also called a haloalkane) forms, in which one hydrogen atom in the alkane has been replaced by a **halogen** atom.
