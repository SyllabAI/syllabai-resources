**BACK**

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_tGFF7ZV4Yj9P3fg2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Alkenes are more reactive than alkanes.


**BACK**

**True.**

**Alkenes** are more reactive than alkanes because the **C=C double bond** can break open to allow other atoms to join the molecule in **addition reactions**. Alkanes have only single bonds and cannot undergo addition reactions.

**FRONT**

Define **unsaturated** (in the context of organic chemistry).


**BACK**

An **unsaturated** compound is one that contains at least one **carbon-carbon double bond** (C=C), meaning it can undergo **addition reactions** by opening the double bond to bond with more atoms.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_FP5GzkZfH8nMsFNN`)

**FRONT**

Why are alkenes described as unsaturated hydrocarbons?


**BACK**

Alkenes are described as **unsaturated** hydrocarbons because they contain a C=C **double bond**. This means the carbon atoms are not bonded to the maximum number of hydrogen atoms possible. The double bond can open to allow more atoms to attach.


Spec links: `spcpt_BjNbYV4N9m6rCwKC`

**FRONT**

Define **addition reaction** (in the context of alkenes).


**BACK**

An **addition reaction** is a reaction in which atoms from a simple molecule add across the C=C **double bond** of an **alkene**, converting it into a single bond and producing a single product.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_xkHDBv63TpzBk33J`)

**FRONT**

What is the result of adding bromine water to an alkene?


**BACK**

Adding bromine water to an alkene causes the solution to **decolourise** from orange to colourless. The bromine atoms add across the C=C double bond, removing free **bromine** from solution.
