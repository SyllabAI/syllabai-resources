## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_K7CsWtSjGC8TCvYh`)

**FRONT**

How does light intensity affect a community?


**BACK**

Light is needed for **photosynthesis**. At low light levels plants **grow more slowly** and produce **less food for herbivores**, which affects the whole food chain.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`


## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_KSTd2rXfns3JCQZp`)

**FRONT**

How does temperature affect population size and distribution?


**BACK**

It affects the rate of **enzyme-controlled reactions**. If it is too hot or too cold, enzymes work inefficiently, reducing **survival and reproduction rates**.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`


## Card 31 — question_and_answer (`fl_kfrg4V3r52ZF2xcC`)

**FRONT**

How does water availability affect an ecosystem?


**BACK**

Water is **essential for life**, so the amount available determines **which plants and animals can survive** there — cacti in deserts, amphibians only in moist habitats.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`


## Card 32 — question_and_answer (`fl_phZ7zgSwWc88GsZ7`)

**FRONT**

How does soil quality affect a community?


**BACK**

Soil provides **water, minerals** and anchorage. **Poor or acidic** soils limit plant growth, which affects the whole food web — heather grows on acidic moorland where grasses do not.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`


## Card 33 — question_and_answer (`fl_qP7vG8PxzyRFVpGN`)

**FRONT**

How does oxygen availability affect aquatic habitats?


**BACK**

Some animals, such as fish, need **high oxygen concentrations**.

In warm, stagnant ponds oxygen levels drop, so animals **die or migrate**.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`

## Card 35 — question_and_answer (`fl_xhmpgwJz8hX5gjyZ`)

How does food availability affect population size?


**BACK**

**More food** means a higher chance of surviving and reproducing, so populations **increase**.

## Card 36 — question_and_answer (`fl_QXqTZHc2dt2nSMVJ`)

**FRONT**

How can a new predator disrupt an ecosystem?


**BACK**

It **upsets the balance** between predator and prey, causing **prey populations to decline** — as red foxes introduced to Australia did to native mammals and birds.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`


## Card 37 — question_and_answer (`fl_wnqMYPnkxWvmjJHp`)

**FRONT**

Why can a new pathogen devastate a population?


**BACK**

The populations there may have **no immunity or resistance** to it, so their numbers **fall sharply** or they are wiped out — as with ash dieback in European ash trees.


Spec links: `spcpt_9KjSMxvKcHrDkNNz`


## Card 38 — question_and_answer (`fl_W5SF8JQY8tYs5TWy`)

**FRONT**

How have grey squirrels affected UK red squirrels?


**BACK**

Grey squirrels **outcompete** them for **food and nesting sites**, and also **carry a virus** that red squirrels are not resistant to, so red squirrel numbers fall.
