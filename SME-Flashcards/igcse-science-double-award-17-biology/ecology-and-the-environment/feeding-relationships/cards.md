## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_KW8xydD2KmNWy3h7`)

**FRONT**

Define **producer**.


**BACK**

An organism at the **first trophic level**, such as a plant or alga, that converts **light energy into chemical energy** stored in glucose by photosynthesis.


Spec links: `spcpt_WHd43yvp9f8dvP3t`


## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_qBhFhkJq27PNTFqZ`)

**FRONT**

Define **primary consumer**.


**BACK**

An organism at the **second trophic level** that **eats producers**, transferring chemical energy from plant tissues into its own.


Spec links: `spcpt_WHd43yvp9f8dvP3t`

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_dx4drZtRbg3rcb7F`)

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_4QMZ2rd2QxGRCcFb`)

**FRONT**

Define **decomposer**.


**BACK**

An organism, such as **bacteria** or **fungi**, that **breaks down dead organisms and waste**, recycling nutrients back into the ecosystem.

**BACK**

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_r6qsg6sMdTssWkpW`)

**FRONT**

Why can a change in one population affect many others?


**BACK**

Because populations in a food web are **interconnected** — a change in one alters the food available to, or the predation pressure on, several others.


Spec links: `spcpt_bD2GJFkg664kHF64`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_XNYZWXHSKhXxFNsD`)

**FRONT**

Give an example of one population change affecting another.


**BACK**

If the number of **rabbits** falls, the **foxes** that eat them have less food, so the fox population **decreases** too.


Spec links: `spcpt_bD2GJFkg664kHF64`

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_4JfVthtFrY64drfK`)

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_F6DKR4mB7GwfmFrs`)

**FRONT**

Give four conventions for drawing a food pyramid.


**BACK**

- Keep the trophic levels in food-chain order, with **producers at the bottom**
- **Label each bar** with its trophic level
- Make the **bar length reflect** the number, biomass or energy
- Keep the bars the **same height**, and **draw to scale** if graph paper is given

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_NdfQhjHHFRNmPVqv`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

A consumer **eats** the level below, **breaks down the carbon compounds** in its biomass, and uses the energy and substances released to **build new biomass** in its own tissues.


Spec links: `spcpt_36P8TTMv4np6sC47`

## Card 32 — question_and_answer (`fl_xGsx4FsrwF5d9c5Q`)

**FRONT**

Why are food chains short?


**BACK**

Because so much energy is lost at each level that the energy available eventually becomes **too small to support another trophic level**.

**BACK**

Organisms **rarely eat every part** of their prey — many predators leave the **bones, fur, teeth or claws**, so the energy in those parts is never transferred.

Because **not all ingested material is digested and absorbed** — plant material in particular is hard to digest and passes out as **faeces**.
