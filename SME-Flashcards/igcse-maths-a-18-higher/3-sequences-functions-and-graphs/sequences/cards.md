## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_wtsvFpJ3DqG5MsZX`)

**FRONT**

What is a **sequence**?


**BACK**

A sequence is an **ordered set** **of (usually) numbers**.



## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_KM28wBcJ7CMPJb8z`)

**FRONT**

In the context of sequences, what is a **term**?


**BACK**

A **term** is **one of the numbers** in a sequence.



## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_Z2MSxqsNgSwtwGwn`)

**FRONT**

In the context of sequences, what is ***n***?


**BACK**

***n*** is the **position of a term** in a sequence.

E.g. when *n* = 3, it is referring to the third term of the sequence.



## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_XkCmhvythzkCcpbC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

For the **first term**, *n* = 0.


**BACK**

**False.**

For the first term, *n* = 1.



## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_xffhGpDgfJBpjTr4`)

**FRONT**

What is** subscript notation **for sequences?


**BACK**

**Subscript notation **is used to talk about a particular term.

For example

- $a_{1}$ is the 1st term
- $a_{7}$ is the 7th term
- $a_{n}$ is the *n*th term



## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZBzpfvd3jWNg463f`)

**FRONT**

What is a **position-to-term** rule?


**BACK**

A **position-to-term** rule gives the ***n*****th term of a sequence** as a **formula** in terms of *n*.



## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_GD4FYDp4bFcvC3NV`)

**FRONT**

How would you find the **first three terms** of a sequence using a **position-to-term** rule?


**BACK**

To find the** first three terms** of a sequence using a **position-to-term** rule, substitute $n=1$, $n=2$ and $n=3$ into the position-to-term formula.



## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_HcqwVp7RdDfpPWxX`)

**FRONT**

What is a **term-to-term **rule?


**BACK**

A **term-to-term** rule tells you how to find a term from the term before it.

I.e., it gives the (*n*+1)th term in terms of the *n*th term.



## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_Y54DykTHmHcy4XW2`)

**FRONT**

What is an **arithmetic sequence**?


**BACK**

An arithmetic sequence is a sequence of numbers that increase or decrease by the same amount from one term to the next.



## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_W9S3ZqjsXGHX9Nfs`)

**FRONT**

Define the **common difference** of an arithmetic sequence.


**BACK**

The common difference is the amount that an arithmetic sequence increases or decreases by from one term to the next.



## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_JHKBdWWXJVCpQ3BK`)

**FRONT**

What is $a$ the notation for in the context of arithmetic sequences and series?


**BACK**

$a$ is the notation for the first term in an arithmetic sequence (or series).



## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_t2THBg4DkpYYVzZw`)

**FRONT**

What is $d$ the notation for in the context of arithmetic sequences and series?


**BACK**

$d$ is the notation for the common difference of an arithmetic sequence (or series).



## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_6VdQZZwFbNtsJYYV`)

**FRONT**

What is the position-to-term formula for an arithmetic sequence in terms of $a$, $d$ and $n$?


**BACK**

The position-to-term formula for an arithmetic sequence in terms of $a$, $d$ and $n$ is  `a plus open parentheses n minus 1 close parentheses d`.



## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_KkcP2FMMvQVm3KcT`)

**FRONT**

What is an **arithmetic series**?


**BACK**

An arithmetic series is where the terms of an arithmetic sequence are added together.



## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_F6yfbNHz2BqGjFY6`)

**FRONT**

What is $S_{n}$ the notation for in the context of arithmetic sequences and series?


**BACK**

$S_{n}$ is the notation for the sum of the first *n* terms of an arithmetic series.
