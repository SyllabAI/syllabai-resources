## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZsW54VT6JjcX7rsv`)

**FRONT**

What is a **square root **of a number?


**BACK**

A **square root** of a number is a value that, when **squared**, gives the **original number**.

E.g. 3 is the square root of 9 because 3<sup>2</sup> = 3 x 3 = 9.



## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_gtHPBF6KX9kH4gwp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False? **

Every **positive **number has **two square roots**.


**BACK**

**True. **

Every positive number has two square roots, **one positive and one negative**.

E.g. $\sqrt{64}=8$ and $-8$

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_TQTMj22dkk77ZPSx`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **reciprocal**.


**BACK**

The **reciprocal **of a number is the number that you **multiply it by to get 1**.

For example, $\frac{2}{3}$ is the reciprocal of $\frac{3}{2}$.



## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_G6fqnVPxJPq3ydsg`)

**FRONT**

How do I **estimate a root**, e.g. the square root of 27?


**BACK**

You can estimate the root of a number by finding the closest integer roots either side of it.

E.g. To find $\sqrt{27}$

$\sqrt{25}=5$ and $\sqrt{36}=6$

So $\sqrt{27}$ is between $5$ and $6$



## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_D5SF6PxmJ43CNvWj`)

**FRONT**

What number do you get when you raise any **non-zero number** to the **power of zero**, e.g. 2<sup>0</sup>?


**BACK**

Any non-zero number raised to the power of 0 is **equal to 1**.

E.g. 2<sup>0</sup> = 1.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_QBxT7T97VpHH69Ns`)

**FRONT**

What do you get if you raise a positive number to the **power of ½, **
e.g. 5<sup>1/2 </sup>?


**BACK**

If you raise a non-zero number to the power of ½ you get its **positive square root**.

E.g. $5^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{5}$.



## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_tW9xTGJWZW8CWKhM`)

**FRONT**

What is the **index law** for $a^{m}\timesa^{n}$?


**BACK**

$a^{m}\timesa^{n}=a^{m+n}$

If you **multiply **two powers with the **same base number**, you **add **the indices together.



## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_vX9nPPRWcjCWVxqq`)

**FRONT**

What is the **index law** for $a^{m}\diva^{n}$?


**BACK**

$a^{m}\diva^{n}=a^{m-n}$

If you **divide **two powers with the **same base number**, you **subtract **one index from the other.



## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_FmfWznJWqyFZqCKd`)

**FRONT**

What is the **index law** for `open parentheses a to the power of m close parentheses to the power of n`?


**BACK**

`open parentheses a to the power of m close parentheses to the power of n equals a to the power of m cross times n end exponent`

If you **raise a power to another power**, you **multiply** the indices.



## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_jm7KvnHdjjRR2GNf`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`a to the power of m over n end exponent equals open parentheses m-th root of a to the power of n end root close parentheses`


**BACK**

**False.**

`a to the power of m over n end exponent equals open parentheses n-th root of a close parentheses to the power of m`

The **denominator **of an index is the **root **and the **numerator **of an index is the **power**.



## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_s9yPTzr6SQgdVh32`)

**FRONT**

What does a number written in **standard form** look like?


**BACK**

A number in **standard form** looks like $a\times10^{n}$, e.g. $5.2\times10^{3}$.

It is a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of ten.



## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_Gv3BKfCzZfpTCG2P`)

**FRONT**

When a number is written in **standard form** $a\times10^{n}$, which **values** can $a$ be?


**BACK**

For $a\times10^{n}$, $a$ is **at least 1 and less than 10**, $1\leqa<10$.

This means there is one non-zero digit before the decimal point.



## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_GsD8YFVymGPFPMWG`)

**FRONT**

**True or False? **

When a number is written in **standard form** $a\times10^{n}$, $n$ can be a fraction.


**BACK**

**False.**

For a number written in standard form, $a\times10^{n}$, the value $n$ must be an **integer**.

It can be zero, positive or negative.



## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_xy9z6h9hYbyJGSKg`)

**FRONT**

When a value **between 0 and 1**, is written in standard form $a\times10^{n}$, what **type of number** will $n$ be?


**BACK**

If a value is between 0 and 1 then when it is written in standard form, $n$ will be a **negative integer**.

E.g. 0.083 would be written as 8.3 x 10<sup>-2</sup>.



## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_N25tNHXGQzPM22mf`)

**FRONT**

How do you **multiply **two numbers that are written in** standard form** and give the answer in standard form **using index laws**?

E.g. `open parentheses 2 cross times 10 cubed close parentheses cross times open parentheses 6 cross times 10 to the power of 5 close parentheses`


**BACK**

Multiply together the **values **of $a$ from both numbers and write this in standard form if needed, e.g. $2\times6=12=1.2\times10^{1}$.

**Use index laws to combine the powers of 10**, e.g. $10^{3}\times10^{5}=10^{8}$.

**Multiply **the two parts together, e.g. $1.2\times10^{1}\times10^{8}$.

**Simplify** if necessary, using index laws again e.g. $1.2\times10^{9}$.



## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_mBhDD3qHjVJynyd6`)

**Combine the powers of 10 using index laws**, e.g. $10^{9}\div10^{2}=10^{7}$
