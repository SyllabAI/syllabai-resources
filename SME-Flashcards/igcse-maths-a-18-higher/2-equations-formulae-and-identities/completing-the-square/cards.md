## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_YMk8xHnnkRJx8XC3`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The coordinates of the **turning point** (vertex) of the quadratic curve $y = ( x + 3 )^{2} + 5$ are $( 3 , 5 )$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The coordinates of the turning point (vertex) of the quadratic curve $y = ( x + 3 )^{2} + 5$ are **not** $( 3 , 5 )$.

The correct coordinates are $( - 3 , 5 )$.

This is a common mistake in the exam! If $y = ( x + p )^{2} + q$ is the curve, then $( - p , q )$ are the coordinates of the turning point.



## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_DWdzpZ9qTWjWNdfP`)

**FRONT**

What is the **first step **to **completing the square** of the quadratic expression   $ax^{2}+bx+c$ where $a$ is** not** equal to 1, e.g. $3x^{2}+12x-8$?


**BACK**

The first step to completing the square of the quadratic expression   $ax^{2}+bx+c$ where $a$ is** **not equal to 1 is to **factorise out** $a$.

This gives $a [ x^{2} + \frac{b}{a} x + \frac{c}{a} ]$.

You can then complete the square inside the big brackets.

E.g. to complete the square of the expression $3x^{2}+12x-8$, the first step would be to factorise out $3$, giving $3 [ x^{2} + 4 x - \frac{8}{3} ]$.



## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_zq4YpVrbP9bbQwf3`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The coordinates of the **turning points** on the curves $y = ( x + 2 )^{2} + 4$ and $y = 3 ( x + 2 )^{2} + 4$ are the** same**.


**BACK**

**True**.

The coordinates of the **turning points** on the curves $y = ( x + 2 )^{2} + 4$ and $y = 3 ( x + 2 )^{2} + 4$ are the** same**.

The coordinates of the turning point on $y = a ( x + p )^{2} + q$ are always $( - p , q )$, regardless of the value of $a$ (even if $a<0$).



## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_3mK5QNnmDms6DnBz`)

**FRONT**

Explain how writing $x^{2}-4x+9$ in the form $( x - 2 )^{2} + 5$ shows that any output of the function $\mathrm{f} ( x ) = x^{2} - 4 x + 9$ is always greater than or equal to 5.


**BACK**

If $x^{2}-4x+9$ can be written as $( x - 2 )^{2} + 5$ by **completing the square**, then  $\mathrm{f} ( x ) = x^{2} - 4 x + 9$ can be written as $\mathrm{f} ( x ) = ( x - 2 )^{2} + 5$.

The coordinates of the **turning point** will be $( 2 , 5 )$.

As this is a **positive quadratic**, the turning point will be a **minimum **and the therefore the value of $\mathrm{f} ( x )$ will always be greater than or equal to 5.
