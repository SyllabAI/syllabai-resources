## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_hJMYBFbq3qBPNGzP`)

**FRONT**

Define **null hypothesis** and **alternative hypothesis**.


**BACK**

The **null hypothesis** $\text{H}_{0}$ states the assumed value of the population parameter, taking it that nothing has changed.

The **alternative hypothesis** $\text{H}_{1}$ states how that parameter may have changed.

A test assumes $\text{H}_{0}$ is true throughout, and ends by either **rejecting** it or not.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_qg63w3zHqy6BJCKJ`)

**FRONT**

Define **population parameter**.


**BACK**

A numerical characteristic that helps define a **population**, such as the probability $p$ of an event happening, or the mean $μ$.

A hypothesis test uses a **sample** to test a claim made about one of these values.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_rCgxDwHFssXfNQXY`)

**FRONT**

Define **significance level**.


**BACK**

The probability threshold a test is judged against, setting how unlikely a result has to be before $\text{H}_{0}$ is rejected.

It must be fixed **before** the test is carried out, and is usually 1%, 5% or 10%.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_YsMxjgTpB6zMtzFP`)

**FRONT**

Define **critical region** and **critical value**.


**BACK**

The **critical region** is the set of values the test statistic could take that would lead to $\text{H}_{0}$ being **rejected**.

The **critical value** is its boundary, the least extreme value that still causes rejection.

The test statistic is also called the **observed value**, and the significance level fixes where the boundary sits.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_MBQTr8DjfJzbWZpW`)

**FRONT**

For a discrete distribution, why is the actual significance level usually below the stated one?


**BACK**

The critical value has to be a **whole value** the variable can actually take, so the region cannot be trimmed to meet the stated level exactly.

The probability of landing inside it, assuming $\text{H}_{0}$ is true, therefore comes out at or below the stated level.

That probability is the **actual significance level**.


Spec links: `spcpt_SXgS5mjTMgfKTPsD`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_SW38bsSDpmcq4KKY`)

**FRONT**

What is compared with the significance level when a test is carried out without finding a critical region?


**BACK**

The **p-value**: the probability, assuming $\text{H}_{0}$ is true, of a value at least as extreme as the one observed.

Use $\text{P} ( X \geq \text{observed value} )$ when testing for an **increase**, and $\text{P} ( X \leq \text{observed value} )$ when testing for a **decrease**.

Reject $\text{H}_{0}$ if the p-value is **less than** the significance level.


Spec links: `spcpt_SXgS5mjTMgfKTPsD`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_zDg8nM4YQG8DJrHT`)

**FRONT**

In a two-tailed test, what is a one-tail probability compared with?


**BACK**

**Half** the significance level, because the level is shared between the two tails.

At 5% each tail holds 2.5%, and at 1% each tail holds 0.5%.

Comparing a one-tail probability with the full level would make rejection twice as easy as intended.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_sd7kvGnr3nvMKGGM`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A p-value of 0.0429 in a one-tailed test gives the same conclusion at the 1% level as at the 5% level.


**BACK**

**False.**

At 5% the p-value is below the significance level, so $\text{H}_{0}$ is **rejected**; at 1% it is above, so $\text{H}_{0}$ is **not rejected**.

A conclusion is only ever a conclusion **at the stated significance level**.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_dyVjhThNpSjDrjBC`)

**FRONT**

How should the conclusion of a hypothesis test be worded?


**BACK**

**In the context of the question**, reusing its own wording, and never definitively.

Rejecting $\text{H}_{0}$ gives **sufficient evidence to suggest** the alternative hypothesis at that significance level.

Not rejecting it gives **insufficient evidence** to suggest the alternative hypothesis.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_CDJMHvTBBJP9dgzr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A hypothesis test carried out correctly can still reach the wrong conclusion.


**BACK**

**True.**

The test weighs the **probability** of what one sample showed, so an unusual sample can point the wrong way.

This is why a conclusion is written as **evidence** rather than as proof.


Spec links: `spcpt_mPVJHc5CKHN5mQyS`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_nkM5ZGZWsxZFydVV`)

**FRONT**

What does a two-tailed test that rejects $\text{H}_{0}$ show, and what does it not show?


**BACK**

It shows there is evidence that the population parameter has **changed**.

It does not show **which direction** the change was in, so the conclusion must not say the parameter increased or decreased.

Only a **one-tailed** test provides evidence about direction.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_wggMZxNWhsrXNr6X`)

**FRONT**

How does the conclusion of a test tell you which error could have occurred?


**BACK**

If $\text{H}_{0}$ was **rejected**, only a **Type I** error is possible.

If $\text{H}_{0}$ was **not rejected**, only a **Type II** error is possible.

The conclusion settles which of the two is available before anything is known about the truth.


Spec links: `spcpt_KCFcWTMfnmj3W3yc`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_YJ3rhMVDCQ4VdN66`)

**FRONT**

What is the probability of a Type I error equal to?


**BACK**

The probability of **rejecting** $\text{H}_{0}$ given that $\text{H}_{0}$ is in fact **true**.

In a completed test this is the **actual significance level**, so it does not have to be worked out separately.


Spec links: `spcpt_KCFcWTMfnmj3W3yc`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_WyGFsZQdFCV2FBQ5`)

**FRONT**

What extra information is needed to find the probability of a Type II error?


**BACK**

The **true value** of the population parameter, which the question has to supply.

The probability is then that of the observed value falling **outside** the critical region, worked out using that true value in place of the one in $\text{H}_{0}$.


Spec links: `spcpt_KCFcWTMfnmj3W3yc`


## Card 17 — true_or_false (`fl_CzJJytk3N58sp7wf`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Lowering the significance level reduces the probability of both types of error.


**BACK**

**False.**

A lower significance level **shrinks** the critical region, so $\text{H}_{0}$ is rejected less often and a **Type I** error becomes less likely.

The same change makes $\text{H}_{0}$ harder to reject, so a **Type II** error becomes more likely.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_k2PfgjZzQhJXvKdj`)

**FRONT**

In which two situations does a hypothesis test make no error?


**BACK**

When $\text{H}_{0}$ is **true** and the test does not reject it, and when $\text{H}_{0}$ is **false** and the test rejects it.

Only the two mismatched pairings of reality and conclusion count as errors.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_FJX9xbt8F83WvctZ`)

**FRONT**

Why might two parties want the significance level set differently?


**BACK**

Because they are exposed to different errors.

Someone defending a claim wants a **Type I** error to be unlikely, so a true claim is not rejected.

Someone challenging it wants a **Type II** error to be unlikely, so a real change is not missed.
