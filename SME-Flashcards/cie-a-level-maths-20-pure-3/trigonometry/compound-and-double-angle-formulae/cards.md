## Card 1 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_QH9YKwd37xvz4QPc`)

**FRONT**

`\sin \left(A + B\right) \equiv \sin A \cos B + \_\_\_\_\_\_`

`\cos \left(A + B\right) \equiv \cos A \cos B - \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

`\sin\left(A + B\right) \equiv \sin A \cos B + \cos A \sin B`

`\cos\left(A + B\right) \equiv \cos A \cos B - \sin A \sin B`

For $\mathrm{sin}$, the sign on the right **matches** the one on the left.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_8J5RMbpBWZbv5gTr`)

**FRONT**

How do the signs work in the $\mathrm{tan}$ compound angle formula?


**BACK**

The sign on the left matches the one in the **numerator**, and is opposite to the one in the **denominator**.

So `\tan\left(A + B\right) \equiv \frac{\tan A + \tan B}{1 - \tan A \tan B}`.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_WdgYM29HSYxj75TV`)

**FRONT**

How is the $\mathrm{tan}$ compound angle formula derived?


**BACK**

Write `\tan\left(A + B\right)` as `\frac{\sin\left(A + B\right)}{\cos\left(A + B\right)}` and expand both parts.

Dividing the numerator and denominator by $\mathrm{cos} A \mathrm{cos} B$ then turns every term into a tangent.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_zsJPwYCpP4PFKvFh`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\cos\left(A - B\right) \equiv \cos A \cos B + \sin A \sin B`


**BACK**

**True.**

For cosine the sign on the right is **opposite** to the one on the left, so a minus outside gives a plus inside.

Sine and tangent behave the other way round, which is why the cosine pair is the one that catches people out.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_5x5gbVpxYWNpnNQF`)

**FRONT**

Why is `\sin \left(A + B\right)` not simply $\mathrm{sin} A + \mathrm{sin} B$?


**BACK**

Because sine is not additive: adding the angles does not add the values.

Arithmetic settles it at once. $\mathrm{sin} 90 ^{\circ} = 1$, but $\mathrm{sin} 45 ^{\circ} + \mathrm{sin} 45 ^{\circ} \approx 1 . 41$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_mJvr3JdCmyCfv84z`)

**FRONT**

How can a compound angle formula give the exact value of $\mathrm{sin} 75 \circ$?


**BACK**

Write $75 ^{\circ}$ as $45 ^{\circ} + 30 ^{\circ}$, both of which have exact values.

Expanding `\sin\left(45^{\circ} + 30^{\circ}\right)` then gives an exact answer in surds.

## Card 7 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_7JMGtX2c3RNwKPhf`)

**FRONT**

Complete the double angle formula for tangent:

`\tan 2 A \equiv \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{1 - \_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$\mathrm{tan} 2 A \equiv \frac{2\mathrm{tan}A}{1-\mathrm{tan}^{2}A}$

Note that it is **not** simply $2 \mathrm{tan} A$: doubling the angle does not double the tangent.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_mpKDdprW8xsYVwN7`)

**FRONT**

What are the three forms of $\mathrm{cos} 2 A$?


**BACK**

They are $\mathrm{cos}^{2} A - \mathrm{sin}^{2} A$, $2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} A - 1$ and $1 - 2 \mathrm{sin}^{2} A$.

The last two come from the first by using $\mathrm{sin}^{2} A + \mathrm{cos}^{2} A \equiv 1$ to remove one of the two squared terms.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_QpY7QVrgQdSY4Zcb`)

**FRONT**

How is every double angle formula obtained from a compound angle formula?


**BACK**

By setting $B = A$, so that $A + B$ becomes $2 A$.

Each of `\sin \left(A + A\right)`, `\cos \left(A + A\right)` and `\tan \left(A + A\right)` then collapses into the corresponding double angle result.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_jBCnzm86WfQcd6RS`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\mathrm{sin} 4 A \equiv 2 \mathrm{sin} 2 A \mathrm{cos} 2 A$


**BACK**

**True.**

The formula holds for **any** angle, so replacing $A$ by $2 A$ throughout is perfectly legitimate.

The same move gives $\mathrm{cos} 6 A$ from the $\mathrm{cos} 2 A$ formula with $A$ replaced by $3 A$.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_d7z8zQdnP6MmfFcF`)

**FRONT**

Which form of $\mathrm{cos} 2 A$ should you choose?


**BACK**

Choose whichever form is written in terms of the function the rest of the equation already uses.

An equation containing $\mathrm{sin} A$ elsewhere is easiest with the form carrying only $\mathrm{sin}^{2} A$, because everything is then in a single function.

What should you do on meeting $2 \mathrm{sin} A \mathrm{cos} A$ in an expression?

The double angle formulae are just as useful read from right to left as from left to right.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_bGy9yhmCFRtXM9g9`)

**FRONT**

What do you do with an equation containing both $\mathrm{sin} 2 θ$ and $\mathrm{sin} θ$?


**BACK**

Use a double angle formula on the $2 θ$ term, so that every term involves the **same angle**.

In $7 \mathrm{sin} 2 θ - 3 \mathrm{sin} θ = 0$ that gives $14 \mathrm{sin} θ \mathrm{cos} θ - 3 \mathrm{sin} θ = 0$, which then factorises.

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_rgPrjfZxhpb6Y3p9`)

**FRONT**

Define **harmonic form**.


**BACK**

Writing an expression such as $a \mathrm{sin} x + b \mathrm{cos} x$ as a single trigonometric function, `R \sin\left(x + \alpha\right)`.

It is the reverse of expanding with a compound angle formula, and can be thought of as factorising.

## Card 15 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_537y5MGNgkSBjybW`)

**FRONT**

For `a \sin x + b \cos x \equiv R \sin \left(x + \alpha\right)`:

`R = \_\_\_\_\_\_` and `\tan \alpha = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

$R = \sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$ and $\mathrm{tan} α = \frac{b}{a}$

$R$ is always taken positive, and $α$ between $0 ^{\circ}$ and $90 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_PcQhh6HjZVy7bMKZ`)

**FRONT**

How do you rewrite an expression in harmonic form?


**BACK**

Expand the target form with the appropriate **compound angle formula**, then **equate coefficients** of $\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$.

That gives $R \mathrm{cos} α = a$ and $R \mathrm{sin} α = b$, which between them fix both unknowns.


Spec links: `spcpt_hXbbmBmmgzKY7p5f`


## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_3MDh3tfVQPBHXDrT`)

**FRONT**

Why does dividing the two coefficient equations give you $\mathrm{tan} α$?


**BACK**

Because the $R$s cancel, leaving $\frac{\mathrm{sin}α}{\mathrm{cos}α} = \frac{b}{a}$.

That quotient is $\mathrm{tan} α$, so $α = \mathrm{tan}^{-1} \frac{b}{a}$.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_fDnn6c3FYmVSSF4T`)

**FRONT**

Why is harmonic form useful?


**BACK**

It turns two trigonometric terms into **one**, which can then be solved or analysed like any single function.

The maximum of `R \sin\left(x + \alpha\right)` is $R$ and its minimum is $- R$, which is immediate once it is written that way.

## Card 19 — true_or_false (`fl_M24jRtVVy2FZVbT2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

There is only one correct harmonic form for a given expression.


**BACK**

**False.**

The same expression can be written with $\mathrm{sin}$ or with $\mathrm{cos}$, and with $+ α$ or $- α$, giving four equivalent forms.

A question will say which of them it wants.
