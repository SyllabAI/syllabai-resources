## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_6dPVhxrbfYmX2wbV`)

**FRONT**

Define a **complex number**.


**BACK**

A **complex number** is a number of the form $z = a + b \text{i}$, where $a$ and $b$ are **real** numbers and $\text{i} = \sqrt{-1}$.

The form $a + b \text{i}$ is called the **Cartesian form** of $z$.

What is the value of $\sqrt{-9}$, and how do you find it?


**BACK**

$\sqrt{-9} = 3 \text{i}$.

Split the surd using $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b}$, which gives $\sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{-1}$, and then use $\text{i} = \sqrt{-1}$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_dsPDYPJ3ggRTsB7v`)

**FRONT**

How do you simplify a high power of $\text{i}$, such as $\text{i}^{23}$?


**BACK**

Write the power in terms of $\text{i}^{2}$, because $\text{i}^{2} = - 1$ is the only value you need.

For example `\text{i}^{23} = \left(\text{i}^{2}\right)^{11} \times \text{i} = \left(-1\right)^{11} \times \text{i} = -\text{i}`, since $- 1$ raised to an odd power is $- 1$.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_YbgYQVqm58xpcRW6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The imaginary part of $3 + 4 \text{i}$ is $4 \text{i}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The imaginary part is the real number $4$, without the $\text{i}$. In general, if $z = a + b \text{i}$ then `\text{Re}\left(z\right) = a` and `\text{Im}\left(z\right) = b`, so both parts of a complex number are themselves real.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_xk3Hpb2qBndPZ5WY`)

**FRONT**

When are two complex numbers equal?


**BACK**

Two complex numbers are equal **if and only if** their real parts are equal **and** their imaginary parts are equal.

So $3 + 2 \text{i}$ and $3 + 3 \text{i}$ are not equal, even though their real parts match.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_WhGhkpXTTfpMsTSX`)

**FRONT**

How do you add or subtract two complex numbers?


**BACK**

Combine the real parts and the imaginary parts **separately**, exactly as you would collect like terms in algebra.

`\left(a + b\text{i}\right) + \left(c + d\text{i}\right) = \left(a + c\right) + \left(b + d\right)\text{i}`

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_DkYPtjb6FhZNM8qM`)

**FRONT**

How do you multiply two complex numbers written in the form $a + b \text{i}$?


**BACK**

Expand the brackets as you would for any two algebraic expressions, then replace $\text{i}^{2}$ by $- 1$ and collect the real and imaginary parts.

For example `\left(3 + 4\text{i}\right)\left(6 + 7\text{i}\right) = 18 + 45\text{i} + 28\text{i}^{2} = -10 + 45\text{i}`.


Spec links: `spcpt_bZGdc2Hcy257xTX7`


## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_zY5gpcjFwc8p3fxv`)

**FRONT**

Define the **complex conjugate** of a complex number.


**BACK**

The **complex conjugate** of $z = a + b \text{i}$ is written $z^{\ast}$, and $z^{\ast} = a - b \text{i}$.

Only the sign of the imaginary part changes.

**FRONT**

For a complex number $z = a + b \text{i}$ and its conjugate, complete these two results.

`z + z^{*} = \_\_\_\_\_\_, z z^{*} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed results are:

$z + z^{\ast} = 2 a , z z^{\ast} = a^{2} + b^{2}$

Both are **real** numbers, and it is the second one that makes the conjugate useful for division.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_wdPMBdCCwxmJkjcG`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

For any complex number $z$, the value of $z - z^{\ast}$ is real.


**BACK**

**False.**

Subtracting cancels the real parts and doubles the imaginary part, giving `\left(a + b\text{i}\right) - \left(a - b\text{i}\right) = 2b\text{i}`, which is **imaginary**. The one exception is $b = 0$, where the result is $0$.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_nHqtkHyPXzSqcMcr`)

**FRONT**

How do you divide one complex number by another?


**BACK**

Write the division as a fraction, then multiply the numerator and the denominator by the **conjugate of the denominator**.

That makes the denominator a real number, so the quotient can then be written in the form $a + b \text{i}$.


Spec links: `spcpt_mChcsKgYY5syK6MZ`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_m7JmsNKQpmCdgs8G`)

**FRONT**

Why does multiplying by the conjugate of the denominator leave the value of the fraction unchanged?


**BACK**

Because the numerator and the denominator are multiplied by the **same** number, which is the same as multiplying the whole fraction by $1$.

For instance $\frac{3+\text{i}}{3+\text{i}} = 1$, so only the form of the fraction changes, not its value.


Spec links: `spcpt_mChcsKgYY5syK6MZ`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Tf2Bft7hY3jw5prm`)

**FRONT**

A division has been simplified to $\frac{-4+22\text{i}}{10}$. How do you finish it?


**BACK**

Split the fraction into two terms, one for each part, and simplify each one separately.

$\frac{-4+22\text{i}}{10} = - \frac{2}{5} + \frac{11}{5} \text{i}$

An answer left over a single denominator is not yet in the form $a + b \text{i}$.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_9vKDySfQC3x9crqp`)

**FRONT**

How do you begin finding the square roots of $5 + 12 \text{i}$?


**BACK**

Assume the square root is itself complex, so write it as $c + d \text{i}$ with $c$ and $d$ **real**.

Squaring that expression gives a second description of $5 + 12 \text{i}$, and the two can then be set equal.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_8X9yCKgjSWGQrWRh`)

**FRONT**

When finding the square roots of a complex number, the working leads to a quartic. How do you solve it?


**BACK**

Treat it as a quadratic in $d^{2}$, then factorise.

For $d^{4} + 5 d^{2} - 36 = 0$ this gives `\left(d^{2} + 9\right)\left(d^{2} - 4\right) = 0`, and each bracket is then solved for $d$.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_N93JfvGSDkjQz7pz`)

**FRONT**

How are the two square roots of a complex number related?


**BACK**

Each one is the **negative** of the other.

If $c + d \text{i}$ is a square root then so is $- c - d \text{i}$, which is why finding one pair of values for $c$ and $d$ delivers both roots at once.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_TwVK8DqjfSddktvH`)

**FRONT**

What tells you that a quadratic with real coefficients has complex roots?


**BACK**

A **negative discriminant**.

Solving then requires the square root of a negative number, which has no real value, so both roots are complex. On a graph, the curve does not cross the horizontal axis.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZtDWHZM9xF3c5hGJ`)

**FRONT**

One root of a quadratic with real coefficients is $2 + 5 \text{i}$. How do you find the quadratic?


**BACK**

The conjugate $2 - 5 \text{i}$ is the other root, so the quadratic is `\left[z - \left(2 + 5\text{i}\right)\right]\left[z - \left(2 - 5\text{i}\right)\right]`.

Grouping it as `\left[\left(z - 2\right) - 5\text{i}\right]\left[\left(z - 2\right) + 5\text{i}\right]` makes it a difference of two squares, which expands much faster.

How many roots does a polynomial equation of degree $n$ have?


**BACK**

Exactly $n$, provided repeated roots are counted separately.

Some may be real and some non-real, but the total is always $n$.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_qTMBDvGQqrQHSBJy`)

**FRONT**

What combinations of real and non-real roots can a cubic or a quartic with real coefficients have?


**BACK**

A **cubic** has either three real roots, or one real root together with a conjugate pair.

A **quartic** has four real roots, two real roots with one conjugate pair, or two conjugate pairs.

Non-real roots always arrive in pairs, so the number of them is even.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_yYV5WQjbX55RPv9F`)

**FRONT**

A cubic with real coefficients has one complex root. How do you find the remaining roots?


**BACK**

Write down the conjugate as a second root, and multiply the two corresponding factors together to get a real quadratic factor.

Then equate the cubic to that quadratic multiplied by `\left(z - \alpha\right)`, and either compare coefficients or use polynomial division to find $α$.

**FRONT**

What extra step does a quartic need, compared with a cubic, once the real quadratic factor is known?


**BACK**

Dividing a quartic by that factor leaves another **quadratic**, rather than a linear factor.

That quadratic then has to be solved, and its two roots may be real or may be a second conjugate pair.

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_KN2tdK82zTjJ4d7x`)

**FRONT**

A cubic with unknown real coefficients has a given complex root. How do you find the coefficients?


**BACK**

Substitute the root into the equation and expand, then group the real terms and the imaginary terms separately.

Setting each group equal to zero gives a pair of simultaneous equations to solve for the unknowns.
