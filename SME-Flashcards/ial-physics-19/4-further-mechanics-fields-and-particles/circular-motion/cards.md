## Card 18 — true_or_false (`fl_bNXNtFhKXDrp5VSz`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Because its speed is constant, an object in uniform circular motion has zero acceleration.


**BACK**

**False.**

Speed being constant does not mean **velocity** is constant — the object's direction is continuously changing, so it has a centripetal acceleration directed towards the centre.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_RKxjdqKTtqbxMdSq`)

**FRONT**

Define **centripetal force**.


**BACK**

The resultant force towards the centre of the circle required to keep a body in uniform circular motion. It is always directed towards the centre of the body's rotation.


Spec links: `spcpt_WDZmNvMdkkYkFncm`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_6RZSYBGvXd8b3vBb`)

**FRONT**

Why does an object moving in a circle require a resultant force to act on it?


**BACK**

Because it is continuously changing **direction**, so it is not in equilibrium, even if its speed is constant.


Spec links: `spcpt_WDZmNvMdkkYkFncm`


## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_B9tMDwQZjdSGT6ng`)

**FRONT**

In which direction do centripetal force and centripetal acceleration act, relative to each other?


**BACK**

In the **same direction**, towards the centre of the circle, following Newton's second law $F = m a$


Spec links: `spcpt_WDZmNvMdkkYkFncm`


## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_FRV5XKTxpnVNzXR8`)

**FRONT**

Is centripetal force a distinct type of force?


**BACK**

No. Centripetal force is **not** a separate force in its own right — it is the name given to whichever resultant force (for example tension, gravity or magnetic force) keeps a body moving on a circular path.

Centripetal force is an additional force that acts alongside gravity, tension or other forces on an object moving in a circle.


**BACK**

Centripetal force is not an extra force added to the others — it is simply the name for the resultant of the existing forces (for example tension and gravity) that points towards the centre.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_sQjGJSpshpyCGsH4`)

**FRONT**

In vertical circular motion, why does the tension in the string vary continuously as the object moves around the circle?


**BACK**

The **direction** of the tension changes as the object moves, while the direction of the **weight** stays constant, so the resultant (centripetal) force needed varies with position.
