## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_KfBxsMskcPXYKbxN`)

**FRONT**

A point source emits a **spherical wave**. Explain, in terms of area, why the intensity of the wave decreases with distance from the source.


**BACK**

As the wave travels outward, its energy spreads over the **surface area of a sphere**, $4 π r^{2}$. Since this area increases with distance, the same power is spread over a **larger area**, so intensity decreases.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_3vCVxdFwWJ2dG3K7`)

**FRONT**

Define **refraction**.


**BACK**

**Refraction** is the change in **direction** and **speed** of light as it passes across a boundary between two different transparent media.


Spec links: `spcpt_kVM7GTyj4VVChjt5`


## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_YDYKJzRFtfVGZT6h`)

**FRONT**

Define **refractive index**, *n*.


**BACK**

The **refractive index** of a material measures how much light slows down when passing through it:

$n = \frac{c}{v}$

where *c* is the speed of light in a vacuum and *v* is the speed of light in the substance.


Spec links: `spcpt_kVM7GTyj4VVChjt5`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_jbfzRyNr22wT3f5y`)

**FRONT**

State Snell's law.


**BACK**

$n_{1} \mathrm{sin} θ_{1} = n_{2} \mathrm{sin} θ_{2}$

where *n*<sub>1</sub> and *n*<sub>2</sub> are the refractive indices of the first and second material, and *θ*<sub>1</sub> and *θ*<sub>2</sub> are the angles of incidence and refraction, both measured from the **normal**.


Spec links: `spcpt_kVM7GTyj4VVChjt5`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_RNTdQQNHzxpZ55Mc`)

**FRONT**

A light ray travelling through air enters a glass block. Describe what happens to its speed and direction.


**BACK**

The light ray **slows down**, since glass is **more optically dense** than air, and it bends **towards the normal**.

## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_dDds8Z5GBqSpnD9W`)

**FRONT**

Define the **critical angle**, *C*.


**BACK**

The **critical angle** is the angle of incidence, in the **more dense** medium, at which the angle of refraction is exactly **90°**, so the refracted ray travels along the boundary.


Spec links: `spcpt_X9gXpWYcjyyZ79gp`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_JcNwTK2J9TS5XCgW`)

**FRONT**

Derive an expression for the critical angle, *C*, at a boundary between a medium of refractive index *n* and air.


**BACK**

From Snell's law with *θ*<sub>1</sub> = *C*, *θ*<sub>2</sub> = 90°, *n*<sub>1</sub> = *n*, *n*<sub>2</sub> = 1:

$n \mathrm{sin} C = \mathrm{sin} 90 ^{\circ}$

`C = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)`

## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_ZcrfM77sktyTjRjw`)

**FRONT**

Define **total internal reflection** (TIR).


**BACK**

**Total internal reflection** occurs when the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle, and the incident refractive index *n*<sub>1</sub> is greater than the refractive index of the material at the boundary *n*<sub>2</sub>, so **all** of the light is reflected back into the first medium.

**BACK**

- The light must be travelling from a **more dense** medium into a **less dense** medium
- The **angle of incidence** must be **greater** than the **critical angle**

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_9hGc9322qHS7g9Rc`)

**FRONT**

Why can only transverse waves be polarised, not longitudinal waves?


**BACK**

**Transverse** waves oscillate in any plane perpendicular to the direction of travel, so they can be restricted to one plane. **Longitudinal** waves oscillate parallel to the direction of travel, so there is no perpendicular plane to restrict.
