## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_HhYwHrgdQpmmFYr3`)

**FRONT**

Define **simple harmonic motion (SHM)**.


**BACK**

An oscillation in which the **acceleration** is directly **proportional** to the displacement from equilibrium, and always acts in the **opposite direction** to the displacement.


Spec links: `spcpt_f43vWM56Th59jXp8`


## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_kHkx49RHztsHTpYF`)

**FRONT**

Define **restoring force**.


**BACK**

A force that always acts to return an oscillator to its **equilibrium position**, given by $F = - k x$


Spec links: `spcpt_f43vWM56Th59jXp8`


## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_m9GqNzr4HV4db48G`)

**FRONT**

State the two conditions for an oscillation to be classed as simple harmonic motion.


**BACK**

- The acceleration is **proportional** to the displacement
- The acceleration is in the **opposite direction** to the displacement

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_jBSNjXhC9fvRgsc9`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A person bouncing on a trampoline is performing simple harmonic motion.


**BACK**

**False.**

While airborne, the restoring force on the person is equal to their **weight**, which is **constant** rather than proportional to their displacement from the equilibrium position, so the motion is not SHM.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_QG7tVWMQ34nfJ2zM`)

**FRONT**

Define **angular frequency** (⍵), and state how it relates to the time period.


**BACK**

The rate of change of angular displacement, measured in rad s<sup>-1</sup>. It is related to the time period *T* by $ω = \frac{2π}{T}$

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_D7H6psFVzJKcyBVJ`)

**FRONT**

State the SHM acceleration equation and explain what the negative sign shows about the direction of acceleration.


**BACK**

$a = - ω^{2} x$

The negative sign shows that acceleration is always directed **opposite** to the displacement, i.e. towards the equilibrium position.


Spec links: `spcpt_SGPWyrGnsPfnXmyX`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_Rnwdznm3FcBbXqqk`)

**FRONT**

Which SHM displacement equation, *x* = *A*cos(⍵*t*) or *x* = *A*sin(⍵*t*), applies when an oscillator is released from its amplitude position, and which applies when it starts at the equilibrium position?


**BACK**

- **Released from amplitude** (*x* = *A* at *t* = 0): $x = A \mathrm{cos} ( ω t )$
- **Starts at equilibrium** (*x* = 0 at *t* = 0): $x = A \mathrm{sin} ( ω t )$


Spec links: `spcpt_SGPWyrGnsPfnXmyX`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_TXzZ9XNQbTqYmtDh`)

**FRONT**

State the equation relating the speed of an SHM oscillator to its angular frequency, amplitude and displacement.


**BACK**

$v = \pm ω \sqrt{A^{2}-x^{2}}$


Spec links: `spcpt_SGPWyrGnsPfnXmyX`


## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_x7Bh28SnVDXbjxrr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An oscillator undergoing SHM has its greatest speed at its amplitude position.


**BACK**

**False.**

The greatest speed occurs at the **equilibrium position** (*x* = 0); using $v = \pm ω \sqrt{A^{2}-x^{2}}$, speed is zero when *x* = *A*.
