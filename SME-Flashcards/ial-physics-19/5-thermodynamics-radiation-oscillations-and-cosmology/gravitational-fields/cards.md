## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_Kzhp6RTZ5HPWKN96`)

**FRONT**

Define **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**.


**BACK**

The gravitational force *F*<sub>G</sub> between two masses *m*<sub>1</sub> and *m*<sub>2</sub> is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation *r*:

$F_{G} = \frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{r^{2}}$


Spec links: `spcpt_5Ch2bCxjwxVGnVbq`

**BACK**

The **inverse square law** describes a $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ relationship between the gravitational force and the separation *r* of two masses.


Spec links: `spcpt_5Ch2bCxjwxVGnVbq`

## Card 25 — keyword_definition (`fl_qP5nsf9k7MVmKKH5`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

**Gravitational potential** is the work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to a defined point in the field, measured in **J kg**<sup>**–1**</sup>.


Spec links: `spcpt_dbsK87zkbpW5jrgC`


## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_Tm7g2pzNzhXSCrH8`)

**FRONT**

Why is gravitational potential always negative?


**BACK**

Gravitational potential is defined as **zero at infinity**, and work must be done **against** gravity to move a mass away from another mass, so the potential at every finite point is **negative**.


Spec links: `spcpt_dbsK87zkbpW5jrgC`

## Card 37 — keyword_definition (`fl_2xxmjBb5q2BvJyRZ`)

**FRONT**

Define **centripetal force** in the context of orbital motion.


**BACK**

In orbital motion, the **centripetal force** is the resultant force acting on a satellite or planet, directed towards the centre of the mass it orbits and **perpendicular** to its velocity. This force is provided by the **gravitational force** between the two masses.

The acceleration of a body moving in a circular orbit, caused by the continuous change in the **direction** of its velocity, directed towards the **centre** of the orbit.
