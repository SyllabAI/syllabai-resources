## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_dHhyczR9Y4cqTjHf`)

**FRONT**

A circle has centre `\left(2 , - 5\right)` and radius $3$. What is its equation?


**BACK**

`\left(x - 2\right)^{2} + \left(y + 5\right)^{2} = 9`.

The $- 5$ turns into $+ 5$ inside the bracket, because subtracting a negative number adds it.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_4YjpbTqBQFp65Tk6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The equation $x^{2} + y^{2} = 16$ describes a circle.


**BACK**

**True.**

It is the case where the centre is the **origin**, so $a$ and $b$ are both zero and each bracket collapses to a single letter.

A circle centred anywhere else keeps its brackets.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_kp2CmSQXsbwXJMqF`)

**FRONT**

A circle has centre `\left(- 5 , - 7\right)` and passes through the point `\left(1 , 1\right)`. What has to be worked out before its equation can be written down?


**BACK**

The **radius**, which is the distance from the centre to that point.

Here the steps across and up are $6$ and $8$, so $r = \sqrt{6^{2}+8^{2}} = 10$ and the equation is `\left(x + 5\right)^{2} + \left(y + 7\right)^{2} = 100`.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_nC2sWt8VByhx6SFp`)

**FRONT**

Must the radius of a circle be a whole number?


**BACK**

No. Square-rooting the right-hand side of the equation often leaves a **surd**.

For `\left(x - 1\right)^{2} + \left(y + 6\right)^{2} = 20` the radius is $\sqrt{20}$, which simplifies to $2 \sqrt{5}$.


Spec links: `spcpt_pYqQwJWmThvrtN5x`


## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_RHkfCQws9TdQWfgH`)

**FRONT**

Why does the equation of a circle have two squared brackets added together?


**BACK**

Because it is **Pythagoras' theorem** in disguise: `\left(x - a\right)` and `\left(y - b\right)` are the steps across and up from the centre to a point on the circle.

Those two steps are the short sides of a right-angled triangle whose hypotenuse is the radius, so their squares add to $r^{2}$.


Spec links: `spcpt_pYqQwJWmThvrtN5x`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_hGWBjD2PMkxm6Z6t`)

**FRONT**

What do you do when a circle equation is not in the form `\left(x - a\right)^{2} + \left(y - b\right)^{2} = r^{2}`?


**BACK**

Rearrange it into that form, usually by **completing the square**. That is the form the centre and radius can be read off from.

**BACK**

**FRONT**

What does the angle in a semicircle property say?


**BACK**

Any angle at the circumference in a **semicircle** is a **right angle**.

Equivalently, if a triangle is right-angled, its **hypotenuse is a diameter** of its circumcircle.

**BACK**

Two ways, depending on what you are given.
