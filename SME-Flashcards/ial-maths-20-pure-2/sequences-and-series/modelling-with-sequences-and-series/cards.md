## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_DQ9XJ6Fq2G6ywZtY`)

**FRONT**

When should a situation be modelled with an arithmetic sequence rather than a geometric one?


**BACK**

When a fixed **amount** is repeatedly added or subtracted.

A fixed **percentage**, or repeated multiplication by a fixed number, calls for a geometric model instead.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_Sj4hG6YdS428kkdv`)

**FRONT**

A salary rising by $\mathrm{\pounds} 1500$ each year is `\_\_\_\_\_\_`; a salary rising by `3 \%` each year is `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

A salary rising by $\mathrm{\pounds} 1500$ each year is **arithmetic**; a salary rising by `3 \%` each year is **geometric**.

A percentage rise multiplies the previous salary, so the increase itself grows each year.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_M5SMBgGCF9gKmmpV`)

**FRONT**

In a savings problem, what is the difference between using the sequence and using the series?


**BACK**

The **sequence** gives the amount at a single point, such as the payment made in year $8$.

The **series** gives the running total, such as everything paid in over the first $8$ years.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_cMQD8DFvyHhB43Mg`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Compound interest is modelled by a geometric sequence.


**BACK**

**True.**

Each year the amount is multiplied by the same factor, such as $1 . 03$ for `3 \%` interest.

The balance after $n$ years is therefore a term of a geometric sequence.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_HqGzk2ZnnhRdC4qn`)

**FRONT**

What feature of a real situation tells you a sequence or series model will fit?


**BACK**

A quantity changing by the **same amount, or the same proportion, at regular intervals**.

Savings, salaries, commission and repeated depreciation all have exactly that shape.
