## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_5qBs3y5gdtmCT6dD`)

**FRONT**

Define **common ratio**.


**BACK**

The **common ratio** $r$ is the fixed number each term of a geometric sequence is multiplied by to get the next one.

It is found by dividing any term by the term before it.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_GcqpHbqVCMD7sxxS`)

**FRONT**

The $n$th term of a geometric sequence is:

`u_{n} = a r^{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

$u_{n} = a r^{n-1}$

The power is $n - 1$ rather than $n$ because the first term has been multiplied by $r$ **no** times yet.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_zjvp8r6bnP8vsFbD`)

**FRONT**

You know the 3rd and 7th terms of a geometric sequence. How do you find $a$ and $r$?


**BACK**

Write both using $u_{n} = a r^{n-1}$, then **divide** one equation by the other.

The $a$ cancels, leaving a single equation in $r$; substituting back then gives $a$.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_yDwNbrZ58ksH4SHX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The terms of a geometric sequence always get bigger.


**BACK**

**False.**

If `\left|r\right| < 1` the terms shrink towards zero, and a **negative** $r$ makes them alternate in sign.

Only $r > 1$ gives a sequence that grows steadily.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_jMTnP7fkGDs7CfBW`)

**FRONT**

How do you check whether a sequence is geometric?


**BACK**

Divide each term by the one before it and see whether you always get the **same** number.

If that ratio changes anywhere, the sequence is not geometric.

## Card 6 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_RtZzk64DGffFymNp`)

**FRONT**

The sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric series is:

`S_{n} = \frac{a \left(1 - r^{n}\right)}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

`S_{n} = \frac{a \left(1 - r^{n}\right)}{1 - r}`

The equivalent form `\frac{a \left(r^{n} - 1\right)}{r - 1}` is more convenient when $r > 1$, because it keeps everything positive.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_6qMcWsgvy77Bj3f9`)

**FRONT**

How is the formula for a geometric series proved?


**BACK**

Write the sum out, write $r$ times the sum underneath it, and **subtract**.

All but two terms cancel, leaving $S_{n} - r S_{n} = a - a r^{n}$, which factorises and rearranges into the formula.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_SgPZPHTZMdq35Nkm`)

**FRONT**

In the proof of the geometric series formula, why do you multiply by $r$ before subtracting?


**BACK**

Because multiplying by $r$ shifts every term along by one place, so nearly all of them line up with a copy of themselves.

Subtracting then wipes out everything except the first term and the last new one.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_Mj6PfHDyxx65wfRs`)

**FRONT**

What is the sum to infinity of a geometric series, and when does it exist?


**BACK**

$S_{\infty} = \frac{a}{1-r}$, and it exists **only** when `\left|r\right| < 1`.

Otherwise the series is **divergent** and has no sum to infinity at all.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_JZZGXcyfGcCwpRcy`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A geometric series with $r = - 0 . 5$ has a sum to infinity.


**BACK**

**True.**

The condition is on the **modulus** of $r$, and `\left|- 0 . 5\right| = 0 . 5`, which is less than $1$.

The terms alternate in sign but still shrink towards zero, so the total settles on a finite value.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_7R42pDWbhVw8TVk8`)

**FRONT**

Why might logarithms be needed in a geometric series question?


**BACK**

Because $n$ appears as a **power**, in $r^{n}$.

Asking how many terms are needed to reach a given total therefore means solving an exponential equation.
