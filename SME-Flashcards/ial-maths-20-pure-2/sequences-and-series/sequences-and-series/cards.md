## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_Fc4DN4Pbdt6tQCs4`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a **sequence** and a **series**?


**BACK**

A **sequence** is an ordered list of numbers with a rule for generating them.

A **series** is what you get by **adding up** the terms of a sequence.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_7h9NGzSSqttmtzXr`)

**FRONT**

What makes a sequence **increasing** or **decreasing**?


**BACK**

Increasing means $u_{n+1} > u_{n}$ for every positive integer $n$, so each term is larger than the one before it.

Decreasing means $u_{n+1} < u_{n}$ for every positive integer $n$.

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_tyqbsC9wYWZGtbh2`)

**FRONT**

Define **periodic sequence**.


**BACK**

A **periodic** sequence is one whose terms repeat in a cycle.

The number of terms in each repeating cycle is called its **order**, or period.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_Z4gmHZBHBfyFBDcb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A sequence that rises for its first ten terms is an increasing sequence.


**BACK**

**False.**

The definition demands that each term beat the one before it for **every** positive integer $n$, not just for the terms you happen to have checked.

A sequence can climb for a while and then turn.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_gwbtw52kRXgyvnSG`)

**FRONT**

Where might a periodic sequence be hiding?


**BACK**

In a sequence defined by a **trigonometric** function.

Because sine and cosine repeat, a sequence built from them cycles through a small set of values.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_NvqSggBnjtJfYbkK`)

**FRONT**

What does `\sum_{r = 1}^{n} u_{r}` tell you to do?


**BACK**

Add up the terms $u_{r}$, taking $r$ from $1$ up to $n$.

The expression to the right of the $Σ$ says what is being summed, and the numbers above and below say where to start and where to stop.

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_sfHxxJcVJG7T43PJ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The lower limit of a sigma expression must be $1$.


**BACK**

**False.**

It can be any integer, as in `\sum_{r = 0}^{4}\left(2 r + 1\right)` or `\sum_{r = 7}^{11}\left(2 r - 13\right)`.

Always read the lower limit before working out how many terms there are.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_HCKgrYtMYMbZpbQH`)

**FRONT**

How many terms are there in `\sum_{r = 7}^{11} \left(2 r - 13\right)`?


**BACK**

**Five**, since $r$ takes the values $7$, $8$, $9$, $10$ and $11$.

The count is the upper limit minus the lower limit **plus one**, and it is that extra one that gets forgotten.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_TvMyC3hGz8DyHvH2`)

**FRONT**

When can two sigma expressions be combined into a single one?


**BACK**

When the expression being summed is the **same** in both and the limits join up end to end.

So `\sum_{r = 1}^{6}\left(4 r + 7\right) + \sum_{r = 7}^{11}\left(4 r + 7\right) = \sum_{r = 1}^{11}\left(4 r + 7\right)`.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_v9VfNPfxycjSpgBM`)

**FRONT**

What should you watch for when a sigma expression contains more than one letter?


**BACK**

Only the letter written under the $Σ$ changes from term to term.

Every other letter stays fixed throughout the sum and behaves as a constant.

## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_tg7RFYF9SxK6qs9G`)

**FRONT**

Define **recurrence relation**.


**BACK**

A **recurrence relation** gives each term of a sequence as a function of the **previous** term, in the form `u_{n + 1} = \text{f}\left(u_{n}\right)`.

It describes how the sequence continues rather than giving any term directly.


Spec links: `spcpt_fKfGVXDRSZvMFXmj`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_VJR5nx2T3gfRZ6nz`)

**FRONT**

Why is a recurrence relation not enough to define a sequence on its own?


**BACK**

Because it only tells you how to get from one term to the next.

Without a stated **first term** there is nothing to start from, so the same relation could generate infinitely many different sequences.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_mGq2XBqXBv9Rwsyg`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Every sequence defined by a recurrence relation is either arithmetic or geometric.


**BACK**

**False.**

A relation such as $u_{n+1} = u_{n}^{2} - 1$ is neither, since the terms are neither added to nor multiplied by a fixed amount.

Recurrence relations reach far beyond those two families.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_NbWNW2MBhqCZwB2C`)

**FRONT**

How do you sum a sequence that is given by a recurrence relation?


**BACK**

If the relation is arithmetic or geometric, use the matching **series formula**.

For any other sequence there is no general formula, so the terms are added directly or a trick has to be found that fits that particular sequence.
