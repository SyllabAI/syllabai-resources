## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_yPjBzgpD7J5zPD9V`)

**FRONT**

Define **trigonometric identity**.


**BACK**

A **trigonometric identity** is a statement that is true for **every** value of the angle, not just for particular ones.

The sign $\equiv$ is used in place of $=$ to say that the two sides are identical.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_CmHCgNCnh6DmbdsY`)

**FRONT**

Where does the identity $\mathrm{tan} θ \equiv \frac{\mathrm{sin}θ}{\mathrm{cos}θ}$ come from?


**BACK**

From dividing $\mathrm{sin} θ$ by $\mathrm{cos} θ$ in a right-angled triangle.

That gives $\frac{\text{opposite}\div\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{adjacent}\div\text{hypotenuse}}$, and the hypotenuse cancels to leave $\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$, which is $\mathrm{tan} θ$.

$\mathrm{tan} θ$ has no value at all when $\mathrm{cos} θ = 0$.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_n2cHZbGC66VctVXK`)

**FRONT**

Which theorem is $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ + \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ \equiv 1$ built from?


**BACK**

It comes from **Pythagoras' theorem**.

Dividing $a^{2} + b^{2} = c^{2}$ through by $c^{2}$ gives `\left(\frac{a}{c}\right)^{2} + \left(\frac{b}{c}\right)^{2} = 1`, and in a right-angled triangle those two fractions are $\mathrm{sin} θ$ and $\mathrm{cos} θ$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_2jmr3dnPh5YtvFs4`)

**FRONT**

You have to show that one trigonometric expression is identical to another. How do you decide what to substitute?


**BACK**

Compare the two sides and look at what has **disappeared**.

If $\mathrm{tan} x$ appears on one side and not on the other, it must have been replaced using an identity, so that is the substitution to make.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_F5f2pRRBtWPWVVyw`)

**FRONT**

Define the **CAST diagram**.


**BACK**

A diagram of the four quadrants showing which trigonometric functions are **positive** in each.

Going anticlockwise from $0 ^{\circ}$: **A**ll in the first quadrant, **S**ine in the second, **T**angent in the third, **C**osine in the fourth.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_QBQrbZjWFHHTJZmv`)

**FRONT**

Once you have the principal value of a trigonometric equation, how does the CAST diagram give you the other solutions?


**BACK**

Draw your angle from the **horizontal** in its own quadrant, then draw the same angle from the horizontal in all four quadrants.

Read off the angles in the quadrants where your function has the right sign. That gives every solution between $0 ^{\circ}$ and $360 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_FYcQWRP4Sqw6Fvrx`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the extra solutions to a trigonometric equation when the interval you are given is wider than one revolution?


**BACK**

Add and subtract $360 ^{\circ}$ from each solution you already have, keeping any that land inside the interval.

For $\mathrm{cos} x = \frac{1}{2}$ with $-360^{\circ}\leqx\leq720^{\circ}$, the solutions $60 ^{\circ}$ and $300 ^{\circ}$ give $- 300 ^{\circ}$, $- 60 ^{\circ}$, $60 ^{\circ}$, $300 ^{\circ}$, $420 ^{\circ}$ and $660 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_HMSnzXH7RtWnNhx6`)

**FRONT**

How do you solve an equation like $\mathrm{cos} ( θ - 30 ^{\circ} ) = 0 . 5$?


**BACK**

**Transform the interval first.** Put $Z = θ - 30 ^{\circ}$ and subtract $30 ^{\circ}$ from each end, so $0 ^{\circ} \leq θ \leq 360 ^{\circ}$ becomes $- 30 ^{\circ} \leq Z \leq 330 ^{\circ}$.

Solve $\mathrm{cos} Z = 0 . 5$ in that interval, then add $30 ^{\circ}$ back to every solution.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_3v4yTHsK2HBkdmrw`)

**FRONT**

How is solving $\mathrm{sin} 2 x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ different from solving an equation like $\mathrm{cos} ( θ - 30 ^{\circ} ) = 0 . 5$?


**BACK**

The interval is **multiplied** rather than shifted: $0 ^{\circ} \leq x \leq 360 ^{\circ}$ becomes $0 ^{\circ} \leq 2 x \leq 720 ^{\circ}$.

Solve for $2 x$ across the wider interval, then **divide** every solution by 2 instead of subtracting a shift.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_X9F6yx4M59McYxmC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If you solve $\mathrm{sin} 2 x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ by finding the values of $2 x$, those values are the solutions of the equation.


**BACK**

**False.**

They are solutions for the **transformed** variable, not for $x$ or $θ$.

Every one has to be converted back: add or subtract to undo the shift, or divide by the multiplier.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_xmYqZcNbSVw9j57F`)

**FRONT**

What makes a trigonometric equation **quadratic**, and what do you do first?


**BACK**

It contains $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ$, $\mathrm{cos}^{2} θ$ or $\mathrm{tan}^{2} x$.

If a plain trigonometric function appears as well, use an identity so that **everything is in terms of one** function, then rearrange so the equation equals zero.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_sD9wdXg98mVDsFwh`)

**FRONT**

When solving a quadratic trigonometric equation, how does replacing the trigonometric function with a single letter help?


**BACK**

It turns the equation into an ordinary quadratic you can factorise on sight.

$2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ - 3 \mathrm{cos} θ - 2 = 0$ becomes $2 C^{2} - 3 C - 2 = 0$, which factorises as $( 2 C + 1 ) ( C - 2 ) = 0$. Put $\mathrm{cos} θ$ back afterwards.

## Card 17 — true_or_false (`fl_N6pBmHmRXYGf4NYM`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A quadratic trigonometric equation always gives two sets of solutions.


**BACK**

**False.**

It gives two values for the trigonometric function, but one of them may be impossible.

$( 2 \mathrm{cos} θ + 1 ) ( \mathrm{cos} θ - 2 ) = 0$ gives $\mathrm{cos} θ = - \frac{1}{2}$ or $\mathrm{cos} θ = 2$, and $\mathrm{cos} θ = 2$ has no solutions at all.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_cmXDmn43cT7fXWJ7`)

**FRONT**

Which values of $k$ make $\mathrm{sin} x = k$ and $\mathrm{cos} x = k$ solvable, and how is $\mathrm{tan} x = k$ different?


**BACK**

$\mathrm{sin} x = k$ and $\mathrm{cos} x = k$ only have solutions when $- 1 \leq k \leq 1$.

$\mathrm{tan} x = k$ has solutions for **every** value of $k$.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_DtNhDTCy8p8kZRtK`)

**FRONT**

Why should you factorise $\mathrm{tan}^{2} x = 2 \mathrm{tan} x$ rather than divide through by $\mathrm{tan} x$?


**BACK**

Dividing by a trigonometric function **loses solutions**.

Dividing gives only $\mathrm{tan} x = 2$. Factorising to $\mathrm{tan} x ( \mathrm{tan} x - 2 ) = 0$ keeps $\mathrm{tan} x = 0$ as well, and both are needed.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_7fTCjrB277MJZWGn`)

**FRONT**

In what order should you deal with an unfamiliar trigonometric equation?


**BACK**

Handle any **function of the angle** first, transforming the interval. Then use an identity to get everything into **one** trigonometric function; finally decide whether what remains is **linear or quadratic**.

Only once the equation reads $\mathrm{sin} x = k$, $\mathrm{cos} x = k$ or $\mathrm{tan} x = k$ is the principal value any use.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

There is one correct method for solving a trigonometric equation.


**BACK**

**False.**

Sketching a graph, using the CAST diagram, applying an identity and factorising a quadratic are all legitimate, and most equations can be done more than one way.

Marks are for correct solutions, not for a particular route.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_9jNkr7TCkSdtmg7v`)

**FRONT**

In solving a trigonometric equation, when is a sketch of the graph more useful than the CAST diagram?


**BACK**

When the interval covers more than one revolution, or when you want to see the pattern the solutions follow.

CAST is quicker for a single revolution, but it only ever gives one revolution's worth, so you still have to extend to the interval.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_9Pjvg8cfDkSpmKgR`)

**FRONT**

What are the two things that most often go wrong at the end of a trigonometric equation?


**BACK**

**Missing solutions.** There is usually more than one in the interval, and stopping at the first is the commonest error.

**Solutions outside the interval.** Check that every value you write down actually lies inside the range you were given.
