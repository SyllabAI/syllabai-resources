## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_qPvdmDY9FDy5nxmk`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition polymerisation**.


**BACK**

**Addition polymerisation** is the process in which many small **monomer** molecules containing C=C double bonds join together to form a large **polymer** molecule, with no other products formed.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_WctBP8YQHf4QYqNh`)

**FRONT**

How do you deduce the monomer from the structure of an addition polymer?


**BACK**

To deduce the monomer, identify the **repeat unit** in the polymer, then change the single bond in the repeat unit back to a **C=C double bond** and remove the bonds at each end.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_bnjkQhXYMTK2SnCW`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The repeat unit of an addition polymer contains a C=C double bond.


**BACK**

**False.**

The repeat unit contains only **single bonds**. The C=C double bond is present in the **monomer**, and one bond breaks during polymerisation to form the chain.

How is the name of an addition polymer derived from its monomer?


**BACK**

The name of an addition polymer is formed by adding the **prefix 'poly-'** to the name of the monomer. For example, the monomer **propene** gives the polymer **polypropene**.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_b6QNmcWV2GFv9smN`)

**FRONT**

Why are addition polymers non-biodegradable?


**BACK**

Addition polymers are non-biodegradable because they are held together by **strong C-C bonds**, making them **chemically inert**. Microorganisms such as decomposers cannot break them down.

## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_d9ftFrDZFN78Fcbv`)

**FRONT**

Define **condensation polymerisation**.


**BACK**

**Condensation polymerisation** is a process in which two different **monomers** join together in a long chain, with the removal of a small molecule (usually **water**) at each linkage.

**FRONT**

**BACK**

The small molecule released during condensation polymerisation is **water**. One water molecule is produced per **linkage** formed.

Identify the **ester linkages** (-COO-) in the polymer, then break the C-O single bond at each linkage and add **water** to complete the molecules. This gives the **dicarboxylic acid** and **diol** monomers.
