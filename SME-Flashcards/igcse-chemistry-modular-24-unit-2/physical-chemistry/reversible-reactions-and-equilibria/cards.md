## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_kDcY3wS9959M6fNd`)

**FRONT**

Define **reversible reaction**.


**BACK**

A **reversible reaction** is one in which the products can react to re-form the original reactants, meaning the reaction can proceed in both the **forward** and **reverse** directions.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_5WTgPyzMHfVS5tVr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The forward reaction in a reversible reaction forms the products.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **forward reaction** converts reactants into products. In a reversible reaction, the **reverse reaction** converts those products back into reactants.

**FRONT**

What happens when **ammonium chloride** is heated?


**BACK**

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_59r2wKbYsfVBrhJn`)

**FRONT**

Why do reversible reactions use the ⇌ symbol rather than a single arrow?


**BACK**

Reversible reactions use the ⇌ symbol to show that the reaction proceeds in **both directions** simultaneously. A single arrow would only indicate a one-way reaction going to **completion**.


Spec links: `spcpt_mzh6RzjSBdh6PpDW`


## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_tvZvyfxGtbt7mjNx`)

**FRONT**

Define **dynamic equilibrium**.


**BACK**

**Dynamic equilibrium** is the state reached in a **reversible reaction** when the rate of the **forward reaction** equals the rate of the **reverse reaction**, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant.


Spec links: `spcpt_5WvtHCTxvrNwg5Wj`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_brvfFTwhRZjWfbGf`)

**FRONT**

What are the two key features of a system at **dynamic equilibrium**?


**BACK**

The two key features of a system at dynamic equilibrium are:

1. The rate of the **forward reaction** equals the rate of the **reverse reaction**
2. The **concentrations** of reactants and products remain constant


Spec links: `spcpt_5WvtHCTxvrNwg5Wj`

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_k53gHrgM9wVZz2ZB`)

**FRONT**

Why does the rate of the forward reaction decrease as a reversible reaction proceeds?


**BACK**

As the reversible reaction proceeds, the concentrations of the **reactants** decrease. Since reaction rate depends on concentration, a lower concentration of reactants means the forward reaction slows down.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

At dynamic equilibrium, reactants are completely used up.


**BACK**

**False.**

At dynamic equilibrium, the **concentrations** of both reactants and products remain constant — neither is fully used up. Both forward and **reverse reactions** continue at equal rates.

Why is equilibrium described as **"dynamic"**?


**BACK**

Equilibrium is described as **dynamic** because the forward and reverse reactions are **still occurring** continuously, even though the overall concentrations of reactants and products appear unchanged.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_r9fdzq27smkfqTks`)

**FRONT**

How does a system at equilibrium respond to a change in conditions?


**BACK**

A system at **equilibrium** responds to **oppose** any change made to its conditions. The equilibrium shifts in the direction that counteracts the imposed change.


Spec links: `spcpt_SYW8CX7tzPPgy3Vk`


## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_8X6Km2XWHcV8hVDm`)

**FRONT**

How does increasing the temperature affect an equilibrium reaction where the forward reaction is **exothermic**?


**BACK**

Increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium in the direction of the **endothermic** reaction to oppose the change. If the forward reaction is exothermic, the equilibrium shifts to the **left**, favouring the reverse reaction and producing more reactants.


Spec links: `spcpt_SYW8CX7tzPPgy3Vk`


## Card 20 — true_or_false (`fl_SC6BMx3kGt76NYgc`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Increasing pressure always shifts the equilibrium to the right.


**BACK**

**False.**

Increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium towards the side with **fewer moles of gas**. If both sides have equal numbers of gas molecules, there is **no effect** on the position of equilibrium.

## Card 23 — keyword_definition (`fl_BC2Bbc4zgWxrTp8c`)

**FRONT**

What is meant by the **position of equilibrium**?


**BACK**

The **position of equilibrium** describes the relative amounts of reactants and products present in a system at equilibrium. It indicates whether the equilibrium lies more to the **left** (more reactants) or to the **right** (more products).

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_6rtQwYFtM77Cf2S7`)

**FRONT**

How does a **catalyst** affect the position of equilibrium?


**BACK**

A **catalyst** does not alter the **position of equilibrium**. It speeds up both the forward and **reverse reactions** by the same amount, so equilibrium is reached more quickly but the concentrations at equilibrium remain unchanged.


Spec links: `spcpt_TRFzg8xNR5DpYnrV`


## Card 25 — true_or_false (`fl_9cYFkhHFHhrxjQcb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A catalyst shifts the position of equilibrium towards the products.


**BACK**

**False.**

A catalyst does not shift the position of equilibrium. It increases the rate of both the **forward** and **reverse reactions** equally, so the same equilibrium concentrations are reached, just more quickly.
