## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_jYq2mp5QNNY5pnZm`)

**FRONT**

Define a **metallic bond**.


**BACK**

A **metallic bond** is the strong force of attraction between positive metal ions and a 'sea' of **delocalised electrons** in a metal lattice.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_xVrcPdB6zJNmkwYY`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In a metal, the outer electrons remain attached to their original atoms.


**BACK**

**False.**

In a metal, the outer electrons leave their original atoms and become **delocalised**, meaning they can move freely throughout the metal lattice.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_j4Zkf8VZKhdQrW5t`)

**FRONT**

Why do metals have high melting and boiling points?


**BACK**

Metals have high melting and boiling points because of the strong **electrostatic attraction** between the positive metal ions and the delocalised electrons. A large amount of energy is needed to overcome these forces.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_JsnzcCTSHnfjNmKf`)

**FRONT**

What are **delocalised electrons**?


**BACK**

**Delocalised electrons** are electrons that have been released from their atoms and are free to move throughout the metal lattice, not belonging to any specific atom.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_M4JDpbdD63fDtMY9`)

**FRONT**

Why can metals conduct electricity?


**BACK**

Metals can conduct electricity because they have **delocalised electrons** that are free to move through the lattice. These electrons carry charge when a potential difference is applied.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_RRCv59sW9yh654V3`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Metals are malleable because their atoms are arranged in layers that can slide over each other.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **atoms** (or ions) in a metal are arranged in regular layers. When a force is applied, these layers can slide over each other, allowing the metal to be bent or hammered into shape.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_QXvb8QWB6wPMJC9k`)

**FRONT**

What is meant by saying a metal is **malleable**?


**BACK**

Saying a metal is **malleable** means it can be hammered or bent into shape without breaking. This is possible because the layers of atoms or ions in the lattice can slide over each other when a force is applied.
