**FRONT**

What are **halogens**?


**BACK**

The **halogens** are the non-metal elements in Group 7 of the periodic table: **fluorine**, **chlorine**, **bromine**, **iodine** and **astatine**. They are all **poisonous**, **diatomic** molecules, and each has seven electrons in its outer shell.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_4MwCvDMFHwq2bn3W`)

**FRONT**

Why do melting and boiling points increase as you go down Group 7?


**BACK**

Melting and boiling points increase as you go down Group 7 because the atoms become **larger**, which strengthens the **intermolecular forces** between molecules. More energy is needed to overcome these forces, so higher temperatures are required to melt or boil the halogen.

**True or False?**

All halogens are diatomic molecules.


**BACK**

**True.**

All halogens exist as **diatomic** molecules, meaning each molecule contains two halogen atoms joined by a **single covalent bond**. For example, chlorine exists as Cl<sub>2</sub>.

What type of compounds do halogens form when they react with metals?


**BACK**

When halogens react with metals, they form **ionic compounds** called **metal halide salts**. The halide ion carries a **-1 charge**, so the number of halogen atoms in the compound depends on the valency of the metal.

**FRONT**

What colour change is observed when chlorine solution is added to potassium bromide solution?


**BACK**

When chlorine solution is added to potassium bromide solution, the solution turns **orange** as **bromine** is produced. Chlorine is more reactive than bromine and **displaces** bromine from the potassium bromide solution.

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_Ygkh37BqwThwPsSX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Bromine can displace chlorine from a solution of potassium chloride.


**BACK**

**False.**

Bromine is **less reactive** than chlorine, so it cannot displace chlorine. Only a **more reactive** halogen can displace a less reactive one from a solution of its halide. Chlorine would displace bromine, not the other way around.

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_PZJyskBq3PwrxS2Q`)

**FRONT**

What is a **displacement reaction** in the context of Group 7?


**BACK**

A **displacement reaction** is when a more reactive **halogen** pushes out a less reactive **halogen** from an aqueous solution of its **halide salt**. Reactivity decreases down Group 7, so chlorine can displace bromine or iodine, and bromine can displace iodine.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_P7Rh3YPfCt3jbFWZ`)

**FRONT**

What is a **halide ion**?


**BACK**

A **halide ion** is formed when a halogen atom **gains one electron** to achieve a full outer shell. Halide ions carry a **-1 charge** and are named by changing the halogen's suffix to "-ide" (e.g., **chloride**, **bromide**, **iodide**).

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_MByYHV7J6nY265fv`)

**FRONT**

Why does reactivity decrease as you go down Group 7?


**BACK**

Reactivity decreases because as you go down Group 7, the outer shell gets **further from the nucleus**, so the **force of attraction** for an incoming electron becomes weaker. This makes it **harder** for atoms to gain an electron, reducing reactivity.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_k6vFh7YfbDHkH4XY`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Fluorine is the most reactive halogen because its outer shell is closest to the nucleus.


**BACK**

**True.**

Fluorine is the smallest halogen, so its outer shell is the **closest to the nucleus** of all the halogens. This means the nucleus can **attract electrons** most strongly, making fluorine the **most reactive** halogen.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_C6jhXMxxPRvPTDXx`)

**FRONT**

What type of ion is formed when a halogen reacts, and what charge does it carry?


**BACK**

When a halogen reacts, it **gains one electron** to form a **halide ion** with a **-1 charge**. This gives the halogen a full outer shell, achieving noble gas configuration.
