## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_nmZdRtqDJTS2hBkC`)

**FRONT**

What are **alkali metals**?


**BACK**

The **alkali metals** are the Group 1 elements: **lithium**, **sodium**, **potassium**, **rubidium**, **caesium** and **francium**. They are **soft**, reactive metals that each have one electron in their outer shell and form **alkaline solutions** when they react with water.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_5P52xjms8CtGhVDJ`)

**FRONT**

Why are Group 1 metals called alkali metals?


**BACK**

Group 1 metals are called **alkali metals** because they form **alkaline solutions** (pH above 7) when they react with water. For example, sodium reacts with water to produce sodium hydroxide solution.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Potassium reacts more vigorously with water than lithium.


**BACK**

**True.**

Potassium is further down Group 1 than lithium, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus and held less strongly, making potassium **more reactive** and its reaction with water **more vigorous**.

**FRONT**

What are the observations when potassium reacts with water?


**BACK**

When potassium reacts with water, it moves very rapidly across the surface, **dissolves quickly**, produces vigorous **fizzing** as hydrogen gas is released, and **burns with a lilac flame**.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_gT7P7f3Mtj4mF79T`)

**FRONT**

What is **tarnishing** in alkali metals?


**BACK**

**Tarnishing** is when **alkali metals** react with **oxygen** in the air to form a dull **metal oxide** coating on the surface. **Alkali metals** tarnish more rapidly the further down the group you go.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_TbR4y2WhMDvVxDyW`)

**FRONT**

How do melting points change as you go down Group 1, and why?


**BACK**

**Melting points decrease** as you go down Group 1. This happens because the **attractive forces** between the outer electrons and the positive metal ions become weaker as atoms get larger, so less energy is needed to melt the metal.

Francium is the least reactive alkali metal.


**BACK**

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_xbm9jyF4dkZy3k96`)

**FRONT**

What is the **electronic configuration** of Group 1 elements?


**BACK**

**Electronic configuration** of Group 1 elements refers to the arrangement of electrons in their shells. All Group 1 atoms have **one electron** in their outermost shell, which they lose when reacting to form 1+ ions with **noble gas configuration**.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_nnCcgxzCDtKGMPMY`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The reactivity of Group 1 metals increases as you go down the group.


**BACK**

**True.**

As you go down Group 1, the outer electron is in a shell **further from the nucleus**, so the **attractive force** between the nucleus and the outer electron is **weaker**. Less energy is needed to remove it, making the metal more reactive.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_cxv3KzRCyZv3B8rw`)

**FRONT**

Why does reactivity increase as you go down Group 1?


**BACK**

Reactivity increases because the **outer electron** gets further from the nucleus as more electron shells are added. This means the **force of attraction** between the nucleus and the outer electron becomes **weaker**, so less energy is needed to remove it and the metal reacts more easily.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_7p6pQyZ85DRWHjkj`)

**FRONT**

What happens to a Group 1 atom when it loses its outer electron?


**BACK**

When a Group 1 atom loses its outer electron, the **next shell down** automatically becomes the outermost shell. Because this shell is already full, the ion has **noble gas configuration**, making it highly stable.

## Card 15 — true_or_false (`fl_X4hvk4Wn7Dmk4fHR`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Lithium is more reactive than potassium because it has fewer electron shells.


**BACK**

**False.**

Lithium is **less reactive** than potassium. Although lithium has fewer shells, its outer electron is **closer to the nucleus** and therefore held more strongly. More energy is needed to remove it, so lithium reacts less easily than potassium.
