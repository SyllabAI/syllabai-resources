## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_DNCs7JpMpGQxJsDG`)

**FRONT**

Define **radian**.


**BACK**

One **radian** is the angle in a sector whose radius and arc length are both $1$.

Radians are an alternative to degrees, and are usually quoted in terms of $π$.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_ChqsWgxS5Y8HknwW`)

**FRONT**

$π$ radians `= \_\_\_\_\_\_^{\circ}`, so a full turn of $2 π$ radians is `\_\_\_\_\_\_^{\circ}`.


**BACK**

$π$ radians $= 180 ^{\circ}$, so a full turn of $2 π$ radians is $360 ^{\circ}$.

Every other conversion follows from $π = 180 ^{\circ}$, so $\frac{3}{4} π = 135 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_X4RpMvG3fcCcyF8V`)

**FRONT**

What is the arc length of a sector of radius $r$ and angle $θ$?


**BACK**

$s = r θ$, with $θ$ in **radians**; in degrees the formula simply does not hold.

A full turn, $θ = 2 π$, gives $s = 2 π r$, which is the whole circumference.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_wpS2f22BgjBHM7gx`)

**FRONT**

What is the area of a sector of radius $r$ and angle $θ$?


**BACK**

$A = \frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$, again with $θ$ in **radians**.

Both sector formulae are just the whole-circle value scaled by $\frac{θ}{2π}$.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_d8bPSY32KZJsqV6h`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

One radian is a little under $60 ^{\circ}$.


**BACK**

**True.**

Since $π$ radians is $180 ^{\circ}$, one radian is $\frac{180}{π} \approx 57 . 3 ^{\circ}$.

Knowing roughly how big a radian is makes a useful check on an answer.

**FRONT**

How can you tell an angle is in radians when no symbol is given?


**BACK**

If it is written in terms of $π$, it is almost always in radians.

Otherwise the symbol $\text{rad}$ is used, or occasionally a superscript $\text{c}$.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_KHz2YQ6tDssfcnHX`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the area of the segment cut off by a chord in a circle?


**BACK**

Work out the **sector** area, then subtract the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.

That triangle has area $\frac{1}{2} r^{2} \mathrm{sin} θ$, since both of its known sides are radii.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_vXnwK8ZCGssqVFv9`)

**FRONT**

Where do the exact values for $30 ^{\circ}$, $45 ^{\circ}$ and $60 ^{\circ}$ come from?


**BACK**

From SOH CAH TOA applied to two special triangles: half an **equilateral** triangle gives $30 ^{\circ}$ and $60 ^{\circ}$, and a right-angled **isosceles** triangle gives $45 ^{\circ}$.

Remembering the two triangles is safer than remembering six separate values.

## Card 9 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_SN752mmcZykyDc3y`)

**FRONT**

`\sin 30^{\circ} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`, `\cos 60^{\circ} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`, `\tan 45^{\circ} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

$\mathrm{sin} 30 ^{\circ} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\mathrm{cos} 60 ^{\circ} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\mathrm{tan} 45 ^{\circ} = 1$

The first two agree because $30 ^{\circ}$ and $60 ^{\circ}$ are the two acute angles of the same triangle, with opposite and adjacent swapping over.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_sC3VfYPFzT53Df5W`)

**FRONT**

What are $30 ^{\circ}$, $45 ^{\circ}$ and $60 ^{\circ}$ in radians?


**BACK**

$\frac{π}{6}$, $\frac{π}{4}$ and $\frac{π}{3}$.

Exact values are quoted in radians as often as in degrees, so both forms need to be recognised on sight.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_kbsdXKzTv74fgxDw`)

**FRONT**

How do you recall $\mathrm{sin}$, $\mathrm{cos}$ and $\mathrm{tan}$ of $0 ^{\circ}$, $90 ^{\circ}$ and $180 ^{\circ}$?


**BACK**

Read them off the **graphs** of the three functions rather than from a triangle.

No triangle can contain an angle of $0 ^{\circ}$, $90 ^{\circ}$ or $180 ^{\circ}$, so the triangle method cannot reach them.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_TBNWmSKtXR7Yhfp3`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the exact value of $\mathrm{sin} 150 \circ$?


**BACK**

Use the symmetry of the graph: $150 ^{\circ}$ mirrors $30 ^{\circ}$, and sine is positive there, so $\mathrm{sin} 150 ^{\circ} = \frac{1}{2}$.

Any multiple of $30 ^{\circ}$, $45 ^{\circ}$ or $60 ^{\circ}$ can be reached the same way.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_G5t8Fvvm3QFth6YF`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\mathrm{tan} 90 ^{\circ}$ has an exact value.


**BACK**

**False.**

$\mathrm{tan} 90 ^{\circ}$ is **undefined**, because $\mathrm{cos} 90 ^{\circ} = 0$ and the tangent divides by it.

The graph has an asymptote there rather than a value.
