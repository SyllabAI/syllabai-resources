## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_HtwtGXWgyVy6gQsk`)

**FRONT**

Define the **period** of a trigonometric function.


**BACK**

The interval after which the graph repeats itself exactly.

$\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$ have a period of $360 ^{\circ}$. $\mathrm{tan} x$ has a period of $180 ^{\circ}$, so it repeats twice as often.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_HMSj9d2gWfWnFKK3`)

**FRONT**

What values can $\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$ take, and how does $\mathrm{tan} x$ differ?


**BACK**

$\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$ always lie between $- 1$ and $1$ inclusive.

$\mathrm{tan} x$ is **unbounded**: it takes every value from $- \infty$ to $+ \infty$.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_R5CPMfmBBygTPSRW`)

**FRONT**

What are the key points of $y = \mathrm{sin} x$ between $0 ^{\circ}$ and $360 ^{\circ}$?


**BACK**

The five points are $( 0 ^{\circ} , 0 )$, $( 90 ^{\circ} , 1 )$, $( 180 ^{\circ} , 0 )$, $( 270 ^{\circ} , - 1 )$ and $( 360 ^{\circ} , 0 )$.

One point every $90 ^{\circ}$ is enough to sketch the curve. $y = \mathrm{cos} x$ has the same shape but starts at $( 0 ^{\circ} , 1 )$.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_yJBfczJWPNBvM9vM`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

For any angle $x$, $\mathrm{cos} ( - x ) = - \mathrm{cos} x$.


**BACK**

**False.**

$\mathrm{cos} ( - x ) = \mathrm{cos} x$, because the cosine graph is symmetrical about the $y$-axis.

It is **sine** that has rotational symmetry about the origin, giving $\mathrm{sin} ( - x ) = - \mathrm{sin} x$.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_cKdtWnVx3zqYZ6x8`)

**FRONT**

For which values of $x$ is $\mathrm{tan} x$ undefined, and what happens to the graph there?


**BACK**

At every odd multiple of $90 ^{\circ}$: $\pm 90 ^{\circ}$, $\pm 270 ^{\circ}$, $\pm 450 ^{\circ}$, and so on.

The graph has a **vertical asymptote** at each one, so the curve breaks into separate branches that run off towards $- \infty$ and $+ \infty$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_C52CxVbCfxTvBKpT`)

**FRONT**

Your calculator gives one solution to $\mathrm{sin} x = - 0 . 25$. Why is that not enough?


**BACK**

It returns only the **principal value**. A trigonometric equation has infinitely many solutions, and the interval you are given usually contains several.

Use the **symmetry** and **periodicity** of the graph to find the others.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_2w7wgnTgkRB3mrJp`)

**FRONT**

How does a sketch give you every solution of $\mathrm{cos}x=-\frac{1}{2}$ in a given interval?


**BACK**

Draw the horizontal line $y=-\frac{1}{2}$ across the sketch and read off **every** crossing point that lies inside the interval.

For $- 180 ^{\circ} \leq x \leq 360 ^{\circ}$ that gives $x = - 120 ^{\circ}$, $120 ^{\circ}$ and $240 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_xr2SCc8Bqnkyp5dC`)

**FRONT**

In $y = n \mathrm{cos} x$, what does $n$ do to the graph, and to the coordinates?


**BACK**

A **vertical** stretch of scale factor $n$.

The $x$ coordinates stay the same and the $y$ coordinates are multiplied by $n$. For $n > 1$ the curve is taller; for $0 < n < 1$ it is squashed towards the $x$-axis.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_CrNqQmjMzD4Kmn5Q`)

**FRONT**

In $y = \mathrm{sin} ( n x )$, what does $n$ do to the graph, and to the coordinates?


**BACK**

A **horizontal** stretch of scale factor $\frac{1}{n}$.

The $y$ coordinates stay the same and the $x$ coordinates are multiplied by $\frac{1}{n}$. For $n > 1$ this squashes the curve, so it repeats more often.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_dPY7fYm3q6Tf4Yqp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The graph of $y = \mathrm{cos} ( x + 30 ^{\circ} )$ is the graph of $y = \mathrm{cos} x$ moved $30 ^{\circ}$ to the right.


**BACK**

**False.**

It moves $30 ^{\circ}$ to the **left**.

A positive $c$ in $\mathrm{cos} ( x + c )$ shifts the graph left, which feels backwards to most people. It is $y = \mathrm{cos} ( x - 30 ^{\circ} )$ that moves $30 ^{\circ}$ to the right.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_Q9yG92pz6vH2ZzT4`)

**FRONT**

A negative $n$ adds a reflection. In which axis, for $y = n \mathrm{cos} x$ and for `y = \sin\left(n x\right)`?


**BACK**

$y = n \mathrm{cos} x$ reflects in the **x-axis**, because it is the $y$ values that change sign.

$y = \mathrm{sin} ( n x )$ reflects in the **y-axis**, because it is the $x$ values that change sign.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_J5cb8RrHMkgBtXbs`)

**FRONT**

How many complete cycles does $y = \mathrm{cos} 3 x$ make between $0 ^{\circ}$ and $360 ^{\circ}$?


**BACK**

Three.

The period is divided by 3, giving $\frac{360^{\circ}}{3} = 120 ^{\circ}$, and three lots of $120 ^{\circ}$ fit into $360 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_5g6GwbPNyhKcWmrf`)

**FRONT**

What is the reliable way to draw a transformed trigonometric graph?


**BACK**

Take the **key coordinates** of the original curve, apply the change to each one, then draw a smooth curve through the new points.

Working point by point is far safer than trying to picture the whole transformation at once.
