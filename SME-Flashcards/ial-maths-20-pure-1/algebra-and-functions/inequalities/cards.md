## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_qNF5Hkf4SQrrCkSk`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between square and round brackets in **interval notation**?


**BACK**

Square brackets **include** the end value and round brackets **exclude** it.

So `\left(4 , 8\right]` means $4 < x \leq 8$.

Infinity always takes a round bracket, because it is never actually reached.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_HvDDy7ZPtHrTtq7S`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\left[- 4 , 9\right)` and `\left\{x : x \geq - 4\right\} \cap \left\{x : x < 9\right\}` describe the same values.


**BACK**

**True.**

Both say $- 4 \leq x < 9$, with the $- 4$ included and the $9$ excluded.

Interval notation and set notation are simply two ways of writing the same range.

**FRONT**

How do you write $x > 9$ in **set notation**?


**BACK**

As `\left\{x : x > 9\right\}`, read as "the set of values of $x$ such that $x$ is greater than $9$".

A range with two ends joins two such sets with $\cap$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_CqF7kJ8W6R66hkbg`)

**FRONT**

Why should you avoid multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number?


**BACK**

Because it **reverses** the inequality sign, and forgetting to reverse it is an easy mistake to make.

Rearranging so the $x$ term is positive avoids the problem: $8 - 3 x \geq 5 x - 4$ becomes $12 \geq 8 x$, so $x \leq \frac{3}{2}$.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_K4Sj7zHD2tp7rFHs`)

**FRONT**

What is the first thing to do when solving a quadratic inequality?


**BACK**

Rearrange it so that one side is zero and the $x^{2}$ term is positive.

A positive $x^{2}$ term makes the sketch a $\cup$ shape, which is far easier to read a solution from.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_f42Gqhb28XMswCyb`)

**FRONT**

A quadratic inequality has been rearranged so the $x^{2}$ term is positive, with roots $x_{1} < x_{2}$. Where are the solutions?


**BACK**

It depends which way the inequality points:

- greater than zero: **outside** the roots, so $x < x_{1}$ or $x > x_{2}$
- less than zero: **between** the roots, so $x_{1} < x < x_{2}$

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_CNNXtBWNvYKsChjJ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

You can multiply both sides of an inequality by $x$ to clear a fraction.


**BACK**

**False.**

If $x$ could be negative the sign would need reversing, and you do not know its sign in advance.

Multiplying by $x^{2}$ keeps the sign safe but can introduce extra values that do not actually work.

**FRONT**

How do you write the solution $x < - 1$ or $x > 2$ in set notation?


**BACK**

As `\left\{x : x < - 1\right\} \cup \left\{x : x > 2\right\}`.

The **union** symbol is used because the solution is two separate ranges rather than one continuous one.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_Hmw99v4cBdVb6yqY`)

**FRONT**

Do $8 > x > 2$ and $2 < x < 8$ mean the same thing?


**BACK**

Yes, both say that $x$ lies **between** $2$ and $8$.

The second is the conventional way of writing it, with the smaller value on the left, and it is much easier to read.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_7VHDpg3zdkvw5yqg`)

**FRONT**

Where do quadratic inequalities come from in discriminant questions?


**BACK**

Setting $b^{2} - 4 a c$ greater than, equal to or less than zero gives an inequality in the unknown constant.

Solving it is then an ordinary quadratic inequality: find the critical values, sketch, and read off the region.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_yZJNWfgFySG5CJ8Q`)

**FRONT**

How do you decide which side of a line satisfies an inequality?


**BACK**

**Test a point on each side**, by substituting its coordinates into the inequality.

The origin is usually easiest: `\left(0 , 0\right)` satisfies $y < x^{2} + 1$, so the side containing the origin is the one you want.
