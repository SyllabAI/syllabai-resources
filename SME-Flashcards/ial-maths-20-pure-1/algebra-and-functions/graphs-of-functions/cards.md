## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_n6DWHQncM9MsfJ4h`)

**FRONT**

Why is a polynomial graph always drawn as one smooth curve?


**BACK**

Because a polynomial is defined for **every** value of $x$, with no gaps and no sharp corners.

That is what separates it from a graph such as $y = \frac{1}{x}$, which breaks at $x = 0$.

**FRONT**

How does the **sign** of $a$ affect the graph of $y = \frac{a}{x}$?


**BACK**

## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_y8p9vpRCjHzFsTYz`)

**FRONT**

Define **constant of proportionality**.


**BACK**

The **constant of proportionality** is the fixed number $k$ that turns a proportionality statement into an equation.

Once found, it holds for every pair of values in the relationship, which is what lets the relationship be used to predict.

## Card 19 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_tJyHCXWtGSMFh7ZJ`)

**FRONT**

Complete the two standard proportional forms, where $k$ is a constant:

`y \propto x \Rightarrow y = \_\_\_\_\_\_`

`y \propto \frac{1}{x} \Rightarrow y = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed forms are:

$y \propto x \Rightarrow y = k x$

$y \propto \frac{1}{x} \Rightarrow y = \frac{k}{x}$

In the first, the two variables rise and fall together; in the second, one rises as the other falls.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_9K2mWy2x9C7sGgj3`)

**FRONT**

$y$ is inversely proportional to the cube of $x$. How do you write that as an equation?


**BACK**

It becomes $y \propto \frac{1}{x^{3}}$ and then $y = \frac{k}{x^{3}}$.

The word **inversely** is what puts the expression in the denominator, and **the cube of** $x$ is what makes that expression $x^{3}$.

## Card 21 — true_or_false (`fl_wkwZRWzrKtPz9cbx`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$, then $x y$ is the same for every pair of values.


**BACK**

**True.**

The constant of proportionality is exactly that product, so multiplying the two variables together always returns it.

Direct proportion works the other way round: there it is the **ratio** $\frac{y}{x}$ that stays the same.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_NZFGTWsdDmfWRh3H`)

**FRONT**

A relationship is modelled by $y = \frac{k}{x^{2}}$, and $y = 3$ when $x = 2$. How do you find $k$, and what is its value?


**BACK**

Substitute the known pair of values into the equation and solve: $3 = \frac{k}{2^{2}}$.

## Card 25 — keyword_definition (`fl_fGMYtSg32FbNmH3S`)

**FRONT**

Define **mathematical model**.


**BACK**

A **mathematical model** simplifies a real-world situation so that it can be described using mathematics, and then used to make predictions.

The path of a stone thrown from a cliff top and the number of toys a factory can produce in a day are both things a model can describe.

## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_7fK39JsVcCTZDWxT`)

**FRONT**

Why are assumptions made when a real-world situation is turned into a mathematical model?


**BACK**

To **simplify** the situation enough for the mathematics to be manageable.

Ignoring air resistance on a thrown stone, or treating a factory's machines as producing at a constant rate, is what makes a workable equation possible.

## Card 27 — true_or_false (`fl_vtTgJcxGNbqX39Rp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

When a reciprocal function models a real situation, both branches of its graph are usually relevant.


**BACK**

**False.**

A modelled quantity such as a time, a length or a volume cannot be negative, so usually only the **positive** branch describes the situation.

The model therefore comes with a restriction on the values its variable is allowed to take.

## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_npzf6NTY6v5BqMdT`)

**FRONT**

What does it mean to **refine** a mathematical model?


**BACK**

It means **improving** it, either because further information has become available or because its predictions have been compared with real-world data.

A thrown-stone model might be refined by taking the stone's mass into account, and a factory model by allowing thirty minutes a day for machine repairs.

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_XyzSYs2gGy9btdx8`)

**FRONT**

A model is `\text{f} \left(d\right) = \frac{L}{d + 1}` for $d \geq 0$, where $d$ is the number of days since the start. What does the constant $L$ represent?


**BACK**

The value of the modelled quantity at the **start**, when $d = 0$.

Substituting $d = 0$ gives `\text{f} \left(0\right) = \frac{L}{1} = L`, which is how you interpret any constant in a model: put in the starting value of the variable.

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_chPzqfVDJdhbm7H3`)

**FRONT**

A gas model gives the pressure $P$ pascals in a container of volume $V$ cubic metres as $P = \frac{1750000}{V}$. If $P$ must stay below $120000$, what happens to the inequality sign when you rearrange to find $V$?


**BACK**

It **reverses**: $\frac{1750000}{V} < 120000$ becomes $V > \frac{1750000}{120000}$.

Making the pressure smaller means making the volume larger, so an upper limit on $P$ sets a **lower** limit on $V$.

Where should you look in a model to find a value of the variable for which it breaks down?
