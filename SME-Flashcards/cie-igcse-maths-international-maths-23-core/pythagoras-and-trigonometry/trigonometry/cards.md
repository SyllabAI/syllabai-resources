## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_dpZdzQZBX9WRGzWz`)

**FRONT**

In a right-angled triangle, how do you label the three sides for trigonometry?


**BACK**

Label them relative to the angle you are using, often marked $θ$.

The **opposite** is the side across from that angle, the **adjacent** is the side next to it, and the remaining side is the **hypotenuse**.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_qT2SqC2nyYZxQD7f`)

**FRONT**

What does **SOHCAHTOA** stand for?


**BACK**

It packs the three ratios into one word: $\text{sin} θ = \frac{O}{H}$, $\text{cos} θ = \frac{A}{H}$ and $\text{tan} θ = \frac{O}{A}$.

Each group of three letters gives one ratio, with the first letter naming the function and the other two naming the fraction.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_VzCmDc9xMq7KFNv2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Using the other acute angle in the same triangle swaps the opposite and adjacent sides.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **opposite** and **adjacent** are named relative to the angle you choose, so they exchange roles.

The **hypotenuse** is unaffected, because it is fixed by the right angle rather than by your choice of angle.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_fwF35HWmSjHRrYbC`)

**FRONT**

A right-angled triangle has an angle of 43°, with the opposite side $x$ and the adjacent side 9 cm. Complete the working:

`\text{tan} \left(43\right) = \frac{x}{9}`, so `x = 9 \times \_\_\_\_\_\_ = \_\_\_\_\_\_` cm


**BACK**

The completed working is:

`x = 9 \times \text{tan} \left(43\right) = 8.39` cm

The unknown sits on top of the fraction, so multiplying both sides by 9 releases it, and the answer is given to 3 significant figures.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_dxFjjt2NHxj67fk8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

These trigonometric ratios work in any triangle.


**BACK**

**False.**

The ratios $\text{sin}$, $\text{cos}$ and $\text{tan}$ apply only to **right-angled** triangles.

Without a right angle there is no hypotenuse for the other two sides to be measured against.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_8595FN89JgrN4sZ9`)

**FRONT**

When finding an angle, how do you choose which trigonometric ratio to use?


**BACK**

Look at which **two sides** you have been given, then pick the ratio built from those two.

Here both lengths are known and the angle is the unknown, which is the reverse of the situation when you are finding a length.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_Jy8v2D9TVKvqB7K9`)

**FRONT**

How do you get from $\text{cos} y = \frac{8}{23}$ to the angle $y$?


**BACK**

Apply the **inverse cosine**, written $\text{cos}^{-1}$.

So `y = \text{cos}^{-1} \left(\frac{8}{23}\right)`, which gives 69.6° to 1 decimal place.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_QRKnbfN9Rqx6wdPt`)

**FRONT**

Where do you find the inverse trigonometric functions on a calculator?


**BACK**

Above the $\text{sin}$, $\text{cos}$ and $\text{tan}$ keys, reached using the **shift** key.

They are labelled $\text{sin}^{-1}$, $\text{cos}^{-1}$ and $\text{tan}^{-1}$.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_T4jXy4KkgwkBq7jN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

You can find an angle using $\text{tan}$ if you know the hypotenuse and the opposite side.


**BACK**

**False.**

$\text{tan}$ is built from the opposite and the **adjacent**, so those are the two sides it needs.

Given the opposite and the hypotenuse you would use $\text{sin}$ instead.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_BP6Nz5ctSkvMxQzg`)

**FRONT**

A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 5 cm and an adjacent side of 12 cm. Which calculation gives the angle?


**BACK**

Take the inverse tangent of $\frac{5}{12}$, because the opposite and the adjacent are the two sides you have.

That is `\text{tan}^{-1} \left(\frac{5}{12}\right)`, giving an angle of 22.6° to 1 decimal place.
