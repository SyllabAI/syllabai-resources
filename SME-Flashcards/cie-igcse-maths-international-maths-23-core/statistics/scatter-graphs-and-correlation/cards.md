The data points in a **strong** correlation **loosely** follow a **straight line**.


**BACK**

**False.**

If the points only follow a straight line loosely, then they show a weak correlation.



## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_8jVKVH4W6rd6ZHYH`)

**FRONT**

Describe the** relationship** between quantity A and quantity B if they are **positively correlated.**


**BACK**

Because the quantities A and B are **positively correlated**, if quantity A **increases**, then quantity B will also **increase**.

E.g. the number of ice creams sold by a café is positively correlated with the air temperature. As the air temperature increases, so does the number of ice creams sold by the café.



## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_N2j49fvnJnH4XWvd`)

**FRONT**

What does the phrase "**correlation does not imply causation**" mean?


**BACK**

The phrase "**correlation does not imply causation**" means that **although a correlation may be noticed** between two different quantities, it does **not** necessarily mean that **a change in one quantity causes the other to change**.

E.g. the number of ice creams sold by a cafe and the number of bottles of suncream sold by a shop may be positively correlated.

More ice creams being sold does** not cause **more bottles of sun cream to be sold, but both quantities may increase because of a third related variable, such as as an increase in the number of hours of sunshine.



## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_dkmtZVRz4W93FxgH`)

**FRONT**

Define a **line of best fit**.


**BACK**

A **line of best fit** is a single straight line drawn through the points on a scatter diagram to show the **trend** in the data.

It is only worth drawing when the points show **positive or negative correlation**, and it can then be used to make **predictions**.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_srYj2TtqJCWcNcfk`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the **mean point** of a set of data on a scatter diagram?


**BACK**

Work out the mean of all the ***x*** values to get $\overline{x}$ and the mean of all the ***y*** values to get $\overline{y}$ separately.

The mean point is then `\left(\bar{x} , \bar{y}\right)` plotted on the diagram alongside the data.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_RWNcshBrDwqHqj92`)

**FRONT**

How should a **line of best fit** be drawn?


**BACK**

Draw a **single ruled line** through the **mean point** so that it **extends across the whole data set**.

Tilt it until there are roughly **equal numbers of points on each side** along its full length.

## Card 9 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_dYYX8CSHDB9XTnbj`)

Complete the two terms for making predictions from a line of best fit.

Predicting inside the region covered by the data is called `\_\_\_\_\_\_` and is reliable, while predicting well outside it is called `\_\_\_\_\_\_` and is much less so.


**BACK**

The completed sentence is:

Predicting inside the region covered by the data is called **interpolation** and is reliable, while predicting well outside it is called **extrapolation** and is much less so.

An extrapolated value assumes the trend carries on beyond the data, which the data itself gives no evidence for.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_mCgF2jHRqbJz468w`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An obvious **outlier** should be included when judging where to draw a line of best fit.


**BACK**

**False.**

A single extreme value that does not fit the general pattern is **ignored** when positioning the line.

Letting it drag the line away from the rest of the points would make every prediction taken from the line worse.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_HB5ZfVNBrQjFqdzr`)

**FRONT**

A line of best fit has been drawn on a scatter diagram. How do you use it to estimate ***y*** when ***x*** is 5?


**BACK**

Go up from 5 on the ***x*****-axis** as far as the line, then across to the ***y*****-axis** and read the value off there.

The estimate is only trustworthy if 5 lies **within the range** of the original data.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_42P7kXSSwRnGgy8d`)

**FRONT**

Eight computers have prices with a mean of 487.5 and running times with a mean of 3.9. Which point must the line of best fit pass through?


**BACK**

The line must pass through the mean point, which is `\left(487 . 5 , 3 . 9\right)` for this data.

Every line of best fit drawn for this data set has to go through that one point, whatever gradient it ends up with.
