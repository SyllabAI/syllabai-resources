## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_BP6BdzVvpt3gy96G`)

**FRONT**

Why is finding a midpoint the same as finding a mean?


**BACK**

Because the midpoint is the same distance from **both** endpoints, so it lies exactly **halfway** between them.

Halfway between two numbers is their average, and the same reasoning applies separately in each direction.

**BACK**

**False.**

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_Sh2mfFGCxnBWy6Hd`)

**FRONT**

$M$ is the midpoint of $A B$, and you know the coordinates of $A$ and $M$. How do you find the coordinates of $B$?


**BACK**

Work out the step that takes you from $A$ to $M$, then apply that same step again starting from $M$.

Since $M$ is the same distance from both ends, repeating the move lands you exactly on $B$.

**FRONT**

For a gradient the order of subtraction matters, but for a length it does not. Why not?


**BACK**

Because each difference is **squared**, and squaring removes any minus sign.

So `\left(3 - 8\right)^{2}` and `\left(8 - 3\right)^{2}` both give $25$, and the distance comes out the same either way.

Repeat that step to get more points, then join them with a ruler.
