## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_VB65dpT72ZRhgzpV`)

**FRONT**

A triangle is drawn in a circle so that one side is a diameter and the third vertex is on the circumference. What can you say about its angles?


**BACK**

The angle at the third vertex, **opposite the diameter**, is $90 ^{\circ}$.

This result is called the **angle in a semicircle**, because only half of the circle is used.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_tBZ7NkbDbG7TxhY5`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The angle in a semicircle is $90 ^{\circ}$ only when the point on the circumference is halfway along the arc.


**BACK**

**False.**

The angle is $90 ^{\circ}$ for **every** position of that point, so the vertex can sit anywhere on the arc between the two ends of the diameter.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_yG2FRJSqfXgJxtbP`)

**FRONT**

Why does the angle in a semicircle theorem need a **diameter** rather than any chord?


**BACK**

Because a diameter passes through the **centre**, it splits the circle into two **semicircles**, with the vertex lying on one of them.

A chord that misses the centre cuts off a segment that is not a semicircle, and the angle there is not $90 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_cC5fFCfSkkpnnbgD`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The angle in a semicircle theorem can be used on a diagram that shows a whole circle, not just one showing a semicircle.


**BACK**

**True.**

All the theorem needs is a **diameter** with a triangle drawn on it, and a diameter can sit inside a whole circle just as easily.

The rest of the circle plays no part.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_s3yN2mqg2tKvPKZb`)

**FRONT**

In a triangle whose side is a diameter, what do the two angles at the ends of the diameter add up to?


**BACK**

They add up to $90 ^{\circ}$.

The right angle opposite the diameter uses up half of the triangle's $180 ^{\circ}$, leaving the other two angles to share the rest.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_yt6wVtD3YpcMj59P`)

**FRONT**

What angle does a **tangent** make with the **radius** at its point of contact?


**BACK**

Exactly $90 ^{\circ}$, so the tangent and that radius are **perpendicular**.

This holds at every point of the circle, since a tangent can be drawn at any point on the circumference.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_SQd7mKNZrSyVyfk2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A tangent meets any chord of a circle at $90 ^{\circ}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The right angle needs a **radius**, which passes through the **centre**, so a chord that misses the centre does not meet the tangent at $90 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 8 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_4CJh8VBT7jG7kX7z`)

**FRONT**

A circle has centre $O$, and $P$ and $Q$ are two points on its circumference. Fill in the two gaps.

Since $O P$ and $O Q$ are both `\_\_\_\_\_\_` of the circle, triangle $O P Q$ is `\_\_\_\_\_\_` and its base angles are equal.


**BACK**

The completed sentence is:

Since $O P$ and $O Q$ are both **radii** of the circle, triangle $O P Q$ is **isosceles** and its base angles are equal.

Any two radii of the same circle are the same length, so this works for every such triangle.

**True or False?**

The radius has to be drawn to the exact point where the tangent touches the circle for the right angle to appear.


**BACK**

**True.**

A radius drawn to any other point on the circumference will meet the tangent at some other angle, or will not reach it at all.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_kW2szdCTQwjkk5H2`)

**FRONT**

A chord and a tangent meet at a point on a circle. How do you find the angle between that chord and the radius?


**BACK**

Subtract the angle between the chord and the tangent from $90 ^{\circ}$.

The chord lies inside the right angle that the tangent and the radius already make, so it splits that $90 ^{\circ}$ into two parts.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_xzJtRwG3B4BtzvZj`)

**FRONT**

How do the lengths of two tangents to a circle from one external point compare?


**BACK**

They are **equal in length**.

The circle's symmetry guarantees this, however far the external point lies from the circle.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The line from the centre of a circle to an external point bisects the angle between the two tangents drawn from that point.


**BACK**

**True.**

The whole figure is symmetrical about that line, so it splits the angle between the two tangents into two equal halves.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_XctfJFNCRPDKx4jD`)

**FRONT**

Two tangents from an external point, together with the radii to their points of contact, form two triangles. Why are these two triangles congruent?


**BACK**

They match by **right angle, hypotenuse, side**.

Each has a right angle where its tangent meets its radius, the two radii are equal, and the line from the centre to the external point is a shared hypotenuse.

The four sides together make a **kite** with its line of symmetry along that shared hypotenuse.

## Card 14 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_Cy4g54xznBtmTqPm`)

**FRONT**

A tangent from an external point $T$ touches a circle at $P$, where the circle has centre $O$ and radius $r$. Fill in the gap.

`P T = \sqrt{\left(O T\right)^{2} - \_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

`P T = \sqrt{\left(O T\right)^{2} - r^{2}}`

This is **Pythagoras' theorem** applied to triangle $O P T$, in which $O T$ is the hypotenuse.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_RWzwdbrb6ctCSBvk`)

**FRONT**

In the quadrilateral formed by two tangents and the two radii to their points of contact, how are the angle at the centre and the angle between the tangents related?


**BACK**

They add up to $180 ^{\circ}$.

The four angles of the quadrilateral total $360 ^{\circ}$, and the two angles at the points of contact are right angles, which uses up the other $180 ^{\circ}$.
