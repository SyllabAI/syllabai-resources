## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_NGN9kfkD4Vy85jJH`)

**FRONT**

What is a **strict** inequality?


**BACK**

A **strict** inequality is one that does not allow the two sides to be equal, so the symbols are $<$ and $>$.

For example $x > 5$ does not include $5$ itself, whereas $x \geq 5$ does.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_KhgDQTDZ6DgHSMhQ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$x > 3$ means that the smallest possible value of $x$ is $4$.


**BACK**

**False.**

Unless the question says that $x$ is an integer, $x$ can take any value greater than $3$, such as $3 . 1$ or $π$.

There is no smallest such value at all.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_FMnY8sjgVJsMngRX`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

There are infinitely many integers that satisfy $x > 2$.


**BACK**

**True.**

Only one end is fixed, so the integers $3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , \dots$ carry on without limit.

An inequality needs **two** end points before the list of integers is finite.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_KsY75BRsM4CsmkRB`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the integers that satisfy **both** $0 < x < 5$ and $x \geq 3$?


**BACK**

List the integers for each inequality **separately**, then pick out the values that appear in **both** lists.

Here that gives $1 , 2 , 3 , 4$ and $3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , \dots$, so the answer is $x = 3$ and $x = 4$.

## Card 8 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_VzgWDnFPb57K597Y`)

**FRONT**

Complete the rule for drawing an inequality on a number line:

For $<$ or $>$, use an `\_\_\_\_\_\_` circle.

For $\leq$ or $\geq$, use a `\_\_\_\_\_\_` circle.


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

For $<$ or $>$, use an **open** circle.

For $\leq$ or $\geq$, use a **closed** circle.

An open circle shows that the end point is left out, and a closed circle shows that it is included.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_vTpcxM3WXK9DVTG2`)

**FRONT**

How do you show $- 2 \leq x < 1$ on a number line?


**BACK**

Draw a **closed** circle at $- 2$ and an **open** circle at $1$, then join them with a horizontal line.

The line between the circles shows that every value in between satisfies the inequality.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_V53JGRJSXYc2vQxg`)

**FRONT**

How do you show $x > 5$ on a number line?


**BACK**

Draw an **open** circle at $5$ and a horizontal **arrow** pointing to the right.

There is no second circle, because the inequality has only one end point and the values carry on without limit.

**FRONT**

For $t < 3$, which way does the arrow on the number line point, and why?


**BACK**

The arrow points to the **left**.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_v9Zck9ngSjrHkJCB`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

On a number line, the left-hand end point of an inequality always has a closed circle.


**BACK**

**False.**

Each end point is decided only by the symbol at that end, not by which side it is on.

So $- 3 < x \leq 4$ is open on the left and closed on the right, while $- 2 \leq x < 1$ is the other way round.
