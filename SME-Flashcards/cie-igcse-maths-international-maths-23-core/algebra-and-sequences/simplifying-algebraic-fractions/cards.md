## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_PfvJCX3Rf2VQmqjJ`)

**FRONT**

Define an **algebraic fraction**.


**BACK**

An **algebraic fraction** is a fraction whose numerator or denominator, or both, contain an **algebraic term**.

So $\frac{x}{4}$, $\frac{48}{y^{2}}$ and $\frac{2p^{3}}{4p^{2}+1}$ are all algebraic fractions.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_2mKyhrTJVX2QdyyP`)

**FRONT**

Why does writing out the powers in full help you simplify $\frac{n^{5}}{n^{2}}$?


**BACK**

Because you can then see each matching $n$ on the top and the bottom and **cancel** them in pairs.

Writing $\frac{n\timesn\timesn\timesn\timesn}{n\timesn}$ and cancelling two pairs leaves $n \times n \times n$, which is $n^{3}$.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_g8rn8V3QfSjhVGmy`)

**FRONT**

When you simplify $\frac{m^{2}}{m^{3}}$, every factor in the numerator cancels. What is left on the top?


**BACK**

A **1** is left on the top, so the answer is $\frac{1}{m}$.

Cancelling removes factors rather than deleting them, so once every factor on the top has gone the number 1 remains.

## Card 4 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_pynRXV2YVmD2b7Sd`)

**FRONT**

Fill in the two gaps to show two other ways of writing the same fraction.

`\frac{5}{6 y} = 5 \times \frac{1}{\_\_\_\_\_\_} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{1}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed statement is:

$\frac{5}{6y} = 5 \times \frac{1}{6y} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{1}{y}$

The number and the letter in a denominator can be separated, because dividing by $6 y$ is the same as dividing by $6$ and then by $y$.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_VxYQXs2JvXb6X79Y`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\frac{3x}{4}$ can also be written as $0 . 75 x$.


**BACK**

**True.**

The fraction $\frac{3x}{4}$ means $\frac{3}{4}$ multiplied by $x$, and $\frac{3}{4}$ written as a decimal is $0 . 75$.
