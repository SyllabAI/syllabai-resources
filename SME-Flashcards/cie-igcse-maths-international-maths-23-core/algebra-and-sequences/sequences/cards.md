## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_Z2MSxqsNgSwtwGwn`)

**FRONT**

In the context of sequences, what is ***n***?


**BACK**

***n*** is the **position of a term** in a sequence.

E.g. when *n* = 3, it is referring to the third term of the sequence.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_xffhGpDgfJBpjTr4`)

**FRONT**

What is** subscript notation **for sequences?


**BACK**

**Subscript notation **is used to talk about a particular term.

For example

- $a_{1}$ is the 1st term
- $a_{7}$ is the 7th term
- $a_{n}$ is the *n*th term

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_HcqwVp7RdDfpPWxX`)

**FRONT**

What is a **term-to-term **rule?


**BACK**

A **term-to-term** rule tells you how to find a term from the term before it.

I.e., it gives the (*n*+1)th term in terms of the *n*th term.

**BACK**

Writing them underneath the sequence makes any pattern much easier to see.

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_hCJDkMbkNVVssSYM`)

**FRONT**

Define a **triangular number**.


**BACK**

A **triangular number** is the result of adding consecutive whole numbers starting from $1$, giving $1$, $1 + 2$, $1 + 2 + 3$, and so on.

The first five are $1 , 3 , 6 , 10 , 15$, and they are so called because that many dots can be arranged into a triangle.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_bRyhXN9TQXpSf9PS`)

**FRONT**

What is a **linear sequence**?


**BACK**

A **linear sequence** is a sequence of numbers that increase or decrease by the **same amount** from one term to the next.

A linear sequence is often called an **arithmetic sequence**.



## Card 16 — keyword_definition (`fl_vC4QmDrTZB4NQSKz`)

**FRONT**

Define the **common difference** of a linear sequence.


**BACK**

The **common difference** is the **amount **that a linear sequence increases or decreases by from **one term to the next**.



## Card 17 — keyword_definition (`fl_7t4MD9KtcZ9v5Qg7`)

**FRONT**

What is $d$ the notation for in the context of **linear sequences**?


**BACK**

$d$ is the notation for the **common difference** of a linear sequence.

E.g. for a sequence 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, ...
$d=4$.



## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_XqBBgRCvM2vqCfkZ`)

**FRONT**

What is $b$ the notation for in the context of **linear sequences**?


**BACK**

$b$ is the value **before the first term** (sometimes known as the zero term).

E.g. for a sequence 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, ...
The common difference is +4, so imagine going back from the first term by subtracting 4. So $b=-1$.



## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_Qvc5GkCbvrq8V4Fr`)

**FRONT**

What is the** position-to-term **formula for a **linear sequence** in terms of $b$, $d$ and $n$?


**BACK**

The **position-to-term** formula (also known as the *n*th term rule) for a linear sequence in terms of $b$, $d$ and $n$ is: $n\mathrm{th} \mathrm{term}=dn+b$.



## Card 20 — keyword_definition (`fl_h7sJ5ZSd9Bz2M4YM`)

**FRONT**

Define a **quadratic sequence**.


**BACK**

A **quadratic sequence** is one whose $n$th term formula contains $n^{2}$.

For example, $2 , 5 , 10 , 17 , 26 , \dots$ has $n$th term $n^{2} + 1$.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_9TN72XkvxwQp9wHV`)

**FRONT**

Once you know a sequence is quadratic, how do you find its $n$th term formula?


**BACK**

Write the **square numbers** $1 , 4 , 9 , 16 , 25 , \dots$ underneath it and compare the two term by term.

If every term is the same amount more, add that amount to $n^{2}$; if every term is a multiple, multiply $n^{2}$ by it.

## Card 24 — true_or_false (`fl_rgSbtT2dcg5Tb4mw`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The sequence $16 , 25 , 36 , 49 , \dots$ has $n$th term $n^{2}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

These are the square numbers starting from $4^{2}$, so the formula is `\left(n + 3\right)^{2}`.

Substituting $n = 1$ gives $4^{2} = 16$, which is indeed the first term.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_6ss6Y4YM2RmX87DX`)

**FRONT**

In a sequence with $n$th term $a n^{2} + b$, how do you find $a$ from the differences?


**BACK**

Take **half** of the second difference.

For the cubic form $a n^{3} + b$ you take a **sixth** of the third difference instead.

## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_9mx66QTNTybWDY4C`)

**FRONT**

Which numbers do you compare a cubic sequence with to find its formula?


**BACK**

You compare it with the **cube numbers** $1 , 8 , 27 , 64 , 125 , \dots$, which are $1^{3} , 2^{3} , 3^{3} , 4^{3} , \dots$

For instance $2 , 9 , 28 , 65 , 126 , \dots$ is each cube number plus $1$, so its $n$th term is $n^{3} + 1$.

## Card 27 — true_or_false (`fl_vbnTnZ5cH74n2wh2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two different quadratic sequences can have exactly the same second differences.


**BACK**

**True.**

The second difference fixes only the number in front of $n^{2}$, so $n^{2} + 1$ and $n^{2} + 5$ both have second differences of $2$.

That is why the sequence still has to be compared with the square numbers.
