## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_pGdhsrdK5WqvGt3g`)

**FRONT**

In the term $a^{n}$, what are $a$ and $n$ called?


**BACK**

$a$ is the **base**, and $n$ is the **index**.

The index is also called the **power** or the **exponent**, and when it is a positive whole number it tells you how many times the base is multiplied by itself.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_cXStP84rxBRf9hvQ`)

**FRONT**

How do you simplify `\left(3 x^{7}\right) \times \left(6 x^{4}\right)`?


**BACK**

Work out the **number** part and the **letter** part separately, then multiply the two results together.

Here $3 \times 6 = 18$ and $x^{7} \times x^{4} = x^{11}$, so the answer is $18 x^{11}$.

## Card 3 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_jSdF7whShW54X99z`)

**FRONT**

Complete the simplification by filling in the missing **number** and the missing **index**:

`\frac{6 x^{7}}{3 x^{4}} = \_\_\_\_\_\_ x^{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed simplification is:

$\frac{6x^{7}}{3x^{4}} = 2 x^{3}$

The numbers are **divided**, giving $6 \div 3 = 2$; they are not subtracted.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_tv9qxy8tbrrbRz5M`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\left(3 x^{7}\right)^{2}` simplifies to $3 x^{14}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The power outside the bracket applies to the $3$ as well as to $x^{7}$, so the $3$ is squared too:

`\left(3 x^{7}\right)^{2} = 3^{2} \times \left(x^{7}\right)^{2} = 9 x^{14}`

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_qVg5gknpZHmWRqtn`)

**FRONT**

Find the value of $x$ if $q^{x} = \frac{q^{2}\timesq^{5}}{q^{10}}$.


**BACK**

$x = - 3$.

**Simplify the right-hand side first** so that it becomes a single power of $q$: the numerator gives $q^{7}$, and dividing that by $q^{10}$ leaves $q^{-3}$.
