## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_SzWFfkSSxgj9cKZJ`)

**FRONT**

Define **linear simultaneous equations**.


**BACK**

**Linear simultaneous equations** are two equations in two unknowns that are solved together, so that the solution satisfies **both at the same time**.

They are **linear** because they contain no $x^{2}$ or $y^{2}$ terms.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_GPPC2NRNYYGJCGyv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

There is more than one pair of values of $x$ and $y$ that satisfies $3 x + 2 y = 11$.


**BACK**

**True.**

Both $x = 3 , y = 1$ and $x = 1 , y = 4$ satisfy it, and there are many more pairs that do.

This is why a **second** equation is needed to pin the answer down to one pair.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_2ScG68wKjx8dbD4Y`)

**FRONT**

In $3 x + 2 y = 11$ and $2 x - y = 5$, how do you make the $y$ terms match so that one can be eliminated?


**BACK**

Multiply **every term** of the second equation by $2$, which gives $4 x - 2 y = 10$.

The first equation is left alone, because its $y$ term is already $2 y$.

## Card 4 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_QCNXrVkR8XCH93xS`)

Complete the rule for eliminating a term:

When the signs in front of the terms you want to eliminate are the **same**, `\_\_\_\_\_\_` the equations.

When the signs are **different**, `\_\_\_\_\_\_` the equations.


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

When the signs in front of the terms you want to eliminate are the **same**, **subtract** the equations.

When the signs are **different**, **add** the equations.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_fBBpqd2qkyRVhnkF`)

**FRONT**

You have eliminated $x$ from a pair of simultaneous equations and found that $y = 1$. How do you find $x$?


**BACK**

Substitute $y = 1$ back into **one of the original equations** and solve it for $x$.

Either original equation will do, and the other can then be used to check that both values are correct.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_3kSjxZqDQtzkK7gk`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Subtracting $6 x - 3 y = 15$ from $6 x + 4 y = 22$ gives $y = 7$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The $y$ terms give `4 y - \left(- 3 y\right) = 7 y`, not $y$, because subtracting a negative term adds it on.

The result is $7 y = 7$, so $y = 1$.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_4r52psZYScV3vxMF`)

**FRONT**

How do you start solving a pair of simultaneous equations by **substitution** instead of elimination?


**BACK**

Rearrange one of the equations into the form $y = \dots$ (or $x = \dots$), then replace every $y$ in the **other** equation with that expression.

Put the expression in **brackets** as you substitute it, so that it is treated as a single quantity.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_N9vfntvkycHDv6qX`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the solution of two simultaneous equations from their graphs?


**BACK**

Plot both equations on the **same axes** and find the point where the two lines **intersect**.

The $x$-coordinate of that point is the solution for $x$, and the $y$-coordinate is the solution for $y$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_DPwSfKcTJ5WQrKyD`)

**FRONT**

What do you need in order to set up a pair of simultaneous equations?


**BACK**

Two letters, one for each unknown, and **two different equations** connecting them.

The two equations have to say genuinely different things about the same two unknowns.

## Card 10 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_ypDS5CZvVBrfxnRx`)

**FRONT**

Five apples and one banana cost $230$ cents. Complete the equation, taking $x$ as the price of an apple and $y$ as the price of a banana, both in cents.

`5 x + \_\_\_\_\_\_ = 230`


**BACK**

The completed equation is:

$5 x + y = 230$

The single banana contributes $y$ on its own, with no number written in front of it.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_WFRvgp8Zfd4gvJVC`)

**FRONT**

Why must you write down exactly what each letter stands for, including its units?


**BACK**

Because a letter with no stated meaning cannot be interpreted at the end, and a price could be read as either dollars or cents.

In the apple and banana example $x$ and $y$ are prices in **cents**, which is why the totals are $180$ and $230$.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_CXkcQ6QXSfGXGG37`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Once you have found the two unknowns, the problem is always finished.


**BACK**

**False.**

The question may ask for something built out of them, such as the **product** of two numbers whose sum is $19$ and whose difference is $5$.

Those numbers are $12$ and $7$, so the answer wanted is $84$.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_DTSQVCvwyF3DkbP5`)

**FRONT**

Solving a problem about apple and banana prices in cents gives $x = 40$ and $y = 30$. What must you still do?


**BACK**

State what those numbers **mean in the context**, with their units: an apple costs $40$ cents and a banana costs $30$ cents.

A bare pair of numbers does not answer a question that was asked in words.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_WRyMX5bnZ3BYPrcn`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A money problem can be set up in dollars or in cents, whichever you prefer.


**BACK**

**True.**

Either works, provided **every** number in both equations uses the same unit.

In dollars the apple and banana equations become $3 x + 2 y = 1 . 8$ and $5 x + y = 2 . 3$, giving $x = 0 . 4$ and $y = 0 . 3$.
