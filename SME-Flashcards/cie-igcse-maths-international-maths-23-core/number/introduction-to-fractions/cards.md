## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_jGNtcqBT74dtNCTn`)

**FRONT**

Define the **denominator** of a fraction.


**BACK**

The **denominator** is the number on the bottom of a fraction, and it tells you how many **equal parts** each whole is split into.

In $\frac{2}{3}$ the denominator is $3$, so each whole is split into three equal parts.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_zVBSxdChRMz5HCwS`)

**FRONT**

Define the **numerator** of a fraction.


**BACK**

The **numerator** is the number on the top of a fraction, and it tells you how many of the equal parts you **have**.

In $\frac{2}{3}$ the numerator is $2$, so you have two of the three equal parts.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_m9mwxf9XwKZgb7vK`)

**FRONT**

How can you tell straight away whether a fraction is less than $1$?


**BACK**

A fraction is less than $1$ when the **numerator is smaller than the denominator**, because you have fewer parts than it takes to make a whole.

So $\frac{2}{3}$ is less than $1$, and $\frac{7}{5}$ is greater than $1$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_Ypfj7gKNWfkCQ8t4`)

**FRONT**

How do you find $\frac{2}{5}$ of $60$?


**BACK**

Divide by the **denominator**, then multiply by the **numerator**.

Here $60 \div 5 = 12$, and then $12 \times 2 = 24$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_WKdWr7FBvSRG9zbm`)

**FRONT**

How do you turn "$\frac{2}{3}$ of $60$" into a multiplication of two fractions?


**BACK**

The word **of** means multiply, and a whole number can be written as a fraction over $1$:

$\frac{2}{3} \text{of} 60 = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{60}{1} = \frac{120}{3} = 40$

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_5DP68PmSwqwdtymr`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_qmPBW94Wp72qtsSS`)

**FRONT**

How do you find a fraction that is equivalent to $\frac{5}{6}$?


**BACK**

Multiply the **top and bottom by the same number**.

For example, $\frac{5\times2}{6\times2} = \frac{10}{12}$ and $\frac{5\times3}{6\times3} = \frac{15}{18}$.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_Hd88kx48q8DPgQ3k`)

**FRONT**

How can you tell that a fraction cannot be simplified any further?


**BACK**

There is no whole number greater than $1$ that divides into **both** the top and the bottom.

For example, $\frac{5}{9}$ cannot be simplified, but $\frac{25}{45}$ can, because $5$ divides into both.

## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_9hbCqbHRGyxTvQWQ`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **top heavy (improper) fraction**.


**BACK**

A **top heavy (improper) fraction** is one where its **numerator is bigger than its denominator**.

E.g. $\frac{4}{3}$ is a top heavy fraction.



## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_gyWHCrHqw5xrMGtg`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **mixed number**.


**BACK**

A **mixed number** is a **whole number** **added **together with a **fraction**.

E.g. $2\frac{3}{5}$is a mixed number.



## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_zZsQPzhNxjTNSsHq`)

**FRONT**

How do you **convert **a **mixed number** into a** top heavy fraction**?


**BACK**

To convert from a mixed number into a top heavy fraction, **multiply **the **whole number** by the **denominator**. **Add **that value to the **numerator**. This becomes the **new numerator**.

The **denominator stays the same**.

E.g. $4\frac{5}{8}=\frac{4\times8+5}{8}=\frac{37}{8}$



## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_3cWkZ8dSJXMqMfdj`)

**FRONT**

How do you **convert **a **top heavy fraction **into a **mixed number**?


**BACK**

To convert from a top heavy fraction to a mixed number, **divide **the** numerator by the denominator**.

The **number of times** that the denominator goes into the numerator is the **whole number** for the mixed number.

The **remainder** is the **numerator **for the mixed number.

The **denominator stays the same**.

E.g. $\frac{22}{7}=\frac{21}{7}+\frac{1}{7}=3\frac{1}{7}$
