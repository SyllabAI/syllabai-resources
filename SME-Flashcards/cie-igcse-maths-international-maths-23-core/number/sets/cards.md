## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_MsdBktyTXktCRRvD`)

**FRONT**

Define a **set**.


**BACK**

A **set** is a collection of **elements**, which may be numbers, letters, coordinates or anything else.

Its elements are listed inside curly brackets, so the factors of 6 form the set `\left\{ 1 , 2 , 3 , 6 \right\}`.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_smtw9MHqvZXtwCDs`)

**FRONT**

What is the **universal set**?


**BACK**

The **universal set** is the set of everything being considered, written $E$.

If a question is only concerned with the factors of 24, then $E$ is the set of those factors and nothing else.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_q7MJc2K5jjY75Fzz`)

**FRONT**

What does `n \left(A\right)` mean?


**BACK**

It is the **number of elements** in set $A$.

If `A = \left\{ 1 , 4 , 9 \right\}` then `n \left(A\right) = 3`.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_NvvJtFFkfTHmWgDm`)

**FRONT**

What does $x \in A$ mean?


**BACK**

That $x$ is an **element of** the set $A$, meaning $x$ is one of the things in $A$.

If `A = \left\{ 2 , 6 , 12 \right\}` then $6 \in A$ is true.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_mvkGs5xQcGNkVvZ2`)

**FRONT**

What does $A \cap B$ mean?


**BACK**

The **intersection** of $A$ and $B$, which is the set of elements that are in **both** sets.

On a Venn diagram it is the region where the two circles **overlap**.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_kXsRcTPTpjZqv3by`)

**FRONT**

What does $A \cup B$ mean?


**BACK**

The **union** of $A$ and $B$, which is the set of elements in **at least one** of the sets.

This includes everything in the overlap, but each element is written only once.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_wWtdPrxJ55PjTjQQ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The rectangle drawn around a Venn diagram represents the universal set.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **rectangle** stands for the **universal set**, and each **circle** drawn inside it stands for one set.

Two circles are drawn overlapping only where the sets share elements.

## Card 8 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_xYmNb8T3xXy86tBD`)

**FRONT**

For `A = \left\{ 2 , 6 , 12 , 14 , 28 \right\}` and `B = \left\{ 7 , 14 , 21 , 28 , 35 \right\}`, complete the two statements:

`A \cap B = \_\_\_\_\_\_`

`n \left(A\right) = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed statements are:

`A \cap B = \left\{ 14 , 28 \right\}`

`n \left(A\right) = 5`

Only 14 and 28 appear in both lists, and $A$ has five elements altogether.
