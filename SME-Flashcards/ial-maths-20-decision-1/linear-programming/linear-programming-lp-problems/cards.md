## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Q4wpznsbCNKNPSD8`)

**FRONT**

Define **linear programming**.


**BACK**

Linear programming is a way of finding the **greatest** or **least** possible value of a quantity in a situation that is restricted by a set of **constraints**.

It is used in problems such as maximising a manufacturer's profit or minimising its costs.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_v2G8T2Rd3H4JNBBW`)

**FRONT**

Define the **decision variables** of a linear programming problem.


**BACK**

The decision variables are the quantities in the problem that can be **varied**, usually written as $x$, $y$ and $z$.

They are typically a 'number of things', such as the number of chairs and the number of tables a manufacturer makes in a day, and the values they may take are limited by the constraints.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_X6CwVz5NfNSW7ZCD`)

**FRONT**

What is the **objective function** of a linear programming problem, and what do the letters $P$ and $C$ usually stand for?


**BACK**

The objective function is the quantity the problem asks you to **maximise** or **minimise**, written as a function of the decision variables.

The letter $P$ is used in maximising problems, standing for **profit**, and $C$ in minimising problems, standing for **costs**.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_c5XXnNNxNgN5Vr4w`)

**FRONT**

Why must the decision variables be defined before the constraints of a linear programming problem can be written down?


**BACK**

Because each constraint is an **inequality in the decision variables**, so until they have been named and given a meaning there is nothing for an inequality to be written in terms of.

Defining them first also fixes what an answer will mean, for example $x$ chairs and $y$ tables per day.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_c8pkZjWHPCGgYJvr`)

**FRONT**

A furniture manufacturer makes $x$ chairs and $y$ tables per day. A chair takes 1 hour to make and a table takes 2 hours, and the factory can run for at most 18 hours a day. Complete the constraint:

`x + \_\_\_\_\_\_ y \_\_\_\_\_\_ 18`


**BACK**

The completed constraint is:

$x + 2 y \leq 18$

Each chair uses **1** hour and each table uses **2**, and 'at most 18 hours' makes the sign **less than or equal to**.


*Blanks: 0 — answers: ['1', '2', 'less than or equal to']*

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_qcvZXDRQ4w6dVWNB`)

**FRONT**

Why is the constraint $x , y \geq 0$ nearly always included in a linear programming problem?


**BACK**

Because the decision variables are almost always a **number of things**, and a negative number of chairs or hours has no meaning.

It is known as the **non-negativity constraint**.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_kFt7y7BZFCh8r3jk`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two linear programming problems can have exactly the same constraints and still have different optimal solutions.


**BACK**

**True.**

The constraints fix which points are **allowed**, but they say nothing about which of those points is best.

That is decided by the **objective function**, so changing it, for instance from maximising profit to minimising cost, can move the answer to a completely different point.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_BF64cmRHkMHtFKZ5`)

**FRONT**

A constraint has come out as $2 x + 4 y \leq 8$. How can it be simplified, and why is that worth doing?


**BACK**

Divide every term by the common factor **2**, giving $x + 2 y \leq 4$.

Simplifying leaves exactly the same set of points satisfying the constraint, but the numbers are smaller and the line is easier to work with.
