**BACK**

**Gradient** is a measure of **steepness**, referring to how steep a line or curve is on a graph. It describes the **rate** at which ***y***** changes with respect to *****x***.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_sThkD9fn5pzVBcB6`)

**FRONT**

What is the **main rule** for **differentiating terms** with powers of $x$?


**BACK**

To differentiate powers of $x$:

- multiply the number in front by the power
- then subtract one from the power

So the derivative of $2x^{3}$ is  `open parentheses 3 cross times 2 close parentheses x to the power of 3 minus 1 end exponent equals 6 x squared`.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_NP2b5H2mkgq5XTpb`)

**FRONT**

What is the **main rule** for differentiating a **number of terms** added or subtracted together?  (For example something like $2x^{3}-3x+4$.)


**BACK**

The **main rule** for differentiating a **number of terms** added or subtracted together is simply to differentiate **each term one at a time**.

So the derivative of  $2x^{3}-3x+4$  is  `open parentheses 3 cross times 2 close parentheses x to the power of 3 minus 1 end exponent minus 3 plus 0 equals 6 x squared minus 3`.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_rk33xqn57pbcmjGz`)

**FRONT**

How can you use the **gradient function** (derivative) to **classify** a turning point?


**BACK**

At a **maximum point**:

- the gradient just **before** the turning point is **positive**
- the gradient **at** the turning point is **zero**
- the gradient just **after** the turning point is **negative**

At a **minimum point**:

- the gradient just **before** the turning point is **negative**
- the gradient **at** the turning point is **zero**
- the gradient just **after** the turning point is **positive**



## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_BBQgF6Ms6VFKz6Kd`)

**FRONT**

How can you use the **second derivative** $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}$ to **classify** a turning point?


**BACK**

At a **maximum point**:

- the **gradient** is **zero**, $\frac{dy}{dx}=0$
- the **second derivative** is **negative**, $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}<0$

At a **minimum point**:

- the **gradient** is **zero**, $\frac{dy}{dx}=0$
- the **second derivative** is **positive**, $\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}>0$

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_B2zTPsyVzVHXYrWj`)

**FRONT**

The area of a shape, $A$ m<sup>2</sup>, is given by $A=x^{2}-6x+14$.

Explain how you would use differentiation to find the minimum area.


**BACK**

The area of a shape, $A$ m<sup>2</sup>, is given by $A=x^{2}-6x+14$.

To find the minimum area

1. Differentiate $A$ to get $\frac{dA}{dx}$
2. Set $\frac{dA}{dx}=0$ to form and solve an equation in $x$
3. Substitute this value of $x$ back into $A$
4. This must be the minimum value of $A$ as $A=x^{2}-6x+14$ is a positive quadratic curve (U-shaped)



## Card 30 — true_or_false (`fl_VqPxtThsnkmKSKfc`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

You can find the maximum or minimum value of $A=x^{2}+2y$ by setting $\frac{dA}{dx}=0$.


**BACK**

**False.**

You cannot find the maximum or minimum value of $A=x^{2}+2y$ by setting $\frac{dA}{dx}=0$ because $A$ needs to be in terms of **one variable only**.

You cannot have both $x$ and $y$ in the equation you want to maximise / minimise.



## Card 31 — question_and_answer (`fl_Mh4BXRDMTSchnptK`)

**FRONT**

A rectangular field measures $x$ m by $y$ m with a perimeter of $100$ m.

Explain how you would find its area, $A$ m<sup>2</sup>, in terms of $x$ only.


**BACK**

A rectangular field measures $x$ m by $y$ m with a perimeter of $100$ m.

To find its area, $A$ m<sup>2</sup>, in terms of $x$ only

1. First find $A$ in terms of $x$ and $y$ 
($A=xy$)
2. Then use the perimeter information to find a relationship between $x$ and $y$ 
($2x+2y=100$)
3. Make $y$ the subject ($y=50-x$) and substitute it into $A$

This gives `A equals x open parentheses 50 minus x close parentheses` which expands to give $A=50x-x^{2}$. This is now in **one variable** only.



## Card 32 — true_or_false (`fl_wPWphq6wpdbFhjn8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The minimum volume of the shape given by $V=x^{3}-12x+20$ is the value of $x$ that satisfies $\frac{dV}{dx}=0$.


**BACK**

**False.**

The minimum volume of the shape given by $V=x^{3}-12x+20$ is not the value of $x$ that satisfies $\frac{dV}{dx}=0$ as the minimum **volume **will be a value of $V$, not $x$.

You need to find the two values of $x$ that satisfies $\frac{dV}{dx}=0$ then substitute them into the equation for $V$ to get a maximum and a minimum value. The smaller value is the minimum value of $V$ required.
