## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_nF5KVgGwGcfJ2q9K`)

**FRONT**

Define the **rate of change** of $y$ against $x$.


**BACK**

It is the amount that $y$ changes for every **1 unit of increase** in $x$, and on a graph it is measured by the **gradient**.

A **straight** line means that rate is constant, and a **horizontal** line means it is zero, so $y$ is not changing at all.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_gc925XTKWW5S4bmR`)

**FRONT**

How do you work out the units of a gradient?


**BACK**

Divide the units on the $y$-axis by the units on the $x$-axis.

A graph of volume in cubic centimetres against time in seconds therefore has a gradient measured in cubic centimetres per second.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_jYHz2zY4McMChqYz`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A rate-of-change graph must always have time on the horizontal axis.


**BACK**

**False.**

Time is the commonest choice, but any two variables can be plotted against each other.

The volume of air in an inflating balloon plotted against its radius is a rate-of-change graph with no time on it at all.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_BJXVWhGK5HcfF7hm`)

**FRONT**

A graph of fuel in litres against distance in kilometres has gradient $- 0 . 09$. What does that mean?


**BACK**

The units are litres per kilometre, so travelling **1 km uses up 0.09 litres** of fuel.

The gradient is negative because the amount of fuel left in the tank is **decreasing** as the distance increases.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_ppVcScpr7HYXCSGt`)

**FRONT**

What does the gradient of a tangent to a curved graph estimate?


**BACK**

It estimates the **rate of change at that particular point**, since the rate is no longer constant along a curve.

Where the curve is **steeper** the rate of change is greater, and the tangent's gradient is only an estimate because the tangent is drawn by eye.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_7Gsw65VhPSzz2YBp`)

**FRONT**

Water flows at a constant rate into a container that gets narrower towards the top. What does its depth-time graph look like?


**BACK**

Its gradient starts **shallow** and becomes **steeper**, because the same volume of water each second fills a narrower space and so raises the depth more quickly.

A container with vertical sides would instead give a **straight** line, since the depth would rise at a constant rate throughout.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_5x54C4HQJBZG5WNR`)

**FRONT**

A straight-line graph is used to model fuel remaining against distance travelled. Why might that model not be realistic?


**BACK**

A straight line assumes fuel is used at a **constant** rate for the whole journey.

In practice the rate changes with speed, traffic and load, so a more realistic graph would be a curve.
