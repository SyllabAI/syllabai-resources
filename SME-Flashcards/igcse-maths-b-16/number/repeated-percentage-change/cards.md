## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_3rtCsHMp534G2jG5`)

**FRONT**

Define **repeated percentage change**.


**BACK**

Repeated percentage change is when several percentage **increases or decreases** are applied to a quantity **one after the other**.

Each change acts on the **new** amount produced by the change before it, not on the original amount.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_h43ysM7PQcdh7dcp`)

**FRONT**

An amount of $10000$ is increased by 10% three times in a row. Fill in the missing multiplier.

`10000 \times \_\_\_\_\_\_^{3} = 13310`


**BACK**

The completed calculation is:

$10000 \times 1 . 1^{3} = 13310$

The multiplier for a **10% increase** is $1 . 1$, and repeating the same change three times means raising that multiplier to the **power 3**.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_kgP54WJsnF5MfhY4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Increasing an amount by 10% three times in a row is the same as increasing it by 30% once.


**BACK**

**False.**

Repeating a 10% increase three times multiplies by $1 . 1^{3} = 1 . 331$, while a single 30% increase multiplies by $1 . 3$.

Starting from $10000$ these give $13310$ and $13000$, because each repeat acts on the **larger** amount left by the one before.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_VZWtZ5fYWvg7995m`)

**FRONT**

A population of $8000$ falls by 5% each year. How many times is the change applied between the start of 2025 and the start of 2028, and what is the population then?


**BACK**

The change is applied **three** times, once for each of the years 2025 to 2026, 2026 to 2027 and 2027 to 2028.

So the population is $8000 \times 0 . 95^{3} = 6859$.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_zFGXNv6dSVNypghZ`)

**FRONT**

An amount is decreased by 14% and then decreased by 9%. How do you find the final amount?


**BACK**

Multiply the original amount by the multiplier for **each** change in turn, so multiply by $0 . 86$ and then by $0 . 91$.

For an original amount of $10000$ this gives $10000 \times 0 . 86 \times 0 . 91 = 7826$.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_WhrwkcgfVYRj3yn4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Increasing an amount by 3.4% and then decreasing the result by 3.4% brings it back to the amount you started with.


**BACK**

**False.**

The multipliers are $1 . 034$ and $0 . 966$, and $1 . 034 \times 0 . 966 = 0 . 998844$, which is **less than 1**.

The decrease is taken from a **larger** amount than the increase was added to, so $250000$ becomes $249711$ rather than returning to $250000$.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_NJpCrGxBhYY9Cd7m`)

**FRONT**

Define **compound interest**.


**BACK**

Compound interest is **interest** added to an amount at regular intervals, with each new lot of interest worked out on the **total so far** rather than on the starting amount.

It applies both to money **saved**, where it increases the amount saved, and to money **owed**, where it increases the debt.


Spec links: `spcpt_WWjHg7QVhBnYQHKs`


## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_psxQbCNpQ7Wxf5Yk`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

With compound interest at 4% per year, more interest is added in the second year than in the first.


**BACK**

**True.**

In the second year the 4% is worked out on the original amount **plus** the interest already added in the first year.

That is what makes the interest **compound**: the amount added grows from one period to the next.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_rcsbHBQFRh3JBxC8`)

**FRONT**

What tells you how many times to apply the change in a compound interest question?


**BACK**

The question states the **time period** for each change, which is often a year but may be a month or a week.

Apply the change once for each of those periods that the money is invested or owed for.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_7HN3g73wbgW8DYjQ`)

**FRONT**

Why can a loan with regular repayments not be worked out using a single multiplier?


**BACK**

A repayment **subtracts** a fixed amount of money, which is not a percentage change, so it cannot be folded into a multiplier.

The amount owed has to be worked out **step by step**: add the interest, subtract the repayment, then repeat for the next period.

## Card 11 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_yhYz5bmdfywBYCYy`)

**FRONT**

A loan of 22000 dollars has 5% interest added, and then 5000 dollars is repaid. Fill in the missing multiplier.

`\left(22000 \times \_\_\_\_\_\_\right) - 5000 = 18100`


**BACK**

The completed calculation is:

`\left(22000 \times 1.05\right) - 5000 = 18100`

The multiplier for a 5% increase is $1 . 05$, and the repayment is **subtracted** afterwards rather than built into the multiplier.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_j5nmMDZ3MD8DgnN4`)

**FRONT**

In a loan question, why does it matter whether the repayment is made before or after the interest is added?


**BACK**

Interest is charged on whatever is owed **at that moment**, so making the repayment first means the interest is worked out on a **smaller** amount.

The two orders therefore give different answers, and the question always makes clear which one applies.

## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_B4KtSYMsJb6WQ7S6`)

**FRONT**

Define **depreciation**.


**BACK**

Depreciation is a **repeated percentage decrease** in the **value** of something over time.

Cars, laptops and mobile phones are the usual examples, and the value normally falls by a percentage each year.


Spec links: `spcpt_mQzsHp4wNfnn9sNH`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_CgPSSHTz92RFPtDj`)

**FRONT**

What does it mean to say that a car depreciates by 15% a year?


**BACK**

Its **value** falls by 15% each year, and each year's 15% is taken from the value at the **start of that year** rather than from the original price.

So the value is multiplied by $0 . 85$ once for each year that passes.

## Card 15 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_jhzYNpCVqPD2v2n7`)

**FRONT**

A car costing $30000$ pounds depreciates by 12% in its first year and by 8% in each of the next two years. Fill in the two missing multipliers.

`30000 \times \_\_\_\_\_\_ \times \_\_\_\_\_\_^{2} = 22344.96`


**BACK**

The completed calculation is:

$30000 \times 0 . 88 \times 0 . 92^{2} = 22344 . 96$

A rate that is **repeated** gets a power, while a rate used **once** is simply multiplied in, so the 8% decrease is squared and the 12% decrease is not.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_9kpWKSXRbkNkKHk4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Depreciation questions are worked out using the same method as compound interest questions.


**BACK**

**True.**

Both are repeated percentage change, and the only difference is that depreciation uses a multiplier **less than 1** while compound interest uses one **greater than 1**.

So the method is the same either way: find the multiplier for one period, then apply it once for each period.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_jwdkdPmxV6VMgPFg`)

**FRONT**

If a value depreciates by 2% each month, what multiplier applies over one year?


**BACK**

The multiplier for one month is $0 . 98$, and a year contains **12** months, so the multiplier for the whole year is $0 . 98^{12}$.

That comes to about $0 . 785$, so the value falls by roughly **21.5%** over the year.
