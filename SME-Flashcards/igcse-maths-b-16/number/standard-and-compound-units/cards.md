## Card 20 — true_or_false (`fl_942VZq3QyzznBw3d`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The **units** of a **compound measure** can be used to find the **equation **needed to calculate it.


**BACK**

**True.**

You **can** use the** units** of a **compound measure** to find the **equation** used to calculate it.

For example, speed is often measured in km / h. 
Km (kilometres) is a measure of distance and h (hours) is a measure of time, so the calculation for speed is:

$\mathrm{Speed}=\frac{\mathrm{Distance}}{\mathrm{Time}}$

Complete the two formulae for these compound measures.

`\text{density} = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{\text{volume}}`

`\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formulae are:

Both are 'per' measures, so density is **mass per unit of volume** and pressure is **force per unit of area**.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_Cn6Kvn89HwtfBVfZ`)

**FRONT**

What kind of speed does $\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$ actually give you?


**BACK**

It gives the **average speed** for the whole journey, not the speed at any particular moment.

A car covering $120$ km in $2$ hours has an average speed of $60 \text{km/h}$ even if it was never travelling at exactly that speed.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_SkqfnG4NVBrHkTB6`)

**FRONT**

A journey has two parts, each covered at a different speed. Why can you not find the average speed for the whole journey by averaging the two speeds?


**BACK**

Average speed is **total distance divided by total time**, and the two parts of the journey usually take different lengths of time.

Averaging the two speeds treats them as equally important, which is only correct when the two parts take exactly the same time.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_JxcKtMY5fHvrNjZB`)

**FRONT**

The density of gold is $19 . 3 \text{g/cm}^{3}$ and a gold bar has a mass of $0 . 454$ kg. What must you do before using the density formula?


**BACK**

Convert the mass into **grams**, so that it matches the units the density is given in.

Here $0 . 454 \text{kg} = 454 \text{g}$, and dividing by a density in $\text{g/cm}^{3}$ then gives a volume in $\text{cm}^{3}$.

## Card 26 — true_or_false (`fl_5qvzypV2fFwN4dwx`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A pressure of $1 \text{N/m}^{2}$ and a pressure of $1$ pascal are the same thing.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **pascal** (Pa) is simply another name for a **newton per square metre**.

So $1 \text{Pa} = 1 \text{N/m}^{2}$, and a pressure may be written either way.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_xjHd64TNQzNhDTtc`)

**FRONT**

Why can you not put an object's mass straight into $\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$?


**BACK**

The formula needs a **force**, and the force an object exerts because of gravity is its **weight**, not its mass.

Mass measures how much matter an object contains, so mass and weight are different quantities measured in different units.
