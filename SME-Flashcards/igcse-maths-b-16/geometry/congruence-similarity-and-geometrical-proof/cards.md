## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_BZYTG4cjvydYmBBD`)

**FRONT**

Define **congruent** shapes.


**BACK**

Two shapes are congruent if they are **identical in both shape and size**.

One may be a reflection, rotation or translation of the other, so they need not be facing the same way.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_BRDFgzSqWNdJtbJy`)

**FRONT**

Why is an enlargement of a shape not congruent to it?


**BACK**

Because congruence needs the two shapes to be identical in **size** as well as in shape, and an enlargement changes the size.

Reflections, rotations and translations all leave the size alone, which is why they do produce congruent images.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_Sgs6HK2grBYYBv7V`)

**FRONT**

What must you show to prove that two shapes are congruent?


**BACK**

That **corresponding sides** are the same length and that **corresponding angles** are the same size.

You do **not** have to show that the two shapes are facing the same way.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_9NrCGkKT84mV8Wnw`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two triangles with all three angles equal must be congruent.


**BACK**

**False.**

Equal angles make the triangles the same **shape** but they may be different **sizes**, so AAA is not a congruence test.

SSA, two sides and an angle that does not lie between them, is not enough either.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_nJgypCr8Q3rh4Ygt`)

**FRONT**

Why are the AAS and ASA congruence tests essentially equivalent?


**BACK**

Knowing two angles of a triangle gives you the third, because all three add up to $180 ^{\circ}$.

So any AAS situation can be rewritten as ASA as soon as the missing angle is worked out.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_KVY9fcZHfjhZgVjW`)

**FRONT**

Two triangles each have angles of $25 ^{\circ}$ and $90 ^{\circ}$, with an equal 6 cm side lying between them. Which test proves congruence?


**BACK**

**ASA**, because two angles are equal and the side **between** them is equal too.

Had the equal side been somewhere else in the triangle, this would have been AAS instead.

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_tQMwzc3BRWgxTPFC`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **similar shapes**.


**BACK**

Two shapes are **similar **if they have the **same shape** and their **corresponding sides **are in **proportion**.

One shape is an** enlargement** of the other.

What is a **scale factor**, in the context of similarity?


**BACK**

A **scale factor** is the** ratio** of **corresponding lengths** in **similar shapes**.



## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_Rmx828gGWMqSTX4G`)

In the context of similarity, a **scale factor cannot** be **negative**.


**BACK**

**True.**

Although you can have a negative scale factor in general enlargement, in the context of **similarity**, a **scale factor cannot** be **negative**.



## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_gQWSh66b8bJjSGcr`)

**FRONT**

What does a **scale factor** that is **greater than 0** but **less than 1** imply?


**BACK**

A **scale factor** that is **greater than 0** but **less than 1** implies that the similar shape is **smaller** than the original shape.



## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_pRwcR9Z7ZDPZqwF4`)

**FRONT**

How can you find a **length scale factor**?


**BACK**

A **length scale factor** can be found by dividing the **length** **of a side** on one shape by the **length** **of a corresponding side** on the similar shape.



## Card 17 — keyword_definition (`fl_NxM4zxn8fDZQKwWq`)

**FRONT**

What is a** geometrical proof**?


**BACK**

A **geometrical proof** involves using **known rules **about geometry to **prove a new statement **about geometry.



## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_9kXw8mXmD2gf8SGb`)

**FRONT**

How should **each step** in a **geometrical proof** be written?


**BACK**

**Each step** should be written in the form "**[fact], [mathematical reason]**".

It is really important to give a reason for each fact that you state.

E.g. Angle ABE = Angle CDE = 60º, vertically opposite angles.



## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_V3cqdK8jP8CKnnhX`)

**FRONT**

There are a number of different rules or facts that you can use in **geometrical proof**.

Name **three types** of rules.


**BACK**

Any of the following **types of rules** may be used in **geometrical proof**:

- Properties of 2D shapes (especially isosceles triangles and quadrilaterals)
- Basic angle properties
- Angles in polygons
- Angles in parallel lines
- Congruence and similarity
- Circle theorems
- Pythagoras' theorem



## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_PjYZS2rKFc46XkFG`)

**FRONT**

What does it mean when a **scale **for a diagram** is given as a ratio**?

E.g. a scale of 1 : 10,000.


**BACK**

When the scale is given as a ratio (e.g., 1:10,000), it means that **1 unit **on the diagram represents **10,000 of the same type of units** in real life.

E.g. 1 cm on map = 10, 000 cm (or 100 m) in real life



## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_xsgtKcGSQ3cqfpNn`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

To convert from a scale length to a real length, **multiply by the scale factor**.

E.g. 3 × 20 000 = 60 000 cm (600 m).
