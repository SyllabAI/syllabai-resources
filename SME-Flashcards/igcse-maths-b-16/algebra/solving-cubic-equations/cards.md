## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_kYHmX85N9JZsDkqf`)

**FRONT**

How many real roots can a cubic equation have?


**BACK**

Always either **one** or **three**, counting a repeated root each time it occurs.

Because a root can repeat, the number of **different** solutions may be one, two or three.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_d5GKtN9r2cjd7c3c`)

**FRONT**

A cubic has three real roots $α$, $β$ and $γ$. Fill in the two missing factors.

`a x^{3} + b x^{2} + c x + d = \left(x - \alpha\right) \left(\_\_\_\_\_\_\right) \left(\_\_\_\_\_\_\right)`


**BACK**

The completed factorisation is:

`a x^{3} + b x^{2} + c x + d = \left(x - \alpha\right) \left(x - \beta\right) \left(x - \gamma\right)`

Each real root gives one **linear factor**, so three real roots give three of them.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_xbv28q9M6FQyv5g3`)

**FRONT**

A cubic has exactly one real root. What does that tell you about its quadratic factor?


**BACK**

The quadratic factor has **no real roots**, so its discriminant $q^{2} - 4 p r$ is **negative**.

The cubic factorises as a linear factor multiplied by that quadratic, and the linear factor supplies the only real solution.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_HkfzGHMDqf62TF6c`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The real roots of a cubic equation are the $x$-intercepts of the graph of the cubic.


**BACK**

**True.**

A root is a value of $x$ that makes the expression equal zero, and the graph meets the $x$-axis exactly where its value is zero.

So a cubic with only one real root crosses the $x$-axis just once.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_9fcCYcDSpQztWw3X`)

**FRONT**

A cubic equation gives you no root to start from. How can you find one?


**BACK**

Substitute small integers such as $1$, $- 1$, $2$ and $- 2$ into the cubic until it comes out as zero.

A value that gives zero is a root, and by the factor theorem it hands you a factor to divide by.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_8kwZHwFshmBkKbYM`)

**FRONT**

You have found that $x = 2$ solves a cubic equation. What can you do next?


**BACK**

Since $x = 2$ is a root, `\left(x - 2\right)` is a factor, so take it out to leave a **quadratic factor**.

Solving that quadratic gives any remaining solutions of the cubic.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_nmyxxvr5hFTczV9N`)

**FRONT**

To factorise $2 x^{3} - x^{2} - 8 x + 4$ you write it as `\left(x - 2\right) \left(p x^{2} + q x + r\right)`. How do you find $p$ and $r$ quickly?


**BACK**

Compare the $x^{3}$ terms and the constant terms, since each involves only one unknown: $2 x^{3} = p x^{3}$ gives $p = 2$, and $4 = - 2 r$ gives $r = - 2$.

The value of $q$ then follows from comparing either the $x^{2}$ terms or the $x$ terms.
