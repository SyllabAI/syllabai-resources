## Card 1 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_CxVPXjsjWGPCwyVv`)

**FRONT**

Fill in the two missing words.

Each number in a sequence is called a `\_\_\_\_\_\_` and where it sits in the sequence is called its `\_\_\_\_\_\_` number.


**BACK**

The completed sentence is:

Each number in a sequence is called a **term** and where it sits in the sequence is called its **position** number.

The letter $n$ is used for position, so $n = 1$ gives the first term, and the subscript form $a_{n}$ means the $n$th term.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_56PsZZCxQhRR7cKj`)

**FRONT**

Define a **term-to-term rule** and a **position-to-term rule**.


**BACK**

A term-to-term rule tells you how to get from one term to the **next**, such as 'add 10 each time'.

A position-to-term rule is an expression in $n$, also called the $n$th term formula, which gives any term **directly** from its position.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_NGFpvg5vT6wVJsKw`)

**FRONT**

A sequence starts at 4 and its rule is to add 10 each time. Write down the first four terms.


**BACK**

The first four terms are $4$, $14$, $24$ and $34$.

A term-to-term rule is applied to the term you are on, so each term is 10 more than the one before it.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_wcCm33DD7xspMkNR`)

**FRONT**

Why does an $n$th term formula let you jump straight to the 100th term?


**BACK**

Because you substitute $n = 100$ directly into the formula, without needing to work out any of the earlier terms.

For the formula $8 n + 2$ the 100th term is $8 \times 100 + 2 = 802$.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_rmR7rbnjx9ZzNF9Q`)

**FRONT**

A sequence has $n$th term $8 n + 2$. How do you decide whether 98 is in the sequence?


**BACK**

Set the formula equal to the value and solve for $n$, so $8 n + 2 = 98$ gives $n = 12$.

Because $n$ comes out as a **whole number**, 98 is in the sequence, and it is the 12th term.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_dcwY738Krrd9HCfN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Solving $8 n + 2 = 124$ gives $n = 15 . 25$, so 124 is the 15th term of that sequence.


**BACK**

**False.**

A position has to be a **whole number**, so $n = 15 . 25$ means 124 is **not** in the sequence at all.

Rounding to the nearest term is not an option: a value either fits the formula exactly or it does not belong.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_qZ9wCMKvndmRFz7G`)

**FRONT**

Define an **arithmetic sequence**.


**BACK**

An arithmetic sequence is one whose terms change by the **same amount each time**, and that amount is called the **common difference**.

Arithmetic sequences are also called **linear** sequences.

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_86WkXZp5WYhkZqcJ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The sequence 11, 8, 5, 2, $- 1$, ... is an arithmetic sequence.


**BACK**

**True.**

Each term is 3 less than the one before, which is a common difference of $- 3$.

A common difference may be **negative**, so an arithmetic sequence can decrease as well as increase.

## Card 9 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_GgstHqjBjb4ZFNX8`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for the $n$th term of an arithmetic sequence.

`u_{n} = a + \left(n - \_\_\_\_\_\_\right) d`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

`u_{n} = a + \left(n - 1\right) d`

Here $a$ is the **first term** and $d$ is the **common difference**.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_VSpDhhz8tJwnm2wc`)

**FRONT**

Why does the arithmetic sequence formula use $n - 1$ rather than $n$?


**BACK**

The first term is $a$ on its own, so when $n = 1$ no common difference has been added yet.

By the $n$th term the difference has been added $n - 1$ times, and that is what the formula counts.


Spec links: `spcpt_425cHYzg3cyxG5f3`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_rXB77JdN3V9wwgGm`)

**FRONT**

An arithmetic sequence has $a = 10$ and $d = 4$. What is its third term?


**BACK**

Substituting $n = 3$ into `u_{n} = a + \left(n - 1\right) d` gives `u_{3} = 10 + \left(3 - 1\right) \times 4 = 18`.

Leaving $n$ in instead gives an expression for any term, `u_{n} = 10 + 4 \left(n - 1\right) = 4 n + 6`.


Spec links: `spcpt_425cHYzg3cyxG5f3`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_KNX3bbdmGHcnQZRJ`)

**FRONT**

The 6th term of an arithmetic sequence is 13 and the 21st term is 43. How do you find $a$ and $d$?


**BACK**

Substitute each term into `u_{n} = a + \left(n - 1\right) d` to get two equations, $a + 5 d = 13$ and $a + 20 d = 43$.

Solving those simultaneously gives $d = 2$, and substituting back gives $a = 3$.
