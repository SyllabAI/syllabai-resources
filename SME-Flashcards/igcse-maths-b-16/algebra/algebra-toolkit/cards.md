## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_qvgDS2CCwcPFmDFC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The items in the following list are all examples of **terms** that you may come across in algebra: $y,a^{2},20,7x$.


**BACK**

**True.**

$y,a^{2},20,7x$ are all examples of **terms** that you may come across in algebra.

A **term** is either:

- a letter (**variable**) on its own, e.g. $y$,
- a variable raised to a power, e.g. $a^{2}$,
- a number on its own, e.g. $20$,
- or a number **multiplied** by a letter, e.g. $7x$.



## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_3j7tn845gDm5vhvN`)

**FRONT**

In algebra, what is a **variable**?


**BACK**

A** variable** is a letter that appears in a term.

E.g. In the expression $x^{2}-2y+6$, the $x$ and $y$ are both variables.



## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_BCSKZZqj6NRZDN8z`)

**FRONT**

In algebra, what is a **constant**?


**BACK**

A** constant** is a term that is a **number only**.

There is no letter (variable) part.

E.g. In the expression $x^{2}+3x-5$, the $-5$ is the only constant term.



## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_dzNjGPYxYmq26c5n`)

**FRONT**

In algebra, what is an **expression**?


**BACK**

An **expression** in algebra is a mathematical statement that does not have an equals sign.

It can include terms that are combined using operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

E.g. $5x^{2}+y$ is an algebraic expression.



## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_Qj3nxFWVWw7BdPbF`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In the expression $4x^{3}+x^{2}-6$, the **coefficient** of the $4x^{3}$ term is $3$.


**BACK**

**False.**

In the expression $4x^{3}+x^{2}-6$, the **coefficient** of the $4x^{3}$ term is **not** $3$.

In an algebraic expression, the **coefficient** of a term is the number in front of the letter (variable).

E.g. In the expression $4x^{3}+x^{2}-6$, the coefficient of the $4x^{3}$ term is $4$.

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_ZpW5GMz8NZFyjy7f`)

**FRONT**

Define the word** formula** in algebra.


**BACK**

A** formula **in algebra is a rule, definition, or relationship between different quantities, written in shorthand using letters.

E.g. $x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}$ is the quadratic formula.



## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_qZqQwb2msgwkXqht`)

**FRONT**

**True or False? **

A **formula** on its own can be **solved**.


**BACK**

**False.**

A formula on its own** cannot** be solved.

You need to** substitute** values into the formula to turn it into an equation that can be solved.

E.g. $a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}$ cannot be solved unless you know two of the variables.



## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_dWTX5MfymNkFSYVq`)

**FRONT**

What does **substitution** mean in algebra?


**BACK**

**Substitution **in algebra** **is the process of replacing letters in a formula with their corresponding numerical values (inputs) in order to calculate an output of the formula.

E.g. If $v=3$ and $a=-5$, substituting them into the formula $a^{2}+2v=t$ would give `open parentheses negative 5 close parentheses plus 2 cross times 3 equals t`.



## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_Mw25bDqJF8wNPHc5`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

When substituting in the number $g=-3$ you should replace $g$* *with `open parentheses negative 3 close parentheses`, not $-3$.


**BACK**

**True.**

It is important to put brackets around any **negative** numbers that you substitute into an algebraic expression.

E.g. Substitute $g=-3$ into the expression  $4g^{2}$.

If you use brackets it will give you the correct answer `4 cross times open parentheses negative 3 close parentheses squared equals 4 cross times 9 equals 36`.

If you don't use brackets it will give an incorrect answer $4\times-3^{2}=4\times-9=-36$.
