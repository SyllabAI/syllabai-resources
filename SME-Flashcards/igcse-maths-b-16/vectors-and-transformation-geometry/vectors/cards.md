## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_rc9YyMy78FKpCVkk`)

**FRONT**

What does the column vector `\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}` mean?


**BACK**

It describes a movement of **6 units to the right** and **3 units up**.

A column vector used this way is also called a **translation vector**, because it says how to get from one point to another.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_Fm86z6K6fjNbcd2C`)

**FRONT**

How do you add or subtract two column vectors?


**BACK**

Work on the **top numbers** and the **bottom numbers** separately, keeping the two apart throughout.

So `\begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ - 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}`.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_3FbYT6BFstDmkDQD`)

**FRONT**

How do you write `2 \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} + 5 \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ - 1 \end{pmatrix}` as a single column vector?


**BACK**

Multiply each vector by the scalar in front of it **first**, which gives `\begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 15 \\ - 5 \end{pmatrix}`.

Then add those results component by component to get `\begin{pmatrix} 25 \\ - 1 \end{pmatrix}`.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_33FmGncCt28n9mF7`)

**FRONT**

Given that `\begin{pmatrix} 2 p - 6 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ q \end{pmatrix}`, how do you find $p$ and $q$?


**BACK**

Two column vectors are equal only when **both** components match, so each pair can be equated separately.

The top components give $2 p - 6 = 4$ and so $p = 5$, while the bottom components give $q = 9$ straight away.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_VtqYm5qfgcvNQgHT`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between $\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$ and $\overset{\rightarrow}{BA}$?


**BACK**

They have the **same length** but point in **opposite directions**.

$\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$ starts at $A$ and points towards $B$, while $\overset{\rightarrow}{BA}$ starts at $B$ and points towards $A$.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_qRxB5zWchKZv9NN2`)

**FRONT**

What does multiplying a vector by $- 2$ do to its arrow?


**BACK**

It **reverses** the direction and makes the arrow **twice** as long.

A negative scalar always reverses the direction, and it is the number after the minus sign that gives the change in length.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_XkGqDG2fW92gFKhs`)

**FRONT**

How do you draw the vector $a + b$?


**BACK**

Draw $a$, then draw $b$ starting from the point where $a$ ends.

The sum is the line from the start of $a$ to the end of $b$, and for $a - b$ you draw $- b$ second instead.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_M4KSvF52HC4Z83sv`)

**FRONT**

Why is $\overset{\rightarrow}{AB} + \overset{\rightarrow}{BC} + \overset{\rightarrow}{CA}$ the zero vector?


**BACK**

Because following those three vectors in turn takes you from $A$ all the way back to $A$.

A journey that returns to where it started has no overall movement, so the result is `\begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}`.

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_tqj5HD75SwdQB5gw`)

**FRONT**

Define the **modulus** of a vector.


**BACK**

The modulus of a vector is its **length**, also called its **magnitude**.

It is always **positive**, and the vector's direction plays no part in it.

## Card 15 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_ZrBtD9BTccgbRFrM`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for the modulus of a vector with components $x$ and $y$.

`\left|\mathbf{a}\right| = \sqrt{x^{2} + \_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

`\left|\mathbf{a}\right| = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}`

This is **Pythagoras' theorem**: the two components are the shorter sides of a right-angled triangle and the vector itself is the hypotenuse.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_6FnQRfjqR3QbVxk9`)

**FRONT**

Points `A \left(- 3 , 5\right)` and `B \left(7 , 1\right)` are given. What is `\left|\overrightarrow{AB}\right|`?


**BACK**

First `\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ - 4 \end{pmatrix}`, found by subtracting the coordinates of $A$ from those of $B$.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_4yf45M8KjTFGRVs8`)

**FRONT**

How do you show that `\begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ - 3 \end{pmatrix}` and `\begin{pmatrix} - 6 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}` are parallel?


**BACK**

**Factorise** each of them: `\begin{pmatrix} 9 \\ - 3 \end{pmatrix} = 3 \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ - 1 \end{pmatrix}` and `\begin{pmatrix} - 6 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = - 2 \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ - 1 \end{pmatrix}`.

Both turn out to be multiples of the same vector, and that is what shows they are parallel.

**FRONT**

Define a **unit vector**.


**BACK**

A unit vector is a vector whose **modulus is 1**.

It carries a direction but no size beyond that, so it is the natural way to describe a direction on its own.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_GTRVKYFSdXF2Nnt4`)

**FRONT**

Why can a unit vector have surds in its components?


**BACK**

Because the modulus you divide by is often not a whole number, and dividing by a surd leaves one behind.

The unit vector in the direction of `\begin{pmatrix} - 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}` is `\begin{pmatrix} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{29}} \\ \frac{5}{\sqrt{29}} \end{pmatrix}`, and that is an exact answer rather than an unfinished one.

## Card 31 — true_or_false (`fl_BhZ7fPM5J2V4MM9B`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If the point P **divides **the line AB in the ratio 1 : 3, then $\overset{\rightarrow}{AP}=\frac{1}{3}\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

If the point P **divides **the line AB in the ratio 1 : 3, then $\overset{\rightarrow}{AP}\neq\frac{1}{3}\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$.
