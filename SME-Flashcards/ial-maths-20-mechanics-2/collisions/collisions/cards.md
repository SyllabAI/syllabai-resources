## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_csQTkHvnzBtnc4jV`)

**FRONT**

How is impulse written for a constant force in two dimensions?


**BACK**

In exactly the same way, with the force and the impulse as **vectors**: $I = F t$, so a force `\left( F_{x}\mathbf{i} + F_{y}\mathbf{j} \right)` N acting for $t$ seconds gives `\mathbf{I} = \left( F_{x}\mathbf{i} + F_{y}\mathbf{j} \right)t`.

The units are unchanged: newton seconds, N s.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_2nfzjm6RJgjnrjhS`)

**FRONT**

A particle of mass $m$ kg has velocity $u$ before an impulse and $v$ after it. Fill in the blank to complete the impulse-momentum principle in vector form:

`\mathbf{I} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed principle is:

`\mathbf{I} = m\left( \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{u} \right)`

Subtract the velocity **vectors** first, component by component, and only then multiply by the mass.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_Kr85wbJ8fQy9rFv4`)

**FRONT**

A 0.8 kg ball's velocity changes from `\left( 2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j} \right)` to `\left( 5\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} \right)\text{ m s}^{-1}`. Find the magnitude of the impulse it received.


**BACK**

Take the change in velocity and multiply by the mass:

`\mathbf{I} = 0.8\left( 3\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j} \right) = \left( 2.4\mathbf{i} - 3.2\mathbf{j} \right)\text{ N s}`

Its magnitude is $\sqrt{2.4^{2}+3.2^{2}} = 4 \text{N s}$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_M6b979JCtvtXznPm`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the direction of an impulse vector, and what must you check in the question first?


**BACK**

Find its two components, sketch them as the sides of a right-angled triangle, and use trigonometry on that triangle.

Check **where the angle is measured from**, since a question may ask for the angle with $i$, with $j$, or as a bearing, and the three answers differ.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_9KvVYVqt6dgWqppD`)

**FRONT**

An impulse is `\left( 2.4\mathbf{i} - 3.2\mathbf{j} \right)` N s. Find the angle between it and the vector $j$.


**BACK**

Sketching it shows the impulse pointing right and **downwards**, so the angle from $j$ must be more than $90 ^{\circ}$.

The angle below the horizontal is `\tan^{-1}\left( \frac{3.2}{2.4} \right) = 53.1^{\circ}`, so the angle from $j$ is $90 ^{\circ} + 53 . 1 ^{\circ} = 143 ^{\circ}$ to 3 significant figures.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_364xKTg8bCj5DHj4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If two particles collide directly in two dimensions, their velocity vectors are scalar multiples of one another.


**BACK**

**True.**

A **direct** collision means both particles travel along the same straight line, so every velocity vector points along that one line and can differ only by a scalar factor.

The problem then reduces to conservation of momentum worked exactly as it would be in one dimension.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_BgyS45NR9q5xhR8Y`)

**FRONT**

After two objects collide, what else might one of them hit, and how do you handle it?


**BACK**

It may go on to hit a **third object**, or a **wall** perpendicular to its motion.

Deal with **each collision separately**, applying the usual before-and-after method to one collision at a time and carrying its results forward into the next.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_Scd97X37WzWpTqGP`)

**FRONT**

Balls A, B and C lie on one line, A hits B, and then B hits C. What are the three ways A and B can collide a second time?


**BACK**

One of them is **stationary** and the other is travelling towards it.

Both are travelling in **opposite directions**, towards each other.

Both are travelling in the **same direction**, and the one in front is the slower of the two.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZxGWhV43Pz2gqQyH`)

**FRONT**

After bouncing off a wall, when does a ball catch the one ahead of it?


**BACK**

When its velocity in that direction is **greater** than the other ball's velocity in the same direction.

The wall reverses the ball's direction, so it is now travelling back towards the other one, but it only reaches it if it is also gaining on it.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_dpgjBq6zHkz9NBs2`)

**FRONT**

To find a ball's speed after it hits a wall, what extra information will the question give you?


**BACK**

Either the **impulse** the wall exerted on the ball, or the **coefficient of restitution** between the ball and the wall.

One or the other is needed because the wall does not move, so there is no second velocity to put into a conservation of momentum equation.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_bRWJ6SrYqM2BYCyH`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A chain of collisions needs one equation for each collision, rather than a single equation for the whole chain.


**BACK**

**True.**

Each collision introduces new unknown velocities, so it has to be solved on its own with a before-and-after diagram before you move on to the next one.

Working the collisions in order is also what tells you which objects are where, and so which of them can meet again.

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_BDR5XS6nKqmSryk6`)

**FRONT**

Define the **coefficient of restitution**.


**BACK**

The **coefficient of restitution**, written $e$, measures how bouncy a collision between two particular objects is, and is also known as **Newton's law of restitution**.

It is a ratio, so it is **dimensionless**, and its value depends on the materials the two objects are made from.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_s9Zdc8cwwg7F92gt`)

**FRONT**

What range of values can $e$ take, and what happens at each end of it?


**BACK**

$0 \leq e \leq 1$.

At $e = 1$ the collision is **perfectly elastic** and no kinetic energy is lost.

At $e = 0$ it is **perfectly inelastic**, and the two objects coalesce and move off together.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_Kdxq4Jn2mgtykDGs`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two objects moving the same way at $5$ and $2 \text{m s}^{-1}$ have a speed of approach of $7 \text{m s}^{-1}$.


**BACK**

**False.**

Moving in the same direction they close on each other by $5 - 2 = 3$ metres every second, so the speed of approach is $3 \text{m s}^{-1}$.

It would be $5 + 2 = 7 \text{m s}^{-1}$ only if they were travelling in **opposite** directions.

## Card 17 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_VHTxqy6tXtnRPFDR`)

**FRONT**

Two objects have velocities $u_{1}$ and $u_{2}$ before a collision and $v_{1}$ and $v_{2}$ after it. Fill in the blanks to complete the coefficient of restitution:

`e = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$e = \frac{v_{2}-v_{1}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}$

In words, that is the **speed of separation** divided by the **speed of approach**; the velocities may be negative, so watch the signs rather than trusting the order of the letters.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_pqK3M8x6hBm9c8M7`)

**FRONT**

How does the formula simplify when an object bounces off a wall?


**BACK**

The wall has no velocity of its own, so the ratio becomes the **speed of rebound** divided by the **speed of approach**.

For an object arriving at $u$ and leaving at $v$ that is simply $e = \frac{v}{u}$.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_kQ6NC4YdcqBs4PTZ`)

**FRONT**

A (2 kg) at $9 \text{m s}^{-1}$ and B (5 kg) at $1 \text{m s}^{-1}$ move the same way and collide, with $e = \frac{3}{4}$. Find their speeds afterwards.


**BACK**

Restitution gives the speed of separation as `\frac{3}{4} \times \left( 9 - 1 \right) = 6`, so $v_{2} - v_{1} = 6$, and momentum gives $2 \times 9 + 5 \times 1 = 2 v_{1} + 5 v_{2}$.

Solving the pair gives $v_{1} = - 1$ and $v_{2} = 5$.

So A has reversed and moves back at $1 \text{m s}^{-1}$, while B goes on at $5 \text{m s}^{-1}$.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_j7Nqxq6PwSC48dWY`)

**FRONT**

A question leaves a velocity in terms of $e$, such as $v = 5 e - 1$. How does that give you a range of possible values of $e$?


**BACK**

Use what the question tells you about the **direction** of travel: if that object must be moving forwards its velocity is positive, so $5 e - 1 > 0$ and therefore $e > \frac{1}{5}$.

Combine that with $0 \leq e \leq 1$, which always holds, to get the full range: $\frac{1}{5} < e \leq 1$.
