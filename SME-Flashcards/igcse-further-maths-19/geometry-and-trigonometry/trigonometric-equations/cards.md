## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_KJxymnmPCXT4VFxk`)

**FRONT**

Why does a trigonometric equation have infinitely many solutions?


**BACK**

Because the functions are **periodic**, so the same value recurs at regular intervals for ever.

A question therefore always supplies an **interval**, and you must find every solution inside it and none outside.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_sK7RhbrQ8vjdDrxS`)

**FRONT**

Once you have the first two solutions, how do you find the others?


**BACK**

Add or subtract whole multiples of $360 ^{\circ}$ to each of them, or $2 π$ if you are working in radians.

Keep going until you leave the given interval, and use $180 ^{\circ}$ instead for $\mathrm{tan}$, whose period is shorter.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_9H7vpV6GsNMR4Dcw`)

**FRONT**

Solve $2 \mathrm{cos} x = - 1$ for $- 2 π \leq x \leq 2 π$.


**BACK**

Isolating gives $\mathrm{cos} x = - \frac{1}{2}$, with a primary value of $\frac{2π}{3}$ and a secondary value of $- \frac{2π}{3}$.

Adding and subtracting $2 π$ brings in $- \frac{4π}{3}$ and $\frac{4π}{3}$, so there are four solutions altogether.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_VxYggjTp7vDPgvnT`)

**FRONT**

How do you solve an equation such as `\cos\left(2 x - 30^{\circ}\right) = k`?


**BACK**

Substitute $u = 2 x - 30 ^{\circ}$ and solve for $u$ instead, which turns it into a basic equation.

**Transform the interval in the same way**, so that you are looking for $u$ over the right range.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_NvhCDP6JzT6Yj7WJ`)

**FRONT**

Why must the interval be transformed as well as the equation?


**BACK**

Because $u$ runs over a different range from $x$, and usually a far wider one.

With $u = 2 x - 30$ and $- 360 ^{\circ} \leq x \leq 360 ^{\circ}$, the new range is $- 750 ^{\circ} \leq u \leq 690 ^{\circ}$, which holds many more solutions.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_PNRm7hYxFT7dngB3`)

**True or False?**

After substituting $u = 2 x - 30 ^{\circ}$, the answers you find are the values of $x$.


**BACK**

**False.**

They are values of $u$, and each one has to be converted back using $x = \frac{u+30}{2}$.

Stopping at $u$ answers a different question from the one that was asked.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_JsNsxcNvkBfjdBZd`)

**FRONT**

Define **quadratic trigonometric equation**.


**BACK**

A **quadratic trigonometric equation** is one containing a squared trigonometric term such as $\mathrm{sin}^{2} x$, $\mathrm{cos}^{2} x$ or $\mathrm{tan}^{2} x$.

It is solved like any other quadratic, treating the trigonometric function itself as the variable.


Spec links: `spcpt_bJnFGBG2HpXdCzhm`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_zzKjYj8WrNr5sr2p`)

**FRONT**

What do you do when an equation contains both $\mathrm{sin}^{2} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$?


**BACK**

Use the Pythagorean identity to replace the squared term, so that only one function is left.

Here $\mathrm{sin}^{2} x = 1 - \mathrm{cos}^{2} x$ turns it into a quadratic entirely in $\mathrm{cos} x$.


Spec links: `spcpt_bJnFGBG2HpXdCzhm`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_TCPTS4zMRZFxpjzB`)

**FRONT**

How can a single letter make a quadratic trigonometric equation easier?


**BACK**

Write $s$ for $\mathrm{sin} x$, so that $5 \mathrm{sin}^{2} x + 11 \mathrm{sin} x - 12 = 0$ becomes $5 s^{2} + 11 s - 12 = 0$.

Factorising or the quadratic formula then works exactly as usual, and you put $\mathrm{sin} x$ back afterwards.


Spec links: `spcpt_bJnFGBG2HpXdCzhm`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_htJvQDWbF6QsqQpq`)

**FRONT**

Why must you check each solution of the quadratic before going on?


**BACK**

Because $\mathrm{sin} x = k$ and $\mathrm{cos} x = k$ only have solutions when $- 1 \leq k \leq 1$.

So a perfectly good root of the quadratic, such as $\mathrm{sin} x = - 3$, produces no angles at all and is discarded.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_Cy9RCbKQTdbcbyNx`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\mathrm{tan} x = 5$ has solutions, even though $5$ is greater than $1$.


**BACK**

**True.**

The tangent graph is unbounded, so $\mathrm{tan} x = k$ has solutions for **every** real value of $k$.

That is the real difference between the three functions, and it is why a $\mathrm{tan}$ root is never rejected for being too large.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_CSq75xtKfqG3nzvt`)

**FRONT**

Solve $11 \mathrm{sin} x - 7 = 5 \mathrm{cos}^{2} x$ for $0 \leq x \leq 2 π$.


**BACK**

Replacing $\mathrm{cos}^{2} x$ with $1 - \mathrm{sin}^{2} x$ gives $5 \mathrm{sin}^{2} x + 11 \mathrm{sin} x - 12 = 0$, which factorises as `\left(5 \sin x - 4\right)\left(\sin x + 3\right) = 0`.

Discarding $\mathrm{sin} x = - 3$ leaves $\mathrm{sin} x = \frac{4}{5}$, so $x = 0 . 927$ or $2 . 21$ to three significant figures.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_CvTqRmG6rtmJfYNz`)

**FRONT**

How many trigonometric solutions can one quadratic produce?


**BACK**

Often **more than two**, since each valid value of the function can give several angles within the interval.

Sketching the relevant graph is the quickest way to check that you have found them all.
