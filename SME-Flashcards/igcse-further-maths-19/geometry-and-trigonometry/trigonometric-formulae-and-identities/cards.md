## Card 1 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_Gv2ZBM5FgzNbX6J3`)

**FRONT**

Complete the sine rule for any triangle:

`\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\_\_\_\_\_\_} = \frac{c}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

$\frac{a}{\mathrm{sin}A} = \frac{b}{\mathrm{sin}B} = \frac{c}{\mathrm{sin}C}$

Any two of the three fractions can be used at a time; you never need all three at once.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_GQJdHnF6T8K35pmB`)

**FRONT**

When can you use the sine rule?


**BACK**

Whenever you have a matching **pair**, a side together with the angle opposite it, plus one further side or angle.

To find an angle it is easier to use the flipped form $\frac{\mathrm{sin}A}{a} = \frac{\mathrm{sin}B}{b}$.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_D2k2BxfB6qcZTfc9`)

**FRONT**

Why might the sine rule give you the wrong angle?


**BACK**

Because inverse sine on a calculator always returns an **acute** angle.

If the diagram shows the angle is obtuse, subtract the calculator's answer from $180 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_R5txcntz5NY6svjX`)

**FRONT**

How should you label a triangle before using either rule?


**BACK**

Capital letters for the angles, and the matching lower-case letter for the side **opposite** each one.

Both rules depend on that pairing, so mislabelling is what makes them produce nonsense.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_m6T4r43zQ4bc5kFq`)

**FRONT**

When do you use the cosine rule rather than the sine rule?


**BACK**

When you have **two sides and the angle between them**, or **all three sides**, and so no matching pair to work with.

It states $a^{2} = b^{2} + c^{2} - 2 b c \mathrm{cos} A$, with $A$ the angle sitting between $b$ and $c$.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_6hQrBCM6S9mDrH6w`)

**FRONT**

How do you rearrange the cosine rule to find an angle?


**BACK**

Make $\mathrm{cos} A$ the subject, giving $\mathrm{cos} A = \frac{b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2}}{2bc}$.

Here $a$ must be the side **opposite** the angle you want, with $b$ and $c$ the two sides enclosing it.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_B2chQ3WXQrygPv8Q`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Knowing all three sides of a triangle is enough to find every angle.


**BACK**

**True.**

The cosine rule, rearranged, finds any angle from the three side lengths alone.

Once one angle is known the sine rule gives the others, so three sides fix the triangle completely.

## Card 8 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_fyf5VJQtCMyQTqNM`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for the area of any triangle:

`\text{Area} = \_\_\_\_\_\_ a b \sin \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} a b \mathrm{sin} C$

The angle $C$ must be the one **between** the two sides $a$ and $b$, which is the condition most often missed.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_zv9fmVbHCWNjV9QX`)

**FRONT**

What do the cosine rule and the area formula become when the angle is $90 ^{\circ}$?


**BACK**

Since $\mathrm{cos} 90 ^{\circ} = 0$, the cosine rule collapses to $a^{2} = b^{2} + c^{2}$, which is Pythagoras.

Since $\mathrm{sin} 90 ^{\circ} = 1$, the area formula becomes $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_8DKQ2Kvwm3964XSF`)

**FRONT**

Define **trigonometric identity**.


**BACK**

A **trigonometric identity** is a statement about $\mathrm{sin}$, $\mathrm{cos}$ or $\mathrm{tan}$ that is true for **every** value of the angle.

The symbol $\equiv$ is sometimes used in place of an equals sign to stress that it always holds.

## Card 11 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_gBfV4gSk6xfBjz5n`)

**FRONT**

Complete the Pythagorean identity:

`\sin^{2} \theta + \_\_\_\_\_\_ = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed identity is:

$\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ + \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ = 1$

Note that $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ$ means `\left(\sin \theta\right)^{2}`, the value squared, and not the sine of $θ^{2}$.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_hVpJVZvgS2SCVvwt`)

**FRONT**

What are the two useful rearrangements of $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ + \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ = 1$?


**BACK**

They are $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ = 1 - \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ$ and $\mathrm{cos}^{2} θ = 1 - \mathrm{sin}^{2} θ$.

Each lets you swap one squared function for the other, which is how an equation containing both is reduced to one.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_N6YfRhCZKfmVb39k`)

**FRONT**

What is $\mathrm{tan} θ$ in terms of $\mathrm{sin} θ$ and $\mathrm{cos} θ$?


**BACK**

It is the quotient $\mathrm{tan} θ = \frac{\mathrm{sin}θ}{\mathrm{cos}θ}$.

This comes straight from the unit circle, where $\mathrm{cos} θ$ and $\mathrm{sin} θ$ are the two coordinates and $\mathrm{tan} θ$ is their ratio.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_s6T7wYjKYtJtdjnM`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The identity $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ + \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ = 1$ holds for obtuse and negative angles too.


**BACK**

**True.**

It holds for **every** angle, which is exactly what makes it an identity rather than an equation.

On the unit circle it is Pythagoras applied to the point `\left(\cos \theta , \sin \theta\right)`, and that works whichever quadrant the point is in.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_gPr3y23rKsFyn9MQ`)

**FRONT**

How do you turn $2 \mathrm{sin}^{2} θ - \mathrm{cos} θ = 0$ into an equation in $\mathrm{cos} θ$ only?


**BACK**

Replace $\mathrm{sin}^{2} θ$ with $1 - \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ$, which gives $2 - 2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ - \mathrm{cos} θ = 0$.

Multiplying through by $- 1$ to make the squared term positive leaves $2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} θ + \mathrm{cos} θ - 2 = 0$.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_Sm5FqVYjFJzCBnXm`)

**FRONT**

How do the plus and minus signs work in the addition formulae?


**BACK**

For $\mathrm{sin}$ the sign on the right **matches** the one on the left, and for $\mathrm{cos}$ it is the **opposite**.

For $\mathrm{tan}$ the numerator matches and the denominator is opposite, so `\tan\left(A + B\right) = \frac{\tan A + \tan B}{1 - \tan A \tan B}`.

## Card 17 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_3ghxZw2DscrZVcqy`)

**FRONT**

Complete the double angle formula for sine:

`\sin 2 A = \_\_\_\_\_\_ \sin A \_\_\_\_\_\_ A`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$\mathrm{sin} 2 A = 2 \mathrm{sin} A \mathrm{cos} A$

The extra $\mathrm{cos} A$ is what catches people out: doubling the angle is not the same as doubling the function.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_ycqxCNfdXP2NZSxt`)

**FRONT**

What are the three forms of the double angle formula for $\mathrm{cos} 2 A$?


**BACK**

They are $\mathrm{cos} 2 A = \mathrm{cos}^{2} A - \mathrm{sin}^{2} A = 1 - 2 \mathrm{sin}^{2} A = 2 \mathrm{cos}^{2} A - 1$.

The last two come from swapping one squared term using the Pythagorean identity, and you pick whichever leaves the function you want.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_v3h2JMZFdPFZ25Fg`)

**FRONT**

What is the double angle formula for $\mathrm{tan} 2 A$?


**BACK**

It is $\mathrm{tan} 2 A = \frac{2\mathrm{tan}A}{1-\mathrm{tan}^{2}A}$.

Note the minus sign in the denominator and the $\mathrm{tan}^{2} A$: it is not simply twice $\mathrm{tan} A$.

## Card 20 — true_or_false (`fl_smcVbYYBbxkbssDT`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The double angle formulae have to be remembered separately from the addition formulae.


**BACK**

**False.**

Each one is just the addition formula with $B$ set equal to $A$.

Deriving them that way takes a single line and removes three more formulae from what you have to hold in your head.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_kwRhjwDqzR8ydD8k`)

**FRONT**

How can the addition formulae give the exact value of $\mathrm{sin} 15 ^{\circ}$?


**BACK**

Write $15 ^{\circ}$ as $45 ^{\circ} - 30 ^{\circ}$ and apply the formula for `\sin\left(A - B\right)`.

Every term is then an exact value you already know, so the answer comes out in surds with no calculator needed.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_42D7KPgsWPc9TfCk`)

**FRONT**

Simplify `\tan\left(x + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)` using the addition formula.


**BACK**

Since $\mathrm{tan} \frac{π}{4} = 1$, the formula gives $\frac{\mathrm{tan}x+1}{1-\mathrm{tan}x}$.

Spotting that $\mathrm{tan} \frac{π}{4} = 1$ is what makes the expression collapse so neatly.
