## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_C9rXy4MVCtWMxKxF`)

**FRONT**

What are the three SOHCAHTOA ratios?


**BACK**

Measured against a chosen angle $θ$:

- $\mathrm{sin} θ = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\mathrm{cos} θ = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
- $\mathrm{tan} θ = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$

They work in a **right-angled** triangle only.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_GJr7TDjDRYsmnydh`)

**FRONT**

How do you label the sides of a right-angled triangle before using SOHCAHTOA?


**BACK**

Pick your angle $θ$ first: the side facing it is the **opposite**, and the side between $θ$ and the right angle is the **adjacent**.

Getting those two the wrong way round is what turns a $\mathrm{sin}$ question into a $\mathrm{cos}$ one.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_WWFH6BPXmKGx7JwF`)

**FRONT**

How can Pythagoras' theorem show that a triangle is right-angled?


**BACK**

Test whether $c^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}$ holds for the three side lengths, taking $c$ as the longest.

If it does, the triangle is right-angled; if it does not, the triangle is not.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The hypotenuse is the side opposite the angle you are working with.


**BACK**

**False.**

The hypotenuse is fixed by the **right angle**, not by whichever angle you happen to be using.

It stays the longest side, opposite the right angle, whichever of the other two angles you choose.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_vw37CHY5BhxPXJMj`)

**FRONT**

What is the 3D version of Pythagoras' theorem?


**BACK**

$l^{2} = x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2}$, where $x$, $y$ and $z$ are the distances in the three directions.

In practice it is usually easier to break the solid into two right-angled triangles and apply the ordinary theorem twice.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_Bt6M96nnqdDRHrzZ`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the angle between a line and a plane?


**BACK**

Drop a perpendicular from a point on the line down onto the plane, which creates a right-angled triangle.

The angle wanted is the one between the line and its **shadow** on the plane, and SOHCAHTOA then finds it.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_74V6krgNYBTxftZp`)

**FRONT**

Define **unit circle**.


**BACK**

The **unit circle** is the circle of radius $1$ centred on the origin.

It extends $\mathrm{sin}$, $\mathrm{cos}$ and $\mathrm{tan}$ to angles of any size, including those beyond $360 ^{\circ}$ and negative ones.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_v5mdRrmmrsxQc7QM`)

**FRONT**

On the unit circle, what do the $x$ and $y$ coordinates give?


**BACK**

The $x$ coordinate is $\mathrm{cos} θ$ and the $y$ coordinate is $\mathrm{sin} θ$, with $θ$ measured from the positive $x$-axis.

Dividing $y$ by $x$ gives $\mathrm{tan} θ$, which is also the **gradient** of the line from the origin to that point.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_jHmK2j8zpZZY9sgr`)

**FRONT**

Which way round are positive and negative angles measured?


**BACK**

**Anticlockwise** from the positive $x$-axis for a positive angle, and **clockwise** for a negative one.

Going past a full turn simply continues, so $420 ^{\circ}$ is one complete revolution and then another $60 ^{\circ}$.

**BACK**

So in the second quadrant only $\mathrm{sin} θ$ is positive, and in the third only $\mathrm{tan} θ$ is.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_9MGSbmfTG72BpBZ8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\mathrm{sin} 210 ^{\circ}$ and $\mathrm{sin} 330 ^{\circ}$ have the same value.


**BACK**

**True.**

Both equal $- \frac{1}{2}$: each is a $30 ^{\circ}$ angle measured from the horizontal, and sine is negative in the third and fourth quadrants alike.

The values match even though the two angles sit in different quadrants.

**BACK**

The completed periods are:

$\mathrm{sin} x \text{and} \mathrm{cos} x \text{repeat every} 360 ^{\circ} \text{, but} \mathrm{tan} x \text{repeats every} 180 ^{\circ}$

In radians those are $2 π$ and $π$, and the shorter period is why $\mathrm{tan}$ gives twice as many solutions in the same interval.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_MYZB5V29mKhXnjsK`)

**FRONT**

What is the range of $\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$?


**BACK**

It is $- 1 \leq y \leq 1$, so neither can ever rise above $1$ or fall below $- 1$.

That is why an equation such as $\mathrm{sin} x = 2$ has no solutions at all.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_ypkhg38XCzkDqj2W`)

**FRONT**

Where is $\mathrm{tan} x$ undefined, and what is its range?


**BACK**

It is undefined at $\pm 90 ^{\circ}$, $\pm 270 ^{\circ}$ and so on, where it has **vertical asymptotes**.

Its range is every real number, so unlike $\mathrm{sin}$ and $\mathrm{cos}$ it is not bounded above or below.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_7NQP4Zt3ZmmJ4KBJ`)

**FRONT**

How are the graphs of $\mathrm{sin} x$ and $\mathrm{cos} x$ related?


**BACK**

They are **translations** of one another: shifting $\mathrm{sin} x$ left by $90 ^{\circ}$ gives $\mathrm{cos} x$.

That is why $\mathrm{sin} x$ passes through the origin while $\mathrm{cos} x$ passes through `\left(0 , 1\right)`.

A calculator gives you every solution of $\mathrm{sin} x = 0 . 5$ in a given interval.


**BACK**

**False.**

How does a sketch tell you how many solutions $\mathrm{sin} x = k$ has?


**BACK**

Draw the horizontal line $y = k$ across the sketch and count where it **crosses** the curve.

The number of crossings inside the given interval is the number of solutions you should finish with.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_WSBK9xTvZ82kpNKm`)

**FRONT**

What two symmetry properties does the graph of $\mathrm{sin} x$ have?


**BACK**

It has **rotational symmetry** about the origin, so `\sin\left(- x\right) = - \sin x`.

It is also symmetric about $x = 90 ^{\circ}$, so `\sin x = \sin\left(180^{\circ} - x\right)`.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_WDMrH82vytxJS3BF`)

**FRONT**

What two symmetry properties does the graph of $\mathrm{cos} x$ have?


**BACK**

It is **symmetric about the vertical axis**, so `\cos\left(- x\right) = \cos x`.

It also repeats about a full turn, so `\cos x = \cos\left(360^{\circ} - x\right)`.
