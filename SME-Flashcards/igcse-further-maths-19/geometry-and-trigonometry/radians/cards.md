## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_y9zHdKbfFQGjGzND`)

**FRONT**

Define **radian**.


**BACK**

One **radian** is the angle at the centre of a circle sector whose arc length is equal to its radius.

Since the whole circumference is $2 π r$, a full turn is $2 π$ radians.

## Card 2 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_h2FDR28bb4VvZBTK`)

**FRONT**

Complete the two basic radian equivalents:

`2 \pi \text{ radians} = \_\_\_\_\_\_^{\circ} \text{ and } \pi \text{ radians} = \_\_\_\_\_\_^{\circ}`


**BACK**

The completed equivalents are:

$2 π \text{radians} = 360 ^{\circ} \text{and} π \text{radians} = 180 ^{\circ}$

Everything else follows from these, so $\frac{π}{2}$ is $90 ^{\circ}$ and $\frac{π}{6}$ is $30 ^{\circ}$.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_JWNPHdzvQdVPpknq`)

**FRONT**

How do you convert between degrees and radians?


**BACK**

Multiply by $\frac{π}{180}$ to go from degrees to radians, and by $\frac{180}{π}$ to go the other way.

Each is just $π \text{radians} = 180 ^{\circ}$ rearranged, so that one fact is all you need to remember.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_PRzvXF3Z68nx3Mmq`)

**FRONT**

Convert $\frac{5π}{4}$ radians to degrees.


**BACK**

Multiply by $\frac{180}{π}$, giving $\frac{5π}{4} \times \frac{180}{π} = \frac{5}{4} \times 180 = 225 ^{\circ}$.

Recognising that $\frac{π}{4}$ is $45 ^{\circ}$ gets there faster, since $5 \times 45 = 225$.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_55xwBKnYgqYhmpGr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An angle in radians must always be written with a unit symbol.


**BACK**

**False.**

When $π$ appears in the angle it is obviously in radians, so no symbol is needed; otherwise **rad** is usually written.

The degree symbol is different: that must never be left off.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_NqxS5Yfzg4SrtKCg`)

**FRONT**

When must you work in radians rather than degrees?


**BACK**

Whenever calculus is being done with trigonometric functions.

The derivative and integral results for $\mathrm{sin}$ and $\mathrm{cos}$ only hold when the angle is measured in radians.

## Card 7 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_mFFxTvPn7dDcZ74s`)

**FRONT**

For $θ$ in radians, complete the arc length and sector area formulas:

`l = \_\_\_\_\_\_ \theta \text{ and } A = \frac{1}{2} \_\_\_\_\_\_ \theta`


**BACK**

The completed formulas are:

$l = r θ \text{and} A = \frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$

The area carries $r^{2}$ because an area is two-dimensional, while an arc is a length and needs only one factor of $r$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_zHwkkwGvWJQk9M2B`)

**FRONT**

What are the arc length and sector area formulas when $θ$ is measured in degrees?


**BACK**

They are $l = \frac{θ}{360} \times 2 π r$ and $A = \frac{θ}{360} \times π r^{2}$.

Each is simply a **fraction of the whole circle**, that fraction being the angle divided by $360$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_kTzjTMPfYCgkNhdr`)

**FRONT**

Why are the radian versions of these formulas so much simpler?


**BACK**

Because $π$ is already built into the radian measure, so no conversion factor is needed at all.

A radian is defined so that the arc length simply **is** $r θ$, with nothing left to scale.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_8FVBJ85Tq5TsBFcF`)

**FRONT**

Define **minor sector** and **major sector**.


**BACK**

A **minor sector** has an angle at the centre of less than $180 ^{\circ}$, and a **major sector** one of more than $180 ^{\circ}$.

The same words describe the arcs they cut off, so the minor arc is the shorter of the two.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_gYQtMmwgmJhDr7YT`)

**FRONT**

What makes up the perimeter of a sector?


**BACK**

The **arc** plus the **two radii**, so for $θ$ in radians it is $r θ + 2 r$.

Forgetting the two straight edges is the commonest slip here: an arc length on its own is not a perimeter.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_w7rGKm6hS6H2Kfh2`)

**FRONT**

Find the exact perimeter of a sector of radius $7$ with angle $\frac{π}{6}$ radians.


**BACK**

The arc measures $7 \times \frac{π}{6} = \frac{7π}{6}$, and the two radii add another $14$.

So the exact perimeter is $\frac{7π}{6} + 14$, left in terms of $π$ rather than rounded.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_QHr5wcw7Yr3KhGzK`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Doubling the radius of a sector doubles its arc length but quadruples its area.


**BACK**

**True.**

The arc length $r θ$ has a single factor of $r$, so it doubles, while the area $\frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$ has $r$ squared, so it grows four times.

That is the ordinary difference between a length and an area, and it holds for any fixed angle.
