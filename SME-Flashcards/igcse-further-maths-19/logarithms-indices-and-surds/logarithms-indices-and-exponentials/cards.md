## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_MKdpf7366zdnFZmx`)

**FRONT**

Why does the graph of $y = a^{x}$ never cross the $x$-axis?


**BACK**

Because a positive number raised to **any** power is still positive, so $a^{x}$ can get very close to zero but never reaches it.

The $x$-axis is therefore a **horizontal asymptote**, and the curve has no maximum or minimum point.

**BACK**

$\text{e}$ is the irrational constant $2 . 718281 \dots$, and `\text{f}\left(x\right) = \text{e}^{x}` is known as **the exponential function**.

Because $\text{e}$ is irrational its decimal expansion never ends or repeats, so exact answers are left in terms of $\text{e}$ rather than rounded.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_wKSrvn4rBgVbWVXk`)

**FRONT**

What is special about the gradient of the curve $y = \text{e}^{x}$?


**BACK**

At every point the gradient is **equal to the** $y$ **value at that point**, so $\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = \text{e}^{x}$.

No other function of the form $a^{x}$ behaves this way, and it is the reason $\text{e}$ is chosen as the base.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_PCTvrm3H6pPTSSD8`)

**FRONT**

How is the graph of $y = \text{e}^{-x}$ related to the graph of $y = \text{e}^{x}$?


**BACK**

$y = \text{e}^{-x}$ is the **reflection** of $y = \text{e}^{x}$ in the $y$-axis.

By the laws of indices `\text{e}^{- x} = \left(\frac{1}{\text{e}}\right)^{x}`, so it is a decreasing exponential whose base $\frac{1}{\text{e}}$ lies between $0$ and $1$.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_vPNSV3DSSQgwxBzj`)

**FRONT**

How are the graphs of $y = a^{x}$ and $y = \mathrm{log}_{a} x$ related?


**BACK**

Each is the **reflection** of the other in the line $y = x$, because $a^{x}$ and $\mathrm{log}_{a} x$ are **inverse functions**.

Reflecting in $y = x$ swaps every $x$ coordinate with its $y$ coordinate, which is why an asymptote running along one axis becomes an asymptote running along the other.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_8CwZh8yNCkxcR2Kh`)

**FRONT**

What does a fractional index such as $a^{\frac{m}{n}}$ mean?


**BACK**

The denominator gives a **root** and the numerator gives a **power**: `a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a^{m}} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{m}`.

Both orders give the same answer, so take the root first when that keeps the numbers smaller.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_bJQM4FwyGjYHdDVb`)

**FRONT**

Why is $a^{0}$ equal to $1$ for any non-zero $a$?


**BACK**

Because dividing a power by itself gives $1$, while the division law makes that same calculation $a^{m-m} = a^{0}$.

The two answers must agree, so $a^{0} = 1$; the case $a = 0$ has to be excluded because $0^{0}$ has no value.

**FRONT**

How can the power $k$ be moved out of $\mathrm{log}_{a} x^{k}$?


**BACK**

The power becomes a multiplier in front of the logarithm: $\mathrm{log}_{a} x^{k} = k \mathrm{log}_{a} x$.

## Card 39 — question_and_answer (`fl_tzgzkQFYgRZcdsMz`)
