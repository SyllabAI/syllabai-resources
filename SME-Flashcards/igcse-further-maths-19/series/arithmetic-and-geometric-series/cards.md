## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_nXrsWB3hRkC4qH37`)

**FRONT**

Define **sigma notation**.


**BACK**

**Sigma notation** uses the capital Greek letter $Σ$ to mean 'the sum of'.

The expression to its right says **what** is being added, and the limits below and above say **which** terms to include.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_Qq9wq9z5nyDD9NGw`)

**FRONT**

What does `\sum_{k = 1}^{6}\left(2 \times 3^{k - 1}\right)` mean?


**BACK**

Work out $2 \times 3^{k-1}$ for $k = 1$, then $k = 2$, and so on up to $k = 6$, and add all six results.

It is the sum of the **first six terms** of that series, written compactly.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The same sum can be written in sigma notation in more than one way.


**BACK**

**True.**

It can be left as `\sum_{k = 1}^{6} u_{k}` or written out in full as `\sum_{k = 1}^{6}\left(2 \times 3^{k - 1}\right)`.

The counter letter is free as well, so $r$ would do the same job as $k$.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_RcGnzkRMvqtthBmh`)

**FRONT**

Why is the letter changed from $n$ to $k$ inside a sigma sum?


**BACK**

Because $k$ is the **counter** that runs through the limits, while $n$ is usually already being used for the number of terms.

Keeping them apart makes `\sum_{k = 1}^{n}` readable: $k$ moves, and $n$ stays fixed.


Spec links: `spcpt_swdRnhvMhXTQXpzj`


## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_XWjH3SgCZRTFbJWJ`)

**FRONT**

How would you write the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric series in sigma notation?


**BACK**

As `\sum_{k = 1}^{n} a r^{k - 1}`, putting the $n$th term formula inside with $k$ as the counter.

An arithmetic series works the same way, as `\sum_{k = 1}^{n}\left(a + \left(k - 1\right) d\right)`.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_rydQ96n36Rn4KMxV`)

**FRONT**

Define **arithmetic sequence**.


**BACK**

An **arithmetic sequence** has a **common difference** $d$ which is added to each term to get the next one.

The first term is called $a$, so $1 , 4 , 7 , 10 , \dots$ has $a = 1$ and $d = 3$.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_vbmXb6WytGDBfkGn`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a sequence and a series?


**BACK**

The **terms** are exactly the same in both; a **series** is what you get when those terms are added together.

So $1 , 4 , 7 , 10$ is a sequence, while $1 + 4 + 7 + 10$ is the matching series.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_cgKc9sXGq6rxrqrB`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

An arithmetic sequence must increase.


**BACK**

**False.**

A **negative** common difference makes the sequence decrease, term by term.

For instance $20 , 17 , 14 , 11 , \dots$ is arithmetic with $d = - 3$.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_KrH77FKtmz7YjRDw`)

**FRONT**

What is the sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic series?


**BACK**

`S_{n} = \frac{n}{2}\left[2 a + \left(n - 1\right) d\right]`, where $a$ is the first term and $d$ the common difference.

The bracket is the first term plus the last, and $\frac{n}{2}$ counts how many such pairs there are.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_3hDzBbxFGHMmxB5K`)

**FRONT**

Given the fourth and ninth terms of an arithmetic series, how do you find $a$ and $d$?


**BACK**

Put each into the $n$th term formula to get two **simultaneous equations**.

With $u_{4} = 10$ and $u_{9} = 25$ that gives $a + 3 d = 10$ and $a + 8 d = 25$, so $d = 3$ and $a = 1$.

## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_nZMYb8WNPsgPFFPp`)

**FRONT**

Define **geometric sequence**.


**BACK**

A **geometric sequence** has a **common ratio** $r$ which each term is multiplied by to get the next one.

The first term is called $a$, so $2 , 6 , 18 , 54 , \dots$ has $a = 2$ and $r = 3$.


Spec links: `spcpt_b5KzyjnFxRygqtmk`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Yzcy8RgJ5s5dbYJN`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the common ratio from two consecutive terms?


**BACK**

Divide a term by the one immediately before it, so $r = \frac{u_{n+1}}{u_{n}}$.

If the sixth term is $486$ and the seventh is $1458$, then $r = \frac{1458}{486} = 3$.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_VvjQ2FPNNphv3V5n`)

**FRONT**

What happens to a geometric sequence when $r$ is negative?


**BACK**

The terms **alternate** between positive and negative values.

Starting at $1$ with $r = - 4$ gives the sequence $1 , - 4 , 16 , - 64 , 256$ and so on.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_J9RDCMQnRR27smpd`)

**FRONT**

What is the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric series?


**BACK**

`S_{n} = \frac{a\left(1 - r^{n}\right)}{1 - r}`, for any common ratio other than $1$.

When $r > 1$ the rearrangement `S_{n} = \frac{a\left(r^{n} - 1\right)}{r - 1}` avoids the negatives and gives the same answer.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_P5yvtc4wSwZZHtxd`)

**FRONT**

With $a = 25$ and $r = 0 . 8$, find the fifth term and the sum of the first five terms.


**BACK**

The fifth term is $25 \times 0 . 8^{4} = 10 . 24$, using $n - 1 = 4$ as the index.

The sum is `S_{5} = \frac{25\left(1 - 0 . 8^{5}\right)}{1 - 0 . 8} = 84 . 04`.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_Zp36KCsYF8cxbrfN`)

**FRONT**

When does a geometric series converge?


**BACK**

When `\left| r \right| < 1`, that is when $- 1 < r < 1$, so the terms creep closer and closer to zero.

The running total then approaches a finite limiting value, called the **sum to infinity**.


Spec links: `spcpt_DqNff4JYrm6vQjyS`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_yvjmt9g672Dpq5fC`)

**FRONT**

What is the sum to infinity of a convergent geometric series?


**BACK**

$S_{\infty} = \frac{a}{1-r}$, which may only be used when `\left| r \right| < 1`.

For $6 , 2 , \frac{2}{3} , \dots$ the ratio is $\frac{1}{3}$, so $S_{\infty} = \frac{6}{1-\frac{1}{3}} = 9$.


Spec links: `spcpt_DqNff4JYrm6vQjyS`


## Card 21 — true_or_false (`fl_DsDpYr5GrfrbChbP`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A geometric series with $r = - \frac{1}{2}$ has a sum to infinity.


**BACK**

**True.**

The condition is on the **size** of $r$, not its sign, and `\left| - \frac{1}{2} \right|` is $\frac{1}{2}$, which is less than $1$.

The terms alternate in sign but still shrink towards zero, so the running total settles on a finite value.
