## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_S4SZB3X9m4YvzSwj`)

**FRONT**

Define **vector** and **scalar**.


**BACK**

A **vector** has both a **magnitude** and a **direction**, while a **scalar** is an ordinary number with size only.

Temperature is a scalar, but a **change** in temperature is a vector, since it has a direction as well as a size.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_vh7kg8qJq3ZKTHGv`)

**FRONT**

What are the two ways of writing a vector from its components?


**BACK**

As a **column vector** `\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}`, or in **i and j notation** as $3 i + 2 j$.

Both say exactly the same thing: $3$ across and $2$ up.

**FRONT**

How are vectors printed, and how should you write them by hand?


**BACK**

Printed vectors are set in **bold**, as in $a$, but bold cannot be produced by hand.

By hand you **underline** the letter instead, and the two mean exactly the same thing.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_hdD5jZRS9mM6jSBK`)

**FRONT**

What does $\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$ mean, and does the order matter?


**BACK**

It is the vector **from** $A$ **to** $B$, and the order matters a great deal.

Reversing the letters reverses the direction, so $\overset{\rightarrow}{BA} = - \overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_Zrwfjy2YSxmxNd8y`)

Complete the results of adding and subtracting these column vectors:

`\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \_\_\_\_\_\_ \\ 5 \end{pmatrix} \text{ and } \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ \_\_\_\_\_\_ \end{pmatrix}`


**BACK**

Tops combine with tops and bottoms with bottoms; the two components never mix.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_5fzmjVP7r8bj6Z4S`)

**FRONT**

What does multiplying a vector by a scalar $k$ do?


**BACK**

Every component is multiplied by $k$, so the whole vector is scaled by that factor.

A **negative** $k$ also reverses the direction, so $- 2 a$ is twice as long as $a$ and points the opposite way.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_FVCv9f8YvfyMP5Hp`)

**FRONT**

How do you travel the wrong way along a vector on a diagram?


**BACK**

Add its **negative** instead, so going backwards along $u$ contributes $- u$ to the journey.

A route that follows $t$ and then reverses $u$ therefore simplifies to $t - u$.

## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_8kVdJ8vKjGttCVCB`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$a + b$ and $b + a$ take you to the same place.


**BACK**

**True.**

Following $a$ and then $b$ ends at the same point as following $b$ and then $a$.

The two routes are the two pairs of sides of a parallelogram, and both reach the opposite corner.

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_3rTn64NWYHXMvJ8S`)

**FRONT**

Define **position vector**.


**BACK**

A **position vector** gives the location of a point relative to a fixed **origin** $O$.

The point $A$ has position vector $a = \overset{\rightarrow}{OA}$, and its components are simply its coordinates.

**FRONT**

What is the position vector of the point `\left(3 , - 2\right)`?


**BACK**

It is $3 i - 2 j$, because a position vector's components are just the point's coordinates.

That correspondence is what lets you move between coordinate geometry and vector work freely.

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_JdqVXfKpxMmBgv4v`)

**FRONT**

Define **displacement vector**.


**BACK**

A **displacement vector** gives the direction and distance **between two points**, rather than a position relative to the origin.

The displacement from $A$ to $B$ is written $\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_WPjKx2CKWHkypVdB`)

**FRONT**

$P$ and $Q$ have position vectors $3 i + 2 j$ and $6 i - 10 j$. Find $\overset{\rightarrow}{PQ}$.


**BACK**

Use $\overset{\rightarrow}{PQ} = q - p$, which gives `\left(6\mathbf{i} - 10\mathbf{j}\right) - \left(3\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}\right)`.

Subtracting component by component leaves $3 i - 12 j$.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_CXP6PcCvGtFP6GKp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\overset{\rightarrow}{AB}$ depends on where the origin has been placed.


**BACK**

**False.**

Moving the origin changes both $a$ and $b$ by the same amount, so the difference $b - a$ is left untouched.

A displacement is about the two points alone, which is why the same arrow can be drawn anywhere on a diagram.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_BZYfRYMxGjNqkV66`)

**FRONT**

Define the **magnitude** of a vector.


**BACK**

The **magnitude** of a vector is its **length**, also called its modulus, and it is written `\left| \mathbf{a} \right|`.

It is a scalar: a magnitude has size but carries no direction.


Spec links: `spcpt_zqGQtK2S4rZfWPy8`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_NZJpdpRXfdKYSNs3`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the magnitude of $a = x i + y j$?


**BACK**

Use `\left| \mathbf{a} \right| = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}`.

The two components form a right-angled triangle with the vector itself as the hypotenuse, so squaring, adding and rooting gives its length.


Spec links: `spcpt_zqGQtK2S4rZfWPy8`


## Card 17 — true_or_false (`fl_Sz5f3fdVFKSSkRhG`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\left| \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} \right|` is the same as `\left| \mathbf{a} \right| + \left| \mathbf{b} \right|`.


**BACK**

**False.**

Add the vectors **first**, and only then take the magnitude of the result.

Two vectors pointing in different directions partly cancel, so the combined length is usually less than the two lengths added together.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_wcQmn9357kgT5RyG`)

**FRONT**

Given $a = 4 i + x j$ and `\left| \mathbf{a} \right| = 5`, how do you find $x$?


**BACK**

Write $\sqrt{4^{2}+x^{2}} = 5$ and **square both sides** to clear the root.

That gives $16 + x^{2} = 25$, so $x = \pm 3$, and both signs are valid unless the question rules one out.

## Card 20 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_BfNndBD9s7FnbRC5`)

**FRONT**

Complete the rule for a unit vector in the direction of $a$:

`\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

`\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{\mathbf{a}}{\left| \mathbf{a} \right|}`

Dividing any vector by its own length always leaves something of length $1$, still pointing the same way.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_QHrytQCp3zZ5CZGJ`)

**FRONT**

How do you turn $3 i - 4 j$ into a unit vector?


**BACK**

Divide by its magnitude, which is `\sqrt{3^{2} + \left(- 4\right)^{2}} = 5`.

Each component is divided separately, giving $\frac{3}{5} i - \frac{4}{5} j$.

**FRONT**

What is a vector path, and why is more than one route allowed?


**BACK**

A **vector path** is any chain of vectors leading from a start point to an end point on a diagram.

Different routes between the same two points always simplify to the **same** answer, so any correct path will do.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_5QvPGkVHmHqMwNg4`)

**FRONT**

How do you write a long vector path in terms of the vectors you are given?


**BACK**

Break the journey into steps that each match a given vector, reversing the sign wherever you travel backwards.

Adding the steps and collecting like terms gives the answer, so $b + b - a - a$ becomes $2 b - 2 a$.

## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_qPh5PG4tsxyPvVKr`)

**FRONT**

How does factorising show that $9 a + 6 b$ and $12 a + 8 b$ are parallel?


**BACK**

They factorise to `3\left(3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}\right)` and `4\left(3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}\right)`, sharing a **common bracket**.

Two vectors built on the same bracket are scalar multiples of each other, so here the second is $\frac{4}{3}$ times the first.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_SrgSB9Gg6JcgG879`)

**FRONT**

Show that $2 p - 4 q$ and $6 q - 3 p$ are parallel.


**BACK**

Factorise both, giving `2\left(\mathbf{p} - 2\mathbf{q}\right)` and `- 3\left(\mathbf{p} - 2\mathbf{q}\right)`.

The second is therefore $- \frac{3}{2}$ times the first, so the two are parallel and point in opposite directions.

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_VPBmtdVr4nT469WH`)

**FRONT**

What two things must you show to prove $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear?


**BACK**

That two of the line segments are **parallel**, and that they share a **common point**.

Parallel alone is not enough: two parallel segments sitting side by side never meet, so the shared point is what forces them onto one line.

If $\overset{\rightarrow}{OB} = 4 \overset{\rightarrow}{OY}$, are $O$, $Y$ and $B$ collinear?

## Card 31 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_KkSgVtQxfB2dTJyk`)

**FRONT**

For non-parallel $a$ and $b$, complete the principle of equating coefficients:

`\text{if } \alpha_{1}\mathbf{a} + \beta_{1}\mathbf{b} = \alpha_{2}\mathbf{a} + \beta_{2}\mathbf{b} \text{ then } \alpha_{1} = \_\_\_\_\_\_ \text{ and } \beta_{1} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`

The completed principle is:

$\text{if} α_{1} a + β_{1} b = α_{2} a + β_{2} b \text{then} α_{1} = α_{2} \text{and} β_{1} = β_{2}$

It works only because $a$ and $b$ are **not parallel**; if they were, one could be absorbed into the other.

## Card 32 — question_and_answer (`fl_Yx23Tj2ZmxtbtG8d`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the point where two lines on a vector diagram cross?


**BACK**

Write two different paths to that point, each carrying its own unknown scalar, then set the two expressions equal.

Equating the coefficients of the two base vectors gives **simultaneous equations** in those scalars, which you solve and substitute back.

## Card 33 — true_or_false (`fl_HtRrvKjnVBpfRmb8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two different vector paths to the same point should use the same scalar letter.


**BACK**

**False.**

Each path needs its **own** unknown, usually $k$ and $λ$, because the fractions travelled along the two lines are different.

Using one letter for both would force those fractions to be equal and give the wrong point.
