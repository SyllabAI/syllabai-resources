Integrate as usual, then substitute both numbers in and subtract the lower from the upper.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_ssRNsr7Kmv6SP2TQ`)

**FRONT**

What are $a$ and $b$ called in `\int_{a}^{b} \text{f}\left(x\right) \text{d}x`?


**BACK**

They are the **integration limits**, with $a$ the **lower** limit and $b$ the **upper** limit.

The integral is read as being 'from $a$ to $b$'.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_Jq9z7zVSnk6Mn49r`)

**FRONT**

Why is no constant of integration needed in a definite integral?


**BACK**

Because it would appear in both substitutions and then cancel.

Working out `\left(\text{f}\left(b\right) + c\right) - \left(\text{f}\left(a\right) + c\right)` leaves `\text{f}\left(b\right) - \text{f}\left(a\right)`, whatever $c$ happens to be.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_Vdv2xpMGJBCWh4DP`)

**FRONT**

Show that `\int_{2}^{4} 3 x\left(x^{2} - 2\right) \text{d}x = 144`.


**BACK**

Expand the brackets first, giving `\int_{2}^{4} \left(3 x^{3} - 6 x\right) \text{d}x = \left[\frac{3}{4} x^{4} - 3 x^{2}\right]_{2}^{4}`.

Substituting gives `\left(192 - 48\right) - \left(12 - 12\right) = 144`.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_ctSMxkB2rZgJmGBR`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the area under a curve between $x = a$ and $x = b$?


**BACK**

Evaluate `\int_{a}^{b} \text{f}\left(x\right) \text{d}x`, which gives the exact area bounded by the curve, the $x$-axis and the two vertical lines.

The limits must be the right way round, with $a$ on the left and $b$ on the right.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_PPNTSB3QTBPW4F2z`)

**FRONT**

What do you do if a boundary of the region is not a vertical line?


**BACK**

It is usually the point where the curve crosses the $x$-axis, so solve `\text{f}\left(x\right) = 0` to find it.

If the $y$-axis is a boundary then that limit is simply $x = 0$.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_D9CRX8xNNNsQT6FP`)

**FRONT**

Find the area bounded by $y = 3 + 2 x - x^{2}$ and the positive axes.


**BACK**

Solving $3 + 2 x - x^{2} = 0$ gives $x = - 1$ or $x = 3$, so the limits are $0$ and $3$.

Then `\int_{0}^{3} \left(3 + 2 x - x^{2}\right) \text{d}x = \left[3 x + x^{2} - \frac{1}{3} x^{3}\right]_{0}^{3} = 9` square units.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_284bnKgTfQwHVzkS`)

**FRONT**

What happens when the region lies entirely below the $x$-axis?


**BACK**

The definite integral comes out **negative**, because the function takes negative values throughout.

Its size is still right, so just drop the minus sign: an integral of $- 36$ means an area of $36$ square units.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_bkwgJfp5D54nBypr`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the area of a region partly above and partly below the $x$-axis?


**BACK**

Split it at the crossing points, found by solving `\text{f}\left(x\right) = 0`, and integrate each part separately.

Make every negative value positive, then add them all together for the total area.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_HHcrBvXBN5GzWsbR`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A definite integral that comes out positive must give the area of the region.


**BACK**

**False.**

If part of the region is below the axis, its negative contribution has already been subtracted from the positive part.

The answer can still come out positive and yet be smaller than the true area, which is why the region has to be split first.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_7N8p9CvqqwtpCZS8`)

**FRONT**

Why does the straight-line boundary not need an integral?


**BACK**

Because the region under a straight line is a **triangle** or a **trapezium**.

So $A = \frac{1}{2} b h$ or `A = \frac{1}{2}\left(a + b\right) h` gives it directly, though an integral would also work.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_959sXNHY4yg9bWFN`)

**FRONT**

Find the area of the region between $y = 10 x - x^{2} - 16$ and $y = 8 - x$.


**BACK**

They meet where $x^{2} - 11 x + 24 = 0$, so at $x = 3$ and $x = 8$, and the region is the difference of the two areas.

The curve gives `\int_{3}^{8} \left(10 x - x^{2} - 16\right) \text{d}x = \frac{100}{3}`, and the line gives a triangle of area $\frac{25}{2}$.

Subtracting leaves $\frac{125}{6}$ square units.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_tqQbB9TGfryjGmHM`)

**FRONT**

What is the integral for the area between two curves?


**BACK**

It is `\int_{a}^{b} \left(y_{1} - y_{2}\right) \text{d}x`, where $y_{1}$ is the **upper** curve and $y_{2}$ the lower one.

There is no triangle shortcut here, since both boundaries are curves and both have to be integrated.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_s8Vm8vgCgp6PQBHv`)

**FRONT**

Why does the upper-minus-lower form avoid negative areas?


**BACK**

Because $y_{1} - y_{2}$ is never negative while $y_{1}$ is genuinely on top, so the integral cannot come out negative.

That holds even if the whole region sits below the $x$-axis, so no splitting or sign-changing is needed.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_SKWQQrpcrprs9G8w`)

**FRONT**

What changes when two curves cross more than twice?


**BACK**

The upper and lower roles **swap** at each crossing.

Each region then needs its own integral with the functions written the right way round, and the separate areas are added at the end.

## Card 18 — true_or_false (`fl_TMzT92Nf99zjsV3w`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Two curves that cross three times enclose one region.


**BACK**

**False.**

Three crossings bound **two** separate regions, one on each side of the middle intersection.

Where `\text{f}\left(x\right) = \left(x - 2\right)\left(x - 3\right)^{2}` meets `\text{g}\left(x\right) = x^{2} - 5 x + 6` at $x = 2 , 3$ and $4$, the two regions must be worked out separately and added.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_bSNTF8RmHrGX9WXk`)

**FRONT**

Define **volume of revolution**.


**BACK**

It is the volume of the solid formed when a region is rotated $2 π$ radians, a complete turn, about an axis.

About the $x$-axis, that region is bounded by the curve `y = \text{f}\left(x\right)`, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x = a$ and $x = b$.

## Card 20 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_X6VdDrk9z6GsMSmv`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for a volume of revolution about the $x$-axis:

`V = \_\_\_\_\_\_ \int_{a}^{b} \_\_\_\_\_\_ \text{d}x`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

`V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} y^{2} \text{d}x`

It is not on the exam formula sheet, and the $π$ outside the integral is the part most often forgotten.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_T2jHTX797tqTwmRC`)

**FRONT**

Why does the volume formula contain $y^{2}$?


**BACK**

Because $π y^{2}$ is the area of the **circular cross-section** of the solid at each value of $x$.

The integral adds up all those thin circular slices between $x = a$ and $x = b$.


Spec links: `spcpt_ZJ82Gv6yR2XWKRT4`


## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_b99MmPHyDmW2Nkdq`)

**FRONT**

Why are the ends of a solid of revolution flat?


**BACK**

Because they are made by rotating the **straight vertical lines** $x = a$ and $x = b$, which sweep out flat discs.

Only the curved surface comes from the curve itself, and three-dimensional sketches often make the ends look rounded.


Spec links: `spcpt_ZJ82Gv6yR2XWKRT4`


## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_ntTp5c3wnb3ccH9W`)

**FRONT**

Find the volume when the region under $y = \sqrt{3x^{2}+2}$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$ is rotated about the $x$-axis.


**BACK**

Squaring first gives $y^{2} = 3 x^{2} + 2$, so `V = \pi \int_{0}^{3} \left(3 x^{2} + 2\right) \text{d}x`.

The integral is `\left[x^{3} + 2 x\right]_{0}^{3} = 33`, so the volume is $33 π$ cubic units.

## Card 24 — true_or_false (`fl_Yy9yzgCDPgnKmx5b`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A square root in $y$ disappears when you set up the volume integral.


**BACK**

**True.**

The formula needs $y^{2}$, and squaring undoes the root.

So $y = \sqrt{4-x}$ becomes simply $y^{2} = 4 - x$, which is far easier to integrate than the original.
