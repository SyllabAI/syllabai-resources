## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_dX4V5CGSwZ3MTRH6`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between displacement and distance?


**BACK**

**Displacement** is measured relative to a fixed point and may be positive, negative or zero.

**Distance** is never negative, and often means the length actually travelled.

A bus back at its depot has zero displacement, but the distance it has travelled is the whole route.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_tGTYRQMYdVvFDdQS`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between speed and velocity?


**BACK**

**Velocity** carries a sign, which tells you the direction of travel.

**Speed** is its magnitude, `\left|v\right|`, so a velocity of $- 6$ is a speed of $6$.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_WfPWjqcz7jkZntjS`)

**FRONT**

Define **particle**.


**BACK**

A **particle** is the general term for the moving object in a kinematics problem.

It is treated as being the size of a single point, so its shape and dimensions can be ignored entirely.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_Nq7Wy3jg3Y7NF3WM`)

**FRONT**

What does it mean for a particle to be at rest?


**BACK**

Its velocity is zero.

**Stationary** and **instantaneously at rest** both say the same thing, that $v = 0$ at that moment.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_RnKbbBJTb2xKJcHq`)

**FRONT**

Which quantity tells you which way the particle is moving?


**BACK**

The **sign of the velocity**, in every case.

A positive velocity means motion in the positive direction and a negative one means motion in the negative direction; the acceleration's sign does not answer this question.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_4cvNqbzMTm7PBWKS`)

**FRONT**

What does $a = 0$ tell you about the motion?


**BACK**

The particle is moving with **constant velocity**.

That does not mean it has stopped: it simply is not speeding up or slowing down at that moment.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_NqcZrqWGkXrKxqkb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A negative acceleration means the particle is slowing down.


**BACK**

**False.**

You have to compare it with the velocity: matching signs mean the particle is speeding up, and opposite signs mean it is slowing down.

So a negative acceleration with a negative velocity is a particle speeding up in the negative direction.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_zXp8yGcRH8rvF4Nd`)

**FRONT**

What do velocity and acceleration mean on a graph?


**BACK**

**Velocity** is the gradient of a displacement-time graph.

**Acceleration** is the gradient of a velocity-time graph, which is the same relationship applied one step further along.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_4d6XSdyqGNJbSBsr`)

**FRONT**

Given `s\left(t\right) = 2 t^{3} - 18 t^{2} + 48 t`, find $v$ and $a$.


**BACK**

Differentiating once gives `v\left(t\right) = 6 t^{2} - 36 t + 48`.

Differentiating again gives `a\left(t\right) = 12 t - 36`.

Powers of $t$ behave exactly as powers of $x$ do, so no new technique is involved.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_P46dYTJzwY83rJ9t`)

**FRONT**

How do you find when a particle is instantaneously at rest?


**BACK**

Solve `v\left(t\right) = 0`.

For `v\left(t\right) = 6 t^{2} - 36 t + 48` that factorises to `6\left(t - 2\right)\left(t - 4\right) = 0`, so the particle is at rest at $t = 2$ and $t = 4$.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_Ppj9NHjcJsSbvBvJ`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the minimum velocity, and why is it not the minimum speed?


**BACK**

Solve $\frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}t} = 0$, which is $a = 0$, then substitute that time back into $v$.

For the motion above $12 t - 36 = 0$ gives $t = 3$ and `v\left(3\right) = - 6\text{ m}\text{ s}^{- 1}`.

The minimum **speed** is $0$, reached at $t = 2$ and $t = 4$, because speed ignores the sign.

## Card 13 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_Gb9WhnY7KsPbNQj7`)

**FRONT**

Complete the two integrals that reverse the chain:

`s = \int \_\_\_\_\_\_ \text{d}t \text{ and } v = \int \_\_\_\_\_\_ \text{d}t`


**BACK**

The completed integrals are:

`s = \int v \text{d}t \text{ and } v = \int a \text{d}t`

Integrating moves you back up the chain, in the opposite direction to differentiating.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_xj9yxwxTXJy7fDKR`)

**FRONT**

What extra information finds the constant in a kinematics problem?


**BACK**

The value of $s$ or $v$ at one stated time.

Watch for the word **initially**, which always means $t = 0$, and substitute that value into your integrated expression to solve for $c$.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_MkSZCnZy9vKSvBqj`)

**FRONT**

A particle has `v\left(t\right) = 6 t^{2} - t + 3` and $s = - 5$ when $t = 0$; find $s$ when $t = 4$.


**BACK**

Integrating gives `s\left(t\right) = 2 t^{3} - \frac{1}{2} t^{2} + 3 t + c`, and substituting $t = 0$ shows $c = - 5$.

Then `s\left(4\right) = 128 - 8 + 12 - 5 = 127\text{ m}`.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_gpgjB3Z39Rv3K7cN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Getting from acceleration to displacement needs two constants of integration.


**BACK**

**True.**

Integrating the acceleration gives the velocity and one constant, and integrating again gives the displacement and a second.

Each needs its own piece of information, so a question of this kind must supply two known values.
