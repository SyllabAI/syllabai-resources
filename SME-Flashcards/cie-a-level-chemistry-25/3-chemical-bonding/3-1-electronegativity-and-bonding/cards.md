## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_sZBRzWkDR9BMvJRp`)

**FRONT**

Define **electronegativity**.


**BACK**

**Electronegativity** is the ability of an atom to attract a pair of electrons towards itself in a covalent bond.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_H53BzYzQjV5RzHvf`)

**FRONT**

Which scale is used to measure electronegativity, and what value does it assign to fluorine?


**BACK**

The **Pauling scale** is used. Fluorine has the highest value of **4.0**, making it the most electronegative element on the periodic table.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_2B864XtcM5pykBbN`)

**FRONT**

Define **shielding (electronegativity)**.


**BACK**

**Shielding** is the reduction in nuclear attraction experienced by outer electrons due to filled inner energy levels masking the positive charge of the nucleus.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_y9jyZygxjCVfXCYK`)

**FRONT**

How does atomic radius affect electronegativity?


**BACK**

A larger atomic radius means outer electrons are further from the nucleus and experience weaker attraction, so electronegativity **decreases** as atomic radius increases.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_bFYHscHxHW6Js29H`)

**FRONT**

State the three factors that affect the electronegativity of an atom.


**BACK**

1. **Nuclear charge** — more protons increase electronegativity.
2. **Atomic radius** — a larger radius decreases electronegativity.
3. **Shielding** — more inner shells decrease electronegativity.


Spec links: `spcpt_GqxmXbGXD9vnQ6wd`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_8zQ3XVW2C7FNsGJf`)

**FRONT**

Why does electronegativity decrease down a group in the periodic table?


**BACK**

Each successive element has an extra filled electron shell, increasing **shielding** and **atomic radius**. Although nuclear charge increases, the overall attraction between the nucleus and outer bonding electrons decreases.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_TYHvfVZCNk2yZVGs`)

**FRONT**

Define **polar covalent bond**.


**BACK**

A **polar covalent bond** is a covalent bond in which the bonding electrons are unequally shared due to a difference in electronegativity between the two atoms, creating partial positive (δ<sup>+</sup>) and partial negative (δ<sup>-</sup>) charges.


Spec links: `spcpt_W79MQwTT3nRz5Dzw`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_2QpcHKnZmvW78dXF`)

**FRONT**

What electronegativity difference (ΔEN) range indicates a polar covalent bond?


**BACK**

A ΔEN of approximately **0.4–1.7** indicates a polar covalent bond. Below <sub>0.4 the bond is non-polar covalent; above </sub>1.7 the bond is mostly ionic.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_9jjK6fNRQgfFsGBM`)

**FRONT**

How does a large difference in electronegativity between two atoms lead to ionic bond formation?


**BACK**

When ΔEN is large (greater than ~1.7), the more electronegative atom attracts electrons so strongly that the less electronegative atom's electron is **transferred** completely, forming a cation (positive) and an anion (negative) held by an ionic bond.
