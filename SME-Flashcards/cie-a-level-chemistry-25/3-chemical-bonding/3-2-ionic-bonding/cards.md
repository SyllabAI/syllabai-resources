# Ionic Bonding

## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_D7CDsPRHjSGsMT7q`)

**FRONT**

Define **ionic bond**.


**BACK**

An **ionic bond** is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions, formed when electrons are transferred from a metal atom (forming a cation) to a non-metal atom (forming an anion).

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_FTswXGWjWxrtRrVn`)

**FRONT**

What electronic configuration do ions typically adopt after ionic bond formation?


**BACK**

Ions adopt the electronic configuration of the nearest **noble gas**, giving them a full outer shell. For example, K<sup>+</sup> and Cl<sup>-</sup> both have the configuration [2,8,8], the same as argon.

**True or False?**

In ionic bonding, the metal atom gains electrons to form a cation.


**BACK**

**False.**

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_Q4ZxzsVNDGRBRGbN`)

**FRONT**

Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?


**BACK**

The **electrostatic attractions** between oppositely charged ions in the lattice act in all directions and are very strong. A large amount of energy is needed to overcome these forces, resulting in high melting points.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_KK5FDFwCZD4B5Msh`)

**FRONT**

How can you use the periodic table to predict the charge on an ion formed by ionic bonding?


**BACK**

The **group number** indicates how many electrons a metal loses (for Groups 1 and 2) or how many a non-metal gains (for Groups 6 and 7), giving the ion charge. For example, Group 2 metals lose 2 electrons to form 2+ cations.

**BACK**

Sodium loses 1 electron to form Na<sup>+</sup> and chlorine gains 1 electron to form Cl<sup>-</sup>. The ionic compound formed is **NaCl** (sodium chloride).

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_PNDHKfFZp9gBw7cq`)

**FRONT**

How many lithium ions are needed to form lithium nitride (Li<sub>3</sub>N), and why?


**BACK**

**Three** Li<sup>+</sup> ions are needed because nitrogen gains 3 electrons to form N<sup>3-</sup>, and each lithium can only donate 1 electron. Three Li<sup>+</sup> ions balance the 3- charge of one N<sup>3-</sup> ion.
