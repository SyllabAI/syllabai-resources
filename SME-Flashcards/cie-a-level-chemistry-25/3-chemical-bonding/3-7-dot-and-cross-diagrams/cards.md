## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_hSS5fF2WMVrqWgHk`)

**FRONT**

Define **dot and cross diagram**.


**BACK**

A **dot and cross diagram** shows the arrangement of outer-shell electrons in an ionic or covalent compound or element, using dots and crosses to represent electrons from different atoms.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_psxnTQZXXBwM8BHC`)

**FRONT**

What three conventions must be followed when drawing a dot and cross diagram for an ionic compound?


**BACK**

1. Only **outer-shell electrons** are shown.
2. The ion is enclosed in **square brackets** to show the charge is spread across the whole ion.
3. The **ionic charge** is written at the top right-hand corner outside the brackets.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In a covalent dot and cross diagram, all electron shells are shown for each atom.


**BACK**

**False.** Only **outer-shell (valence) electrons** are shown in dot and cross diagrams.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_SmjqCggMgMcfnjsT`)

**FRONT**

Define **coordinate (dative covalent) bonding**.


**BACK**

**Coordinate bonding** (also called dative covalent bonding) is a type of covalent bond in which **one atom provides both electrons** needed to form the shared pair.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_XB9VtYjQChXW5x3B`)

**FRONT**

What is the expanded octet rule and which type of element most commonly shows it?


**BACK**

The expanded octet rule applies when an atom has **more than eight electrons** in its outer shell. It is most commonly shown by elements in **Period 3 and beyond** (e.g. P, S) that have available d orbitals, such as in PCl<sub>5</sub> and SF<sub>6</sub>.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_Y7YdWp5tZc2bWpVr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

BF<sub>3</sub> obeys the octet rule because boron forms three covalent bonds.


**BACK**

**False.** BF<sub>3</sub> is an example of the **incomplete octet rule** — boron has only **six electrons** in its outer shell after forming three bonds, making it electron-deficient.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_T7snSSD6HnMK3XPH`)

**FRONT**

Describe what is shown by the dot and cross diagram of NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> and identify the type of bonding present.


**BACK**

The diagram shows four N–H bonds around a central nitrogen atom, all within square brackets with a 1+ charge. Three bonds are ordinary covalent bonds (one electron from N, one from H) and one is a **dative covalent (coordinate) bond** in which the nitrogen lone pair provides both electrons to bond with H<sup>+</sup>.
