## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_k2KBW7SmVFyb93j3`)

**FRONT**

Define **ester**.


**BACK**

An **ester** is a compound with a -COOR functional group, characterised by a sweet, fruity smell. The first part of its name comes from the alcohol and the second from the carboxylic acid.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_pBRvYd9zDJZkgMN2`)

**FRONT**

What reagents and conditions are required for esterification?


**BACK**

A **carboxylic acid** and an **alcohol** are heated under reflux with concentrated **H**<sub>**2**</sub>**SO**<sub>**4**</sub> as a catalyst. The reaction is a condensation reaction that also produces water.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_jDSY6khmFZNMjQvZ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Hydrolysis of an ester with dilute acid is an irreversible reaction.


**BACK**

**False.**

Acid hydrolysis of an ester is **reversible** — an equilibrium is established. Only alkaline hydrolysis is irreversible.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_MhJYmKHWKFbkg8sg`)

**FRONT**

What products are formed when an ester undergoes alkaline hydrolysis, and what additional step is needed to obtain the carboxylic acid?


**BACK**

Alkaline hydrolysis produces a **sodium carboxylate salt** and an **alcohol**. The carboxylate ion (-COO<sup>-</sup>) must then be **acidified** (e.g. with HCl) to produce the free carboxylic acid.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_s5cJmHpWTFcmMZYm`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Alkaline hydrolysis of an ester is irreversible because the carboxylate salt formed cannot readily re-form the ester.


**BACK**

**True.**

Alkaline hydrolysis is irreversible. The ester is fully broken down to a sodium carboxylate salt and an alcohol. Acid hydrolysis, by contrast, is reversible.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_nTyBvgggZH2rRCRP`)

**FRONT**

Define **acid hydrolysis of esters**.


**BACK**

An **acid hydrolysis of esters** is a reversible reaction in which an ester is heated under reflux with dilute acid, reforming the carboxylic acid and alcohol in an equilibrium mixture.
