## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_bBhbkdpyZCzWcWXw`)

**FRONT**

Define **nucleophilic substitution**.


**BACK**

A **nucleophilic substitution** is an organic reaction mechanism in which a nucleophile attacks a carbon atom carrying a partial positive charge, replacing an atom or group carrying a partial negative charge.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_tCygRypXb2KNsFrY`)

**FRONT**

How do you distinguish between an aldehyde and a ketone using Fehling's solution?


**BACK**

Fehling's solution gives a **positive result** (blue to red/orange precipitate) with **aldehydes** only. **Ketones** do not react with Fehling's solution. Both give an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH, so this reagent cannot distinguish them.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_3VJXx9t48NY3SqKV`)

**FRONT**

What colour change is observed when acidified potassium permanganate reacts with an alkene, and what organic product is formed?


**BACK**

The purple KMnO<sub>4</sub> solution turns **colourless**. Acidified KMnO<sub>4</sub> causes **oxidative cleavage** of the C=C double bond, producing **carboxylic acids** (from CH<sub>2</sub>= or CH= groups) or **ketones** (from C= groups with no attached hydrogen).

A **hydroxynitrile** is an organic compound containing both a hydroxyl (OH) group and a nitrile (CN) group. It is formed by nucleophilic addition of HCN (or NaCN) to a carbonyl compound such as an aldehyde.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_fhrKP7kNV2CT9NNS`)

**FRONT**

Why does adding KCN to a halogenoalkane increase the carbon chain length by one?


**BACK**

The CN<sup>-</sup> nucleophile adds **one carbon atom** (as part of the CN group) when it replaces the halogen. After hydrolysis of the nitrile to a **carboxylic acid**, the product has one more carbon atom than the original halogenoalkane.


Spec links: `spcpt_x9Dsh6x8GkYtQFYg`

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Nucleophilic addition of HCN to an aldehyde produces a hydroxynitrile with the same number of carbon atoms as the aldehyde.


**BACK**

**False.**

Nucleophilic addition of HCN increases the carbon chain by one. The CN group adds across the C=O bond, so the hydroxynitrile has one more carbon than the original aldehyde.
