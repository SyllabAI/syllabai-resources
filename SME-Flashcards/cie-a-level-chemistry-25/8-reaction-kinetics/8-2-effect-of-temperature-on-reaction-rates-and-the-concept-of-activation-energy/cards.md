## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_KPqbDVWRfgsWjJcd`)

**FRONT**

Define **activation energy**.


**BACK**

An **activation energy** (*E*<sub>a</sub>) is the minimum energy that reactant particles must possess for a collision to be effective and a chemical reaction to occur.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_d7MhJ2yJf6g7Yrry`)

**FRONT**

Why is the activation energy relatively larger for endothermic reactions than for exothermic reactions?


**BACK**

In **endothermic** reactions, the products are at a higher energy level than the reactants. The activation energy (*E*<sub>a</sub>) is the energy needed to reach the transition state above the reactants, so *E*<sub>a</sub> tends to be **larger** for endothermic reactions than for **exothermic** ones, where the products lie at lower energy than the reactants.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_XStzWyBhGC3B9VRz`)

**FRONT**

Define **Boltzmann distribution curve**.


**BACK**

A **Boltzmann distribution curve** is a graph showing the distribution of energies among molecules in a sample at a given temperature. The area under the curve to the right of *E*<sub>a</sub> represents molecules with enough energy to react.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_YtVCB5g67yNsGwNw`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

At a given temperature, most molecules in a sample have energies close to or greater than the activation energy.


**BACK**

**False.**

Only a **small proportion** of molecules have energies equal to or greater than *E*<sub>a</sub>. Most molecules have intermediate energies and the curve peaks well below *E*<sub>a</sub> for most reactions.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_Bs3GrBVKmJ7MkTbR`)

**FRONT**

Give two reasons why increasing temperature increases the rate of a chemical reaction.


**BACK**

1. Particles move faster, so **collision frequency** increases, giving more effective collisions per unit time.
2. A **greater proportion** of molecules have kinetic energy exceeding *E*<sub>a</sub>, so a higher fraction of collisions are successful.

**BACK**

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_sGTqTTMB6x9M9kWP`)

**FRONT**

On a Boltzmann distribution curve, how is the effect of increasing temperature shown, and what does this indicate about the rate of reaction?


**BACK**

The curve **flattens** and the peak moves to **higher energy**. The area under the curve to the right of *E*<sub>a</sub> **increases**, showing more molecules have sufficient energy to react and the rate of reaction increases.
