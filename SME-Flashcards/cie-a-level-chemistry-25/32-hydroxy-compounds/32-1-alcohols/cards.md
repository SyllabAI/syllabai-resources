## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_c3SdTcNRQFhNPtk7`)

**FRONT**

Define **acyl chloride**.


**BACK**

An **acyl chloride** is an organic compound containing a -COCl functional group, where the carbonyl carbon is electron-deficient and susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_QKQCfv98kgbm3w3W`)

**FRONT**

Why is forming an ester from an acyl chloride more effective than using a carboxylic acid?


**BACK**

Acyl chlorides are **more reactive** so the ester forms faster, and the reaction **goes to completion** so the maximum yield of ester is produced.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_wrZn9H468TpntpSn`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Acyl chlorides react with alcohols vigorously, producing white fumes of HCl gas.


**BACK**

**True.**

The C-Cl bond breaks during nucleophilic attack by the alcohol's -OH group, releasing HCl as white fumes.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_gQ4sRz9HhZJDXDwn`)

**FRONT**

What conditions are required for an acyl chloride to react with phenol to form an ester?


**BACK**

A **base** (e.g. NaOH) is required. The base deprotonates phenol to form the **phenoxide ion**, which is a better nucleophile and can attack the carbonyl carbon. The reaction proceeds at **room temperature**.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_C8FnCDFW2qcq9mCt`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Phenol reacts with acyl chlorides under the same conditions as aliphatic alcohols.


**BACK**

**False.**

Aliphatic alcohols react vigorously with acyl chlorides at room temperature **without a base**. Phenol also reacts at room temperature but requires a **base** (e.g. NaOH) to form the **phenoxide ion** before nucleophilic attack can occur.


Spec links: `spcpt_Mwdm52Dj75yJ22tw`


## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_nHF86Z3y3pSwPXC9`)

**FRONT**

**phenoxide ion**


**BACK**

A **phenoxide ion** is the conjugate base of phenol, formed when phenol is deprotonated by a base. It is a better nucleophile than phenol because the negative charge makes the oxygen more electron-rich.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_8F4SYPTBTMTfGqyn`)

**FRONT**

What is the mechanism type for the reaction of acyl chlorides with alcohols or phenols?


**BACK**

**Addition-elimination**: the nucleophile (alcohol or phenoxide) adds across the C=O bond, then HCl is eliminated to form the ester.
