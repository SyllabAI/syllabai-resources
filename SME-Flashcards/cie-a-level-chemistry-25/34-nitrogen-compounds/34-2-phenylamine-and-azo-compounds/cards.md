## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_tRZbJjDYGQww5C4V`)

**FRONT**

Define **phenylamine**.


**BACK**

A **phenylamine** is an organic compound consisting of a benzene ring with an -NH<sub>2</sub> functional group. It is prepared from benzene via nitration, reduction and deprotonation.


Spec links: `spcpt_ZrYv26MGZKXyPXbQ`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_DYXvr2VxYZSDyZsd`)

**FRONT**

What are the three main steps in the preparation of phenylamine from benzene?


**BACK**

1. **Nitration** of benzene using concentrated HNO<sub>3</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> at 25–60 °C to form nitrobenzene.
2. **Reduction** of nitrobenzene with hot tin (Sn) and concentrated HCl under reflux.
3. **Deprotonation** with NaOH to form phenylamine.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Phenylamine reacts with bromine water at room temperature to form 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine.


**BACK**

**True.**

The lone pair on nitrogen donates electron density into the benzene ring, activating it toward electrophilic substitution. Bromination occurs at the 2, 4 and 6 positions under mild conditions.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_RDF7KVT6sKMfH4Qj`)

**FRONT**

What is the order of basicity of ethylamine, ammonia and phenylamine, from strongest to weakest?


**BACK**

**Ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine.**

The ethyl group donates electron density to nitrogen (increasing basicity). Ammonia has no such group. The benzene ring in phenylamine delocalises the lone pair (decreasing basicity).

**BACK**

## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_gzW3YmHxJCj5QTDB`)

**FRONT**

Define **dative covalent bond**.


**BACK**

A **dative covalent bond** is a covalent bond in which both electrons are donated by one atom. In base chemistry, the nitrogen lone pair is donated to a proton (H<sup>+</sup>) to form this bond.


Spec links: `spcpt_xK4XH2bxzdj2FHn4`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_BM7dPSD2YgWJbb8M`)

**FRONT**

Why is phenylamine the weakest base of the three species: ethylamine, ammonia and phenylamine?


**BACK**

In phenylamine, the lone pair on nitrogen **overlaps with the conjugated pi system** of the benzene ring and becomes delocalised. This makes the lone pair **less available** for dative covalent bonding with a proton.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_dmK5Tm9H9pMQhrTn`)

**FRONT**

What are the three steps used to make an azo dye from phenylamine?


**BACK**

1. Form **nitrous acid** (HNO<sub>2</sub>) from NaNO<sub>2</sub> and dilute HCl below 10 °C.
2. **Diazotisation**: react phenylamine with HNO<sub>2</sub> to form benzenediazonium chloride below 10 °C.
3. **Coupling reaction**: react the diazonium ion with alkaline phenol.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_t89f6GZJyGgcMhdY`)

**FRONT**

Why are azo compounds very stable?


**BACK**

The -N=N- group acts as a **bridge** between two benzene rings. The pi electrons from both rings are **delocalised** through the azo group, creating an extended conjugated system that gives the compound stability.
