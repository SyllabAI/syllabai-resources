## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Ftqz9sbRvSwkpYZY`)

**FRONT**

Define **primary amine**.


**BACK**

A **primary amine** is an organic compound with an -NH<sub>2</sub> functional group attached to an alkyl or aryl group.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_B4fD7pjwwKWmmHmn`)

**FRONT**

What type of reaction occurs when a halogenoalkane reacts with excess hot ethanolic ammonia under pressure?


**BACK**

A **nucleophilic substitution** reaction. The lone pair on nitrogen in ammonia attacks the carbon bonded to the halogen, displacing it and forming a **primary amine**.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_gXrJRVzRgHZpvKkP`)

**FRONT**

Why must excess ammonia be used when producing a primary amine from a halogenoalkane?


**BACK**

Excess ammonia drives the reaction toward the **primary amine** product. Without excess, the primary amine formed can act as a nucleophile and react further with the halogenoalkane to give secondary and tertiary amines.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_NDr8JqGrtWX65KBW`)

**FRONT**

Why is carboxylic acid a less effective reagent than acyl chloride for making amides?


**BACK**

Carboxylic acids are **less reactive** than acyl chlorides, so the reaction is **slower** and does not go to completion. Acyl chlorides give a faster, more complete reaction.

## Card 15 — true_or_false (`fl_cfhXCBh76fhnyVR8`)

**FRONT**

The carbonyl carbon in an acyl chloride is electron-rich, making it susceptible to nucleophilic attack.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_qj9DWZPy4GHTWpqM`)

**FRONT**

Define **base (in the context of amines)**.


**BACK**

A **base** is a species that can donate its lone pair of electrons to form a **dative covalent bond** with an electron-deficient species such as a proton (H<sup>+</sup>).


Spec links: `spcpt_KR2xn6FYs3kB52pj`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_3WJJbzTWbhJBwxHf`)

**FRONT**

How does an alkyl group affect the basicity of an amine?


**BACK**

Alkyl groups have a **positive inductive effect**, donating electron density to the nitrogen atom. This makes the lone pair more available for dative covalent bonding, **increasing** the basicity of the amine.


Spec links: `spcpt_KR2xn6FYs3kB52pj`


## Card 21 — true_or_false (`fl_r9YZG2fpQ5GFZxVN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Phenylamine is a stronger base than ethylamine.


**BACK**

**False.**

Phenylamine is a **weaker** base than ethylamine. The benzene ring delocalises the lone pair on nitrogen, making it less available for dative covalent bonding with a proton.


Spec links: `spcpt_KR2xn6FYs3kB52pj`


## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_dpD94m7YtsR3RZp3`)

**FRONT**

Why does delocalisation of the nitrogen lone pair into a benzene ring decrease basicity?


**BACK**

When the lone pair overlaps with the conjugated system of the benzene ring, it becomes **delocalised** and **less readily available** to form a dative covalent bond with a proton, making the amine a weaker base.
