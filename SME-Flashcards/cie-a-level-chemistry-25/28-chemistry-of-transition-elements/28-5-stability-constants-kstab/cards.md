## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_p7m9dPM4THKTt9n6`)

**FRONT**

Define **stability constant (*****K*****<sub>stab</sub>**).


**BACK**

A **stability constant** (*K*<sub>stab</sub>) is the equilibrium constant for the formation of a complex ion from its constituent central metal ion and ligands in solution. A larger value indicates a more stable complex.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_xvKD5Px3YRRyrZJf`)

**FRONT**

Why is the concentration of water not included in the K<sub>stab</sub> expression?


**BACK**

Water is present in **excess** as the solvent. Any water produced by the ligand substitution is negligible compared to the water already present, so its concentration is treated as constant and omitted.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_CzvhnQpT2FJcnFnD`)

**FRONT**

What is meant by stepwise stability constants in ligand substitution?


**BACK**

In stepwise substitution, each individual ligand replacement has its own equilibrium constant (K<sub>1</sub>, K<sub>2</sub>, etc.). The **overall stability constant** *K*<sub>stab</sub> summarises all the stepwise constants combined.

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_3dpzB6twXds9YxTd`)

**FRONT**

Define **competing equilibrium**.


**BACK**

A **competing equilibrium** in ligand exchange is the simultaneous existence of multiple substitution equilibria in solution, with the most stable complex (highest *K*<sub>stab</sub>) being preferentially formed.


Spec links: `spcpt_HMKbZWcN6bZRcnxk`


## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_YD4GHSBWgP7WRcH2`)

**FRONT**

Why does ligand exchange occur in a transition metal complex?


**BACK**

Ligand exchange occurs because the incoming ligands form a **more stable** complex, with a **larger *****K***<sub>**stab**</sub>, than the original complex. The equilibrium shifts to favour the more stable product.
