## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_qpczJJNXhYqHrkBq`)

**FRONT**

Define **cis-trans isomerism in complexes**.


**BACK**

**Cis-trans isomerism in complexes** is a type of stereoisomerism found in square planar and octahedral complexes with two pairs of different ligands. In the cis isomer, identical ligands are adjacent; in the trans isomer, they are opposite each other.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4RnpQntxWyPBX4vq`)

**FRONT**

Why is cisplatin medically active while transplatin is not?


**BACK**

Cisplatin is **polar** because the two electronegative Cl ligands are on the same side, creating a charge imbalance that allows it to bind to DNA. Transplatin is **non-polar** because the ligands are opposite each other, so the charges cancel.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_hBbk3DdB8hZNhdB9`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Octahedral complexes with three identical bidentate ligands can exhibit optical isomerism.


**BACK**

**True.** Such complexes have no plane of symmetry and exist as non-superimposable mirror images — the defining feature of **optical isomers**.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_FvSXw3MCBC3x8fc7`)

**FRONT**

What structural feature allows octahedral complexes to display cis-trans isomerism?


**BACK**

An octahedral complex displays cis-trans isomerism when it has **two ligands that are different** from the other four. These two different ligands can be either adjacent (cis) or opposite (trans).

Optical isomers of complexes rotate the plane of polarised light in the same direction.


**BACK**

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_4FZy38hyRnZ2skyh`)

**FRONT**

Define **optical isomers**.


**BACK**

**Optical isomers** are non-superimposable mirror images of each other. They have no plane of symmetry and differ only in the direction in which they rotate the plane of polarised light.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_pFmwVwrNwYmQKN8W`)

**FRONT**

Why is the cis isomer of [Co(NH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>4</sub>(H<sub>2</sub>O)<sub>2</sub>]<sup>2+</sup> polar?


**BACK**

In the cis isomer, the two H<sub>2</sub>O ligands are on the same side. The oxygen atoms in water are more electronegative than the nitrogen atoms in NH<sub>3</sub>, creating a partial negative charge on one side and making the complex **polar**.
