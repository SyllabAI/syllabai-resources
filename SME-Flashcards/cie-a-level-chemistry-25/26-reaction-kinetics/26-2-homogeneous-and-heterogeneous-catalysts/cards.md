**FRONT**

Define **homogeneous catalyst**.


**BACK**

A **homogeneous catalyst** is a catalyst that is in the **same phase** as the reactants in the reaction mixture.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_j8KqyVSY9jwzYG6d`)

**FRONT**

Describe the three main steps in the mode of action of a heterogeneous catalyst.


**BACK**

1. **Adsorption** — reactant molecules diffuse to and bond to the catalyst surface (chemisorption weakens bonds within reactants).
2. **Reaction** — reactant atoms react with each other on the surface.
3. **Desorption** — product molecules break away from the surface and diffuse away.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_rk5tjvgvFYFQfcM8`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In the Haber process, the iron catalyst is a homogeneous catalyst because it speeds up the reaction between nitrogen and hydrogen.


**BACK**

**False.**

Iron is a **heterogeneous** catalyst. It is in the solid phase while the reactants N<sub>2</sub> (g) and H<sub>2</sub> (g) are in the gas phase, so they are in different phases.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_8bQZVbdc94f3MwZQ`)

**FRONT**

In the Fe<sup>3+</sup>-catalysed reaction between S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>8</sub><sup>2-</sup> (aq) and I<sup>-</sup> (aq), why is Fe<sup>3+</sup> a more effective catalyst than using no catalyst?


**BACK**

S<sub>2</sub>O<sub>8</sub><sup>2-</sup> and I<sup>-</sup> are both **negatively charged**, so direct collision requires overcoming large **repulsive forces**. Fe<sup>3+</sup> reacts with each ion separately in two redox steps, avoiding repulsion and significantly lowering the activation energy.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_zFjvSNDWMJ9nMtR2`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Nitrogen dioxide (NO<sub>2</sub>) can act as a homogeneous catalyst in the formation of acid rain.


**BACK**

**True.**

NO<sub>2</sub> catalyses the oxidation of SO<sub>2</sub> to SO<sub>3</sub> and is then regenerated as NO is re-oxidised back to NO<sub>2</sub> by O<sub>2</sub>. It is in the same gas phase as the reactants.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_SYRSsqX8SHsZhNF9`)

**FRONT**

What metals are used as heterogeneous catalysts in catalytic converters, and what harmful gases do they help to remove?


**BACK**

**Platinum, palladium and rhodium** are used. They convert nitrogen oxides (NO<sub>x</sub>) into harmless nitrogen gas (N<sub>2</sub>) and carbon monoxide (CO) into carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) by adsorption on the metal surface.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_9gryBHfh49DPB9Rv`)

**FRONT**

How does homogeneous catalysis by transition metal ions involve changes in oxidation state?


**BACK**

The transition metal ion is first **reduced** (e.g. Fe<sup>3+</sup> → Fe<sup>2+</sup>) by one reactant, then **oxidised** back (Fe<sup>2+</sup> → Fe<sup>3+</sup>) by the other reactant. This allows it to be regenerated at the end and used repeatedly as a catalyst.
