## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_pCjFz2GppDz9Zpz4`)

**FRONT**

Define **entropy**.


**BACK**

**Entropy** (*S*) is a measure of the number of possible arrangements of particles and their energy in a system. It is a measure of disorder: the greater the disorder, the higher the entropy.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_Mx7tjJk7vP3BnkPh`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Gases have greater entropy than liquids, which in turn have greater entropy than solids.


**BACK**

**True.**

The order of entropy is: gas > liquid > solid. Gases are most disordered, solids are most ordered.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_FyBsv89rwXXHfVVP`)

**FRONT**

Explain why the decomposition of CaCO<sub>3</sub> results in an increase in entropy.


**BACK**

CaCO<sub>3</sub> (s) → CaO (s) + CO<sub>2</sub> (g)

A **gaseous** CO<sub>2</sub> molecule is formed from a solid. Gases are far more disordered than solids, so the number of possible arrangements increases and **entropy** increases.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_KbSbmX24wcGS5tHs`)

**FRONT**

What happens to the entropy of a substance when it melts, and why?


**BACK**

Entropy increases when a substance melts. The regularly arranged **lattice** of particles becomes a more randomly arranged liquid in which particles can rotate and slide over each other, increasing **disorder**.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Dissolving a solid in a solvent causes a decrease in entropy.


**BACK**

**False.**

Dissolving a solid increases entropy. The particles spread out from the ordered solid lattice into a more disordered solution, increasing the number of possible arrangements.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_mrBcXVFZjt764v5Z`)

**FRONT**

Why does entropy decrease during crystallisation of a salt from solution?


**BACK**

During **crystallisation**, particles move from a disordered solution into a regular, ordered **solid lattice**. The number of possible arrangements decreases, so entropy decreases.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_688jxtGsd3NwT5RR`)

**FRONT**

Why is a negative value of Δ*S*<sub>system</sub><sup>θ</sup> expected for the reaction 2Mg (s) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 2MgO (s)?


**BACK**

A gas molecule (O<sub>2</sub>) is consumed and no gas is produced. The products are all **solid**, which is more ordered than the reactants. The number of possible arrangements decreases, so Δ*S*<sub>system</sub><sup>θ</sup> is **negative**.
