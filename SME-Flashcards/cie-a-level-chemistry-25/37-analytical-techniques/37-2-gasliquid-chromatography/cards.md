## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_VqqkcsXMz5R78Yrg`)

**FRONT**

Define **retention time**.


**BACK**

A **retention time** is the time taken for a component to travel from the injection point through the GLC column to the detector, used to identify unknown compounds by comparison with data book values.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_cmTbdxRKfRRw6dFj`)

**FRONT**

What is used as the stationary phase in gas-liquid chromatography?


**BACK**

The stationary phase is a **non-polar, long-chain, non-volatile hydrocarbon** with a high boiling point, mounted onto a solid support (e.g. small silica particles packed in a glass column).

In GLC, the mobile phase is a liquid solvent that carries sample molecules through the column.


**BACK**

**False.** In **GLC**, the mobile phase is an **inert carrier gas** (e.g. helium or nitrogen), not a liquid solvent.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_8YfswdFYf7CQ5shr`)

**FRONT**

How are the retention times from a GLC chromatogram used to identify unknown compounds?


**BACK**

The **retention times** of sample peaks are compared with **data book values** for known compounds under the same conditions. A match identifies the unknown component.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_87TkD5VdnSzjmDkM`)

**FRONT**

Define **gas-liquid chromatography**.


**BACK**

**Gas-liquid chromatography** is an analytical technique that separates volatile components of a mixture by their different interactions with a non-polar liquid stationary phase, using an inert carrier gas as the mobile phase.

**FRONT**

Why do silica particles in a GLC column improve the separation of components?

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_hB26yMYxR8HtvG6m`)

**FRONT**

How is the approximate percentage composition of one component calculated from a GLC chromatogram?


**BACK**

The **percentage** of a component = (peak area or height of that component / sum of all peak areas or heights) × 100. This gives its approximate proportion in the mixture.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_z6xTJbsTCPXsy4tK`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Non-polar components in a GLC mixture have shorter retention times because they interact more strongly with the non-polar stationary phase.


**BACK**

**False.** Non-polar components have **longer** retention times because they are more attracted to the **non-polar stationary phase** and travel more slowly through the column.


Spec links: `spcpt_GrDHnRRH7rRvMNSr`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_b8MTWNr7xm4rvVsq`)

**FRONT**

Why do polar components tend to have shorter retention times in GLC?


**BACK**

**Polar components** have weaker interactions with the **non-polar stationary phase** than non-polar components do. They spend less time in the stationary phase and more time in the **mobile phase** (carrier gas), travelling faster through the column and giving shorter **retention times**.


Spec links: `spcpt_GrDHnRRH7rRvMNSr`


## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_83nVRrrrnjHgzh6H`)

**FRONT**

Define **chromatogram**.


**BACK**

A **chromatogram** is the output from a chromatography experiment showing peaks at different retention times, where each peak represents a separate component of the analysed mixture.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If a peak in a GLC chromatogram is too small to measure its area accurately, the peak height can be used instead.


**BACK**

**True.** When peak areas are too small or difficult to determine, the **peak height** is used as an alternative measure of relative concentration.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_8QpxpbNrsBy8bCth`)

**FRONT**

Explain why compounds with lower boiling points tend to have shorter retention times in GLC.


**BACK**

Compounds with lower **boiling points** are more easily vaporised and spend more time in the **gaseous mobile phase**, interacting less with the stationary phase and so eluting faster.
