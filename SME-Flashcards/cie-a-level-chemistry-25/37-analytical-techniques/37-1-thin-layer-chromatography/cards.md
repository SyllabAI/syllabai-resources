## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Dc3mN8RWPnmFPhqp`)

**FRONT**

Define **stationary phase**.


**BACK**

A **stationary phase** is a polar solid coating (typically silica or alumina) on a metal sheet, to which solute molecules adsorb and across which they are separated.


Spec links: `spcpt_m4QWsjGjw2rcHj2b`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_FvYg3YhkQ9fx3trT`)

**FRONT**

In TLC, what is the solvent front and why must the baseline be above the solvent level?


**BACK**

The **solvent front** is the furthest point the mobile phase travels up the plate. The baseline must be above the solvent level so the sample spot dissolves in the mobile phase rather than washing directly into it.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_FZ8HvbvYTYzDg2SB`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

More polar components have higher Rf values in TLC because they interact more strongly with the polar stationary phase.


**BACK**

**False.** More **polar** components have **lower** R<sub>f</sub> values because they are more strongly attracted to the polar stationary phase and travel shorter distances.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_GJ7wnnFW9HywRCtT`)

**FRONT**

Why must the baseline on a TLC plate be drawn in pencil rather than ink?


**BACK**

Pencil is inert and does not react with the sample components or the solvent. **Ink** would dissolve in the mobile phase and interfere with the separation.


Spec links: `spcpt_m4QWsjGjw2rcHj2b`


## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_XP6FqFgjRZjdJ38N`)

**FRONT**

Define **R****<sub>f</sub>**** value**.


**BACK**

An **R**<sub>**f**</sub>** value** is the ratio of the distance travelled by a component to the distance travelled by the solvent front in chromatography, used to identify substances by comparison with reference data.


Spec links: `spcpt_B8TWJXzXhRwkNnVW`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_pMQ5SXHwfTxGffRQ`)

**FRONT**

How can uncoloured components be identified on a TLC plate after development?


**BACK**

Uncoloured components can be identified by viewing the plate under **UV light** or by treating it with **ninhydrin**, which reacts with certain compounds (e.g. amino acids) to produce coloured spots.


Spec links: `spcpt_m4QWsjGjw2rcHj2b`


## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_kJDTPSD2CsFrVkSq`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A less polar solvent (mobile phase) will cause polar components to travel further up the TLC plate.


**BACK**

**False.** A **less polar** mobile phase is less effective at carrying **polar** components, so they remain closer to the baseline. A more polar mobile phase increases the R<sub>f</sub> of polar substances.
