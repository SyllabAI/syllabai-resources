## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_cS3KyjwftVZhN4Jm`)

**FRONT**

Define **limiting reagent**.


**BACK**

A **limiting reagent** is the reactant that is completely consumed in a reaction. It determines the maximum amount of product that can be formed and is not present in excess.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_HKrgKF7HpHXq2D7G`)

**FRONT**

What is the molar volume of any gas at room temperature and pressure (RTP), and what are the RTP conditions?


**BACK**

At RTP (20 °C, 1 atm), one mole of any gas occupies **24.0 dm**<sup>**3**</sup>. This is Avogadro's hypothesis applied to gases.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_5qDYSrzmBydTHq5B`)

**FRONT**

25.0 cm<sup>3</sup> of 0.050 mol dm<sup>-3</sup> Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> is neutralised by 20.0 cm<sup>3</sup> HCl. What is the concentration of HCl? (Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H<sub>2</sub>O + CO<sub>2</sub>)


**BACK**

Moles Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> = 0.025 x 0.050 = 0.00125 mol. Moles HCl = 2 x 0.00125 = 0.00250 mol. Concentration HCl = 0.00250/0.020 = **0.125 mol dm**<sup>**-3**</sup>.

Equal volumes of different gases at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of molecules.


**BACK**

**True.**

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_C2xWk6xHRjCVcXc4`)

**FRONT**

In a reaction of 9.2 g Na with 8.0 g S to form Na<sub>2</sub>S, which is the limiting reagent? (2Na + S → Na<sub>2</sub>S; A<sub>r</sub>: Na = 23.0, S = 32.1)


**BACK**

Moles Na = 9.2/23.0 = 0.40 mol. Moles S = 8.0/32.1 = 0.25 mol. To react 0.40 mol Na requires only 0.20 mol S. S is in excess, so **Na is the limiting reagent**.
