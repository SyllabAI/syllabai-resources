## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_QknWmNtJGmGHCt8C`)

**FRONT**

Define **isotopes**.


**BACK**

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element that have the same number of **protons** and electrons, but a different number of **neutrons**. They have the same **atomic number** but different **mass numbers**.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_g7BqPnC424RhHpBm`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Isotopes of the same element have the same **mass number**.


**BACK**

**False.**

Isotopes of the same element have the same **atomic number** (same number of protons) but **different mass numbers** because they contain different numbers of **neutrons**.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_8sF3rhTZXyxm7Qw8`)

**FRONT**

How are **isotopes** named?


**BACK**

Isotopes are named using the **chemical symbol** (or element name) followed by a dash and the **mass number**. For example, carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon with 6 and 8 neutrons respectively.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_zmzs85hv5Y75mYnT`)

**FRONT**

Why do **isotopes** of the same element have identical **chemical** properties?


**BACK**

Isotopes have the same number of **electrons** and the same **electron configuration**. Because chemical behaviour is determined by **electrons** (especially those in the outer shell), all isotopes of an element react in the same way.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_zw2sQzTQVh3JFhVz`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Isotopes of the same element have the same **density**.


**BACK**

**False.**

Isotopes have different numbers of **neutrons**, so they have different **masses**. This leads to differences in **density** and **relative atomic mass**, even though their chemical properties are identical.


Spec links: `spcpt_wMmgX5bpCKqKvXws`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_J4Sq6pzcMQ83b8p5`)

**FRONT**

Why do **isotopes** of the same element differ in **physical** properties?


**BACK**

Isotopes differ in the number of **neutrons**, which changes the nuclear **mass** without affecting charge. This leads to different **relative atomic masses**, **densities** and behaviour in processes such as **mass spectrometry**.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_5jRxfqSq6jT5q6rW`)

**FRONT**

Carbon-14 has an atomic number of 6 and mass number 14. How many **protons**, **neutrons** and **electrons** does a neutral carbon-14 atom have?


**BACK**

A neutral **carbon-14** atom has:

1. **Protons:** 6 (= atomic number)
2. **Neutrons:** 8 (= 14 − 6)
3. **Electrons:** 6 (neutral atom, so electrons = protons)
