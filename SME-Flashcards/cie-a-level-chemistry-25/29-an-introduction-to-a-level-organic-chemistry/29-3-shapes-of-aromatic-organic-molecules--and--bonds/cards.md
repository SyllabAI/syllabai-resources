## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_ZC8p5Xg5qY9B7mFk`)

**FRONT**

Define **conjugated π system**.


**BACK**

A **conjugated π system** is a region of alternating single and double bonds in which electrons are delocalised across adjacent p orbitals, spreading electron density over the whole system.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_hqMx3zCvyMw5sjJ4`)

**FRONT**

What type of hybridisation do the carbon atoms in benzene have, and what does this mean for their bonding?


**BACK**

Carbon atoms in benzene are **sp**<sup>**2**</sup>** hybridised**. Each carbon forms three σ bonds using its sp<sup>2</sup> orbitals and has one remaining p orbital that overlaps laterally with neighbouring p orbitals to form the **delocalised π system**.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_QySZ5R9QdbPGq7wR`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

All carbon–carbon bond lengths in benzene are equal.


**BACK**

**True.**

Because electrons are delocalised across the ring, all C–C bonds have the same length with both single and double bond character. This equal bond length is evidence for the delocalised ring structure.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_cdNHvY6JJXyNPmBp`)

**FRONT**

Define **aromatic stabilisation**.


**BACK**

An **aromatic stabilisation** is the extra stability gained by a molecule due to the delocalisation of π electrons over a conjugated ring system, making the compound more stable than predicted by a simple alternating double bond model.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_f2yXjCMjG3BvGC3y`)

**FRONT**

How does the p orbital overlap in benzene lead to delocalisation of electrons?


**BACK**

Each sp<sup>2</sup>-hybridised carbon retains one **p orbital** perpendicular to the ring. These p orbitals on adjacent carbons overlap laterally around the ring, allowing electrons to spread freely across all six carbon atoms rather than being **localised** in fixed double bonds.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_vf8P956Nhk2h4jCv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In sp<sup>2</sup> hybridisation, the carbon's s orbital mixes with all three of its p orbitals.


**BACK**

**False.**

In sp<sup>2</sup> hybridisation, the s orbital mixes with only two of the three p orbitals, forming three sp<sup>2</sup> hybrid orbitals. The remaining p orbital is unhybridised and participates in the π system.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_FJdmdNvjn5kq774h`)

**FRONT**

What evidence supports the delocalised ring structure of benzene?


**BACK**

All C–C bonds in benzene are the **same length**, **intermediate** between a C–C single bond and a C=C double bond. If benzene had alternating single and double bonds, two different bond lengths would be observed.
