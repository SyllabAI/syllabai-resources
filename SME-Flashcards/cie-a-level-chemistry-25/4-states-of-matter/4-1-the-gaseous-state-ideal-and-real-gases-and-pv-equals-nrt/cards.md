## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Q4GRD5QzRvYVymbD`)

**FRONT**

Define **gas pressure**.


**BACK**

**Gas pressure** is the force exerted per unit area on the walls of a container, caused by gas molecules constantly colliding with the walls.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_99WRgHSkRHgmYrk2`)

**FRONT**

What happens to the pressure of a gas when its volume is decreased at constant temperature?


**BACK**

The **pressure** increases. Reducing the volume squashes molecules closer together, increasing the **frequency of collisions** with the container walls.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_Xpj3PVJt72XXn8hK`)

**FRONT**

Define **ideal gas**.


**BACK**

An **ideal gas** is a theoretical gas that obeys the kinetic theory assumptions: molecules have negligible volume, no intermolecular forces exist, collisions are perfectly elastic and temperature is proportional to average kinetic energy.

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_jTyQW5yP8jDGbMMj`)

**FRONT**

Define **elastic collision**.


**BACK**

An **elastic collision** is a collision in which no kinetic energy is lost. It is one of the key assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases: gas molecules lose no energy when they collide with each other or with the container walls.


Spec links: `spcpt_sFJ6ZPXvkT79fC2f`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_P47T6HdcH8tKvH7Q`)

**FRONT**

Why does the volume of an ideal gas increase when it is heated at constant pressure?


**BACK**

Heating gives molecules more **kinetic energy**, causing more frequent collisions. To maintain constant pressure, the molecules must spread further apart, so the volume increases. Volume is **directly proportional** to temperature at constant pressure.


Spec links: `spcpt_sFJ6ZPXvkT79fC2f`

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_fJnQD2vc6wD7SD2G`)

**FRONT**

Give two reasons why real gases deviate from ideal gas behaviour at high pressure.


**BACK**

At high pressure: (1) **intermolecular forces** (e.g. London forces) become significant, reducing the actual pressure below the ideal value. (2) The **volume of the gas molecules** themselves can no longer be ignored, so the free volume is less than predicted.
