## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_szSm9WsNHQBr9Mr8`)

**FRONT**

Define **electrolysis**.


**BACK**

An **electrolysis** is the breakdown of a compound into its elements using an electric current passed through a molten ionic compound or concentrated aqueous solution of ions.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_NFNX9MfCmKYsVFpb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

During electrolysis, reduction takes place at the anode and oxidation takes place at the cathode.


**BACK**

**False.**

Oxidation takes place at the anode (anions lose electrons). Reduction takes place at the cathode (cations gain electrons). Remember: RED CATS — REDuction at the CAThode.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_6PZTQSjPXdVT5mnq`)

**FRONT**

What determines which cation is discharged at the cathode during electrolysis of an aqueous solution?


**BACK**

The cation with the most positive **electrode potential** (*E*<sup>θ</sup>) is discharged, as it is most easily **reduced**. If concentrations are high enough, the more concentrated ion may also be preferentially discharged.

**FRONT**

Why is water present in an aqueous electrolyte solution and what ions does it contribute?


**BACK**

Water is a **weak electrolyte** that **partially ionises**: H<sub>2</sub>O ⇌ H<sup>+</sup> + OH<sup>-</sup>. This adds H<sup>+</sup> and OH<sup>-</sup> ions to the solution, which compete with ions from the dissolved salt during electrolysis.

Define **Faraday's constant**.


**BACK**

A **Faraday's constant** is the amount of electric charge carried by one mole of electrons or one mole of singly charged ions. Its value is 96 500 C mol<sup>-1</sup>.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_MkV8tgYjBWsTQjtN`)

**FRONT**

Describe how Avogadro's constant (*L*) can be determined experimentally using electrolysis.


**BACK**

Copper electrodes are weighed before and after electrolysis of CuSO<sub>4</sub> (aq). The **charge passed** and the **mass of copper deposited** are measured. The charge per mole of electrons is calculated and divided by the **charge on one electron** (1.60 × 10<sup>-19</sup> C) to give *L*.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_WdzbwMRJPVmD6WKV`)

**FRONT**

In an electrolysis experiment to determine Avogadro's constant, why is the mass decrease of the anode often used rather than the mass increase of the cathode?


**BACK**

The solid copper deposited at the **cathode** may not adhere properly to the cathode surface, leading to an underestimate in mass gained. The mass lost by the **anode** gives a more reliable measurement.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_mZgfyrh4xB7gWQTv`)

**FRONT**

Outline the four steps to calculate the mass of a substance deposited at an electrode during electrolysis.


**BACK**

1. Write the **half-equation** at the electrode.
2. Determine the charge (in C) needed to deposit one mole using **Faraday's constant**.
3. Calculate the charge transferred: *Q* = *I* x *t*.
4. Use simple proportion with the **molar mass** to find the mass deposited.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_PxcwtzTMpvsvsTZP`)

**FRONT**

Why is the time converted to seconds (not minutes) when using *Q* = *I* × *t* in electrolysis calculations?


**BACK**

Because current is measured in **amperes** (A = C s<sup>-1</sup>) and the resulting charge *Q* must be in **coulombs** (C). Using minutes would give an incorrect value of *Q* and an incorrect mass or volume.
