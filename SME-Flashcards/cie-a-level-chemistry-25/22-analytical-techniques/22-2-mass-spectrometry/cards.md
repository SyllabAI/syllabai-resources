## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_Dzfsfr2THHzxWTfC`)

**FRONT**

What is the m/e ratio, and how is it used to separate ions in a mass spectrometer?


**BACK**

The m/e ratio is the **mass (m)** of an ion divided by its **charge (e)**. Ions are separated by deflection in a magnetic field: ions with a smaller m/e ratio are **deflected more** and detected first.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_YSDHbW27tFf5Ft8t`)

**FRONT**

Why do only positively charged fragment ions reach the detector in a mass spectrometer?


**BACK**

The fragments are accelerated by an electric field and then deflected by a magnetic field. Only **positively charged ions** respond to these fields and are directed to the detector. **Neutral molecules and radicals** are not deflected and do not reach the detector.

**BACK**

## Card 28 — keyword_definition (`fl_JDwGfPpmQMhKQpHM`)

**FRONT**

Define **M+1 peak**.


**BACK**

An **M+1 peak** is a small peak one mass unit above the molecular ion peak in a mass spectrum. It arises from molecules containing a <sup>13</sup>C atom instead of <sup>12</sup>C. Its size relative to M<sup>+</sup> can be used to calculate the number of carbon atoms.


Spec links: `spcpt_YrYDsWrSzvMs3n9H`


## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_bTpDQTBD3bW5CFbJ`)

**FRONT**

State the formula used to calculate the number of carbon atoms from the M+1 peak.


**BACK**

**n** = (100 × abundance of [M+1]) ÷ (1.1 × abundance of M<sup>+</sup>)

where **n** is the **number of carbon atoms** and abundances are relative (e.g. from peak heights).
