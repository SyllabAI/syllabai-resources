## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Z9d7yhBqT2zx8xDZ`)

**FRONT**

Define **halogenoalkane**.


**BACK**

A **halogenoalkane** is an alkane in which one or more hydrogen atoms have been replaced by a **halogen** atom. They are classified as primary, secondary or tertiary based on the substitution of the carbon bearing the halogen.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_88mpCwcmkKNBxgzy`)

**FRONT**

Name three routes by which halogenoalkanes can be produced.


**BACK**

1. **Free-radical substitution** of alkanes with Cl<sub>2</sub> or Br<sub>2</sub> in UV light.
2. **Electrophilic addition** of HX or X<sub>2</sub> to alkenes.
3. **Substitution** of an alcohol using HX, PCl<sub>3</sub>, PCl<sub>5</sub> or SOCl<sub>2</sub>.

**FRONT**

What is the major product rule when HBr adds to an unsymmetrical alkene to form a halogenoalkane?

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_d2bzhp5ZrKg3knzC`)

**FRONT**

How can aqueous silver nitrate be used to identify which halogen is present in a halogenoalkane?


**BACK**

## Card 36 — question_and_answer (`fl_yrVHtrYpbdNnc3Mp`)

**FRONT**

Why does a primary halogenoalkane not form a primary carbocation as an intermediate in nucleophilic substitution?


**BACK**

A **primary carbocation** is much less stable than a tertiary carbocation because it has only **one alkyl group** donating electron density via the inductive effect. Primary halogenoalkanes therefore follow the S<sub>N</sub>2 pathway, avoiding the unstable primary carbocation.
