## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_vnc5VMpCR8DNJtVB`)

**FRONT**

Define **polyester**.


**BACK**

A **polyester** is a condensation polymer in which monomers are linked by ester bonds (-COO-), formed from a diol and a dicarboxylic acid (or a hydroxycarboxylic acid).


Spec links: `spcpt_vbGjQ8vGtVPVDVyC`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_kK9B34QxZhcsWVtj`)

**FRONT**

What two types of monomer are required to form a polyester by condensation polymerisation?


**BACK**

A **diol** (containing two -OH groups) and a **dicarboxylic acid** (containing two -COOH groups). A small molecule of **water** is eliminated each time a new ester link forms.


Spec links: `spcpt_vbGjQ8vGtVPVDVyC`

**BACK**

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_xq2QQWrR6KTqTfwX`)

**FRONT**

How can you identify whether a polymer was made by addition or condensation polymerisation from its structure?


**BACK**

If the **backbone** consists only of carbon atoms with no functional groups, it is an **addition polymer**. If the backbone contains ester links (-COO-) or amide links (-CONH-), it is a **condensation polymer**.


Spec links: `spcpt_sFmfct63pVC8QMMJ`

A condensation polymer made from a dioyl chloride and a diamine expels water as the small molecule.


**BACK**

**False.**

When a **dioyl chloride** reacts with a diamine, the small molecule expelled is **HCl** (the Cl from the chloride and H from the amine). Water is expelled when a dicarboxylic acid is used instead.

## Card 22 — question_and_answer (`fl_MmBznb5kzDVssFhJ`)

**FRONT**

How do you identify the monomers from a section of a condensation polymer?


**BACK**

Locate the **linking groups** (ester or amide bonds). Consider which small molecule was expelled (H<sub>2</sub>O or HCl). Split the chain at each link and add back the expelled atoms to reconstruct the original monomer structures.
