## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_mPKhJCfYqvZzT9XT`)

**FRONT**

What functional group in a monomer indicates that addition polymerisation will occur?


**BACK**

A **C=C double bond**. The double bond opens to allow monomers to add on either side, forming a (poly)alkene chain without expelling any small molecule.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_byf5bjpdbPJNWbqg`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Addition polymers have functional groups in their polymer backbone.


**BACK**

**False.**

Addition polymers have a **plain carbon chain** as their backbone with no functional groups. Functional groups only appear as side chains (e.g. benzene rings, halogen atoms or -CN groups).

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_yX2DzygSNg3zhKmT`)

**FRONT**

Define **copolymer**.


**BACK**

A **copolymer** is a polymer produced when two or more different alkene monomers undergo addition polymerisation together.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_GKbfFXD4tQfMzCyp`)

**FRONT**

How can you deduce from a polymer structure that it was formed by condensation polymerisation?


**BACK**

Look at the **polymer backbone**. If it contains functional groups such as **ester links** (-COO-) or **amide links** (-CONH-), it was formed by condensation polymerisation.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_2kb4HNnMVnHkX7vw`)

**FRONT**

Monomers containing only C=C double bonds undergo **..........**  polymerisation. Monomers containing -NH<sub>2</sub> and -COOH groups undergo **..........**  polymerisation to form a polyamide.


**BACK**

Monomers containing only C=C double bonds undergo **addition** polymerisation. Monomers containing -NH<sub>2</sub> and -COOH groups undergo **condensation** polymerisation to form a polyamide.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_BHpFSYtrvhXtVCND`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Acyl chlorides can react with alcohols to form polyesters by condensation polymerisation, expelling HCl.


**BACK**

**True.**

Acyl chlorides (-COCl) react with alcohols (-OH) in condensation polymerisation to form polyesters. HCl is the small molecule expelled at each ester link formed.


Spec links: `spcpt_Zn8ynqWMthW3YHXw`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_Qy8cNZMMp2mWc9Vc`)

**FRONT**

An amino acid contains both -NH<sub>2</sub> and -COOH groups. What type of polymer does it form and what is the link called?


**BACK**

It forms a **polyamide** by condensation polymerisation. The link formed between adjacent amino acids is called a **peptide link** (amide link), and water is eliminated.


Spec links: `spcpt_t6tGYYB2sptyfJfN`


## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_GGqqXVSbDYyPDrMw`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition polymerisation**.


**BACK**

An **addition polymerisation** is a type of polymerisation in which monomers with C=C double bonds join together to form a polymer chain without expelling any small molecule.

## Card 9 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_6WbzpYwkw4hcx5Js`)

**FRONT**

A polymer with ester links in its backbone is a **..........** . A polymer with a plain carbon-chain backbone and no backbone functional groups is an **..........**  polymer.


**BACK**

A polymer with ester links in its backbone is a **polyester**. A polymer with a plain carbon-chain backbone and no backbone functional groups is an **addition** polymer.


*Blanks: 2 — answers: ['polyester', 'addition']*
