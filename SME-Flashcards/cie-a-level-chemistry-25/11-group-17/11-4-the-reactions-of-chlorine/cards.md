## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_Kp3BT9KnJKVdXpfW`)

**FRONT**

Define **disproportionation**.


**BACK**

A **disproportionation** reaction is one in which the same element is simultaneously **oxidised** and **reduced**. The reaction of chlorine with alkali is a classic example.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_jQmrvXBkkq3N845v`)

**FRONT**

Write the ionic equation for the reaction of Cl<sub>2</sub> with cold dilute NaOH and identify which species is oxidised and which is reduced.


**BACK**

Cl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + 2OH<sup>-</sup> (aq) → Cl<sup>-</sup> (aq) + ClO<sup>-</sup> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

Cl<sub>2</sub> is **oxidised** to ClO<sup>-</sup> (oxidation number 0 → +1) and **reduced** to Cl<sup>-</sup> (oxidation number 0 → −1). This is a **disproportionation** reaction.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_xwrHjvbRGpxCFdDN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

When Cl<sub>2</sub> reacts with hot alkali at 70°C, the chlorine is oxidised to chlorate(I) ions (ClO<sup>-</sup>).


**BACK**

**False.**

In hot alkali (70°C), chlorine is oxidised to **chlorate(V) ions** (ClO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, oxidation number +5), not ClO<sup>-</sup> (+1). Cold alkali produces ClO<sup>-</sup>.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_W4tQDQHMxwv39Yb5`)

**FRONT**

How does chlorine make drinking water safe?


**BACK**

Chlorine reacts with water in a disproportionation reaction to form **chloric(I) acid (HClO)**, which **sterilises** water by killing bacteria. HClO can further dissociate to form **ClO**<sup>**-**</sup> (aq), which is also a sterilising agent.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_V6n4skKBBr3RVYVk`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In the reaction of Cl<sub>2</sub> with cold NaOH, the oxidation number of Cl in ClO<sup>-</sup> is +1.


**BACK**

**True.**

In ClO<sup>-</sup>, oxygen is −2 and the overall charge is −1, so chlorine must be **+1**. This represents the **oxidation** of Cl<sub>2</sub> (oxidation number 0) in the disproportionation reaction.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_VynX7PcmwFbn3xTj`)

**FRONT**

Write the equation for the further dissociation of HClO in water.


**BACK**

HClO (aq) → H<sup>+</sup> (aq) + ClO<sup>-</sup> (aq)

Both **HClO** and **ClO**<sup>**-**</sup> act as sterilising agents, making chlorine an effective treatment for drinking water.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_NV5w2K9q96GjZdMz`)

**FRONT**

Write the ionic equation for the reaction of Cl<sub>2</sub> with hot NaOH (70°C).


**BACK**

3Cl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + 6OH<sup>-</sup> (aq) → 5Cl<sup>-</sup> (aq) + ClO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> (aq) + 3H<sub>2</sub>O (l)

This is a **disproportionation** reaction in which Cl<sub>2</sub> is both **oxidised** (to ClO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>, +5) and **reduced** (to Cl<sup>-</sup>, −1).
