## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_SNW5PgGrRfCrbSXZ`)

**FRONT**

Why does the reducing power of halide ions increase going down Group 17?


**BACK**

Going down Group 17, the halide ions become **larger**. Their outermost electrons are further from the nucleus and experience more **shielding**. The electrons are held less tightly and are therefore more easily **lost (donated)**, increasing reducing power.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_757G7RFBFrn6pY96`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The addition of AgNO<sub>3</sub> to a solution containing I<sup>-</sup> ions gives a yellow precipitate.


**BACK**

**True.**

Iodide ions form **AgI**, a **yellow** precipitate when silver nitrate solution is added. Chloride gives white (AgCl) and bromide gives cream (AgBr).

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_8Nhj5FKjDM43XVzC`)

**FRONT**

Define **reducing agent**.


**BACK**

A **reducing agent** is a substance that donates electrons to another species, causing itself to be **oxidised**. In Group 17, halide ions are reducing agents, and their reducing power increases going down the group.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_cPfzN525nVVssfdP`)

**FRONT**

What observations are made when concentrated H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> is added to NaBr?


**BACK**

First, **white fumes** of HBr are produced. Then concentrated H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> oxidises HBr, producing **reddish-brown** fumes of Br<sub>2</sub> (g) and **SO**<sub>**2**</sub> (g): 2HBr (g) + H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> (aq) → Br<sub>2</sub> (g) + SO<sub>2</sub> (g) + 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l).

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_6pbSTt2VNq3J4Ssp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

When concentrated H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> reacts with NaCl, the H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> is reduced to SO<sub>2</sub>.


**BACK**

**False.**

With NaCl, concentrated H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> only produces **HCl (g)** (white fumes). The sulfuric acid is **not** reduced because HCl is not a strong enough reducing agent to reduce it. Only HBr and HI are strong enough to reduce H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_MnR3HYbmr2RNbb3w`)

**FRONT**

Write the ionic equation for the reaction of silver ions with iodide ions.


**BACK**

Ag<sup>+</sup> (aq) + I<sup>-</sup> (aq) → AgI (s)

A **yellow precipitate** of silver iodide forms. Unlike AgCl and AgBr, AgI does not dissolve in either dilute or concentrated ammonia solution.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_pXbhg9TScMv9rBfJ`)

**FRONT**

Describe the test for halide ions using silver nitrate solution, and explain why nitric acid is added first.


**BACK**

**Nitric acid** is added to remove any carbonate or sulfate ions that could also precipitate with Ag<sup>+</sup>. Silver nitrate solution is then added. A **white** (Cl<sup>-</sup>), **cream** (Br<sup>-</sup>) or **yellow** (I<sup>-</sup>) precipitate of the silver halide confirms the halide present.
