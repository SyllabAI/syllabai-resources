## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_Kdm9nbRdVX6GkBRY`)

**FRONT**

An element forms a chloride that reacts with water to give a solution of pH 1. What does this suggest about the element's group?


**BACK**

A low pH (strongly acidic) solution suggests the chloride is **hydrolysed** in water. This is characteristic of **non-metallic (covalent) chlorides**, placing the element in Groups 13–17 rather than Groups 1–2.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_SSNrgxpQnPktGbXw`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Group 1 and Group 2 chlorides react with water to form strongly acidic solutions.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Group 1 and Group 2 chlorides** dissolve in water to form neutral or near-neutral solutions (pH 6.5–7.0). It is **covalent chlorides** (e.g. Group 15) that hydrolyse to produce strongly acidic solutions.

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_mvjXc5FWGvdDvJyp`)

**FRONT**

Define **periodicity**.


**BACK**

**Periodicity** is the repeating patterns of physical and chemical properties observed across the periods and groups of the periodic table, which allow predictions about unknown elements based on position.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_3WPnHpxzsNZ2XDmp`)

**FRONT**

An element forms an oxide with a very high melting point that does not react with NaOH. What group is the element likely in?


**BACK**

A very high melting point oxide with a giant covalent structure and no reaction with NaOH (unlike Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>) points to **Group 14**. Aluminium (Group 13) oxide reacts with NaOH, so Group 14 is the more likely assignment.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_wr8XcZNbh8krDSSV`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

White fumes produced when a covalent chloride reacts with water are hydrogen chloride gas.


**BACK**

**True.**

When covalent chlorides such as SiCl<sub>4</sub> or PCl<sub>5</sub> are hydrolysed by water, **HCl (g)** is produced. This appears as **white fumes** in the air.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_RD3K68Yj4whhSw6z`)

**FRONT**

How can the physical properties of an element be used to predict its position in the periodic table?


**BACK**

Properties such as **melting point**, **electrical conductivity** and the **type of bonding** in oxides or chlorides can indicate the group and period. For example, a high melting point giant covalent oxide places an element in Group 14, while ionic bonding in the oxide suggests a metal in Groups 1–2.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_M8twCkmZ49xDYWQQ`)

**FRONT**

Selenium is in Group 16 and Period 4. Predict whether it reacts with water.


**BACK**

**Selenium** is predicted to form **simple molecules** (like sulfur). Simple molecular non-metals do not react with water. However, a selenium chloride (SeCl<sub>2</sub>) could react with water vapour in air to form **HCl**, analogous to sulfur's chloride.
