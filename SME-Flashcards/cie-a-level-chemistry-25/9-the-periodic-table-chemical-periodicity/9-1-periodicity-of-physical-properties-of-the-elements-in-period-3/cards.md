## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_bQKC5S7btt9kCygZ`)

**FRONT**

Why does atomic radius decrease across Period 3?


**BACK**

Atomic radius decreases across Period 3 because the **nuclear charge** increases with each additional proton, while the number of electron shells stays the same (so **shielding** is constant). The stronger attraction pulls the outer electrons closer to the nucleus.


Spec links: `spcpt_hXGsNB87dP7gcCSZ`


## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_knw5pQyJWhXC6Fhp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Shielding increases across Period 3, causing atomic radius to decrease.


**BACK**

**False.**

Shielding remains roughly constant across Period 3 because all elements have the same number of electron shells. It is the increasing **nuclear charge** that pulls electrons closer, reducing the atomic radius.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_GPWdMWw7RGQgwdFZ`)

**FRONT**

Why are Period 3 anions larger than their parent atoms?


**BACK**

Anions gain one or more electrons into their outer shell, increasing **electron-electron repulsion**. The **nuclear charge** stays the same, so the electron cloud spreads out, making the ion larger than the parent atom.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_S2PGcMKThYHFmd6w`)

**FRONT**

Define **atomic radius**.


**BACK**

An **atomic radius** is the distance between the nucleus and the outermost electron of an atom, measured as half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms of the same element.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_728tXjWxnCMF46PS`)

**FRONT**

Explain the trend in ionic radius from Na<sup>+</sup> to Si<sup>4+</sup> across Period 3.


**BACK**

From Na<sup>+</sup> to Si<sup>4+</sup>, ionic radius **decreases** because the increasing **nuclear charge** attracts the electrons in the second principal quantum shell more strongly, pulling them closer to the nucleus.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_bwWZq8k7WVs5DPHP`)

**FRONT**

Why does the melting point increase from Na to Al across Period 3?


**BACK**

Na, Mg and Al have **giant metallic lattice** structures. Going from Na to Al, each atom donates more electrons to the delocalised 'sea' (1, 2 and 3 respectively), increasing the strength of **metallic bonding** and raising the melting point.

## Card 11 — keyword_definition (`fl_j6WDtwbm2PTTcnXD`)

**FRONT**

Define **metallic bonding**.


**BACK**

**Metallic bonding** is the electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and a surrounding 'sea' of delocalised electrons.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_PYwhsmmrQNwRyxtJ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Silicon is a good electrical conductor because it has a giant covalent structure.


**BACK**

**False.**

Although **silicon** has a giant molecular (covalent) structure, it has no delocalised electrons, so it cannot conduct electricity easily. It is classified as a **semimetal** (metalloid).

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_rBc4K7BQwsdQZjbW`)

**FRONT**

Why does sulphur (S<sub>8</sub>) have a higher melting point than phosphorus (P<sub>4</sub>)?


**BACK**

**Sulphur** exists as larger S<sub>8</sub> molecules compared to the smaller P<sub>4</sub> molecules. Larger molecules have stronger **instantaneous dipole-induced dipole** forces between them, so more energy is needed to separate them.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_jwn6ZF4MMC9QrQNX`)

**FRONT**

Why does silicon have the highest melting point in Period 3?


**BACK**

**Silicon** has a **giant molecular structure** in which each Si atom is covalently bonded to four neighbouring Si atoms. These strong covalent bonds require a large amount of energy to break, giving silicon the highest melting point in Period 3.

**FRONT**

Define **semimetal**.


**BACK**

A **semimetal** (metalloid) is an element with properties intermediate between metals and non-metals, such as **silicon**, which has a giant covalent structure but only very limited electrical conductivity.
