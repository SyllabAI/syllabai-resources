## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_g7MDJ4gT9XXCNtgk`)

**FRONT**

Why does the thermal stability of Group 2 carbonates and nitrates increase going down the group?


**BACK**

Going down the group, the metal cations become **larger** and have a **lower charge density**. This reduces their polarising effect on the carbonate or nitrate anion, so more heat is required to decompose the compound.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_txkvSDyTtQpQbj2R`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The solubility of Group 2 sulfates increases going down the group.


**BACK**

**False.**

The solubility of Group 2 sulfates **decreases** going down the group. The enthalpy of solution becomes more endothermic because the lattice enthalpy decreases more slowly than the enthalpy of hydration.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_yPggHV4YmgyxPRQw`)

**FRONT**

Define **standard enthalpy of solution (Δ*****H*****<sub>sol</sub>**).


**BACK**

The **standard enthalpy of solution (Δ*****H***<sub>**sol**</sub><sup>**o**</sup>**)** is the energy absorbed or released when one mole of an ionic solid dissolves in sufficient water to form a dilute solution under standard conditions. It can be exothermic or endothermic.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_P2KPjwS5z7892mDb`)

**FRONT**

What is the relationship between enthalpy of solution, enthalpy of hydration and lattice enthalpy?


**BACK**

Δ*H*<sub>sol</sub><sup>o</sup> = Δ*H*<sub>hyd</sub><sup>o</sup> − Δ*H*<sub>latt</sub><sup>o</sup>

The enthalpy of solution reflects the balance between the energy released on **hydration** of gaseous ions and the energy needed to overcome the **lattice**.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_rYGbs2Vp6Xz8m9Tb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The solubility of Group 2 hydroxides increases going down the group.


**BACK**

**True.**

For hydroxides, the lattice enthalpy decreases faster than the enthalpy of hydration going down the group, making the enthalpy of solution more exothermic and the hydroxides more soluble.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_PWrqnghR94Nhgn2P`)

**FRONT**

Explain why small Group 2 cations at the top of the group have a greater effect on polarising carbonate and nitrate ions than larger cations lower in the group.


**BACK**

Smaller cations have a **greater charge density** (same 2+ charge packed into a smaller volume). This produces a stronger electric field that distorts the electron cloud of the anion more, making the anion more polarised and easier to decompose thermally.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_w3xvjyZ8VqKD6rZH`)

**FRONT**

Why does the enthalpy of hydration of Group 2 cations become less exothermic going down the group?


**BACK**

Going down the group, the cations become **larger**. The ion-dipole interactions between the cation and water molecules weaken, so less energy is released when the gaseous ions are hydrated.
