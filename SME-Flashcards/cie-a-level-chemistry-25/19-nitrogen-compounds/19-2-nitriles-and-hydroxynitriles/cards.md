## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_8VN6ZKgrJM4B4hQY`)

**FRONT**

Define **nitrile**.


**BACK**

A **nitrile** is a compound containing the -CN functional group, prepared by nucleophilic substitution of a halogenoalkane with ethanolic KCN.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_cWZVhgf6n3MtnpGm`)

**FRONT**

What reagents and conditions are needed to convert a halogenoalkane into a nitrile?


**BACK**

The halogenoalkane is heated under reflux with an **ethanolic solution of potassium cyanide (KCN in ethanol)**. The CN<sup>-</sup> ion acts as the nucleophile.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_tHZDVmVWNgZSgVcQ`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The reaction of a halogenoalkane with ethanolic KCN increases the length of the carbon chain by one carbon atom.


**BACK**

**True.**

The CN<sup>-</sup> ion adds a carbon atom to the chain as it substitutes the halogen. This makes the reaction useful for chain extension in synthesis.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_jQmB6YsCJBHmfy8d`)

**FRONT**

Describe the two-step mechanism by which HCN adds to ethanal to form a hydroxynitrile.


**BACK**

**Step 1:** The CN<sup>-</sup> ion attacks the carbonyl carbon of ethanal, forming a negatively charged intermediate.

**Step 2:** The oxygen of the intermediate is protonated by H<sup>+</sup> (from HCN, water or dilute acid) to give **2-hydroxypropanenitrile**.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_9Tmr86HGCqffRh3d`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Hydrolysis of a nitrile does not change the number of carbon atoms in the chain.


**BACK**

**True.**

Hydrolysis converts the -CN group into -COOH without adding or removing carbon atoms. The chain length is preserved, unlike nitrile formation which adds one carbon.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_VPQ7MdBfzC8YsXpb`)

**FRONT**

Define **hydroxynitrile**.


**BACK**

A **hydroxynitrile** is a compound with both -OH and -CN groups on the same carbon, formed by nucleophilic addition of HCN to an aldehyde or ketone.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_tPfFFFSKf9v3vqjT`)

**FRONT**

Why is the use of ethanolic KCN (rather than aqueous KCN) important when making a nitrile from a halogenoalkane?


**BACK**

Using **aqueous** KCN risks forming an **alcohol** (via hydrolysis) instead of the nitrile. **Ethanolic** KCN ensures the nucleophilic substitution proceeds to give the nitrile product.
