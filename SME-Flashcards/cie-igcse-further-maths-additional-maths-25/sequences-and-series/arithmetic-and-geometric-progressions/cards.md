## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_9gnrRFvRb2sq9fch`)

**FRONT**

Define a **sequence** and a **term**.


**BACK**

A **sequence**, also called a progression, is an ordered list of numbers together with a rule for generating them.

Each number in it is a **term**, and terms are usually labelled $u_{1}$, $u_{2}$, $u_{3}$ and so on.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_FKxTQf6y5z4cmkdM`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a sequence and a series?


**BACK**

A **sequence** is the list of terms; a **series** is what you get by **adding** those terms together.

So $1 , 3 , 5 , 7$ is a sequence, while $1 + 3 + 5 + 7$ is the matching series.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_f6NMvJVQcB4jgyTP`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$S_{n}$ means the $n$th term of a sequence.


**BACK**

**False.**

$S_{n}$ is the **sum** of the first $n$ terms, so $S_{n} = u_{1} + u_{2} + \dots + u_{n}$.

The $n$th term on its own is written $u_{n}$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_7437KYb7XyZcFdRM`)

**FRONT**

A sequence is defined by $u_{n} = 5 - 2 n$. Find the first five terms and $S_{5}$.


**BACK**

Substituting $n = 1$ to $5$ gives the terms $3$, $1$, $- 1$, $- 3$, $- 5$.

Adding those together gives $S_{5} = - 5$, which is negative because the later terms outweigh the early ones.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_sZjzGzt77wnG4kMn`)

**FRONT**

What does the symbol $Σ$ tell you to do?


**BACK**

$Σ$ is the Greek capital sigma and stands for **sum**.

The expression to its right says what is being added, and the numbers below and above it say where the counting starts and stops.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_j8ffgz4wJQ4Tfbz6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The number below the $Σ$ is always 1.


**BACK**

**False.**

The lower limit can be any value, so `\sum_{k=0}^{4}(2k+1)` and `\sum_{k=7}^{14}(2k-13)` are both perfectly ordinary sums.

Always read the limits rather than assuming the count begins at 1.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_pSStvjKSxp8z3Fmg`)

**FRONT**

Define an **arithmetic progression**.


**BACK**

An **arithmetic progression** is a sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

That constant is called the **common difference**, $d$, and the sequence starts at the **first term**, $a$.

Complete the formula for the $n$th term of an arithmetic progression:

`u_{n} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$u_{n} = a + ( n - 1 ) d$

Here $a$ is the first term and $d$ the common difference.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_nvqyftNDVgcJpfDf`)

**FRONT**

Why does the formula use $( n - 1 ) d$ rather than $n d$?


**BACK**

Because the first term has had **no** common differences added to it yet.

Check it with $n = 1$: the formula gives $a + 0 \times d = a$, which is right, whereas $n d$ would wrongly add one difference straight away.

## Card 10 — true_or_false (`fl_8Hng46tcYqTJgfCf`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The common difference of an arithmetic progression can be negative.


**BACK**

**True.**

A negative common difference simply makes the sequence **decrease**, as in $20 , 17 , 14 , 11 , \dots$ where $d = - 3$.

The word progression says nothing about the direction of travel.

## Card 11 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_YrVJVtBP6VHnsTbQ`)

**FRONT**

Complete the two forms for the sum of the first $n$ terms:

`S_{n} = \frac{1}{2}n(a + \_\_\_\_\_\_)`

`S_{n} = \frac{1}{2}n(2a + \_\_\_\_\_\_)`


**BACK**

The completed forms are:

$S_{n} = \frac{1}{2} n ( a + l )$

$S_{n} = \frac{1}{2} n ( 2 a + ( n - 1 ) d )$

Here $l$ is the **last** term, and the second form is just the first with $l$ replaced by $a + ( n - 1 ) d$.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_2PPHWWwD7xqCwrBJ`)

**FRONT**

Which form of the sum formula should you use?


**BACK**

Use $\frac{1}{2} n ( a + l )$ when you know the **first and last** terms.

Use $\frac{1}{2} n ( 2 a + ( n - 1 ) d )$ when you know the **first term and the common difference** but not the last term.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_WbWTd3NGhTPPQVjH`)

**FRONT**

An arithmetic progression has first term 3 and twentieth term 60. Find $S_{20}$.


**BACK**

Both the first and last terms are known, so use the shorter form:

$S_{20} = \frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times ( 3 + 60 ) = 630$

No common difference is needed, which is why this form is worth spotting.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_tvfctjsGW7sCmWsH`)

**FRONT**

A question gives you $S_{n}$ and the first term, and asks for the common difference. What do you do?


**BACK**

Substitute everything you know into $S_{n} = \frac{1}{2} n ( 2 a + ( n - 1 ) d )$ and solve the resulting equation for $d$.

The formulas work in any direction, so an unknown inside one is found the same way as the sum itself.

## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_7qJnrNsZ65WnnJZC`)

**FRONT**

Define a **geometric progression**.


**BACK**

A **geometric progression** is a sequence in which consecutive terms are related by a constant multiplier.

That multiplier is the **common ratio**, $r$, and the sequence starts at the **first term**, $a$.


Spec links: `spcpt_z8nfcb8kZqJ49yXb`


## Card 16 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_cBB5MWDmQjKW7BKF`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for the $n$th term of a geometric progression:

`u_{n} = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$u_{n} = a r^{n-1}$

The power is $n - 1$ for the same reason as in an arithmetic progression: the first term has not yet been multiplied by anything.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_Twk2SYVp4xNWwC3y`)

**FRONT**

What happens to a geometric progression when $r$ is negative?


**BACK**

The terms **alternate** between positive and negative.

For instance $1 , - 4 , 16 , - 64 , 256 , \dots$ has $r = - 4$, and the signs flip with every multiplication.


Spec links: `spcpt_z8nfcb8kZqJ49yXb`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_PXQ2v6XNcf3WVnwb`)

**FRONT**

You are given two consecutive terms of a geometric progression. How do you find $r$?


**BACK**

Divide a term by the one immediately before it, since that division undoes the multiplication.

With $r$ known, substituting either term into $u_{n} = a r^{n-1}$ then gives the first term.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_k2MfPwcXkBJjNkCF`)

**FRONT**

What is a geometric series?


**BACK**

It is the **sum** of the terms of a geometric progression.

So the progression $2 , 6 , 18 , 54 , \dots$ has the matching series $2 + 6 + 18 + 54 + \dots$

## Card 20 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_zcnYCCsb9pBc28ns`)

**FRONT**

Complete the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric progression:

`S_{n} = \frac{a(1 - \_\_\_\_\_\_)}{1 - r}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$S_{n} = \frac{a(1-r^{n})}{1-r}$

There is an equivalent form, $\frac{a(r^{n}-1)}{r-1}$, and both require $r \neq 1$.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_vm7kfxNf3hwd8bVW`)

**FRONT**

Which form of the geometric sum formula is more convenient?


**BACK**

Use $\frac{a(1-r^{n})}{1-r}$ when $r < 1$, so that both the top and bottom stay positive.

Use $\frac{a(r^{n}-1)}{r-1}$ when $r > 1$, for the same reason, since the two are just each other multiplied through by $- 1$.

## Card 22 — true_or_false (`fl_77rK2brXqjjKgzWm`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Every geometric series has a sum to infinity.


**BACK**

**False.**

A sum to infinity exists only when `|r| < 1`, and it is then $S_{\infty} = \frac{a}{1-r}$.

If `|r| \ge 1` the terms do not shrink away, so the total grows without limit and the series diverges.
