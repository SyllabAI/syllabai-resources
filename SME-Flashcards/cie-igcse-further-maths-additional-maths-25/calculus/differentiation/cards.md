## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_hKsR7SwWJccWdqQN`)

**FRONT**

What is the gradient of a curve at a point?


**BACK**

It is the gradient of the **tangent** to the curve at that point.

Unlike a straight line, a curve's gradient is constantly changing, so it has to be quoted at a particular point.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A tangent to a curve can never cross that curve anywhere.


**BACK**

**False.**

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_bz277C4yDMFBJKtx`)

**FRONT**

Define the **derivative** of a function.


**BACK**

The **derivative** is a function giving the gradient of the original curve at any value of $x$.

It is also called the **gradient function**, so the derivative of $y = x^{2}$ is $2 x$, meaning the gradient at $x = 5$ is $10$.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_bM8nnnpz4zrwR92V`)

**FRONT**

How do you recognise that you need the chain rule?


**BACK**

You have a **composite function**, a function of a function, which shows up as the variable **not appearing alone**.

So $\mathrm{sin} x$ needs no chain rule, but $\mathrm{sin} ( 3 x + 2 )$ does, because $x$ is tripled and shifted before the sine is applied.

Complete the product rule, where $u$ and $v$ are functions of $x$:

`y = uv \Rightarrow \frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = \_\_\_\_\_\_ + \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed rule is:

$\frac{\text{d}y}{\text{d}x} = u \frac{\text{d}v}{\text{d}x} + v \frac{\text{d}u}{\text{d}x}$

In dash notation this is $y ' = u v ' + v u '$: each function in turn is differentiated while the other is left alone.

**FRONT**

A cuboid has volume $V = 12 x - 20 x^{2}$. Find the value of $x$ giving the maximum volume.


**BACK**

Differentiate and set to zero:

## Card 65 — question_and_answer (`fl_GkCY6wYZcXR9QfpJ`)

**FRONT**

How do you connect two known rates to find a third?


**BACK**

Multiply them with the chain rule, choosing the parts so that the shared variable cancels.

For example $\frac{\text{d}V}{\text{d}t} = \frac{\text{d}V}{\text{d}r} \times \frac{\text{d}r}{\text{d}t}$, where the $\text{d} r$ terms behave as though they cancel.

## Card 67 — question_and_answer (`fl_Fhvx3GrRWJVgCcFG`)

**FRONT**

Why must you be clear which letters are variables and which are constants?


**BACK**

Because they behave completely differently under differentiation: a constant differentiates to zero, while a variable does not.

These questions carry a lot of letters, so treating a fixed radius as a variable, or the reverse, changes the answer entirely.
