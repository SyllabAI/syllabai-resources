## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_wvPTrFyqX8Kmmvq6`)

**FRONT**

Define **displacement**.


**BACK**

**Displacement** is the distance of a particle from a fixed point, measured **relative** to that point.

It can be negative, meaning the particle is on the negative side of the fixed point, and it is zero whenever the particle returns there.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_mFTSzxDmTmfwzMH6`)

**FRONT**

A bus leaves its depot, drives a full route and returns. What are its displacement and its distance travelled?


**BACK**

Its **displacement is zero**, because it has ended where it began.

The **distance travelled** is the whole length of the route, because distance ignores direction and is always positive.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_CM6B6QZbzSP64nbw`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between velocity and speed?


**BACK**

**Velocity** is the rate of change of displacement and carries a sign showing the direction of travel.

**Speed** is its magnitude, `\left|v\right|`, so a velocity of $- 6 \text{m s}^{-1}$ is a speed of $6 \text{m s}^{-1}$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_vHvrcRzg9QFZb5Wn`)

**FRONT**

What does it mean for a particle to be "at rest"?


**BACK**

Its **velocity is zero**, so $v = 0$.

That is not the same as being at the origin, which would be $s = 0$.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_vKr6xk4cx8WNzmmW`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A particle with negative acceleration is slowing down.


**BACK**

**False.**

What matters is whether velocity and acceleration have the **same sign**: if they do the particle speeds up, and if they differ it slows down.

A particle with negative velocity and negative acceleration is speeding up, in the negative direction.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_ssfSVvvJvx7XNn4h`)

**FRONT**

What does a horizontal line on a velocity-time graph mean?


**BACK**

The velocity is **constant**, so the acceleration is zero.

Above the axis the particle moves forwards at that steady velocity, and below it moves backwards.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_FVGkZhWRB7ZbsZhR`)

**FRONT**

On a velocity-time graph, how do you get the distance rather than the displacement?


**BACK**

**Add** all the areas, treating those below the axis as positive.

Displacement instead **subtracts** the areas below the axis from those above, which is why the two answers differ whenever the particle turns round.

**BACK**

It is **zero**, since the particle is momentarily at rest as it changes direction.

On a velocity-time graph this is where the line crosses the horizontal axis.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_6t9tFbNsRHW6jS7J`)

**FRONT**

How is acceleration related to displacement directly?


**BACK**

It is the **second derivative**, $a = \frac{\text{d}^{2}s}{\text{d}t^{2}}$.

Differentiating displacement once gives velocity, and differentiating again gives acceleration.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_9nGJStYyPwwYKPtM`)

**FRONT**

A particle has displacement $x ( t ) = 2 t^{3} - 27 t^{2} + 84 t$. Find its velocity.


**BACK**

Differentiate with respect to $t$:

$x ' ( t ) = 6 t^{2} - 54 t + 84 = 6 ( t - 2 ) ( t - 7 )$

Factorised form is useful here, since it shows the particle is instantaneously at rest at $t = 2$ and $t = 7$.

`v = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed relationships are:

`s = \int v\,\text{d}t`

Integration runs the chain backwards, from acceleration to velocity to displacement.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_HHBZgx8KCmsDTYDK`)

**FRONT**

What does `\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} v(t)\,\text{d}t` give you?


**BACK**

The **displacement** of the particle between those two times.

Any interval where the velocity is negative contributes a negative amount, so this is the net change in position.

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_VcQKhyff2MhpQ245`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

`\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} v(t)\,\text{d}t` gives the distance travelled.


**BACK**

**False.**

That integral gives the **displacement**; for the distance you need `\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}} \left|v(t)\right|\text{d}t`.

The two agree only while the particle keeps moving one way, and differ as soon as it turns round.

## Card 17 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_pkGvKJWGRXG8dp86`)

Fill in the two operations that move between the three quantities:

Going from $s$ to $v$ to $a$ you `\_\_\_\_\_\_`, and going from $a$ to $v$ to $s$ you `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

The completed sentence is:

Going from $s$ to $v$ to $a$ you **differentiate**, and going from $a$ to $v$ to $s$ you **integrate**.

Each integration brings in a constant, so going backwards needs extra information each time.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_QBJM22mQ98Y3BXKZ`)

**FRONT**

On a velocity-time graph, what do the gradient and the area represent?


**BACK**

The **gradient** is the acceleration and the **area** is the displacement.

The units confirm it: a gradient in $\text{m s}^{-1}$ per second is $\text{m s}^{-2}$, and an area in $\text{m s}^{-1} \times \text{s}$ is metres.

What does the gradient of a displacement-time graph give?

Its units are metres per second, which is exactly what a gradient of metres against seconds produces.

## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_vGh9wHmFmvXCZ4nv`)

**FRONT**

A displacement-time graph is a cubic. What shapes are the velocity-time and acceleration-time graphs?


**BACK**

The velocity-time graph is a **quadratic** and the acceleration-time graph is **linear**.

Each differentiation drops the degree by one, so the three sketches follow from the first.

**FRONT**

A velocity-time graph is a straight line with negative gradient. What does the acceleration-time graph look like?
