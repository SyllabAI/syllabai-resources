## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_QyY87gBQsBSTMPVz`)

**FRONT**

Define a **radian**.


**BACK**

One **radian** is the angle at the centre of a sector whose arc length equals its radius.

So in a circle of radius 1, an arc of length 1 subtends exactly 1 radian.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_hZRBwzYWZbv9ZJdt`)

**FRONT**

How do you convert between degrees and radians?


**BACK**

Everything follows from $π$ radians $= 180 ^{\circ}$.

To go from radians to degrees multiply by $\frac{180}{π}$, and to go from degrees to radians multiply by $\frac{π}{180}$.

Each is $180 ^{\circ}$ divided by the number underneath, which is worth knowing well enough to build others from.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_QSqG6JhCB5DSqXKd`)

**FRONT**

Convert $43 . 8 ^{\circ}$ to radians, giving an exact answer.


**BACK**

Divide by $180 ^{\circ}$ and multiply by $π$:

$\frac{43.8}{180} \times π = \frac{73}{300} π$

Leaving it as a fraction of $π$ keeps the answer exact, and it is about $0 . 764$ as a decimal.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_576xc393q9fGhWyR`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

You may leave the units off an angle in radians, but never off an angle in degrees.


**BACK**

**True.**

An angle written as a multiple of $π$ is understood to be in radians, so $\frac{π}{6}$ needs no label, though **rad** may be written.

A bare number could be either, so a degree measurement always needs its $\text{^{\circ}}$ symbol.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_V93DxWXBC6ykNHHf`)

**FRONT**

Why is it easier to work in radians when a question already involves $π$?


**BACK**

Because the angles are themselves multiples of $π$, so the $π$ terms usually **cancel** during the working.

Converting to degrees would replace an exact multiple of $π$ with a decimal, which then has to be rounded.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_2tfvCKzPNdYb4zfM`)

**FRONT**

Define an **arc** and a **sector** of a circle.


**BACK**

An **arc** is a part of the **circumference**, the curved edge on its own.

A **sector** is the region enclosed by two radii and an arc, so it is the slice rather than just its crust.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_bpSRgrSDTFVWYWsW`)

**FRONT**

What makes an arc or sector **minor** rather than **major**?


**BACK**

The angle at the centre: less than $180 ^{\circ}$ gives the **minor** one, more than $180 ^{\circ}$ gives the **major** one.

The minor sector is a single slice of the pizza, and the major sector is everything left after that slice is taken.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_NYRfwwy2nxT4CWh4`)

**FRONT**

Where does $l = r θ$ come from?


**BACK**

An arc is a fraction of the whole circumference, and in radians that fraction is $\frac{θ}{2π}$.

So $l = \frac{θ}{2π} \times 2 π r$, and the $2 π$ cancels to leave $r θ$.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_48t9CYxqGGGXp8Mb`)

**FRONT**

A slice of angle $\frac{π}{6}$ is cut from a circular pizza of radius 12 cm. Find the perimeter of the piece left behind.


**BACK**

The remaining angle is $2 π - \frac{π}{6} = \frac{11π}{6}$, so the major arc is $12 \times \frac{11π}{6} = 22 π$.

The perimeter also includes the two straight cut edges, each a radius, giving $22 π + 24$ cm.

## Card 12 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_GszVgxYz9vPfsYYs`)

**FRONT**

Complete the sector area formula, where $θ$ is in radians:

`A = \_\_\_\_\_\_`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$A = \frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$

It comes from taking the fraction $\frac{θ}{2π}$ of the whole area $π r^{2}$ and cancelling.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_n3DsCdfvFRQ2dbgp`)

**FRONT**

A sector of radius 6 cm has area 30 cm<sup>2</sup>. Find the angle at the centre, in radians.


**BACK**

Substitute into $A = \frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$:

$30 = \frac{1}{2} \times 36 \times θ$

So $18 θ = 30$, giving $θ = \frac{5}{3}$ radians.

## Card 14 — true_or_false (`fl_8SPwj9mfgNWJjnvr`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The formulas $l = r θ$ and $A = \frac{1}{2} r^{2} θ$ work whether $θ$ is in degrees or radians.


**BACK**

**False.**

Both are derived using $2 π$ for a full turn, so they hold **only** when $θ$ is in radians.

With degrees you need the longer forms, $l = \frac{θ}{360} \times 2 π r$ and $A = \frac{θ}{360} \times π r^{2}$.
