## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_vxyd7h5k3kZyPDYv`)

**FRONT**

What form must a quadratic equation be in before you factorise it?


**BACK**

It must be rearranged to $a x^{2} + b x + c = 0$, with **zero on one side**.

The method depends on one side being zero, so factorising an equation such as $x^{2} + 3 x = 10$ before rearranging it will not work.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_D4RThG332kGJFty4`)

**FRONT**

Why does $( x - 3 ) ( x + 7 ) = 0$ tell you that $x = 3$ or $x = - 7$?


**BACK**

Because if two things multiply to give zero, at least one of them must **be** zero.

So either $x - 3 = 0$, giving $x = 3$, or $x + 7 = 0$, giving $x = - 7$.

Notice the solutions come out with the **opposite signs** to the numbers in the brackets.

## Card 24 — keyword_definition (`fl_pGfpJnmPKQwSp93Q`)

**FRONT**

Define a **hidden quadratic** equation.


**BACK**

A **hidden quadratic** is an equation with the structure $a ( \text{something} )^{2} + b ( \text{something} ) + c = 0$, where the something is not simply $x$.

For example $x^{4} - 3 x^{2} - 4 = 0$ is a quadratic in $x^{2}$, because $x^{4} = ( x^{2} )^{2}$.

## Card 25 — question_and_answer (`fl_qWsYfqyTjyvBrVPy`)

**FRONT**

How do you solve a hidden quadratic equation?


**BACK**

Substitute a single letter for the repeated expression, so that $u = \text{something}$ turns the equation into $a u^{2} + b u + c = 0$.

Solve that easier quadratic, then replace $u$ with what it stood for and solve each resulting equation separately.

Those final equations may have two solutions, one, or none at all.

## Card 29 — keyword_definition (`fl_wtm4ywpyYdyzbsw5`)

**FRONT**

Define the **discriminant** of a quadratic.


**BACK**

The **discriminant** of $a x^{2} + b x + c$ is $b^{2} - 4 a c$, the part of the quadratic formula that sits **under the square root**.

It is often written as the Greek capital delta, $Δ$.

## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_DVd4gHrj7Z9SmnrG`)

**FRONT**

How does the value of the discriminant decide the number of real roots?


**BACK**

If $b^{2} - 4 a c > 0$ there are **two different real roots**, because the square root gives a non-zero amount to add and subtract.

If $b^{2} - 4 a c = 0$ there is **one repeated root**, because adding and subtracting zero gives the same answer twice.

If $b^{2} - 4 a c < 0$ there are **no real roots**, because you cannot square root a negative number.


Spec links: `spcpt_NvHCD8kCbVz7tb7N`

## Card 52 — question_and_answer (`fl_kYMXZjxBkM8qNpbY`)

**FRONT**

You have drawn a boundary curve and need to know which side satisfies the inequality. What is the quickest way to decide?


**BACK**

Pick a point clearly on one side and substitute it into the inequality.

The origin is usually easiest: if $( 0 , 0 )$ satisfies $y < x^{2} + 1$, then the whole side containing the origin is the side you want.
