## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_ZSrmqdXXdFVhqZfb`)

**FRONT**

Define **simultaneous equations**.


**BACK**

**Simultaneous equations** are two or more equations that must be satisfied by the **same** values at the same time.

With two unknowns you need two equations, and solving them means finding the pair of values making both true.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_xsQrBYgp33J46nNX`)

**FRONT**

What is the first step of the elimination method?


**BACK**

Multiply one or both equations by a constant so that the coefficients of **one** unknown match.

For $3 x + 2 y = 11$ and $2 x - y = 5$, multiplying by 2 and 3 turns both $x$ terms into $6 x$.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_z5WYJ8BYmCJkWg2T`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

To eliminate a variable whose coefficients are $+ 6$ and $- 6$, you subtract one equation from the other.


**BACK**

**False.**

When the matching coefficients have **different** signs you **add** the equations, since $6 x + ( - 6 x ) = 0$.

You subtract only when the signs are the same.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_kqnGCK7QVrs25nhf`)

**FRONT**

How does the substitution method work?


**BACK**

Rearrange one equation to make an unknown the subject, such as $y = 2 x - 5$.

Put that expression into the other equation, which leaves a single equation in one unknown, then work back to find the second unknown.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_SsmssgPxr9ftRmmB`)

**FRONT**

You rearrange $2 x - y = 5$ into $y = 2 x - 5$. Which equation do you substitute this into?


**BACK**

The **other** equation, $3 x + 2 y = 11$.

Putting it back into the equation it came from just returns a statement that is always true, which tells you nothing about $x$.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_QcpzrtNfszT6Bwpw`)

**FRONT**

Why substitute $y = 2 x - 5$ into $3 x + 2 y = 11$ as $3 x + 2 ( 2 x - 5 )$, with brackets?


**BACK**

The brackets make the 2 multiply **both** terms, giving $4 x - 10$ rather than just $4 x$.

Without them the constant is left behind and the equation is no longer equivalent to the one you started with.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_Z84T6XddbXSXC76x`)

**FRONT**

What do the solutions of a pair of linear simultaneous equations mean on a graph?


**BACK**

They are the **coordinates of the point where the two lines cross**.

So solving $2 x - y = 3$ and $3 x + y = 4$ gives the intersection $( 2 , 1 )$, meaning $x = 2$ and $y = 1$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_2Jcsy49hKZ2cx9zq`)

**FRONT**

What makes a pair of simultaneous equations **quadratic**?


**BACK**

One of them contains an $x^{2}$, a $y^{2}$ or an $x y$ term, so it is not a straight line.

They are also called **non-linear** simultaneous equations.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_J8JFG2yvVqCzRpXs`)

**FRONT**

Which equation do you substitute into which?


**BACK**

Rearrange the **linear** equation and substitute it into the **quadratic** one.

Going the other way does not work, because putting a quadratic expression into the linear equation leaves you with something no simpler than you started with.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_HCn8Cqx6V2GTc5Bj`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$( 2 y + 6 )^{2} = 4 y^{2} + 36$


**BACK**

**False.**

A squared bracket must be expanded as a **double bracket**, giving $4 y^{2} + 24 y + 36$.

Squaring the terms one at a time loses the middle term completely, which changes the equation you go on to solve.

## Card 13 — true_or_false (`fl_QsnM4c7sWGPpkXwz`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A pair of quadratic simultaneous equations can have two completely different solution pairs.


**BACK**

**True.**

A line can cut a curve at two points, and each crossing gives its own pair of values.

For example $x^{2} + y^{2} = 25$ with $y - 2 x = 5$ gives both $x = 0$, $y = 5$ and $x = - 4$, $y = - 3$.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Wf7sfH7qftPn4hN6`)

**FRONT**

Why must you keep track of which $x$ value belongs with which $y$ value?


**BACK**

Because each pair is one **point of intersection**, and only those combinations satisfy both equations.

Mixing values from different pairs gives a point that lies on neither graph, even though both numbers came from correct working.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_tdMZWDy2PWMDbwMw`)

**FRONT**

You have solved the quadratic and found the values of $y$. How do you find the matching values of $x$?


**BACK**

Substitute each $y$ back into the **linear** equation, which is much less work than using the quadratic one.

Then check each finished pair in both original equations.
