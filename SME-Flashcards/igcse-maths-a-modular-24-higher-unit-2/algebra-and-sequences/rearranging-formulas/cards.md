## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_VHqB88S74fRYD3v4`)

**FRONT**

Define the term **subject** in the context of formulas.


**BACK**

The **subject **is the **variable that is by itself** on one side of the formula.

You can find its value if you substitute in the values for all the variables on the other side of the formula.

Rearranging a formula to get a different variable by itself on one side is known as '**changing the subject**'.



## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_kbKmnjv78yxtycFT`)

**FRONT**

**True or false?**

The **methods** for **changing the subject** of a formula are the same as the **methods** for **solving an equation**.


**BACK**

**True.**

The **methods** for **changing the subject** of a formula are the same as the **methods **for **solving an equation**.

For example, if you want to make $a$ the subject of a formula, 'solve' the formula for $a.$

The only difference is that the 'answer' will be another formula, not a number.

**True.**

To **rearrange** a formula with **fractions**, e.g. $\frac{2a}{7}+b=c$, you should first **multiply both sides **of the equation by the **denominator(s)**.


**BACK**

**True.**

To **rearrange** a formula with **fractions**, you should first **multiply both sides **of the equation by the **denominator(s)**.

This gets rid of the fractions and makes the formula easier to rearrange.

E.g.

`table row cell fraction numerator 2 a over denominator 7 end fraction plus b end cell equals c row cell 2 a plus 7 b end cell equals cell 7 c end cell end table`



## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_fYTk4Fdjpd6bBSfx`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If the letter you want to make the **subject **appears** twice** in a formula,

e.g. $b=\frac{2a}{c-a}$, you will **not **need to **factorise **to **change the subject**.


**BACK**

**False.**

If the letter you want to make the **subject **appears** twice** in a formula, you will often need to **factorise **to **change the subject**.

For example, to make $a$ the subject of $b=\frac{2a}{c-a}$:

- Multiply both sides by $c-a$:   `b open parentheses c minus a close parentheses equals 2 a`
- Expand the brackets:   $bc-ab=2a$
- Get the terms with $a$ onto one side:   $bc=2a+ab$
- **Factorise** the right-hand side:   `b c equals a open parentheses 2 plus b close parentheses`
- Divide both sides by $2+b$:   $\frac{bc}{2+b}=a$
