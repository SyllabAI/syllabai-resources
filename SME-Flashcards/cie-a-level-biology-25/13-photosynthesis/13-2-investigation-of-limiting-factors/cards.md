## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_WnQptqnx4GgMbYST`)

**FRONT**

Define **limiting factor**.


**BACK**

A **limiting factor** is the factor that is in **shortest supply** and so **directly limits** the rate of a process such as photosynthesis.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_W9ms9Mz6GgMFjw9W`)

**FRONT**

How can a **suspension of chloroplasts** and DCPIP be used to investigate the effect of **light wavelength** on photosynthesis?


**BACK**

Illuminate identical chloroplast and DCPIP mixtures with different **colours (wavelengths) of light**, using coloured filters and keeping light intensity constant.

Time how long the DCPIP takes to decolourise at each wavelength.


Spec links: `spcpt_6f2wrDJfjXYfXMg7`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_7ymxdnCZf2vgJH2W`)

**FRONT**

Which **wavelengths** of light give the fastest decolourisation of DCPIP, and why?


**BACK**

**Red** and **blue** light give the fastest rate.

These wavelengths are **absorbed most** by chlorophyll, so more electrons are released to reduce the DCPIP. **Green** light is mostly reflected, so the rate is slowest.


Spec links: `spcpt_6f2wrDJfjXYfXMg7`


## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_gZPpvRpg8Nq7tYYd`)

**FRONT**

How can an **aquatic plant** be used to investigate the effect of **carbon dioxide concentration** on the rate of photosynthesis?


**BACK**

Place the plant in solutions containing different concentrations of **sodium hydrogencarbonate** (a source of carbon dioxide).

Keep light intensity and temperature constant, and count the **oxygen bubbles** released per minute at each concentration.


Spec links: `spcpt_BQVPfYJ6D27Gg92z`

**FRONT**

How can an **aquatic plant** be used to investigate the effect of **temperature** on the rate of photosynthesis?

Measure the **volume of oxygen** produced, or count bubbles, per unit time at each temperature.
