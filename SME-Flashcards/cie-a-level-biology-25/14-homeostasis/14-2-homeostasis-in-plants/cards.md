## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_xxs7cpVP4Mqn4TDN`)

**FRONT**

What is a **stoma** (plural **stomata**)?


**BACK**

A **stoma** is a **pore** in the **epidermis** of a leaf, bordered by two **guard cells**, that allows **gas exchange** and **water vapour** loss.


Spec links: `spcpt_vdMkd9ZPRSF6Pmsf`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_N3QCXJZW2m2YmVrY`)

**FRONT**

Why must the **stomatal aperture** be regulated?


**BACK**

It is a **compromise**: stomata must be **open** enough to allow **carbon dioxide** uptake for **photosynthesis**, but **closed** enough to **minimise water loss** by **transpiration**.


Spec links: `spcpt_vdMkd9ZPRSF6Pmsf`


## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_8TWqfSbrJ6bQwt5J`)

**FRONT**

When are stomata typically **open** and **closed** over a day?


**BACK**

Stomata show a **daily rhythm**.

They are usually **open during the day** (for photosynthesis) and **closed at night**, when there is no photosynthesis.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_V4HMq48fjqfpZ8Gq`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Closing the stomata reduces water loss without any drawback for the plant.


**BACK**

**False.**

Closing stomata reduces **water loss**, but it also reduces **carbon dioxide** uptake, slowing **photosynthesis**.

## Card 6 — keyword_definition (`fl_NscgR3FX89PW4mh4`)

**FRONT**

What are **guard cells**?


**BACK**

**Guard cells** are pairs of specialised epidermal cells that surround each **stoma** and control its **opening and closing** by changing their **turgor**.


Spec links: `spcpt_G45qRtZr5DmYqb2m`


## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_TdYKjM59K4rTKffv`)

**FRONT**

How do guard cells cause a stoma to **open**?


**BACK**

**Potassium ions (K^+^)** are actively pumped **into** the guard cells.

This **lowers** their water potential, so **water enters** by **osmosis**, making the cells **turgid** and bowing apart to open the stoma.


Spec links: `spcpt_G45qRtZr5DmYqb2m`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_xrSjbj9g2bNwCSYP`)

**FRONT**

How do guard cells cause a stoma to **close**?


**BACK**

**K^+^** leaves the guard cells, **raising** their water potential.

**Water leaves** by osmosis, the cells become **flaccid**, and the stoma **closes**.


Spec links: `spcpt_G45qRtZr5DmYqb2m`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_2qyrsYGYGfzJmdRg`)

**FRONT**

Why does the shape of a guard cell change so it **bends** when turgid?


**BACK**

The **inner wall** (next to the pore) is **thicker** and less elastic than the outer wall.

When turgid, the thin **outer wall** stretches more, so the cell **curves**, pulling the stoma open.


Spec links: `spcpt_G45qRtZr5DmYqb2m`

## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_DMdJXcBdX5WD66nP`)

**FRONT**

What is **abscisic acid (ABA)**?


**BACK**

**Abscisic acid (ABA)** is a **plant hormone** that triggers the **closure of stomata** during times of **water stress**.


Spec links: `spcpt_4KjT4YxSc462dTKx`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_MSN8yXzZKfYqyVBj`)

**FRONT**

When is **abscisic acid** produced, and what does it do?


**BACK**

**ABA** is produced when the plant is under **water stress** (short of water).

It binds to receptors on the **guard cells** and triggers the **stomata to close**, reducing further water loss.


Spec links: `spcpt_4KjT4YxSc462dTKx`


## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_sQysQM9K8vZDnXC9`)

**FRONT**

What acts as the **second messenger** in ABA-triggered stomatal closure?


**BACK**

**Calcium ions (Ca^2+^)** act as the **second messenger** inside the guard cells.


Spec links: `spcpt_4KjT4YxSc462dTKx`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_vbXvbs243mQWjmvn`)

**FRONT**

Describe how **ABA** causes a stoma to **close**.


**BACK**

**ABA** binds to receptors on the guard cell surface membrane.

This raises **calcium ions (Ca^2+^)** inside the cell as a **second messenger**, opening channels so **K^+^** leaves.

Water then leaves by **osmosis**, the guard cells become **flaccid**, and the stoma **closes**.
