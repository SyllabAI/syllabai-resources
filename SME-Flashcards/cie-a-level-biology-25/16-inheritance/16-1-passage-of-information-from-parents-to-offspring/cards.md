## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_GbcSjhGP5x3kt34d`)

**FRONT**

Define **haploid (n)**.


**BACK**

A **haploid** cell contains a **single set** of chromosomes — one of each type, with **no homologous pairs**.

Gametes are haploid.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_vBtP4BbR89k3PRVJ`)

**FRONT**

Define **diploid (2n)**.


**BACK**

A **diploid** cell contains **two complete sets** of chromosomes (2n) — one set inherited from each parent, arranged in **homologous pairs**.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_w4SygFWp4j3k8NKF`)

**FRONT**

Why is a **reduction division** needed during meiosis in the production of gametes?


**BACK**

Meiosis **halves** the chromosome number from **diploid (2n)** to **haploid (n)**.

This means that when two gametes fuse at fertilisation, the **diploid number is restored** in the zygote.


Spec links: `spcpt_S8CtpMwBWB6j5JRP`


## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_kg3x2PjqrV9Trpmy`)

**FRONT**

What would happen to the chromosome number over generations if gametes were **diploid**?


**BACK**

The chromosome number would **double every generation**.

The reduction division in meiosis prevents this by keeping gametes haploid.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_YRwvDjMQFgFbPQNR`)

**FRONT**

What is a **homologous pair** of chromosomes?


**BACK**

A pair of chromosomes — **one maternal and one paternal** — that:

- carry the **same genes** at the **same loci**

- are the **same length** with the centromere in the **same position**

The alleles they carry may differ.


Spec links: `spcpt_WQGcBPQv8BvKYWmc`

Do **homologous chromosomes** carry identical alleles?


**BACK**

**No.**

They carry the **same genes** at the **same loci**, but the **alleles** of those genes may be **different**.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_YGhWncM2SjbZTs9n`)

**FRONT**

What are the **two divisions** of meiosis, and what separates in each?


**BACK**

**Meiosis I** (reduction division): **homologous chromosomes** are separated.

**Meiosis II**: **sister chromatids** are separated.

The result is **four haploid** cells.


Spec links: `spcpt_6J4sj9NzgFbkm8kY`

**BACK**

**Meiosis I:** prophase I → metaphase I → anaphase I → telophase I

**Meiosis II:** prophase II → metaphase II → anaphase II → telophase II


Spec links: `spcpt_6J4sj9NzgFbkm8kY`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_Y8JmVYyTKKHr8VdN`)

**FRONT**

What happens during **prophase I** of meiosis?


**BACK**

- Chromosomes **condense** and become visible

- The **nuclear envelope** breaks down

- **Homologous chromosomes pair up** to form bivalents

- **Crossing over** occurs

- The **spindle** begins to form


Spec links: `spcpt_6J4sj9NzgFbkm8kY`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_Sg8MZbJVNs4KvdJb`)

**FRONT**

What happens during **metaphase I** and **anaphase I**?


**BACK**

**Metaphase I:** bivalents line up at the equator, attached to spindle fibres, with **random orientation**.

**Anaphase I:** **homologous chromosomes** are pulled to opposite poles (sister chromatids stay joined).

## Card 26 — keyword_definition (`fl_GwGyddsj759ptRvr`)

**FRONT**

Define **crossing over**.


**BACK**

During **prophase I**, homologous chromosomes exchange sections of **chromatid**, swapping **alleles** between them.

This produces **new combinations** of alleles.


Spec links: `spcpt_cmSk2yK23Hn2QdjF`


## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_XCJhgjwRrJWp4pbN`)

**FRONT**

What is **random orientation** (independent assortment) in meiosis?


**BACK**

In **metaphase I**, each homologous pair lines up at the equator **independently** of the others.

So maternal and paternal chromosomes are distributed to gametes in **random combinations**.


Spec links: `spcpt_cmSk2yK23Hn2QdjF`


## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_RXPSrPZwHQVxMBsv`)

**FRONT**

How does **crossing over** produce genetic variation?


**BACK**

It swaps alleles between homologous chromosomes, creating **new allele combinations** on each chromosome and therefore **genetically different gametes**.


Spec links: `spcpt_cmSk2yK23Hn2QdjF`


## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_mqxbvhvRyBMBFHSQ`)

**FRONT**

How does **random orientation** of chromosomes produce variation?


**BACK**

Homologous pairs (and sister chromatids in meiosis II) can line up in **many different combinations**.

This mixes maternal and paternal chromosomes, giving a huge variety of possible **gametes**.


Spec links: `spcpt_cmSk2yK23Hn2QdjF`


## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_vXnhHJRSY7BGSp5w`)

**FRONT**

How does the **random fusion of gametes** at fertilisation produce variation?


**BACK**

Any male gamete can fuse with any female gamete.

Because the gametes are already genetically different, this random fusion produces **genetically different individuals**.
