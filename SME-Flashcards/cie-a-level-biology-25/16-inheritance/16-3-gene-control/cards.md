## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_MRB8wysFscRwhcgZ`)

**FRONT**

Define **structural gene**.


**BACK**

A gene that codes for a protein used in the cell's **metabolism or structure** (e.g. an enzyme or structural protein) — not involved in regulating other genes.


Spec links: `spcpt_W58FtXmB9Zh2FZxG`


## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_3ZmdCyVBFNJBvpzn`)

**FRONT**

Define **regulatory gene**.


**BACK**

A gene that codes for a protein (such as a **repressor** or transcription factor) that **controls the expression** of other genes.


Spec links: `spcpt_W58FtXmB9Zh2FZxG`


## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_PxKQMzBhNQqJR86N`)

**FRONT**

Define **inducible enzyme**.


**BACK**

An enzyme that is **normally absent** and is only **synthesised** when its **substrate (inducer)** is present.


Spec links: `spcpt_W58FtXmB9Zh2FZxG`


## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_CCwRfCvZWfKXb3kh`)

**FRONT**

Define **repressible enzyme**.


**BACK**

An enzyme that is **normally produced**, but whose synthesis is **switched off** when the end-product accumulates.


Spec links: `spcpt_W58FtXmB9Zh2FZxG`


## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_YkjJRgzyGcZKbmD5`)

**FRONT**

What is the key difference between **structural** and **regulatory** genes?


**BACK**

**Structural genes** code for proteins used in metabolism or structure.

**Regulatory genes** code for proteins that **control the expression** of other genes.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_hwz2kJfqvSZQRSts`)

**FRONT**

What is the **operator** in the lac operon?


**BACK**

A region of DNA next to the structural genes where the **repressor protein** can bind to block transcription.


Spec links: `spcpt_btgtPGdWMPHrt9fW`


## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_SWJ6RBTPN6nkD7rW`)

**FRONT**

In the lac operon, what happens when **lactose is absent**?


**BACK**

The **regulatory gene** produces a **repressor protein**.

The repressor binds to the **operator**, blocking **RNA polymerase**, so the structural genes are **not transcribed** and no enzymes are made.


Spec links: `spcpt_btgtPGdWMPHrt9fW`


## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_DKyfgVhH2RFNXV9V`)

**FRONT**

In the lac operon, what happens when **lactose is present**?


**BACK**

**Lactose binds** to the repressor protein, changing its shape so it can no longer bind the operator.

**RNA polymerase** can now transcribe the structural genes, so the enzymes to metabolise lactose are made.


Spec links: `spcpt_btgtPGdWMPHrt9fW`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_ZPYQSJbBMyGyHKqV`)

Are the lactose-metabolising enzymes **inducible** or **repressible**?


**BACK**

**Inducible** — they are only made when **lactose** (the inducer) is present.


Spec links: `spcpt_btgtPGdWMPHrt9fW`

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_Yy5tYD4Zdv273ws4`)

**FRONT**

How does **gibberellin** activate genes?


**BACK**

Gibberellin causes the **breakdown of DELLA protein repressors**.

This releases the transcription factors that promote **transcription**, so the target genes are switched on.
