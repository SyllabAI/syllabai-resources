## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_B3zmZ2PwYbkgB7xT`)

**FRONT**

Define **enzyme**.


**BACK**

An **enzyme** is a **biological catalyst** that increases the **rate** of a reaction and remains **unchanged** at the end of the reaction.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_Vw2YnMSs9dyg8Vnx`)

**FRONT**

Describe the **lock-and-key hypothesis** of enzyme action.


**BACK**

The **active site** has a **rigid, fixed shape** that is **complementary** to only one substrate.

The substrate fits into it like a **key into a lock**.


Spec links: `spcpt_SQBJcHQvQzxzY6DM`


## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_BFfgkwZgtjwFFZ5M`)

**FRONT**

Describe the **induced-fit hypothesis** of enzyme action.


**BACK**

The **active site** is **flexible**, not rigid.

As the substrate binds, the active site **changes shape slightly** to fit more closely around it.


Spec links: `spcpt_SQBJcHQvQzxzY6DM`

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_M2WJvfdTfB6dckR8`)

**FRONT**

Define the **induced-fit hypothesis**.


**BACK**

A model in which the **active site** is not exactly complementary at first, but **changes shape** slightly to fit closely around the substrate.


Spec links: `spcpt_pfk32Wj43vx3M5YT`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_G4PGMZ47XWmFbHx5`)

**FRONT**

How does the **induced-fit** hypothesis differ from the **lock-and-key** hypothesis?


**BACK**

In **lock-and-key**, the active site is **already complementary** to the substrate.

In **induced-fit**, the active site **moulds/changes shape** around the substrate as it binds.


Spec links: `spcpt_pfk32Wj43vx3M5YT`


## Card 21 — question_and_answer (`fl_jY64K5vfRF6cv6Yf`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

It **changes shape** to fit more closely around the substrate, forming an **enzyme-substrate complex**.


Spec links: `spcpt_pfk32Wj43vx3M5YT`
