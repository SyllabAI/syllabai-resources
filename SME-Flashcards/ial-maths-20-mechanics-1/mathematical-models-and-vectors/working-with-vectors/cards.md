## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_8ycvFCdhFbfwzsBN`)

**FRONT**

Define a **unit vector**, and say what $i$ and $j$ are.


**BACK**

A **unit vector** is a vector whose **magnitude is 1**.

$i$ and $j$ are the unit vectors in the positive **horizontal** and positive **vertical** directions, and they are perpendicular to each other.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_sFp8n9bxhGBKsSb4`)

**FRONT**

The column vector `\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -7 \end{pmatrix}` can also be written $3 i - 7 j$. What does each of the two numbers tell you?


**BACK**

The 3 is the number of units moved in the positive **horizontal** direction, so 3 units to the **right**.

The $- 7$ is the number of units moved vertically, and the minus sign makes it 7 units **down**.

The two forms mean exactly the same thing, but a final answer should be given in $i$, $j$ form, with column vectors kept for the working.

## Card 3 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_kwgc4cfXwqGKbdSX`)

**FRONT**

Fill in the blanks to complete the formula for the magnitude of a vector:

`\left| x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} \right| = \sqrt{\_\_\_\_\_\_ + \_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

`\left| x\mathbf{i} + y\mathbf{j} \right| = \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}`

It is Pythagoras' theorem: the two components are the perpendicular sides of a right-angled triangle, and the vector itself is the hypotenuse.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_TbfJBPCB7DpGDY88`)

**FRONT**

Two forces are given in $i$, $j$ form. How do you find their resultant, and what does the resultant represent?


**BACK**

Add the $i$ components together and add the $j$ components together, collecting like terms exactly as you would in algebra.

The **resultant** is the single force that has the same effect on the particle as the two original forces acting together.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_4gHsQ2Xgt8BSVPrT`)

**FRONT**

A resultant force is `\left( -3\mathbf{i} + 9\mathbf{j} \right)` N. Find its magnitude, leaving your answer as a simplified surd.


**BACK**

Square both components, add them and take the square root:

`\sqrt{\left( -3 \right)^{2} + 9^{2}} = \sqrt{90} = 3\sqrt{10}\text{ N}`

The minus sign disappears when the component is squared, so a magnitude comes out positive whichever way the force acts.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_VZ6M8Jzw28StFHgc`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If two position vectors have the same $j$ component, the one with the greater $i$ component is due east of the other.


**BACK**

**True.**

Equal $j$ components mean the two points are the same distance north, so the only thing separating them is how far east each one is.

The greater $i$ component is therefore further east, and the direction from the other point to it is due east.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_c2sVB945mqMSDDx7`)

**FRONT**

The magnitude of a displacement vector and the magnitude of a velocity vector each have their own name. What are they?


**BACK**

The magnitude of a **displacement** is a **distance**, and the magnitude of a **velocity** is a **speed**.

In each case taking the magnitude keeps the size and drops the direction, which is what turns the vector into a scalar.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_JCN9FqhQgR9FpZ2z`)

**FRONT**

You need the direction of a vector as a **bearing**. Which unit vector points north, which points east, and how is a bearing measured?


**BACK**

$j$ points **north** and $i$ points **east**.

A bearing is measured from **north**, turning **clockwise**.

So a particle moving due north has a velocity of the form $k j$ with $k > 0$, and one moving due east has a velocity of the form $k i$.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_VQ36R59CDnQVGmVq`)

**FRONT**

Why should you sketch a vector before using $\mathrm{tan}^{-1}$ to find its direction?


**BACK**

Because $\mathrm{tan}^{-1}$ of the two components only gives you the **acute angle** the vector makes with the horizontal.

The sketch shows which way the vector actually points, and so what has to be done to that acute angle.

For $- 3 i + 9 j$ the acute angle is `\tan^{-1}\left( \frac{9}{3} \right) = 71.6^{\circ}`, and the bearing turns out to be $270 + 71 . 6 = 341 . 6 ^{\circ}$.
