## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_4RxxNd2MkXKnSnHM`)

**FRONT**

Define **fixed costs**.


**BACK**

**Fixed costs** are costs that do not change as the level of output changes, such as rent, insurance and management salaries.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_RvMY97dpHXZBRb6z`)

**FRONT**

Define **variable costs**.


**BACK**

**Variable costs** are costs that vary directly with output, such as raw materials and the wages of workers directly involved in production.


Spec links: `spcpt_YdcBn6gZsXRYdZb7`


## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_BRTxpWvzywVs5smp`)

**FRONT**

Define **total cost**.


**BACK**

**Total cost** is the sum of total fixed costs and total variable costs.

**Total revenue** is the total value of all sales a firm generates, calculated as selling price multiplied by quantity sold.

**BACK**

**BACK**

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_NjGWG6wnhQHzKbnr`)

**FRONT**

When does a firm break even?


**BACK**

A firm **breaks even** when **total revenue equals total costs (TR = TC)**, covering all its costs without making a profit or a loss.


Spec links: `spcpt_cJjMc8Q8MhjGyw5B`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_tGpjBSscSsmhy25r`)

**FRONT**

How is profit calculated?


**BACK**

**Profit** is calculated as **total revenue minus total costs (TR − TC)**.


Spec links: `spcpt_cJjMc8Q8MhjGyw5B`

**BACK**

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_jkkCbYKN6fRvQQF6`)

**FRONT**

Define **economies of scale**.


**BACK**

**Economies of scale** are the efficiencies a firm gains as it increases its scale of output, which lower its average cost of production.


Spec links: `spcpt_7h2HTcHgs6v6ntXx`


## Card 15 — keyword_definition (`fl_r74D4zM8cgzSHN6D`)

**FRONT**

Define **diseconomies of scale**.


**BACK**

**Diseconomies of scale** occur when a firm increases its scale of output beyond a certain point, causing its average costs to rise.


Spec links: `spcpt_7h2HTcHgs6v6ntXx`


## Card 16 — keyword_definition (`fl_RSFZQZNYzTdGB5Md`)

**FRONT**

Define **internal economies of scale**.


**BACK**

**Internal economies of scale** occur as a result of the growth in the scale of production within the firm itself.


Spec links: `spcpt_cPBGtXRrjNcvNnPx`


## Card 17 — keyword_definition (`fl_s3Xw3bk89bXnWqs7`)

**FRONT**

Define **external economies of scale**.


**BACK**

**External economies of scale** arise from external changes in the locality and industry that lower a firm's average costs as the industry grows.


Spec links: `spcpt_MMRCfVsqJNqz9mTB`


## Card 18 — keyword_definition (`fl_pMfV438Hf9Qm3y87`)

**FRONT**

Define **internal diseconomies of scale**.


**BACK**

**Internal diseconomies of scale** occur when an increase in the scale of output raises the cost per unit due to factors generated inside the business.


Spec links: `spcpt_sgJJSFtnynCFrNfs`


## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_8f4XfZwmfwHhpBXd`)

**FRONT**

**BACK**

A **purchasing economy of scale** lowers average cost because large firms buy raw materials in greater volumes and receive a **bulk purchase discount**.

**BACK**

Large firms enjoy a **financial economy of scale** because they often receive **lower interest rates** on loans, as they are perceived as less risky.
