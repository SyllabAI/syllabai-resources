What does the constant $m$ represent in the **equation of a straight line** $y=mx+c$?


**BACK**

In the **equation of a straight line** $y=mx+c$, the constant $m$ represents the **gradient **of the line.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_7cFKthVpJgWjhJTw`)

**FRONT**

How do you find the **equation **of a line if you know the **gradient **and a **point **on the line?


**BACK**

If you know the **gradient **of a line and a **point **on the line, then to find the equation:

- write $y=mx+c$ where $m$ is the gradient
- Substitute the coordinates of the point into the equation for $x$ and $y$
- Solve the equation to find $c$



## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_mhpbyF3mCKxJdgDK`)

**FRONT**

How can you find the equation of a straight line if you have the coordinates of **two points** on the line?


**BACK**

If you have the coordinates of **two points** on a straight line then you can find the equation of the line $y=mx+c$.

1. Find the gradient, $m$, using the coordinates (e.g. form a right-angled triangle)
2. Substitute one pair of coordinates into $y=mx+c$ to find $c$



## Card 8 — true_or_false (`fl_j442P8ggDBF3GZSy`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The **gradient **of the line with the equation $2x+y+5=0$ is 2.


**BACK**

**False.**

The **gradient **of the line with the equation $2x+y+5=0$ is **not **2. You need to write it in the form $y=mx+c$ first, giving $y=-2x-5$. Therefore the **gradient **is **-2**.
