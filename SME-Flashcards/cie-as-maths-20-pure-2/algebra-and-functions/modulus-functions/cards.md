## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_R7NyZMtWfnYGpzNp`)

**FRONT**

Define the **modulus function**.


**BACK**

The **modulus** of a number is its size with any minus sign removed, so `\left|x\right| = x` when $x \geq 0$, and `\left|x\right| = - x` when $x < 0$.

It is also called the **absolute value**, so `\left|5\right| = 5` and `\left|- 5\right| = 5`.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_nPKvgC4Mg5HrHFBt`)

**FRONT**

Starting from the line $y = a x + b$, how do you sketch `y = \left|a x + b\right|`?


**BACK**

Sketch the line first, then reflect the part below the $x$-axis in the $x$-axis.

Whatever was already above the axis stays exactly where it is, which is why the result has a sharp corner rather than a smooth turn.

**BACK**

The completed coordinates are:

The sign flips because the vertex sits where the bracket is zero, and $x + p = 0$ gives $x = - p$.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_vyM49NFKjwFVcykf`)

**FRONT**

What decides whether `y = a \left|x + p\right| + q` opens upwards or downwards?


**BACK**

The sign of $a$ decides it: the graph opens upwards into a V shape when $a > 0$, and is turned upside down when $a < 0$.

A modulus is never negative, so only a negative $a$ can send the graph downwards.

## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_yj75nGTQB3J3XP6T`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

For a function $\text{f}$, the graphs of `y = \left|\text{f} \left(x\right)\right|` and `y = \text{f} \left(\left|x\right|\right)` are the same.


**BACK**

**False.**

Take `\text{f} \left(x\right) = 2 x - 6`: the graph of `y = \left|2 x - 6\right|` has its corner at `\left(3 , 0\right)`, while `y = 2 \left|x\right| - 6` has its corner at `\left(0 , - 6\right)` and dips below the $x$-axis.

The modulus is applied to the **output** in one and to the **input** in the other, and those are different operations.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_zbMMkZt8Hnj7WnNq`)

**FRONT**

For $a \neq 0$, how many roots can the graph of `y = a \left|x + p\right| + q` have, and what decides the number?


**BACK**

It can have **zero, one or two** roots.

Which of the three you get depends on where the vertex sits relative to the $x$-axis and which way the graph opens: a vertex on the axis gives exactly one root, and a graph opening away from the axis gives none at all.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_j4JwkdXbW7QzQQrb`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Whatever the function $\text{f}$, the graph of `y = \text{f} \left(\left|x\right|\right)` is symmetrical about the $y$-axis.


**BACK**

**True.**

Replacing $x$ by $- x$ leaves `\left|x\right|` unchanged, so the graph reaches the same height at $- x$ as it does at $x$.

That holds for every $\text{f}$, because the reflection happens to the input before $\text{f}$ is applied to it.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_5bWHc2zQkZCYbffz`)

**FRONT**

Two non-parallel straight lines meet once. Why can an equation involving a modulus have more solutions than that?


**BACK**

Because the modulus **reflects part of a graph** upwards, and the reflected piece can cross the other graph as well.

That produces an intersection the unreflected lines never had.

## Card 9 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_GVYbvvtJSdGZyT7m`)

**FRONT**

To solve `\left|\text{f} \left(x\right)\right| = \left|\text{g} \left(x\right)\right|`, solve both `\text{f} \left(x\right) = \text{g} \left(x\right)` and `\text{f} \left(x\right) = \_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

To solve `\left|\text{f}\left(x\right)\right| = \left|\text{g}\left(x\right)\right|`, solve both `\text{f}\left(x\right) = \text{g}\left(x\right)` and `\text{f}\left(x\right) = - \text{g}\left(x\right)`.

The two cases arise because each side can take either sign once the modulus is removed.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_yTq5ym8SKdg3Pvkz`)

**FRONT**

What should you do before solving a modulus equation algebraically?


**BACK**

**Sketch both graphs**, including the reflected parts, and locate the intersections.

The sketch tells you how many solutions there should be, and therefore which algebraic answers to keep.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_GRmTFsk3PB8nRp7Q`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Every solution of $x - 4 = 2 x - 5$ is also a solution of `\left|x - 4\right| = 2 x - 5`.


**BACK**

**False.**

$x = 1$ satisfies the first, but `\left|1 - 4\right| = 3` while `2 \left(1\right) - 5 = - 3`, so it fails the second.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_7yqHQMzXrhKtBstV`)

**FRONT**

How do you solve `\left|\text{f} \left(x\right)\right| = \text{g} \left(x\right)`, with a modulus on one side only?


**BACK**

Solve both `\text{f}\left(x\right) = \text{g}\left(x\right)` and `- \text{f}\left(x\right) = \text{g}\left(x\right)`.

Then test each answer in the original equation, because a modulus can never be negative, so any root making `\text{g}\left(x\right)` negative has to be thrown out.
