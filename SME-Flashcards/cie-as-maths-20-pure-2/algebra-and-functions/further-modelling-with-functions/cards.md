## Card 1 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_ZFd6nSsw5qqT8rB4`)

**FRONT**

Complete the three jobs done in mathematical modelling:

An **assumption** simplifies a real-life situation to make the mathematics `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.

A **criticism** points out a way in which the model is not `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.

A **refinement** changes the model to make its predictions more `\_\_\_\_\_\_`.


**BACK**

The completed sentences are:

An **assumption** simplifies a real-life situation to make the mathematics **easier**.

A **criticism** points out a way in which the model is not **suitable**.

A **refinement** changes the model to make its predictions more **realistic**.

A criticism only identifies the flaw, whereas a refinement does something about it.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_wpKyPqzBDvwmmxjz`)

**FRONT**

In the model $N = 64 + 4 m$, $m$ is the number of months after 1 January. Why does the number sold **during January** correspond to $m = 1$?


**BACK**

Because January is not over until one whole month has passed, so the end of January is $m = 1$ rather than $m = 0$.

Putting $m = 0$ would give the position at the very start of January, before any of that month's sales had happened.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_brwgFB8ym9s69rqN`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A quadratic function modelling a thrown stone describes only part of the curve.


**BACK**

**True.**

The stone is only in the air between the throw and the landing, so the model applies over a limited interval such as $0 \leq t \leq 10$ and the rest of the parabola describes nothing real.

Outside that interval the curve is still a perfectly good parabola, it simply stops corresponding to anything the stone does.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_pCYN5r6pMFKPxFbJ`)

**FRONT**

When does a modelling question need you to solve an equation rather than substitute?


**BACK**

Substitute when you are given the **input** and asked for the output, and solve when you are given the **output** and asked for the input.

Asking for the height of a stone after $4$ seconds is a substitution, whereas asking how long it takes to reach the sea sets the height to $0$ and solves for the time.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_Txfv3VdZJnVxzWQs`)

**FRONT**

Why write a quadratic model in completed square form?


**BACK**

Because it hands you the model's **maximum or minimum** directly, with no differentiation needed.

Writing $h = 32 + 4 . 8 t - 0 . 8 t^{2}$ as `h = - 0.8 \left(t - 3\right)^{2} + 39.2` shows at once that the greatest height is $39 . 2 \text{m}$, reached at $t = 3 \text{s}$.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_m5n6TkNHfHSjgzC6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Every solution of an equation coming from a model is a valid answer to the modelling question.


**BACK**

**False.**

A model carries restrictions from the situation it describes, and any solution that breaks one of them has to be discarded.

Solving $32 + 4 . 8 t - 0 . 8 t^{2} = 0$ for the time a stone hits the sea gives $t = 10$ and $t = - 4$, and only $t = 10 \text{s}$ makes sense for a time measured from the throw.
