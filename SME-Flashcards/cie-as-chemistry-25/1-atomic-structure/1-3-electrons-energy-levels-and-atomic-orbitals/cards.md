## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_xDFDFkJWTZv7wSyV`)

**FRONT**

Why is the **4s orbital** filled before the **3d orbital**?


**BACK**

The **4s orbital** has slightly lower **energy** than the **3d orbital**, despite being in a higher principal quantum shell. Electrons always fill the **lowest energy** orbital first, so 4s fills before 3d.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_N8GbkdTSNHwgGVmZ`)

**FRONT**

Define **orbital**.


**BACK**

An **orbital** is a region of space around the **nucleus** where there is a high probability of finding an **electron**. Each orbital can hold a maximum of **2 electrons**.


Spec links: `spcpt_z2rXCjQxxfBQW8d3`


## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_PGTzFytNZs3CT7j6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A higher **principal quantum number** corresponds to a higher energy **shell**.


**BACK**

**True.**

As the **principal quantum number** (*n*) increases, the shell is further from the nucleus and has higher **energy**. Electrons in outer shells are held less tightly than those in inner shells.

**FRONT**

**BACK**

**Degenerate** orbitals are orbitals within the same **subshell** that have identical **energies**. For example, the three **p** orbitals (p<sub>x</sub>, p<sub>y</sub> and p<sub>z</sub>) within a given shell are all degenerate.

## Card 15 — true_or_false (`fl_gGZCqdcsRS4VBSgc`)

**FRONT**

All orbitals within the same **subshell** have the same **energy**.


**BACK**

**True.**

Orbitals within the same subshell are **degenerate**, meaning they have identical energies. For example, p<sub>x</sub>, p<sub>y</sub> and p<sub>z</sub> all have the same energy within a given **p** subshell.

**BACK**

The three **p** orbitals (p<sub>x</sub>, p<sub>y</sub> and p<sub>z</sub>) are oriented along the **x**, **y** and **z** axes. They point **perpendicular** to one another, so the lobes of each orbital are at right angles to the other two.
