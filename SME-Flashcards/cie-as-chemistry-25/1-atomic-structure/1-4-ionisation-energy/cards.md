## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_3mRfkdg9dcrd6Fjx`)

**FRONT**

Define **ionisation energy**.


**BACK**

An **ionisation energy** (**IE**) is the energy required to remove one mole of electrons from one mole of gaseous atoms of an element, forming one mole of gaseous 1+ ions.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_4rSWgkDZvsH4WN8f`)

**FRONT**

Why are ionisation energies always positive values?


**BACK**

**Ionisation** is endothermic, so energy must be supplied to overcome the force of attraction between the electron and the positive nucleus.

**FRONT**

Why must atoms in ionisation energy equations be in the gaseous state?


**BACK**

Ionisation energy is defined specifically for gaseous species. Including solid or liquid atoms would incorporate the energy of vaporisation, which is not part of the IE definition.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_4nCWm69HYqMvNpWB`)

**FRONT**

Define **electron shielding**.


**BACK**

An **electron shielding** is the reduction in effective nuclear charge experienced by outer electrons, caused by repulsion from inner-shell electrons that partially block the attraction of the nucleus.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_rDwS7ht7w8PKc5jd`)

**FRONT**

Explain why first ionisation energy decreases down a group.


**BACK**

Down a group, atomic radius increases and shielding increases as more electron shells are added. Both effects weaken the attraction between the nucleus and outer electrons, outweighing the increased nuclear charge.

**True or False?**

Successive ionisation energies for a given element always increase.


**BACK**

**True.**

Removing each electron leaves an increasingly positive ion, so the remaining electrons are held more tightly by the nucleus and require more energy to remove.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_CKWhDXdfcSQyxQy8`)

**FRONT**

Why is the first ionisation energy of oxygen lower than that of nitrogen?


**BACK**

Oxygen has two electrons paired in the 2p<sup>x</sup> orbital. Spin-pair repulsion makes it easier to remove one of these paired electrons, lowering the first IE relative to nitrogen, where each 2p orbital holds only one electron.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_QXKDnxvhFT8Rf443`)

**FRONT**

State four factors that affect ionisation energy.


**BACK**

1. Nuclear charge: higher charge increases IE.
2. Distance from the nucleus: greater distance decreases IE.
3. Electron shielding: more inner shells decrease IE.
4. Spin-pair repulsion: paired electrons in an orbital decrease IE.

**FRONT**

Define **successive ionisation energies**.


**BACK**

**Successive ionisation energies** are the energies required to remove each subsequent mole of electrons from one mole of a gaseous element, one electron at a time from an increasingly positive ion.

**FRONT**

How can successive ionisation energy data be used to determine an element's group?


**BACK**

Identify the largest jump between consecutive ionisation energies. The number of electrons removed before that jump equals the number of valence electrons, which gives the group number for s- and p-block elements.
