## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_YmkTWvNNfyb46DF8`)

**FRONT**

Define **oxidation number**.


**BACK**

An **oxidation number** (or oxidation state) is a number assigned to each atom in a compound to track electron transfer. A positive value indicates loss of electrons and a negative value indicates gain of electrons.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_w9nCdFNgyGNhFGS8`)

**FRONT**

What is the oxidation number of sulfur in SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>?


**BACK**

Oxygen contributes 4 x (-2) = -8. The overall charge is -2. So S = -2 - (-8) = **+6**.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The oxidation number of oxygen is always -2 in all compounds.


**BACK**

**False.**

Oxygen is usually -2, but it is -1 in peroxides (e.g. H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>) and +2 in F<sub>2</sub>O, where fluorine is more electronegative.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_bGD6GY7mQgw7qTYs`)

**FRONT**

Define **disproportionation reaction**.


**BACK**

A **disproportionation reaction** is one in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced. For example, chlorine reacting with hot concentrated NaOH forms Cl<sup>-</sup> (reduced) and ClO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> (oxidised).

List the five steps for balancing a redox equation using oxidation numbers.


**BACK**

1. Write the unbalanced equation and identify atoms that **change in oxidation number**. 2. Deduce the **oxidation number changes**. 3. **Balance the oxidation number changes**. 4. **Balance the charges**. 5. **Balance the atoms**.
