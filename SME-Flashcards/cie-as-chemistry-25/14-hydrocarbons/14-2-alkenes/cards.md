## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_gXSP4Bf5xsBjfm6q`)

**FRONT**

What are the three methods by which alkenes can be produced?


**BACK**

Alkenes can be produced by:

1. **Elimination** reaction of a halogenoalkane with ethanolic NaOH.
2. **Dehydration** of an alcohol using a hot Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> catalyst.
3. **Cracking** of long-chain hydrocarbons from crude oil.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_YCPFWSjPCWz2jSxr`)

**FRONT**

Why must oxygen be excluded during cracking of crude oil?


**BACK**

Oxygen would cause **combustion** of the hydrocarbons, producing CO<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub>O rather than the desired smaller alkane and **alkene** molecules.

**BACK**

## Card 38 — question_and_answer (`fl_CmDMXjDNgqXhWWFx`)

**FRONT**

Why is a tertiary carbocation more stable than a primary carbocation?


**BACK**

A tertiary carbocation has **three alkyl groups** attached to the positively charged carbon. Each alkyl group donates electron density towards the cation through the **inductive effect**, spreading the positive charge and making the ion more stable.
