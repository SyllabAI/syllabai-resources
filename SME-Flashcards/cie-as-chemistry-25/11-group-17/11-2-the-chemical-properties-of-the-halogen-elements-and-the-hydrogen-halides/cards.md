## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_z6cBNKSt8ByVZJ5p`)

**FRONT**

Why does the oxidising power of halogens decrease going down Group 17?


**BACK**

Going down Group 17, the **atomic radius** increases, so the outer shells are further from the nucleus. An incoming electron experiences greater **shielding** from the nuclear charge. The halogen's ability to attract (accept) electrons — its **oxidising power** — therefore decreases.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_nzW7TRsdjZRxX9bm`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Bromine can displace chloride ions from a solution of NaCl.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Chlorine** is more reactive than bromine, not the other way around. A more reactive halogen can displace a less reactive one. Bromine cannot displace chloride ions because Cl<sub>2</sub> is the stronger **oxidising agent**.

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_zNqC7FMKKGBnhKFb`)

**FRONT**

Define **hydrogen halide**.


**BACK**

A **hydrogen halide** is a compound formed by the reaction of a halogen with hydrogen gas (e.g. HF, HCl, HBr, HI). They are all polar covalent molecules that dissolve in water to form acidic solutions.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_xkJz9N4xHzJ6nZ84`)

**FRONT**

Describe the conditions under which H<sub>2</sub> reacts with each of F<sub>2</sub>, Cl<sub>2</sub>, Br<sub>2</sub> and I<sub>2</sub>.


**BACK**

H<sub>2</sub> + F<sub>2</sub>: **explosive**, even in cool/dark conditions. H<sub>2</sub> + Cl<sub>2</sub>: explosive **in sunlight**. H<sub>2</sub> + Br<sub>2</sub>: reacts **slowly on heating**. H<sub>2</sub> + I<sub>2</sub>: forms an **equilibrium mixture** on heating (reversible reaction).

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_zBbxz3wBxpJ6vh8h`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

HF is more thermally stable than HI.


**BACK**

**True.**

**HF** has a short, strong H–F bond (fluorine is small), giving it high **thermal stability**. **HI** has a longer, weaker H–I bond due to iodine's larger atomic radius, so it is far less thermally stable.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_pm3c9fcYm39h73kK`)

**FRONT**

Write the ionic equation for the displacement of bromide ions by chlorine.


**BACK**

Cl<sub>2</sub> (aq) + 2Br<sup>-</sup> (aq) → 2Cl<sup>-</sup> (aq) + Br<sub>2</sub> (aq)

Chlorine is the **oxidising agent** (it gains electrons); bromide ions are **oxidised** to bromine.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_RDfFRbbyC4sCPMjh`)

**FRONT**

Explain why the thermal stability of HI is lower than that of HCl.


**BACK**

Iodine has a much larger atomic radius than chlorine. The **H–I bond** is longer and therefore **weaker** than the H–Cl bond. Less energy is needed to break H–I, so HI decomposes more easily on heating and has **lower thermal stability**.
