## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_9XDmdTgzZFnqj3Nt`)

**FRONT**

Define **Hess's Law**.


**BACK**

**Hess's Law** states that the total enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the route taken, as long as the initial and final conditions are the same.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_BSFJ2Dz2WDFqf7X3`)

**FRONT**

Using Hess's Law, write the equation to calculate ΔH<sub>r</sub> from standard enthalpy changes of formation.


**BACK**

Δ*H*<sub>r</sub> = ΣΔ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>f</sub> (**products**) - ΣΔ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>f</sub> (**reactants**). This is derived from the cycle: ΔH<sub>2</sub> = ΔH<sub>1</sub> + ΔH<sub>r</sub>, so ΔH<sub>r</sub> = ΔH<sub>2</sub> - ΔH<sub>1</sub>.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_gVZsfY54PTSrJRmW`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Hess's Law can be used to calculate enthalpy changes that cannot be measured directly by calorimetry.


**BACK**

**True.**

For example, the enthalpy of formation of propane cannot be measured directly because hydrogen and carbon do not react under standard conditions. Hess's Law allows it to be calculated indirectly.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_56Mg3WXVpm8ZWkTJ`)

**FRONT**

Define **standard enthalpy change of atomisation**.


**BACK**

A **standard enthalpy change of atomisation** (Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>at</sub>) is the enthalpy change when one mole of gaseous atoms is formed from an element in its standard state. For example: ½H<sub>2</sub> (g) → H (g).

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_YzYvVyDqYbWrjw8x`)

**FRONT**

Calculate Δ*H*<sub>r</sub> for 2NaHCO<sub>3</sub> (s) → Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub> (s) + CO<sub>2</sub> (g) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l), given: Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>f</sub> [NaHCO<sub>3</sub>] = -950.8, [Na<sub>2</sub>CO<sub>3</sub>] = -1130.7, [CO<sub>2</sub>] = -393.5, [H<sub>2</sub>O] = -285.8 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.


**BACK**

Δ*H*<sub>r</sub> = ((-1130.7) + (-393.5) + (-285.8)) - (2 x -950.8) = **+91.6 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_2C67CV8thnZ49V9D`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

When applying Hess's Law, the number of moles of each compound must be taken into account when using Δ*H*<sub>f</sub> values.


**BACK**

**True.**

If two moles of a compound appear in the equation, the Δ*H*<sub>f</sub> value for that compound must be multiplied by 2.

## Card 8 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_5nr7Q6T3b9n4qQHB`)

The average C-H bond enthalpy in methane is found by calculating Δ*H* for breaking all four bonds, then **..........**  by 4. Using Δ*H*<sup>θ</sup><sub>f</sub> [CH<sub>4</sub>] = -74.8 and atomisation data, Δ*H* = +1664.5, giving an average bond enthalpy of **..........**  kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.
