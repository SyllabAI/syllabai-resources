## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_6QjthPpyYq5h28qg`)

**BACK**

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_BZzGvg9h4cX7M9sK`)

**FRONT**

Why does free rotation about a C=C double bond not occur, but free rotation about a C–C single bond does?


**BACK**

Rotation about a C=C bond would break the **π bond** (which requires energy to re-form), so it does not occur freely. A C–C single bond has only a **σ bond**, which allows free rotation.


Spec links: `spcpt_d2F29zJhG2yRcw8x`


## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_NsvXTZzbxrFmfYrK`)

**FRONT**

Define **chiral centre**.


**BACK**

A **chiral centre** is a carbon atom bonded to **four different** atoms or groups of atoms, giving rise to two non-superimposable mirror image optical isomers.


Spec links: `spcpt_X7vDgdxQs3VpCRb6`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_NXQGvV8M8q3zGWRZ`)

**FRONT**

How many optical isomers can a molecule with two chiral centres form?


**BACK**

A molecule with two chiral centres can form up to **four** optical isomers (2<sup>2</sup> = 4). The maximum number of stereoisomers is **2**<sup>**n**</sup>, where n is the number of chiral centres.


Spec links: `spcpt_pzjCCN5WFBRwTq6P`


## Card 21 — true_or_false (`fl_fkgHXGpDjmCz5hXx`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Enantiomers are superimposable mirror images of each other.


**BACK**

**False.**

Enantiomers are **non-superimposable** mirror images. Just as a left hand cannot be placed exactly over a right hand, one enantiomer cannot be superimposed on the other.


Spec links: `spcpt_X7vDgdxQs3VpCRb6`

**BACK**

## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_zZG6fp6FfXXfhswt`)

**FRONT**

What is the systematic approach for deducing all possible isomers of an organic compound?


**BACK**

1. Draw the structural formula.
2. Decide whether **stereoisomerism** or **structural isomerism** applies.
3. For structural isomers, check for **chain**, **positional** and **functional group** types. For stereoisomers, check for **geometrical (cis/trans)** and **optical** types.
