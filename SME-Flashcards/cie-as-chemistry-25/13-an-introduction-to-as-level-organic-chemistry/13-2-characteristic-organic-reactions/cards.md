## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_FHrhvQzyM237TZfP`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition reaction**.


**BACK**

An **addition reaction** is an organic reaction in which two or more molecules combine to give a **single product** with no other products formed.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4H3cYgVcvwZFn7y2`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference between a substitution reaction and an elimination reaction?


**BACK**

A **substitution** reaction replaces one atom or group with another, whereas an **elimination** reaction removes a small molecule (such as H<sub>2</sub>O or HCl) from an organic molecule to form a new bond.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_FRWBB9w2KGmr4JfV`)

**FRONT**

In the initiation step of free-radical substitution, UV light breaks the Cl–Cl bond by homolytic fission.


**BACK**

**True.**

UV light provides energy to break the **Cl–Cl** bond homolytically, giving each chlorine atom one electron and forming two **chlorine radicals** (Cl<sup>•</sup>).

**FRONT**

What is nucleophilic substitution and what acts as the leaving group?


**BACK**

## Card 16 — true_or_false (`fl_f8fpKcCCzzSgVk7p`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

In the termination step of free-radical substitution, only one product is possible.

Multiple products are possible in termination depending on which two radicals combine. For example, <sup>•</sup>CH<sub>3</sub> + <sup>•</sup>CH<sub>3</sub> → CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>3</sub> is an **undesirable** by-product.
