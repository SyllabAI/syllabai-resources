# Addition Polymerisation

Course: cie-as-chemistry-25 · Section: 20. Polymerisation

## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_rvgYfVjX5VS6GSnB`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition polymerisation**.


**BACK**

An **addition polymerisation** is a reaction in which many monomers containing at least one C=C double bond join together to form long polymer chains as the only product.


Spec links: `spcpt_DPwmzhM5Sk4TDQht`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_dB556mB9FVgPs63h`)

**FRONT**

What bond change occurs in the monomer during addition polymerisation?


**BACK**

The **π-bond** in the C=C double bond breaks. The monomers then link together to form new **C-C single bonds**, producing the polymer chain.


Spec links: `spcpt_DPwmzhM5Sk4TDQht`


## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_cVFMFQc9WW7Prwdq`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Addition polymerisation produces two products: the polymer and a small molecule such as water.


**BACK**

**False.**

Addition polymerisation produces only one product — the polymer. No small molecules are released, which distinguishes it from condensation polymerisation.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_NxSR8DkW7c3yCzZn`)

**FRONT**

Define **repeat unit**.


**BACK**

A **repeat unit** is the smallest group of atoms that, when connected one after another, makes up the polymer chain. It is shown in square brackets in structural and displayed formulae.


Spec links: `spcpt_DPwmzhM5Sk4TDQht`


## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_3mKcVN2cd4RhCP4k`)

**FRONT**

How does the repeat unit of a poly(alkene) differ from its monomer?


**BACK**

The repeat unit is identical to the monomer except that the **C=C double bond** is replaced by a **C-C single bond**. The repeat unit shown in brackets in a displayed formula is not the monomer itself.


Spec links: `spcpt_DPwmzhM5Sk4TDQht`

**BACK**

Chloroethene (CH<sub>2</sub>=CHCl) undergoes addition polymerisation. The C=C bond opens and the monomers link to form poly(chloroethene), commonly called PVC.

**BACK**

A **non-biodegradable** material is one that cannot be broken down by microorganisms. Poly(alkenes) are non-biodegradable and can take hundreds of years to decompose in landfill.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_6NwjqvrPBfPfCpt6`)

**FRONT**

Why are poly(alkenes) described as resistant to chemical attack?


**BACK**

Poly(alkenes) are very large **alkane-like molecules**. They are **unreactive** and do not undergo chemical reactions, making them **resistant to chemical attack** by most reagents.
