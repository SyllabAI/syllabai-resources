## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_H33b8gVprGbzXhfN`)

**FRONT**

Define **hydrogen bond**.


**BACK**

A **hydrogen bond** is the strongest type of intermolecular force, formed between a highly polarised O-H, N-H or F-H bond and a lone pair on an O, N or F atom in an adjacent molecule.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_64KjMMWQGmvNBzP5`)

**FRONT**

State the two conditions required for hydrogen bonding to occur between molecules.


**BACK**

1. A molecule must contain a highly **electronegative** atom (**O, N or F**) with a **lone pair** of electrons.
2. A hydrogen atom must be covalently bonded directly to **O, N or F**, making the bond highly **polar**.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_k8x5VyK3q7xhtpqH`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Ice is denser than liquid water because the molecules in ice are more tightly packed.


**BACK**

**False.**

Ice is **less dense** than liquid water. In ice, water molecules form a rigid open 3D hydrogen-bonded lattice with relatively long bond lengths, so the molecules are slightly further apart than in liquid water.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_J2RQgH5NjpNVB7D6`)

**FRONT**

Why does water have a high surface tension?


**BACK**

Surface molecules are pulled **downwards** by hydrogen bonds with interior molecules. This compresses and tightens the surface layer, resisting external forces. Interior molecules are pulled in all directions so the net force is zero.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_GRYqr6dFxV5mqkcm`)

**FRONT**

Explain why CH<sub>3</sub>Cl is a polar molecule but CCl<sub>4</sub> is not, even though both contain polar C–Cl bonds.


**BACK**

In CH<sub>3</sub>Cl the four polar bonds (three C–H and one C–Cl) do not cancel each other out, giving a **net dipole** towards the electronegative Cl atom. In CCl<sub>4</sub> the four identical C–Cl bond dipoles are arranged **symmetrically** and cancel each other out, so there is no overall dipole.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_C2txyzYCsv2cMpC2`)

**FRONT**

What causes an instantaneous dipole to form in a non-polar molecule?


**BACK**

The electron charge cloud is constantly moving. At any moment it can be more concentrated on one side of the molecule than the other, creating a **temporary uneven distribution of charge** — an instantaneous dipole.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_MjjVw67yWvc9H5cF`)

**FRONT**

Propanone has a higher boiling point than butane, even though both molecules have the same number of electrons. Explain why.


**BACK**

Propanone is polar so it has **permanent dipole – permanent dipole forces** between its molecules. Butane is non-polar and only has weaker id–id forces. For molecules with the same number of electrons, pd–pd forces are **stronger** than id–id forces, so more energy is needed to separate propanone molecules.

## Card 27 — question_and_answer (`fl_wfxHHVcfT9KwCZG9`)

**FRONT**

Why is the δ+ charge on H particularly large in molecules that form hydrogen bonds?


**BACK**

H is bonded to a highly electronegative O or N atom. The O or N atom draws nearly all the electron density from the O–H or N–H bond towards itself, leaving the H with a **large δ+** and the O/N with a **large δ−**.

What are the three types of intramolecular force and how does each form?


**BACK**

2. **Covalent bonding** — formed when outer electrons of two atoms are shared.
3. **Metallic bonding** — electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons in a metal lattice.
