## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_KwttzfC9w36Mgvkd`)

**FRONT**

Define **VSEPR theory**.


**BACK**

**VSEPR theory** (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion theory) states that electron pairs around a central atom repel each other and arrange themselves as far apart as possible to minimise repulsion, determining the shape of the molecule.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_FqGdZgy52tDVvHHk`)

**FRONT**

State the three key rules of VSEPR theory.


**BACK**

1. All **electron pairs** (bonding and lone pairs) spread out as far apart as possible.
2. **Lone pairs** repel more strongly than **bonding pairs**.
3. **Multiple bonds** behave like a single electron domain when determining shape.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_kfttjGkt6khdJrCc`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Lone pairs repel neighbouring electron pairs more strongly than bonding pairs do.


**BACK**

**True.**

Lone pairs are held closer to the nucleus and spread over a larger region of space, causing greater repulsion. This reduces bond angles compared to the regular geometry.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_mwYx8xqH9KkqTZs5`)

**FRONT**

What shape and bond angle does SF<sub>6</sub> adopt, and why?


**BACK**

SF<sub>6</sub> adopts an **octahedral** shape with bond angles of **90°**. Sulfur forms 6 single bonds with no lone pairs, so all six bonding pairs repel each other equally.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

CO<sub>2</sub> has a bent shape because of lone pairs on the carbon atom.


**BACK**

**False.**

CO<sub>2</sub> is **linear** (180°). Carbon has no lone pairs and forms two double bonds, each counted as one electron domain. The two domains arrange linearly.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_FtFgvbtPqMXNMZ2b`)

**FRONT**

Why does H<sub>2</sub>O have a bond angle of 104.5° rather than the tetrahedral angle of 109.5°?


**BACK**

Oxygen in H<sub>2</sub>O has **two lone pairs** in addition to two bonding pairs. The lone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs, compressing the H-O-H bond angle from 109.5° to **104.5°**.

## Card 9 — true_or_false (`fl_vWhd2X2vBMcTYmZd`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

BF<sub>3</sub> has a trigonal planar shape with bond angles of 120°.


**BACK**

**True.**

Boron forms 3 bonding pairs and has no lone pairs. The three electron domains arrange with **trigonal planar geometry**, giving bond angles of **120°**.
