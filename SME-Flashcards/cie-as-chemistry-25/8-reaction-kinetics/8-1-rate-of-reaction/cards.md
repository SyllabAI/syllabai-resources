## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_tHNm6cM56dKWfjW2`)

**FRONT**

Define **rate of reaction**.


**BACK**

A **rate of reaction** is the change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time: rate = Δconcentration / time. Units are mol dm<sup>-3</sup> s<sup>-1</sup>.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_CHhJRDr6c8KzkfSq`)

**FRONT**

State two conditions that must both be met for a collision between reactant particles to be effective.


**BACK**

1. The particles must collide with the correct **orientation**.
2. The particles must have sufficient **energy** (at least equal to the activation energy *E*<sub>a</sub>).

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_wb2BY2GtvMH7FZV9`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

All collisions between reactant particles result in a chemical reaction.


**BACK**

**False.**

Only **effective collisions** produce a reaction. Ineffective collisions occur when particles have insufficient energy or the wrong orientation, and the particles simply bounce off each other.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_KVdtCQ5V6wGbQQGY`)

**FRONT**

Why does increasing pressure increase the rate of a gas-phase reaction?


**BACK**

At higher pressure, the same number of gas molecules occupy a **smaller volume**. The particles are closer together, so the **collision frequency** increases, leading to more effective collisions per unit time and a **faster rate of reaction**.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by increasing the collision frequency.


**BACK**

**False.**

A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by providing an **alternative reaction pathway** with a lower activation energy, not by increasing collision frequency.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_KC7sM7RwfhvJnJ7k`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The rate of reaction remains constant throughout a reaction.


**BACK**

**False.**

As the reaction proceeds, reactant concentration decreases and the rate **decreases**. The concentration-time curve becomes progressively shallower over time.

## Card 12 — keyword_definition (`fl_3tj3BwM5frNKcmgg`)

**FRONT**

Define **concentration-time graph**.


**BACK**

A **concentration-time graph** is a graph that plots the concentration of a reactant or product (mol dm<sup>-3</sup>) against time. The gradient at any point gives the rate of reaction at that moment.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_jshGqCRsVb927PyR`)

**FRONT**

Why does rate of reaction decrease as time goes on in a typical reaction?


**BACK**

As the reaction proceeds, reactant concentration **decreases**. Fewer reactant particles are present in the same volume, so collision frequency falls, reducing the number of effective collisions per unit time.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_6qfWpd3KhhSyQhxf`)

**FRONT**

Why should temperature be kept constant when collecting concentration-time data for a reaction?


**BACK**

**Temperature** affects the rate of reaction. If temperature changes during the experiment, the rate will change for reasons unrelated to concentration, making the data **unreliable** for drawing rate-concentration graphs.
