## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_gpQKZFjds6Ns8rss`)

**FRONT**

Define **alcohol**.


**BACK**

An **alcohol** is a compound containing at least one hydroxy (-OH) functional group, with general formula C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+1</sub>OH.

## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_j5dnFxnrQZzmTG2B`)

**FRONT**

Are alcohols stronger or weaker acids than water? Give a reason.


**BACK**

Alcohols are **weaker** acids than water. The alkyl group in the alkoxide ion donates electron density to the oxygen, making it more likely to re-accept a proton and reform the alcohol, so [H<sup>+</sup>(aq)] is lower than in water.


Spec links: `spcpt_tV46RtJmb4cvCkFv`

Alcohols dissociate to a greater extent than water when dissolved in solution.

Alcohols have a low degree of dissociation. The equilibrium ROH ⇌ RO<sup>-</sup> + H<sup>+</sup> lies far to the left, giving a lower [H<sup>+</sup>] than water.

## Card 32 — question_and_answer (`fl_xs83KrdHNfzJ5J52`)

**FRONT**

Why does the alkoxide ion (RO<sup>-</sup>) tend to re-accept a proton more readily than the hydroxide ion (OH<sup>-</sup>)?


**BACK**

The alkoxide ion has an **electron-donating alkyl group** attached to the oxygen. This increases **electron density** on the O<sup>-</sup> atom, making it more likely to attract and accept a proton. The hydroxide ion has no such alkyl group, so its oxygen is less electron-rich.
