## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_bDmHbhKZGf4d92RP`)

**FRONT**

Define **amine**.


**BACK**

An **amine** is a compound containing the -NH<sub>2</sub> functional group, formed by nucleophilic substitution of a halogenoalkane with ammonia.

**BACK**

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_T64MtC5h8rfqzFnC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The nitrogen atom in ammonia acts as a nucleophile because it has a lone pair of electrons.


**BACK**

**True.**

The lone pair on the nitrogen of NH<sub>3</sub> attacks the partial positive carbon of the C-X bond in the halogenoalkane, replacing the halogen in a nucleophilic substitution reaction.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_Dm4X8ZpnS64sdkk5`)

**FRONT**

Why does the C-X bond in a halogenoalkane have a partial positive charge on the carbon?


**BACK**

The halogen atom is more **electronegative** than carbon, so it draws electron density away from the C-X bond towards itself. This gives the carbon a partial positive charge (δ+) and the halogen a partial negative charge (δ-).

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_5rvM6B2mnChgf4kw`)

**FRONT**

Define **nucleophile**.


**BACK**

A **nucleophile** is an electron-pair donor that attacks electron-deficient (partial positive) atoms. The nitrogen lone pair in NH<sub>3</sub> acts as a nucleophile in the production of amines.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_73T3RhZGPjjhCjkg`)

**FRONT**

Why is an ethanolic solution of ammonia used rather than an aqueous solution to produce amines from halogenoalkanes?


**BACK**

Aqueous ammonia contains water, which can act as a nucleophile and cause **hydrolysis** of the halogenoalkane, producing an **alcohol** as a competing product. Using **ethanolic ammonia** removes water, ensuring NH<sub>3</sub> is the only nucleophile present and the reaction selectively produces the **amine**.
