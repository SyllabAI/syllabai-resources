## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_nTWpSMzfCDDRTtwz`)

**FRONT**

Define **giant ionic lattice**.


**BACK**

A **giant ionic lattice** is a regular, repeating three-dimensional arrangement of oppositely charged ions held together by electrostatic forces. The type of lattice depends on the relative sizes of the cation and anion.

## Card 5 — keyword_definition (`fl_F6dxxgTgXFypFNch`)

**FRONT**

Define **simple molecular lattice**.


**BACK**

A **simple molecular lattice** is a structure in which small, discrete molecules are arranged in a regular pattern. Intermolecular forces hold the molecules together. Examples include iodine, ice and buckminsterfullerene.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_6vTZrpM5dv8W5NbS`)

**FRONT**

Why do giant molecular lattices generally have higher melting and boiling points than simple molecular lattices?


**BACK**

**Giant molecular lattices** require more energy to break because **strong covalent bonds** (intramolecular forces) extend throughout the whole structure. **Simple molecular lattices** only require overcoming weaker **intermolecular forces** between discrete molecules.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_Vr6mq2jksy9JDHfx`)

**FRONT**

Under what conditions can ionic compounds conduct electricity, and why?


**BACK**

Ionic compounds conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in solution. In these states, the ions are free to move and carry charge. In the solid state, ions are in fixed positions and cannot move.

Define **malleable**.


**BACK**

A **malleable** material can be hammered or shaped without breaking. Metals are malleable because their layers of ions can slide over each other while the delocalised electrons re-form the metallic bonds throughout the new shape.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_Qw67zQZtSPG9Ddgy`)

**FRONT**

Why do simple covalent substances have low melting and boiling points?


**BACK**

Simple covalent substances have weak **intermolecular forces** between **discrete molecules**. Only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome these forces, so they melt and boil at low temperatures.

## Card 15 — true_or_false (`fl_xc6T4szYgzrft65m`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Metals can conduct electricity in both the solid and liquid state.


**BACK**

**True.**

In both states, metals have mobile delocalised electrons that can move freely and carry charge. This distinguishes metals from ionic compounds, which only conduct when molten or in solution.


Spec links: `spcpt_YvVs8rP9WBKRWmt8`


## Card 16 — keyword_definition (`fl_gy7mhFmB5NC4nkYH`)

**FRONT**

Define **charge density**.


**BACK**

**Charge density** is the ratio of an ion's charge to its size (ionic radius). Ions with higher charge density exert stronger electrostatic forces, leading to higher melting and boiling points in ionic compounds.
