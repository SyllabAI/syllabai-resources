## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_nSctrHkf74ffSCv8`)

**FRONT**

Why is nitrogen gas very unreactive?


**BACK**

Nitrogen forms a **triple covalent bond** (N≡N) with a bond enthalpy of 1000 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>. This very strong bond requires a large amount of energy to break. Nitrogen is also **nonpolar**, making it unlikely to react with other molecules.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_z4kt5yxdQm8TprfW`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Nitrogen gas is polar because it contains a triple bond.


**BACK**

**False.**

Nitrogen gas (N≡N) is **nonpolar**. The two identical nitrogen atoms have the same electronegativity, so the electrons are shared equally and there is no overall dipole.

## Card 4 — keyword_definition (`fl_hCNGhKVzTQWtZtrc`)

**FRONT**

Define **Brønsted-Lowry base**.


**BACK**

A **Brønsted-Lowry base** is a proton (H<sup>+</sup>) acceptor. **Ammonia** acts as a Brønsted-Lowry base, using its lone pair on nitrogen to accept a proton and form the ammonium ion (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>).

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_z5kRyfPxPxMgvfXn`)

**FRONT**

Describe the shape of ammonia (NH<sub>3</sub>) and the ammonium ion (NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup>), and explain the difference.


**BACK**

**NH**<sub>**3**</sub> has a **pyramidal** shape due to the lone pair of electrons on nitrogen repelling the three bonding pairs. **NH**<sub>**4**</sub><sup>**+**</sup> has a **tetrahedral** shape because the lone pair has been donated to form a dative bond with H<sup>+</sup>, leaving four equal bonds.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_YS5zCN7YZXDNGRtv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Ammonia is a strong base because it reacts completely with water.


**BACK**

**False.**

Ammonia is a **weak base**. The equilibrium NH<sub>3</sub> (aq) + H<sub>2</sub>O (l) ⇌ NH<sub>4</sub><sup>+</sup> (aq) + OH<sup>-</sup> (aq) lies well to the **left**, so only a small proportion of NH<sub>3</sub> molecules ionise.

## Card 10 — question_and_answer (`fl_VddDYKd9sBZQvD5w`)

**FRONT**

How are nitrogen oxides formed in car engines?


**BACK**

Inside a car engine, the high **temperature** and **pressure** cause atmospheric **nitrogen** and **oxygen** to react, forming nitrogen oxides (NO and NO<sub>2</sub>). These are released into the atmosphere through exhaust fumes.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_CWkfF4sFkKbwqXD6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Catalytic converters convert nitrogen oxides into harmless nitrogen gas.


**BACK**

**True.**

In a catalytic converter, NO is reduced on the surface of a hot metal catalyst (e.g. platinum): 2CO (g) + 2NO (g) → 2CO<sub>2</sub> (g) + N<sub>2</sub> (g). **N**<sub>**2**</sub> is released as a harmless gas.

## Card 13 — keyword_definition (`fl_YQxKn3jmv9WvrS6y`)

**FRONT**

Define **secondary pollutant**.


**BACK**

A **secondary pollutant** is one that is not emitted directly from a source but is formed in the atmosphere through chemical reactions involving **primary pollutants**. Peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN) is an example, formed from nitrogen oxides and VOCs in sunlight.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_MJt2S2FtfPkz3HHv`)

**FRONT**

Write the equation showing how NO<sub>2</sub> and water contribute to the formation of acid rain.


**BACK**

4NO<sub>2</sub> (g) + 2H<sub>2</sub>O (l) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) → 4HNO<sub>3</sub> (aq)

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_djsJfFhZtKtfyPcd`)

**FRONT**

Distinguish between primary and secondary pollutants, giving one example of each.


**BACK**

A **primary pollutant** is emitted directly from a source (e.g. **NO** from car exhausts). A **secondary pollutant** forms through atmospheric reactions between primary pollutants (e.g. **PAN**, formed from VOCs and nitrogen oxides in sunlight).

**FRONT**

Write the equation for the reaction of NO with oxygen in the atmosphere and explain its environmental significance.


**BACK**

2NO (g) + O<sub>2</sub> (g) ⇌ 2NO<sub>2</sub> (g)

**NO**<sub>**2**</sub> is a more reactive pollutant than NO. It contributes to **acid rain** by reacting with water and oxygen to form HNO<sub>3</sub>, and to **photochemical smog** by reacting with VOCs in sunlight.
