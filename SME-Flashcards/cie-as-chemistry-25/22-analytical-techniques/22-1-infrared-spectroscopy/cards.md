## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_JX3TzVpVFX4XM4yg`)

**FRONT**

Define **fingerprint region**.


**BACK**

A **fingerprint region** is the area of an IR spectrum below 1500 cm<sup>-1</sup>. It contains many complex absorption peaks unique to each compound and is used to identify a specific molecule by comparison with reference spectra.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_d39g2DxB84PQQwNf`)

**FRONT**

What is the characteristic IR absorption range for a C=O bond in a carboxylic acid (carboxyl group)?


**BACK**

The C=O bond in a carboxyl group absorbs in the range **1670-1740 cm**<sup>**-1**</sup>. This appears as a strong, sharp peak. The carboxylic acid also shows a **broad O-H peak** at 2500-3000 cm<sup>-1</sup>.

## Card 3 — true_or_false (`fl_BrmdG8dvdHrVYggC`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The O-H absorption in alcohols appears as a broad peak due to hydrogen bonding.


**BACK**

**True.**

Hydrogen bonding in alcohols causes the O-H bond to vibrate over a range of frequencies, producing a broad absorption peak in the region 3200-3600 cm<sup>-1</sup>.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_gnY8jbM6RxhRpgQf`)

**FRONT**

How can IR spectroscopy be used to distinguish between propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol?


**BACK**

Both isomers have the same functional groups and show similar O-H and C-O absorptions above 1500 cm<sup>-1</sup>. They are distinguished by comparing their **fingerprint regions** (below 1500 cm<sup>-1</sup>) with **reference spectra** from a database.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_9VFQ33y6pXddHhQH`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A strong, sharp absorption at around 1710 cm<sup>-1</sup> in an IR spectrum indicates the presence of a carbonyl (C=O) group in a ketone.


**BACK**

**True.**

Ketone C=O groups absorb strongly and sharply in the range 1680-1750 cm<sup>-1</sup>. A sharp peak around 1710 cm<sup>-1</sup> without a broad O-H absorption confirms a ketone rather than an alcohol.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_Kj2qDK3yZg7hJcs6`)

**FRONT**

Define **wavenumber**.


**BACK**

A **wavenumber** is the number of wave cycles per centimetre, expressed in cm<sup>-1</sup>. It is the reciprocal of wavelength and is used to describe the position of absorption peaks in an IR spectrum.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_zxqkk79zPYXwPypd`)

**FRONT**

Why is IR spectroscopy often used alongside mass spectrometry when identifying an unknown organic compound?


**BACK**

Some IR absorption bands overlap, making it impossible to assign all peaks to specific bonds with certainty. Mass spectrometry provides **complementary information** (**molecular mass** and **fragmentation pattern**) that helps confirm the identity of the compound.
