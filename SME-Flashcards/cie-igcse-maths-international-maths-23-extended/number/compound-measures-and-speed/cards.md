## Card 1 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_snnx536TtYmB4YpP`)

**FRONT**

Complete the formula for **average speed**.

`\text{speed} = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formula is:

$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$

The word "speed" here always means **average speed** across the whole journey, not the speed at a single moment.

## Card 2 — true_or_false (`fl_cCNy9zjYhZyH58Jk`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

To find the **average speed** for a journey made in two parts, add the two speeds and halve the result.


**BACK**

**False.**

You can only average the two speeds when each part of the journey takes the **same length of time**, which is unusual.

Otherwise the longer part of the journey counts for more.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_vWSDmwX5697SHZF7`)

**FRONT**

A runner covers 10 km in three quarters of an hour. What is their average speed in **metres per second**, to 3 significant figures?


**BACK**

Convert both quantities first, since 10 km is **10 000 m** and three quarters of an hour is **2700 s**.

Dividing gives $10000 \div 2700 = 3 . 7037 \dots$ which rounds to **3.70 m/s** as the answer.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_VHXYjKc6JxYb2TRg`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A speed in $\text{km/h}$ can be used directly with a time measured in minutes.


**BACK**

**False.**

The units have to **match** before you multiply or divide, so either change the time into **hours** or change the speed into kilometres per **minute**.

Using hours and minutes together makes the answer wrong by a factor of **60**.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_sZ2k6DfbZFjjcYVz`)

**FRONT**

How far does a car travelling at a steady $60 \text{km/h}$ go in 2.5 hours?


**BACK**

The car travels **150 km**, because $60 \times 2 . 5 = 150$ when the speed is multiplied by the time.

No conversion is needed here, because the speed is in kilometres per **hour** and the time is already in **hours**.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_VY94F5VdRVd5Xc9Z`)

**FRONT**

A sprinter runs 100 m at an average speed of $8 . 85 \text{m/s}$ throughout. How long does the run take, to the nearest hundredth of a second?


**BACK**

Rearranging gives $\text{time} = \frac{100}{8.85}$ which works out as $11 . 29943 \dots$ seconds.

Rounded to the nearest hundredth of a second that gives **11.30 s** as the answer.

## Card 7 — true_or_false (`fl_TyC27SZrR3BT8wv7`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A speed of $1 \text{m/s}$ is the same as a speed of $3 . 6 \text{km/h}$ exactly.


**BACK**

**True.**

In one hour an object moving at 1 metre per second covers $1 \times 3600 = 3600$ metres.

That is 3.6 kilometres, so the two speeds are the same.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_CNgJkG2FYcrqt6gP`)

**FRONT**

Define a **compound measure**.


**BACK**

A **compound measure** is a quantity worked out from **more than one** other measurement.

Compound measures are used to describe **rates**, which say how much one quantity changes for each unit of another.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_tPHZCvMGcPMVDJrD`)

**FRONT**

A quantity is measured in units written in the form **a/b**. What does that tell you about its formula?


**BACK**

The formula is whatever **a** measures **divided by** whatever **b** measures.

For example a rate of pay in **dollars per hour** is an amount of **money** divided by a **time**, because dollars measure money and hours measure time.

## Card 10 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_YJPfty3pTW83RJBC`)

**FRONT**

Complete these two compound measure formulas.

`\text{density} = \frac{\_\_\_\_\_\_}{\text{volume}}`

`\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\_\_\_\_\_\_}`


**BACK**

The completed formulas are:

$\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$

$\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$

Both follow the units, since a density in $\text{kg/m}^{3}$ is a mass over a volume and a pressure in $\text{N/m}^{2}$ is a force over an area.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_bkQQ684YCxpB25QC`)

**FRONT**

What does **population density** measure, and what are its units?


**BACK**

**Population density** is the number of **people per unit of area**, so it is a population divided by an area.

It is usually given in people per square kilometre, written as $\text{people/km}^{2}$ in units form.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_YMx2cnDS7VSTpfP6`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

**Fuel consumption** always means the volume of fuel used divided by the distance travelled.


**BACK**

**False.**

It depends on the units: a fuel consumption in $\text{km/litre}$ is a **distance divided by a volume**, while one in $\text{litres/km}$ is the other way round.

Read the units first, then decide which quantity goes on top.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_MSKXr6MzxMFRqWYY`)

**FRONT**

How do you use a **formula triangle**?


**BACK**

**Cover up** the quantity you want to find.

If it sat on the **top** of the triangle, **multiply** the two beneath it; if it sat on the **bottom**, **divide** the top one by the other.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_FmjHwh7x7C4XDQXY`)

**FRONT**

A pump has a flow rate of 720 litres per minute. How many litres does it deliver in 3.1 seconds?


**BACK**

Change the rate into litres per **second** first, since $720 \div 60 = 12$ litres pass each second.

Then multiply by the time, giving $12 \times 3 . 1 = 37 . 2$ litres altogether.
