## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_XFJvX2srNDp2hq4z`)

**FRONT**

Define a **translation** of a graph.


**BACK**

A **translation** is a transformation that **slides** a graph up, down, left or right in the $x y$ plane.

The **shape**, **size** and **orientation** of the graph are all unchanged, and only its position moves.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_mFKvNdhZq9QMT2dp`)

**FRONT**

What does `y = \text{f} \left(x\right) + a` do to the graph of `y = \text{f} \left(x\right)`?


**BACK**

It **translates** the whole graph **vertically**, without changing its shape at all.

The graph moves **up** when $a$ is positive and **down** when $a$ is negative.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_3dKJ6DB8Fff4vkcb`)

**FRONT**

How is a translation of a graph described using a **vector**?


**BACK**

As a column vector `\begin{bmatrix} \text{horizontal} \\ \text{vertical} \end{bmatrix}`, giving the movement across and then the movement up.

So `\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}` is 1 unit up with no sideways movement, and `\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}` is 2 units across with no vertical movement.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_BFzF7wcsPzrYrhXH`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

The graph of `y = \text{f} \left(x + 3\right)` is the graph of `y = \text{f} \left(x\right)` moved **3 units to the right**.


**BACK**

**False.**

It moves **3 units to the left**, which is the opposite of what most people expect.

A positive number added inside the bracket shifts the graph in the **negative** $x$ direction.

## Card 5 — fill_in_the_blanks (`fl_vggyMmWr8DgjZNcb`)

**FRONT**

A translation always leaves one set of coordinates untouched. Complete both rules:

A **vertical** translation leaves every `\_\_\_\_\_\_` coordinate unchanged, while a **horizontal** translation leaves every `\_\_\_\_\_\_` coordinate unchanged instead.


**BACK**

The completed rules are:

A **vertical** translation leaves every $x$ coordinate unchanged, while a **horizontal** translation leaves every $y$ coordinate unchanged instead.

A point only moves in the direction of the translation, so its other coordinate has no reason to change.

## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_G8BZXqYCDqY47RCc`)

**FRONT**

The graph $y = x^{2} - 3 x + 7$ is transformed to `y = \text{f} \left(x - 6\right)`. Write out the new equation.


**BACK**

Replace **every** $x$ in the equation by `\left(x - 6\right)`, brackets included.

That gives `y = \left(x - 6\right)^{2} - 3 \left(x - 6\right) + 7`, which may be left in that form or expanded to $y = x^{2} - 15 x + 61$.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_5cXB8kmk3McKBZH7`)

**FRONT**

Which part of a translation does each letter carry in `y = \text{f} \left(x - p\right) + q`?


**BACK**

$p$ sits **inside the bracket** and carries the **horizontal** part, while $q$ sits **outside** it and carries the **vertical** part.

So $y = 3 x^{2}$ becoming `y = 3 \left(x + 1\right)^{2} + 2` is one combined translation, horizontal and vertical at the same time.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_Rqy78xyssvJGpKyr`)

**FRONT**

What happens to a graph's **asymptotes** when the graph is translated?


**BACK**

They move with it, except that an asymptote **parallel to the direction of travel** is left where it is.

Moving $y = 2^{x}$ up by 3 lifts its horizontal asymptote from $y = 0$ to $y = 3$, because that asymptote lies at right angles to the movement.
