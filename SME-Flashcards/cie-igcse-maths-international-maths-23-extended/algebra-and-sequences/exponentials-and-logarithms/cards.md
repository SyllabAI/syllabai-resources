## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_8mG9tchw6GVcmgnz`)

**FRONT**

In the model $y = b a^{x}$ what does the value of ***b*** represent?


**BACK**

***b*** is the **initial amount** of the quantity being modelled.

When $x = 0$ the power term equals 1, so $y = b$ at the very start of the model.

**BACK**

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_3kYfvWX9bFszXMJP`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

A model of the form $y = a^{x}$ shows decay when ***a*** is negative.


**BACK**

**False.**

The base ***a*** can never be negative in this model at all.

A negative base would make ***y*** flip between positive and negative as ***x*** moved from one whole number to the next, which no real quantity does.

## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_bvqdN6Bhx7ry5Xmb`)

**FRONT**

Define **exponential growth**.


**BACK**

**Exponential growth** is a quantity **increasing** from a starting amount by the **same scale factor** in each equal time interval.

That interval need not be a year: it can be a day, an hour or a minute, as long as it is the same one each time.

## Card 8 — keyword_definition (`fl_PDjPKpymtfgh3fBR`)

**FRONT**

Define a **logarithm**.


**BACK**

A **logarithm** is the **inverse** of an exponential: `\log_{a} \left(b\right)` is the **power** you raise ***a*** to in order to get ***b***.

So $a^{x} = b$ and `x = \log_{a} \left(b\right)` say exactly the same thing in two different ways.

## Card 11 — true_or_false (`fl_NTP9dG4H28fJYNgj`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

$\mathrm{log}_{5} 1$ is equal to 0.


**BACK**

**True.**

The power you raise 5 to in order to get 1 is **zero**, since $5^{0} = 1$ always holds.

The same happens whatever the base, so a logarithm of 1 is always 0.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_XzCG2hsKbXDbjqpg`)

**FRONT**

Can a logarithm be negative, or a fraction? Give an example of each.


**BACK**

Yes to both, because the **power** itself can be negative or fractional.

For instance `\log_{5} \left(\frac{1}{5}\right) = - 1` and `\log_{5} \left(\sqrt{5}\right) = \frac{1}{2}` show each case.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_MbwvPWnYjzQNzn7w`)

**FRONT**

How do you solve $2^{x} = 10$ using logarithms?


**BACK**

Make ***x*** the subject to get $x = \mathrm{log}_{2} 10$ and evaluate that on a calculator.

Working in base 10 throughout gives the same value, since $x = \frac{\mathrm{log}10}{\mathrm{log}2} = 3 . 3219 \dots$ as well.

## Card 14 — question_and_answer (`fl_krMsQj6HMvg6YYRF`)

**FRONT**

Solve `\log \left(8 x\right) = 4` without a calculator, where the base is 10.


**BACK**

Rewrite it as an exponential statement, so $10^{4} = 8 x$ follows directly.

That gives $8 x = 10000$ and therefore $x = 1250$ as the solution.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_Pd6PyxYCvZnbnvpZ`)

**FRONT**

An amount of 500 is invested at 5% per year. After how many whole years does it first exceed 900?


**BACK**

Solve $500 \times 1 . 05^{n} = 900$ by making ***n*** the subject with logarithms.

That gives $n = \mathrm{log}_{1.05} 1 . 8 = 12 . 05 \dots$ so the amount first exceeds 900 after **13** whole years.
