## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_SbMVGn4GjZTZsGVP`)

**FRONT**

What is a **quadratic equation**?


**BACK**

A quadratic equation is an equation of the form $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$, 
where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants, and $a$ is **not** equal to zero.



## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_W2xJ3V3QnxM9MtcJ`)

**FRONT**

What does it mean to **solve a quadratic equation **by** factorising**?


**BACK**

**Solving a quadratic equation **by **factorising **means:

1. Making sure it is in the form $ax^{2}+bx+c=0$  (i.e. with zero on one side).
2. Factorising the quadratic.
3. Setting each bracket equal to zero to find the solutions.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_j7MHy4vcyNWwZjgZ`)

**FRONT**

**True or false?**

The solutions of the equation `open parentheses x minus 2 close parentheses open parentheses x plus 5 close parentheses equals 0` are $x=-2$ and $x=5$.


**BACK**

**False.**

To find the solutions of the equation `open parentheses x minus 2 close parentheses open parentheses x plus 5 close parentheses equals 0`, you should solve the linear equations $x-2=0$ and $x+5=0$.

The solutions are $x=2$ and $x=-5$.

Note that the signs in front of 2 and 5 are the 'other way round' to how they appear in the brackets.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_34HpsSdc6BT8DVmz`)

**FRONT**

Before using the quadratic formula, what must the equation look like, and what do you read off it?


**BACK**

The equation must have **zero on one side**, in the form $a x^{2} + b x + c = 0$ first.

Each coefficient **takes the sign in front of it**, so in $2 x^{2} - 8 x - 3 = 0$ you have $b = - 8$ and $c = - 3$ here.

**True or False?**

The brackets can be left out when substituting a negative value of ***b*** into the quadratic formula.


**BACK**

## Card 9 — keyword_definition (`fl_NWJzTv4Cp835Ztq4`)

**FRONT**

Define the **discriminant** of a quadratic equation.


**BACK**

The **discriminant** is the expression $b^{2} - 4 a c$ found under the square root in the quadratic formula.

Its **sign** tells you how many solutions the equation has, without having to solve it.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_Sn64YrKG9YF9yD7J`)

**FRONT**

How many solutions does $3 x^{2} - 2 x - 4 = 0$ have?


**BACK**

Its discriminant is `\left(- 2\right)^{2} - 4 \times 3 \times \left(- 4\right) = 4 + 48 = 52` here.

That value is **positive**, so the equation has **two different solutions**.

## Card 12 — true_or_false (`fl_bn6rhc2RbrMy5gFn`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

If a quadratic with **integer coefficients** has a discriminant that is a **perfect square**, it could have been factorised.


**BACK**

**True.**

A perfect square under the root leaves a whole number, so both solutions are **rational** and the brackets have integer coefficients.

Checking the discriminant first therefore tells you whether factorising would have been the quicker route.

**BACK**

The coefficients to enter are then **6**, **10** and **−19** in that order.

If a solution is going to be used in further working, you should round it before carrying on.


**BACK**

**False.**

Rounding early makes a small error grow through everything that follows it.
