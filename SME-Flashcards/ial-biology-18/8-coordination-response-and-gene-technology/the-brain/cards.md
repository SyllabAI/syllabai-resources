**BACK**

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_32c7phX5jYmSTrPB`)

**FRONT**

Where are the main regions of the brain located?


**BACK**

The **cerebral hemispheres** form the large upper part of the brain.

The **hypothalamus** sits beneath them, just above the **pituitary gland**, which hangs below it.

The **cerebellum** lies at the back, underneath the cerebral hemispheres.

The **medulla oblongata** is at the base of the brain, continuous with the spinal cord.

**BACK**

A beam of **x-rays** is aimed at the head from all angles and picked up by detectors. **Denser tissue** absorbs more radiation and appears **lighter** on the scan, producing a cross-section image of brain **structure**.

**BACK**

**Dopamine**, which is involved in **muscle control**. In Parkinson's disease, dopamine-producing neurones are lost, so **insufficient dopamine** is produced.


Spec links: `spcpt_7PFMdTJZKjFXnmtC`


## Card 29 — question_and_answer (`fl_tqfYFdZbrhxxGs5c`)

**FRONT**

Explain how a lack of dopamine leads to the symptoms of Parkinson's disease.


**BACK**

**Less dopamine** is released into the synaptic cleft, so less binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane. **Fewer sodium channels open**, so depolarisation does not occur, giving **fewer action potentials** and symptoms such as tremors and slow movement.


Spec links: `spcpt_7PFMdTJZKjFXnmtC`


## Card 30 — question_and_answer (`fl_jnsGwZxrYw2CMCRr`)

**FRONT**

How do dopamine agonists treat Parkinson's disease?


**BACK**

They **bind to and activate dopamine receptors** on the postsynaptic membrane, producing the **same effect as dopamine**.


Spec links: `spcpt_7PFMdTJZKjFXnmtC`
