## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_5QrfTf4FXxWCF58t`)

**FRONT**

Define **phytochrome**.


**BACK**

A **phytochrome** is a plant **pigment** that reacts to different wavelengths of light to bring about plant responses such as germination and flowering.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`


## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_8hV7hwPPZSJ5pvfw`)

**FRONT**

What are the two forms of phytochrome, and which is the active form?


**BACK**

P<sub>R</sub> is the **inactive** form, which absorbs **red** light (660 nm). P<sub>FR</sub> is the **active** form, which absorbs **far-red** light (730 nm).


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_xD4wmYgwztHRrB7z`)

**FRONT**

How does phytochrome influence transcription in plant cells?


**BACK**

In red light, P<sub>R</sub> is converted to **P**<sub>**FR**</sub>, which moves into the nucleus and binds to the protein **PIF3**. The P<sub>FR</sub>–PIF3 complex acts as a **transcription factor** that **initiates transcription** of genes controlling growth and development.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`

In long-day plants, how do high levels of P<sub>FR</sub> bring about flowering?


**BACK**

High levels of the active form **P**<sub>**FR**</sub> act as a **transcription factor**, activating **expression of genes** that stimulate **flowering**.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`


## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_MtfDMYpGFWRKnwhp`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Seeds exposed to far-red light will germinate.


**BACK**

**False.**

Far-red light converts P<sub>FR</sub> back into P<sub>R</sub>, which **prevents germination**; a burst of **red** light forms P<sub>FR</sub> and triggers germination.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`


## Card 7 — keyword_definition (`fl_KZYGJc76HhN8VTcG`)

**FRONT**

Define **indoleacetic acid (IAA)**.


**BACK**

**IAA** is a type of **auxin**, a plant growth factor that brings about responses such as **phototropism** by altering the **transcription** of genes involved in cell growth.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`


## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_mpmz63SYWDpxSc8T`)

**FRONT**

How does IAA cause a shoot to bend towards a light source?


**BACK**

IAA is transported to the **shaded side** of the shoot. The higher IAA concentration there increases the rate of **cell elongation**, so the shoot bends **towards** the light.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`


## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_TGMJjpYjpqRmtpvH`)

**FRONT**

Why does IAA have opposite effects on elongation in shoots and roots?


**BACK**

In **shoots**, a high IAA concentration **increases** cell elongation. In **roots**, a high IAA concentration **inhibits** cell elongation, so the lower side grows more slowly and the root bends **downwards**.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_8MH845TD7xyzWS7Z`)

**FRONT**

How do gibberellins bring about germination in barley seeds?


**BACK**

Gibberellins made by the **embryo** stimulate the **aleurone layer**, increasing the **transcription** of genes coding for **amylase**. The amylase hydrolyses **starch** into maltose to provide energy for the embryo.


Spec links: `spcpt_38yVPB7H2hnkqVW2`
