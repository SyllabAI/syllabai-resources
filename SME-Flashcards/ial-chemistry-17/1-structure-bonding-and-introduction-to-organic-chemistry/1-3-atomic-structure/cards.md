## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_mYwSyZXRmbN79Nnw`)

**FRONT**

What are **subatomic particles**?


**BACK**

**Subatomic particles** are the particles that make up an atom: protons, neutrons and electrons. Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus; electrons occupy orbitals around the nucleus.

**BACK**

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_dSNdYhZn5gyqzWJP`)

**FRONT**

What is the equation for calculating relative atomic mass (*A*<sub>r</sub>) from isotope data?


**BACK**

*A*<sub>r</sub> = sum of (**relative abundance** of each isotope x **mass** of that isotope) / 100.

Relative abundances must sum to 100.

**BACK**

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_2YTYKZb7Cn8Yc8BJ`)

**FRONT**

What is the **[M+1] peak** in a mass spectrum?


**BACK**

The **[M+1] peak** is a small peak one mass unit above the molecular ion peak, caused by the natural abundance of <sup>13</sup>C isotopes in the compound. Larger molecules with more carbon atoms have a taller [M+1] peak.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_FD64NSVKcDjGqX2N`)

**FRONT**

A compound contains one chlorine atom. What molecular ion peaks appear in its mass spectrum, and what is the peak height ratio?


**BACK**

Two molecular ion peaks appear: M<sup>+</sup> (containing <sup>35</sup>Cl) and [M+2] (containing <sup>37</sup>Cl). The ratio of peak heights is **3:1**, reflecting the relative abundance of <sup>35</sup>Cl to <sup>37</sup>Cl.


Spec links: `spcpt_VZrcYDFMJ3VWxhPm`


## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_nNq43JJW4yptRN92`)

**FRONT**

Why do isotopes of the same element have the same chemical properties?


**BACK**

Isotopes have the same number of **electrons**, and it is electrons that determine how an atom reacts chemically. The different neutron counts only affect the mass, not the electronic structure.

## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_YSPM9gzBNx6vBtWQ`)

**FRONT**

How does the number of carbon atoms in a molecule affect the [M+1] peak in its mass spectrum?


**BACK**

The more carbon atoms in the molecule, the **taller** the [M+1] peak. This is because each additional carbon atom increases the chance that at least one is <sup>13</sup>C.
