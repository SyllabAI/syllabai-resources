## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_dhXNm5RpHDhHmZy7`)

**FRONT**

Define **addition reaction**


**BACK**

**Addition reaction** is one in which two reactants combine to form a single product.

It is most commonly seen with alkenes, where the C=C double bond reacts with a small molecule such as H<sub>2</sub>, Br<sub>2</sub> or H<sub>2</sub>O.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_MmDM3p2QGfx937zY`)

**FRONT**

In organic chemistry, how is **oxidation** defined in terms of hydrogen and oxygen?


**BACK**

**Oxidation** involves the loss of hydrogen or the gain of oxygen.

For example, an aldehyde is oxidised to a carboxylic acid: CH<sub>3</sub>CHO + [O] → CH<sub>3</sub>COOH.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_b5X5hCF9gZhRQRBP`)

**FRONT**

Define **elimination reaction**


**BACK**

An **elimination reaction** is one in which a small molecule is removed from a reactant, forming an alkene as one of the products.

For example, a halogenoalkane can lose HBr to form an alkene.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_nwvb8TB6gc6mSbVS`)

**FRONT**

In organic chemistry, how is **reduction** defined in terms of hydrogen and oxygen?


**BACK**

**Reduction** involves the gain of hydrogen or the loss of oxygen.

For example, an aldehyde is reduced to an alcohol: CH<sub>3</sub>CHO + [H] → CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>OH.

**FRONT**

Define **substitution reaction**


**BACK**

A **substitution reaction** is one in which an atom or group of atoms in a compound is replaced by another atom or group of atoms.

For example, CH<sub>3</sub>CH<sub>2</sub>Br + OH<sup>-</sup> → C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH + Br<sup>-</sup>

**BACK**

For example, CH<sub>3</sub>Cl + H<sub>2</sub>O → CH<sub>3</sub>OH + HCl.

An elimination reaction always produces exactly one product.


**BACK**

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_cbWjKrwtNpKbJq2D`)

**FRONT**

Define **curly arrow** (in reaction mechanisms)


**BACK**

A **curly arrow** is a symbol used in reaction mechanisms to show the movement of electrons.

Double-headed curly arrows show the movement of an electron pair. Single-headed (fish-hook) curly arrows show the movement of a single electron.

Single-headed curly arrows show the movement of an electron pair.


**BACK**

Define **homolytic fission**


**BACK**

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_gfJcdzcWn642vvG5`)

**FRONT**

Define **heterolytic fission**


**BACK**

**Heterolytic fission** is the breaking of a covalent bond in which both electrons go to one atom, forming a positive ion and a negative ion.

It is shown using double-headed curly arrows.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_KXHXyBv83cGpJGTP`)

**FRONT**

How does **bond polarity** help predict the reaction mechanism when ethene reacts with HBr?


**BACK**

HBr contains a **polar** bond, suggesting **heterolytic** fission and a mechanism involving electrophiles or nucleophiles.

The C=C double bond of ethene attracts electrophiles, so the mechanism is most likely **electrophilic addition**.

**FRONT**

Define **electrophile**


**BACK**

An **electrophile** is an electron-deficient species that accepts a pair of electrons to form a new covalent bond.

Electrophiles are produced when heterolytic fission forms a positive ion.

Define **nucleophile**


**BACK**
