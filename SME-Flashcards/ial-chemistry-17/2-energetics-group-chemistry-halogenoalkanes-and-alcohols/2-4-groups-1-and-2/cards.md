**FRONT**

What is the **first ionisation energy** of an element?


**BACK**

The **first ionisation energy** is the energy needed to remove the first outer electron from one mole of gaseous atoms of an element to form one mole of gaseous ions with a +1 charge.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_k8HQKv6sfJBnQdP3`)

**FRONT**

Why does ionisation energy decrease going down Group 2?


**BACK**

Going down Group 2, the **shielding effect** increases and the **distance** between the outermost electrons and the nucleus increases. These factors outweigh the increased nuclear charge, so it becomes easier to remove the outer electrons.

**True or False?**

A positive test for sulfate ions produces a white precipitate of barium sulfate.


**BACK**

**True.**

When barium chloride solution is added to a solution containing sulfate ions, an insoluble **white precipitate** of **barium sulfate** (BaSO<sub>4</sub>) forms. The ionic equation is: Ba<sup>2+</sup> (aq) + SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2–</sup> (aq) → BaSO<sub>4</sub> (s).

## Card 64 — question_and_answer (`fl_SmBN5J4CSkVSMpsR`)

**FRONT**

What alternative observation confirms ammonia gas is present during the ammonium ion test?


**BACK**

Ammonia gas produces **white smoke** when it comes into contact with fumes of **hydrogen chloride** (from concentrated HCl). The white smoke is **ammonium chloride**: NH<sub>3</sub> (g) + HCl (g) → NH<sub>4</sub>Cl (s).
