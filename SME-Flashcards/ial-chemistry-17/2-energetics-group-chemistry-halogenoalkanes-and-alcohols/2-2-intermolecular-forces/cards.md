## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_JtkY9gzFFhP964Tn`)

**FRONT**

What are **intermolecular forces**?


**BACK**

**Intermolecular forces** are weak attractive forces that act between molecules, as opposed to intramolecular forces, which act within molecules.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_zbHH8tdQHPBn2Jbw`)

**FRONT**

What are **induced dipole-dipole forces**?


**BACK**

**Induced dipole-dipole forces** (also called London dispersion forces or van der Waals forces) are temporary attractive forces between molecules caused by fluctuations in the electron charge cloud creating temporary dipoles.


Spec links: `spcpt_x9nH8JyrcwXJStpw`


## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_yZJScXTfbgwtQVjX`)

**FRONT**

How does a temporary dipole arise in a non-polar molecule?


**BACK**

The electron charge cloud constantly moves, so at any instant it may be unevenly distributed, creating a **temporary dipole**. This can then **induce** a dipole in a neighbouring molecule.


Spec links: `spcpt_x9nH8JyrcwXJStpw`


## Card 5 — true_or_false (`fl_fnX83vfXq4bYQcfg`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Intermolecular forces are stronger than intramolecular forces.


**BACK**

**False.**

**Intramolecular forces** (such as covalent bonds) are stronger than intermolecular forces. For example, a hydrogen bond is about one tenth the strength of a covalent bond.


Spec links: `spcpt_x9nH8JyrcwXJStpw`


## Card 6 — question_and_answer (`fl_DwpDfJJCb48RBx7f`)

**FRONT**

What conditions are required for hydrogen bonding to occur between molecules?


**BACK**

A molecule must contain a **hydrogen atom** covalently bonded to a highly electronegative atom: **O**, **N** or **F**.

The H becomes sufficiently δ+ to attract the lone pair of an O, N or F atom in a neighbouring molecule.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_dDj6bTzcFBSkQnMz`)

**FRONT**

Why does water have unexpectedly high melting and boiling points compared to other Group 16 hydrides?


**BACK**

Water molecules form **hydrogen bonds** with each other. These are much stronger than the induced dipole-dipole forces in H<sub>2</sub>S, H<sub>2</sub>Se and H<sub>2</sub>Te, so significantly more energy is needed to separate water molecules.

Ice is denser than liquid water.


**BACK**

**False.**

In ice, water molecules are arranged in an open lattice held apart by **hydrogen bonds**, making ice about 9% **less dense** than liquid water.

Why is ice less dense than liquid water?


**BACK**

In ice, water molecules form an **open lattice** structure held together by **hydrogen bonds**. The relatively long bond lengths space the molecules further apart than in liquid water, reducing the density.

## Card 14 — keyword_definition (`fl_mM7YQcjYn5ctGrkx`)

**FRONT**

What is **surface tension**?


**BACK**

**Surface tension** is the ability of a liquid surface to resist external forces, caused by the net inward pull of intermolecular forces on surface molecules.


Spec links: `spcpt_DKZt39n6ZkQpjWvB`


## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_p3c3cYvk7gHHxfzn`)

**FRONT**

How does hydrogen bonding give water a high surface tension?


**BACK**

Surface water molecules are pulled **downwards and inwards** by hydrogen bonds with molecules below them. This compresses the surface layer, increasing its resistance to external forces.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_RQj9TMKKQHd8ggcJ`)

**FRONT**

How does molecular branching affect boiling point, and why?


**BACK**

Branching **reduces** the surface area of a molecule, decreasing contact between adjacent molecules. This weakens **London dispersion forces** and lowers the boiling point.

## Card 28 — question_and_answer (`fl_zr4sbCTKWWPSSHyC`)

**FRONT**

What is the general principle that explains why substances dissolve in particular solvents?


**BACK**

**'Like dissolves like'**: non-polar solutes dissolve in non-polar solvents and polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents, because the solute-solvent interactions must be comparable in strength to the forces within the solute.
