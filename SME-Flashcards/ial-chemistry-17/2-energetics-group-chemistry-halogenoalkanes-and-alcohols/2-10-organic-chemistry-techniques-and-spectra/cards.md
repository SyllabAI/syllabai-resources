## Card 1 — question_and_answer (`fl_F9KSHTC6zY78HVYh`)

**FRONT**

What is the difference in purpose between **heating under reflux** and **distillation** in organic chemistry?


**BACK**

Heating under reflux keeps all chemicals inside the reaction vessel to allow a reaction to proceed as fully as possible.

**Distillation** is used to separate a desired product from a mixture by exploiting differences in **boiling point**.

## Card 2 — keyword_definition (`fl_CqbDrCnmtYXF7BWD`)

**FRONT**

Define **molecular ion** in mass spectroscopy


**BACK**

The **molecular ion** (M<sup>+</sup>) is formed when a molecule is bombarded with high-energy electrons and loses one electron, producing a positively charged ion with one unpaired electron.

The peak with the highest m/z value in a mass spectrum corresponds to the molecular ion and gives the molecular mass of the compound.

## Card 3 — keyword_definition (`fl_dKTGCSnBv7kF7wvd`)

**FRONT**

Define **infrared (IR) spectroscopy**


**BACK**

**Infrared spectroscopy** is an analytical technique used to identify compounds based on the absorption of infrared radiation by bonds in a molecule.

When bonds absorb IR radiation at their resonance frequency, they vibrate by stretching, bending or twisting, and the absorbed frequencies are characteristic of specific functional groups.

**FRONT**

Why is an **electric heating mantle** used instead of a Bunsen burner when distilling organic compounds?


**BACK**

Electric heating mantles allow the temperature to be **controlled** more precisely.

They are also safer because organic compounds are often **flammable**, and an open Bunsen burner flame poses a fire or explosion risk.

**BACK**

It is the tallest peak in the spectrum and is given a relative abundance of 100%.

**FRONT**

Define **wavenumber** in infrared spectroscopy


**BACK**

**Wavenumber** (units: cm<sup>-1</sup>) is the reciprocal of the wavelength of infrared radiation.

It is used on the x-axis of an IR spectrum to show at which frequencies specific bonds absorb IR radiation.

## Card 8 — question_and_answer (`fl_zT2c6cvxSJJVKY3K`)

**FRONT**

What causes the **[M+1] peak** in a mass spectrum?


**BACK**

The [M+1] peak is caused by the natural abundance of the **carbon-13** isotope (just over 1%).

Molecules containing C-13 instead of C-12 have a mass one unit higher than the molecular ion. The larger the number of carbon atoms in the molecule, the taller the [M+1] peak.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_8623vDMhXJ336R8X`)

**FRONT**

What do the **width** and **intensity** of an IR absorption peak tell us about a bond?


**BACK**

The width indicates whether the absorption is **broad** or **sharp**: for example, O-H bonds in alcohols absorb broadly due to hydrogen bonding.

The intensity indicates whether it is **strong** or **weak**: the C=O carbonyl group gives a strong, sharp absorption peak.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_5Wgyw7PSTp8cys4c`)

**FRONT**

How is the **m/z ratio** calculated and which ions are deflected most in a mass spectrometer?


**BACK**

The m/z ratio is calculated as the mass (m) of the ion divided by its charge (z).

Ions with a **small** m/z ratio (small mass or high positive charge) are deflected the most and are detected first.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_Mf5yHXZjM8XYmsTN`)

**FRONT**

Describe how a **separating funnel** is used to isolate an organic product from an aqueous layer.


**BACK**

The mixture is transferred to a **separating funnel** and inverted with the stopcock open to release pressure, repeating this several times.

The two layers are allowed to separate, then the lower **aqueous layer** is drained off through the stopcock and the **organic layer** is collected separately.

## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_BrWXsXvSdD5x3ZcF`)

**FRONT**

Why do **halogenoalkanes** show two peaks of similar height near the molecular ion in a mass spectrum?


**BACK**

Halogens exist as **isotopes** with different masses (e.g. <sup>79</sup>Br and <sup>81</sup>Br in roughly equal natural abundance).

Each isotope produces a molecular ion with a different m/z value, resulting in two peaks of approximately equal height at M and M+2.

**FRONT**

Why should **IR spectroscopy** be used alongside **mass spectroscopy** to identify an unknown compound?


**BACK**

Some IR absorption bands overlap each other, making it impossible to distinguish between certain functional groups from IR alone.

Mass spectroscopy provides **molecular mass** and **fragmentation** data that complement IR data to give a more complete structural identification.

**FRONT**

Why is **fragmentation** useful when identifying an unknown compound by mass spectrometry?


**BACK**

**Fragmentation** produces characteristic peaks at specific m/z values that reveal structural information about the molecule.
