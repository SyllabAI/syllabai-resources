## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_ddBHP77RPzhkTGwX`)

**FRONT**

What are **halogens**?


**BACK**

**Halogens** are the Group 7 elements: fluorine, chlorine, bromine, and iodine. They are non-metals that exist as diatomic molecules at room temperature and become progressively darker in colour going down the group.

A **halogen displacement reaction** is a reaction in which a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from an aqueous solution of its halide salt.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_qMytBz2jNXvQ2Pcv`)

**FRONT**

Why does the reducing power of halide ions increase going down Group 7?


**BACK**

Going down the group, the halide ions become larger and have more electron **shielding**. Their outer electrons are held less tightly by the nucleus, so it is easier to lose electrons, making larger halide ions stronger **reducing agents**.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_sRQdwk92gWmtQs6Q`)

**FRONT**

Why do the boiling points of the halogens increase going down Group 7?


**BACK**

Going down the group, the halogen molecules have more electrons, so **London dispersion forces** between molecules become stronger. More energy is needed to overcome these forces, so the boiling point increases.

**BACK**
