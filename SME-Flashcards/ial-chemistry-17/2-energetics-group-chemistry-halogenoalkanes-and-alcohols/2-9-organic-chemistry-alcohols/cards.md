**FRONT**

Define **alcohol**


**BACK**

An **alcohol** is an organic compound containing a **hydroxyl** functional group (-OH).

The general formula is C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+1</sub>OH and alcohols are named using the suffix -ol.

## Card 13 — question_and_answer (`fl_k5PcVsgSPrrGPjHn`)

**FRONT**

What are the **conditions and reagents** for converting an alcohol to a bromoalkane?


**BACK**

The alcohol is warmed with a mixture of **potassium bromide** (KBr) and **50% concentrated sulfuric acid**.

The KBr and H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> first react to produce HBr, which then reacts with the alcohol to form the bromoalkane and water.

## Card 15 — question_and_answer (`fl_YgpH4cvdcm5PPcF3`)

**FRONT**

What are the **conditions and reagents** for converting an alcohol to an iodoalkane?


**BACK**

The alcohol is heated under reflux with a mixture of **red phosphorus** and **iodine** (I<sub>2</sub>).

The red phosphorus and iodine react first to form PI<sub>3</sub>, which then reacts with the alcohol to produce the iodoalkane and H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>3</sub>.

## Card 16 — question_and_answer (`fl_BxB98rNrNQx4wwkQ`)

What reagent is used to **dehydrate** an alcohol to form an alkene?


**BACK**

**Concentrated phosphoric acid** (H<sub>3</sub>PO<sub>4</sub>) is used as the dehydrating agent.

The alcohol is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid, removing the -OH group and a hydrogen from an adjacent carbon to form a C=C double bond.

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_4MnYcjF9GT9DRpDP`)

**FRONT**

Why is **50% sulfuric acid** (not concentrated) used in the bromination of alcohols?


**BACK**

Concentrated sulfuric acid would **oxidise** the bromide ions (Br<sup>-</sup>) to bromine (Br<sub>2</sub>), giving different and unwanted products.

Using 50% sulfuric acid prevents this oxidation while still generating sufficient HBr to react with the alcohol.


Spec links: `spcpt_sDR6pCRBQXhYBfk3`


## Card 18 — question_and_answer (`fl_phdGDYdYyKQY48XM`)

**FRONT**

What are the oxidation products of **primary**, **secondary** and **tertiary** alcohols?


**BACK**

Primary alcohols are oxidised first to **aldehydes** and then further to **carboxylic acids**.

Secondary alcohols are oxidised to **ketones** only. Tertiary alcohols are **not oxidised**.

## Card 19 — keyword_definition (`fl_8FTDjRMXrc54tcyt`)

**FRONT**

Define **Fehling's solution**


**BACK**

**Fehling's solution** is an alkaline solution containing copper(II) ions that acts as an oxidising agent.

When warmed with an aldehyde, the clear blue solution turns opaque due to the formation of a red precipitate of copper(I) oxide. Ketones give a negative result.


Spec links: `spcpt_s2tbsnsfBTgQWs3B`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_cPMsWfCCg8gX6BfG`)

**FRONT**

What **colour change** is observed when acidified potassium dichromate(VI) is used to oxidise an alcohol?


**BACK**

The solution changes from **orange** to **green**.

The orange Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub><sup>2-</sup> ions are reduced to green Cr<sup>3+</sup> ions as they oxidise the alcohol.


Spec links: `spcpt_s2tbsnsfBTgQWs3B`


## Card 21 — keyword_definition (`fl_88CjrqPsHftJ2JSW`)

**FRONT**

Define **Tollens' reagent**


**BACK**

**Tollens' reagent** is an aqueous alkaline solution of silver nitrate in excess ammonia (ammoniacal silver nitrate).

When warmed with an aldehyde, Ag<sup>+</sup> ions are reduced to silver atoms, forming a silver mirror on the inside of the test tube. Ketones give a negative result.

## Card 23 — question_and_answer (`fl_Mm3qFZkkVrmprjz4`)

**FRONT**

How do you obtain an **aldehyde** (not a carboxylic acid) from the oxidation of a primary alcohol?


**BACK**

Use **distillation with addition**: the oxidising agent (acidified K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>) is heated while the alcohol is slowly added.

The aldehyde distils off as soon as it forms due to its lower boiling point, preventing further oxidation to a carboxylic acid.

## Card 24 — question_and_answer (`fl_hXrYJF37sZFDFZN5`)

**FRONT**

How do you obtain a **carboxylic acid** from the full oxidation of a primary alcohol?


**BACK**

Heat the alcohol under **reflux** with excess acidified K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>.

Refluxing keeps all products in the reaction mixture, allowing the aldehyde intermediate to be further oxidised to a carboxylic acid.
