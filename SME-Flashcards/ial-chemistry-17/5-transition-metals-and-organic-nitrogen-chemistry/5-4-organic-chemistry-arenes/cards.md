## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_rdDNC5W9w3hRtfPY`)

**FRONT**

Define **delocalised pi system** in benzene


**BACK**

The **delocalised pi system** in benzene is formed by the lateral overlap of p orbitals on all six carbon atoms, creating two ring-shaped clouds of electron density above and below the plane of the ring, with all C-C bonds identical in length.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_4mTXMvpPVGqq9dvp`)

**FRONT**

What bond length is observed for all C-C bonds in benzene, and what does this indicate?


**BACK**

All C-C bonds in benzene are **140 pm** — intermediate between a single bond (154 pm) and a double bond (134 pm). This indicates that all bonds are **identical**, consistent with a delocalised ring rather than the Kekulé structure.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_w5m2GtRC4JP28wbh`)

**FRONT**

What enthalpy of hydrogenation does the Kekulé structure of benzene predict, and what value is actually measured?


**BACK**

The Kekulé structure predicts **-360 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup> (3 × -120 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> for three C=C bonds). The actual measured value is **-208 kJ mol**<sup>**-1**</sup>, which is far less exothermic, indicating **extra stability** from delocalisation.

## Card 4 — true_or_false (`fl_hbMcCKJXZBMnWNyv`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Benzene decolourises bromine water in the same way as cyclohexene.


**BACK**

**False.**

Benzene does **not** decolourise bromine water. Unlike cyclohexene, benzene has no localised areas of high electron density to polarise the Br<sub>2</sub> molecule, so electrophilic addition does not occur.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_fstvYXn6nBjBkf2w`)

**FRONT**

What four types of evidence support the delocalised model of benzene over the Kekulé structure?


**BACK**

1. **Enthalpy of hydrogenation** (less exothermic than predicted)
2. **C-C bond lengths** (all 140 pm, not alternating 134/154 pm)
3. **Saturation tests** (benzene does not decolourise Br<sub>2</sub> water)
4. **Infrared spectroscopy** (no peak at 1650 cm<sup>-1</sup>)

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_7gdHNycYDRb7s6QC`)

**FRONT**

What are the four main types of reaction that benzene undergoes?


**BACK**

1. **Combustion** (with oxygen)
2. **Halogenation** (with halogen carrier)
3. **Nitration** (with conc. HNO<sub>3</sub>/H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>)

**BACK**

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_sFwFnCNwpGCKdz73`)

**FRONT**

How is aromaticity restored in Step 3 of the electrophilic substitution mechanism?


**BACK**

Aromaticity is restored by **heterolytic cleavage** of the C-H bond, where the bonding pair of electrons enters the pi bonding system, regenerating the **delocalised ring** and releasing H<sup>+</sup>.

## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_H487CwSmFgRMgb5d`)

**FRONT**

Why does phenol react with bromine water without a catalyst, whereas benzene requires a halogen carrier?


**BACK**

In phenol, a lone pair of electrons on the **oxygen** atom of the -OH group overlaps with the pi system, increasing the **electron density** of the ring. This makes phenol more susceptible to electrophilic attack without the need for an activated electrophile.
