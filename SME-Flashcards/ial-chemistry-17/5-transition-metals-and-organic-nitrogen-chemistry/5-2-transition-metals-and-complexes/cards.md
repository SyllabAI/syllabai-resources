## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_wbHPDr3hscS6Wvrv`)

**FRONT**

Define **transition metal**


**BACK**

A **transition metal** is a d-block element that can form at least one stable ion with an **incomplete d-subshell**.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_SG6cFcn8QPySRMYV`)

**FRONT**

Why are **scandium** and **zinc** not classified as transition metals?


**BACK**

Scandium only forms Sc<sup>3+</sup> ([Ar] 3d<sup>0</sup>) and zinc only forms Zn<sup>2+</sup> ([Ar] 3d<sup>10</sup>), so neither has an **incomplete d-subshell** in its common ion.

## Card 19 — question_and_answer (`fl_mnDWsTzh4TVd67cG`)

**FRONT**

Why do most transition metal compounds appear **coloured**?


**BACK**

Ligands cause the 3d orbitals to **split** into two energy levels. Electrons absorb visible light to move to the higher level, and the **complementary colour** to the absorbed colour is transmitted.


Spec links: `spcpt_wyG2hvKD4Fft432p`


## Card 20 — question_and_answer (`fl_MR9f3NDQTB4Sns92`)

**FRONT**

Why are Zn<sup>2+</sup> and Sc<sup>3+</sup> ions **colourless** in solution?


**BACK**

Zn<sup>2+</sup> has a **completely filled** 3d<sup>10</sup> subshell and Sc<sup>3+</sup> has an **empty** 3d<sup>0</sup> subshell, so neither can undergo d-electron promotion to absorb visible light.
