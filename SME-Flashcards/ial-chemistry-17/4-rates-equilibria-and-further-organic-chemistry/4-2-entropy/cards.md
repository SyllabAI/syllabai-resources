## Card 1 — keyword_definition (`fl_3KgjsqrGP9DDjFDB`)

**FRONT**

Define **entropy**


**BACK**

**Entropy** (*S*) is the number of possible arrangements of particles and their energy in a system — a measure of disorder or chaos. Units: J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup>.

## Card 2 — question_and_answer (`fl_2HmfFSm4dQKHjxWH`)

**FRONT**

What happens to the entropy of a system when it becomes more disordered?


**BACK**

Entropy **increases**. A more disordered system has more possible arrangements of particles and energy, making it **energetically more stable**.

## Card 3 — question_and_answer (`fl_25qbWgvbQ6ZYn6Gj`)

**FRONT**

Why does enthalpy change alone not explain why endothermic reactions occur?


**BACK**

In endothermic reactions, products are at a **higher energy** state than reactants, so a decrease in enthalpy cannot be the driving force. The driving force is an **increase in entropy**, which makes the system energetically more stable.

## Card 25 — keyword_definition (`fl_y7HRzPHnFBt3g9pt`)

**FRONT**

What is **Gibbs free energy**?


**BACK**

**Gibbs free energy** is a thermodynamic quantity that combines enthalpy and entropy to determine whether a reaction is feasible.

The Gibbs equation is: ΔG<sup>ꝋ</sup> = ΔH<sub>reaction</sub><sup>ꝋ</sup> − TΔS<sub>system</sub><sup>ꝋ</sup>

A reaction is feasible when ΔG<sup>ꝋ</sup> ≤ 0.


Spec links: `spcpt_ywMxrFB6V8KHdZz4`


## Card 26 — question_and_answer (`fl_TMKr5TKDCCnHz3QR`)

**FRONT**

What is the condition for a reaction to be **feasible**?


**BACK**

A reaction is feasible when **ΔG**<sup>**ꝋ**</sup> is **equal to or less than zero** (ΔG<sup>ꝋ</sup> ≤ 0).

This means the combined effect of enthalpy and entropy changes makes the reaction thermodynamically spontaneous.


Spec links: `spcpt_ywMxrFB6V8KHdZz4`

## Card 31 — question_and_answer (`fl_hD4rqYxwnyxcZQjf`)

**FRONT**

How do you calculate the **temperature at which a reaction becomes feasible**?


**BACK**

Set ΔG<sup>ꝋ</sup> = 0 and rearrange:

1. ΔG<sup>ꝋ</sup> = ΔH<sup>ꝋ</sup> − TΔS<sup>ꝋ</sup> = 0
2. T = ΔH<sup>ꝋ</sup> / ΔS<sup>ꝋ</sup>

Remember to convert ΔS from J K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup> to kJ K<sup>-1</sup> mol<sup>-1</sup> first.

What is **thermodynamic stability**?


**BACK**

**Thermodynamic stability** refers to whether a reaction is **feasible** under given conditions.

A substance is thermodynamically stable if ΔG for its decomposition is positive (reaction is not feasible).

**FRONT**

What is **kinetic stability**?


**BACK**

**Kinetic stability** refers to how fast a reaction occurs.

A reaction that is thermodynamically feasible may still be kinetically stable if the **activation energy** (E<sub>a</sub>) is too high for it to proceed at a measurable rate.
