What is an **aldehyde**?


**BACK**

An **aldehyde** is a carbonyl compound where the C=O group is at the end of the carbon chain. Aldehydes have the general formula RCHO and are named with the suffix -al.

## Card 4 — question_and_answer (`fl_QZVJnzyFqNrxmq2S`)

**FRONT**

Why do aldehydes and ketones have lower boiling points than their corresponding alcohols?


**BACK**

Aldehydes and ketones cannot form **hydrogen bonds** with each other. They only have permanent dipole-dipole interactions and London forces, which are weaker than the hydrogen bonds in alcohols.

## Card 5 — question_and_answer (`fl_MPrDGc8Sm2QQDRPz`)

**FRONT**

Why can small aldehydes and ketones dissolve in water, but larger ones cannot?


**BACK**

Smaller carbonyls can form **hydrogen bonds** with water via the lone pairs on their oxygen atom. In larger carbonyls, the long hydrocarbon chain disrupts water's hydrogen bonding without forming new ones, so they do not dissolve.

## Card 6 — true_or_false (`fl_MStDFhkSFTBwHtK4`)

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Aldehydes and ketones can hydrogen bond with each other.


**BACK**

**False.**

Aldehydes and ketones have no O-H or N-H bonds, so they cannot form hydrogen bonds **with each other**. They can, however, hydrogen bond with water molecules.

## Card 7 — question_and_answer (`fl_jYp4CVW2zGM8XVwc`)

**FRONT**

What intermolecular forces exist between carbonyl molecules?


**BACK**

Carbonyl molecules experience **permanent dipole-dipole interactions** and **London dispersion forces**. The electronegative oxygen of the C=O group creates a dipole.

## Card 9 — question_and_answer (`fl_d4MzVkG5CfNvJ2Sj`)

**FRONT**

How can acidified potassium dichromate(VI) distinguish between an aldehyde and a ketone?


**BACK**

An **aldehyde** is oxidised by acidified K<sub>2</sub>Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>7</sub>, producing an orange to green colour change. A **ketone** is not oxidised, so no colour change occurs.

## Card 10 — keyword_definition (`fl_nnW8zp9KMDDFYpdY`)

**FRONT**

What is **Tollens' reagent** and what is a positive result?


**BACK**

**Tollens' reagent** is ammoniacal silver nitrate, containing [Ag(NH<sub>3</sub>)<sub>2</sub>]<sup>+</sup> ions. A positive result is a **silver mirror**: the aldehyde reduces Ag<sup>+</sup> to solid silver. Ketones give no reaction.

## Card 11 — question_and_answer (`fl_GDpN5dJv6vs9dGrT`)

**FRONT**

What colour change indicates a positive result with Fehling's solution?


**BACK**

A positive result is a **blue to brick red** colour change. The aldehyde reduces Cu<sup>2+</sup> ions to Cu<sup>+</sup>, forming a brick red precipitate of copper(I) oxide, Cu<sub>2</sub>O.

## Card 12 — question_and_answer (`fl_j2vQV58XdkQkp9DD`)

**FRONT**

What type of reaction is the addition of HCN to a carbonyl compound, and what type of product is formed?


**BACK**

It is a **nucleophilic addition** reaction. The CN<sup>-</sup> ion attacks the carbonyl carbon, and the intermediate is then protonated to form a **hydroxynitrile**.

**FRONT**

What is the iodoform test and what does a positive result indicate?


**BACK**

The iodoform test uses iodine in alkaline solution. A positive result is a **yellow precipitate** of CHI<sub>3</sub> (tri-iodomethane), indicating the presence of a CH<sub>3</sub>CO- group (methyl ketones or ethanal).

## Card 17 — question_and_answer (`fl_QSBWm6s72c8F5bVG`)

**FRONT**

Why is the C=O group in aldehydes and ketones susceptible to nucleophilic attack?


**BACK**

The electronegative oxygen draws electron density towards itself, leaving the **carbonyl carbon** slightly positive (δ+). This makes it susceptible to attack by nucleophiles such as CN<sup>-</sup>.

**FRONT**

**True or False?**

Ketones give a positive result with Fehling's solution.


**BACK**

**False.**

Ketones are **resistant to oxidation** and do not react with Fehling's solution. The solution remains blue. Only aldehydes give a positive (brick red precipitate) result.
