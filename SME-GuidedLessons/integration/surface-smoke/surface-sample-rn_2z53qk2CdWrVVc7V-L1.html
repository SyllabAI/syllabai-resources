The carboxyl group and the general formula

Carboxylic acids are the family whose members carry the -COOH group at the end of a chain. This lesson fixes the functional group, the general formula and the trap inside the letter n, which counts only the alkyl chain and never the whole molecule.

Key points

  • Carboxylic acids are compounds containing the carboxyl functional group, -COOH.
  • The general formula is CnH2n+1COOH, which shortens to RCOOH.
  • The R stands for a varying hydrocarbon chain.
  • In the general formula, n counts only the alkyl chain, never every carbon.
  • Methanoic acid has n equal to zero, the classic point of confusion.

Depth layers

Low: Carboxylic acids all carry the -COOH group at the end of a chain. The general formula CnH2n+1COOH shortens to RCOOH, where R is the chain and n counts only its carbons. (The -COOH group is like a family crest pinned to the end of a scarf; however long you knit the scarf, the same crest goes on the same end.)

Medium: Members carry the carboxyl functional group, -COOH, giving the general formula CnH2n+1COOH, written RCOOH for short. The letter n counts only the alkyl chain: methanoic acid has n = 0, and propanoic acid has n = 2 because one of its three carbons belongs to the group.
