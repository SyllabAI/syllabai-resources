Primer

You already know from Rates of Reaction how fast a reaction proceeds and what speeds it up or slows it down. That idea is the engine of this note, because equilibrium is not about reactions stopping but about two rates meeting. Here you will meet reversible reactions and the balanced, dynamic state they settle into inside a sealed container.

Dynamic equilibrium

Some reactions can undo themselves: the products re-form the reactants as readily as the reactants form the products. This lesson defines the special state such a reaction settles into, called dynamic equilibrium, and the one condition it demands — a closed container. Two features mark that state, and they are the ones the exam asks for.

Key points

  • A reversible reaction is one that can run in both the forward and reverse directions.
  • Equilibrium is reached when the rates of the forward and reverse reactions become equal.
  • At equilibrium the molecules keep changing into each other, which is why it is called dynamic.
  • The concentrations of reactants and products stay constant while nothing else changes.
  • A closed system is required, so no species can leave the vessel and nothing can enter.

Depth layers

Low: In a sealed container, a reversible reaction settles at the point where both directions run at the same speed, with steady concentrations. (Imagine a shop where customers enter and leave at the same pace: the queue never changes even though people are always moving through.)

Medium: Dynamic means nothing stops: forward and reverse reactions keep converting the molecules into each other at equal rates, so the composition holds steady unless temperature or pressure changes.
