# An irrational inequality

> Excerpt: Students are asked to think about how we can prove that $e^\pi> \pi^e$. How do we even define those quantities given that $e$ and $\pi$ are irrational?

## Problem

It is astonishing that \(e^\pi\) and \(\pi^e\) are defined. It is even more amazing that we can prove \[e^\pi> \pi^e.\]

How would we go about convincing someone that this amazing inequality is true?

## Suggestion

There are several ways to get a feel for this inequality, here are some suggestions:

1. what do the functions \(e^x\) and \(x^e\) look like?

---

1. how could we approximate \(e\) and \(\pi\)?

---

1. do we know any functions that change a question about indices into a question about something else?

How do we define \(e^\pi\) and \(\pi^e\) given that both numbers are transcendental?
