# Ecology and the environment

## The Organisms in the Environment

* **Population:** All the organisms of a particular species found in an ecosystem at a certain time
* **Community:** The population of all species found in an ecosystem at a particular time
* **Habitat:** A place where an organism lives
* **Ecosystem:** A community of living organisms together with their non-living environment
* **Biodiversity:** The amount of variation shown by organisms in an ecosystem. It is a measure of the number of species and the relative abundance of each of the species present in the ecosystem.
* The biological factors of an ecosystem are known as its **biotic factors**, and its physical components are described as **abiotic factors**.
* **Sampling the population size of an organism**
  * To sample two areas and compare the population of a particular species, you must use a quadrat.
  * A **sample** is a smaller representative part of the population in which the experiment would be carried out.
  * A **quadrat** is a square, usually made of wood, metal or plastic, used in ecological investigations to sample animals or plants.
* **Let’s take the example of sampling two areas to compare the population of sunflowers in a field:**
  * Randomly sample the first area using a quadrat.
  * Be sure to place the quadrat in random positions using coordinates generated by a random number generator.
  * Count the number of sunflowers in the quadrat.
  * Repeat at least 10 times.
  * Find the average number of sunflowers in Area 1 by calculating the mean.
  * Repeat the same procedure for Area 2
* **For non-sedentary organisms, using a pitfall trap or nets is more advisable.**
  * **Pitfall traps** involve a jar sunk into the soil, and small species fall into it as they move across.
  * A **potter** is used to retrieve the animals that get trapped in the jar.
  * A **net** is used for flying organisms.
* **Systemic Sampling**
  * Place a transect along a line or a pre-determined pattern of the area that would be sampled.
  * Place the quadrat lining next to the transect at equal intervals. This shows the change along a gradient of the same environment.
* A tropical rainforest like the Amazon Rainforest would have a higher biodiversity than a desert like the Sahara Desert. This is because there is a higher variety of plant and animal species in a rainforest than in a desert region.
* A **predator** is an animal that feeds on another animal.
* A **parasite** is an organism that lives in or on a ‘host’, acquiring its nutrition from that organism. For example, mosquitoes are parasites that are the host-carrying plasmodium.
* Biotic and abiotic factors influence the population size.
* **Abiotic factors include:**
  * Temperature
  * Water availability
  * Light intensity
  * Hours of daylight
  * Soil conditions.
* **Biotic factors include:**
  * Availability of food
  * Competition for food resources
  * Predation
  * Parasitism
  * Disease
  * Presence of pollinating insects.
* **One of the main factors affecting large ecosystems is climate.**
  * Climate affects the growth of plants and vegetation.
  * It also affects how animals behave and interact with their environment.
  * A drastic change in the climate may lead to the migration of animals and the death of plants.
