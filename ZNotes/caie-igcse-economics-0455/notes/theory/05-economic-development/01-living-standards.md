# Economic Development

## Living Standards

**What are Living Standards?**

* **Living standards** refer to the **quality of life** people have in a country.

  It’s not just about **how much money** they earn, but also:

  * What they can **afford**,

  * The **public services** they get (like schools, hospitals)

  * How **safe and clean** their environment is,

  * And how **happy or healthy** they feel.

  **How is it Linked to Economic Development?**

  As a country **develops**, it **should improve** the living standards of its people.

  Economic development = **Growth** in the economy **\+** **improvements in people’s lives**

  So, it’s not just about **rising GDP**, but about **how that growth helps real people**.

### **How Do We Measure Living Standards?**

### 1) **GDP per capita**

* GDP ÷ population = average income per person

* Gives a **basic idea**, but doesn’t show income inequality

### 2) **Human Development Index (HDI)**

Combines:

* **Income** (GNI per capita)

* **Education** (mean years of schooling)

* **Health** (life expectancy)

A score between 0 and 1 – closer to 1 = higher development

### 3) **Other Indicators**

* **Poverty rate**

* **Infant mortality**

* **Access to clean water**

* **Literacy rate**

* **Unemployment rate**

---

### **Factors That Improve Living Standards (Through Economic Development)**

**Higher Incomes** → More spending power → Better homes, food, healthcare

**Better Education** → More skilled workers → Higher wages & job satisfaction

**Improved Healthcare** → Longer, healthier lives

**Infrastructure Development** → Roads, electricity, internet = better connectivity & business

**Access to Clean Water & Sanitation** → Directly improves health & life quality

**Employment Opportunities** → Reduces poverty and boosts dignity & confidence

---

### **Why Economic Growth Doesn’t Always Improve Living Standards**

**Income Inequality**
The rich get richer, the poor don’t benefit.

**Environmental Damage**
Pollution, deforestation, and overcrowding can ruin health and happiness.

**Overworking & Stress**
More jobs and longer hours, but less time with family or for rest.

**Inflation**
If prices rise faster than wages, people **can’t afford more**, even if GDP is rising.

**Urban Problems**
Slums, congestion, lack of housing in fast-growing cities.
