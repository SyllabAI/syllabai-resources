## Poverty

### **What is Poverty?**

Poverty is when people **don’t have enough income to meet their basic needs** – like food, clean water, clothing, shelter, healthcare, and education.

There are two main types:

1) **Absolute Poverty**

* When people **cannot afford the most basic necessities** for survival.

* Often measured using the **international poverty line**:

* Living on less than **US $2.15 a day** (as of 2023 World Bank definition)

Common in many **developing countries**.

---

2) **Relative Poverty**

* When people **are poor compared to others** in their own country.

* They might have a home and food, but they lack things that most others take for granted (like internet access, transportation, or decent education).

Common in **developed countries**.

---

### **Causes of Poverty**

1) **Unemployment**

* No job = no income.

* Long-term unemployment traps people in poverty.

2) **Low Wages**

* Some people work full-time but still earn too little to afford basic needs.

3) **Lack of Education and Skills**

* Without qualifications, it’s harder to get well-paid jobs.

4) **Overpopulation**

* In some countries, rapid population growth puts pressure on jobs, schools, and healthcare.

5) **Poor Health and Disability**

* Sick or disabled people may be unable to work and may face extra costs.

6) **Conflict and War**

* Destroy homes, schools, and businesses. People are forced to flee (refugees), often with nothing.

7) **Natural Disasters**

* Floods, droughts, or earthquakes can wipe out homes, jobs, and crops.

8) **Inequality**

* If economic growth benefits only the rich, the poor stay poor.

---

### **Consequences of Poverty**

Poverty doesn’t just affect individuals — it affects **entire economies**.

**On Individuals:**

* Poor diet → Illness and shorter life expectancy

* No access to good education → trapped in a cycle of poverty

* Homelessness, stress, and mental health issues

**On the Economy:**

* **Lower productivity** (sick, uneducated population)

* **Higher government spending** (on welfare, healthcare)

* **Social unrest** (protests, rising crime)

---

### **How Can Poverty Be Reduced?**

1) **Creating Jobs**

* Government and private sector investment in industries

2) **Improving Education & Training**

* Skills = better jobs = higher income

3) **Welfare Support**

* Unemployment benefits, pensions, free school meals, healthcare

4) **Progressive Taxation**

* Tax the rich more, use funds to support the poor

5) **Access to Healthcare**

* Healthy people can work and earn more

6) **Encouraging Economic Growth**

* A growing economy can create more jobs and raise living standards
