## **Differences in economic development between countries**

Differences in economic development between countries - Economic development refers to the improvement in living standards and quality of life in a country. The indicators of economic development are listed and explained below:

* **GDP per capita.** The GDP per capita represent the average income per person. A higher GDP per capita usually means a higher standard of living due to higher disposable income per person since their incomes have increases, on average.

* **Literacy rate.** Higher literacy means a more skilled workforce. Since education and training are a medium to boost literacy rate, the workers become more experienced once exposed to them due to having better knowledge about their work.

* **Life expectancy.** A higher life expectancy is usually caused by better healthcare and living conditions. As healthcare becomes more advanced, mortality rates fall, and the spread and sustainability of chronic diseases reduce.

* **Infant mortality rate.** The infant mortality rate is the number of deaths of children under one year of age per 1,000 live births in a given year. A lower rate is caused by better healthcare and nutrition by addressing many causes of death (like infections, low birth weight etc.).

### Reasons for differences in development

* **Natural resources:**

  * Countries with more resources (oil, minerals) may develop faster.

* **Education and skills:**

  * More educated workforce leads to higher levels of productivity.

* **Healthcare:**

  * Better health leads to higher productivity in workers.

* **Investment:**

  * More investment in capital leads to higher output.

* **Infrastructure:**

  * Good transport, electricity, and communication supports growth.

* **Technology:**

  * Advanced technology leads to higher efficiency.

### Problems faced by developing countries

* Low income levels

* Poor healthcare and education

* High unemployment

* Rapid population growth

* Dependence on primary products

### Consequences of differences in development

* Inequality between countries

* Migration from less developed to more developed countries by the citizens, in search for a better quality of life.

* Differences in standards of living
