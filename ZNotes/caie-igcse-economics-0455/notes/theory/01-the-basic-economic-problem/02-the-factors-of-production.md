## The Factors of Production

### **Factors**

Factors of Production are resources that are used for the production of goods and services. In total, there are 4 factors of production.

1) **Land** - Natural (Non man-made) resources available for production of goods and services. Example - Oil, wood, rivers.
2) **Labour** - Human input and contribution towards production of resources, in
   return for wages or salaries. Example - Factory worker, engineer, teacher.
3) **Capital** - Man-made resources available for production of goods and services. Example - Machines, softwares, factories.
4) **Enterprise** - Taking risks in setting up or running a business/firm. An
   entrepreneur is responsible for the production of goods and services, and makes decisions regarding resource allocation.

Mnemonic to remember Factors of production: **L**ittle **L**ions **C**hase **E**lephants.

### **Rewards For The Factors Of Production**

The **reward** **received for selling** the factors of production is known as **factor income**.

1) The factor income for land → **Rent**
2) The factor income for labour → **Wages**
3) The factor income for capital → **Interest**
4) The factor income for entrepreneurship → **Profit**

### **The Mobility of the Factors of Production**

Mobility of factors of production refers to how easily land, labour, capital, and enterprise can move between different locations and industries, to make the most of their efficiency. There are mainly two key aspects of factor mobility -

Geographical mobility refers to how easily a factor of production can **move from one location to another**. Example - A doctor moving from India to the U.S. for work (labor).

Here, the doctor’s **profession is the same,** but his **location has changed, which is a key example of geographical mobility.**

|

Occupational mobility refers to how easily a factor of production can **shift from one type of work or use to another**. Example - A factory worker learning coding and becoming a software engineer (labor).

Here, the factory worker’s **profession has changed,** but his **location is the same. P.S -** Labour is the most mobile factor of production.

### Mobility of Different Factors of Production -

* **Labour** – Generally the **most mobile** because it can move geographically and occupationally, but may face barriers such as skills, qualifications etc.

* **Capital** – Often **highly mobile due to involving** machines and money, which can be transferred between firms and countries (though fixed capital is less mobile than working capital).

* **Enterprise** – Fairly mobile. Entrepreneurs can switch industries, but risk and lack of knowledge can easily limit mobility.

* **Land** – **Perfectly immobile geographically.** Land cannot be moved, but its **use** can change (e.g. farming land used for housing), proving it’s immobility, geographically.

  #### Additionally, here are a few causes of Immobility of Labour -

  * Lack of skills or qualifications

  * Cost of moving houses or country

  * Language barriers

  * Differences in wages and living costs

  * Legal restrictions (e.g. visas)

For Land, influences affecting mobility of FOPs are -

* Flexible urban development plans.

* High financial incentives to convert land (e.g., turning farmland into profitable housing).

For labour, influences affecting mobility of FOPs are -

* * High availability of training programs.

  * Government relocating subsidies.

  * Better access to job information through digital employment platforms.

For Capital, influences affecting mobility of FOPs are -

* * Global financial markets allowing instant money transfers.

  * Multi-purpose machinery that can easily be adapted for different industries.

  * Free trade agreements and open-border economic policies.

For Enterprise, influences affecting mobility of FOPs are -

* Strong educational backgrounds in general business management.

* Availability of venture capital and government startup grants.

* Low administrative barriers to registering a new business.

### **Changes in the Quantity and Quality of the Various Factors Of Production**

**Changes in Quantity** - A change in the number or amount of factors available.

Common examples -

1. Labour - An increase in the QUANTITY of labour would be seen by population growth or immigration. A decrease would be shown through an increase in the ageing population (Higher number of people retired and unavailable for work) or emigration (moving out of the country)

   Effect:

   More labour = Higher output for firms.

   Too much labour = Unemployment due to very high competition for jobs.

2. Capital - An increase in the QUANTITY of capital would be seen by investment (More capital funded) in machinery or infrastructure (Increase in the quantity of buildings), while a decrease could be shown by depreciation (lower value of capital) or low investment (Lesser capital funded).

   Effect:

   More capital = More production possible.

3. Enterprise - An increase in the QUANTITY of enterprise would be seen by an increase in the number of entrepreneurs and businesses.

   Effect:

   More innovation \+ job creation (since more businesses would require more labour, which would open up more jobs).

**Changes in Quality** - A change in the productivity or efficiency of a factor.

Common examples -

1. Labour (Quality) - The quality of labour available can be improved by education, training and healthcare (since these would help labour improve their skills for jobs, which would make them more efficient and improve their quality).

   Effect:

   Higher productivity (Due to increased efficiency) and better output (since tasks would be completed faster)

2. Capital (Quality) - Improved by new technology (improves capital’s efficiency due to being more advanced) and modern machinery.

   Effect:

   Faster and more efficient production (Due to technological advancements which would improve efficiency).

3. Land (Quality) - Improved by fertilizers (add nutrients to the soil) and irrigation (provides water to land which prevents crops from drying out).

   Effect:

   Higher agricultural output since fertilizers help grow crops faster, and irrigation increases the longevity of crops.

4. Enterprise (Quality) - Improved by better skills (More knowledge about work, so quality of work improves) and innovation (Helps make resources more efficient and therefore, improves their quality).
