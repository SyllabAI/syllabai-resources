## Opportunity Cost

* Opportunity Cost is the **next most desired option that is given up due to a choice being made**. Due to the problem of scarcity, choices have to be made. A choice is when an **alternative is considered due to the scarcity of resources**.

* **Example**: A consumer deciding between buying a new phone or saving money.

  * If the consumer ends up buying a phone, **he is giving up saving the money** for benefit in the future.

  * If he ends up saving, **he is giving up being up to date with technology and fashion**.

### Influence of opportunity cost on decision making

* Choice is based on lowest opportunity cost. Any rational decision maker chooses the option that involves the lowest sacrifice.

* This makes consumers choose goods which give them highest satisfaction compared to sacrifice.

* This causes firms to allocate resources that give them the highest amount of profit.

* Workers choose between different jobs by considering their opportunity costs. For example, they may decide to leave Job A for Job B despite a lower pay due to better working conditions. 

* Workers therefore consider both wage and non-wage factors when making occupational choices.

* This causes governments to spend on projects where benefits outweigh the opportunity cost.

* When opportunity cost rises, options become less attractive and vice versa.

**Example (Real life) -**

A student has 2 hours. He has 2 options -

* Option A: Study for Economics Test

* Option B: Play sports.

  * If they choose to study, the opportunity cost = playing sports.

  * However, if that student has his exam the next day, the opportunity cost of studying is high. Therefore, they’d prefer to study instead of play. If the exam is far away, the opportunity cost of studying is low, which would mean the student would likely play.

This proves that the higher the value of the next best alternative (i.e opportunity cost), the less likely the option is to be chosen.
