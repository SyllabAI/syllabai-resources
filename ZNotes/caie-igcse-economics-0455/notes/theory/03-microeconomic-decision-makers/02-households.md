## Households

### Role of Households

**As Consumers**

* Households demand and consume goods and services produced by firms.

* They decide how to allocate their disposable income between:

  * **Consumption (spending)**

  * **Saving**

  * **Borrowing**

**As Workers / Owners of Factors of Production**

* Households supply the **factors of production** (land, labour, capital and enterprise) to firms.

* In return, they earn **factor incomes**:

  * **Rent** (land)

  * **Wages** (labour)

  * **Interest** (capital)

  * **Profit** (enterprise)

    ### Household Expenditure

* **Disposable income**: income after taxes and transfers; determines spending ability.

* **Marginal propensity to consume (MPC)**: proportion of extra income spent on consumption.

* **Wealth**: ownership of assets (houses, shares) that boosts spending power.

* **Interest rate**: higher rates discourage consumption (cost of borrowing rises).

* **Engel’s Law**: as income rises, % of income spent on necessities falls, luxuries rise.

### Household Saving

* **Motives**: precautionary (emergencies), retirement, future large purchases, to earn interest.

* Influenced by:

  * **Income level** – higher income → more saving capacity.

  * **Real interest rates** – reward for saving after adjusting for inflation.

  * **Consumer confidence** – optimism increases spending, pessimism increases saving.

  * **Inflation** – high inflation discourages saving (reduces real value).

* **Marginal propensity to save (MPS)**: proportion of extra income saved.

### Saving

* Reasons: precautionary, retirement, future spending, earn interest.

* Influenced by **income, real interest rates, inflation, confidence**.

* **MPS (marginal propensity to save)** = share of extra income saved.

### Borrowing

* For **durables** (cars, furniture) and **investment goods** (houses, education).

* Depends on income, wealth (collateral), **cost of credit** (interest rates), and confidence.
