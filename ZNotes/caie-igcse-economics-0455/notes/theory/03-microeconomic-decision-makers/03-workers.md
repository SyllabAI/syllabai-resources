## Workers

### Factors affecting an individual’s choice of occupation -

Workers choose jobs based on wage, non-wage, and other factors.

### Wage Factors -

Higher wages pay workers more. Since this improves their standard of living (i.e quality of life) and satisfies their wants more, more workers are naturally attracted to accept higher wage salaries. Salaries typically include the basic salary, overtime oay and bonuses, all of which affect how much a worker is paid.

### Non-Wage Factors -

Salaries aren’t the only things that affect workers’ decision making on which job to accept. Non-wage factors play a major role as well. Common non-wage factors are job satisfaction (How satisfied workers are with their job), working conditions (whether the safe or dangerous), working hours (how many number of hours workers are expected to work), location (close to workers’ houses or far from there), job security (how unlikely/likely workers are to lose their job) etc.

### Skills & Qualifications -

If workers are better educated than the rest, they have access to better jobs since firms are willing to hire workers with more understanding and knowledge of their work.

**Opportunity Cost -**

Workers have to choose one job in the end. If they have been accepted to multiple jobs, they are forced to give up another, which indicates opportunity cost playing a big role. Workers tend to usually choose jobs which give them the highest net benefit.

### Mobility of Labour -

* Occupational Mobility - This is the ability of workers to switch between jobs and how conveniently they can do so.

* Geographical Mobility - This is the ability of workers to move locations and how easily that’s possible.

* A low mobility limits workers’ choices.

### Labour Market Imperfections -

Labour markets do not always operate efficiently due to various imperfections that prevent the efficient allocation of labour.

* Discrimination - Workers may be treated unfairly based on characteristics such as gender, race, age, disability, or religion, resulting in unequal employment opportunities or wage differences despite having similar skills and qualifications.

* Imperfect Information - Workers may not know about all available job opportunities, while employers may not have complete information about workers' skills, qualifications, or productivity. This can lead to unemployment, labour shortages, or workers accepting jobs that are not the best match for their abilities.

### Wage Determination -

Wages are determined by the demand and supply of labour. The demand for labour comes from firms, who hire workers to work for them. It depends on the demand for products (i.e. derived demand), productivity of workers, and profitability. A higher demand for workers means higher willingness from firms to pay them, which means higher wages. The supply of labour comes from workers, who are actively seeking jobs and are willing to be hired. This is influenced by the wage level, skills of workers, the conditions workers are required to work in and their education/training. A higher supply means more competition amongst workers and more willingess from them to settle for a lower pay to stand out, which lowers wages.

The equilibrium wage is where labour demand = labour supply. The equilibrium wage determines the wage level and number of workers employed.

### Factors Affecting the Demand for Labour -

The demand for labour is a derived demand, meaning firms demand workers because they are needed to produce goods and services that consumers demand.

The main factors affecting the demand for labour are:

1. Demand for the Product (Derived Demand)

   * If consumer demand for a firm's product increases, the firm will need to hire more workers.

   * If demand falls, the demand for labour also decreases.

2. Labour Productivity

   * More productive workers produce more output per hour.

   * Firms are therefore willing to employ more productive workers because they generate higher revenue and profits.

3. Profitability of Firms

   * Higher profits enable firms to expand production and hire more workers.

   * Lower profits may force firms to reduce employment.

4. Cost of Labour (Wage Rate)

   * If wages rise significantly, employing workers becomes more expensive, so firms may reduce the quantity of labour demanded.

5. Technology

   * New technology may increase the demand for skilled workers but reduce the demand for workers performing routine tasks.

### Reasons for Differences in Earnings -

* **Skills & Qualifications** - The more skilled and qualified workers are, the more they stand out to firms, and the more likely they are to be paid higher wages.

* **Demand & Supply** - If the demand for labour is high, but the supply of labour is low, firms will have to compete for labour. In order to stand out, they’d be willing to offer higher wages to attract more workers to choose to work for them instead of other firms. On the other hand, if the demand for labour is low and the supply is high, wages will usually be low because workers will find it tough to find jobs due to their limited supply. In order to stand out to firms, they’d be willing to work for a lower pay (wage), which would help them stand out amongst other workers.

* **Risk/Danger** - The jobs that carry high levels of risk (like mining and construction) often attract a smaller pool of workers due to the lack of safety. Due to this, the overall supply of labour is low, causing firms to naturally want to pay higher wages to attract more workers.

* **Length of Training** - The longer the training required for a job (like doctors), the more the firms are willing to pay due to the lack of supply for the job (since lesser people choose it as a career path due to the requirment for extensive training), which causes wages to stay high for them.

* **Location** - Urban areas are more concentrated with skill-intensive industries (like finance and tech), which are higher paying. Due to this, the wages there are usually higher than in rural areas. Rural areas offer more agriculture and farm job opportunities, which are not high paying, causing wages to naturally be on the lower end there.

### Division of Labour/Specialization -

Division of labour is when production is broken into smaller tasks assigned to workers. It is a form of specilization, which is a result of being an expert in a specific area of tasks.

**Advantages -**

* Increases productivity through specialisation.

* Saves time by reducing the need to switch between different tasks.

* Lowers average costs as workers become more efficient.

* Allows mass production of goods.

**Disadvantages -**

* Repetitive work may reduce motivation and productivity.

* Workers become highly specialised and less flexible.

* Production may be disrupted if one worker or machine fails.

* Limited opportunities to develop a wide range of skills.
