## Firms and Production

### Production

* **Definition:** Process of converting inputs (FoPs) into outputs (goods/services).

* **Short run:** At least one FoP is fixed (usually capital).

* **Long run:** All FoPs are variable.

* **Stages of production:**

  1. Primary → raw materials (farming, mining)

  2. Secondary → manufacturing/processing

  3. Tertiary → services

### Productivity

* **Definition:** Output per unit of input.

* **Formula:** Productivity = Total output ÷ Input.

* **Importance:** Higher productivity → lower average cost → higher competitiveness.

* **Factors affecting productivity:** Training, technology, motivation, infrastructure.

### Demand for factors of production

Demand for factors of production is a derived demand and depends on the demand for the final goods/services they help produce. Firms demand labour and capital becuase they are needed to produce goods and services.

* **Factors that affect demand:**

  * **Demand for the product:** Higher demand for goods leads to firms requiring more capital and labour, which eventually leads to higher demand for labour and capital.

  * **Price of the factor:** Higher wages and rent lead to lower revenue firms have to spend on hiring more capital and labour. Due to this, the demand is lower overall for labour and capital.

  * **Price of substitutes:** If machines become cheaper, firms may replace labour with machines, reducing demand for labour.

### Labour-intensive and capital-intensive production

* **Labour-intensive production:** Production that uses more labour than capital. It is used when labour is cheap and easily available.

  **Advantages:** Creates more employment since labour is demanded more.

  **Disadvantages:** Lower productivity since labour is more inefficient than capital. Quality of goods may also not be as good due to labour having margin for error.

* **Capital-intensive production:** Production that uses more capital than labour. It is used when labour is expensive or technology is available to improve efficiency for a lower cost.

  **Advantages:** Higher productivity since capital has higher efficiency and consistent quality due to lower margin of error.

  **Disadvantages:** High initial cost since machinery is expensive to set up. It may also lead to unemployment since labour would be lesser in demand.
