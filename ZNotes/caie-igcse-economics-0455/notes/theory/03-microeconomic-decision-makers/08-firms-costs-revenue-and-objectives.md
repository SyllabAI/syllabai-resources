## Firms’ Costs, Revenue and Objectives

* **Fixed Costs:** Costs that have to be paid regardless of the output, e.g. interest on loans
* **Variable Costs:** Costs that change with the output. The higher the output, The higher the variable costs
* **Breakeven:** where total revenue = total cost
* **Total Revenue:** the total receipts a seller can obtain from selling goods or services to buyers
* **Average Revenue:** the revenue generated per unit of output sold

**Profit:** The amount remaining after subtracting total costs from total revenue.

$$
\mathbf{\text{Average Fixed Cost}} = \frac{\text{Fixed Costs}}{\text{Output}}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Average Variable Cost}} = \frac{\text{Variable Costs}}{\text{Output}}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Total Variable Cost}} = \text{Variable Costs} \times \text{Output}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Total Cost}} = \text{Total Variable Cost} + \text{Total Fixed Cost}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Average cost}} = \frac{\text{Total Cost}}{\text{Output}}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Total Revenue}} = \text{Price Per Unit} \times \text{Quantity Sold}
$$

$$
\mathbf{\text{Profit or Loss}} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Total Cost}
$$

### Types of Profit -

**1) Normal Profit**

* Normal profit is the minimum profit a firm must earn to remain in business.

* It occurs when Total Revenue = Total Cost (including opportunity costs).

* The firm covers all its costs but earns no extra economic profit.

**2) Supernormal (Abnormal) Profit**

* Supernormal profit is profit earned above normal profit.

* It occurs when Total Revenue \> Total Cost (including opportunity costs).

* It may arise due to innovation, strong branding, market power, or a lack of competition.

### Output Decisions of Firms

Firms use information about costs and revenue to decide the level of output that best meets their objectives.

**Profit Maximisation**

* If a firm is earning a profit, it aims to produce the level of output that maximises the difference between total revenue and total cost.

* Firms may increase or decrease output depending on whether doing so increases profit.

**Loss Minimisation**

* If a firm cannot make a profit in the short run, it may continue producing if it can cover its variable costs.

* The firm chooses the level of output that minimises its losses while deciding whether conditions are likely to improve.

* If the firm cannot cover its variable costs, it may be forced to shut down production.

### Objectives of Firms -

**1. Survival**

* The firm's objective is to remain in business, especially during periods of low demand and/or intense competition.

**2. Profit Maximisation**

* The firm aims to maximise the difference between total revenue and total cost.

**3. Revenue Maximisation**

* The firm aims to maximise total revenue rather than profit.

**4. Sales Maximisation**

* The firm aims to maximise the quantity of goods or services sold.

* Managers may pursue higher sales to increase market share, earn bonuses, or improve the firm's reputation, even if profits are not at their maximum.

**5. Growth**

* The firm aims to expand its size by increasing output, opening new branches, entering new markets, or acquiring other businesses.

**6. Satisficing**

* Instead of maximising one objective, the firm aims to achieve acceptable levels of profit, growth, sales, and other goals.

**7. Social Welfare**

* Some firms aim to benefit society by protecting the environment, supporting communities, and producing goods and services.
