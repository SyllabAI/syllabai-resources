## Market Structure

### **Competitive Markets**

**Characteristics of Perfect Competition**

* Many buyers and sellers – There are many buyers and sellers in the market, so no single buyer or seller can influence the market price.

* Homogeneous (identical) products – All firms sell identical products, so consumers do not have a preference for one firm's product over another.

* Free entry and exit – Firms can easily enter the market when profits are high and leave when profits are low, encouraging competition.

* Perfect information – Buyers and sellers have complete information about prices, quality, and market conditions.

* Price takers – Individual firms must accept the market price because they are too small to influence it.

**Implications of Perfect Competition**

* Businesses will charge the same price, a minimum price they can charge without going out of business.

* Price will be equivalent to the lowest average cost of producing
  goods

* The average cost of production would be the same as the average revenue for selling

* No firm would risk charging more than the market price

### **Monopoly Markets**

* Firms with monopolistic powers control all of the market shares
* Able to influence the price; **price makers**
* Can restrict competition with artificial barriers to entry & other
  pricing strategies
* One firm controls the entire market supply
* May use predatory pricing to force competing firms out
* Other firms deterred from competing due to a lack of capital

**Non-Price Competition**

In imperfectly competitive markets, firms often compete using methods other than price to attract and retain customers.

**Examples of non-price competition include:**

* Advertising and marketing to increase brand awareness.

* Product quality by improving features, durability, or performance.

* Customer service, such as after sales support and warranties.

* Branding and packaging to differentiate products from competitors.

* Innovation by introducing new or improved products.

**Importance of Non-Price Competition**

* Helps firms differentiate their products from competitors.

* Builds customer loyalty.

* Increases sales and market share without reducing prices.

* Allows firms to compete even when price competition is limited.

**Advantages of Monopolies**

* It avoids duplication & wastage of resources
* Economics of scale: benefits can be passed to consumers
* High profits can be used for research & development
* Monopolies may use price discrimination, which benefits the economically weaker sections of the society
* Monopolies can afford to invest in the latest technology & machinery to be efficient & avoid competition

**Disadvantages of Monopolies**

* May supply less & charge higher prices
* May offer less consumer choice and lower quality products than if
  they had to compete with other firms
* They may have higher production costs because they are poorly managed
* Restrict competition using barriers to entry
