## The Role of Markets in Allocating Resources

### **The Market System**

* The market system is an economic system in which **prices, determined by demand and supply, change in response to shortages and surpluses to allocate resources**.

* Here, **changes in prices caused by shortages and surpluses** help businesses decide how much to produce, invest, and sell.

### **Key Resources Allocation Decisions**

In order to fix the basic economic problem of scarcity of resources, **3 key resource allocation questions are asked**.

* **What gets produced? -** Since resources available for production are limited, and eventually supply is limited too, suppliers need to decide on what needs to be produced. Example - More schools for better education.

* **How is it produced? -** This question highlights who would be used for the production of good/services. Is it the labour force for higher employment rates, or is it machinery for more efficiency?

* **For whom is it produced? -** Eventually, whatever is produced needs to be sold. This questions highlights who will be the user of the service provided. Will it be public and available to all, or only a specific type of user force?

### **Price Mechanism**

These 3 key resource allocation decisions are guided by the **price mechanism**. The price mechanism refers to how changes in prices influence the decisions of producers and consumers in a market economy. It works through **supply and demand**:

1. **Rising Prices** – When **demand** for a product **increases**, its **price goes up**. This **signals producers to supply more** since higher prices mean higher potential profits.

2. **Falling Prices** – When **demand decreases or supply increases, prices drop**. This **discourages production** and shifts resources to more profitable uses.

3. **Resource Allocation** – Scarce resources are directed toward their **most valuable uses** because businesses and consumers respond to price changes by adjusting their decisions.

**Features of Price Mechanism -**

* No government intervention involved when private economic agents allocate resources, meaning firms are fully independent.

* The price mechanism allocates factors of production by directing them towards uses that provide the highest financial returns, such as higher wages, rent, interest, or profit.

* Competition increases choice and opportunities by encouraging firms to improve efficiency, lower prices, and raise quality in order to attract consumers.
