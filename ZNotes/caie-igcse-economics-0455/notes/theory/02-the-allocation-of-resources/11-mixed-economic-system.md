## Mixed Economic System

A **mixed economic system** is one where **both the government and private individuals** play important roles in making economic decisions.

In other words, it combines elements of:

* A **market economy** (where decisions are made by private individuals and businesses)

* And a **planned economy** (where the government controls resources and production)

In a mixed economy:

* **Private businesses** produce most goods and services.

* **Consumers** decide what to buy, and **firms** decide what to produce, just like in a market economy.

* However, the **government also steps in** to provide certain services (like healthcare, education, roads) and to regulate the economy (e.g. setting minimum wages, controlling pollution).

The goal is to get the **efficiency of the market system** with the **fairness and stability provided by government involvement**.

Reasons for government intervention in mixed economies -

1) To reduce inequalities

2) To provide public goods

3) To protect the environment

4) To manage the economy

5) To protect consumers and workers

Examples of Mixed Economies are **USA, India, UK** etc.

**Maximum Prices (Price Ceiling) -**

Maximum prices are the highest legal price that can be charged for a good or service.
The government sets them below the market equilibrium price to make things more affordable.

**Minimum Prices (Price Floor) -**

Minimum prices are the lowest legal price that can be charged for a good or service.
They are set above the market equilibrium price to support producers, especially when prices fall too low.

### Other Government Microeconomic Policy Measures -

1. **Regulation** - Rules imposed to control business behavior to Protect consumers and workers.

2. **Privatisation** - Transfer of ownership from public sector to private sector to Increases efficiency and competition, and improve quality.

3. **Nationalisation** - Transfer of ownership from private sector to government to Ensures access to essential services.

4. Direct Provision of Goods to Ensures universal access and reduce inequality.

### Effectiveness of Government Intervention -

**Advantages -**

1. Corrects market failure

2. Reduces negative externalities

3. Encourages merit goods

4. Improves equity and fairness

**Limitations -**

1. Government failure (poor decision-making)

2. High administrative costs

### Evaluation of Government Intervention

* Government intervention is effective when it targets the specific cause of market failure.

* Policies such as taxes, subsidies, and regulations can improve resource allocation and increase social welfare.

* But, excessive or poorly designed intervention may create **government failure**, or could reduce market efficiency.

* Therefore, the effectiveness of government intervention depends on the quality of policy design and implementation.
