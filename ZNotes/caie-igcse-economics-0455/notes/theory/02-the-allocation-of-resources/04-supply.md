## Supply

**Supply** refers to the quantity of a good or service that producers are **willing and able** to sell at different prices over a given period, ceteris paribus (all other factors remaining constant).

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/1cae503f10d135677a71f6ab.webp =956x)

For supply, as **prices increase, supply increases**. This means that there is a **positive relationship** between the price and quantity supplied.

$$
\text{Quantity supplied} \propto \text{Price}
$$

### **Movement Along A Supply Curve**

**Movement along the supply curve** happens when the price of a good changes, ceteris paribus (all other factors remain constant).

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/b98346b992f7ffe83e3526ca.webp =1047x)

**Extension in Quantity Supplied (A → B)**

* When the **price increases**, the **quantity supplied increases**.

* This results in a movement **upward** along the supply curve.

**Contraction in Quantity Supplied (A → C)**

* When the **price decreases**, the **quantity supplied decreases**.

* This results in a movement **downward** along the supply curve.

### **Shift In The Supply Curve**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/786f01ecb4413d5c53face47.webp =1053x)

**Rightward Shift (Increase in Supply)**

* The supply curve shifts from **S0 to S1**.

**Leftwards Shift (Decrease in Supply)**

* The supply curve shifts from **S0 to S2**.

### **Factors that affect supply**

* Costs of Production (COP)

* Indirect Taxes

* Subsidies

* New Technology

* Change in the number of firms in the industry

* Weather Related Events

The link Between Individual and Market Supply (Aggregation) is that Market supply is the total quantity supplied by all firms in a market and is formed by aggregating (horizontally adding) the individual supply curves of all producers at each price level.

### Factors of Shifts in supply curves -

1. Changes in cost of production

2. Changes in technology

3. Changes in the number of firms

4. Changes in indirect taxes/subsidies

**Examples of shifts in supply curves -**

1. A fall in wage costs increasing the supply of manufactured goods.

2. New restaurants opening increasing the market supply of fast food.

3. A subsidy to farmers increasing the supply of wheat.
