## **Price Changes**

### **Causes of Price Changes**

* Markets in the real world are constantly changing and are known as **dynamic markets**.

* The balance between supply and demand can vary rapidly, adjusting **within seconds** in certain industries (such as foreign exchange or cryptocurrency) or over **extended periods** in others (like real estate or automobiles).

* Any change in factors influencing **supply or demand** will affect this balance, triggering **natural market mechanisms** to correct the **excess demand or supply**.

### **Consequences of Price Changes**

Here’s a real life example of price changes:

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/2ac861f850a96fc9281c292e.webp =764x)

During summers, local farms grow lots of strawberries, so supermarkets have **more supply**.

* To sell them quickly (since strawberries go bad fast), stores and supermarkets **lower the price**.

* **Result:** More people buy strawberries because they’re cheaper, which results in **higher demand**.

**Diagram Analysis**

* **Demand Curve Shift (D₁ → D₂):**

  * The diagram shows a **rightward shift in demand** from **D₁ to D₂**, meaning more people want to buy strawberries.

* **New Equilibrium (Higher Price & Quantity):**

  * The shift in demand leads to a **new equilibrium** where:

    * Price **increases** from **P₁ to P₂**.

    * Quantity **increases** from **Q₁ to Q₂**.

### **Consequences of Price Changes**

A **change in price** leads to a **movement along the demand and supply curves**, causing changes in the **quantity demanded and quantity supplied**.

Example of consequences of an increase in price - An increase in the price of iPhones reduces quantity demanded for them. But, this also encourages firms to supply more smartphones, creating excess supply.

Example of consequences of a fall in price - A fall in the price of movie tickets increases quantity demanded but discourages supply, leading to excess demand.

Please Note: A change in price causes a **movement along** the demand and supply curves, **not a shift**.
