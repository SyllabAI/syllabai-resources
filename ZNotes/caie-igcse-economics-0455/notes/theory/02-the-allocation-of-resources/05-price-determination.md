## Price Determination

Price of goods is determined by the interaction between demand and supply. This interaction determines whether a market is in equilibrium or disequilibrium.

### Market Equilibrium -

A market is a place where buyers and sellers meet and exchange goods. As read earlier, when consumers purchase goods, it is known as the quantity demanded, and when sellers sell goods, it is known as the quantity supplied.

The point where the quantity demanded = the quantity supplied is known as the equilibrium. It is caused when the equilibrium price = equilibrium quantity. In such cases, there is no pressure for the price to change since the supply and demand both match perfectly. It can be shown in the diagram below

### Market Disequilibrium -

A market disequilibrium is achieved when the **quantity demanded ≠ quantity supplied.** It can occur due to excess demand (more demand for a service than its supply) or excess supply (more supply for a service than its demand).

* **Excess Demand (Shortage)** – Here, **quantity demanded \> quantity supplied** at a given price, and the prices are too low. This causes shortage of goods (since there is limited quantity available to be sold) and causes consumers to compete (since consumers have to fight for each good due to its limited supply). To deal with this, firms raise their prices (by doing so, consumers who are not willing to pay the revised profit stop demanding the good) and this eliminates excess demand, and makes the market move closer to the equilibrium.

* **Excess Supply (Surplus)** – Here, **quantity supplied \> quantity demanded** at a given price, and the prices are too high. The effect of this is surplus (due to excess stock no consumers are demanding). To deal with this, firms lower prices (this makes the good stand out and attracts more consumers since they feel they are getting goods for a cheap price), which helps move the market closer to the equilibrium point.
