## Question 5

**5(a)** *Price elasticity of demand measures how much the quantity demanded of a good changes in response to a change in its price.* Determinants of price elasticity of demand include whether the good is a luxury or necessity and the proportion of income the price of the good takes up.

**5(b)** The economic problem describes **unlimited wants exceeding limited resources**. Consumers have limited incomes so they cannot buy everything they want. If they choose to buy one product, they may have enough money to buy another product they want.

**5(c)** *A trade union is an organisation of workers that seek to protect and improve workers’ pay and condition.* To join a trade union, worker often have to pay a **monthly fee** (a subscription). Workers may choose not to join a trade union because they cannot afford to pay for a subscription because of their low income. If workers are **satisfied with their income and working conditions**, they will not have a use in joining a trade union and collectively bargaining with their employers.

**5(d)**

*State pension is a regular payment made to retired people, funded by the government.*

* The government should reduce the money it gives to each state pensioner (people receiving state pension). This is because in the future due to advances in healthcare, a significant proportion of the population may be over 65 years of age. The amount the government has to spend on state pensions will increase by a large amount and cause a budget deficit (government expenditure exceeding government revenue). Reducing state pension could reduce a budget deficit. By spending less on pensions, more money can be spent on, for example, education, which could benefit a large part of the population. Additionally, reducing state pensions may encourage people to work for longer, increasing the quantity of goods and services produced, as they know that will not be able to receive a high pension when they are retired and will try to earn as much as they can while working.

* However, the living standards of state pensioners will worsen as they have less money to spend and may not be able to enjoy as many goods and services that they would like. Also, with less money to spend, pensioners will demand less as they are able to afford less. Pensioners will spend less if pension is reduced which could lead firms to produce less output and firms may even reduce their size, increasing unemployment. Reduced pensions could mean more people living in absolute poverty, which is defined as the condition where an individual does not have the financial means to afford basic necessities. Retired workers may not receive enough pension to afford necessities like food, if pension is reduced.
