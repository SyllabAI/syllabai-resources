## Question 3

**3(a)** A minimum price is a price set by the government which is above the equilibrium price (price at which demand and supply are equal).

**3(b)** Capital-intensive production **eliminates human error** as capital is used instead of labour, so there is less wastage of resources. Additionally, capital goods will not take **industrial action** like human labour might, so the production process will not be disrupted.

**3(c)** *Living standards refer to the quality of life and wellbeing of a population, determined by access to goods and services, income, health, and education.* Farmers with low income may **not be able afford basic necessities** (such as food). **Banks may not lend to these farmers**, as banks feel that they cannot pay back loans due their low income. Because they don’t receive loans, farmers **may not be able to buy appliances** like refrigerators, which could improver their quality of life. Farmers with low income may not be able to afford **high quality healthcare** and **nutritious foods** and so may suffer with poor health. They are likely to do **physically demanding work** without proper rest and so are likely to fall ill.

**3(d)**

*The macroeconomic aims are economic growth, full employment, price stability, income redistribution and balance of payments stability.*

* A subsidy on the export of sugar would make **exports cheaper**. Demand for exports would increase and **export revenue will also increase**, provided that demand for sugar exports is price elastic. The increased revenue will improve the current account balance will improve, achieving the aim of balance of payments stability. Additionally, the higher output of sugar will contribute to **economic growth**, which is defined as the increase in quantity of goods and services produced by an economy. The subsidy may encourage producers to expand and take up more workers to produce more sugar. This will help achieve the aim of **full employment**.

* Even with the subsidy, the sugar produced by other countries may be cheaper, so people may choose to buy their exports instead so export revenue will not increase. Sugar sellers may not pass on the lowered costs as they want more profit so the price of sugar may still be high. Farmers and mill owners may become lazy and inefficient because they may believe they don’t have to work as hard as they are given a subsidy. There is an opportunity cost involved in providing a subsidy. The government could instead use this money to spend on education, for example, which could be more beneficial.
