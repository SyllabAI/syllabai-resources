**2(d)**

Private sector firms are not owned by the government and operate with the primary goal of making a profit. Public sector firms are owned by the government.

* Private sector firms may charge lower prices than public sector firms. This is because in the private sector, firms face a lot of **competition**. To prevent customers from buying from other firms, a firm in the private sector may charge low prices to make their products more affordable, attracting customers. Private sector firms may **more funds to invest** in research and development, and may be able to create cheaper products. Higher investment (spending on capital goods, like machinery) of private sector firms can cut **costs of production** by, for example, using capital-intensive methods of production. Low costs of production allow firms to charge lower prices without worrying about decreasing profit.

* However, because private sector firms are mainly motivated by profit, they may try to earn more profit by charging high prices. Private sector firms could be **monopolies**, which charge high prices to earn profit as they know that customers have no other firms to buy from and will continue to buy from the monopoly. Public sector firms may be given a **subsidy** (a government payment made to a producer to encourage production), so their costs of production reduces and they can afford to charge lower prices. Additionally, public sector firms may have the goal of **public welfare** and want to ensure that their products are affordable by all, even those with low income. To achieve this goal, public sector goods may charge low prices.
