**2(a)** Merging (combining two or more firms to create one large firm) with a firm **reduces competition** and **greater revenue** can be made.

**2(b)** Workers who lose their jobs may lose their sole **source of income** and they may be forced to spend less on education or housing, resulting in lower living standards. Additionally, being unemployed makes it **easier to go into debt** as loans may need to be taken to be able to buy products. Workers who lose their jobs may find it difficult to pay back past loans because of lack of consistent income.

**2(c)** An increase in labour mobility could refer to **occupational** or **geographical mobility**. An increase in occupational mobility of labour is the increase in the ability of workers to change jobs. Workers may be able to improve their skills and productivity by changing jobs. An increase in geographical mobility of labour is the increase in the ability of workers to move from one location to another. Workers may be able to relocate to a better area or live closer to their families.

**2(d)**

* P*rice elasticity of demand is a measure of how responsive the change in quantity demand is to change in price.* Demand for chocolate may be price elastic as chocolate has many substitutes. Chocolate may be seen as a luxury and considered to be an unnecessary purchase. This means that consumers are likely to switch to cheaper alternatives if the price of chocolate increases. In the long term, it will be easy for consumers to change their spending patterns and demand for chocolate will be more price elastic.

* However, demand for chocolate may be price inelastic because it can be considered to be addictive. Some may see chocolate to be a necessity. It may be relatively cheap and only take up a small part of customers’ income. These factors explain how quantity of demand may only fall by a small percentage despite a large percentage increase in price.
