**3(a)** The output of **two different types of products** (such as consumer goods and capital goods) are measured on the axes of a PPC curve.

**3(b)** A merit good is a good that is underconsumed as consumers do not appreciate its benefits, and therefore underproduced. The private sector may produce it as it is **rival and excludable**. An example of a merit good is education. A public good is only produced by the government as it is **non-rival and non-excludable**, meaning that people can enjoy the benefits of the product without paying for it. An example of a public good is defence.

**3(c)** *Division of labour is the process of splitting a complex production into smaller, specialised tasks which individual workers can focus on.* Division of labour causes workers to **specialise** in what they are best at producing. This specialisation often **saves time and reduces mistakes**, as workers become more efficient by repeatedly performing the same task. This means that **average costs are lowered** and firms are able to increase their output.

**3(d)**

* If only one firm produces shampoo, it is likely to have a high level of output and experience economies of scale. Increased output causes the firm to have low average costs, allowing for the firm to charge low prices. This leads to high profits for the firm, which can be invested in research and development to produce high quality products. Additionally, customers spend less time and effort deciding which shampoo to buy when there is only one option to choose.

* However, the lack of competition may make the only firm producing shampoo to become inefficient. They can charge high prices and produce low quality shampoos and continue to make profit as customers do not have any alternatives to switch to. Customer choice is very limited so they may not be able to buy the shampoos they want. A larger output can cause the firm to experience diseconomies of scale and high average costs.
