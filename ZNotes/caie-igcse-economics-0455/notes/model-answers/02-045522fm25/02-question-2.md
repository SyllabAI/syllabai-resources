## Question 2

**2(a)** Economic goods are in **limited** supply, free goods are in **unlimited** supply.

**2(b)** Opportunity cost is the next best alternative forgone. In this case, if tax revenue is spent on an infrastructure project, it cannot be spent on something else, such as healthcare.

**2(c)** Supply is said to be elastic when the quantity supplied changes by a percentage change greater than the percentage change in price. If a product can be **stored** easily, its supply will be elastic. If the **time taken to produce** the product is less, supply will be elastic as output can quickly be increased to take advantage of higher prices. If the **cost of altering the supply** is high, supply will be inelastic. Additionally, the longer the **time period**, the more elastic supply becomes as producers have more flexibility to adjust prices.

**2(d)**

* A government should ban the burning of wood in homes as burning wood may generate external costs (costs incurred by third parties not directly involved in the production/consumption of the wood), such as pollution and can damage the health of the population. By banning the burning of wood, fuels that are more environment-friendly can be introduced instead and wood can be used for other purposes like building houses.

* However, the poor may only be able to afford wood as a fuel source if other fuels are more expensive, so banning it could prevent the poor from having fuel which is a basic necessity, increasing poverty. Other fuels may be more polluting and could create more external costs than wood. Instead of imposing a ban, the government can tax wood as a fuel, and use the tax revenue to subsidise alternative fuels.
