**3(c)** Small firms can connect with customers personally, and therefore be more able to cater to their customers specific requirements, helping them maintain loyal customers. Small firms can also easily adapt and respond to changes in market conditions as they produce a relatively low output. Small firms may be subsidised by the government. This means that they can keep their overall costs low and charge low prices while earning revenue and maintaining profit.

**3(d)**

* An increase in money supply causes interest rates to lower. Consumers may borrow and spend more because a lower amount of interest has to be paid when the borrowed amount is paid back. Bank lending will also increase. Increased consumer spending increases demand for products, encouraging firms to expand and take up more workers, reducing unemployment and increasing economic growth.

* However, demand increases, firms may not be able to increase supply enough to match the increased demand. The price level increases - this inflation is demand-pull inflation. As there is more money to spend in the market, more imports may be purchases and a deficit on the current account balance (import expenditure exceeding export revenue) could increase. Additionally, an increase in bank lending could result in more households and firms being in debt.
