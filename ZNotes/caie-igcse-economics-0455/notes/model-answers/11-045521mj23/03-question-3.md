## Question 3

**3(a)** Death rates may vary from country to country because of factors like quality of healthcare and natural disasters like earthquakes.

**3(b)** An increase in the size of the labour force (people who are actively working or seeking work) can be caused by an increase in **immigration** as many people who come into the country may be of working age. It can also be caused by an increase in **retirement age**. For example if the retirement age is raised from 65 to 75, people younger than 75 years old will continue to work and the size of the labour force will be greater.

**3(c)** Labour productivity is the output produced per number of workers. If output per worker increases, then labour productivity increases. Less workers can be employed as the workers work efficiently and produce a high output. This means that the cost of wages paid to workers can be cut down and the average cost of production decreases. More output is generated, which can be exported to other countries. Because labour productivity can make prices lower, demand for the country’s exports increases. Revenue from exports rises and the surplus on the current account of the country’s balance of payments increases.

**3(d)** Inflation is a general rise in the price level.

* Advances in technology can lead to more efficient capital equipment, which can replace labour so wages do not need to be paid. The cost of production will fall and firms can charge lower prices while earning a profit. The power of trade unions may fall so workers can no longer successfully bargain for wage increases. This lowers the cost of production of firms, which allows them to charge lower prices while still making profit. **Cost-push inflation** (rise in price level due to increase in cost of production) is prevented. Government policy measures like contractionary fiscal policy which involves increasing tax rates and decreasing government spending reduces total demand, because poeple have less disposable income to spend (increased income tax) and government spending is a component of total demand. Reduced demand means that **demand-pull inflation** (rise in price level due to increase in demand for products).

* However, consumers may become be confident in future economic prospects and spend more, expecting to earn back what they have spent in the future. Governments may also increase their spending on, for example, healthcare. The rate of interest, which is set by the central bank, may fall, encouraging people to spend more as it becomes easier to pay interest on loans obtained. As consumer spending and government spending increase, total demand increases. This is because total demand = **consumer spending** + investment + **government spending** + net exports. Producers may not be able to increase total supply to match total demand, resulting in a shortage. Price increases so that less people are willing to and able to buy the products and supply and demand become equal. Additionally, resources like coal and basic necessities like food may run out in the future, increasing their price which is inflation.
