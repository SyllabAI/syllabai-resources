## Question 5

**5(a)** Free goods (goods that do not require resources to produce) include sunlight and wind.

**5(b)** One factor of the demand for factors of production is the demand for the product produced. If the product is in high demand, demand for the factors of production will likely increase. Another factor is price of the factor. If the price of the factor is low then demand for it will likely be high.

**5(c)** If demand for football shirts increases, demand for football shorts will likely increase. This is because the shirts and shorts are part of set and are complement goods. However, demand for basketball shirts is likely to fall when demand for football shirts increases because basketball shirts and football shirts may be substitutes. Consumers are likely to switch between both shirts as trends and preferences change.

**5(d)**

* An increase in government spending on housing might benefit an economy as the price of housing may reduce, and homelessness may decrease as more people can afford to buy a house. As a result, poverty may reduce and living standards may improve. Additionally, making housing more affordable may increase the purchasing power of individuals, so they can buy more goods and services. Total demand will increase, boosting economic growth.

* However, there is an opportunity cost to spending on housing as it may mean that spending on, for example, education may have to be reduced. The quality of housing may be poor so living standards of the people living in these houses may not improve, even if homelessness decreases. There may be external costs, (costs incurred by third parties who are not directly involved in consumption/production) such as pollution, during the construction of housing which decrease the living standards of those living nearby.
