## Question 3

**3(a)** Microeconomics is concerned with individual markets, while macroeconomics is concerned with the entire economy.

**3(b)** Less education may reduce skills of a person, so productivity may be low and the person’s chance of gaining a promotion may decrease. Additionally, less education may cause a person to be uninformed of job vacancies and therefore unable to find jobs with high wages.

**3(c)** A minimum wage is the lowest amount that an employer can pay a worker, known as the price floor. Imposing a minimum wage can increase the supply of labour, causing a surplus of labour and resulting in increased unemployment. However, higher wages may increase total demand, encouraging firms to take up more workers and produce more output. This may reduce cyclical unemployment, which is unemployment due to lack of demand.

**3(d)**

* Inflation is the rise in price level. An economy with a high inflation rate might have a low economic growth rate as a high inflation rate will make the country’s products less internationally competitive, as they are more expensive than products in other countries. This may cause imports from other countries to increase, as they are cheaper, and exports to increase, as they are more expensive. If inflation is caused due to rise in cost of production (cost-push inflation), output and GDP may decrease. Hyperinflation (a very high rate of inflation) may result in collapse on the economic system.

* However, inflation may be caused due to increasing demand (demand-pull inflation) which may encourage firms to produce higher output. Real cost of borrowing may decrease, so consumer spending and investment may increase. The inflation rate of a country may be less than other that of other countries, making its products more internationally competitive.
