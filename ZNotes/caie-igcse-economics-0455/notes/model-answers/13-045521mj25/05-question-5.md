## Question 5

**5(a)** An MNC may share some of its **profits** to its home country, increasing the **income** of the country.

**5(b)** Import tariffs are taxes imposed on imports. Raising import tariffs increases the price of imported raw materials and capital which may be imported by domestic firms. The cost of production of domestic firms increases and so they raise the price of products to continue to make a profit (this rise in price level is **cost-pull inflation**). Free trade is reduced and imports from other countries become expensive, so consumers switch to from importing to buying from domestic firms. Domestic firms have less competition so may charge higher prices as customers have no choice but to keep buying from them and a higher profit will be made.

**5(c)** Human Development Index (HDI) is a measure of living standards, based on **health**, **education**, and **income**. Investment is a component of **total demand**, so as investment increases, total demand will increase. More goods and services will be produced to match increased demand, so GDP rises. As GDP rises, GDP per head may also rise. Investment in healthcare may increase, so that people live longer, healthier lives, which is an indicator of HDI. Investment in education may increase so that more people learn skills and gain qualifications in school. They can produced more goods and work productively, increasing GDP per head, improving HDI.

**5(d)** Having a floating exchange rate means that if there is excess demand for the currency increases, the price of the currency increases and if there is excess supply for the currency, the price of the currency decreases.

* If a country has a current account deficit (import expenditure more than export revenue, so there is excess supply of the currency in the market), the exchange rate will fall in price (for example, the rate increases from $1 = ₹80 to $1 = ₹90). Exports will become cheaper and imports will become more expensive. Demand for exports will increase and more export revenue can be earned, while demand for imports will decrease and import expenditure will decrease. The current balance deficit will then be eliminated. Unlike the fixed exchange rate where foreign currency is bought and sold on the market, floating exchange rate does not require reserves of foreign currency to be kept. By using a floating exchange rate, the government can focus on other aims as they do not need to keep the exchange rate fixed.

* A floating exchange rate makes it difficult for firms to plan as the exchange rate may fluctuate. This makes it harder to predict how much firms will have to pay for imports and earn from exports. Because the exchange rate is not fixed, uncertainty is created and investors are less likely to invest more.
