## Question 4

**4(a)** The macroeconomic aims of **full employment** and **price stability** may conflict. *This is because as the economy moves towards full employment, more people earn income and are able to and willing to buy more products. This increase in demand may be greater than the supply that can be produced, so the price level increases resulting in inflation.*

**4(b)** Population growth rate will increase if there is a high birth rate, which may be due to a lack of education on birth control and family planning. If the quality of healthcare increases, less people will die of disease, the death rate will decrease and the population growth rate will increase.

**4(c)** Corporation tax is a tax imposed on profits made by firms. Reducing the rate of corporation tax means that companies can **retain more of their profit**. **MNCs** may be attracted to countries with low corporation tax for this reason. Firms will have more money to spend on capital goods (**investment**). By paying less taxes, firms can spend more on wages so they may choose to employ more workers to expand production. This creates job opportunities and reduces **unemployment**.

**4(d)**

* Government spending is a component of total demand, so reducing government spending will reduce total demand. Lower total demand may reduce **demand-pull inflation**. Demand-pull inflation occurs when total demand increases and total supply cannot be increased enough to equal to demand, so price levels rise. If governments reduce their expenditure, they will have more funding and can reduce tax rates as they do not need so much tax revenue.

* Government spending includes spending on subsidies, education and training, infrastructure. Less subsidies will increase the cost of production of firms, so firms will increase the prices of their products (cost-push inflation). Lower spending on education can lower the quality of education provided, reducing productivity of workers and increasing the cost of production of firms. Less spending on infrastructure may increase transportation costs as it takes longer to move from one location to another. The increased cost of production will increase cost-push inflation, as firms have to increase prices to maintain a profit. There may be other causes of inflation apart from reduced government spending, like an rise in wages.
