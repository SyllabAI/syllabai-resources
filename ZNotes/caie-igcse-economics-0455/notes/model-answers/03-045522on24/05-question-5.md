## Question 5

**5(a)** Two examples of labour employed in the tourism industry are tour guides and hotel managers.

**5(b)** A good transport system could lower firms’ cost of production as it makes it cheaper to move goods. Food and other perishable items are likely to arrive fresh so wastage is reduced and higher profits can be earned on the sale of food. Mobility of labour will increase as it easier to get to work so productivity may increase. Productivity of firms increases, as mobility of labour increases, attracting MNCs, raising GDP. 

**5(c)** A competitive market has many firms producing the product, so consumers have more choice. Firms will compete to gain a greater market share and make a profit, so they will charge **low prices** to increase demand for their products and prevent consumers from buying from other firms. The high level of competition can increase **efficiency and encourage innovation**, so high quality products may be produced. Firms respond to consumer preferences, giving consumers greater control over the market.

5(d)

*Trade unions are organisations of workers that aim at promoting and protecting the interest of workers.*

* An increase in trade union membership might benefit an economy as trade unions can bargain so that workers may receive **higher wages and improved working conditions**, increasing the productivity of workers. Higher wages may also increase **total demand and GDP**, as workers have more purchasing power. By improving working conditions, **living standards** are also improved as workers are healthy and safe. **Conflicts** between employers and employees may reduce, because trade unions provide a channel of communication. Trade unions also assist with **training**, which could lead to increased output.

* However, higher wages as a result of bargaining may cause cost-push **inflation**. As wages increase, the cost of production of the firms employing the workers increases and they will increase prices to maintain a profit. Trade unions may take **industrial action** like strikes which disrupts the production processes, lowering output. Trade unions may prevent **newer technologies** from replacing labour, which may reduce efficiency.
