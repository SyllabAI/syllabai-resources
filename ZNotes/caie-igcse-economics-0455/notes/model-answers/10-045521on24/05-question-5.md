## Question 5

**5(a)** Labour mobility is the ability of workers to move geographically (geographical mobility) or take up another job (occupational mobility).

**5(b)** An ageing population indicates that **more people are above the age of retirement** and have retired. As more people retire, more people do not earn an income and so cannot save money. An ageing population **will likely not save** money for the future. Instead, retired people **will** **spend the savings** they have accumulated over years to support their living. Therefore, the spending level will rise and saving level will fall.

**5(c)** Inflation is the sustained rise in the price level. Increased tourism would **increase demand for resources** like food, from tourists. Suppliers may not be able to keep up with the increased demand and the price of the products in demand will increase to match the reduced supply and increased demand. This is **demand-pull** inflation (inflation due to increased demand). Additionally, with more tourists coming into the country, more resources (like food) will be consumed by tourists. This decreases the supply of resources. Because they are limited, the prices of resources increases. This increases the cost for firms to manufacture products, and so firms charge higher prices. This is known as cost-pull inflation.

**5(d)** Fiscal policy is a government policy which uses government spending and taxation to attain macroeconomic goals, like economic growth and full employment.

* Increased government spending on education and training helps people in the labour force gain skills and qualifications, making it easier for them to get accepted in job vacancies and earn income. Skilled worker also earn higher wages, which brings them out of poverty. Increased government spending on unemployment benefits will give more unemployed people money to spend on basic necessities, like food, which reduces the level of poverty. Reducing the rate of indirect taxes (taxes imposed on spending like VAT) makes it more affordable to buy goods and services. So, more poor people can buy more basic necessities with their limited income, bringing more people our of absolute poverty, which is the state where people lack the basic necessities like food.

* Contractionary fiscal policy uses increased taxes and reduced government spending, can lead to reduced demand for goods and services. Firms may reduce their output to match the reduced demand and workers may be unemployed to cut down on costs. Unemployed workers may not be able to afford basic necessities without their income, increasing poverty. Even if the government increases its spending (expansionary fiscal policy) on education, education may not be accessible to people in poverty and people in poverty may not be aware of the education provided. People receiving state benefits may decide to not find work and keep receiving benefits. Because they remain unemployed, they do not receive income and continue being dependent on state benefits, leaving them in poverty.
