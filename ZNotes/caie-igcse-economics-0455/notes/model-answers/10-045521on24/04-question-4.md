## Question 4

**4(a)** Methods of trade protection include **import quotas** (a limit on the quantity of goods/services that can be imported into a country) and **embargoes** (a total ban on trade between one country to another).

**4(b)** When the price of coffee substitutes increases, the **demand for coffee substitutes decreases** and **demand for coffee increases**. This is because consumers switch from coffee substitutes to coffee, as it is relatively cheaper. When the price of coffee complements increases, the **demand for the complements decreases** as it is more expensive and less people will be able to afford its complements and coffee together. So **demand for coffee will fall**. For example, if the price of sugar increases, less people will buy sugar to make coffee and demand for coffee will fall.

**4(c)** Governments want to intervene in a market when the **market fails to efficiently allocate resources**. This includes shortages, surpluses, and information failure. Market economies do not provide **public goods**, like defence, because they are non-rival and non-excludable. This means that even people who do not pay for these goods can benefit from them and profit cannot be made, so producers in the private sector have no incentive to produce them. The government will have to intervene to provide public goods. The government also intervenes by creating laws that **prevent monopolies** (single seller of a product) from arising. This is because if a monopoly arises, it may charge high prices and reduce quality of products because the monopoly knows that customers have no other firms to buy from. This means that they will have no choice but to continue to buy products from the monopoly. To ensure that customers have choice and enjoy products at low prices and high quality, governments intervene to reduce monopoly power.

**4(d)** A tariff is a tax imposed on imported goods.

* High tariffs increase the price of imported goods (for example, if a imported good costs \$5 and a tariff of 20% is imposed, the price of the good increases to \$6), which reduces the demand for imports, as less people can afford to buy imported goods. Consumers will switch to buying domestic products as they are relatively cheaper than imported goods. This increases the demand for domestic products and also increases the demand for the domestic workers to produce more products. As domestic firms expand to match the increased demand for domestic products, they will take in more workers to increase their output, which reduces unemployment. Additionally, tariffs are a source of tax revenue for the government. This tax revenue can be spent on education and training of unemployed people. This can increase the skills and qualifications of the unemployed, making it easier for them to be employed.

* However, if high tariffs are imposed, then demand for domestic products will increase. Domestic production may not be able to match this increased demand because resources have been used up and there are not enough to produce more domestic products. This will result in a shortage, where demand for product exceeds its supply, so prices will increase. This rise in price level is demand-pull inflation. Additionally, firms may have to import raw materials to produce output. If high tariffs are imposed then these raw materials will be more expensive and cost of production will increase. Firms may make less profit (revenue $-$ costs) and start producing less output.
