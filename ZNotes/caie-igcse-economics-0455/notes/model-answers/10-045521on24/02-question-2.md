## Question 2

**2(a)** Employment may be measured through **claimant count** (counting the number of people receiving unemployment benefits) and labour force survey.

**2(b)** Living standards refer to the well-being and happiness of a population, which depends on several factors. If people earn more **income**, they can afford to buy more goods and services that they can enjoy. For example, if a worker earns a high income, the worker can afford to buy products that they enjoy, like a ticket to a luxury cruise, which improves their happiness and therefore living standards. Additionally, if high quality **healthcare** is provided, people will likely live longer lives and face less health problems.

**2(c)** An **increase in demand for a product** will increase the demand for the labour that produces the product. For example, if demand for air travel increase, demand for pilots will increase. This increased demand will increase their wages as more people consume their services. Products can be produced using labour or capital. If the **price of the capital** equipment (like machines) producing the product increases, then firms will switch to using labour to cut down on costs and make a greater profit. Additionally, a **decrease in the supply** of labour may increase wages if it is caused by an decrease in the number of workers qualified for the job. For example, if the number of people who studied medicine decrease, the number of qualified doctors will decrease, and doctors will get paid more.

**2(d)** Free trade is the exchange of goods and services between countries, without restrictions like government barriers (including quotas and tariffs)

* With free trade, each country can specialise what they are best at producing. For example, if a country can easily produce cars with a low cost of production, the country can focus on producing and exporting cars and can import products they cannot produce easily like rice. Firms can import raw materials and capital from other countries if it is cheaper. Free trade gives firms a larger market (they can sell to customers in other countries too). There may be increased demand for their products, encouraging them expand their output and they may earn more revenue.

* Infant industries, industries in their early stage of development which lack the efficiency and scale to compete with foreign competitors, may be stopped from growing if free trade is introduced. This is because people in the country will import products from the foreign competition as it is likely to be cheaper than the products produced by the infant industry. Without tariffs (tax on imports), the government may not receive enough tax revenue, which they use to spend on education and healthcare. Firms may encourage in unfair practices to increase their revenue, like dumping. Dumping involves firms exporting their products to countries at a very low price, customers buy these products instead of those produced by domestic firms. Reduced demand for domestic firms can cause domestic firms to lose profit and even shut down.
