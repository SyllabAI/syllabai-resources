## Question 3

**3(a)** A fixed cost is a cost that does not change with output and must be paid even when output is zero, for example: rent.

**3(b)** Structural unemployment occurs when there are changes in demand and supply conditions. In structural employment, workers may not have the skills that are required for job vacancies. Cyclical unemployment occurs when there is a lack of demand for labour and often occurs during a recession.

**3(c)** Supply-side policies include education & training and lowering tax rates. Education can increase the qualifications of an individual and training can increase skills of an individual. This can make it easier for unemployed workers to be hired in jobs that require skilled labour. Lowering the income tax incentivises the unemployed to find work quickly, as disposable income (reward for labour) is greater. Lowering the corporation tax increases the retained profit of firms and may encourage them to invest profits and expand output.

**3(d)**

* Having fewer firms in a market might benefit consumers as firms may be larger and can take advantage of economies of scale, including purchasing economies (buying in bulk). This will lower costs of production which allows firms to charge low prices. Firms may have larger market share, allowing them to make more profit. This profit can be reinvested in research and development, improving the quality of products.

* However, firms that are too large may experience diseconomies of scale, leading to problems in communication and poor industrial relations. Consumers have less choice with fewer firms and the availability of products may be less. Additionally, lack of competitive pressure could result in lower quality of products and high prices. Despite this, consumers will continue buying from the firm as there are no other firms to buy the products from.
