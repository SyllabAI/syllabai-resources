## Benefits and Drawback of Using Budgets

| Advantages | Possible Disadvantages |
|----|----|
| Budgets help managers plan for the future and set realistic targets. For instance, a sales budget guides departments on production levels and spending on promotions. | Fixed budgets without room for changes can become unrealistic if unexpected events occur, leading to demotivation among employees.         |
| Budgets helps the business by ensuring they don’t overspend. They provide a clear plan for how resources and money will be divided among different department/areas. | Budgets are created usually for a short period such as 12 months. Managers might make short-term decisions that harm the long-term success of the business, like cutting talented staff members from its department to stay within budget. |
| Effective coordination between departments is needed to allocate resources and achieve targets. Departments must work together once budgets are set. Otherwise, the business will never meet its target. | Managers might spend extra money at the end of a budget period to avoid having a large surplus, which could make it hard to justify the same budget next year. |
| Targets adjusted by SMART criteria helps motivate people to work better. Budget holders or managers are more driven/incentivised  when they have the responsibility of meeting budget goals. | Managers need thorough training to manage and stick to budgets effectively. |
| Regular assessment of business operation are necessary to monitor performance and ensure budgets are followed. Plans should be adjusted if circumstances change. | Estimating budgets for new or unique projects is challenging and often inaccurate. Therefore without flexibility, it won’t succeed fulfillment of its departmental goals as much as it was targeted |
| At the end of the budget period, variance analysis is used to compare actual results to the budget to assess performance and departmental success. | If managers have spent less than their budget, they might spend extra before budget period ends to avoid having a large surplus, which could make it hard to fight for the same or higher budget for its area in the next year. |




### Essential features of effective budgeting:




* A budget is not a forecast as a forecast predicts what might happen in the future under certain conditions, whereas, Budget is a plan that businesses aim to achieve.
* Budgets can be set for any measurable part of an organisation, including sales, capital spending, labour costs, and profit. It also means most cost and profit centre have budget sets.
* It’s important for departments to coordinate when setting budgets to avoid conflicting plans.
* Managers responsible for meeting budget targets should be involved in setting them. Those who are responsible for fulfilling budget should also be involved. This involvement helps create targets that are highly realistic which can motivate the team. This approach is also known as delegated budget.
* *B*udgets help evaluate the performance of managers in charge of cost or profit centres, identifying those who meet or fail to meet targets.
