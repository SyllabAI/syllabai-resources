## Primary Research

* Also known as field research
* It is when businesses collect first-hand data for their own needs
* Benefits –
  * Up-to-date information
  * Relevant information
  * Confidential
* Drawbacks –
  * Expensive
  * Time-consuming
  * Doubts over accuracy and validity (due to sample size)


### **Primary Data Collection Methods**

| Method | Details | Benefits | Limitations |
|----|----|----|----|
| Questionnaire (Postal, in-store, online, and mobile phone, etc.) | Questionnaires can include detailed, open questions which give qualitative and quantitative data. | Cost-effective for reaching a large audience.
 | Low response rates may skew results. | Easy to analyse and compare feedback. | Limited depth of information due to predefined answers |
| Interview (Face to face or mobile phone.) | Interviews allow personal contact with respondents. Follow-up questions can be asked. | Provides in-depth, qualitative data. | time-consuming and expensive.  |
|    |    | Allows for follow-up questions, which assists in a detailed understanding of feedback. | Potential interviewer bias can influence the responses of interviewees.  |
| Observation (seeing which décor or advertisement of a shop attracts customers.)  | If people know they are being observed, they could behave differently. | It captures candid behaviour in real-time.  | Can be intrusive to potential customers, making them uncomfortable , altering their behaviour.  |
|    |    | Useful for understanding consumer behaviour according to context and environment. | Difficult to interpret without additional data. |
| Test Marketing (In certain geographical location.) | The results might indicate whether the national launch of a new product will achieve sales targets. | Provides real-world feedback before a full launch.
 | Costly and time-consuming. |
|    |    | Decreases the risk by identifying issues early and rectifying them. | Competitors might observe and react accordingly. |
| Focus Group (Conversation with potential or existing consumers, with goal of gaining qualitative data) | It encourages debate between consumers about a product or advertisement. Discussion is observed and recorded. | Assists gathering diverse opinions and ideas quickly.  | Group dynamics can lead to biased results. | It encourages discussion and deeper insights. | Expensive and might be challenging to manage effectively. |
