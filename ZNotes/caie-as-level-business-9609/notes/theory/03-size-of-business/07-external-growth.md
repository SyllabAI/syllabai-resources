## External growth

* Merger: shareholders and managers of 2 firms come together with both owning shares
* Takeover: company buys more than 50% of another companies shares

### Types of integration:

* Horizontal: same industry at same stage of production
* Vertical forward: same industry as a customer of existing business
* Vertical backward: same industry as a supplier of existing business
* Conglomerate: different industry
* Friendly merger: takeover with consent of the target business
* Hostile takeover: takeover without consent of the target business

<tr>
<td>Horizontal Integration - merging with companies in the same industry and production stage</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Removes one competitor</li>
<li>It can be used to reduce the costs by increasing production efficiency</li>
<li>Greater control over suppliers by being a larger business</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Reducing costs may lead to negative publicity</li>
<li>Could trigger monopoly investigations if the market share becomes too large</li>
<li>Customers might oppose the brand because of the merger resulting in potentially lower number of choices and competition</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Consumers have fewer choices</li>
<li>It might result in redundancies due to cost-cutting</li>
<li>Workers might be demotivated due to fall in job security</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Vertical Integration (Forward) - merging with a business in the same industry that is a customer</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Control over product promotion and pricing</li>
<li>Secure a market for your products, eliminating the need for competitors' products</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Customers might view this negatively if it reduces competition</li>
<li>Lack of experience in this sector can lead to problems</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Workers will potentially have better job security due to secure outlets</li>
<li>Employees might be more motivated due to higher job security resulting in an increase in efficiency</li>
<li>More varied job opportunities, but possible customer dissatisfaction due to less choice</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Vertical Integration (Backward) - merging with a supplier in the same industry</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Better control over quality, pricing, and delivery</li>
<li>They can collaborate on research and development</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>The firm might lack experience managing a supplier</li>
<li>A successful producer might not be a good manager of supply chains</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Possible career growth for workers</li>
<li>Consumers may benefit from better quality products</li>
<li>Supplies may be more reliable but could limit competition</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Conglomerate Integration - merging with a business in a different industry</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>It helps diversifies the business, reducing reliance on one industry and mitigating risk</li>
<li>It spread risk across different markets, making the business more secure</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Business can lack sufficient experience in the new industry</li>
<li>It can lead to loss of focus on the core business</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Career opportunities may increase due to the variety of industries</li>
<li>Consumers may have access to a wider range of products, but the company's focus might become diluted</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
