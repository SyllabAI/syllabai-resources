## Marginal Costing:

The additional cost of producing one more unit of output.




### Benefits and Limitations of Marginal Costing:

| Benefits | Limitations |
|----|----|
| It is appropriate for short-term decision-making, such as evaluating the impact of changes in production volume or pricing strategies. | It does not allocate fixed costs, which can lead to incomplete cost assessments.  |
| Marginal costing highlights the impact of variable costs on profitability, making it easier to identify areas for cost reduction. | Marginal costing is suited for short-term decisions and may not provide a complete picture for long-term strategic planning. |
| It allows for quick adjustments in pricing and production strategies based on variable cost information due to flexibility. | It can be misleading if fixed costs are significant and need to be considered for accurate financial planning and performance evaluation. |
