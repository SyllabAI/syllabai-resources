## Pricing methods

### Cost based pricing

* **Mark-up pricing**
  * When a percentage of fixed mark-up is added to the cost of the product.
  * The mark-up size depends on the strength of demand, number of substitutes, stage of PLC (Product life cycle), etc.
* **Cost-plus pricing**
  * It involves setting a price by calculating the unit cost and adding a fixed profit margin.
  * This ensures all costs are met
  * Easy to calculate
  * Suitable for firms with high market shares
  * But, doesn’t take into account external factors like economic conditions
  * Inflexible method
* **Contribution-cost (or marginal-cost) pricing**
  * Prices are set based on the variable costs and a contribution amount for fixed costs and profits is added.
  * Contribution per unit = selling price – variable cost per unit
  * Break even point = fixed costs/ contribution
  * Ensures variable costs are covered
  * Flexible method
  * Fixed costs may not be covered
  * If prices are varied too much, consumers maybe discouraged and business will face menu costs
* **Competitive pricing**
  * Prices are based on the price set by competitors, analysing them and retailing at similar price point.
  * Scenarios where it is suitable:
    * Following the market leader
    * Avoid a price war
    * Destroyer pricing – force others out of the market
    * Based on study of conditions




### Advantages and Disadvantages of Cost-Based Pricing:

| Methods | Benefits | Drawbacks |
|----|----|----|
| Cost-plus Pricing | Easy and to calculate by adding a fixed percentage or amount to the cost, causing the process to to take less time to complete. | It doesn't consider competitor prices or customer willingness to pay.   |
|    | It ensures that all costs are covered and a profit margin is added. | If costs are high, prices might be set too high, potentially reducing sales. |
|    | It helps in understanding how much each unit contributes to covering fixed costs and profit. | It ignores fixed costs in pricing, which might lead to incomplete financial planning. |
| Contribution-Cost Pricing | Useful for making decisions like accepting special orders. | It might be misleading if not combined with full cost information for comprehensive decision-making. |
| Competitive Pricing | Prices are set based on competitors' prices, helping to stay competitive.   | Competing solely on price can lower profit margins if prices are set too low. |
|    | It draws customers' attention, specifically those who are looking for better deals compared to competitors. | It doesn't account for the unique value or quality of the product, which can be overlooked in price comparisons. |
| Mark-Up Pricing | Easy to apply by adding a fixed percentage to the cost of the product.   | It doesn't consider customer demand or competitor prices, which can lead to setting the price too high or too low. |
|    | It ensures a consistent profit margin on each product sold. | This can result in pricing that doesn’t reflect the actual market value, making this method at times inefficient. |
