## Market-oriented pricing

* **Psychological pricing**
  * Also known as customer-value/perceived-value pricing
  * Prices are set based on the value customers place on the product
  * Used for products with inelastic demand
* **Price discrimination**
  * It involves charging different prices to different consumer groups for the same product
  * For example, bus or train companies offer lower prices to the elders than they do so to other adults.
  * Another example might be mass transit system such as sky-train offering lower prices during off-rush hours than rush hours.
* **Dynamic pricing**
  * Dynamic pricing is flexible and based on real-time data. Hence, this pricing method involves the use of flexible prices to reflect changing market conditions. 
  * Changing prices, frequently, to respond to changes in demand
  * It is based on demand level and ability of consumers to pay
  * For example, Airlines use dynamic pricing. It depends on the number of seats left, the lower the seats, the higher the prices are usually.




### Advantages and Disadvantages of Market-Oriented Pricing:

| Methods | Benefits | Drawbacks |
|----|----|----|
| Dynamic Pricing | It adjusts prices based on demand and competition, potentially earning more money. | Frequent price changes can annoy customers who feel they are being charged unfairly, eroding brand perception. |
|    | Quickly adapt to market changes to optimize pricing, ensuring maximum reach to highest possible revenue and profitability. | It requires advanced systems and data analysis, which can be expensive and complicated. |
| Psychological Pricing | The business use tactics like pricing items at $9.99 instead of $10 to make them seem cheaper and encourage purchase, boosting sales.   | Some customers might feel tricked by these pricing tricks, which can damage trust and erode customer perception of the brand. |
|    | It makes products look like a better deal to customers as it appears cheaper in comparison to similar products. | It may not always boost sales significantly if the product doesn’t actually offer good value. |
| Price Discrimination | It can charge different prices to different groups, increasing overall revenue as well as profit margin from each segment. | This might upset customers who see others paying less for the same product. |
|    | It broadens the market by creating products that are affordable to different income levels, reaching more customers. | Firms Might face legal issues if the pricing is seen as unfair. |
