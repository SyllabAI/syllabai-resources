## Capacity Shortage

When demand for a products of the business exceeds its production capacity.

To raise production capacity for long-term operation, a business considers:

* Should the business increase production level by acquiring additional production resources?
* Should they maintain existing capacity but outsource or subcontract work to other businesses?
* Should they operate at full capacity without expansion due to potential risk of declining demand.

Businesses use the following in order to reduce long-term capacity shortage and they are:

1. Use subcontractors or outsource suppliers:


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Requires no major capital investment.|Lesser control over output quality.|
|Comparatively quick to arrange.|It might raise administration and transport costs.|
|It provides greater flexibility than expanding facilities. Contracts can be ended if demand decreases.|Potential uncertainty regarding delivery times and reliability.|
||Unit costs might be higher due to the supplier fulfilling its profit margin.|

2. Invest in capital for expansion of production facilities:


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Increases long-term capacity.|High capital costs and difficulty can occur when raising funds.|
|Might give access economies of scale.|The higher capacity can become a problem if demand for products of the business decreases over an extended period.|
|New facilities enables the business to use of the latest equipment and methods.|Building new facilities and equipping it with appropriate machineries takes time and customers might go to another business.|
|Business has control over quality and delivery times.||
