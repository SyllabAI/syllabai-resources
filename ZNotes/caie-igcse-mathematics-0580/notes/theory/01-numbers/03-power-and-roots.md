## Power and Roots

### Squares and Square Roots

* **Square** of a number means multiplying it by itself.

  * $n^2 = n \times n$

* **Square Root** is the number that, when squared, gives the original number

  * $3^2 = 9$, so the square root of 9 is 3

  * $(-3)^2 = 9$, so both +3 and -3 are square roots of 9.

  * $\sqrt{n^2} = n$

* **Examples:**

  * $4^2 = 16$

  * $\sqrt{25} = 5$

  * $(-7^2) = 49$

  * $\sqrt{169} = 13$

---

### Cube and Cube Roots

* **Cube of a number** means multiplying it by itself three times:

  * $n^3 = n \times n \times n$

* **Cube Root** is the number that, when cubed, gives the original number

  * $\sqrt[3]{n^3} = n$

* **Examples:**

  * $2^3 = 8$

  * $\sqrt[3]{27} = 3$

  * $(-3)^3 = -27$

  * $\sqrt[3]{-64} = -4$

---

### Other Powers and Roots

* Numbers can have **fractional**, **negative,** or **higher powers**
* A **power** tells you how many times to multiply a number by itself.
  * **Example:** $2^4 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 16$
* A **root** is the inverse of a power; it ‘undoes’ the power
  * **Example:** $\sqrt[4]{16} = 2$, because $2^4 = 16$
* The nth root of a number $x$ is the value which, when raised to the power of $n$, gives
  $x$.
  * $\sqrt[n]{x} = y$, $y^n = x$
* **Fractional Indices** represent roots and powers together
  * $x^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{x}$

  * $x^{\frac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{x}$

  * $x^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{x^m}$
