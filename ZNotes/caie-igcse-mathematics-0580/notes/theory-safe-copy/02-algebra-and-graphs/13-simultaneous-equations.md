## Simultaneous Equations

### Elimination

* **Worked Example**

$\text{Q. Solve } \begin{cases} 3x + 2y = 12 \text{   (Equation 1)} \\ 2x - 2y = 4 \text{   (Equation 2)} \end{cases}$

**Step 1:** Add both equations (since $+2y$ and $-2y$ will cancel)

$(3x+2y) + (2x-2y) = 12+4$

$5x=16$ $\rightarrow$ $x = \frac{16}{5}$

**Step 2:** Substitute $x = \frac{16}{5}$ into Equation 2

$2 \left( \frac{16}{5} \right) - 2y = 4$

$\frac{32}{5} - 2y = 4$

$-2y = 4 - \frac{32}{5} = \frac{-12}{5}$

$y = \frac{6}{5}$

**Final Answer:** $\boxed{ (x,y) = \left( \frac{16}{5} , \frac{6}{5} \right) }$

### Substitution

**Example 1 (Linear - Linear)**

$\text{Q. Solve } \begin{cases} x + y = 10 \text{   (Equation 1)} \\ 2x - y = 4 \text{    (Equation 2)} \end{cases}$

**Step 1:** From Equation 1,

$x = 10 - y \text{     (Equation 3)}$

**Step 2:** Substitute into Equation 2

$2(10-y) -y =4$

$20 - 2y - y = 4$

$20 - 3y = 4$

$3y = 16$

$y = \frac{16}{3}$

**Step 3:** Substitute $y = \frac{16}{3}$ into Equation 3

$x = 10 - \frac{16}{3}$

$x = \frac{14}{3}$

**Final Answer:** $\boxed{ (x,y) = \left( \frac{14}{3} , \frac{16}{3} \right) }$

**Example 2 (Linear - Quadratic)**

$\text{Q. Solve } \begin{cases} y = x + 1 \text{  (Equation 1)} \\ y = x^{2} + 3x \text{  (Equation 2)} \end{cases}$

**Step 1:** Equate Equation 1 and Equation 2

$x + 1 = x^{2} + 3x$

**Step 2:** Rearrange

$ x^{2} +3x - x -1 = 0$

$x^{2} + 2x -1 =0$

**Step 3:** Solve the quadratic
$x = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{2^{2} -4(1)(-1)} }{2(1)}$

$x = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 4} }{2}$

$x = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{8} }{2}$

$x = -1 \pm \sqrt{2}$

**Step 4:** Find the corresponding $y$

* If $x = -1 + \sqrt{2}$:

  $y = x + 1 = (-1 + \sqrt{2} ) +1 = \sqrt{2}$

* If $x = -1 - \sqrt{2}$:

  $y = x + 1 = (-1 - \sqrt{2} ) +1 = - \sqrt{2}$

**Final Answer:** $\boxed{ (x,y) = \left( -1 + \sqrt{2} , \sqrt{2} \right) \text{ or } \left( -1 - \sqrt{2} , - \sqrt{2} \right) }$

### Graphical Method

* For linear + linear:

  * Plot both equations on the same axis

  * The solution is the **point of intersection**

  * If no solution $\rightarrow$ no solution

  * If lines overlap $\rightarrow$ infinitely many solutions

* For linear + quadratic:

  * Intersection = line crosses the curve

  * Number of Intersections tells the number of solutions
