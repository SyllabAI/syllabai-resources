## Co-ordinate Geometry

**<span class="underline">Question 39:</span>**

Equations of a Line (gradients, mid-points, perpendicular & parallel
lines) A line joins the points *A* (–2, –5) and *B* (4, 13).

a) Calculate the length *AB* (3 marks)

b) Find the equation of the line through *A* and *B*. Give your answer in the form *y = mx + c*. (3 marks)

c)  Another line is parallel to *AB* and passes through the point (0,
    –5). Write down the equation of this line. (2 marks)

d)  Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of *AB*. (5 marks)

**<center>Solution:</center>**

**Part (a):**

*Length of a line is given by*

$$\sqrt{\left( y_2 - y_1 \right)^{2} + \left( x_2 - x_1 \right)^{2}}$$

*Here, that is*

$$\sqrt{\left( 13 + 5 \right)^{2} + \left( 4 + 2 \right)^{2}}$$

$$= \sqrt{324 + 36}$$

$$= \mathbf{18.97}$$

**Part (b):**

*The gradient of the line can be found as*

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

$$= \frac{13 + 5}{4 + 2}$$

$$= 3$$

*Using the straight-line equation*

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

*With our gradient and the point B we get*

$$y - 13 = 3\left( x - 4 \right)$$

$$\rightarrow y = 3x - 12 + 13$$

$$\rightarrow \mathbf{y = 3}\mathbf{x + 1}$$

**Part (c):**

*Parallel means it has the same gradient. This new line, using the same
straight-line equation as before, is*

$$y + 5 = 3\left( x - 0 \right)$$

$$\rightarrow \mathbf{y = 3}\mathbf{x - 5}$$

**Part (d):**

*Perpendicular bisector means that it has a perpendicular gradient to
line AB and it cuts through the midpoint. The perpendicular gradient is*

$$- 1 \div 3$$

$$= - \frac{1}{3}$$

*The midpoint is*

$$M = \left( \frac{4 - 2}{2},\ \frac{13 - 5}{2} \right)$$

$$= (1,\ 4)$$

*The perpendicular bisector then has the equation*

$$y - 4 = - \frac{1}{3}\left( x - 1 \right)$$

$$\rightarrow 3y - 12 = - x + 1$$

$$\rightarrow \mathbf{x + 3}\mathbf{y - 13 = 0}$$
