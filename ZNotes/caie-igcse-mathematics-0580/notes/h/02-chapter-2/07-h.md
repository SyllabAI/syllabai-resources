* **Paper chromatography**

  * Separates a mixture of **solutes** in a **solvent**

    * *Ex. Ink*

  * ++Paper++ is quite **polar**, and the ++solvent++ must also be **polar** (*ex. water, ethanol*)

  * **Mobile phases** is a substance that the molecules can move in

  * **Stationary** **phases** is a substance that molecules cannot move in

  * Each solute has a **retention factor** ($R_f$)

    $$ R_f=\frac{ \text{distance travelled by solute} }{ \text{distance travelled by eluent} }$$

    * Retention factor ($R_f$) helps identify substances by comparing how far they travel relative to the solvent front

  * Two-way chromatography

    * If two solutes reached similar distances, the paper can be turned 90°, using a different solvent to separate them

  * Thin-layer chromatography

    * Similar to paper chromatography, but using a thin layer of a solid (ex. alumina, silica)

    * Each of the components are separated pure

      * The component is scraped off and dissolved in a suitable solvent, then evaporated to purify it
