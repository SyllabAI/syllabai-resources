# **The Earth**

## **Our Earth**

### **The Age of Our Earth**

* Earth is estimated to be 4.54 billion years old, plus or minus about 50 million years.

* Scientists have scoured the Earth searching for the oldest rocks to radiometrically date. In northwestern Canada, they discovered rocks about 4.03 billion years old. Then, in Australia, they discovered minerals about 4.3 billion years old.

* Researchers know that rocks are continuously recycling, due to the rock cycle, so they continued to search for data elsewhere. Since it is thought the bodies in the solar system may have formed at similar times, scientists analyzed moon rocks collected during the moon landing and even meteorites that have crash-landed on Earth. Both of these materials dated to between 4.4 and 4.5 billion years.

### **What Fossils Tell Us**

* Fossils are the preserved remains or traces of animals, plants, and other organisms from the past. Fossils range in age from 10,000 to 3.48 billion years old.

* The observation that certain fossils were associated with certain rock strata led 19th-century geologists to recognize a geological timescale. Like extant organisms, fossils vary in size from microscopic, like single-celled bacteria, to gigantic, like dinosaurs and trees.

* Fossils may also consist of the marks left behind by the organism while it was alive, such as footprints or feces. These types of fossils are called **trace fossils,** or **ichnofossils,** as opposed to body fossils.

  * biomarkers: Markers left by the past life that cannot be seen but can be detected in the form of biochemical signals

### **From the Big Bang to The Sun**

* Scientists believe that the moment the universe came into existence, it was an extremely hot, tiny white spot with an enormous mass.

* It expanded rapidly in an explosion that has been named the Big Bang about 14 billion years ago.

* As the universe expanded, it cooled down, and the atoms of the first element (hydrogen and helium) formed.

* The first stars and the gases and dust from these first giants and supernovas formed a second set of stars called second-generation stars.

  * These behaved just like the original stars and produced more of the same elements.

  * The gas and dust from them also spread out through space.

  * The next set of the stars (third-generation) is the set to which our sun belongs.

### **The Mantle**

* The mantle is made of rocky material and is 2900 km thick.

* It is composed mainly of the elements iron, silicon, oxygen, and magnesium.

  * The main compounds in the mantle are called **silicates.**

* The mantle is very hot; this is above the boiling point of the rock, but the pressure of the materials above it keeps the rock solid.

* The upper mantle, near the crust, is cooler and is under less pressure. This allows the rocky material to behave like a very thick liquid.

### **The Crust**

* The earth's crust is made from much cooler rocks than the mantle.

* Although the rocks at the surface can feel cool or cold, miners and cavers can feel an increase in temperature as they go down into the Earth’s crust.

## **Types Of Rocks**

There are 3 types of rocks:

### **Igneous Rocks**

* Igneous rocks are also called **fire rocks**

* They formed from magma

* There are two kinds:

  * Extrusive igneous rocks (research the earth’s surface)

    * Examples: Basalt

    * Used to form protective shielding in nuclear power stations to prevent the escape of harmful material.

  * Intrusive igneous rocks (forms within the crust)

    * Examples: Granite

    * Used for decorative stonework

* Both rocks are made from crystals and minerals that stick together. However,

  * Basalt is formed from small crystals because it is exposed to the air while the hot rocks cool down.

  * Granite is formed from large crystals because it is remaining in the crust. It stays warmer for longer and cools down more slowly.

* The way that hot rocks cool down:

  * Rapid cooling

    * Some rocks cool down so rapidly when they emerge into the air that crystals do not have time to form

    * Example: Obsidian, pumice

  * Volcanoes

    * The first rocks to be identified as igneous rocks were those that formed from volcanic eruptions. There are two kinds of volcanoes:

    * **Andesitic volcanoes**: Builds up the pressure and then erupts in a huge explosion, sending the rocks and dust high into the atmosphere.

    * **Basaltic volcanoes**: Eruptions from basaltic volcanoes are usually much less violent than andesitic volcanoes. Mostly occur under the sea.

### **Sedimentary Rocks**

There are 3 kinds of sedimentary rocks:

* **Rocks from rocky fragments**

  * Rocky fragments are produced by weathering and form small particles like sand grains.

  * They have carried down rivers and settle out when the water current slows down.

    * The layer particle build-up. The weight of the layers increases, the particles being squashed together,

  * Example: Sandstone, conglomerates (made with pebbles), shale (mixture of clay, mudstones, and siltstones)

* **Rocks from parts of living things**

  * The shells of ancient living things have formed sediments that have turned to rock.

  * Example: Limestone (from the shells of sea creatures), chalk (from tiny shells of protoctists)

* **Rocks from dried-up seas**

  * Seawater contains many chemicals. They have dissolved out of the minerals in the rocks and been washed down rivers into the seas and oceans.

  * In the process of evaporating, leaving the minerals behind to make the remaining seawater more concentrated. Eventually, there is too little water left for all the chemicals to remain in the solution, and some of them joined together and form crystals. Rocks that form in this way are called **evaporites.**

  * Examples: Rock salt and gypsum

### **Metamorphic Rocks**

* Metamorphic rocks are formed from igneous and sedimentary rocks that have been heated or squashed in the Earth’s crust.

* The change in temperature caused the rocks to change in their form, or metamorphose.

* Examples:

  * When limestone is heated and squashed, it changes into marble.

  * Shale in the sedimentary rock is made from very tiny particles similar to those in clay and mud. When it changes or metamorphoses into slate, the tiny particles or grains line up and make sheets of rocks that can be split again.

## **Minerals and Ores**

### **Minerals**

* A mineral is a substance that has formed from one or more elements in the Earth.

* Gold, silver, and copper are examples of the very few elements that are found on their own.

* Most other minerals are compounds, made from the atoms of two or more elements that have joined together.

* The atoms of the elements in a mineral usually join together to form a crystal structure.

  * Examples: The atoms of silicon and oxygen form a crystal substance called quartz

* Each mineral can be recognized by observing its crystal shape, color, luster, hardness, and the color of the streak.

* Some rare minerals have a pleasing color, shiny surface, or sparkle when light passes through them.

  * Examples: Opal, diamond, and beryl

### **Ores**

* During the formation of some rocks, particularly sedimentary rocks, metal elements gather in large quantities.

* They are usually combined with the atoms of other elements.

* Rocks that possess large quantities of metal compounds are called **ores.**

* Bauxite is an ore rich in aluminum and haematite is an ore rich in iron.

* Ores are mined and then processed, usually using heat, to release the metal from its compounds in the rock.

## **Soil**

* The soil is usually covered by plants that are growing in it.

* Soil allows plants to set down their roots and hold their position in a habitat.

* It also stores water and minerals for the plants to use as they grow.

### **How Does Soil Form?**

* The main part of the soil is made from small particles or fragments of rock. They are made when larger rocks break up.

  * This process in which rock breaks up to form fragments is called **weathering**.

There are two kinds of weathering: Physical weathering and chemical weathering:

### **Physical weathering of rocks above ground**

Physical weathering is caused in four ways: Change in temperature, the effect of ice, abrasion, and the effect of plants.

* These processes break rocks down into smaller pieces without changing their chemical composition.

### **Change in temperature**

* When the rocks heat up, the minerals in them expand.

* The minerals expand in different amounts, and they push each other.

* When the rocks cool down, the minerals contract and spaces develop between them. After being heated and cooled many times, the minerals become loose and fall away.

### **The effect of ice**

* When it rains on a porous rock like sandstone, the water can stay in the rock for some time.

* If the weather becomes colder, the water in the rock may freeze. When the water freezes and forms ice, it expands.

* The ice pushes on the sides of the pores and makes the rock crumble. Water that gathers in the cracks of any rocks also expands when it freezes. This makes it push on the sides of the crack and can cause pieces of rocks to snap off.

### **Abrasion**

* This occurs when pieces of rocks rub together when pebbles are carried in a fast-flowing river.

* For example: When they blow in the wind in a sandstorm, they rub on rock surfaces and wear them down.

### **The effect of plants**

* Particles of rock blown by the wind settle in cracks in rocks and form a soil in which plant roots can grow.

* If the seed of a tree or bushlands in a crack and germinates, the root can grow in the soil.

* Trees and bushes have strong, woody roots that grow larger every year, so if they grow in a crack they push on their sides and break up the rock.

### **Chemical weathering of rocks above ground**

* Chemical weathering is caused in two ways:

  1. **Rainwater**

  * Rainwater is naturally slightly acid due to carbon dioxide from the air dissolving in it. The acid in rainwater falling on rocks such as granite can break it up into smaller particles.

    
    2\. **Hot and wet weather conditions**

  * Hot and wet weather conditions speed up chemical reactions such as those that break down rocks.

  * Wet weather conditions such as rainforest regions, rocks break down more quickly than in places with a cold, dry climate, such as the interior of the Antarctic continent.

## **The Properties Of Soil**

There are four properties of soil that are easy to investigate. They are texture, drainage, the amount of air, and the soil pH

* These properties help determine how well plants can grow in different types of soil.

  **Soil texture**

* The soil texture is a description of how the soil feels when you rub it between your finger and thumb.

* Example: Sandy soil feels gritty, silty soil feels silky, and clay soils feel sticky and cannot be squeezed as well as the other soils.

### **Drainage and water boiling**

* The same amount of water is poured onto each soil sample and the water is allowed to drain for the same amount of time. This gives an indication of each soil’s water holding capacity.

### **Air in the soil**

* The spaces between the soil particles contain air.

* This allows the plant roots and the soil organism to respire. When water drains through the soil it flows through the air in the space.

* Soil is added to a measuring cylinder until 50 cm3 has been added. Water is poured on top of the soil until the level of the water reaches the 100 cm3 mark on the scale.

* The soil is then stirred to help the water enter the air spaces. When there is no more bubble, the water lever should have fallen and its new position is noted on the scale. The volume of water now in the soil is found by subtracting the second water level and the first.

### **Soil pH**

* The pH of the soil is a measure of how acidic or alkaline the soil is. It can be found by using universal indicators.

  * A universal indicator is then added and its color is observed to find the pH of the soil.

## **Type of Soils**

### **Soil Formation and Colour**

* In some regions in Africa, the rocks are rich in iron minerals and hence, the soil they form is red due to the iron.

* In tropical regions that have wet and dry seasons, such as in India, weathering of the rocks produces soils rich in clay.

  * They have cracks in their surface in dry weather but after rain, the soil swells out to fill them.

### **Soil pH**

* Soils have a low pH (acidic) if they form on granite, and have a high pH (alkaline) if they form on limestone.

* In places with heavy rainfall, the water may wash alkaline substances out of the soil and make it more acidic.

### **Soil pH and Plant Growth**

* Most plants are adapted for growing in neutral soil or slightly acidic soil of pH 6.

  * But some thrive in soils that are more acidic.

    * Example: Rhododendrons, blueberries, pines, and ferns.

* There are some plants that grow well in alkaline soil.

  * Example: Oregano, asparagus, beech, lilac, and sagebrush.

### **Types of Soil Based on Composition**

* A soil with a large number of sands is simply called sandy soil.

* One with a large amount of silt is silty soil.

* A soil containing a large amount of clay is clay soil.

### **Loam Soil**

* However, if the soil is made from 40% sand, 40% silt, and 20% clay, it is called a loam.

* A loam also contains large amounts of humus and this binds the fragments together to make soil crumbs.
