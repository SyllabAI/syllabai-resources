  Normal Reaction Force

  |
  
  $F_N$ or $R$

  |
  
  **Contact Force:** Electromagnetic repulsion between electron clouds of two touching surfaces. Acts strictly **perpendicular ($90^\circ$)** to the contact plane.

  |
  
  Prevents objects from passing through each other. $F_N = mg \cos\theta$ on an incline.

  ||
  ||
  
  Tension Force

  |
  
  $T$

  |
  
  **Contact Force:** Electromagnetic pulling force transmitted through a stretched string, rope, cable, or spring. Created by inter-atomic bonds resisting separation.

  |
  
  Pulls along the length of the string away from the object. For ideal massless strings, tension is uniform throughout.

  ||
  ||
  
  Frictional Force

  |
  
  $F_f$ or $f$

  |
  
  **Contact Force:** Electrostatic interactions and surface roughness opposing relative sliding motion parallel to contact surfaces.

  |
  
  Static: $F_{f,\text{max}} = \mu_s F_N$

  Dynamic: $F_f = \mu_d F_N$

  ||
  ||
  
  Electric Force

  |
  
  $F_e$

  |
  
  **Non-Contact (Field) Force:** Interaction between objects possessing net electric charge. Opposite charges attract; like charges repel.

  |
  
  Coulomb's Law: $F_e = \frac{k \vert{}q_1 q_2\vert{}}{r^2}$ or $F_e = qE$

  ||
  ||
  
  Magnetic Force

  |
  
  $F_m$

  |
  
  **Non-Contact (Field) Force:** Interaction experienced by moving charged particles or magnetic dipoles within a magnetic field.

  |
  
  Lorentz component: $F_m = q v B \sin\theta$ (charges) or $F_m = I L B \sin\theta$ (current-carrying wires).

  ||
  ||
  
  Elastic Restoring Force

  |
  
  $F_h$

  |
  
  **Contact Force:** Intermolecular forces resisting deformation when a material is stretched or compressed.

  |
  
  Hooke's Law: $F = -kx$

  ||
  ||
  
  Viscous Drag Force

  |
  
  $F_d$

  |
  
  **Contact Force:** Fluid friction resisting movement through a liquid or gas due to momentum transfer and fluid viscosity.

  |
  
  Stokes' Law (spheres): $F_d = 6 \pi \eta r v$

  ||
  ||
  
  Buoyant Force (Upthrust)

  |
  
  $F_b$

  |
  
  **Contact Force:** Upward force exerted by a fluid due to the hydrostatic pressure gradient (deeper fluid exerts greater upward pressure).

  |
  
  Archimedes' Principle: $F_b = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{submerged}} g$
