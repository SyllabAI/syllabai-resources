In the 1840s, doctors' training was a **4-year course** with **lectures** and **practical lessons.** Surgical procedures ranged from basic to life-threatening, like **cutting open boils, amputations,** and **tumor removal.**

During this period, pain relief options were limited to **alcohol, a form of opium** or **being knocked unconscious.** Patients were often awake during operations and held down by assistants.

The most successful surgeon was not necessarily the most skilled, but the quickest.

During surgical procedures, surgeons faced **blood loss issues** and used **tourniquets** to reduce blood flow by wrapping a cloth around a limb. Infections caused fatalities due to operations in patients' homes with unsterilized equipment.

In *1846,* Robert Liston used **Ether** for a leg amputation, impressing onlookers with the patient's calm demeanor. However, Ether's drawbacks included **lung irritation** and **prolonged periods of unconsciousness.**

James Simpson discovered **Chloroform** as a more effective anaesthetic than Ether, popularized after **Queen Victoria used it during childbirth.** While Chloroform had benefits, **overdosing was common** until **John Snow's** invention of the **Chloroform inhaler** in *1848,* which controlled dosage and reduced fatalities.

**Chloroform** enabled surgeons to conduct **longer, complex procedures** but led to **increased deaths** from **sepsis** and **gangrene** due to limited understanding of blood loss and infections. This became known as the **‘Black Period.’**
