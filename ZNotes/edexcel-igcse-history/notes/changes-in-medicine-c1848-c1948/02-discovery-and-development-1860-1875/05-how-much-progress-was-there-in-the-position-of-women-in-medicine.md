Elizabeth Garrett, from a wealthy family, faced opposition to becoming a doctor. Initially a nurse, she overcame rejections from medical schools, completed private training, and opened St. Mary's Dispensary in1866. In 1873, she became the first woman member

of the British Medical Association.

Following Garrett's lead, Sophia Jex Blake and Edith Pechey obtained medical degrees. In 1876, an Act of Parliament mandated universities and medical associations to admit female students.
