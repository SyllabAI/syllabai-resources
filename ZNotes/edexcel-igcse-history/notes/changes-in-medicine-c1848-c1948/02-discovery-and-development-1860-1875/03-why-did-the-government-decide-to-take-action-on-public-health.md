Between 1848-1860, Chadwick and Snow established the connection between hygiene and health.

John Snow's work on Cholera and The Great Stink led to the funding and construction of London's sewer system by Chief Engineer Joseph Bazalgette.

Using 6.5 Million Pounds Bazalgette constructed Londons first future-proofed sewer system that consisted of over 2000km of sewers and four pumping houses.

At that time, the government's approach was shifting towards “Laissez-Faire," translating to "Leave Alone."

The public initially opposed government involvement to avoid tax increases. The Sanitary Act of 1866, influenced by Chadwick and Snow, led to local governments providing clean water access.

The 1875 Public Health Act assigned local authorities tasks such as providing clean water, managing sewage, constructing public toilets, and ensuring housing standards.
