Florence Nightingale, an expert on nursing and hospitals,

established the Nightingale School for Nurses in 1860 and a school for midwives in 1861 in London after returning to Britain in 1856.

Local Cottage Hospitals, established around 1860, were typically managed by local General Practitioners with around 12 beds. Inlarger towns, Infirmaries provided outpatient services where people

queued to see a Doctor.

Throughout history, women cared for the sick at home. Florence

Nightingale helped women become nurses and challenged societal norms. Women also joined suffragists to campaign for voting

rights.
