## Types of Cash Books

### Single column cash book

* Single column cash book (also referred to as simple cash book) presents entries for the **cash received** on the **left** side or the **debit** side and **cash payments** on the **right** side or the **credit** side.

### Double column cash book

In a double column cash book, there is an additional column that is reserved for the **discounts**.

* Therefore, in a double column cash book, also known as two column cash book, the **cash receipts** and **transactions** are recorded in **one column**.

* While the **second column** records **discounts received** and **discounts allowed**.

  * Discounts allowed are cash discounts allowed by the business to its customers when they pay their accounts quickly and are considered an **expense** for the business. Due to this, the column appears on the **debit** side according to the golden rules of accounting.

  * Discounts received are cash discounts received by the business from its suppliers when they pay their accounts quickly and are considered an **income** for the business. Due to this, the column appears on the **credit** side according to the golden rules of accounting.

### Triple column cash book

In a triple column cash book, two columns are similar to the double column cash book. While the additional column is for **bank transactions**.

* Most firms deal in cheques and therefore, the presence of a bank column in a cash book is helpful in understanding the transactions properly.

### Petty cash book

As the name suggests, this cash book is for very small transactions that take place within the organization and have little significance.

* These transactions are repetitive in nature which could put an extra unnecessary load on the general cash book and therefore separately recorded.

  * Examples may be : stationary, postage, food bills, travel expenses, etc.
