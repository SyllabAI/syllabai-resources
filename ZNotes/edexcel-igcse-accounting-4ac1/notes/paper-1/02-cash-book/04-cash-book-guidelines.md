## Cash Book Guidelines

* Opening **balance** is always on the debit side.

* The only exception to this rule is **Bank Overdraft,** which is always on the credit side.

* The word **cheque** refers to the bank column, therefore record the transaction’s value in the bank column.

* **Dishonored Cheque** means that the cheque has to be returned and therefore is considered as a payment, so always write it on the credit side.

* A **Standing Order** is a specific set of instructions given to the bank to pay fixed amounts of money on specified dates, such as monthly subscriptions.

  * Since it is an order to the bank, the transaction’s value should be recorded in the bank column.

* An **Internal Transfer** of money is recorded on both of the sides of the cash book. For example: transferred £400 from cash account into the bank account. In cases like this, we follow the rule of cash banked or banked cash.

  * On the debit side, in the details, write Cash and in the Bank column, write your amount. On the credit side, in the details, write Bank and in the Cash column, write your amount. An easy way to remember this is **CB/BC**.

* There are two types of discount questions to be asked :

  * In the case where the amount of money is placed before the discount, it is considered **far**, therefore use the formula :

$$Real amount x Percentage value = Discounted Amount$$

$$Real amount - Discounted Amount = Amount actually paid$$

* Enter the discounted amount into the discount column of the cash book and the amount actually paid into either bank or cash depending on whether the question mentions if it had a cheque or not.

  * In the case where the discount is placed before the amount of money, it is considered **near**, therefore use the formula :

$$(Amount actually paid x 100)/(100-Percentage value) = Real amount$$

$$Real amount – Amount actually paid = Discounted amount$$

* Enter the discount amount into the discount column of the cash book and the amount actually paid into either bank or cash depending on whether the question mentions if it had a cheque or not.

* When attempting to **close the balance** of the cash book, balance both the cash column and bank column separately, there are cases where your closing balances end up on different sides.

  * When bringing down the balance to the new financial year/month, if the closing balance is on the credit side, it will become an opening balance on the debit side, if the closing balance is on the debit side, it will become a bank overdraft on the credit side.

  * This is a negative consequence for the account owner as it shows that the account owner is withdrawing more money than they actually own.
