## Private Sector Organization

Every profit-making business that are not run by the government are **private sector organizations**. However, companies in the private sector that expand go through the process of incorporation.

* Companies are limited due to the owner and the business having separate legal identities. This means the owner can only lose the equity they have invested in the business.

* Limited liability also mean that the owner is not liable for the company’s debts, and therefore makes it easier for the business to raise finance.

* The separate legal status allows for a **divorce of ownership**. In the case that a owner ‘divorces’ their business, they are no longer responsible for running the business’s day to day operations.

* When a business and owner are ‘divorced’, a **manager** is hired to run the business in their stead and make decisions on the behalf of the owners.

The accounts of a limited liability company needs to be checked and approved by an independent accountant known as an **auditor**.

* An auditor acts on the behalf of the owners to check that the accounts are accurate and show a ‘true and fair’ view of how the business is doing. This is called an **audit**.

There are two types of limited liability companies:

1. Private Limited Companies

2. Public Limited Companies

### Private Limited Companies

A private limited company is owned by a group of shareholders, ranging from 2 to 200 who all own shares of the company, these shares are not available to the public.

* Ownership is typically restricted to family members, friends and selected investors.

* Shares cannot be transferred without approval from the other shareholders.

### Public Limited Companies

A public limited company is owned by shareholders from the general public, there is no limit to how many shareholders there can be and these shares are traded freely through stock exchange.

* Anyone can own a share in a public limited company.

* Ownership can change easily due to share transactions.
