9a. The first victim was **drunk** and **smelled of liquor.** The second victim was **ill** and had a **black cane.**

9b. One difference between Piliavin and Perry is that they **used stooges in different ways.** Piliavin used a **human stooge** that the participant did not know as acting ill or drunk. Perry used **animated stooges** that the participants met in various scenarios until they felt that the stooge was invading their personal space.

Another difference is the **sample.** In Perry a sample of **only 56 males** was used for both experiments. On the other hand Piliavin had a large sample of **4450 men and women** in the subway. This means that Piliavin had greater generalizability in comparison to Perry.

10\. The study by Baron-Cohen had many strengths and weaknesses.

Firstly, one strength is that the study **collected quantitative data.** This makes it **easier to make meaningful comparisons between groups.** They counted the number of correct emotions identified from looking at the eyes. As a result, they could compare performance across the four groups of participants to see any trends between the AS/HFA groups and the other three groups.

Another strength is that the study had **high level of standardization.** The revised eyes test had a standardized procedure in which each participant saw the same 36 pairs of eyes. Additionally, the images were always the same size, in black and white, with four options, including three foils. **This means that the study is easily replicable and other researchers can check the reliability of the findings** and re-examine non-significant findings, such as the difference between males and females.

However, one weakness of the study is that it may **not have been measuring theory of mind.** The study **only measured the first stage of theory of mind**, which is determining the mental state of a person. Theory of mind also has a **second stage** of where the the content of a mental state has to be attributed to a person, **which the eyes test cannot measure.** Therefore, the test **loses internal validity.**

Lastly, a weakness is that **the findings may not be generalizable to other individuals with HFA/ AS.** The sample had only 15 male adults who had volunteered to take part. This means that they were particularly motivated to take part in the research or had a special interest in the study, so the sample might have had similar characteristics that other with HFA/AS may not have. This reduces the generalizability of the findings to other adults with HFA/AS such as females, limiting external validity.
