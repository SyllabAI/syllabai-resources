9a. Operant conditioning is learning through consequences and includes the use of primary and secondary reinforcers. A primary reinforcer is something that fulfills a biological need, a secondary reinforcer is used as an exchange for a primary reinforcer. Operant conditioning essentially means that after a behavior is performed, rewarding it will result in it being performed again.

9b. Similarity -  Fagen et al and Bandura both used observation as part of their data collection techniques. Specific behaviours were be operationalized in order to improve validity. For example, in Fagen there was trunk down which meant the trunk was hanging loose and in Bandura gun play was pretending to shoot at an object in the room.

Difference - One difference between Fagen et al and Bandura is that Fagen studied elephants in their normal environment, while Bandura used a laboratory setting. This shows that Fagen has greater ecological validity as the animals are likely to have acted as they would in a real world setting where they are being taught a behavior. As Bandura took place in a lab there is less likelihood of this happening in real-life and therefore, lower ecological validity.

10\. The study by Baron-Cohen et al. had many strengths and weaknesses.

One strength of this study is that it was highly standardized. The revised eyes test had a standardized procedure with each participant seeing the same sets of eyes. The images were also all in black and white, same size, four options, three foils, etc. The participants were all provided with the same glossary. This makes it easier for the study to be replicated to be checked for reliability.

Another strength is that quantitative data was collected through the tests. This means that no researcher bias is involved in the analysis making it completely objective. Since it was a fixed choice answer they simply had to mark whether it was right or wrong. This means the study has more internal validity.

On the other hand, a weakness of this study is that it was unethical. The process identifying emotions from eyes could have been stressful as it was predicted that they would find it difficult, therefore it could have led to psychologically harm. Also, if they were not able to identify the words even with the glossary the participants might have felt bad, leading to stress.

Lastly, another weakness is that the sample only had 15 male AS/HFA participantsThis makes it difficult to generalize any findings they might not be representative of females with HFA/AS. Also, the 15 people may have been particularly outgoing since it was a volunteer sample and they might not be representative of others more reserved.
