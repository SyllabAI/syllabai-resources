9b. One difference was that Holzel et al. used an MRI scan to be able to see brain density changes after a MBSR. Dement and Kleitman used an EEG that could only monitor brain wave activity. Therefore, Holzel’s method looked at structure whereas Dement and Kleitman looked at real-time function.

Another difference was that Holzel et al. used an independant measures design when looking at how MBSR impact grey matter and brain density. Dement and Kleitman used repeated measures. This shows that Holzel can avoid order effects such as practice and fatigue effects, making results more valid than Dement and Kleitman’s.

10\. The study by Bandura et al. had many strengths and weaknesses.

Firstly, one strength of this study is that the participants were rated on aggression prior to the study, so Bandura could be more confident it was the IV affecting the DV, increasing the internal validity of the study.

Additionally, another strength is that the study was highly standardized. Each participant had the same layout of toys and all children in the aggression conditions saw the same exact routine being performed. The length of time observed was also kept the same at 20 minutes. This ensures that the study is easily replicable and reliable.

However, one weakness of this study is that there was only one stooge in both conditions throughout the experiment. The children could have imitated the model based on a certain feature unique to them rather than their gender. This confounding variable could have made the data invalid.

Lastly, another weakness is the risk to the children of psychological harm. One third of the participants witnessed the aggressive behavior of a model and were subsequently more likely to imitate this. It is possible that the children continued to demonstrate imitation of the aggressive behavior even after the study had ended, or to feel in an aggressive state of mind when they returned home.
