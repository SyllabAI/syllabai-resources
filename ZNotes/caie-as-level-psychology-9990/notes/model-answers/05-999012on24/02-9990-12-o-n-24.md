9a. The sample was 40 males aged 20–50 years. It was a volunteer sample who responded to a newspaper advertisement.

9b. One difference is that The studies used stooges in different ways. Milgram used a human stooge that the participant met beforehand but did not see during the duration of the procedure. In the Perry study the participants met the animated stooge in various scenarios until they felt the stooge was invading personal space.

Another difference is that Perry et al. obtained informed consent from each of the participants while Miligram did not, showing that Miligram had worse ethics than Perry et al.

10\. The study by Andrade had many strengths and weaknesses.

Firstly, one strength is that the participants in the doodling group were encouraged to doodle, this helps Andrade be more confident it was the doodling that was affecting the level of memory recall, increasing the internal validity of the study.

To add on, another strength is high level of standardization. All participants listened to the same recording meaning the pace and tone was identical, they all completed the study in the same quiet and dull room, and the intervention period was always one minute. This increases the replicability of the study and therefore the reliability.

On the other hand, a weakness of this study is that the sample was taken from another study that immediately ended, it was opportunity sampling. The sample could have been fatigued and tired so their answers may not have been accurate to what they would have actually chosen if not tired, reducing the validity of the study.

Lastly, another weakness is the decision to mark misheard answers as correct. When a participant wrote Craig instead of Greg it was marked as correct however they might not have actually misheard the name and wrote whatever came to mind. This assumption makes the analysis more subjective in nature.
