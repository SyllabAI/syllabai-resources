9a. The sample was an opportunity sample of 40 members, aged 18 to 55 years, out of which were 35 females and 5 males.

9b. Similarity - Both Andrade and Pozzulo were experimental in nature. For example, both manipulated the independent variable. In Andrade, there were two conditions of doodling and non-doodling. In Pozzulo, there were two conditions of the age of the participants. Therefore, both studies were trying to establish a cause and effect relationship between the independent and dependent variables.

Difference - In Andrade the participants were deceived about the true aims of the study. They were told that the tape would be rather boring and they did not have to remember anything in it, which was not true because they were quizzed on it afterwards. In Pozzulo, the participants were informed before the study that they would be asked about the videos afterwards and the target may or may not be present in the photo line-ups shown as well. This means that fully informed consent can be taken from the participants, unlike in Andrade which was not possible, but it was necessary for the study.

10\. The study by Hasset et al. has many strengths and weaknesses.

A strength of the study is that the researchers followed the housing ethics by making sure the monkeys had a proper enclosure which was 25x25. They were also fed monkey chow everyday along with fruits and vegetables, water was also provided. They were socially housed as troops which is how monkeys prefer to live in the wild as well. This shows that the monkeys were taken good care of and were not subjected to any more stress than usual.

Another strength is that researchers used a standardized and operationalized behavioral checklist to observe behaviors that the monkeys were exhibiting. This checklist had the behavior as well as a description, meaning that the two observers should have coded behaviors in the same way. This improves the overall reliability of the study as the checklist can be used again if the study were to be replicated.

A weakness is that one of the trials was abandoned because a monkey ripped up one of the plush toys. This prevented the researchers from being able to tell which toy the monkeys preferred leading to the trial ending seven minutes early. Since the data collection period is reduced the reliability of the observations falls.

Lastly, a weakness is that the sample only consisted of 34 rhesus monkeys. Some of the troop were too young to be reliably identified as either male or female, and others took part in a previous study, so they were not part of the sample. They might not represent the entire troop, making it difficult to generalize the findings to all rhesus monkeys. It will be difficult to apply these findings to those living in the wild where toy choice is unavailable.
