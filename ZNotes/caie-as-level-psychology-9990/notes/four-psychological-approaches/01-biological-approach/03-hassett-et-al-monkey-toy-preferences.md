## Hassett et al. (Monkey Toy Preferences)

**Title**: Sex differences in rhesus monkey toy preferences parallel those of children

**Year**: 2008

**Psychology being investigated**:

1. ***Socialization*:** the method of learning behaviours that are socially accepted. These behaviours differ for the two genders.

2. It has been difficult to determine if differences in behaviour between females and males are more due to biological factors or social differences due to socialization.

3. ***Gender stereotype:*** There is a difference in male and female brain structure and hormones, resulting in sex differences. bias of assigning particular traits to a particular gender (e.g assigning aggression as a male-typed trait).

4. ***Play:*** Play is defined as an intrinsically motivate, self-directed, and imaginative state of mind, characterised by valuing the process over the end result. Childhood behaviours are usually done for fun, which may or may not involve social interaction and objects called ‘toys’. 

### **Background**

* Infants are exposed to hormones during the prenatal stages of gestation, which impacts toy preferences in children.
* A few females have experienced a genetic condition which increases male hormones during foetal development- these girls are seen to prefer more ‘male’ type toys in comparison to other female.
* Primates are similar to humans genetically and, therefore, can be used as a model to show if gender differences in behaviour are due to biological or social factors.
* Female and male primates have differences in hormones, just like humans.

### **Aims**

To investigate whether toy preference in monkeys is similar to those in children to test for a biological explanation for sex differences in toy choice.

**Method**

* Research Method: A field experiment.
* Experimental Design: Independent Measures Design.
* Independent Variable (IV): Gender, Monkey’s rank on social hierarchy
* Dependent Variable (DV): Frequency and duration of play with each toy type
* **Sample**: 21 males and 61 female rhesus monkeys living in natal groups at the Yerkes primate research station in the USA.
* **Sampling technique**: Opportunity sampling

**Procedure**

 1. The natal group was housed in a 25\*25-meter outdoor area with admission into a temperature-controlled indoor area.

 2. Toys were divided into two categories based on their properties: 'wheeled toys', which matched with toys typically for boys, and 'plush toys' to match girl toys.

 3. 6 wheeled toys: wagon, truck, car, shopping cart

 4. 7 Plush toys: teddy bear, Koala hand puppet

 5. For every group formed from the natal group, 7 trials occurred, all lasting for 25 minutes.

 6. The rhesus monkeys were observed using two video cameras.

 7. For each of the 7 trials, all monkeys were initially indoors while one plush toy and one wheeled toy were placed 10 metres apart in the outdoor area.

 8. Each video camera was focused on one of the toys, with the positions of wheeled and plush toys being counterbalanced between right and left locations.

 9. After the 25-minute trial, the toys were removed, and two observers analyzed the videotape to achieve a consensus that resulted in high inter-observer reliability.

10. Specific behavioural categories were formed after watching the animal interact with toys. A few behavioural categories include extended touch, sitting on the toy, dragging, carrying in hand, turning the entire toy, and sniffing.

11. The exact time of each activity was also recorded, along with the frequency of each behaviour.

12. For data analysis, each monkey's behaviour was converted into an average frequency and duration.

### **Results**

* Females did not have a preference for plush toys over wheeled toys.
* Females interacted with plush toys more than males did on average.
* Male monkeys prefer wheeled toys over plush toys.
* Rank on social hierarchy was positively correlated to the total frequency of interactions.
* When compared to data from Berenbaum and Hines (1992), it was seen that rhesus monkeys and children both showed higher preference marks in males than females.
* Mean time:
  * Male rhesus monkeys spent 201.5 seconds with the wheeled toys, and 60.2 seconds with the plush toys.

  * Female rhesus monkeys spent 89.8 seconds with the wheeled toys, and 172.1 seconds with the plush toys.

**Strengths**

* **High internal validity:** Animal studies reduce human socialisation influences but may not eliminate all environmental influences. Behavioural categories were accurately defined, counterbalancing of wheeled toy and plush toy position.
* **High standardisation**: The observation process was the same for all monkeys, with a standard length of 25-minute sessions.

**Weaknesses**

* **Methodological differences**: Differences in the children and rhesus monkeys were compared; the toys offered were chosen for object properties rather than female or male-typed toys, and children were assessed using different toys and only measuring the duration of play, not frequency.
* **Generalizability**: There are differences between rhesus monkeys and human so the results have low generalizability on human.

**Issues and Debates**

* **Application**: Help cause awareness regarding gender-neutral socialization. This would minimize the divide between males and females early in childhood. This study increases understanding on the relative influence of biology and socialization on child development.
* **Individual versus Situational**: This illustrates a role of biology that supports the individual side of the debate; however, exposure to gender stereotyping in society counts for a situational explanation.
* **Nature versus Nurture**: Sex differences due to biological aspects of brain structure and hormones count for nature, but socialization in society is mainly due to nurture.
