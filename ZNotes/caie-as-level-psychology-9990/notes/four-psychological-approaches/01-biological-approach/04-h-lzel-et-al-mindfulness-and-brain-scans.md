## Hölzel et al. (Mindfulness and Brain Scans)

**Title:** Mindfulness practice to increase regional brain grey matter density

**Year:** 2011

**Psychology being investigated:**

1. **Mindfulness**: State achieved through meditation that aims to increase awareness of the present-moment experience. Mindfulness involves undistracted and non-judgmental awareness of the present moment, being aware of ones thoughts and feelings for conceptual completeness.

2. **Localization of function**: Particular brain areas are responsible for different activities.

**Background:**

* Mindfulness involves cognitive processes and sensory experiences that impact brain function.
* Early studies used EEG to infer brain activity, but now MRI provides more detailed information by measuring brain structures and changes in grey and white matter.
* Comparative studies suggest differences in brain grey matter between individuals practising mindfulness meditation and those who don’t. However, caution is needed due to potential pre-existing individual differences that could affect results.

**Aims:**

* Use the Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) course to compare grey matter concentration in specific brain areas and the whole brain before and after the course (pre and post-stages)

**Procedure:**

* **Research Method**: Laboratory Experiment with longitudinal design
* **Experimental Design**: Independent Measures Design
* **Independent Variable**: Exposure to an 8 week mindfulness-based intervention, the MBSR course
* **Dependent Variable**: Change in the grey matter assessed using MRI scans and Response on questionnaires to measure five mindfulness scales (before and after intervention)

**Sample**:

* **Experimental Group**

  16 participants- 7 Males and 9 Females

  Mean age- 38 years

  There were 18 participants in the experimental group, but 2 males withdrew due to discomfort.

* **Control Group**

  
  17 participants- 11 Males and 6 Females

  Mean age- 39 years

The control group participants had the same eligibility criteria as the experimental group for admission into the mindfulness course.

**Common points for both groups:**

* Participants for this study were recruited from individuals already enrolled in 4 MBSR courses at the University of Massachusetts
* Overall Age Range- 25 to 55 years
* All participants reported being physically and psychologically healthy
* All participants reported not being on any medication
* All participants reported having limited experience with meditation classes
* All participants reported as having the same educational level
* Incentive of discounted MBSR course fee was given to all

**Sampling technique**: Opportunity Sampling.

* Both groups completed the FFMQ (Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire) with 39 questions and five subscales.

* The 5 subscales had statements that measured observing, describing, acting with awareness, non-judging of inner experience, and non-reactivity to inner experience.

* Each statement had to be answered on a five-point Likert-type scale, and the total score was calculated.

* Some questions were reverse scored, and from each group, 14 participants gave complete data at both time points.

* MRI brain scans of both group participants were taken at two-time points.

* For the experimental group:

  * two weeks before MBSR.

  * two weeks after MBRS course (average gap of 56 days).

* For the control group: The two scans were taken with an average gap of 66 days between them.

* The data from MRI scans was utilized in two ways:

  1. To analyze the regions of interest (i.e Hippocampus and Bilateral insulae)
  2. To analyze the whole brain to explore other areas affected by the mindfulness course.

* Lastly, participants reported the time they spent over 8 weeks’ time on formal homework and the three assigned exercises (body scans, yoga and sitting meditation)

**Results**

* No significant correlation between time spent on body scans, yoga and sitting meditation
* Participants in the MBSR group significantly increased mindfulness scores on the 3 subscales (acting with awareness, observing and non-judging) between pre and post-testing

**Specific areas of interest:**

* In the specific area of interest i.e Left Hippocampus. There were increases in grey matter concentration for the MBSR group between the pre and post-stages
* In the specific area of interest- the Left Hippocampus, there were no changes in a grey area concentration for the control group between pre and post-stages.
* No correlation between grey matter in specific areas and the amount of homework or changes in FFMQ scores

**Whole-brain analysis:**

* Four clusters of the brain showed a significant increase in grey matter concentration for the MBSR group between the pre and post-stages.

* These four clusters showed a significant increase in grey matter concentration for the control group relative to other brain area.

  (The four clusters were Posterior cingulate cortex, left temporoparietal junction and two parts of the cerebellum).

  * No correlation between other parts of the brain and the amount of homework or changes in FFMQ scores.

**Conclusion:**

The concentration of grey matter was associated with increases in regions in the hippocampus, posterior cingulate cortex (PPC), left temporoparietal junction (TJP) and cerebellum in individuals who practiced an 8-week Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) course.

**Ethical Issues:**

Risk of not protecting participants from harm by not assisting control group participants until at least eight weeks

**Strengths:**

* The increased external validity of findings derived from longitudinal studies stems from the extended duration, which helps stabilize fluctuating participant variables such as mood, attentiveness, and health, among others, that could influence mindfulness. Longitudinal study allows visible change in the brain structure to occur, which is easier to compare before and after.
* **High amounts of standardisation**: for instance, a fixed number of exercises to be performed and fixed total duration, make the study highly replicable, increasing reliability.

**Weaknesses:**

* Lack of generalisability of the sample as they were all similar due to already being enrolled in MBSR courses and having the same level of education.
* The unknowns regarding the accuracy of the interpretation of the MRI and the lack of objectivity in the interpretations of the data produced decrease the internal validity.
* The lack of correlation between grey matter and time spent on mindfulness exercise homework shows that the exercises did not directly cause brain changes, which also decreases internal validity.

**Issues and Debates:**

* **Application to everyday life:** Provides a method to help people with temporoparietal junction (TPJ) impairment, and the two areas of the cerebellum where grey matter changes were observed can be used for emotion regulation and healthy psychological functioning.
* **Nature versus Nurture:** The biological aspects are highly determined by the processes of nature. However, the study showed that nurture through MBSR can affect those structures and processes.
