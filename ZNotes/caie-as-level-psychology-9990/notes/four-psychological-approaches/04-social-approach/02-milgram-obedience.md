## Milgram (Obedience)

**Title**: Behavioural Study of Obedience

**Year**: 1963

**Psychology being investigated**:

### Agency Theory

* **Context:** Milgram developed this theory *after* his original 1963 study to explain obedience, rather than testing it as the direct focus during the procedure.

* **Overview:** The theory states that people switch between two distinct psychological states—autonomy and agency—when interacting with an authority figure.

* **Agentic State:** People see themselves as an agent of another and follow orders while seeing the authority figure as responsible because they are not personally responsible for their actions. This arises from the fact that obedience is rewarded and disobedience is punished, causing us to give up our free will.

* **Autonomous State:** Behaviour is voluntary, and people are aware of their decisions and the consequences of those decisions. In this state, we actively choose to be obedient.

* **Moral Strain:** We experience this in the agentic stage when we go along with the demands of the authority, even though we know it is wrong and do not agree with it.

**Background**

* Destructive obedience: When people obey orders which cause harm
  or distress to another person
* Early psychological research on the Holocaust focused on the idea that something distinctive about German culture or personality led to the high levels of conformity for the genocide to take place. This is known as the dispositional hypothesis.
* Milgram set out to question this dispositional attribution of the Germans.
* He believed that the situation had led to the inhumane behaviour of the Nazis and, therefore that anybody in the same situation as those committing such atrocities would have done the same in the same circumstances.
* Milgram argued that people would commit atrocities if required to do so by an authority figure.
* This argument is an example of a situational attribution as it argues that the behaviour resulted from a person's situation.

**Aims**

* Overall aim: To investigate how obedient people would be to
  orders from a person in authority that would result in pain and
  harm to another person.
* Specific aim: To see how large an electric shock participants
  would give to a helpless man when ordered to.

**Procedure**

* Research Method: Lab Experiment. However, as there is no control condition (i.e. all of the participants took place in the same experimental procedures) it is not, strictly speaking, an experiment. (more accurately, it is a controlled observation)

* Experimental Design: Laboratory Experiment

* IV: The main study did not have any IV since it was a controlled laboratory observation with only 1 condition. The ‘Prods’ given were part of the standardized procedure.

* DV: Measure of obedience: the maximum shock level administered, and whether the participant continued to 450 volts.

* Sample: 40 males aged 20 - 50 were drawn from the New Haven district of Connecticut, USA. They were recruited through a newspaper advertisement, which promised $4.50 for their time if they participated in the study and that the money was theirs no matter what happened after they arrived. Participants believed they were to participate in a study of memory and learning at Yale University. Participants were from various backgrounds: 37.5% were manual labourers, 40% were white-collar workers, and 22.5% were professionals.

* Sampling Technique: Volunteering

* Milgram created a phoney ‘shock generator’, which in the 1960s looked very impressive and realistic. The phoney shock generator had 30 switches marked clearly in 15-volt increments from 15 to 450 volts. It also had buzzers, flashing lights and moving dials.

* When the participants arrived at the psychology laboratory, they were introduced to the relationship between punishment and learning.

* They were introduced to the experimenter, played by a 31-year-old Biology teacher, who introduced himself as Jack Williams. He wore a grey technician’s coat and appeared stern and emotionless throughout the experiment.

* They were introduced to Mr. Wallace and made to believe he was another participant. Mr. Wallace was a confederate, a 47-year-old Irish-American accountant.

* They drew slips of paper to know who would play the role of the learner of victim. This was rigged so that Mr Wallace was always the victim.

* The learner sat in an ‘electric chair’ with straps to prevent
  excessive movement during electric shocks that would be given
  from a shock generator.

* An electrode was attached to the learner’s wrist, and the paste was applied to avoid blisters and burns.

* The learner was told, “Although the shocks can be extremely painful, they cause no tissue damage”.

* These tactics made the experiment seem much more real to the participants. The final trick was to give the participant a sample shock of 45 volts (from an external battery since the shock-generator wasn’t supposed to work).

* The participant was then seated in an adjacent room and asked to
  read word pairs to the learner. The learner had to memorize the
  pairs.

* The teacher then tested the learner by pairing one of the words with four other words. The learner had to choose the previously paired word with the first word. The next word on the list was read if his answer was correct. If it was incorrect, the teacher had to state the correct answer and administer an electric shock (starting from 15 volts).

* For every wrong answer, the shock level would increase by 15 volts (15, 30, 45… … increments up to 450 volts). The learner gave a predetermined set of answers with about three wrong answers for every right answer.

* At 300 volts, the learner would start hitting the wall and shouting. He stopped answering questions. Here, the participant usually looked at the experimenter for guidance. They were told to interpret no answer as wrong, meaning the learner would have to be shocked again.

* The learner’s pounding continued after 315 volts, after which
  the learner went completely silent.

* If the participant again asked for advice, he would be met with
  a sequence of firm standardised ‘prods’:

  * “Please continue” or “Please go on”

  * “The experiment requires you to continue”

  * “You must continue”

  * “You have no choice, you must go on”

* If the participant said that the learner did not want to continue,
  another prod was used: “Whether the learner likes it or not, you
  must go on until he has learned all the word pairs correctly, so
  please go on.”

* The experiment ended if the participant refused to continue
  after the four official prods, or if 450 volts were
  administered.

* A participant who stopped before reaching 450 volts was classed as a “defiant participant”, while those who went up to 450 volts were classed as an “obedient participant”. The experiment was filmed, and observers watched through a one-way mirror.

* Participants were then interviewed, during which they rated how painful they thought the last few shocks were on a scale from 0 to 14. Projective measures and attitude scales were employed too.

  ### **Purpose of Projective Measures and Attitude Scales**

  * **Differentiating Participant Typologies:** To identify underlying personality traits or cognitive differences between **defiant** and **obedient** individuals.

  * **Investigating Moral Strain:** To measure the depth of the internal conflict and emotional distress experienced during the procedure.

  * **Ruling out Sadism:** To verify that the administration of maximum shocks was caused by situational authority pressures rather than an innate desire to inflict pain.

  * **Validating Quantitative Self-Reports:** To cross-reference and confirm the accuracy of the 0–14 subjective pain ratings given during the post-experimental interview.

* After the experiment, the participants were debriefed, and an open-ended questionnaire was given. Some psychometric tests were taken to make sure there was no emotional harm.

  ## **Psychometric Tests Include:**

* **Post-Experimental Assessment (Immediate):**

* **Open-Ended Questionnaire & Interview:** Administered immediately after the session to qualitatively evaluate participants' thoughts and feelings about their behavior.

* **Subjective Pain Scale:** A 14-point interval scale used to assess how much pain the teacher believed they were inflicting on the learner.

* **Projective Measures & Attitude Scales:** Specific psychometric instruments (such as tests evaluating attitudes toward the victim, the authority figure, and obedience) used to access the participants' deeper emotional responses to the procedure and differentiate obedient from defiant typologies.

  **Long-Term Follow-Up Assessment (One Year Later):**

* **Psychiatric Interview & Standardized Mental Health Scales:** Formal psychometric tracking administered one year later by an independent psychiatrist to explicitly check for any long-term emotional or psychological harm.

* As soon as the experiment ended, the participant was reunited with the learner (the confederate).

* This was done to show the participant that the learner was just an actor who was completely safe and unhurt, which helped immediately lower the participant's intense stress and guilt.

* The participants were told that their reaction was expected, and rigorous debriefing and follow-up procedures were take to ensure they left the experiment without mental discomfort. These measures included:

  * Friendly recounciliation between the participant and learner.

  * Explicit explanation regarding the shock generator being fake and no electrical current ever being passed through the learner.

  * Emphasizing that their obedience or hesitation was normal, reducing the immediate guilt or shame of their actions.

  * Long-Term Psychiatric Follow-Up to ensure there was no long lasting damaged on the participants. 

* **Note**: Before the study was conducted, Milgram asked 14 Yale
  University psychology students to read a description of the
  study to estimate the percentage of participants who would give 450-volt shock. The students estimated that a mere 1% to 3% of participants would comply fully.

**Results**

* 26 participants were obedient and administered a complete round
  of electric shocks, while only 14 were defiant.
* 65% compliance rate, with 26 participants completing the maximum 450-volt path.
* Conversely, a 35% defiance rate was recorded, with 14 participants terminating the procedure before reaching the maximum threshold.
* When the experimenter was the one to end the experiment (at 450
  volts), many of the participants sighed in relief or shook their
  heads in what seemed like regret.
* Most of the men were sweating, shaking, stuttering or fidgeting
  during the study.
* 14 participants giggled nervously. The participants who had
  nervous laughter fits were keen to point out that they weren’t
  sadistic and that their laughter wasn’t a sign that they enjoyed
  shocking the learner.
* 3 participants had uncontrollable seizures and the procedure was terminated for one of them.
* **Perceived Pain Severity Finding:** Quantitative self-report data collected during the post-experimental interview showed that participants perceived the shocks to be highly painful, yielding a mean intensity score of **13.42** measured on an unlabeled **14-point interval scale** (where 0 indicated "no pain" and 14 represented "extremely painful").

**Conclusions**

* People were much more obedient to destructive orders from an authoritative figure.
* They find this process highly stressful despite obeying them.
* Results supported the situational hypothesis rather than the
  dispositional hypothesis.

**Ethical Issues**

* Deception in terms of the study and whether the shocks were real.
* Participants experienced stress, but Milgram used debriefing, reunion with the learner, and follow-up procedures to reduce harm.
* The right to withdraw was compromised because the prods pressured participants to continue.

**Strengths**

* **High Internal Validity:** The extensive use of experimental controls minimized confounding variables, ensuring the situation directly caused the obedience. Examples include the **standardized verbal prods**, **fixed 15-volt shock increments**, and **predetermined learner responses** (like wall pounding at 300V).
* **High Reliability:** The highly standardized procedure ensures the study can be easily replicated to check for consistency in findings. Every participant experienced the exact same laboratory environment, experimenter persona, and apparatus setup.

**Weaknesses**

* Serious ethical concerns.
* **Unrepresentative Sample (Gender Bias):** The all-male sample fails to represent the general population, making results un-generalizable to females due to potential differences in socialization and empathy.
* **Ethnocentric Bias:** Using only American men from New Haven creates a cultural bias, failing to account for how obedience rates vary across different cultures and societies.
* **Low Ecological Validity:** The artificial laboratory setting and the unusual task of delivering electric shocks lack mundane realism and do not reflect real-world situations.

**Issues and Debates**

* Application to everyday life: Explains why ordinary people commit atrocities under orders. This applies to military war crimes, workplace misconduct, and medical errors where individuals obey a superior's harmful directives.
* Individual and situational explanations: Strongly supports the situational explanations. It proves that environmental pressures such as the prestigious Yale lab, a stern experimenter, and relentless verbal prods can completely override a person's individual moral conscience.
