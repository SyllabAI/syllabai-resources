## Baron-Cohen et al. (Eyes Test)

**Title**: The “Reading the Mind in the Eyes” Test Revised Version: A Study with Normal Adults and Adults with Asperger Syndrome or High-functioning Autism

**Year**: 2001

**Psychology being investigated**:

* The main idea of the eye test was to investigate the theory of mind.
* This is the ability to attribute mental states to oneself or another person, which is how we make sense of or predict another person’s behaviour.
* The notion is that many autistic individuals do not understand that other people have their plans, thoughts, and points of view.
* It appears that they have difficulty understanding other
  people's beliefs, attitudes, and emotions.

**Background**

* In 1997, the “Reading the Mind in the Eyes” test was developed to assess the theory of mind. This appeared to discriminate between adults with Asperger syndrome (AS) or high-functioning autistic (HFA) adults and control adults.
* The two former groups scored significantly worse. However, the
  researchers were not happy with elements of the original study
  and wanted to “upgrade” their measures to make it better.

**Aims**

* To test a group of adults with AS or HFA on the revised version of the eyes test. This was to check if the deficits in this group found in the original study could be replicated.
* To test if, in a sample of normal adults, an inverse (negative) correlation would be found between performance on the (revised) Eyes Test and the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ).
* To test whether females scored better on the Eyes Test than
  males.

**Procedure**

* Research Method: Natural Experiment (the IV is a naturally occurring variable while the Eyes Test and AQ were measurement tools) and Questionnaire.

* Experimental Design: Independent Groups

* IV: Four groups of participants (naturally occurring)

* DV: Score on Eyes Test and Autism Spectrum Quotient (Eyes Test score was the main dependent measure, while AQ score was used for correlational analysis)

* Sample: *Group 1:* 15 male adults with AS or HFA. Recruited via adverts in the UK National Autistic Society Magazine or equivalent support groups. They spanned an equivalent range of socioeconomic classes and educational levels, as seen in group 2.

  *Group 2:* 122 normal adults drawn from the adult community and educational classes in Exeter or from public library users in Cambridge. They had a broad range of occupations and educational levels.

  *Group 3:* 103 normal adult students (53 male; 50 female) studying for undergraduate degrees at Cambridge University (71 in sciences, 32 in other subjects). This group is not representative of the general population and can be considered to have a high IQ.

  *Group 4:* Randomly selected 14 adults from the general population who
  were matched for their IQ with group 1. This eliminates the chance that difference in Eyes Test score is due to IQ.

* Initially, the first two authors of this study chose the ‘correct’
  word and the ‘foil’.

* They were then piloted on 8 judges (4 males, 4 females). Pilot study is important to review the Eyes Test before the pps actually do it.

* For the correct word and its foil to be used, 5 out of 8 had to
  agree with the choice.

* No more than 2 judges should pick the foil

* Participants tested individually in a quiet room in Cambridge or
  Exeter.

* Participants in the AS/HFA group were asked to judge the gender of each eye pair additionally.

* Groups 1, 3 and 4 completed a questionnaire to measure their AQ.

* Participants were asked to read through the glossary and ask if they were unsure of any word. They were also reassured that they could refer to the glossary anytime.

* ### Sampling Technique: Opportunity and Volunteer Sampling

  ## Results

  * **Group Performance:** Group 1 performed significantly worse on the Eyes Test compared to the other groups.

  * **Gender Differences:** Females scored higher than males on the Eyes Test, though the difference was not statistically significant.

  * **AQ Test Scores:** Group 1 scored higher on the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ) test than participants in groups 3 and 4.

  * **Correlations:**

    * No correlation was found between Eyes Test scores and IQ.

    * A **significant negative correlation** was observed between the Eyes Test and the AQ test.

  ## Conclusion

  * The revised Eyes Test successfully proved to be a more sensitive measure of social intelligence in adults.

  * The negative correlation between the AQ test and the Eyes Test supports the hypothesis that both can be used effectively to gauge the severity of autistic traits.

  ## Evaluation

  ### Strengths

  * **High Internal Validity:** Achieved through methodological improvements such as four response options, balanced male/female eye pairs, and a universal glossary.

  * **Glossary Utility:** Prevented misunderstandings and stopped participants from having to randomly guess due to vocabulary barriers.

  * **Gender Balance:** Equal gender representation prevented gender bias.

  * **Standardized Procedure:** Images were consistent in size, presented in black and white, and structured with four options (one target, three foils).

  ### Weaknesses

  * **Low Ecological Validity:** In real-life settings, eyes are not static, and body language cues often assist in interpreting emotional states.

  * **Low Generalizability:**

    * Only 15 males were used in the ASD group, making findings difficult to generalize to females with ASD.

    * The student comparison group consisted entirely of students from a highly selective university (Cambridge University), which does not represent the intellect of all regular students.

    * AS/HFA participants were recruited through advertisements and support groups, introducing potential **volunteer bias**.

  * **Ethical Concerns:** Participants unable to guess emotions correctly could experience distress, embarrassment, or lowered self-esteem, leading to potential psychological harm.

  ## Issues and Debates

  * **Application:** Findings can be used to plan targeted support lessons or therapy for students or individuals with AS/HFA.

  * **Reductionism:** The test can be viewed as reductionist because it does not capture the full picture of human emotion, as emotions are expressed through more than just the eyes.
