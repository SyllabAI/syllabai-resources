## Ideographic Versus Nomothetic

This debate splits psychology down the middle regarding its ultimate goal: deciding whether the discipline should focus on exploring the subjective traits that make every individual different, or on discovering the universal standardized laws that make all humans the same.

* **Idiographic Approach:** This approach argues that because human experience is deeply unique, we must study individuals one-by-one using rich, qualitative data like case studies. It rejects the idea that you can accurately fit everyone into a single box.

* **Nomothetic Approach:** This approach treats psychology like a hard science. It focuses on gathering massive amounts of quantitative (numerical) data from large groups to establish global averages, statistical benchmarks, and universal laws of behaviour.

### Ideographic Examples from Study

* **Saavedra & Silverman (Button Phobia Study)**

  * A case study tracking a single 9-year-old Hispanic boy's therapeutic treatment for a phobia of buttons.

  * The researchers were not trying to find a universal law that explains every phobia on Earth. Instead, they focused entirely on gathering rich, highly personalized, qualitative data about this specific boy's unique cognitive distortions (his personal disgust evaluations) and his historical trauma (the art class incident). While you cannot take his exact timeline and apply it as a rigid template for all children, the study provides an unmatched, deeply human understanding of how an individual phobia develops and heals.

### Nomothetic Examples from Study

* **Andrade (Doodling Study)**

  * A highly standardized laboratory experiment comparing a group of 20 doodlers against a group of 20 non-doodlers during a monitoring task.

  * Andrade’s entire goal was to establish a universal cognitive law: doodling improves information processing during a monotonous task. The data gathered was purely quantitative, counting the exact numbers of names and places correctly recalled. The study completely filters out the individual personalities, moods, or unique backgrounds of the participants to create a generalized benchmark (29% better recall) meant to apply to all human minds.

* **Pozzulo et al. (Eyewitness Testimony Study)**

  * Testing large groups of children (aged 4–7) and adults using standardized video line-ups.

  * This study gathers immense quantitative data to establish generalized developmental laws regarding memory and social compliance. By calculating group averages, Pozzulo established a population-wide rule: when the target is absent, children are universally more vulnerable to making false-positive identifications than adults due to social pressure.
