## The Use of Children in Psychological Research

### Why Psychologists use children for research

* Children allow researchers to investigate how cognitive, social, and emotional behaviours develop, grow, and change from infancy to adulthood.

* Children have had far less exposure to societal conditioning, media, and life experiences than adults. This makes them ideal for studying whether a behavior is innate (nature) or learned (nurture).

* Studying children helps psychologists spot behavioural or developmental issues early, allowing for the creation of effective educational or therapeutic programs.

### Benefits of using children

* Children are generally less likely than adults to guess the true aim of an experiment. They tend to act more naturally and spontaneously, reducing demand characteristics and increasing the internal validity of the behavioral data.

* Discoveries made using children can positively alter the entire trajectory of a person's life by improving parenting strategies, school environments, or child-focused therapies.

### Ethical Concerns and Issues

* Children under the age of 16 are not legally old enough to give true informed consent. Therefore, researchers must obtain consent from a parent or legal guardian.

* Modern ethics dictate that parental consent is not enough. Researchers must also obtain assent from the child which means the child must verbally or behaviourally agree to take part in a way they can understand, and they have the absolute right to withdraw at any point if they show distress.

* Children are far more vulnerable to psychological distress, fear, and long-term behavioral changes than adults.

* Because children are still developing their worldview, any negative or aggressive behavior they learn or experience during a study could stay with them, potentially causing lasting emotional or behavioural damage.

### Examples from Core Study

* **Bandura et al. (Bobo Doll Study)**

  * A controlled observation task tracking child imitation of adult aggression.

    * **Why children were used**: Bandura explicitly needed children (3 to 6 years old) because their minds are highly malleable, making them ideal for testing how social behaviours are learned from scratch through observation.

    * **Ethical Issues (Harm & Informed Consent):** While parents gave consent, the children themselves could not fully understand what they were participating in. The study intentionally failed to protect them from psychological harm. By encouraging the children to imitate violent behavior, the researchers risked altering the children’s long-term moral development and behavioural tendencies.

* **Pozzulo et al. (Eyewitness Testimony Study)**

  * A video-watching and line-up identification task comparing child and adult witness accuracy.

    * **Why children were used:** Pozzulo needed to compare children (4 to 7 years old) against adults to discover if age affects memory distortion and social compliance during police investigations.

    * **Ethical Issues (Distress & Right to Withdraw):** Watching a staged crime and being asked to identify a culprit can be highly stressful and intimidating for a young child. Pozzulo carefully managed this by ensuring the task was framed as a game, keeping the setting familiar, and monitoring the children closely for any signs of distress so they could be withdrawn immediately.
