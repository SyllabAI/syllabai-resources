## Determinism Versus Free-will

This debate balances whether our thoughts, choices, and behaviours are entirely dictated by internal or external forces beyond our conscious control (determinism), or whether humans possess the genuine personal agency and autonomy to choose their own paths (free-will).

* **Biological Determinism:** The belief that human behaviour is entirely hardwired into our physical makeup. It argues that our actions are dictated by genetic programming, brain anatomy, and evolutionary or hormonal pathways.

* **Environmental Determinism:** The belief that our choices are entirely caused by external forces in our surroundings. This includes the presence of authority figures, past conditioning, or intense situational social pressure.

* **Free-Will:** The belief that human beings are active, conscious agents who have genuine control over their choices, giving them the power to reject biological impulses or resist environmental pressures.

### Deterministic Examples from Study

* **Milgram (Obedience Study)**

  * Participants were placed in a high-pressure laboratory environment and ordered by a stern experimenter to administer increasingly severe electric shocks to an innocent learner.

  * This research heavily supports environmental determinism. The ordinary, psychologically healthy participants did not enter the lab wanting to hurt anyone. Instead, their personal agency was completely overridden by situational variables such as the prestige of Yale University, the authoritative lab coat, and strict verbal prods. The environment effectively forced them to behave as agents, dictating their destructive obedience.

* **Hölzel et al. (Mindfulness and Brain Plasticity Study)**

  * Physical changes in grey matter density within the brain were measured using MRI scans before and after an 8-week mindfulness course.

  * At first glance, brain architecture operates deterministically; for example, a highly active amygdala pre-determines a heightened physiological stress response. However, this study beautifully highlights the interaction with free will. The participants had to consciously choose to exert the personal effort required to complete the daily 8-week meditation course. Their free will actively rewired their own deterministic biology via neuroplasticity.

### Free-will Examples from Study

* **Piliavin et al. (Subway Samaritan Study)**

  * Passengers on a moving New York subway train had to choose whether or not to step forward and assist a collapsed victim.

  * While the researchers explained helping behaviour using a calculative Cost-Reward Model, the actual data reveals a powerful display of human free will. Even when the victim was made to appear drunk which significantly increased the personal costs of helping (such as fear of disgust or conflict), passengers still stepped forward to help 50% of the time. They consciously chose to overcome situational discomfort to perform a moral act, demonstrating active personal agency.
