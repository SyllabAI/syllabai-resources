## Individual and Situational Explanations

The extent to which a person’s beliefs or behaviour are controlled by individual and situational explanations

* **Individual Explanation**: Behaviour is driven by internal factors like personality, genetics, biology, and personal attitudes.

* **Situational Explanation**: Behaviour is driven by external factors like social pressure, authority, environment, and context.

### Situational Explanations

* **Milgram (Obedience Study)**

  * An astonishing 65% of ordinary, psychologically healthy participants administered a lethal 450V electric shock to an innocent learner purely because they were told to do so by an experimenter.

  * This study heavily supports the situational explanation. Milgram argued that the participants were not inherently cruel people (dispositional). Instead, the features of the situation of the prestigious setting of Yale University, the authoritative lab coat worn by the experimenter, and the stern verbal prods created a high-pressure environment that compelled destructive obedience.

* **Piliavin et al. (Subway Samaritan Study)**

  * People were incredibly fast to help victims on a moving New York subway train (95% of spontaneous help for the ill victim; 50% for the drunk victim), with virtually no evidence of diffusion of responsibility.

  * This supports the situational explanation through the Cost-Reward Model. The researchers argued that the features of the environment dictated the helping behavior. Since the participants were trapped in a confined, moving subway carriage, they could not easily walk away from the emergency. This increased their situational arousal (discomfort), forcing them to help quickly to escape the distressing situation.

### Individual Explanations

* **Baron-Cohen et al. (The "Reading the Mind in the Eyes" Study)**

  * Adults with Autistic Spectrum Disorder (ASD) scored significantly lower on the revised "Reading the Mind in the Eyes" test (averaging 21.9 out of 36) compared to neurotypical control groups.

  * This strongly supports the individual explanation. The study demonstrates that differences in social cognitive performance i.e. the ability to attribute emotional states to others are determined by an individual's stable, internal psychological condition (being on the autism spectrum), rather than the situation they are placed in.

### Individual and Situational Explanation

* **Perry et al. (Personal Space Study)**

  * The study looked at how much personal distance people preferred when imagining social interactions, finding that preferences shifted depending on both a biological hormone and internal traits.

  * This study perfectly illustrates how individual and situational factors interact to dictate human behaviour:

    * **Individual Factor:** Participants with naturally low empathy traits preferred much larger personal space distances compared to those with high empathy traits.

    * **Situational Factor:** The administration of oxytocin (or a placebo) acted as a situational, physiological trigger. When given oxytocin, highly empathetic individuals felt comfortable letting people get closer, whereas low-empathy individuals actually preferred even more distance.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|The debate helps psychologists understand the relative contribution of individual/personal factors and environmental/contextual factors in explaining behaviour more accurately|It is not always easy to separate individual and situational factors|
|When researchers look at how both sides connect rather than forcing an "either/or" choice, it creates a much more accurate understanding of human nature|Studies might be unethical to gain more valid results|
