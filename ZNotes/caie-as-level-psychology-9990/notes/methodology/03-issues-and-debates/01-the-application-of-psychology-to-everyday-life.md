# Issues and Debates

## The Application of Psychology to Everyday Life (Usefulness)

* The application of psychology refers to using psychological theories, concepts, and research findings to solve real-world problems and improve people's lives.
* **Conducting useful research:** Psychology must go beyond theory to improve human welfare, safety, and productivity. Useful research provides tangible tools, therapies, and behavioural interventions that directly fix societal issues. Governments, schools, hospitals, and courts rely on objective evidence to design fair rules, effective teaching strategies, and safer public environments.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|It can be used to improve human behaviours|Studies might be unethical to gain more valid results|
|High practical worth and social benefit|Studies need to be high in ecological validity to be of more use to society, but this can be quite difficult if they are conducted in a laboratory.|
|Enhances the credibility of psychology|Risk of generalizability and sample biases|

### Dement & Kleitman (Sleep & Dreams Study)

* **Practical Application**: Sleep Disorder Assessment and Diagnosis

  * By proving that REM sleep is directly correlated with dreaming and that eye movements correspond to dream imagery, this research allowed clinical psychologists and doctors to use objective physiological measurements (EEG and EOG) to assess patients.

  * It is used in modern sleep clinics to diagnose and treat sleep disorders like insomnia, sleep apnea, or narcolepsy by identifying exactly which sleep stage is disrupted.

### **Andrade (Doodling Study)**

* **Practical Application:** Improving Concentration During Learning

  * Andrade demonstrated that participants who doodled while listening to a boring telephone call recalled 29% more information than those who did not doodle, proving that doodling prevents minds from daydreaming without distracting from the primary task.

  * This can be used by educational psychologists, teachers, and corporate managers to encourage simple, passive kinetic tasks like doodling or using fidget tools during long lectures or business meetings to help students and employees maintain focus and retain information.

### Pozzulo et al. (Eyewitness Testimony Study)

* **Practical Application:** Improving Eyewitness Testimony Procedures

  * This study explored how cognitive factors and social pressure affect line-up identifications, specifically showing that children are significantly more likely than adults to make false positive identifications when the actual culprit is absent from a line-up.

  * It is used by the police and legal systems to design more reliable line-up protocols. For example, when interviewing child witnesses, police should use "target-absent" practice line-ups and explicitly tell the child that the criminal "may or may not" be in the lineup to reduce the social pressure to just pick someone.

### Fagen et al. (Elephant Training Study)

* **Practical Application:** Animal Welfare and Humane Training

  * The study investigated the use of Positive Reinforcement Training (PRT) using rewards like food treats to train young Asian elephants to voluntarily participate in the trunk washing procedure without using physical punishment or force.

  * This is applied globally by zoo keepers, veterinarians, and conservationists to manage captive or endangered animals safely and humanely, drastically reducing animal stress and eliminating the need for cruel, dominant-based training methods.

### Perry et al. (Personal Space)

* **Practical Application:** Understanding Social Interactions and Designing Environments

  * This study explored how oxytocin, the "social hormone", affects our preferences for personal space depending on an individual's empathy levels, showing that highly empathetic people are more comfortable with closer social distances.

  * This is used by architects, interior designers, and counselors to optimize social environments. For instance, designing waiting rooms, classrooms, or therapy offices with adaptable seating distances can make social interactions more comfortable for individuals with varying social sensitivities or anxiety disorders.
