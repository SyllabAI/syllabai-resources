## Nature Versus Nurture

In the contemporary time period, most psychologists now recognize an interaction between nature and nurture rather than viewing them as completely separate, competing explanations.

* Nature: The extent to which behaviours, feelings or thinking results from innate genetic factors.
* Nurture: The extent to which behaviours, feelings or thinking results from environmental influences such as learning or other people.

### Nature Example from Study

* **Hassett et al. (Monkey Toy Preferences Study)**

  * An observational task assessing toy choice and preferences in monkeys.

  * This study supports the nature explanation. When given a choice between masculine toys like trucks and feminine toys like dolls, male monkeys showed a strong, statistically significant preference for the wheeled toys.

  * Because these monkeys were not raised in human society and had no exposure to gender socialization or advertisements, their toy preferences point to an innate, biological difference likely driven by evolutionary adaptations or prenatal hormonal exposure (testosterone).

### Nurture Examples from Study

* **Bandura et al. (Bobo Doll Study)**

  * A controlled observation task tracking child imitation of adult aggression.

  * This study heavily supports the nurture explanation. Children who observed an aggressive adult model beating up an inflatable Bobo doll imitated those exact physical and verbal behaviors, whereas children in the non-aggressive control group showed virtually zero aggression.

  * This directly proves that complex human behaviour can be entirely acquired from the social environment via observational learning and modeling.

* **Saavedra & Silverman (Button Phobia Study)**

  * A clinical case study tracking a child's phobia treatment using disgust/fear hierarchy scales.

  * This supports the nurture explanation by illustrating how abnormal behaviours are learned through experience. The 9-year-old boy did not inherit a genetic fear of buttons; his phobia was accidentally acquired at school during an art class when a bowl of buttons fell on him.

  * This experience created a conditioned emotional response, proving that phobias and emotional distress can be entirely shaped by negative environmental events.

### Nature and Nurture Example from Study

* **Hölzel et al. (Mindfulness and Brain Plasticity Study)**

  * An MRI brain-scanning task measuring gray matter density before and after an 8-week Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) course.

  * This study perfectly demonstrates the interactionist approach through the concept of neuroplasticity:

    * **Nature Factor:** The physical structure of our brain (like the hippocampus, which controls learning and memory, and the amygdala, which regulates stress) is an entirely biological, physical matrix.

    * **Nurture Factor:** Engaging in a structured, environmental behaviour by practicing meditation for 8 weeks actively altered that biological matrix, physically increasing gray matter density in the brain.

  * It proves that our environmental experiences (nurture) directly change and reshape our biological architecture (nature).


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|The debate helps psychologists understand the relative contribution of individual/personal factors and environmental/contextual factors in explaining behaviours more accurately which contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of human behaviours|Forcing a behaviour into a strict "nature only" or "nurture only" box oversimplifies the true complexity of human psychology|
|Identifying whether a behaviour is driven by nature or nurture allows psychologists to design specific treatments.|Both extreme sides of this debate lean toward determinism, implying that humans have very little free will or control over their own choices|
|Research focusing on the nature side of the debate often utilizes highly objective, scientific equipment, which removes human bias from the data|Studies might be unethical to gain more valid results|
