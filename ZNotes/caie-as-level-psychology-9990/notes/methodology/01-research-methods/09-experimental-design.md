## Experimental Design

* This is the way in which participants are allocated to the different
  levels of the IV.
* An **independent measures design** is an experimental design in which a different group of participants is used for each IV level. If the IV is naturally occurring, the researcher must use it.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|As participants take part on only one condition, they are less likely to guess the aim of the study, hence reducing the effects of demand characteristics|There may be participant variables affecting the DV rather than the IV|
|There are no order effects that can reduce the validity of the findings|More participants are required for this type of design as compared to repeated measures|

* A **repeated** **measures design** is an experimental design in which participants perform at every IV level. It cannot be used if the IV is naturally occurring. Counterbalancing can be used to reduce order effects. One example is the ABBA design. Because not all repeated-measures studies use ABBA counterbalancing.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|Eliminates any participant variables as all of them take part in all conditions|There is a chance of demand characteristics as participants may work out the aim or change their behaviour.|
|Fewer participants are needed as compared to independent measures|Order effects can affect the findings of the study and reduce its validity. This could be overcome using counterbalancing.|

* A **matched pairs design** is an experimental design in which participants are arranged in pairs. Each pair is similar in important ways to the study, and one member performs at a different level of the IV.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|Matching participants as closely as possible on measurable traits may help control for individual differences affecting the results.|It is difficult to match participants perfectly on all relevant characteristics.|
|This design is better suited when a repeated measures design may not work due to an order effect that may affect results.|Trying to match people exactly is impossible as there will always be individual differences from one person to another. Therefore, the study may lack internal validity if these differences affect the IV rather than the experimental condition.|
||The sample may be smaller as trying to find a large sample of people matching similar traits may be difficult. This may mean the findings may lack external validity.|
