## Longitudinal study

**Definition**

* Study that follows the **same group (cohort)** over time

* Measures variables at multiple time points (weeks → decades)

**Key Features**

* Tracks **changes within individuals**

* Can study **development** and **effects of experiences/interventions**

* Uses a **single cohort** (same age/stage at start)

**Uses**

* Observe **patterns of change** (e.g. emotions over time)

* Study **relationships/correlations** (e.g. multicultural exposure & prejudice)

* Assess **effects of interventions** (e.g. therapy, education)

**Design**

* Often like a **long-term repeated measures design**

* Includes:

  * **Baseline (pre-intervention)**

  * **Post-intervention \+ follow-ups**

* Can be considered **quasi-experimental**

**Advantages**

* Avoids **cohort effects** (same group = same background)

* Shows **true change over time**

* Useful for **cause-and-effect (with interventions)**

**Disadvantages**

* **Time-consuming & expensive**

* **Participant dropout (attrition)**

* Practice effects (participants get used to tests)

### **Cross-Sectional Studies**

**Definition**

* Compares **different groups** at one point in time

* Groups differ by **age/stage**

**Example**

* Compare 10-, 20-, 30-, 40-, 50-year-olds

**Advantages**

* **Quick and cheap**

* No attrition

**Disadvantages**

* **Cohort effects** (different upbringing/time periods)

* Cannot show **true development over time**
