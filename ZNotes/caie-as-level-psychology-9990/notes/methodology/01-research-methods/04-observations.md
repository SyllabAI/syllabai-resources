## Observations

* The observation method in psychology involves directly and systematically witnessing and recording measurable behaviors, actions, and responses in natural or controlled setting/environment.

  Overt: when the participants know who the researcher is
  and that they are being observed.

  |
  
  Structured: the observer records
  only a limited range of behaviour.

  |
  
  Participant: one who watches from the perspective of
  being part of the social setting of the participants.

  |
  
  Naturalistic: a research method in which the researcher studies behavior in its natural setting without intervention or manipulation.
  It involves observing and recording behavior as it naturally occurs, providing insights into real-life behaviors and interactions in their natural context.

  ||
  ||
  
  Covert: observer could be present
  in the group of participants observing them but they do not know who
  it is.

  |
  
  Unstructured: one in which the observer records a range of behaviours, which is
  usually confined to a pilot study to refine the behavioural
  categories.

  |
  
  Non-participant: observer does not become involved in the situation
  being studied.

  |
  
  Controlled:  a research method for studying behavior in a carefully controlled and structured environment.
  The researcher sets specific conditions, variables, and procedures to systematically observe and measure behavior, allowing for greater control and comparison of different conditions or groups.

* Behaviors can be systematically recorded and quantified. Data can be analysed statistically with minimal bias

  * **Participant Observation:**

#|
||

Strengths

|

Weaknesses

||
||

High ecological validity as the participants are observed in a real-life setting

|

There are ethical problems of informed consent

||
||

As the observers become involved with the group, they are more likely to understand the motives and reasons for behaviours which increases the validity of the findings

|

Participants may alter their behavior if they are aware that they are being observed (reactivity effect).This lowers the validity of the findings

||
|#

* **Non-participant Observation:**

#|
||

Strengths

|

Weaknesses

||
||

Participants behaviour will not be affected by observation because the observer is out of sight

|

It can be difficult to make detailed observations and to produce qualitative data that allows understandings as to why the behaviours are occurring

||
||

Researchers’ observations are more likely to be objective as they are detaching from the people they are observing

|

​

||

* **Structured Observation:**

#|
||

Strengths

|

Weaknesses

||
||

The behavioural checklist allows objective quantitative data to be collected which can then be analysed statistically

|

The sampling of observed behaviour tends to be restricted and does not give an idea of the reasons as to why the behaviours are occurring

||
|#

* **Unstructured Observation:**

#|
||

Strengths

|

Weaknesses

||
||

They can generate in-depth, rich qualitative data that can help explain why behaviours are occurring

|

Observers may easily be drawn to salient behaviours or more noticeable behaviours and hence may not fully represent all the behaviours

||
|#

* **Naturalistic Observation:**

#|
||

Strengths

|

Weaknesses

||
||

As participants are unaware that they are being watched, they should behave more naturally, removing the likelihood of demand characteristics

|

Very little control over extraneous variables which makes it difficult to draw a cause and effect relationship

||
||

High ecological validity as the observation takes place in a natural setting

|

Replication may be difficult as there cannot be a totally standardised procedure, hence making it difficult to test for reliability

||
|#

* **Controlled Observation:**

#|
||

Strengths

|

Weaknesses

||
||

As the set-up is controlled, the observers can be more confident about what is causing the behaviours

|

An artificial situation can easily influence participants’ behaviour

||
||

Less chance of extraneous variables affecting participants’ behaviour

|

Low ecological validity as the setting is artificial

||
