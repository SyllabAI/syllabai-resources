## Types of Data

* There are four types of data: qualitative or quantitative; objective or subjective.
* Qualitative data is descriptive, in-depth data that cannot be measured by numbers. It answers ‘how’ and ‘why’ by exploring qualities, and characteristics of a psychological phenomenon or variable.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|In-depth, detailed accounts given in the words of participants are collected. It can be argued that using qualitative data is not reductionist, which means ignoring some sides of the whole truth.|Interpretation of data could be subjective as these are words rather than numbers|
|As the data comes directly from the participant, we can understand **why** the participant thinks, feels or acts in a particular way|There may be researcher bias - the researcher may only select data that fits into the hypothesis or aim of the study|

* Quantitative data is the numerical data about the quantity of a
  psychological measure. Answers questions like “how much”, “how many”…


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|As the data are numerical, it allows easier comparison and statistical analysis to take place.|As the data are numerical, they may not provide insight into the reasons underlying participants' responses.|
|Numerical data are objective and scientific, they reduce researcher interpretation bias i.e. minimal chances of researcher/ psychologists miscalculating the data and drawing invalid conclusions.|Using quantitative data can be seen as reductionist as it involves researchers reducing complex ideas and behaviours to a number or percentage.|
