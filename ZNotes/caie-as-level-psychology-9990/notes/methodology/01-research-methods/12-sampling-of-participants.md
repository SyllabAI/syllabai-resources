## Sampling of Participants

* A population is a group sharing one or more characteristics from which a sample is drawn.
* A sample is a group of people selected to represent the population in a study.
* The sampling technique is the method used to select a subset of individuals or sample from a larger population. There are three types of sampling techniques.
  * **Opportunity sampling**
  * **Volunteer (self-selected) sampling**
  * **Random sampling**

Opportunity Sampling

|

Researchers select participants who are readily available at the time of data collection.

|

Large numbers of participants can be obtained relatively quickly and easily because researchers use people who are around

|

Researchers are unlikely to gain a wide variety of participants to allow for generalisation

|

A psychology student stands outside the school library and asks the first 30 students who walk by to complete a stress questionnaire.

||
||

Volunteer Sampling

|

Participants are invited to participate in studies via advertisements or emails.

|

Participants are more likely to participate if they have already volunteered so the drop-out rate should be lower, making generalisations stronger

|

Researchers are unlikely to gain a wide variety of participants to allow for generalisation because participants will only be of a certain type

|

A researcher posts an online survey about social media use, and anyone interested clicks the link and completes it.

||
||

Random Sampling

|

The main feature of random sampling is 'equal chance' i.e. each individual in a sample has an equal chance of being selected as a study participant.

|

Researchers can generalise the target population (TP) with more confidence

|

Obtaining details of the TP to use to draw the sample may be difficult - a representative sample cannot be guaranteed

|

A school has 1,000 students. Each student is assigned a number, and a computer randomly selects 100 numbers to participate in a study on sleep habits.
