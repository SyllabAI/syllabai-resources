## Aims and Hypotheses

* An aim tells you the purpose of the investigation. It is generally
  expressed in terms of what the study intends to show. It is written
  before the experiment is carried out and does not predict the
  outcomes.
* A hypothesis is a testable statement predicting a difference between
  levels of the IV in an experiment or a relationship between
  variables in a correlation.
* Null hypothesis: No difference. All difference is due to random chances.
* An alternative hypothesis is the testable statement which predicts
  the difference or relationship between the variables in a particular
  investigation.
* There are three types of hypothesis:
  * Null vs Alternative hypothesis

    * A null hypothesis is a testable statement stating that any
      difference or correlation in the results is due to chance.

    * Alternative hypothesis supports that any difference or correlation in results is due to a real, systematic effect, cause, or relationship, rather than just random chance.  

  * Directional vs Non-directional hypothesis

    * A directional (one-tailed) hypothesis is a statement predicting
      the direction of a relationship between variables.
    * A non-directional (two-tailed) hypothesis is a statement
      predicting only that one variable will be related to the other,
      not the direction of the relationship.
