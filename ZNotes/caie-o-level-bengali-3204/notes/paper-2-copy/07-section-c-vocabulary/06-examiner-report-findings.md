## Examiner Report Findings

### Mistakes That Come Up Every Year

* **Answering in English:** Candidates who write the meaning in English receive no marks. This error has persisted from the earliest reports (November 2003) right through to June 2025. You must understand this before the exam.

* **Writing multiple synonyms without crossing out:** When candidates list several options and leave incorrect ones uncrossed, the examiner cannot pick a correct answer. One correct synonym followed by one incorrect one, with neither crossed out, scores zero. Candidates must cross out every attempt except the one they want marked.

* **Ignoring context:** Many candidates give definitions that are accurate in everyday use but wrong for the passage. Reading the sentence containing the target word before answering is not optional, it is the whole exercise.

* **Paraphrasing instead of giving a synonym:** The question asks for a single word or short phrase. Writing a sentence-long explanation scores zero, no matter how accurate the explanation is.

* **Wrong part of speech:** If the target word is a noun, the synonym must also be a noun. Adjective answers for noun targets do not receive credit even when the meaning is close.

* **Spelling errors in the synonym:** Identifying the right word but spelling it incorrectly costs marks. Observed examples include writing সম্পূর্ন instead of সম্পূর্ণ, and সনতুস্ট instead of সন্তুষ্ট.

* **Guessing or giving the opposite meaning:** Examiners frequently note candidates writing words with completely opposite meanings (e.g., "plenty" for a word meaning "rare"), or words with no clear connection to the target at all.

### What Candidates Who Score Well Actually Do

Top performers rely on basic habits that weak candidates usually skip:

* They **re-read the sentence** in the passage before attempting the synonym.

* They write a **single word or short phrase**.

* They **match the grammatical form** of the target.

* They **check the spelling** before moving on.
