## Studying During The School Year

* **Analyze past papers.** The words look random, and most students treat them as random and just try to memorise as much as possible. But the data across 20 years of past papers shows that words are indeed repeated. নয়নাভিরাম appeared in 2010 and again in 2022. সূচনা in 2020 and 2024. প্রচণ্ড in 2012 and 2017. স্মরণীয় in 2008 and 2023. নিত্য/নিত্যদিন in 2010 and 2024. None of these were retired after their first appearance. So, go through the words that came in the past papers and their meanings.

* **Study the answer lists**, not just the question words. When আদেশ was tested in 2017, নির্দেশ appeared in the answer list. In 2023, নির্দেশ became the question word itself. The same recycling happened with বিরল (answer in 2017, question in 2018), সূচনা (answer in 2004, question in 2020 and 2024), and উপেক্ষা (answer in 2005, question in 2014). Any word currently that is currently an answer in the past paper mark schemes is a potential future question. Also, words at higher risk for upcoming exams, based on the recycling pattern, are those that have appeared in recent answer lists but never yet as question words. Not a guarantee, but this is historically where some new questions have come from.

* **Practice producing multiple synonyms**, not just recognizing one. রোজ and প্রতিদিন are both correct for নিত্য. সন্তুষ্ট and খুশি both work for তৃপ্ত. One synonym is enough to pass, but the ability to produce several in quick succession means you are not dependent on remembering one specific word under pressure.

* **Do not skip the obvious words**. দ্রুত, ভিন্ন, বিশাল, কল্পনা get dropped from revision because students assume everyone knows them. Writing three solid synonyms for বিশাল under exam conditions is harder than it looks. These are words that cost students marks every year.

* **Read Bengali newspaper editorials**. Prothom Alo and Anandabazar Patrika use commonly use words tested by the exam. One editorial a day for a month will absorb words like প্রসার, নিরলস, বিকাশ, উন্নয়ন through context. It also trains the instinct for formal sentence construction, which matters when answering other parts of the paper.

* **Keep a word journal**. Every time you encounter an unfamiliar word anywhere, write it down with two or three synonyms, not definitions, synonyms. After two or three months you will have built a list of 200 to 300 words that reflects specifically what you struggled with, which is more useful than any generic word list in the final week before the exam. However, one week before the exam, go through every synonym list from the last five years. For each answer word, check whether it has ever appeared as a question. If not, add it to your final revision. The list will be short and it is the most targeted revision you can do.
