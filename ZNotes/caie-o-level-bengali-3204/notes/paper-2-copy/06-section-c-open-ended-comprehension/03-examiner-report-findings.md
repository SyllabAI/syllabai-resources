## Examiner Report Findings

### Mistakes That Come Up Every Year

* **Direct Text Lifting (The Biggest Issue):** Across all reports from 2003 to 2025, the most persistent mistake is copying entire sentences, clauses, or paragraphs directly from the text. This automatically caps or disqualifies candidates from earning linguistic quality marks, even if the content is technically correct.

* **Superficial "Manipulation":** Many candidates mistakenly believe that omitting a few words or simply rearranging the word order counts as writing in their own words. Examiners do not reward this; it is still treated as copying.

* **Blindly Following Text Order (Answer-Swapping):** Candidates frequently swap the answers for adjacent questions: most commonly **Questions 33 & 34** and **Questions 36 & 37** (and occasionally **37 & 38**). This happens because they write answers based purely on the chronological flow of the passage instead of reading what each specific question is actually asking.

* **Careless Reading:** Candidates often throw away marks on avoidable errors, such as bringing in external information not found in the text (like naming a country not mentioned) or answering about a character's professional work when the question explicitly asked about their personality.

* **Grammar and Syntax Flaws:** Even otherwise strong candidates frequently struggle with the hierarchical transformation of verbs in Bengali, leading to structurally broken sentences.

* **Writing in Bullet Points or Notes:** Giving short, truncated notes or bulleted lists instead of full sentences severely damages the language quality score.

* **Repetition:** Resorting to repeating the exact same information or answer across multiple different questions.

### What Candidates Who Score Well Actually Do

* **Read the Questions First:** Before writing a single word, they read the passage carefully and then pre-read all six questions. This simple routine prevents them from accidentally swapping answers on adjacent questions.

* **Master the "Writing in Your Own Words" Requirement:** They completely change the sentence structures of the passage rather than just swapping words around. While they use the necessary vocabulary from the text, the phrasing is entirely their own, which unlocks the highest language marks.

* **Write Exclusively in Full Sentences:** They avoid shortcuts, lists, or brief notes, ensuring every single answer is a structurally complete, grammatically correct Bengali sentence.

* **Target the Prompt Directly:** They ensure their answers strictly match what the question asks, and they carefully vary their points so they never repeat the same information across different questions.
