## **Exam Strategies**

### **When approaching this section**

1. Read all six questions before reading the passage. You need to know what to look for.

2. Match each question to its paragraph before you start writing.

3. Once you have enough for the current question, stop reading. Moving ahead risks pulling in content that belongs to a later question.

4. After drafting, re-read the question and check that your points actually answer it.

**Understanding what the mark scheme wants**

The mark scheme lists 5 to 7 valid points per question and you only need any 4. You are not looking for one specific right answer. If you are unsure about a point, a different valid detail from the same paragraph can take its place. Write five points where possible as a safety buffer in case one is rejected. The mark scheme also uses "or similar wording" against almost every content point, so you do not need to match it exactly. Clear meaning, in your own words, from the right part of the passage is enough.

### **Avoiding lifting**

Dropping one or two words from a copied sentence is not paraphrasing. The mark scheme requires that you both change at least one key word and restructure the sentence. If your version follows the same order and phrasing as the original, it counts as lifted even with minor swaps. That is an automatic 0 on language.

### **Reading the question wording**

Not every question asks for four points on one topic. Some use "দুটি করে" (two each) rather than "চারটি" (four), which means a 2\+2 split: two examples from one category, two from another. Read the question wording every single time.

### **Writing the answer**

Write in full paragraph form. No bullet points. Open with a sentence that directly addresses the question, work through your four or five points in the body, and close with a brief sentence reflecting the overall tone or conclusion of the passage. The opening and closing do not earn content marks on their own, but they help your answer read as coherent and well-structured, which supports your language marks.

Before writing, check:

* Does each point come from the right paragraph?

* Do I have four genuinely separate points, not the same idea said four ways?

* Have I restructured each sentence, not just swapped a word or two?

* Does the answer address what the question actually asked?
