## Examiner Report Findings

### **Mistakes That Come Up Every Year**

* **Selecting an answer without reading the sentence.** The root cause of most errors in this section. Candidates go straight to the list, pick the idiom that looks most familiar, and write it down. This has been flagged since the earliest available reports (November 2003) and still appears in 2025. The sentence must be read first. The list comes second.

* **Swapping answers between questions.** Several candidates write the correct idiom against the wrong question number, or place the answer for Q6 in the space for Q9 and vice versa. This was observed in 2022 and earlier sessions. Checking that each answer is written against the right number before moving on takes seconds and prevents a straightforward loss of marks.

* **Writing more than one answer without crossing out.** If a candidate changes their mind and writes a second answer without crossing out the first, the question scores zero even if one of the answers is correct. Cross out any answer you are not submitting.

### **What Candidates Who Score Well Actually Do**

* **Read the sentence first, the list second.** This sounds obvious. Most candidates do the opposite. Understanding what the sentence is expressing before looking at the options means the correct answer is often identifiable without needing to compare every item on the list.

* **Confirm meaning, not just familiarity.** The question is not which idiom you recognize. It is which idiom matches what the sentence is saying. Candidates who score full marks treat each option as a candidate to be checked against the sentence, not a word to be chosen just because it looks right.

* **When two options seem similar, go back to the sentence.** The close pairs in the list (idioms that share a theme, proverbs with overlapping meanings) are there deliberately. When you notice two options that could both fit loosely, return to the sentence and decide what it is specifically expressing, then choose on that basis rather than on impression.

* **Write one answer and cross out anything else.** One idiom, one question number, nothing left uncrossed. This is a mechanical habit that prevents a category of mark loss that has nothing to do with knowledge.

* **Build idiomatic knowledge through reading, not lists.** Examiners have said directly across multiple sessions that this question tests cultural knowledge of how Bengali is used. Candidates who score well have encountered these idioms in context, not just memorised definitions. Reading Bengali prose, stories, and non-fiction regularly is the preparation that is recommended for this section. Drilling a vocabulary list of idioms in isolation is less reliable because it does not give the contextual understanding that the sentence-level task requires, however learning these by examples is a great way to cover that gap.
