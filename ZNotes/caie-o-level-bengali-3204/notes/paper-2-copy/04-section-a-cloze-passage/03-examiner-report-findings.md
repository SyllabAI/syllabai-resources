## Examiner Report Findings

### Mistakes That Come Up Every Year

* **Falling for Near-Synonym Distractors:** Candidates frequently pick a word that fits the gap generally but not precisely. The word list is intentionally designed with overlapping meanings, causing loose readers to choose words like **লম্বা** (long/tall) instead of **আকাশচুম্বী** (sky-high), or **তৈরি** (made) / **প্রবাহিত** (flowed) instead of **নির্গত** (emitted).

* **Blind Guessing in Clusters:** Attempting to fill gaps at random, particularly during mid-passage clusters like **Questions 22, 23, and 24**, without reading the text as a whole first.

* **Reusing Words Across Gaps:** Forgetting that each word in the provided list can only be used once, leading to cascading errors across the passage.

* **Running Out of Options at the End:** Mismanaging choices early on, leaving weaker candidates to struggle heavily with **Questions 24 and 25** because they have already used up the words needed to complete the final gaps.

### What Candidates Who Score Well Actually Do

* **Read for Overall Meaning First:** They read the entire passage completely before attempting to fill a single gap, establishing the vital context needed to resolve tricky options.

* **Analyze the Surrounding Sentence Structure:** They read the sentence containing the gap multiple times, checking their selected word against the surrounding text to ensure a precise grammatical and contextual fit.

* **Differentiate Subtle Nuances:** They carefully evaluate the word list to spot deliberate distractors, actively looking for the word that fits *exactly* rather than just *plausibly*.

* **Treat the List as a One-to-One Mapping:** They strategically manage the available choices, ensuring that each word is used exactly once and tracking their options carefully so they don't run out of correct answers by the end of the passage.
