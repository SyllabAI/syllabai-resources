# Vocabulary

## Numbers

* The cardinal numbers (used for counting) are as follows in Spanish:
  * 1 → uno/una
  * 2 → dos
  * 3 → tres
  * 4 → cuatro
  * 5 → cinco
  * 6 → seis
  * 7 → siete
  * 8 → ocho
  * 9 → nueve
  * 10 → diez
  * 11→ once
  * 12 → doce
  * 13 → trece
  * 14 → catorce
  * 15 → quince
* Until 15, you will need to memorize the names. From 16 onwards, the names literally become ‘ten’+’number\[eg: one, two, three..\]
  * 16 → dieciséis
  * 17 → diecisiete
  * 18 → dieciocho
  * 19 → diecinueve
* The multiples of 10 \[eg: 20,30,40…\] all have specific names you will need to memorize
  * 20 → viente
  * 30 → trienta
  * 40 → cuarenta
  * 50 → cincuenta
  * 60 → sesenta
  * 70 → setenta
  * 80 → ochenta
  * 90 → noventa
  * 100 → cien
* Numbers 21-29 follow the same format as 16-19 (names of the numbers are ‘twenty’+’number\[eg: one, two, three..\]
  * 21 → vientiuno
  * 22 → vientidos
  * 23 → vientitres (and so on by adding the literal name for one, two… in front of the tens)
* From 31 onwards, the format becomes generalized for the rest of the numbers. It is \[tens (eg: 30, 40)\] + ‘y’ + \[number (eg: one, two..)\], meaning it is literally ‘thirty and one’, ‘thirty and two’, etc.
  * 31 → trienta y uno
  * 32 → trienta y dos
  * 43 → cuarenta y tres
  * 57 → cincuenta y siete
