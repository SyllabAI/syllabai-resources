## **Delegation and Accountability**

### **Delegation**

Delegation is the process of passing down authority from higher to lower levels in an organization, allowing subordinates to perform tasks and make decisions. It plays a crucial role in organizational structure and employee motivation.

### Key Aspects of Delegation:

* Managers assign tasks to subordinates but retain overall responsibility.

* Employees gain authority to make decisions related to their tasks.

* Wider span of control (more subordinates per manager) leads to more delegation.

**Example:**

* A sales manager with 15 employees cannot personally oversee each sale. Instead, they delegate pricing decisions and client management to their team.

### **Accountability:**

The obligation of the individual to account for and explain their actions and to disclose the results of their work honestly.

**Accountability in Delegation:**

* While tasks and authority are delegated, accountability remains with the subordinate.

* If the job is done poorly, the worker is responsible and may face feedback, training, or discipline.

* Clear guidelines and performance expectations help ensure accountability.

* E.g. If a project deadline is missed due to poor planning, the responsible employee is held accountable by their manager.

**Processes of Accountability:**

Effective accountability in the workplace requires more than simply telling employees to perform well. It involves a structured approach that sets clear expectations, provides necessary support, and ensures fair assessment. The process includes the following **five key steps:**

* **Set Clear Expectations:**

  * Employees should know exactly what is expected of them before they begin a task.

  * Job roles, responsibilities, and objectives must be clearly defined.

  * E.g. A salesperson is given a monthly sales target of $50,000.

* **Ensure Employees Have the Necessary Skills:**

  * Employees must have the appropriate skills and knowledge to complete the task.

  * If necessary, training and development should be provided.

  * E.g. A company introduces a new software system and provides training to employees before expecting them to use it.

* **Establish Two-Way Communication:**

  * Employees should receive regular feedback throughout the task, not just at the end.

  * Open communication allows employees to ask questions and seek guidance when needed.

  * E.g. A manager checks in with a new employee weekly to discuss progress and address challenges.

* **Define Performance Measurements:**

  * Employees must know how their performance will be evaluated (e.g., quality of work, deadlines met, sales targets).

  * This ensures fairness and clarity in performance assessments.

  * E.g. A customer service agent is assessed based on response time, customer satisfaction ratings, and complaint resolution rates.

* **Clarify the Consequences of Performance:**

  * Employees must understand the rewards for good performance (e.g., bonuses, promotions) and the consequences of poor performance (e.g., additional training, warnings).

  * E.g. An employee who exceeds targets may receive a bonus, while consistent underperformance may lead to further training or disciplinary action.
