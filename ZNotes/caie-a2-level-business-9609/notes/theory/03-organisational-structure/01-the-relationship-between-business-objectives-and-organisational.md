# Organizational Structure

## **The relationship between Business objectives and Organisational structure**

An organizational structure is the internal, formal framework that defines how management is organized, how authority is distributed, and how employees are linked within a business. It provides clarity on roles, responsibilities, and decision-making.

### **Without a clear structure, it is difficult to run a firm as we don’t know:**

* Who does which job? (Task allocation).

* Who is responsible for which decisions? (Authority & accountability).

* Who reports to whom? (Hierarchy & chain of command).

### **Having an Organizational Structure Helps in:**

* Clearly defining roles and responsibilities to avoid confusion.

* Ensuring employees understand who to report to.

* Dividing tasks efficiently across teams and departments.

* Improving coordination and decision-making within the company.

### Why does structure of an organization change?

* If the firm expands its operation or wish to raise sales internationally, it may change to geographical divisional structure, which makes managing each region separately easier.

* If firm becomes more efficient through technological advancement, it may adapt flat structure, where it encourages flexibility and outsourcing can be done if deemed necessary.

* If there is leadership changes, then the firm may change to hierarchical structure if the new CEO is autocratic.  This enhances fast decision making, which is helpful in time of crisis. However, if the new CEO is democratic and willing to hear employees, it may change to matrix structure. It promotes cross over of ideas, boosting innovation in the firm.

* If the competition in the industry is too fierce, the firm needs to adapt flat structure in order to be flexible to the dynamic market condition and take advantage of potential opportunities.
