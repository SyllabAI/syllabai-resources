## Product development

* Fast-changing markets **require constant new product development** to stay competitive.
* Without it, **businesses risk selling outdated products.**
* This is **crucial in consumer electronics, cars, cameras, pharmaceuticals, and industrial machinery**, where innovation drives profits and competitiveness.
* **For a new product to succeed, it must:**
  * Offer **desirable features** consumers will pay for.
  * Stand out with a **unique selling point**.
  * Be **marketed effectively** to inform consumers.

### **The process of new product development**

* **NPD includes seven stages:**

### **1) Generating new ideas: sources of new ideas for product development**

* **New product ideas come from various sources:**

  * **R&D Department** – Costly, not all businesses have one.

  * **Competitor Adaptation** – Avoid copyright/patent issues. Similar products may drive consumers to the original.

  * **Market Research** – Focus groups reveal desired products.

  * **Employees** – Generate ideas through product/customer interaction. Boosts motivation.

  * **Sales Employees** – Offer insights from direct consumer contact.

  * **Group Brainstorming** – Collaborative idea generation surpasses individual efforts.

### **2) Idea screening**

* This stage eliminates ideas unlikely to succeed commercially. Developing and marketing new products is costly, so only viable ideas should move forward.

* **Key screening questions include:**

  * How will target consumers benefit?

  * Is manufacturing technically feasible?

  * Will it be profitable at the expected price?

### **3) Concept development and testing**

* This stage refines the product idea by evaluating features, costs, and target consumers.

* **Key points to consider:**

  * Who are the likely consumers?

  * What features and benefits should the product have?

  * How will consumers respond? Use market research to test reactions.

  * What are the most cost-effective manufacturing methods?

  * What will production cost?

### **4) Business analysis**

* This stage evaluates the new product’s impact on costs, sales, and profits. Pricing is set based on customer feedback and competitor data. Sales, market share, and break-even levels are estimated.

* **Key considerations include:**

  * Is financing available for development?

  * Can the product be patented?

  * Does it align with the existing product mix?

  * How might economic changes affect future sales?

### **5) Product testing**

* This stage ensures the product meets technical standards and consumer expectations.

* **Testing includes:**

  * Developing a prototype

  * Testing in typical-use conditions

  * Gathering feedback from focus groups

  * Adapting the product based on results and customer input

### **6) Test marketing**

* A small, representative test market is selected to observe consumer behavior and gather feedback before a full launch. Benefits include:

  * Measuring real consumer responses

  * Reducing risk and poor publicity from product failure

  * Addressing product weaknesses based on feedback

* Limitations include high costs and exposure to competitors.

* If results are poor, products may be withdrawn.

* Cheaper alternatives include distributing free samples for consumer feedback.

### **7) Commercialisation**

* This stage is the full-scale product launch, marking the introduction phase of the product life cycle.

* A promotional strategy informs the market, and distribution channels are stocked to ensure availability.

* The first weeks or months are critical, with marketing closely monitoring sales data.

### **Research and Development**

* Effective R&D is vital for maintaining market leadership and competitive advantage. Firms like Apple, Samsung, Toyota, and Honda have boosted performance through R&D.

### **The importance of R&D**

* Inventions drive new product possibilities, some becoming profitable innovations that help businesses grow and stay competitive.

* Innovative products offer unique selling points, allowing premium pricing and higher margins.

* **However**, R&D is costly and uncertain, with top UK pharmaceutical firms projected to spend over 12 billion dollars in 2020, often on drugs years from market.

* Some businesses avoid R&D, opting to license or copy products, a safer but less imaginative follow-the-leader strategy that risks legal issues.

* **R&D strategies vary:**

  * **Offensive** – Lead with innovation to gain market share and dominance, reinvesting profits into more R&D (e.g., Apple).

  * **Defensive** – Learn from innovators, improving or diversifying products to avoid falling behind.

### **R&D: an evaluation**

* **Not all R&D results in breakthroughs**. For instance, despite billions spent, the drug industry has not found a cure for the common cold.

* Many inventions also fail to reach the market due to design flaws, manufacturing issues, or competition. **Even launched products can fail due to:**

  * Inaccurate market research

  * Poor marketing or pricing

  * Obsolete technology

  * Superior competitor products

* Despite these risks, many businesses continue investing in R&D, believing long-term successes will outweigh short-term failures.

* However, short-term pressures to satisfy shareholders can **reduce R&D investment**.

### **Factors that influence the level of R&D expenditure by a business**

* **Several factors influence R&D investment:**

  * **Industry nature** – Rapid technology changes in sectors like pharmaceuticals, defense, and automotive require substantial R&D.

  * **Competitor spending** – To maintain market share and leadership, businesses often need to match or exceed competitors' R&D budgets.

  * **Business expectations** – Optimism about economic growth and consumer demand encourages higher R&D spending.

  * **Risk profile** – A business’s willingness to invest long-term, rather than focusing on short-term profits, affects R&D budgets.

  * **Government policy** – Grants and tax incentives for R&D influence business decisions.
