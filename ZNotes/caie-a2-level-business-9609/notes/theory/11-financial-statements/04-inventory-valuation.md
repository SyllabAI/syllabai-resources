## Inventory valuation

### **The difficulties of valuing inventory**

* **Changing Costs**: the value of raw materials or goods may go up or down. making it harder to have reliable values.

* **Damaged or Obsolete goods**: Some inventory depreciates or gets damaged, making it harder to know how much it's still worth.

* **Choosing a Method**: businesses must choose a method like FIFO, LIFO, or weighted average. Each gives a different value and affects profits and taxes.

* **Counting Mistakes**: Physical counts of inventory could be in error due to theft, loss, or mistake.

* **Estimates:** Inventory is sometimes estimated instead of being counted, so estimates are inaccurate.

* **Returns and Discounts**: Customer returns or discounts from suppliers make inventory harder to track and estimate accurately.

### **The net realisable value method**

* Inventories are valued on the financial statement at either their **purchase price (historical cost)** or **net realizable value (NRV)**, *whichever is lower*

  * **net realisable value = the amount for which the existing inventory can be sold – cost of selling it**

* It is used on financial statements **only when NRV is below historical cost**

* Consider these examples:

  * **Example 1:** A shoe shop buys ten pairs of shoes at **10 dollars** each. By year-end, three pairs remain unsold and out of fashion, with an estimated sale price of **8 dollars** each, setting their NRV at **24 dollars**. This lower NRV, not the original **30 dollars**, should appear on the financial statement. The conservatism principle dictates recording losses as soon as they are anticipated.

  * **Example 2:** A furniture retailer has a damaged dining table, originally bought for **60 dollars**, that requires a **20 dollar** repair. After repair, the table could sell for **70 dollars**, but it should not be valued at **70 dollars** (assuming profit) or **60 dollars** (ignoring damage). The NRV is 70 - 20 = 50, which is lower than the historical cost and thus becomes its value on the financial statement.
