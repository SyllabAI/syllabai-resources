## **Statement of profit or loss**

### **The meaning of the statement of profit or loss**

* Shows gross profit and profit from operations, detailing how profits are divided between shareholder dividends and retained earnings

* Also known as **income statement** and **profit or loss account**

* A detailed profit or loss statement is created for internal use, providing managers with essential financial information. It should be produced as frequently as managers need information, perhaps once a month

  * A less detailed profit or loss summary is included in published accounts for external users, produced at least annually
  * Each country's legislation defines its content, providing minimum information since published accounts are accessible to competitors. Detailed data could reveal rivals' strengths and weaknesses.

* The profit or loss statements in this chapter are based on the published accounts of public limited companies, with added information where it helps understanding.

### **The purpose of statements of profit or loss**

* **The information in these statements has multiple uses:**
  * Used to measure and compare business performance over time or with other firms, often using ratios
  * Actual profit data can be compared to budgeted profit levels
  * Bankers and creditors use this information to decide on lending
  * Prospective investors assess profit performance to guide share purchases
* **Users of profit data should consider the limitations of published accounting data and the quality of recorded profit.**
  * For instance, high profit from *selling an asset above expected value* is **low-quality and non-recurring**
  * Profit from *unique product designs* is **high-quality**, as it provides ongoing profit potential.

### **The contents of a statement of profit or loss**

A statement of profit or loss has three sections: trading account, profit or loss section, and appropriation account, each providing a different profit figure

### **The Trading account**

* This account shows how **gross profit** (or loss) is generated from the business's trading activities
* It is **crucial** to understand that, since **not all sales are cash transactions**, the revenue figure differs from cash received. The formula for calculating total revenue is:
  * **total revenue = selling price × quantity sold**
* **Revenue is not profit**. Costs are *subtracted* from revenue to calculate profit. There are multiple measures of profit.
* The **cost of sales** figure usually differs from the total value of goods purchased, as some items may be added to or taken from inventories. Only goods used and sold during the year are included in cost of sales.
  * **Gross profit = revenue - cost of sales**
  * **Cost of sales = (opening inventories + purchases) - closing inventories**
* This can be shown as:


|**Details**|**$**|
|:---|:---|
|Opening inventories (at start of the year)|500|
|Purchases during the year|2500|
|Total inventories available for sale|3000|
|Closing inventories (at end of the year)|1000|
|**Cost of sales**|**2000**|

### **Profit or loss account section**

* **This section of the statement of profit or loss has three profit figures:**
  * **Profit from operations (or operating profit):** Calculated by subtracting overhead expenses from gross profit. Overheads are business expenses not tied to production or sales volume, including rent, business rates, management salaries, marketing costs, and depreciation
  * **Profit before tax:** calculated by subtracting interest costs from profit from operations
  * **Profit for the year:** calculated by subtracting profit (corporation) tax from profit before tax/profit after interest

### **Appropriation account**

* This final section of the profit or loss statement shows how annual profit is divided between shareholder dividends and retained earnings

### **Amendment of a statement of profit or loss**

* Accountants often adjust statements when new financial data or key variables change, requiring a revised statement
* **Remember these basic rules when adapting accounts:**
  * Use the same profit or loss statement format shown in the case study.
  * Changes in units produced and sold typically affect both revenue and cost of sales
  * Some overheads, like annual promotion or transport costs, may vary with sales levels

### **The impact of a change on the statement of profit or loss**

* The table below explains the impact on the statement of profit or loss following a change in one of components (assuming no other change occurs):


|**Change**|**Impact on statement of profit or loss**|
|:---|:---|
|**Increase in price**|Revenue, gross profit, profit from operations, profit after interest, profit for the year and retained earnings will increase|
|**Increase in direct cost per unit**|Cost of sales will increase; gross profit, profit from operations, profit before tax/profit after interest and profit for the year will fall|
|**Increase in expenses**|Profit from operations, profit after interest, profit for the year and retained earnings will fall|
|**Reduction in the rate of profit (corporation) tax**|Profit after tax and retained earnings will  increase|
|**Directors decide to increase dividends**|Retained earnings will fall|
