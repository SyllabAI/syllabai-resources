## The Statement of Financial position

### The meaning and purpose of the statement of profit or loss

* The net worth or equity of the company, the difference between its assets and liabilities

* The statement of financial position records a business's net wealth or shareholders’ equity at a specific moment

* In a company, this net wealth belongs to shareholders

* Most businesses aim to grow shareholders' equity by increasing asset value beyond liability growth

* **Shareholders' equity has two main sources:**

  * The first source is the capital invested in the company through share purchases, known as share capital
  * The second source is retained earnings accumulated over time through operations. These are sometimes called reserves, which can be misleading as they do not represent cash reserves

### **Points to note:**

* Companies must publish the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position for the previous financial year for easy comparison. These are not included in the above examples for clarity.
* The titles of both accounts are crucial as they identify the account and the company
* While the statement of profit or loss covers the entire financial year, the statement of financial position reflects the company's value at a specific moment, typically at year-end

### Non-current assets

* Common examples of fixed assets include land, buildings, vehicles, and machinery. These tangible assets have physical existence and typically decline in value over time (this is known as depreciation).

* Businesses can own intangible assets, which are not visible but add value. **For example** patents, trademarks, copyrights, and goodwill.

* **Important points about intangible assets:**

  * Difficult to value
  * According to accepted accounting rules, statements of financial position do not record intangible assets unless acquired through a takeover or merger.
  * For many companies, especially in the knowledge-based economy, intangible assets are the primary source of future earnings, including those in scientific research, publishing, music, and well-known brands
  * The market value of companies with significant intangible assets often exceeds their net asset value

* Intangible assets like patents and copyrights are referred to as intellectual capital. They enhance a business's market value by increasing the total value of its assets

* The reputation and prestige of an established business add value beyond its physical assets, known as goodwill. Goodwill typically appears on a statement of financial position only when a company acquires another at a cost exceeding its net assets. It usually doesn't show on accounts because business reputation can diminish quickly, such as during product safety scares

* Disputes can arise among accountants regarding the valuation of intangible assets, such as well-known brand names. There is room to adjust the value of these and other intangibles on the statement of financial position to better reflect the company’s status. This practice is a form of window dressing that can compromise the objectivity of published accounts.

### Current assets

* Current assets are crucial for a business as they significantly impact liquidity
* Examples include inventories, trade receivables (debtors who purchased on credit), and cash/bank balances

### Current liabilities

* #### Examples include trade payables (credit from suppliers), bank overdrafts, unpaid dividends, and unpaid taxes

### Net current assets

* Net current assets = current assets - current liabilities

### Net assets

* Net assets is the difference between total asset and total liability

### Non-current liabilities

* Non-current liabilities are long-term loans owed by the business, payable over more than one year
* **Examples** include loans, commercial mortgages, and debentures
* The ratio of non-current liabilities to total capital employed is a key measure of the risk undertaken by the company's management

### Shareholders’ equity

* This is sometimes called shareholders’ funds or equity
* It includes the capital initially invested by shareholders (share capital) plus retained earnings, also known as reserves
  * **reserves = shareholders' equity - share capital**
* Other reserves may appear on the statement of financial position if a company believes its fixed assets have increased in value (revaluation reserve) or if it sells shares above their nominal value (share premium reserve). Shareholders’ equity represents the permanent capital of the business and is not repaid to shareholders unless the company ceases trading, unlike loans, which are repaid to creditors.
* A common misunderstanding about reserves is that they are cash reserves available for financing. They are not. Retained earnings result from profits not distributed as taxes or dividends and have already been reinvested in the business through asset purchases. Therefore, retained earnings are not liquid funds. The only cash available in the business is shown under ‘cash’ in the current assets section.

### **Amendments to statements of financial position**

* When preparing a new statement of financial position, it is to common start with the previous year-end statement and make necessary amendments.
* The table below shows some of the possible amendments and effect on statement of financial position
* A balancing double-entry adjustment is then needed to ensure it balances (total assets = shareholders' equity + liabilities)


|**Cause of amendment**|**Impact on statement of financial position**|**Balancing entry in the account**|
|:---|:---|:---|
|Sale of inventories for cash for the same price as valued in the accounts|Value of inventories falls|Cash balance increases|
|Sale of inventories for cash at a higher price than valued in the accounts|Value of inventories falls|Cash balance increases. Shareholders’ equity  increases by the value of the profit recorded|
|Depreciation of equipment|Value of non-current assets falls|Shareholders’ equity falls as the company is ‘worth less’ than before|
|Intangible assets, such as goodwill, are re-valued|Value of non-current assets increases|Value of shareholders’ equity increases|
|Trade payables ask for immediate payment|Value of trade payables falls|Cash balance falls|
|Additional shares are sold and the share capital raised is used to buy property|Value of share capital increases under shareholders’ equity|Value of property in non-current assets increases|

### **The relationships between items in the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position**

* Though these two are separate financial accounts, they have important links


|**Change on statement of profit or loss**|**Change on statement of financial position**|
|:---|:---|
|Retained earnings are made|Shareholder equity increases. Cash increases (if retained earnings are in cash form)|
|Loss is made|Shareholder equity decreases. Cash falls or non-current liabilities will rise to make up the loss|
|Non-current assets are depreciated, recorded as an expense|Value of non-current assets decreases. Shareholder equity decreases|
|Value of closing inventory is reduced as it is out-of-date; increasing cost of sales. Gross profit and retained profit decreases|Inventory valuation decreases. Shareholder equity decreases|
