## Depreciation

* **Assets decline in value for two main reasons:**
  * Normal wear and tear
  * Technological obsolescence

### **Role of depreciation in the accounts**

* Nearly all fixed/non-current assets depreciate over time, so recording each year's depreciation cost on the annual income statement seems reasonable. This approach addresses both issues mentioned above.
  * Assets retain some value on the financial statement until fully depreciated or sold, shown as net book value
    * **NBV = original cost - accumulated depreciation**
  * Profits are adjusted by each year's depreciation, ensuring accurate profit figure

### **How depreciation is calculated: the straight line method of depreciation**

* There are multiple ways to calculate depreciation, however, for this course, you only need to understand the **straight line method** of calculating depreciation. This method's title reflects how depreciation is calculated.

  * Annual depreciation is calculated by this formula:
    $\frac{\text{original cost of asset - expected residual value}}{\text{expected useful lie of asset (years)}}$

* **To calculate annual depreciation, you need:**

  * The asset’s original cost
  * The expected useful life
  * The estimated residual value at the end of its life.

* **Example:** A business buys three computers at **3000 dollars** each, with an expected lifespan of four years and an estimated residual value of **200 dollars** per computer. Using straight-line depreciation, the annual depreciation charge will be:

  $\frac{\text{9000-600}}{\text{4}}$ = $\frac{\text{8400}}{\text{4}}$ = 2100

* Each year for the four-year lifespan, a depreciation charge of **2100 dollars** should be recorded as overhead expenses on the profit and loss statement. On the financial statement, this amount is deducted from the computers’ value. After four years, each computer will be valued at **200 dollars**. The table below shows the decline in net book value over this period.


|**Year**|**Annual depreciation charge**|**NBV of the three computers**|
|:---|:---|:---|
|Present|0|$9000|
|1|$2100|$6900|
|2|$2100|$4800|
|3|$2100|$2700|
|4|$2100|$600|

* If the computers are sold at the end of the fourth year for more than their residual value, a profit is recorded on the profit and loss statement. For example, if sold for **900 dollars**, the business gains **300 dollars**. However, if scrapped due to obsolescence, a **600 dollars** loss on disposal is recorded.

### **Straight line depreciation: an evaluation**

* The straight-line method of depreciation is easy to calculate and understand, making it popular among limited companies. You can find this method commonly used in the annual accounts of public companies
* However, it relies on estimates for life expectancy and residual value, which can lead to inaccurate depreciation charges if incorrect
* Additionally, assets like cars, trucks, and computers often depreciate more rapidly in the first two years, a trend not captured by the straight-line method, which applies a constant annual charge
* Lastly, as assets age, repair and maintenance costs typically increase, reducing profitability, but this is not reflected in the fixed depreciation charge of the straight-line method

### **Impact of depreciation and the financial statement**

* **The annual depreciation charge impacts the main financial statements in the following ways:**
  * As a business expense, it reduces profit from operations on the statement of profit or loss
  * It decreases the net book value of non-current assets, leading to a lower value on the statement of financial position
