## Labor Productivity, Absenteeism, and Measurement of Employee's Performance

### **Labor Productivity**

Labor productivity is output per employee per hour.

* ### It increases when output per worker rises.

**How to raise labor productivity:**

* Higher employee motivation and effort.

* Using capital equipment more efficiently.

* Improving employees through training.

* Greater worker involvement in problem-solving.

* Enhancing operational efficiency. Example: Avoiding inventory shortage.

**Measurement of productivity in different industries:**

* Transport: Kilometers per driver per year.

* Hotels: Guest nights per employee per year.

* IT & Finance: Revenue per employee per year.

* Manufacturing: Output per employee per hour.

* Retail: Sales per employee per year.

**Why it is more challenging to measure productivity in service industries:**

* Harder to improve productivity than in manufacturing.

* Luxury hotels need constant staff availability, limiting cost cutting.

* IT can boost efficiency in support services like booking and billing.

### **Absenteeism:**

**How to Measure Absenteeism:**

Number of days of worker’s absence/total no. of workers x 100.

It can also be calculated individually for each employee.

**Impact of Absenteeism on Businesses:**

* Disrupts operations, especially in consumer services.

* Poor service due to absence can drive customers away.

* Covering absences is costly (extra hires or overtime).

* Absenteeism as a Motivation Indicator:

* Motivated employees avoid unnecessary absences.

**How Herzberg’s Hygiene Factors Matter in Case of Absenteeism:**

* Poor working conditions? It can be related to illness.

* Over-supervision? It can cause stress.

**How HRM Can Deal With Absenteeism To Reduce It:**

* Identify the cause (health, family issues, or low motivation).

* Support employees with genuine concerns.

* Take disciplinary action if absenteeism is due to laziness or a second job.

### **Other ways to Measure Employee’s Performance:**

* Wastage Rates: Proportion of materials wasted/proportion of total output damaged.

* Quality Levels & Reject Rates: Percentage of output that is unacceptable.

* Consumer Complaints: Complaints as a proportion of total customers served.

* Performance Evaluation: Comparing individual performance against pre-set targets/SMART criteria.
