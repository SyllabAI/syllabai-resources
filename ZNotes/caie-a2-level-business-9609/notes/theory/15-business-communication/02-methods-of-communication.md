## **Methods of communication**

### **Spoken communication:**

Conveying message by word of mouth.

* One-on-one conversations, interviews, meetings

* Allows two-way communication and feedback

* Improves worker motivation

* Message can be reinforced with body language

* May be ambiguous

* No written record

* Costly, time taking

### **Written communication:**

Any kind of message that utilizes written words to convey.

* Letters, memos, notices

* Can be referred to whenever needed

* Allows detailed data to be shared

* No immediate feedback

### Advantages and Disadvantages of Written Communication

#|
||

Advantages

|

Disadvantages

||
||

Written messages can be read multiple times, helping the receiver understand better.

|

Body language or non-verbal cues cannot be used to support the message.

||
||

It allows detailed information, like numbers and diagrams, to be shared.

|

Feedback cannot be given right away, so it takes longer to clear up misunderstandings.

||
||

It creates a permanent record, which can be useful for legal purposes (e.g., employment contracts).

|

There is proof the message was sent, but not that it was received or understood.

### **Electronic communication:**

Conveying messages through the means of internet.

* Emails, website, blog, text messaging, voicemail and video messaging.

* Quick

* Written record (might not be if it is video related).

* Need workers to be trained

* Reduces social contact, interpersonal contact is lost

* Security issues

* Information overload

### Advantages and Disadvantages of Electronic Communication

#|
||

Advantages

|

Disadvantages

||
||

Messages can be sent very quickly using electronic media, often in just a few seconds.

|

Employees may need training to use electronic communication, and younger employees are often better at it than older ones.

||
||

The internet allows global communication, reaching millions of people worldwide.

|

It reduces face-to-face interaction, which can make people feel isolated and miss social connections.

||
||

After paying for equipment and training, electronic communication is cheap. For example, SMS is cheaper than sending letters, and video calls save money on travel.

|

Employees might waste work time sending personal messages.

||
||

Feedback can be exchanged instantly with electronic communication.

|

Direct personal contact is lost, and body language cannot be used to support the message.

||
||

Managers can manage large or global businesses more easily, especially multinational companies.

|

Security and technical issues, like viruses, can be a problem, so backups are often needed.

||
||

Emails can serve as legal proof of contracts and transactions in most countries.

|

Too many messages, like emails, can cause information overload, leading to stress and important messages being overlooked.

### **Visual communication:**

Displaying information and ideas to the receiver.

* Accompany oral or written

* Diagrams, pictures, whiteboards

* Increased impact, color and movements

* Useful during training, marketing

### Advantages and Disadvantages of Visual Communication

#|
||

Advantages

|

Disadvantages

||
||

It works well for people who are not fluent in the language, especially in international communication.

|

Creating visual content, such as training videos, can be expensive and take a lot of time.

||
||

Visuals like graphs, pictures, and diagrams make spoken messages more impactful and easier to understand.

|

It cannot replace written communication when detailed technical or financial data is needed.

||
||

Complex information, like an organizational chart, is easier to explain visually.

|

Visual communication does not include body language or other non-verbal cues.

||
||

Messages presented visually are often quicker to understand than long pages of text.

|

Dramatic visuals might distract the audience from the main message.
