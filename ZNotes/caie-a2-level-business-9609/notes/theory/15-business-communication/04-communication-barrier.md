## **Communication Barrier:**

* Reasons why communication can fail.

### **Possible Communication Barriers**

#|
||

Category

|

Description

||
||

Inappropriate Method

|

Using the wrong method, like explaining complex details over a phone, can lead to misunderstandings.

||
||

Forgetfulness

|

Long verbal messages might be forgotten; a written version is better.

||
||

Misleading or Incomplete Messages

|

Vague messages can be misinterpreted, like "send soon" meaning different things.

||
||

Technical Language

|

Using too much technical language can confuse the receiver, especially if it's not translated.

||
||

Information Overload

|

Too much information can be confusing and prevent understanding of important points.

||
||

Long Communication Channels

|

Messages through long chains can be slow and get distorted or changed.

||
||

Poor Attitudes

|

If the sender is not trusted, or if workers are unmotivated, communication will suffer.

Intermediaries might not pass on messages correctly if they are poorly motivated.

If the sender has a poor opinion of the receiver, they might not ensure the message is clear.

||
||

Physical Reasons

|

Noisy environments and geographical distance can make communication difficult.

Modern electronic methods like videoconferencing can help overcome geographical distance issues.

||
|#

### How To Overcome Communication Barriers

#|
||

Best practice

|

Description

||
||

Clear and Precise Messages

|

Ensure the message is clear, precise, and adequately detailed to avoid misunderstandings.

||
||

Short Communication Channels

|

Keep the communication channel as short as possible to prevent delays and distortions.

||
||

Understanding Communication Channels

|

Make sure all involved parties understand the channels of communication being used.

||
||

Feedback Mechanism

|

Include feedback in the communication process to quickly identify and resolve any issues with receiving or understanding the message.

||
||

Establishing Trust

|

Build trust between senders and receivers by valuing and encouraging all staff contributions.

||
||

Appropriate Physical Conditions

|

Ensure the physical environment is suitable for messages to be received or heard, especially in oral communication.

||
