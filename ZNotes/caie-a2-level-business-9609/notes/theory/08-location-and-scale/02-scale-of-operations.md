## Scale of operations

### **Factors that influence the scale of a business**

1. **Owner’s objectives and goals:** owners may choose to maintain the business at a manageable scale or pursue aggressive expansion.

2. **Access to finance/capital:** access to finance of a business determines its ability to expand.

3. **Market size and demand:** businesses that are in niche markets may remain small intentionally.

4. **Economies of scale:** firms may grow to exploit economies of scale.

5. **Skills and capacity of management:** if a business lacks managerial expertise it may limit growth potential.

6. **Legal constraints and regulation:** regulations, licensing, and other laws may limit expansion.

7. **Technology:** Use of more advanced technology can help scale up the business.

### **Internal Economies of Scale:**

Factors that cause reductions in unit (average) costs of production as the business expands its scale of operations.

### Types of internal economies of scale:

**Purchasing (Bulk Buying) Economies of Scale:**

* Large firms receive discounts on bulk orders because suppliers save on processing and delivery costs.

* Big firms hire specialist buyers to source materials at the lowest prices, often globally.

* E.g. Supermarkets like Smart Bazaar or Costco negotiate lower prices from suppliers due to their high-volume purchases.

**Technical Economies of Scale:**

* Large firms can afford expensive, advanced machinery and automated production lines.

* High production levels spread fixed costs over more units, reducing per-unit costs.

  E.g. Car manufacturers like Toyota and BYD use robotic assembly lines to improve efficiency.

**Financial Economies of Scale:**

* Large businesses get loans at lower interest rates due to their established reputation.

* Public companies raise capital more cheaply through share issuance since high fixed costs (legal fees, prospectus, etc.) are spread over large amounts.

  E.g. Companies like Samsung and Microsoft easily secure low-interest loans due to their strong financial position.

**Marketing Economies of Scale:**

* Advertising and promotional costs are spread over a higher volume of sales.

* Large firms negotiate better deals with advertising agencies.

  E.g. Pepsi and McDonald's invest in global marketing, reducing per-unit advertising costs.

**Managerial Economies of Scale:**

* Large firms can hire specialized managers for finance, HR, marketing, etc., improving efficiency.

* Small businesses often rely on general managers, who handle multiple tasks less efficiently.

* E.g. Amazon has specialized teams for logistics, customer service, and AI-driven operations, optimizing efficiency.

### Internal Diseconomies of Scale:

As businesses grow beyond a certain size, they may experience diseconomies of scale, where unit costs start to rise due to management and operational inefficiencies. These diseconomies prevent some industries from being dominated by just a few massive firms.

### How to Reduce Diseconomies of Scale:

**Management By Objective (MBO):**

* Setting clear and aligned objectives for all departments ensures coordination.

* Employees understand how their work contributes to company goals, improving motivation.

**Decentralization:**

* Granting autonomy to divisions allows them to operate independently while still aligning with overall strategy.

* Decisions are made by managers with hands-on experience, improving efficiency.

**Rationalization of Product Line/Reducing Diversification:**

* Businesses focusing on core activities can reduce complexity and coordination challenges.

* Demerger Strategy: it is when a company splits into two or more parts, which enhances strategic focus of the separated entities as it decreases bureaucracy, allowing faster decision-making. It results in higher managerial efficiency.

### External Economies of Scale:

While internal economies of scale arise within a company, external economies of scale occur when businesses benefit from the overall growth of their industry in a particular region.

### Economies and Diseconomies of Scale and Their Impact on Unit Costs

The relationship between economies of scale, diseconomies of scale, and unit costs is complex and does not have a precise turning point.

![](../../../assets/8bb269717d35-allimagesa5c7b2d63f1436bfd1514468.45)

### The cost curve follows three key phases:

1. **Economies of Scale Phase (Decreasing Unit Costs)**

* As a business expands, average (unit) costs fall due to factors like bulk purchasing, better specialization, and efficient technology.

* This phase benefits businesses with high fixed costs, such as automobile manufacturers or software firms.

2. **Optimal Scale (Lowest Unit Cost Point)**

* At some level of output, the business achieves minimum average cost (MES - Minimum Efficient Scale).

* Expansion beyond this point starts introducing inefficiencies.

3. **Diseconomies of Scale Phase (Increasing Unit Costs)**

* Beyond a certain size, the company faces coordination problems, communication inefficiencies, and rising labor costs, leading to higher average costs.
