# Location and Scale

## Location

### Optimal Location:

A business location that gives the best combination of **quantitative** and **qualitative** factors.

* Quantitative factors: Issues that managers should consider that can be measured in financial or numerical terms.

* Qualitative factors: Issues that managers should consider that are not measurable in financial or numerical terms.

**Key aspects of Location Decision:**

* Strategic Nature: Long-term impact on the entire business.

* Irreversibility: Relocation is costly and difficult to reverse.

* Top-Level Decision: Made by senior management, not delegated.

### **Factors determining Location and Relocation Decisions**

### Quantitative Factors

### **When businesses decide on a location or consider relocation, they analyze various quantitative factors to ensure profitability and efficiency. These factors include:**

**Site and Fixed Costs:**

* Costs of purchasing or renting land and buildings differ by region and country.

* "Greenfield" sites (undeveloped land) may require significant initial investment, whereas developed sites might need adaptation costs.

**Labour Costs:**

* Labour-intensive businesses (e.g., call centers) prioritize areas with lower wages.

* Capital-intensive industries (e.g., Flow production related operation such as Candy products) are less concerned with wage costs.

* Offshoring occurs when businesses relocate to countries with cheaper labour.

**Transport Costs:**

* Raw material-heavy industries (e.g., steel plants) prefer locations near suppliers to minimize costs.

* Businesses whose products increase in bulk prefer sites near customers.

* Service-based businesses (hotels, retail stores) focus on customer accessibility rather than transport costs.

**Break-even Analysis:**

* Helps compare the output needed to cover costs at different locations.

* Lower break-even output = lower financial risk.

* Break-even analysis has its limitations, as it assumes constant costs and sales.

**Government Grants/Subsidies:**

* Many governments provide financial incentives to attract businesses.

* Grants can lower relocation costs, especially in high-unemployment areas.

* Drawback is that it can also lead to locating at a non-optimal location to receive the grant.

**Profit Estimates:**

* Businesses compare expected revenues and costs for different locations.

* A higher profit potential may not always justify a significantly higher capital investment.

**Investment Appraisal:**

* Methods like Net Present Value (NPV) and Payback Period help assess long-term returns.

* However, future cost and revenue estimates are uncertain.

**Potential Revenue Impact:**

* Location influences customer perception, affecting sales and pricing.

  High-end businesses may prefer prestigious locations (e.g., Park Lane Street, Wall Street).

### Qualitative Factors

Quantitative factors focus on measurable costs and revenues, qualitative factors influence location decisions based on non-financial considerations. These include:

**Safety:**

* Some industries (e.g., chemical plants, nuclear power stations) pose risks to public health and safety.

* Such businesses may be required to operate in remote locations, increasing transport costs.

**Expansion Opportunities:**

* Relocating can be costly and disruptive, so businesses prefer locations with room for future growth.

* If a site is too small, long-term expansion may be challenging.

**Managers' Preferences:**

* Small business owners may choose locations based on personal lifestyle choices (e.g., living near the workplace).

* In large organizations, shareholder returns take priority over personal preferences.

**Ethical Considerations:**

* Relocating may lead to job losses, damaging the company's reputation.

* Moving to a country with weak labor laws may attract criticism from pressure groups and stakeholders.

**Infrastructure Quality:**

* Transport and communication networks impact business efficiency.

* Bad quality of such leads to late delivery, lengthier production process, delay in communication etc.

* For example, Shipping firms prefer locations with major ports (e.g., Singapore).

* Tech companies need high-speed internet and reliable power.

* E-commerce businesses may prefer warehouses over retail spaces due to online shopping growth.

**Planning Restrictions:**

* Governments regulate business locations through zoning laws and industrial development policies.

* Some regions create industrial estates and business parks to attract companies while maintaining environmental balance.

### **Differences Between Local, National, and International Location.**

### Local Location Decisions:

* Typically involve small businesses with a single branch or office.

* Often located near the owner's home or within their residence.

* Influenced by personal preference, convenience, and community demand.

* Examples: Independent hairdressers, small retail shops, freelance businesses.

### National Location Decisions:

* Involve businesses expanding across multiple regions within a country.

* Key factors include site costs, customer accessibility, and regional market size.

* Common among supermarket chains, retail franchises, and service providers.

* Examples: Walmart (within a country), national bank chains, Sim companies.

### International Location Decisions:

* Involve multinational businesses operating across different countries.

* Driven by factors like cost advantages, tax benefits, supply chain efficiencies, and market expansion.

* Common in manufacturing, retail, and outsourcing industries.

* Examples: Samsung, Nike, Toyota.

### **Benefits of Multi-site Location:**

**Better Market Reach:**

* Expanding into multiple locations allows businesses to serve a larger customer base.

* Retailers, banks, and service providers benefit from increased accessibility.

**Risk Reduction:**

* Businesses in multiple locations avoid over-reliance on a single site.

* Useful for primary industries like mining, where resource depletion is a risk.

**Economies of Scale:**

* Large companies can distribute production across multiple sites to lower costs.

* E.g. Global manufacturers setting up plants in different regions to reduce transportation costs.

**Customer Convenience:**

* Banks, supermarkets, and service providers expand to offer localized services.

* E.g. Starbucks opening branches in various cities for easy customer access.

**Adaptation to Local Markets:**

* E.g. McDonald's adjusts its menu to suit local tastes in different countries.

* Businesses can tailor products or services based on regional customer preferences.

### The Reasons For and Impact of Offshoring and Reshoring

### **Offshoring**

The relocation of a business process from one country to another country.

### Reasons for Offshoring:

**Lower cost:**

* Companies relocate operations to countries with lower wages to remain competitive.

* AMEX offshored customer service to Philippines.

* Ford offshored IT services to India.

* Tommy Hilfiger offshored manufacturing services to Bangladesh.

**Supply of highly qualified Workforce:**

* Countries investing in education and workforce training benefit most from offshoring.

* India and China provide a skilled workforce at lower costs for IT and customer service industries.

* Bangladesh provides trade skilled workers for jobs in construction.

**Access to Global Market:**

* Expanding to emerging markets allows businesses to increase sales.

* Facebook expanded to India to access its mass population.

**Avoiding Protectionist Trade Barriers:**

* Setting up operations inside a country can help businesses bypass tariffs and trade restrictions.

* Toyota built a factory in France to avoid trade barriers on Japanese car imports.

**Government Financial Support:**

* Some governments offer incentives such as tax breaks and subsidies to attract foreign businesses.

**Overcoming Exchange Rate Fluctuations:**

* Producing goods in the same country where they are sold reduces the risk of price instability caused by currency fluctuations.

### **Reshoring**

Transferring a business operation that was moved overseas back to the country where it was originally located.

### Reasons for Reshoring:

**Language and Communication Barriers:**

* Offshore operations can suffer from miscommunication due to language differences.

* Lack of face-to-face contact makes coordination more challenging.

**Cultural Differences:**

* Different work cultures may not align with business operations.

* Toyota struggled in Mexico because its teamwork-based system clashed with Mexican workers' independent work style

**Product Quality and Service Concerns:**

* Customer service (e.g., call centers) and technical support may be negatively impacted by time zones, language barriers, and service inconsistencies.

* Some companies have faced backlash due to poor offshore customer support.

**Supply Chain Risks:**

* Offshore suppliers may not meet quality or delivery expectations.

* Zara keeps production local to ensure fast delivery and maintain high quality in its "fast fashion" model.

**Ethical Considerations:**

* Offshoring can lead to job losses in the home country, causing consumer backlash.

* Companies sourcing from factories with poor labor conditions risk negative publicity.

* Chocolate companies often face criticism for conducting deals with cacao beans firms known for poor work condition and wage.

**Overdependence on International Supply Chain:**

* COVID-19 disruptions highlighted risks of relying on offshore suppliers.

* Companies are reshoring to avoid future supply chain failures.

### **The impact of Globalisation on Location and Relocation decisions**

* **Globalisation** is the process by which the world’s economies, businesses, and cultures become increasingly interconnected and interdependent.

* **Globalisation** can affect where business decide to locate or relocate in many ways:

  1. **Access to larger markets:** firms may relocate production or open offices closer to major international markets to reduce delivery times and adapt to local preferences.

  2. **Lower production Costs:** globalisation increases access to countries with cheaper labour, raw materials, or lower taxes acting as an incentive to relocate manufacturing to reduce costs.

  3. **Economies of Scale:** producing for global demand rather than local demand encourages larger scale facilities in more strategic locations to achieve economies of scale.

  4. **Risk diversification:** firms may spread operations across multiple countries to reduce dependence on any single market or region, reducing risk.

  5. **Competition:** access to local skills, cultural understanding, and compliance with regulations influences location decisions.

  6. **Trade agreements and free trade zones:** trade blocs (EU, ASEAN, NAFTA) encourage businesses to locate within member countries to benefit from tariff-free access.
