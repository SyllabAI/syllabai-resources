## Social and Demographic

### **Corporate Social Responsibility**

* When a business accepts its legal and moral responsibilities to all stakeholders, not just investors, it shows **corporate social responsibility (CSR)**
* How the business decisions impact society and the environment identifies the importance of CSR for the business

### **CSR and Accounting Practices**

* Is it **socially responsible** to report misleading profits to convince shareholders a company is a good investment? Is it **socially responsible** to inflate a business's value to encourage lenders to give loans?

* These practices, and other methods of making accounts appear more favourable, are known as **"accounting window dressing"**

* The common view is that deliberately distorting a company's profitability or value to mislead is **socially irresponsible and should be illegal**

### **CSR and Social Auditing**

* There is increasing demand for businesses to report annually how socially responsible they have been.
* An annual social report indicates the social impact of a business over a period and is called **social audits**. They usually include
  * Health and safety record
  * Pollution levels
  * Contributions to local community events and charities
  * Proportion of ethically sourced supplies
  * Employee benefit schemes
  * Customer and supplier feedback on the business's ethical practices
  * Annual social responsibility targets


|Benefits|Limitations|
|:---|:---|
|Identifies what social responsibilities the business is meeting and what needs to be achieved|May not be taken seriously by stakeholders|
|Managers can set targets for social performance|Costly and requires time|
|Improved public image|Some consumers may not care about the social responsibility of a business and only want cheap goods|

### **Why Businesses need to Consider Community Needs**

* Improved public image, more attractive to consumers and investors

* Increased likelihood that the community accepts business decisions like expansion or relocation

* Increased chances of government grants and subsidies

* Reduced risk of negative action taken by **pressure groups**

### **Pressure Groups**

A **pressure group** is an organisation created by people with a common interest or aim who put pressure on businesses and governments to change policies so that an objective that seeks to influence elected officials to take action or make a change on a specific issue

**International examples of pressure groups**

* **Greenpeace:** campaigns for greater environmental protection by businesses adopting greener strategies and governments implementing stricter anti-pollution laws.

* **Fairtrade Foundation:** tries to achieve a better deal for agricultural producers in low-income countries

* **Amnesty International:** supports human rights, especially in countries where these are at risk

* **Extinction Rebellion:** encourages demonstrations to force governments to combat climate change

**Pressure groups want to change in three areas**

* Business policies that, for example, harm the environment

* Consumer spending habits, so businesses with proper policies face increased sales and those that do not see sales fall

* Government policies and laws to support the aims of the group

**Pressure groups try to reach these aims in multiple ways**

* **Publicity through Media Coverage:** using press releases and coverage of events like meetings and demonstrations to induce bad publicity for targeted companies so that they have a greater chance of changing their policies

* **Influencing consumer behaviour:** if the pressure group influences consumers to stop buying a company's products for long enough, then there is greater chance of the company changing its policies

* **Lobbying of government:** This means presenting their arguments to government officials to push for legal changes. If a campaign impacts the government's popularity, it increases the likelihood of policy changes.

### **Demographic Changes**

**The size and structure of the population can change at**

* Local level

* National level

* Global level

**Recent changes in global social and demographics include**

* An ageing population in several high-income countries

* Change in the role of women

* Better education facilities and opportunities, improving literacy

* Earlier retirement in numerous high-income countries

* Increasing divorce rates

* Job insecurity

### **Social and Demographic Influences on Business Activity**

**An Ageing Population**

* A larger part of the population is over retirement age

* A smaller proportion is below 25 years of age

* Increasing number of dependents on social benefits, resulting in greater tax burden on the working population

**The influences on the business of such changes are most obvious in two ways:**

* **Changing patterns of demand:** Older consumers demand different types of products. Market research will need to be conducted to forecast changes in demand for products as a result of the ageing population.

* **Age structure of the workforce:** Due to the decreasing number of young employees available, workforce planning may require catering to older workers or retaining workers for longer than usual. Younger staff are generally more adaptable and easier to train in new technologies, while older workers often bring loyalty and valuable experience that can enhance customer service.

### **Patterns of Employment**

* **The main features of changing employment patterns in many countries are**

  * Transfer of labour from secondary to tertiary sector

  * Transfer of labour from old established industries to new hi-tech industries

  * The number of women employed is increasing

  * Increase in part-time employment

  * Learner employment on a part-time basis is rising. Some industries are staffed vastly by learners and part-timers

  * Increase in temporary and flexible employment contracts

  * Flexible work patterns are more common, which can benefit both employer and employee

  * The ageing population increases the dependency ratio

  * More women tend to stay in full-time employment for extended periods of time

  * More women taking maternity leaves and then returning to work

  * Several countries are multicultural, affecting the pattern of women at work.

  * The percentage of women seeking full-time employment in the UK has risen to 75%
