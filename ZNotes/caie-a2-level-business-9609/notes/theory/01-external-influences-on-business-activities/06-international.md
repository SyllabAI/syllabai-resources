## **International**

### International Influences

All countries participate in international trade. The opportunities that it presents mean that a rising percentage of businesses use imported materials or export products, or even both.

**Influence and Significance of International Trade**

* The expansion of global trade has increased significantly in recent years.
* The significant trade growth between countries like China, the USA, and the EU has greatly impacted their economic development
* Countries can also build enhanced business, political, and social links through trading together
* **International trade** can have drawbacks that governments must consider. Selective support may be necessary for firms and groups most affected.

**These Drawbacks Include:**

* Domestic firms being unable to compete effectively with imports may face loss of output and jobs
* Imports may lead to a decline in domestic industries producing essential goods, putting the country at risk in case of conflicts or disruptions in import supply
* Shifting from goods that cannot compete with imports to those with comparative advantage may take a long time, leading to job losses and factory closures before other production increases
* New businesses may struggle to survive against established importers, preventing **'infant industries'** from growing domestically
* Some importers may **'dump'** goods below cost to drive out domestic competition
* If the value of imports exceeds the value of exports for several years, it may lead to a loss of foreign exchange

### **Effect of international trade agreements**

* Recent international trade agreements have significantly reduced protectionism, with common trade barriers including tariffs, quotas, and voluntary export limits

* **Recent shifts towards free international trade with less protectionism have been driven by:**

  * **The World Trade Organization (WTO):** countries dedicated to removing trade restrictions through negotiated agreements

  * **Free-trade blocs:** A group of countries that trade freely with each other but impose trade barriers on others to gain a competitive advantage. For example: ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations) and the European Union (EU)

* **Benefits of international trade**

  * International trade widens consumer choice by providing access to products that may not be available domestically due to the production facilities not existing

  * Same principle applies to raw materials

  * Imports of raw materials can speed up industrialisation in developing economies

  * Import competition encourages domestic industries to lower costs, keep prices down, and produce higher quality goods

  * Countries can specialise in products they can produce most efficiently and import those they produce less efficiently, known as comparative advantage

  * Specialisation can result in economies of scale, leading to lower costs and prices

  * Some imports are cheaper than domestically produced alternatives

  * Living standards should increase for consumers in all trading countries

### **The role of technology in international trade**

* Improved communication through the internet has significantly boosted international trade. Future growth will be further driven by:

  * **Blockchain:** Speeds up finance arrangements and reduces the cost of trade finance

  * **Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning:** Used to identify the most cost-effective shipping routes, manages port traffic, and translates e-commerce queries

  * **New Digital Platforms:** Connect service providers with global customers, transforming international trade in services

  * **Mobile Payments:** Technologies like Apple Pay and M-Pesa enable online purchases, particularly in developing countries with weak banking systems, allowing low-income groups to become global consumers

### **Multinational businesses and relationships with government**

* A multinational business is not just an importer or exporter. It has its headquarters in one country and operations in several countries

* The largest multinationals generate revenues greater than many countries' economies.

* The size and influence of multinationals can pose challenges for governments. Many have headquarters in Western Europe or the USA but operate in less-developed countries. When cutting costs, jobs in head office countries are often the last to go

* Host countries must carefully compare the benefits versus drawbacks of multinational operations

### **Reasons to become a multinational**

* It brings the business closer to its main market, lowering transport costs and better market information about consumer taste

* Lower production costs due to cheaper wages, rent, and fewer government restrictions.

* Avoid import restrictions by producing locally.

* Gain access to natural resources unavailable in the home country.

### **Possible problems for multinationals**

* Poor communication with headquarters

* Language, legal and culture difference makes communication difficult

* Hard to coordinate with other multinational plants

* Skill level of local employees may be low, requiring significant training investment
