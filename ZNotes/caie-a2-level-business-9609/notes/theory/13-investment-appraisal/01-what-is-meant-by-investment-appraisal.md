
# Investment Appraisal

## The concept of investment appraisal

* Investment is the purchase of capital goods in expectation of future profits. All businesses make investment decisions, involving strategic decisions like relocation or adopting new technology.
* **Investment appraisal** assesses an investment decision's profitability, typically using quantitative techniques to compare future returns to costs. Managers also consider nonfinancial factors through qualitative appraisal.
* Business investments involve capital expenditure as an initial cash outflow, with expected cash inflows over the asset’s useful life. Quantitative appraisal methods compare these outflows to expected inflows.

### Quantitative investment appraisal: what information is necessary?

* **When appraising the profitability of investment projects using quantitative techniques, the following information is needed:**
  * Initial capital costs (e.g., buildings and equipment)
  * Estimated lifespan of asset
  * Residual value at the end of the asset's useful life (leading to further cash inflow)
  * Forecasted net cash flows (expected returns minus operating costs)
* Financial data is rarely certain, so quantitative techniques depend heavily on the reliability of estimates and forecasts

### Forecasting cash flows in an uncertain environment

* **Investment appraisal techniques require forecasts of future cash flows, called forecasted net cash flows. It is assumed that:**

  * Cash inflows are equal to annual revenues earned from the project
  * Cash outflows include the initial capital cost and annual operating costs

* Forecasting cash flows is challenging and rarely 100% accurate, especially for long-term investments that require forecasts several years ahead. External factors may reduce forecast accuracy. **For example, when appraising a new airport's construction, revenue forecasts may be impacted by external factors, such as:**

  * An economic recession could reduce business and tourist traffic
  * Rising oil prices might increase air travel costs, lowering demand
  * A new high-speed rail link could shift some travelers to rail transport

* Future uncertainties are inevitable in investment appraisals, and managers must consider the risk of unforeseen events impacting cash flow forecasts. All investment decisions carry risk, so the key question is whether future profits will justify these risks.
