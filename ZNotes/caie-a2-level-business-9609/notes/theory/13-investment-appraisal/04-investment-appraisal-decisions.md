## Investment Appraisal Decisions

* Major investment projects are assessed financially and quantitatively, but other factors also play a crucial role in decision-making
* Earlier cards covered the three key quantitative appraisal methods, focusing on capital recovery speed and investment profitability. Businesses often set criteria that future projects must satisfy before approval
* These include:
  * Payback within three years

  * Minimum 15% accounting rate of return

  * Net present value at least 15% of initial capital

### **Qualitative factors and their impact on investment decisions**

* Investment appraisal techniques provide numerical data important for decision-making
* However, managers must also consider factors that cannot be easily measured numerically but have a significant impact on a decision.
* Such qualitative factors include:
  * **Impact on the environment and the local community:** Environmental groups and bad publicity can dissuade projects in sensitive areas, prioritising CSR over profits

  * **Refusal of planning permission:** Local authorities may block or require revisions to protect community interests

  * **Aims and objectives of the business:** Investments conflicting with goals, like personal customer service, may be avoided despite potential savings

  * **Impact on the workforce:** Automation plans might be reconsidered to maintain employee relations

  * **Acceptability of risk:** Risk acceptance of managers varies, influenced by past experiences and potential failure
