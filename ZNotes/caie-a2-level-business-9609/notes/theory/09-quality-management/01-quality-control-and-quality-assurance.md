# Quality management

### Quality Control and Quality Assurance

**What is Quality?**

**Quality Product:** A good or service that meets customers' expectations and therefore fulfils its intended purpose.

**What can “quality” mean?**

* It can be meeting customer’s expectation. Quality can be whether the product meets customer expectations at its price point.

* It can be of the highest standards or mediocre, therefore it is varied. Some products, like aircraft parts, require extreme precision, while others, like clothing, do not. Overengineering can make products too costly.

* Low-cost products can still be high quality if they perform as expected, while expensive ones can be low quality if they fail to meet expectations.

* Quality is not absolute; it depends on the product, its price, and consumer perceptions.

### **Quality Standards:**

Quality standards refer to the minimum acceptable production or service levels based on customer expectations.

**They apply to both goods and services:**

* Goods: Examples consisting the reliability of cars or the durability of clothing.

* Services: Quality can be measured by factors like:

  * Speed and manner of customer service (e.g., answering calls).

  * Waiting times (e.g., in banks or stores).

  * Accuracy (e.g., error rates in financial transactions).

  * Expertise (e.g., quality of financial advice).

### **Importance of Quality:**

* Satisfied customers are more likely to be loyal to the brand and recommend the brand.

* Fewer complaints, replacements, and compensation expenses; thus it lowers cost.

* Reliable products have a long life cycle and remain in demand for longer.

* A strong quality reputation reduces the need for heavy marketing.

* Quality products can justify businesses charging premium prices.

### **Quality and Business Competitiveness:**

* Consistent quality is often more effective than lowering prices.

* High quality builds brand reputation, allowing businesses to charge premium prices.

* As incomes grow, customers demand higher quality, making brand differentiation essential.

* Businesses must balance quality improvements with potential gains in competitiveness.

### Quality Control

**Quality control** ensures product or service quality through inspection and checking. It typically occurs at the end of production or during service delivery.

### Methods of Quality Control:

* Prevention: Designing quality into the product will reduce the need for later corrections.

* Inspection: Checking finished products, though costly and requiring skilled inspectors.

* Correction & Improvement: Fixing faults and improving processes to prevent future defects.

### **Quality Assurance**

A system of agreeing and meeting quality standards at each stage of production to ensure consumer satisfaction.

### Methods of Quality Assurance:

* It puts highest priority on prevention as it designs products for easy, fault-free manufacturing to get quality right the first time.

* Encouraging workers to aim for zero defects to reduce errors and costly rework.

* Setting and maintaining targets from product design to final delivery, boosting quality standard.

* It leads to checking raw materials and components upon arrival to avoid defects later.

* Ensuring high-quality delivery, service, and after-sales support.

* Workers check predetermined quality standards at every stage of vehicle factories like Toyota.

* Telephone banking organizations setting strict response times and service quality standards for customer requests.

### **ISO 9000:**

an internationally recognised standard that acknowledges the existence of a quality assurance method that meets certain conditions.

Requirements to obtain the ISO 9000 certification:

* Regular training and performance evaluation to maintain quality standards.

* Ensuring raw materials and components meet required standards.

* Applying consistent quality management, including after-sales service.

* Clear procedures for identifying, correcting, and preventing quality failures.

* Achieving ISO 9000 enhances credibility, efficiency, and customer satisfaction.

### **Total Quality Management (TQM)**

An approach to quality that aims to involve all employees in quality improvement.

**Internal customers:** people within the organisation who depend upon the quality of work being done by others.

**Zero defects:** a way of thinking to ensure that products meet customer expectations every time.
