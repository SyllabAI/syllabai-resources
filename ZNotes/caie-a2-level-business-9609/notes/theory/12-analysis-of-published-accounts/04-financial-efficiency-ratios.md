
## Financial efficiency ratios

### The meaning and importance of financial efficiency

* Various ratios assess how efficiently management uses business resources. High financial efficiency means effective asset use, minimizing borrowing needs and financing costs.
* The key financial efficiency ratios are **inventory, receivables, and payables turnover.**

### Rate of inventory turnover: calculation and interpretation

* Lower capital tied up in inventories is best for the business.
* Inventory turnover measures how often inventory is **bought and resold in a period**.
* An increasing ratio indicates reduced inventory investment and better financial efficiency. If inventory is bought once a year, turnover would be 2, with high inventory investment.
  * **Rate of inventory turnover = $\frac{\text{Cost of sales}}{\text{Average inventory}}$**
  * **Average inventory = $\frac{\text{Opening inventory + Closing inventory}}{\text{2}}$**


||**Cost of sales $M**|**Average inventory $M**|**Rate of inventory turnover**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|125|40|125/40 = 3.125|
|**BSK**|2400|200|2400/200 = 12|

* The table above shows average inventory figures for the year, not the difference between current and liquid assets.
* BSK’s inventory turnover rate reflects more efficient inventory management, with lower inventories relative to cost of sales than PLS.
* Adopting a JIT system could help PLS reduce inventory levels and improve turnover rates.
* **Points to note:**
  * Inventory turnover is measured in the number of times inventory is sold in a year, not as a percentage.
  * A higher turnover indicates efficient inventory management, with JIT systems achieving high rates.
  * Turnover varies by industry; for example, fresh fish retailers typically have higher rates than car dealers, making cross-industry comparisons unreliable.
  * This metric is irrelevant for service firms like insurance companies that do not hold inventory.

### Trade receivables turnover (days): calculation and interpretation

* **Trade receivables turnover (days)** measures the average time to collect payments from credit customers. Shorter periods indicate better working capital management.
  * **Trade receivables turnover = $\frac{\text{Trade receivables}}{\text{Credit sales}}$ x 365**


||**Trade receivables**|**Revenue**|**Trade receivables turnover**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|45|250|45/250 x 365 = 65.7 days|
|**BSK**|500|3200|500/3200 x 365 = 57 days|

* Both companies allow about two months for customers to pay, likely due to competition in the sports clothing market. Longer credit periods may attract orders but increase working capital needs.
* **Points to note:**
  * Trade receivables turnover varies by industry, with low turnover often reflecting stricter credit policies.
  * High turnover may attract customers with extended credit but could also indicate poor management of receivables and repayment periods.
  * Shortening credit terms, such as reducing from 60 to 30 days, or refusing credit to late payers, can improve results. However, such changes must balance sales growth goals with financial control priorities.

### Trade payables turnover (days): calculation and interpretation

* **Trade payables turnover (days)** measures the average time a business takes to pay its suppliers. A longer period reduces working capital needs. The formula can use either credit purchases or cost of goods sold.
  * **Trade payables turnover = $\frac{\text{Trade Payables}}{\text{Credit purchases}}$ x 365**


||**Trade payables ($)**|**Credit purchases ($)**|**Trade payables turnover**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|16|100|16/100 x 365 = 58.4 days|
|**BSK**|280|1920|280/1920 x 365 = 53.2 days|

* **Points to note:**
  * **BS**K pays suppliers faster than **PLS**, creating a cash flow problem as it needs more working capital.
  * While **PLS** takes longer to pay suppliers, it still faces the same issue due to delayed customer payments.
  * Both companies could improve financial efficiency.

### Methods of improving financial efficiency

* Financial efficiency can improve by reducing the need for high working capital.


|**Method**|**Evaluation**|
|:---|:---|
|Adopt JIT to boost inventory turnover.|Risk of zero-inventory from supply delays.|
|Reduce customer credit to lower debt collection period.|Customers may choose businesses with longer credit periods.|
|Delay supplier payments to extend creditor payment period.|Supplier discounts for quick payment may decrease, or they may demand faster payments.|
