## Profitability ratios

### **The meaning and importance of profitability**

* Measuring profit against targets is important, as profit is a major objective for many businesses.

* A high profit does not always indicate high profitability if sales and capital are also high. Consider the profit figures from the two sports clothing manufacturers, PLS and BSK.


||**2021 operating profit ($000)**|
|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|50|
|**BSK**|500|

* We cannot assess the performance of the two companies from these results. The only conclusion is that BSK made an operating profit ten times greater than PLS.
* Now look at other information about these two businesses


||**2021 Revenue ($000)**|
|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|250|
|**BSK**|3200|

* Additional data provides a clearer picture of the companies' performance, especially when comparing revenue and profit.
* Profitability, which measures profit relative to sales or capital invested, is a better indicator of business performance than overall profit.
  * Higher profitability increases the potential for high returns to investors.
* The three key profitability ratios in the A Level Business syllabus are gross profit margin, operating profit margin, and return on capital employed.

### **Return on capital employed: calculation and interpretation**

* The return on capital employed (RoCE) ratio, often called the primary efficiency ratio, measures profitability by comparing operating profit to invested capital.
* It is measured by:
  * **Return on capital employed ratio = $\frac{\text{Operating profit}}{\text{Capital employed}}$ x 100**
  * **Capital employed = non-current liabilities + shareholders' equity**
  * **Shareholders’ equity = issued shares + reserves**


||**Operating profit $M**|**Capital employed $M**|**Return on capital employed**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|50|400|50/400 x 100 = 12.5%|
|**BSK**|500|5000|500/5000 x 100 = 10%|

* The results show PLS's management is more effective at generating profit from invested capital.
* **Points to note:**
  * A higher RoCE indicates a greater return on invested capital.
  * Comparing RoCE with other companies and past performance reveals profitability trends.
  * Results can be compared to bank interest rates to assess whether capital could earn more with less risk.
  * RoCE should exceed borrowing costs, as lower returns than interest reduce shareholder gains.
  * RoCE improves through efficient, profitable use of assets purchased with the capital employed.

### **Gross profit margin: calculation and interpretation**

* The gross profit margin ratio assesses how effectively a business converts revenue into gross profit and is widely used to measure company performance
* It is measured by the formula:
  * **Gross profit margin ratio = $\frac{\text{Gross profit}}{\text{Revenue}}$ x 100**


||**Gross profit $000**|**Revenue $000**|**Gross profit margin**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|125|250|125/250 x 100 = 50%|
|**BSK**|800|3200|800/3200 x 100 = 25%|

* **Points to note:**
  * Despite BSK's higher gross profit, it has a lower gross profit margin due to its revenue being 12.8 times greater than PLS, but gross profit only 6.4 times greater.
  * The lower margin may result from BSK's low-price strategy or higher cost of sales per unit.
  * Higher costs could stem from increased material or direct labor costs, suggestingBSK's management may be less effective at controlling costs.
  * The gross profit margin indicates how well managers add value to sales.
  * Comparing ratios across industries is misleading due to differing risks and profit margins.

### **(Operating) Profit margin: calculation and interpretation**

* The operating profit margin ratio uses operating profit, calculated by subtracting overhead expenses from gross profit. It measures how effectively a business converts sales into operational profit.
* It is measured by the formula:
  * **Operating profit margin ratio = $\frac{\text{operating profit}}{\text{revenue}}$ x 100**


||**Operating profit $000**|**Revenue**|**Operating profit margin**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|50|250|50/250 x 100 = 20%|
|**BSK**|500|3200|500/3200 x 100 = 15.6%|

* **Points to note:**

  * The profitability gap between BSK and PLS has narrowed, with a smaller difference in operating profit margins despite a substantial gap in gross profit margins.
  * BSK’s higher overhead expenses result in lower operating profit relative to revenue.
  * BSK could improve by reducing overheads or increasing revenue without raising expenses.
  * Comparing results over time would reveal trends in performance and profitability, offering a clearer view of management effectiveness.
  * The operating profit margin and its trend over time reflect how well management converts revenue into profit after expenses.
