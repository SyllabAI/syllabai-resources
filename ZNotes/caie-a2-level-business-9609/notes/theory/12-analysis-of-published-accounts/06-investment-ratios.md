## Investment ratios

* Shareholders and potential investors will closely analyze company accounts to assess investment potential.

### **The meaning and importance of return to investors**

* **Investment ratios** are important to shareholders and potential investors.
* They show potential returns from capital gains (rising share price) and dividends, unless profits are low or losses occur.

### **Dividend yield: calculation and interpretation**

* The **dividend yield ratio** measures the return a shareholder gets from the dividend per share at the current share price, calculated using this formula:
  * **Dividend yield ratio = $\frac{\text{Dividend per share}}{\text{Market share price}}$**
  * **Dividend per share = $\frac{\text{Total annual dividends}}{\text{Total number of issued shares}}$**


||**Dividends ($M)**|**Number of issued shares ($M)**|**Dividend per share ($)**|**Market share price ($)**|**Dividend yield %**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|21|140|0.15|$1.50|$0.15 x 100/$1.5 = 10%|
|**BSK**|140|200|0.70|$10.00|$0.7 x 100/$10. = 7%|

* Shareholders in PLS earn a higher return than those in BSK.

* Potential investors should consider other ratios alongside the dividend yield before making a decision.

**Points to note:**

* If the share price rises with an unchanged dividend, the dividend yield falls.
* If the dividend increases without a share price change, the yield rises.
* Shareholders compare this return with other investments and similar companies.
* The result should be compared to previous years and industry peers for effective analysis.
* High dividend yields may attract potential shareholders if the share price is stable.
* Directors may pay dividends from reserves to maintain shareholder loyalty, even in low-profit or loss years.
* Directors may reduce dividends to increase retained earnings, supporting business expansion and lowering the gearing ratio.
* A high dividend yield may signal a poor investment if the share price has fallen, indicating market concerns about the company's future.

### **Dividend cover: calculation and interpretation**

* The **dividend cover ratio** shows how many times a company's profit can cover its ordinary dividends. A higher ratio indicates stronger dividend payment ability and allows more profit to be reinvested into the business.
  * **dividend cover ratio = $\frac{\text{Profit for the year}}{\text{Annual dividends}}$**


||**Dividends ($)**|**Profit for the year ($)**|**Dividend cover**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|21|30|30/21 = 1.4|
|**BSK**|140|400|400/140 = 2.9|

* The dividend cover ratio suggests BSK's directors could have paid a higher dividend but may be retaining profits to fund business expansion, benefiting shareholders in the future.
* **Points to note:**
  * If directors increase dividends without higher profits, the dividend cover ratio would drop, raising concerns about sustainability.
  * A low ratio also suggests minimal profit retention, potentially affecting future expansion.

### **Price/earnings ratio: calculation and interpretation**

* The **price/earnings (P/E)** ratio is essential for shareholders and reflects investor confidence in a company's future prospects. A high P/E ratio indicates expectations of higher earnings growth, while a ratio of 1 suggests **low confidence** in future
  earnings.
  * **P/E ratio = $\frac{\text{Market share price}}{\text{Earnings per share}}$**
  * **Earnings per share = $\frac{\text{Profit for the year}}{\text{Number of shares issued}}$**


||**Current share price ($)**|**Earnings per share ($)**|**Price/earnings ratio**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|1.50|0.21|7.1|
|**BSK**|10.00|2.00|5.0|

* **Points to note:**
  * The P/E ratio reflects investor confidence, with PLS’s ratio of 7.1 indicating investors pay 7.10 dollars for each dollar of earnings and expect a 7.1-year payback at current earnings levels.
  * The ratio should only be compared within the same industry, as optimism varies across sectors.
  * Comparing the P/E of a high-growth industry, like technology, to a low-growth one, like utilities, is not meaningful due to differing growth prospects.

### **Methods of improving investor return**

* Increasing profits improves returns to investors by enabling higher dividends and raising dividend yield.
* Business growth and profitability also push up share prices, benefiting shareholders through both dividends and capital gains.
* **However, there is a short-term trade off:**
  * Increasing profit may require investing in assets.
  * Reducing dividends improves retained profit and internal finance.
  * This lowers short-term shareholder returns.
  * Successful expansion raises profits, benefiting dividends and share prices, but only in the long-term.
