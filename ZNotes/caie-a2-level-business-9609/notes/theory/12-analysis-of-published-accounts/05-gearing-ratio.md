## Gearing ratio

* When a business takes out a loan, it funds expansion but increases business risk.

### **The meaning and importance of gearing**

* The **gearing ratio** shows how much business capital comes from debt.
* Higher reliance on loans means higher gearing, increasing the risk of repayment issues if profits drop.
* Gearing is crucial to assess.

### **Gearing ratio: Calculation and interpretation** 

* The gearing ratio is measured by the formula:
  * **Gearing ratio = $\frac{\text{Non-current liabilites}}{\text{Capital employed}}$ x 100**


||**Non-current liabilities**|**Capital employed**|**Gearing ratio**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|40|400|40/400 x 100 = 10%|
|**BSK**|2000|5000|2000/5000 x 100 = 40%|

* **PLS** relies less on bank loans than **BSK**, reducing risk but possibly missing profitable opportunities. If profits drop during a recession, **BSK** may regret its heavy reliance on long-term loans.
* **Points to note:**
  * The ratio shows how much capital is financed by long-term debt. A result over 50% indicates high gearing.
  * Higher gearing increases shareholder risk due to:
    * Higher debt leads to more interest payments, affecting dividends and retained earnings, especially with high interest rates and low profits.
    * Interest must still be paid even if profits fall.
    * High debt repayment can lead to low liquidity.
  * A low gearing ratio suggests a safer strategy but may disappoint shareholders seeking rapid returns.
  * High-gearing businesses with growth strategies offer faster returns compared to slower-growth, low-gearing companies.

  ### Methods of improving gearing

  
  |**Methods**|**Evaluation**|
  |:---|:---|
  |Sell shares to raise capital and repay loans.|This dilutes existing shareholders' control. Dividend payments must increase to maintain yield. Poor economic conditions may lead to shares being sold at a low price.|
  |Reduce dividends, retain profits, and use them to repay loans.|Low profits may reduce dividends, leading to lower returns for shareholders|
  |Sell assets to raise finance and repay loans.|Quick asset sales may lower prices, reducing business value and limiting expansion, unless the assets are unnecessary (e.g., an empty office).|
