## Liquidity ratios

### The meaning and importance of liquidity

* **Liquidity** measures how easily a business can meet its short-term debts or liabilities.
* **Liquidity ratios** are an important measure of a business's ability to continue trading.
  * **Working capital** is essential for liquidity. Insufficient working capital can lead to illiquidity, inability to pay debts, loss of supplier trust, and denied bank loans
  * Excess **working capital** ties up funds that could be invested more profitably elsewhere.
* **There are two liquidity ratios:**
  * Current ratio
  * Acid-test ratio

### Current ratio: calculation and interpretation

* The formula for current ratio is:
  * **Current ratio = $\frac{\text{current assets}}{\text{current liabilities}}$**
* The result is generally expressed as a ratio *(For e.g 2:1)*
  * This result indicates that the business can cover its short-term debts **twice**, indicating **strong liquidity**
* There’s no universal benchmark for liquidity. Accountants often suggest 1.5:1 to 2:1, but it depends on the industry and trends.
  * A 1.5:1 ratio may raise concern if it was significantly higher previously.
* Review the accounting data for two sports clothing manufacturers, Port Louis Sports (PLS) and Bengaluru Sports Kit (BSK), in the table below.


||**Current assets**|**Current liabilities**|**Current ratio**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|100|50|100/50 = 2:1|
|**BSK**|720|720|720/720 = 1:1|

* **Points to note**
  * **PLS** is in a safe liquidity position, with **2 dollars** in current assets for every **1 dollar** of liabilities, ensuring stability.
  * **BSK’s** liquidity is more concerning, as it only has **1 dollar** in assets per **1 dollar** of debt. This could cause issues if creditors demand repayment or if assets are not easily converted to cash.
  * Low current ratios are normal in industries like food retailing, where steady cash inflows cover short-term debts.
  * Ratios over 2:1 might indicate excess funds tied up in inventories, receivables, or cash, which could be better invested in productive assets like equipment.
  * A low ratio may require management to boost current assets.

### **Acid test ratio**

* The acid test ratio AKA quick ratio, is a stricter test of a firm’s liquidity and is measured by:
  * **Acid test ratio = $\frac{\text{liquid assets}}{\text{current liabilities}}$**
  * **Liquid assets = current assets - inventories**
* The acid test ratio **excludes inventories**, the least liquid current assets, as they may not sell quickly.
  * This provides a clearer view of the business's ability to pay short-term debts.
* Using the two businesses PLS and BSK again, look at the table below


||**Liquid assets**|**Current liabilities**|**Acid test ratio**|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|**PLS**|50|50|1:1|
|**BSK**|540|720|0.75:1|

* **Points to note**

  * Results below 1:1 indicate less than $1 of liquid assets per dollar of short-term debt, suggesting **BSK** may face liquidity issues. Trends over recent years offer better context.
  * For instance, if last year's acid test was 0.5:1, a current 1:1 ratio shows improved liquidity, whereas a drop from 1:1 signals a decline.
  * Businesses with high inventory levels, like furniture retailers, often have significant differences between current and acid test ratios, which may not be concerning. However, for industries like tech manufacturing, where inventories lose value quickly, this could be problematic.
  * Selling inventory for cash won’t change the current ratio but will improve the acid test ratio, as cash is more liquid than inventory.

  ### Methods of improving liquidity

  
  |**Methods to increase liquidity**|**Examples**|**Evaluation of method**|
  |:---|:---|:---|
  |**Sell fixed assets for cash and lease them back if needed.**|Land and property sold to a leasing company|Selling assets quickly may undervalue them, and leasing needed assets increases overheads, reducing the operating profit margin.|
  |**Sell inventories for cash**|Inventories of finished goods sold off discount raising cash.|Selling inventories at a discount reduces the gross profit margin and may damage the brand image.|
  |**Use JIT inventory management.**|JIT inventory management will clear inventories held.|Inventories may be needed for changing demand, and JIT could be difficult to implement in some industries.|
  |**Increase loans to boost cash and working capital.**|Take long-term bank loans if prospects are strong|These will increase the gearing ratio and higher interest costs will reduce annual profit|
