## What was the significance of Solidarity in Poland for the decline of Soviet influence in eastern Europe?

### Economic crisis in Poland and rise of solidarity movement

- In the 1970s, Poland descended into an economic crisis; price of different goods, including meat, increased by 1980; restriction on wage rises led to widespread protests by polish workers. 
- 14 august 1980: workers led by Lech Walesa protest at Lenin Gdansk; sets up 21 demands including creation of trade union “solidarity”, by end of the month gov agrees to all demands after negotiations due to pressure from the public.
- Solidarity grew quickly and had membership of a third of the workforce by January 1981.

### Reasons for success 

- Public support: over 30% workers joined; support of catholic church
- Well organized: its own newspaper; expert speakers; popular leader.
- Demands: addressed popular issues while stressing importance of non-violence
- West: western media supported the cause
- Industries: many members were in shipbuilding and heavy industry; general strike in those= disaster.

### Fall of Solidarity

- New leader, Jarezelski, suspicious of Walesa;december 1981, communist government introduces martial law, arrests over 10,000 solidarity leaders including walesa, stating that Jarezelski discovered plans for a Coup.
- Soviet government was NOT willing to give solidarity political control because that would threaten the stability of warsaw pact.
- Crushing the movement was easier because as it grew, it became less and less organized and there were many factions within the union, disagreeing with each other.

### Impact of Solidarity

- Popularity of solidarity= communism was not providing good standard of living;
- Showed inefficiency and corruption of soviet leaders
- Showed it was possible to stand against communism and that people would support.
