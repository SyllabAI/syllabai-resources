## How similar were events in Hungary in 1956 and in Czechoslovakia in 1968?



| Factor | Hungary 1956 | Czechoslovakia 1968 |
| -------- | -------- | -------- |
| Causes | Poor living standards; hated presence of soviet troops; lack of freedom; inspiration from polish reforms| Poor living standards; economic depression; need to make communism less restrictive|
| Aimes of Rebels| Free elections; withdrawal from warsaw pact and withdrawal of soviet troops| More rights to the people; reduce censorship; remain a loyal member of warsaw pact.|
| Events| Students and reformers led protests; soldiers joined; Rákosi replaced by Nagy; rebels ready to fight Soviet troops| Dubček's Communist Party led reforms; Prague Spring sparked new ideas and criticism of the regime and corruption|
| Soviet Response| Khrushchev allowed Nagy to lead some reforms but denied Hungary's exit from the Warsaw Pact. Soviet troops ended the rebellion, and Nagy was executed and replaced by Kádár| Brezhnev feared the Prague Spring's spread, sent troops to Czechoslovakia under pressure, faced little resistance, removed Dubček, and announced the Brezhnev Doctrine|
| Consequences| Two weeks of fighting left 3,000 Hungarians dead and 200,000 fleeing. The USSR made some reforms, but the West only protested, and Hungary remained in the Warsaw Pact| No resistance; the West condemned the invasion but accepted Czechoslovakia as Soviet. Romania protested, but Czechoslovakia stayed in the Warsaw Pact.|
