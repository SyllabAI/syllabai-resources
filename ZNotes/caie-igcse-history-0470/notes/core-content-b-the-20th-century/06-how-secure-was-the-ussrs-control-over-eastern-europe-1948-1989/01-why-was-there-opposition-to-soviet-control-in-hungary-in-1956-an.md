# How Secure was the USSR’s Control Over Eastern Europe, 1948-1989?

## Why was there opposition to Soviet control in Hungary in 1956 and Czechoslovakia in 1968, and how did the USSR react to this opposition?

### Hungarian Uprising, 1956

- Hungarians resented soviet influence and communist rule in Hungary- from loss of freedom of speech, permanence of soviet army presence, banning of the catholic church, poor standards of living, imposition of russian language and culture, to threat of secret police.
- June, 1956: Moscow forced Rákosi to ‘retire’ after reformers appealed to Khrushchev. Many reformers and opponents were encouraged by krushchev’s new approaches.
- October, 1956: students in Budapest protest end to soviet rule and implementation of “true socialism”; police makes arrests and soviet soldiers in Hungary were used to protect key buildings and roads; Hungarian workers and soldiers join the protest; Imre Nagy appointed leader.
- Nagy promises reforms like free elections and withdrawal of soviet troops; political prisoners released and Nagy announces plans to withdraw from the Warsaw pact.
- November 1956; Krushchev refuses, sends 1000 tanks and troops to hungary fierce fighting breaks out; over 2000 hungarians and 1000 troops died; west was too occupied by suez crisis to help; protests was successfully stopped; Nagy executed; replaced by pro-Soviet regime led by Kadar.

### Czechoslovakia- Reforms and Prague Spring, 1968

- Student protests take place, hard line leader replaced by Dubcek, promises reforms like removal of censorship, freedom of speech, rights to trade unions and “socialism with a human face”; did not want to leave warsaw pact BUT saw poor standards of living of his citizens; reforms open criticism of communist rule and spread of new and radical ideas in “Prague Spring”; USSR becomes suspicious.
- August: Brezhnev responds by ordering warsaw pact troops to move into czechoslovakia, west does nothing because “soviet sphere of influence”; concern that reforms of Prague spring might spark across the entire eastern bloc, weakening soviet control and warsaw pact; Dubcek expelled and czech government monitored for any evidence of disloyalty.
- Brezhnev Doctrine: communist nations MUST have a one party system and would be permanently part of the Warsaw pact. If one country tried to leave, the rest would use force to re-establish control.
