# How far was Hitler’s Foreign Policy to blame for the outbreak of war in Europe in 1939?
## Long term Consequences of ToV

### Dissatisfaction of Major powers
- Italy did not gain as many colonies as it wished
- Japan had its racial equality clause rejected
- Germany resented the treaty “Stab in the back” theory  German signatories became known as the “November Criminals”
- Division of german population led to increasing nationalism 
- Many in Britain started saying the treaty was “too harsh” wanted germany to be a strong buffer against the USSR
- The french felt the treaty was not harsh enough.

### Hitler’s rise to power
1. **Early Events:**
- Munich Putsch (1923): Hitler’s failed attempt to overthrow the Weimar government; gained national attention and reinforced his determination to achieve power legally.
- Mein Kampf (1925): Outlined plans to overturn the Treaty of Versailles, rearm Germany, and expand for Lebensraum (living space).
2. **Key Moments:**
- Reichstag Fire (1933): Used by Hitler to claim a communist threat, consolidate power, and push through the Enabling Act.
- Hitler becomes Chancellor in 1933 and begins systematically dismantling the Treaty of Versailles.

### Timeline
- 1933: Hitler walks out disarmament conference; leaves the league; begins rearmament in secret
- 1934: attempted annexation of Austria, stopped by Mussolini
- 1936: conscription reintroduced and Rhineland reoccupied.
- 1937: Germany enters spanish civil war, supports Franco’s right wing rebels, new german air force is tested.
- 1938: Anschluss of austria; Sudetenland invaded
- 1939: invasion of Czechoslovakia as a whole; invasion of Poland; war begins.
