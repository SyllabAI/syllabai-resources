## The 1930s: great depression and league rulings

### Effect of depression

- Economic turmoil; member states would be even more reluctant to impose economic sanctions as they wished to retain what trade they had left
- Armament was reduced to cut off expenditure, leading to military sanctions being extremely weakened
- Rise in extremism, for eg. Nazis in Germany
- Encouraged militarism in Japan
- Put pressure on countries to find new markets and sources of raw materials= colonization and annexation of other nations.

### Japanese invasion of manchuria

- **Manchuria**: north-east province in China; rich in mineral wealth, forestry agricultural land;
- **Causes**: japan badly affected by great depression, not self sufficient in food as it depended on imports to feed an exponentially growing population; imports depended on japanese exports; export was silk= luxury item; exports declined; growing economic crisis; manchuria= answers to japan’s problems; sources for food+raw materials, market for japanese exports, AND land for ever increasing japanese population.
- **Events**: MUKDEN RAILWAY INCIDENT, september 1931, japanese soldiers stage an incident on railway line, accusing chinese bandits, launching invasion; China appeals to league but japanese army occupies whole Manchuria, renames it to Manchukuo in 1932.
  The League would not call sanctions BECAUSE European powers did not want American firms to take over lost business in the Far East; military sanctions NOT favored because chances of victory were doubtful; Britain and France did not want Japan to attack their Far East colonies due to imposition of sanctions.
- LYTTON COMMISSION: Lord Lytton to lead a commission of enquiry to Manchuria; concluded that invasion was not justified; took one year for assembly to consider the report (February 1933); findings accepted 42-to-1; Japan responds by leaving the league.
- **Consequences**: League criticized for not acting quickly enough; lack of key members like USA and Russia= military sanctions could not be used, LINK to weakness in league membership; world recession= economic sanctions achieving positive result seemed impossible; league seemed Euro-centric= belief that league would take action if this was a european invasion; Mussolini and Hitler watched with interest.

### World disarmament conference of 1932-34

- Organized by the League's disarmament commission, in Geneva, to resolve issues surrounding disarmament.
- **Members failed to agree**: France, Poland, and Czechoslovakia feared national security in face of a potential future German attack; France willing to disarm upon guarantee from US and Britain= both nations not prepared to give guarantee; Hitler uses France’s fear to point out French hypocrisy, “germany should also have a chance to defend its borders”, walked out, and Germany stepped out the league; general disarmament would not happen.
- Soon, rearmament began ; first Germany, followed by Japan and Italy.

### Abyssinian Crisis- Italian invasion of Abyssinia

- **Background**: ruled by Haile Salassie; despite SOME mineral resources, MINOR economic value; Abyssinain army had outdated equipment which was NOT a match for Italian army with modern equipment
- **Causes**: need for Italian revenge, as in 1896 Italian army had been defeated and humiliated by Abbysinian forces; invasion would act as propaganda to boost Mussolini’s popularity following the economic crisis in Italy; resolve disappointment from 1919 when Italy failed to gain significant German and Turkish colonies.
- **Events**: ABYSSINIAN INVASION- Wal Wal incident in december 1934, Mussolini builds up forces in his colonies in Eritrea and Somaliland, attack launched in late 1935, consisted of occupying Wal-Wal Oasis (80 km inside Abyssinia) and claiming it as Italian land, entire world shocked at Italian ruthlessness as NO declaration of war from Mussolini before the attack.
- LEAGUE RESPONSE- invasion was seen as act of aggressions and NOT justified;  moral disapproval followed by economic sanctions; HOWEVER oil and coil were included in items prohibited in trade between Italy and League members; Britain refused to close Suez Canal on fear of possible Italian attack on oversea British colonies.
- HOARE-LAVAL PACT- Fearing loss of Italy as an ally to Hitlr, december 1935, France and Britain sign a pact with Mussolini to end war, ⅔ Abyssinian land will go to Italy in return; Mussolini accepted BUT news leaked to french press= PUBLIC protests in Britain and France SO plan canceled; April 1936 Addis Ababa captured by Italian forces; July 1936 sanctions are lifted.
- **Consequences**: work of committees and commissions continued BUT unpublicised in 1930s; league was ignored in major issues of global policy, EVIDENCE= played no part in diplomatic ties leading up to German union with Austria in 1938, AND not consulted in Sudetenland crisis OR munich conference; britain and france went against league aims to appease to dictators and try to keep the peace; no league meetings during WW2 and dissolved after last meeting of April 1946; HOWEVER, league organizations ILO and ICJ were built into the UN; UN followed a similar framework to LoN; LoN ultimately failed, BUT central idea of collective security and togetherness was supported by many leading politicians.

### Summary: Failures of the League in the 1930s

1. **French and British Self-Interests:**

- Britain and France prioritized their own interests over League principles.
- Manchuria: Refused military action or sanctions against Japan to protect their Far East colonies and trade.
- Abyssinia: Hoare-Laval Pact secretly offered Mussolini ⅔ of Abyssinia, undermining the League's credibility.

2. **Absence of Strong Powers:**

- USA: The absence of the USA weakened economic sanctions, as nations could trade with non-member states like the US.
- USSR: Not a member until 1934, so the League lacked military support from a large power.
- Germany: Left the League in 1933, further undermining collective efforts.

3. **Ineffective Sanctions:**

- Economic sanctions were inconsistent and avoided critical resources.
- Manchuria: No sanctions imposed due to fear of losing trade to US firms.
- Abyssinia: Oil and coal were excluded from sanctions; Britain refused to close the Suez Canal, allowing Italian supply lines to remain open.

4. **Lack of Military:**

- The League had no standing army and relied on member states to provide military force, which they were unwilling to do:
- Manchuria: No military action taken, as members feared Japanese retaliation.
- Abyssinia: Britain and France feared provoking Mussolini, especially during rising tensions in Europe.

5. **Reaching Decisions Very Slowly:**

- Manchuria: Took over a year to publish the Lytton Report, by which time Japan had solidified control and renamed Manchuria to "Manchukuo."
- Abyssinia: Delay in sanctions and public protests over the Hoare-Laval Pact weakened confidence in the League’s decisiveness.

6. **Economic Issues:**

- The Great Depression severely limited the willingness of nations to impose sanctions or contribute to military efforts
- Nations prioritized their own economies, avoiding costly military action or trade restrictions.
- Economic turmoil fueled extremism and militarism, as seen with Japan, Germany, and Italy.
