# To what extent was the League of Nations a Success?

## Background

### When and by Whom was the League of Nations formed?

- Founded january 10, 1920
- The covenant of the league was part of the treaty of versailles.
- Primarily aimed at resolving disagreements between nations peacefully, avoiding war as
  a resort.

### Aims of the League

- Maintain peace and security
- Solve disputes using peaceful means
- Achieve world disarmament
- Encourage free trade amongst all nations
- Improve the standard of living
- Enforce and Uphold the Treaty of Versailles

### Collective Security

- Aimed to prevent aggression and maintain peace amongst member states
- The idea was that attack against one member of the league would be considered an
  attack against all,
- Prompting collective response to aggressor states.
- These methods were:

1. Moral disapproval: the league would publicly condemn the action of an aggressive
   nation, hoping that international public opinion would pressure the aggressor into
   complying with the league
2. Economic and trade sanctions: if moral disapproval fails, the league would impose
   economic and trade sanctions on the aggressor, restricting financial relations between
   league members and the aggressor, weakening its economy, forcing it to comply.
3. Military sanctions: last resort; the league would rally member states’ military forces to collectively act against the aggressor.

### What was the problem with collective security methods?

- The league did not have an army of its own, and would have to rely on the army of its
  members; this would be difficult as most member states would not want to enter a
  conflict after the first world war just ended.
- The absence of the USA and USSR meant that placing sanctions would be less
  effective than if they were members
- Most members would prioritize national interests over international interests
