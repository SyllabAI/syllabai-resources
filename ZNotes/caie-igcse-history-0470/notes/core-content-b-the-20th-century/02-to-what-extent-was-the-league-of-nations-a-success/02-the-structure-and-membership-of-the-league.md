## The structure and membership of the league

### Structure

- **Assembly**: main representative body of the league; all member states represented;
  voted on topics of general policy; advised the council; decided the budget; met up once
  per year; each country had one vote; unanimous decision making required.
- **Council**: had permanent members (Great Britain, France, Italy, Japan, and later
  Germany); non-permanent members erected by the assembly; handled disputes
  between nations, and the imposition of sanctions; permanent members had veto power;
  unanimous decision making.
- **Secretariat**: administrative arm of the league; handled day-to-day running of the league;
  prepared reports and organized meetings; kept communication between different bodies
  of the league running; extremely understaffed.
- **Permanent Court of International Justice**: Based in The Hague, it resolved international disputes between countries; Gave legal opinions on disputes and treaty interpretation.

### League commissions and committees

- **Mandate Commission**:  in charge of distributing former German and Ottoman territories
  to League members, with the ultimate goal of self-determination
- **Disarmament Commission**: promoted global disarmament
- **Health Organization**: worked to improve public health, one of the more successful
  committees of the league, laid the groundwork for the World Health Organization(WHO)
- **Refugee Commission**: tasked with relocating displaced populations after World War one, helped stamp out Cholera from refugee camps and helped 400,000 Turkish refugees find homes
- **Slavery Commission**: aimed to combat slavery, forced labor, and human trafficking
  around the world.
- **Economic Commission**: focused on global economies and solving financial crises

### Membership

- Allies and nations that didn’t lose the war
- Nations such as Germany that lost the war were initially not invited
- USSR was not invited
- The US did not join because the American Senate voted against it, even though the
  league was Wilson’s idea.

### How far did weaknesses in the League’s organization and membership make failure inevitable?

- Absence of the United States led to economic and military sanctions being weakened as
  the US had a strong army and the strongest economy at the time.
- The League was criticized for being a “winners club” as its initial membership consisted
  of only countries that won the war.
- Unanimous decision making made it hard to take swift decisions
- Lack of a league military meant reliance on member states for military sanctions, BUT
  nations would often be reluctant, BECAUSE disputes would either not directly involve
  national interests, OR because they did not want to enter another war; limiting the
  league’s ability to impose sanctions.
- The permanent members of the council held more power than the rest, and their national interests would often take precedence over international security.
