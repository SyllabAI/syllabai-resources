## Impacts of the Treaty

### Social Impacts

- German resentment and humiliation: The "War Guilt Clause" (Article 231) caused widespread anger and a sense of betrayal among Germans.
- Population displacement: Territorial losses (e.g., Alsace-Lorraine to France, Polish Corridor) left millions of Germans living outside Germany's borders.
- Rise of nationalism: Anti-Versailles sentiment fueled radical political movements like the Nazi Party.

### Political Impacts

- Weimar instability: The treaty undermined the legitimacy of the Weimar Republic, as it was associated with the "stab-in-the-back" myth. Weimar Republic is referred to as November Criminals.
- Weakened Germany: Severe military restrictions (100,000 soldiers, no air force or submarines) reduced Germany's ability to defend itself.
- Redrawing of Europe: New nations (e.g., Poland, Czechoslovakia) were created, but ethnic tensions arose in these areas due to the principle of self-determination being inconsistently applied.

### Economic Impacts

- Harsh reparations: Germany was required to pay 132 billion gold marks, crippling its economy and contributing to hyperinflation in 1923.
- Territorial losses: Loss of industrial regions like the Saar Basin and Upper Silesia weakened Germany's economic base.
- Global economic instability: The punitive terms hindered European recovery, contributing to wider economic challenges during the inter-war period.
