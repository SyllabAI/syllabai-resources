## Road to Peace

### The Paris Peace Conference- The “Big Three”

- Following the armistice, a peace conference was organized in the Palace of Versailles in Paris, France.

- Goal: To determine peace terms to bring an official end to the war.

- Attitude: Public anger towards Germany; they blamed Germany.

- The big three leaders of the emerging powers at the end of the war were USA, Great Britain and France. All three had different ideas and attitudes about the losing side, particularly Germany.

  - President Woodrow Wilson of USA, “The Idealist”: US did not suffer as much as Britain and France in the war.

    - Wanted a fair peace.

    - Wanted to ensure Germany wasn’t punished as he believed Germany’s economic revival to be important to Europe’s economic revival.

    - Wanted treaties based off his 14 points.

- Fourteen points: Including but not limited to self determination for nations, free trade during war and peace, disarmament of all nations, and the creation of a League of Nations.

   1. Open Diplomacy: An end to secret treaties and alliances; international agreements must be conducted openly.

   2.  Freedom of the Seas: Absolute freedom of navigation on the seas outside territorial waters, in peace and war.

   3.  Free Trade: The removal of economic barriers and trade tariffs to establish equal trade conditions among all nations.

   4.  Global Disarmament: Reduction of national armaments to the lowest point consistent with domestic safety.

   5.  Colonial Adjustment: Impartial adjustment of colonial claims, balancing the interests of native populations with imperial claims.

   6.  Russia: Evacuation of all Central Powers from Russian territory, allowing Russia to independently define its own independence.

   7.  Belgium: Full evacuation and restoration of Belgian sovereignty and independence.

   8.  France: Restoration of all French territories, including the return of the disputed **Alsace-Lorraine** region.

   9.  Italy: Re-adjustment of Italian frontiers along clearly recognizable lines of nationality.

  10.  Austria-Hungary: Freedom and self-determination opportunities for the various nationalities within the former empire.

  11.  The Balkans: Redrawing of Balkan borders along historical lines of nationality, restoring Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro, and giving Serbia secure sea access.

  12.  Ottoman Empire: Creation of a secure Turkish state with guaranteed free passage and trade through the Dardanelles for all nations.

  13.  Poland: Creation of an independent Polish state out of territories with indisputably Polish populations, with secure access to the sea.

  14.  League of Nations: Creation of a general international association of nations to peacefully resolve disagreements and guarantee political independence.

- Prime Minister David Lloyd George of Great Britain, “The Realist”: He won the election via promise of revenge on Germany. UK suffered a lot during the war.

  - Not too heavy reparation to satisfy the public for election but ensure future trade with Germany.

  - Expand British empire.

  - Wanted Britain to control more sea for trade and expansion of territory.

- President George Clemenceau, “The Vendettist”. France suffered the most during war, destruction of the Western Front \+ greatest number of lives lost were French.

  - Wanted strict punishment for Germany to take blame for war.

  - Wanted crippling reparations, all colonies and lots of land.

  - Proposed Germany to be broken down into a bunch of smaller, independent states to destroy its political and economical dominance

- Lloyd George was often the mediator between Clemenceau and Wilson.

### The Versailles Peace Settlement

- Collective name to refer to all peace treaties drawn at the Paris peace conference.
- Each treaty dealt with a different member of the triple entente.
- Treaty of Versailles dealt with Germany.

### Terms of the Treaty

- War guilt: Clause 231
  - Take responsibility for the war. 

  - Set up legal basis for reparations.

  - “You admit to damage, you pay for it” 

  - Hurt German pride.
- Reparations: Sum of 6.6 billion pounds set after signing of the treaty. Germany complaints of signing a blank cheque, economic turmoil, hurts German economy and leads to economic crisis in 1923.
  - US lent significant amounts of money to Germany through the Dawes Plan and the Young Plan (reduced £6.6 billion to £2.2 billion) to help them stabilize the economy
- Disarmament:
  - Demilitarized Rhineland.

  - Army limited 100,000 men and 15,000 naval sailors.

  - Conscription banned.

  - No airplanes, tanks, submarines and only 6 battleships.
- Colonies: All of Germany's oversea colonies would be taken away as mandates of Britain and France.
- Land:
  - Alsace-Lorraine given to France

  - Saar Basin was placed under League of Nations mandate for 15 years pending a plebiscite to decide whether it would go to France, Germany, or remain under League control.

  - Upper Silesia and West Prussia to Poland; Danzig became a free port city administrated by the League and became a part of the polish corridor.
- League of Nations: Germany to accept the covenant of an international League of Nations to resolve disagreements peacefully and without war.

### Why did the victors not get everything they wanted?

- Each leader had different aims and motives
- Different public pressures
- Main clashes in negotiating peace terms:

1. Clemenceau wanted the Rhineland to become a free city and the Saar to be given to France but Wilson believed in self-determination and wanted Germany’s economic revival for more trade opportunities so negotiation took place leading to Saar being placed under League of Nations mandate for 15 years pending plebiscite, and Rhineland demilitarized.
2. Clash of empires: Wilson wanted self-determination and free access to international waters, but that would harm British and French empires so self-determination was awarded to states in Eastern Europe but Britain and France built up their empires by absorbing German colonies.
3. Heavy reparations: Clemenceau wanted crippling reparations but Lloyd George and Wilson wanted German economic recovery so reparations were given but not as much as Clemenceau wanted.

- Ultimately, the victors did not get everything they wanted because they did not have the same aims and motives and had to cater to different demographics while ensuring the economic, geographical, and political benefits they could exact.
