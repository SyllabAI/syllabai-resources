# How Effectively Did USA Contain the Spread of Communism?

## Events in Korea, 1950-53

### The Korean War
- By 1948, north of Korea (“Democratic People’s Republic of Korea") surrendered to Soviet union and was led by communist leader Kim Il Sung.
- South of Korea (“Republic of Korea”) led by President Rhee under an anti-communist government, supported by USA
- Line dividing the two republics was known as the 38th parallel
- Both governments claimed to be the legitimate government of Korea
- By 1950, DPRK invaded ROK
- WHY?
1. north had support of soviet union (Stalin) and communist China (Mao) who sent DPRK military and financial aid; 
2. DPRK had a much stronger army; 
3. USSR had successfully tested its first atomic bomb; recent speeches made by US leadership implied that Korea was NOT a region of interest for US.

### US Response

- Truman sends supplies, warships and advisors to ROK, but needs international support to wage war.
- US presents resolutions to UN Security Council, urging for response to DPRK’s aggression
- Resolution passed BECAUSE soviet union was boycotting the UN for refusal of admission of communist china into UN
- 18 member states provided armies to support ROK, led by American General McArthur, who would often take orders from Truman instead of UN
- US was also made the largest portion of the international armed forces assembled by the UN.

### Roadmap of the war

- North Korean success (June–September 1950) Most of South Korea was overrun by the communists except a small area around Pusan in the south-east.
- UN counter-attack (September– October 1950) MacArthur launched an attack from behind enemy lines with UN forces heading northwards from Pusan. The capital Seoul was captured, and the communists were pushed back across the 38th Parallel.
- Attack from China (November, 1950–January 1951) Mao launched a counter-attack as he feared an invasion. Seoul was recaptured by the communists and the UN forces were forced to retreat back across the 38th Parallel
- UN counter-attack (January–July 1951) The UN forces retook Seoul. MacArthur argued for the use of a nuclear attack against China. He was relieved of his command by Truman, who feared Soviet involvement.
- Stalemate and armistice (July 1951–July 1953) There was little tactical advantage gained by either side. Peace talks began. Stalin’s death in 1953 helped push North Korea to sign an armistice

### Consequences of the war

- Success for containment as US showed willingness to use force against communism and ROK was protected
- UN proved more successful than LoN in dealing with aggressive nations.
- DPRK remained communist with help from china,
- Relations between US and Japan developed and cold war was now waged in both Europe and East-Asia.
- MASSIVE casualties
- The USA furthered its anti-communist alliances by setting up SEATO in South-east Asia and CENTO in Central Asia and the Middle East. 
- Khrushchev (new USSR leader) creates warsaw pact in fear of USA creating anti-communist enemies around the USSR.
- Arms race between US and USSR begins
- war= containment had limits, not COMPLETE pushback of communism.
