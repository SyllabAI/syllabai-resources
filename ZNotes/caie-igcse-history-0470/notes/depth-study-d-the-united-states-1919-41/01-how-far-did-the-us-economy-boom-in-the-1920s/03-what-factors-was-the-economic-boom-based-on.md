## What Factors was the Economic Boom based on?

* **Economic boom:** A period of time when the economy improves quickly, people earn more, and the standard of living goes up

### **First World War**

* America was minimally involved in WWI, so it retained its workforce and troops and didn’t suffer damage on its soil.
* The Americans first tried to stay out of the fighting but instead they lent money and supplies to the Allies.
* The Americans took over the European trade industry and its economy boomed.
* The USA outstriped Germany in the supply of chemical products. New explosives were made and new materials were produced.
* Aircraft technology was also improved and they were also now used for civilian uses.
* They had money and booming industries from selling weapons, food, and ammunition during the war.
* Production demands and export rates were high.
  * Iron ore production almost doubled within 3 years.
  * Coal, petrol, and wheat industries boomed between 1914 and 1917.
  * Coal production increased from 422 to 551 million tons.
  * Income from chemical export increased by over 1000%.
  * Wheat, iron, and steel incomes more than doubled.
* They were paid back money they had loaned to the British for reparations.
* The war didn't drain the USA's resources like it did to Europe's.
* Soon the USA readjusted but by 1922 the American economy was growing fast once again.

### **State of Mind**

* This gave people more confidence in the boom, people had invested in new industries and experimented with new ideas. 
* Seeing this even ordinary Americans had confidence to buy goods even on credit.

### **New Industries**

* Through the 1920s, new industries and new methods of production were developed in the USA. 
* The country had it become the foundation of an economic boom in consumer goods. 
* There was also a lot of mass production and things that used to be luxuries now became available to the ordinary American.

### **Industrial Strength**

* By the 1920s, the USA had a prosperous economy and many new industries had started growing.
* The reason for this is that the USA had many raw materials, such as oil, steel, coal and gas as well as iron.
* These natural resources were also easy to obtain and they did not need to export any materials as they already had them in the USA.
* Most of the population were now living in towns and cities and they were working in industry and commerce, and earning higher wages than they did while farming.
* The USA also developed new technologies and new industries were growing such as the chemical end film industry.
* The USA also increased selling their products - not only in North America, but also in Europe, Latin America and Asia
* Agriculture and Industry were equal but soon the industry became more prosperous than American Agriculture.

### **Assembly Line**

* Pioneered by Henry Ford
* A new method that allowed mass production at cheap rates
* Initially expanded the automobile industry, but was soon utilised in other forms of production, like radios and fridges
  * The first mass-produced car was the Model T - by 1928, it was priced at only $295
  * One car was produced every 10 seconds
* Low prices and reliable production attracted customers, with high demand increasing production and turning profits.

### **Expansion of the automobile industry**

* Further expanded the use of the assembly line
* Cars went from a privilege to a necessity, and high demand meant more production.
* Stimulated production in other industries, like petrol and glass
  * By 1925, 90% of petrol, 80% of rubber, and 75% of plate glass was consumed by cars
* A boom in multiple industries increased profits

### **Development of electricity**

* A cheap and effective source of power
* In 1920, only 35% of households had electricity - this increased to 68% by 1929
* Prices dropped, but demand increased so greatly that it created massive profits.

### **Technological developments**

* Appliances like refrigerators, radios, and washing machines became common in households
* Thanks to the assembly line, mass production was able to satisfy high demand
* Electricity was able to cheaply power these appliances as well as the factories that produced them
* In the 1920s, average annual sales grew to over $7 billion.

### **Entertainment**

* Newspapers and magazines became more widely circulated
  * In 1919, the first tabloid newspaper was published
  * Sports, fashion, and cinema became popular topics, and more articles were published
  * By 1922, some magazines circulated amongst more than 2.5 million people
  * Journalism grew as a field and an area of employment
* Sports grew as an entertainment industry
  * Thousands of people watched and listened to sports matches live
  * With the spread of radios, they also listened to live broadcasts
  * Baseball was the most popular and became a highly profitable business
  * Millions of dollars rolled in through tickets to matches
* Cinema grew in popularity
  * Hollywood was the film capital of the world
  * Cinema was one of the most common leisure activities
  * 40 million tickets were sold per week in 1920; this number more than doubled by 1930
  * Films became a mass production industry, and 3 movies were made per week in Hollywood
  * Million-dollar movies populated studios, and contracts worth thousands were signed
  * Film life gained popularity as a topic in media, helping circulate tabloids and magazines

### **Advertisement**

* Entertainment, technology, and other mass-produced commodities were commonly advertised.
* Billboards were large and colourful, and catchphrases became popular
* Radio advertising was a new and popular venture
* Adverts also circulated in newspapers, magazines, and printed cartoons
* Made mass-produced commodities look appealing due to low prices and encouraged consumers to buy things they did not necessarily need
* Advertisement boosted sales across the decade and rolled in profits in multiple industries

### **Credit and Hire Purchase**

* Due to the fact that the govt wanted more people to invest into the american economy and consumer goods, hire purchase and buying on credit was introduced. 
* The idea was that people could purchase goods without paying in full right away or by paying in installments with their banks.
* The banks were also more willing to allow loans to buy consumer goods.
