## How far did the roles of women change during the 1920s?

### **Before the World War**

* Tradition women – WASPS believed the more kids, the more religious you are.
* Most women couldn’t vote, and any paid work was limited to occupations such as domestic service, secretarial work, and teaching.
* Restrictive dresses - corsets and full-length skirts along with dresses with tight waists.
* Controlled public behaviour - modest, polite, and discrete.

  No drinking or smoking, and if unmarried, no male company without a chaperone.

### **During the First World War**

* Women performed men’s work in the war industries as men were required in the armed services.

### **After the First World War**

* Women were becoming more independent and got the right to vote. Made up 50% of the electorate.
* Many women entered the workplace – nurses and librarians. The number of people in employment increased by 25% to 10 million by 1929.
* Modern women did not believe their job was only to raise kids. The birth rate declines.
* Flapper – emancipated young women (wealthy, middle, and upper-class women) who were free and embraced the new fashions and urban attitudes.

  Corsets were abandoned, and shorter, lighter skirts and dresses that were often sleeveless were introduced.

  The new fashions and materials permitted greater movement and self-expression.

### **Lifestyle**

* Women from richer families had better living standards and more free time thanks to labour-saving devices being introduced.
* Women began smoking, drinking, and kissing in public. Chaperones were no longer required.
* Women drove cars. Henry Ford introduced coloured cars in 1925 for them.
* Short hair and makeup became symbols of new freedom.
* Women were acting with more independence.

**The role of the average American woman changed very little during the 1920s.**

The difference was only in upper-middle-class women, who made up only a minority of the female population in America.
