## Who was to blame for the failure of prohibition

### **The American people who carried on going to illegal speakeasies making Prohibition difficult to enforce.**

* Despite the fact that many federal prohibition agents seized over 280,000 distilleries (or stills), and possibly more as the American people illegally had them.
* Therefore 280,000 could only be the stills that were seized that were recorded on paper as well as the fact that there could almost certainly be more stills that the people owned yet they weren't captured by the law enforcement.
* But most American people did not need stills to make their own illegal whisky (moonshine) as they could just go to their nearby Speakeasy to get alcohol.
* In fact there were more Speakeasies in American cities by 1925 than there were saloons in the year 1919.
* This all clearly goes to show that the speakeasies were only filling up a gap in the market and “supplying a public demand. And the demand was huge" (Al Capone's views on Prohibition as a business).

### **The law enforcers who were corrupt and ignored the law brea.kers.**

* Prohibition was a cause of mass corruption within law enforcement.
* Another thing to be noted was that many prohibition law enforcement officers were also consumers of alcohol themselves and were involved in the liquor trade.
* Many big breweries and speakeasies still were in business (and making lots of profit) during the prohibition era despite not being an easy business to hide.
* This was due to the fact that many breweries stayed in operation by bribing the local government officials, prohibition agents and the police to leave them alone.
* In Fact, in some cities, police officers would be the ones prepared to show people where the speakeasies were and even those people who were caught and arrested barely had any consequences because many bribed the senior officers and even the judges.
* One in twelve prohibition agents were dismissed for corruption.

### **The bootleggers who continued supplying and selling alcohol.**

* The bootleggers were the main suppliers of illegal alcohol. And most of them made a vast fortune selling alcohol.
* About two thirds of the illegal alcohol came from Canada. This was because the huge border between the USA and Canada was almost impossible to patrol.
* Other bootleggers brought in alcohol by sea, they would simply wait in the waters outside US control until an opportunity to land their cargo presented itself.
* One of the most famous was Captain McCoy, who specializes in the finest Scotch whisky. This is where the phrase “the real McCoy” comes from.

### **The gangsters who controlled the trade through violence and made huge profits.**

* It is estimated that gangsters and organized gangs made about $2 billion out of the sale of illegal alcohol.
* The rise of the gangster tells us a lot about American society at this time.
* The gangsters generally came from immigrant backgrounds and were poorer even in these communities.
* And they were often poorly educated, but they were also clever and ruthless.
* The gangs fought viciously with each other to control the liquor trade and also the prostitution, gambling and protection rackets that were centered on the speakeasies.
* They made use of new technology, especially automobiles and the Thompson submachine gun, which was devastatingly powerful but could be carried around and hidden under an overcoat.
* In Chicago alone, there were 130 gangland murders in 1926 and 1927 and not one arrest.
* Fear and bribery made law enforcement ineffective.
