## Why did unemployment persist despite the New Deal?

When Roosevelt became president, unemployment was at 12.8 million, 25% of the workforce.

By 1941, unemployment was down by 5.6 million.

In the 1930s, it was never below 5 million despite Roosevelt’s efforts.

**Reasons**

**a. The home market**

* Low incomes during the 1930s meant less money was spent on American goods.
* This happened in rural areas as they depended on the recovery of the farming industry. Wheat and cattle prices reached 1929 levels after the US entered WW2.

**b. The foreign market**

* Overseas customers also suffered from the depression; hence, expansion of the exporting industries was limited.
* Firm tariffs reduced the scope for international growth.

**c.  Improved production methods**

* Agriculture – more machinery (assembly line productions and automatic tools), less labour, hence reduced working opportunities.

**d. The business cycle**

* The world economy improved between 1933 and 1937 but went back into recession in 1938.

**e. Self-inflicted unemployment**

* Business efficiency is most important for surviving through a recession.
* If there is overproduction, production capacity has to be reduced, hence the loss of jobs.
* This happened in the farming industry

**f.  The limitations of the New Deal**

* Roosevelt couldn’t take complete control of the economy as Congress wouldn’t allow this, so he took part of the problem and gave solutions for individual parts.
* Hence, the economy recovered at a very slow pace.
