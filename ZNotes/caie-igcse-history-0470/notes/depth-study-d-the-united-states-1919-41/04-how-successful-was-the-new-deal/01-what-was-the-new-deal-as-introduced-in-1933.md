# How Successful was the New Deal?

## What was the New Deal as introduced in 1933?

**The First Hundred Days – between March and June 1933**

Three main aims of the new deal

1. Relief or assistance for the unemployed or poor
2. Rebuilding the American economy
3. Reforms to create a fair and just society

* Roosevelt made a deal for 100 days, and it was known as the 100-day deal.
* The new deal marked a Change from the laissez-faire attitude the govt had previously taken toward the economy
* It established the idea that the govt was responsible
* The new deal permanently increased the size and power of the federal government
* 'New deal legislation - Relief, Recovery, Reform (in this order ONLY)
* In 1932, in an inaugural address, he reassured the American public with his famous quote, “We have nothing to fear but fear itself”.
* In the first hundred days of office, FDR got Congress to pass many proposed laws.
* The program would become known as alphabet agencies

**Relief**

* Providing immediate emergency aid, cash, and temporary jobs to stop people from starving and losing their homes.

* It prevented mass starvation and offered immediate dignity through work rather than just standard charity hand-outs.

* Millions of people were put to work almost overnight, restoring public morale and faith in the US government.

* Conservatively minded critics argued that funding these relief programs through deficit spending was too expensive and made people overly reliant on the government.

**Recovery**

* FDR felt the way to recovery was to stimulate demand for goods
* FDR differed from Hoover's policy of giving money to the public.
* FDR wanted to put money into consumers’ hands, hoping to increase their spending, thereby increasing demand.

**Reform**

* Reforms were aimed at fixing the defects in the American economy so the depression wouldn't happen again
* These Reforms were based on the belief that the government should protect individuals against risks that they could not handle on their own
* This would increase the power and presence of the national govt in the lives of all Americans into the future.

**Banks**

* Relief was given to all banks
* Shut down all banks and give a bank holiday. 4-day national bank holiday
* Bank closings soon decreased as he gave the people time to process and have faith. Public confidence was restored.
* FDR explained what he was doing through national radio broadcasts.

1. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation - insured banks so people would not lose any savings in the event of a. bank failure restored faith in banks.
2. Emergency Banking Act – 5% of banks shut down completely; the rest helped with grants and advice.

* the government provided money to 5000 trustworthy banks to restart the banking system.
* All banks were closed for a four day holiday and only well - run ones could re-open, with loans to support them.
* over $1 billion was redeposited by customers in 1933 and confidence was restored.

3. Security Exchange Commission: the government also lent money to banks to help them stay afloat. The SEC also tightened up regulations to stop reckless speculations and to prevent another crash.

**Unemployed**

13 million people were unemployed 1933, 25% of the workforce

**a. Civilian Conservation Corporation (CCC)** -

* Gave young men voluntary employment.
* Jobs included planting trees, cleaning up parks, and living in government camps.
* Low-wage paying jobs but provided 2 million men with jobs.

**b. Public works administration (PWA)** -

* Created jobs initiating major public construction projects like building schools, roads, and bridges.
* Spent $7 billion creating jobs

**c. Civil Works Administration(CWA)** –

* Provided temporary work in the winters of 1933-4.

* 4 million jobs were created. The scheme ended in April 1934

* National Labour Relation Act- gave employees permission from trade unions

* Norris-la Guardia Act- prohibited courts or the govt from stopping peaceful union strikes

**Farmers**

* Agricultural Adjustment Act - paid farmers to plant less to increase crop prices
* Million acres of sown land ploughed and 6 million piglets slaughtered
* Prices rose, and farmers’ incomes doubled
* However, many farm labourers ended up unemployed.

**Industry**

**National Industrial Recovery Act.**

* **National recovery administration (NRA)** - set business codes to stabilize production and price and improve working conditions and pay.
* Voluntary codes for each industry for regulation of prices, output, hours, and wages.
* The public was encouraged to buy from businesses that implemented these codes.
* This led to improvement in working conditions and reduced child labour.
* However, favoured large firms and forced smaller firms out of business.
* Supreme Court ruled the scheme unconstitutional and wasn’t revived again.

**The Poor**

* Federal Emergency Relief Administration (FERA) - gave money for emergency relief to those in need. Budget - $500 million.
* Funded soup kitchens and set up work schemes and nursery schools.

**What was the TVA?**

* Tennessee Valley Authority aimed to restore the prosperity of the Tennesee Valley area as its local agriculture was severely affected by floods, and 4.5 million people depended on government relief schemes.
* Forests were planted, dams were built, and flood prevention schemes were introduced.
* New industries attracted and hence became a showcase for the New Deal.
