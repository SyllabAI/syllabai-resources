## Did the fact that the New Deal did not solve unemployment mean that it was a failure?

**The New Deal was a failure**

| **Unemployment** | Never fell lower than 14% of the workforce in the 1930s |
|----|----|
| **Trade unions** | Businessmen and industrialists weren’t happy with the encouragement under the Wagner Act. Violence was opted by some businesses to break up strikes. |
| **Small farmers** | Tenant farmers, labourers, and sharecroppers were forced off the land by government plans to reduce agricultural production. |
| **Black Americans** | Didn’t end racial discrimination. Support of the democrats in the south was needed; hence, civil rights laws couldn’t be passed as they would oppose it. |
| **Industry** | Recovery was held back due to an increase in rules and regulations, taxation, and the formation of trade unions. |
| **Second World War** | Dec 1941 – America began defence spending and supplying armaments to Britain and France. Arguably said that this was the reason for the economic revival of 1940 and 1941 rather than the New Deal. |




**The New Deal was a success.**

| **Unemployment** | Fell by over 30% between 1933 and 1939 |
|----|----|
| **Trade unions** | Memberships increased to 7 million following the Wagner Act. Disputes settled in workers’ favour.·  Working conditions and pay improved |
| **Farmers** | Large-scale farmers benefited. Acreage and livestock reduction, price increases. |
| **The poor** | Welfare payments and other emergency benefits helped millions of Americans. America was seen as more compassionate. |
| **Industry** | Roosevelt’s Emergency Banking Act saved many. Future industrial development and prosperity from the construction projects. |
| **Morale** | FDR’s policies gave people hope, confidence, and a sense of purpose. The New Deal ensured the survival of America without resorting to extreme measures. |
