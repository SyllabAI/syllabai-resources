# What were the causes and consequences of the Wall Street Crash?

## How far was speculation responsible for the Wall Street Crash?

### **What are Shares?**

A share is a percentage of ownership in a company or a financial asset. Investors who hold shares in any company are known as shareholders.

* During the 1920s, the American stock market was steadily rising.
* Speculators bought shares and sold them within a few weeks to pocket an easy gain.
* In the mid-1920s – speculators bought shares “on the margin” – borrowing money from banks to fund the share purchase.
* The loan was repaid when shares were later sold. Banks lend up to 90% of the share price.

In October 1929, panic selling of shares in the market led to a “crash”, which marked the beginning of the Great Depression.

**Timeline of the crash.**

* 18th October - prices began falling.
* 19th October - 3.5 million shares sold. Prices fall.
* 21st Oct - over 6 million shares change hands.
* 24th Oct- Black Thursday - 13 million shares sold. No buyers
* 26th October - President Hoover, the fundamental business of the country is on a spend and secure basis
* 29th Oct - 16 million shares sold. No buyers.
* Prices on the stock market collapsed

### **Causes of the Wall Street Crash**

**a. The actions of speculators**

People started selling stocks altogether, and the market collapsed as anticipation of stock market prices going down scared people.

**b. Exports**

* The US has limited opportunities for exporting its products.
* European customers were impoverished and had not yet recovered from the financial strains of the First World War.
* American tariffs – tariffs set by customers that made it difficult for American exporters to operate in foreign markets.

**c. Uneven distribution of income**

* Between 50 and 60% - of Americans were too poor to take part in the consumer boom of the 1920s
* Low wages and unemployment in traditional industries and among blacks and immigrants.
* 5% of the population was receiving 33% of the income in 1929.
* Too much money in too few hands.
* Mass production – mass consumption – higher wages.

**d. Overproduction**

By 1929, there was more production of consumer goods than there were consumers.

The market saturated as Americans with the money bought everything they needed.

**e. Signs of an economic slow-down**

* Signs of boom ending before October 1929
* By 1927, fewer new houses were built, sales of cars were declining, and wage increases were levelling off.
* Stock levels in warehouses are increasing, suggesting the economy is slowing down.
* Made investors nervous and anxious to sell shares at the first sign of serious trouble.
