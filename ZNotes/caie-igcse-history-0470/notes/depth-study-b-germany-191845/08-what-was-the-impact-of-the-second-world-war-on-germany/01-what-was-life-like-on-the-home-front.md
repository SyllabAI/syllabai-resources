# What was the impact of the Second World War on Germany?

## What was life like on the home front?

* ##   **1939–41**

  * **High morale and limited disruption (early war years)**

    * Early military successes (such as quick victories in Poland and Western Europe) created confidence and optimism among German civilians.

    * Many people believed the war would be short, so there was no immediate panic or hardship.

    * Because Germany was not yet being bombed heavily, everyday life remained relatively stable compared to later years.

  * **Controlled economy with mild rationing**

    * The government introduced rationing of food, clothing, and fuel, but shortages were not extreme in this period.

    * Supplies were supported by resources taken from conquered territories, helping to maintain living standards.

    * The war economy was not fully mobilised yet, meaning civilian life was still partly protected from total war conditions.

  * **Strong propaganda and government control**

    * The Nazi Party tightly controlled newspapers, radio, and films to shape public opinion.

    * Propaganda praised German victories and encouraged loyalty to Adolf Hitler.

    * Negative news (e.g. losses or hardships) was hidden, which helped to maintain morale and prevent opposition.

  * **Changing roles and labour shortages**

    * As men were sent to fight, Germany began to face labour shortages in industry and agriculture.

    * Women were encouraged to take on some jobs, especially in farming and factories, but the Nazis did not fully mobilise women due to their traditional views.

    * To fill gaps, Germany increasingly relied on foreign workers and prisoners from occupied countries.

  * **Early civil defence measures with limited danger**

    * The government introduced blackouts, air raid precautions, and shelters to prepare for possible attacks.

    * However, before 1941 there was little bombing of German cities, so these measures were more precautionary than necessary.

    * As a result, civilians did not yet experience the fear and destruction seen later in the war.

##  1941–1943

* **Declining morale due to military setbacks and bombing**

  * The invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941 and especially the defeat at Stalingrad (1943) marked a turning point.

  * Germans began to realise the war would be long and difficult, reducing earlier optimism.

  * At the same time, Allied bombing raids on cities like Hamburg and Cologne caused destruction, deaths, and fear, lowering morale further.

* **Total war and tighter economic control**

  * By 1942–43, the government moved towards a “total war” economy, using all resources for military purposes.

  * Rationing became much stricter, and shortages of food, fuel, and basic goods became more noticeable.

  * Civilian living standards declined significantly as more production was focused on weapons.

* **Increased propaganda and repression**

  * The Nazi Party intensified propaganda to keep people loyal despite worsening conditions.

  * After defeats, propaganda tried to present them as temporary setbacks or blamed enemies.

  * Opposition was dealt with harshly, and fear of punishment helped to limit resistance.

* **Expansion of labour force and exploitation**

  * Labour shortages became severe as more men were sent to fight.

  * Women were increasingly pushed into factory work and essential services, especially after 1943.

  * Germany relied heavily on forced labour from occupied countries and prisoners of war to sustain production.

* **Growing impact of war on daily life**

  * Frequent air raids meant civilians had to spend time in air raid shelters and follow strict blackout rules.

  * Many people lost homes and had to evacuate, especially children sent to safer rural areas.

  * Daily life became more stressful, dangerous, and uncertain.

##  1944–1945

* **Collapse of morale and widespread fear**

  * Continuous defeats and the advance of Allied forces into Germany destroyed any remaining confidence in victory.

  * Civilians faced constant bombing, loss of homes, and fear of invasion, especially from the Soviet east.

  * Many Germans lost faith in Adolf Hitler, though open opposition remained dangerous.

* **Severe destruction and humanitarian crisis**

  * Cities such as Dresden and Berlin were heavily destroyed, leaving millions homeless.

  * Transport systems, factories, and infrastructure collapsed, making daily survival extremely difficult.

  * Large numbers of refugees fled from advancing armies, creating a humanitarian crisis.

* **Breakdown of economy and shortages**

  * The war economy began to collapse as bombing destroyed factories and supply lines.

  * Food, fuel, and medical supplies were extremely scarce, leading to hunger and hardship.

  * Basic services like electricity, transport, and communication often failed.

* **Desperate measures and total mobilisation**

  * The Nazi regime introduced desperate policies, including the Volkssturm (a militia of old men and young boys).

  * Women, children, and the elderly were all expected to contribute to the war effort.

  * Forced labour continued but became less effective as the system broke down.

* **End of Nazi control and chaos**

  * The authority of the Nazi Party weakened as defeat became inevitable.

  * Government control, propaganda, and organisation became less effective in maintaining order.

  * By 1945, Germany faced total collapse, leading to surrender and the end of the war in Europe.
