**1(a)** 

 

Reason 1: To reduce unemployment 

Governments may support business startups due to the fact that with new businesses come new job vacancies. Start-ups usually create employment for the economy, and as a macroeconomic objective, it is in the government’s interest to promote this. 

 

Reason 2: To increase competition

New business start-ups may receive grants or financial help from the government in order to promote competition between businesses. As competition forces businesses to keep their prices low at the market price, start-ups will be forced to try and be as efficient as possible. E.g. A clothing brand startup may have to keep the prices of their t-shirts in line with existing clothing brand companies in order to attract sales.

 

Reason 3: To increase tax revenue 

Governments have an incentive to receive tax revenue from firms– this includes startup businesses which are new to the market. New start-ups will have to pay corporate taxes as do other firms, and governments use this to finance public spending, pensions, etc. 

 

Reason 4: To increase customer choice 

Governments may support business start-ups to boost the level of consumer choice and variety in the market, as start-ups may offer new products/new varieties of goods that customers may benefit from. E.g. a tech start up may be offering new software or digital tools that are foreign to the market and can help other companies.

 

Therefore, governments will have the interest of supporting business startups as these firms usually generate economic activity that is healthy for a country. 

**1(b)** 

Increase in the legal minimum wage: AF already pays its 10 skilled employees above minimum wage, so their wages may not change. However, if wage requirements rise, AF could face higher costs. If AF doesn't raise wages while competitors do, it risks losing employees. Still, higher pay could motivate staff and improve productivity. This could therefore increase profits if employees are able to make cost savings through higher productivity. 

 

Additional health and safety regulations: New health and safety regulations will increase costs for AF, as Sam must provide employee training. However, this may also boost productivity by reducing workplace accidents and improving morale, as employees may feel valued. On the other hand, additional costs may arise from purchasing required safety equipment. These costs may reduce the profit margin, thereby reducing profit generated. 

 

Employees can only work a maximum of 50 hours each week: If employees are limited to 50 hours per week, Sam may need to hire additional staff, increasing costs through wages, training, and recruitment. However, reduced hours could boost employee motivation and performance. This change may be less convenient during peak tourist seasons when demand is higher, and can lead to AF directly losing out on market share if they lose customers due to this implication. 

 

Conclusion: The new legal controls on employee working hours will affect AF the most. This is because Sam will have to employ additional workers to keep up with the high demand in the Main city, and this will greatly increase his direct costs. If he does not employ more workers, AF may lose business if unable to manage the high demand.  Additionally, lower wages for the current workers who will work fewer hours may discourage and demotivate them, and reduce their productivity when cooking. Minimum wage laws may not affect AF, since Sam is already paying his employees above the minimum wage. Safety regulations may also not have a direct impact on the business if they are a one-time cost, and increase productivity and worker welfare.
