**4(a)** 

Retained profit is the earnings of a business left over once all costs, tax and dividends have been paid. 

Advantage 1: does not have to be repaid 

Since retained profit does not have to be repaid to any entity, it helps keep the costs of financing the new investments low, unlike external sources such as bank loans, which will have to be repaid. 

 

Advantage 2: No collateral or security needed 

Retained profit does not require collateral or security to be put down when using it as a source of finance. This can ensure that AF does not need to take assets out of work to use as collateral, as would be done if using an external source of finance. 

 

Disadvantage 1: may not be sufficient 

Since Sam has only been in a partnership with his brother for 2 years, they may not have accumulated enough retained profit to use as finance for an investment. This may leave them with insufficient funds, and they may have to turn to external sources for the rest. 

 

Disadvantage 2: reduces profit to owners 

If retained profit is used as a source of finance, it may discourage Sam and his brother as there will be less returned to them as a reward for risk taking in AF. This could lead to the opportunity cost of reduced future investment if Sam or his brother are not happy with the returns on their investment.

The higher price of meal 1 may also make customers think it is of a higher quality, which may make them more willing to try it.
