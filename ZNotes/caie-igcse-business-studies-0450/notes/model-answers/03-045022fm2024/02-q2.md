Reasoning: AF may be able to identify a gap by segmenting the market, as it opens the business to specific market segments whose needs require catering to. AF can create meals for a specific target market segment, such as vegans who are looking for authentic street food, and satisfy their wants. This can help them gain more sales compared to making general meals for the whole population, and also brings them more customers, as these niche markets may not have other food stalls to go to for their needs. 

 

Benefit 2: May be able to charge a higher price

Reasoning: If AF segments its market, it may be able to charge a high price for meals such as “fusion street food” that offers a mix of different cuisines in one meal or “gluten free street food” that takes into account strict dietary needs. Since many customers may not be able to eat these meals elsewhere, they may be more willing to pay higher prices if their niche needs are being met at AF. This can lead to higher revenue for AF, as well as a loyal customer base. 

 

**2(b)** 

Price - using penetration pricing: penetration pricing can help attract customers to try the new meal as it keeps the price low– incentivizing more customers to purchase it. This can help AF gain revenue when the new meal is first launched to the market. However, a low price may indicate to customers that the meal is low quality– and may tarnish AF’s reputation in the Main city. Penetration pricing may also not be useful as the target market is people from country A who are high income earners– and may want to splurge on quality food. Therefore, it may be more sensible to use price skimming for the new meal. 

 

Place- – selling to food retailers in Main City: selling the new meal through food retailers may help AF gain new exposure to a wider population of individuals, as food retailers may have access to a wider customer base. This can help attract and create more sales. However, food retailers also stock competitors' meals, which may cause customers to choose those meals over AF’s new meal, which can result in losses for AF. Retailers also require a markup on the products they sell, so AF may be required to lower the price of their meals when selling them to retailers– leaving lower profit margins for AF. Therefore, Sam may be better off with AF selling their own meals through street market stalls. 

 

Advertising – using social media: social media is used by millions of people, so AF may be exposed to a wider customer base by advertising the new meal on social media platforms. Using targeted advertising, AF may also be able to reach the 100,000 people from country A who are currently working in country X–this may be through integrating the language or customs of country A into the social media adverts– and makes it likelier that they purchase the new meal. Social media may also encourage customers to leave good reviews, comments or posts about AF’s meals– which can increase publicity and boost the brand reputation. 

 

Conclusion: The marketing mix will be effective if a different pricing strategy is used. For AF’s new meals, penetration pricing will not effectively convey the quality of the meal, making it less likely that higher income earners from country A will purchase them. AF may be better off using price skimming, which will create the illusion of a high-quality meal, and also increase the revenue brought in on the new meals.
