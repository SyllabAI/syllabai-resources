Banks are a very important user of business accounts. Banks may judge on whether or not to provide a loan to a business based on their published accounts, by assessing their assets and liquidity. Banks may also judge a businesses ability to pay back their loans based on their accounts, and thus decide whether it is safe to continue lending to a business or not.

Other users of accounts may be suppliers. Suppliers of a business may use their accounts to assess their liquidity position to see if the business is able to pay them back. They may use this as an indicator on whether or not they should maintain relationships with the business and continue supplying to them, or if the business poses a risk of being unable to pay them back.

Banks are most likely to be the main users of business accounts. This is because banks may be lending large sums and loans to businesses, and are at higher risk if the business is unable to pay them back. Suppliers may still lend to businesses on credit if they have good relations with the business, and may only reduce the discounts they offer if the business is unable to pay them back on time.
