## Genre and Mode

* **Mode:** the way in which language is used in a text. It ranges from spoken to written language and, increasingly, to multimodal texts which combine both. It just means how meaning is made — for example, through words, images, sound, gesture, movement or layout.

* **Multimodal texts:** a text that uses more than one mode (or way) of communicating meaning. So, if a text combines writing and pictures, or writing and speech, it is multimodal. It is also important to understand different types of mode, such as synchronous (happening in real-time, e.g. face-to-face conversation) and asynchronous (not happening in real-time, e.g. emails or letters).

* The **genre** of a text is essentially its category or type — for example, poetry, fiction, newspaper articles and so on. This is influenced by factors such as **content, form and audience**. 

* **Genre conventions** are the accepted rules or norms associated with different types of text. For example, a romantic letter may use warm and emotional language, while a news report would maintain a formal and objective tone. 

* Both **mode and genre** can significantly impact how a message is conveyed and received. Understanding these can provide valuable insights when analysing texts of all sorts. It is integral to recognize that both mode and genre can blend together. Consider an online news article (mode) which falls under the genre of journalism. It can include elements from other modes or genres, such as embedded video clips or personal blog-style commentary.
