# Velocity of waves on water

Apparatus:
- Ripple tank (with transparent base)
- Ruler
- Stopwatch
- Power supply (to generate waves)
- Lamp (to illuminate waves on the screen below the tank)
- White card or paper (for clear viewing of the wave pattern on the surface)

Method:

- Fill the ripple tank with a shallow layer of water and place it over a white paper or screen to project the wave pattern clearly.
- Adjust the light source so it illuminates the wave patterns from above.

- Turn on the ripple tank’s motor to create regular, consistent waves on the water’s surface.
- Use a ruler placed on the white paper below the tank to measure the distance between two consecutive wave crests. This distance is the wavelength. 
- Measure the distance across several crests, then divide by the number of wavelengths to get an average.
- The motor in the ripple tank has a set frequency, which should be given (e.g., 10 Hz). Alternatively, you can count the number of waves passing a fixed point in a certain amount of time and divide by the time to find the frequency.
- Use $\text{speed} = \text{frequency} * \text{wavelength}$ to find the speed
