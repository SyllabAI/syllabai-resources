## Electric Fields

- An electric field is a region around a charged object where other charged objects experience a force.
- The direction of the electric field is defined as the direction in which a positive test charge would move if placed in the field.
- Field lines show the strength and direction of an electric field. The closer the lines, the stronger the field. They point away from positive charges and towards negative charges.
- Around a point charge, the electric field is radial, with lines radiating outwards from a positive charge and inwards towards a negative charge. Between charged plates, the field is uniform, with parallel lines running from the positive plate to the negative plate.
- Static electricity effects, such as attraction and repulsion, occur because charged objects create electric fields that exert forces on other charged objects within the field.
