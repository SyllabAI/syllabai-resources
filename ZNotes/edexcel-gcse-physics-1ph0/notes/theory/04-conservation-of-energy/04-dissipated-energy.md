## Dissipated Energy

* Dissipated energy is defined as *the energy that is lost to the surroundings*, usually as heat and sometimes as sound, and can no longer be used to do useful work.

* When temperature in a system increases due to mechanical processes, some energy is dissipated to the surroundings as heat.

* This reduces the efficiency because less input energy is transferred to useful output.

* For instance, some of the electrical energy supplied to a light bulb is dissipated as heat to the surroundings. This energy can no longer be used to produce light and reduces the bulb’s efficiency.

* In every system, some energy is dissipated and the efficiency is lowered. However, this dissipated energy is never completely destroyed— it just spreads out to surroundings.
