## The law of Conservation of Energy

* The law of conservation of energy states that **‘energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred into its surroundings’.**

  For example when a ball is dropped from a height, the stored gravitational potential energy of the ball at the top is transferred into kinetic energy of the ball as it falls. At the top, all energy is gravitational potential; at the bottom just before impact, all energy is kinetic.

* One example of an energy transfer for a car engine is stored chemical potential energy in the fuel to thermal energy store of particles to kinetic energy of the moving parts of the motor.

* However, not all of the energy produced is useful energy, some of it is also wasted.

* For example, a TV is designed to produce images and sounds, thermal energy is wasted as you don’t want a TV to heat up at all. We say the waste energy is dissipated (spreads out) to heat the surroundings.

**Efficiency**

* Efficiency is a ratio that compares the useful energy output to the total energy input. Since both quantities are measured in the same unit (J), the units cancel out Therefore, efficiency does not have any units.

* To calculate the percentage efficiency of a product (percentage of useful energy produced) you use the formula

  $$
  efficiency = \frac{\text{useful energy transferred by the device}}{\text{total energy supplied to the device}}  \times100.
  $$

**Example question:**

Q1) A hairdryer use 200 J of electrical energy. Only 50 J of this energy is converted  to kinetic energy in the moving air.

a) How much energy was wasted?

b) Calculate the efficiency of the hairdryer.

**Answer:**

Part a)

Energy wasted = Total input energy - Total output energy

= 200 J - 50 J

= 150 J

Part b)

Efficiency = $\frac{\text{useful energy transferred by the device}}{\text{total energy supplied to the device}}$ x 100.

= $\frac{50J}{200J}$ x 100.

= 25%

Q2) A machine is 40% efficient and takes in 500 J of energy. What is the useful energy output of this machine?

**Answer:**

efficiency **=** $\frac{\text{useful energy transferred by the device}}{total energy supplied to the device}$ x 100

40 = $\frac{\text{Useful energy output}}{500J}$ x 100.

Useful energy output = 200 J
