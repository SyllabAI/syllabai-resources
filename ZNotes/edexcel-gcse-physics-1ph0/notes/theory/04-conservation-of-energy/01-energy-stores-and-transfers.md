### **Gravitational Potential:**

* It is the energy stored in an object, due to its position or state, that has the potential to do work.

* The higher the height for an object, the higher its gravitational potential energy.

* If two objects have equal height and equal gravity but different masses, the one that has the higher mass has the highest gravitational potential.

* Change in gravitational potential energy (J) = mass (kg) * gravitational field strength (10 N/Kg) * change in vertical height (m).

  $$
  ∆GPE = m× g ×∆h
  $$

### **Kinetic Energy:**

* Kinetic energy is the energy an object possesses due to being in motion.
* The formula for the change in kinetic energy (J) is $\frac{1}{2} mv^2$ where m is the mass (Kg) and v is the velocity (m/s).
* The higher the mass and velocity the higher the kinetic energy.

$$
KE =\frac{1}{2} mv^2
$$

**Note: It is important to square the velocity.**

### **Example question:**

Q1) A ball of mass 5kg is dropped from a platform 6m high. Calculate

a) the GPE of the ball

b) the speed of the ball just before hitting the ground.

**Answer:**

Part a) ∆GPE = m× g ×∆h

∆GPE = 5kg $\times10 N/kg \times6m$

=3000J

Part b) We assume all the GPE of the ball is transferred to the KE of the ball just before hitting the ground, therefore

$\Delta GPE$ = KE

KE = 3000 J

KE = $\frac{1}{2} mv^2$

3000 J = $\frac{1}{2} (5kg \times v^2$)

$\sqrt{v^2}$  = $\sqrt{\frac{3000 J\times2}{5kg}}$

v = 34.6 m/s
