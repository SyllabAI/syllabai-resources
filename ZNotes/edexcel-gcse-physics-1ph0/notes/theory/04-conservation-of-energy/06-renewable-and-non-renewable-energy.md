## **Renewable and Non-Renewable Energy**

### **Renewable Energy:**

* Renewable energy is energy derived from natural sources that are replenished at a higher rate than they are consumed.
* Some examples of renewable energy are solar energy and wind energy.
* The table shows how useful energy is produced from renewable resources, and the advantages and disadvantages for each type of source,


|Energy|How it is used to produce  useful energy|Advantages|Disadvantage|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|Solar energy|Solar panels convert sunlight into electrical energy|Can power remote regions since sunlight is abundant|Can only be used at day time|
|Tidal Energy|In areas with strong tidal activity, underwater turbines use the kinetic energy    ( KE) of the rising and falling tides to generate electricity|Produces  energy both when tides rise and fall|Expensive and is limited to coastal regions with suitable tide range|
|Wave Energy|Surface waves pass through floating or underwater devices connected to generators produce electricity|Constant wave motion offers a steady energy potential|Difficult and costly to build and maintain|
|Hydroelectric Energy|Turbines installed in areas of flowing or falling water produce electricity as their blades, connected to generators, rotate|Provides a steady controllable source of power that can easily meet energy demands|Few areas suitable|
|Geothermal Energy|High temperatures beneath the Earth’s surface produce steam that rotates turbines, powering generators to produce electricity|Provides  a constant, reliable power source|Requires deep drilling and expensive|
|Wind Energy|Moving air spins turbine blades connected to a generator producing electricity|Offshore turbines can harness strong coastal winds without using up land|Only effective in areas with frequent, strong winds|
|Biofuel|Burning or decomposing organic matter produces heat, which can be used to drive power generators|Uses waste materials and reduces landfill|It requires a large area of land, and burning releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas|

### **Non-renewable Energy**

* Non-renewable energy is energy that is used at a faster rate than produced rendering them finite.
* They produce carbon dioxide emissions which contribute to global warming.
* Some examples are natural gas, nuclear energy, oil and coal.

### **Global patterns and trends in the use of energy resources**

* Non-renewable resources, especially fossil fuels (coal, oil, and natural gas), are still widely used because they are readily available, inexpensive, and provide a reliable, continuous power supply. Developing countries continue to depend heavily on these resources as their main energy source.

* However, the use of renewable energy is increasing globally as awareness of the environmental impacts of fossil fuels continues to grow. Developed nations are steadily investing in research and advanced technologies to make the use of natural resources such as wind and solar energy, more efficient and cost-effective.

* In the future, the global energy patterns are expected to shift strongly towards renewable resources, while the reliance on finite energy sources is likely to experience a significant decline.

* Overall, global energy trends are shifting from non-renewable to renewable resources as the awareness of sustainability grows and  technologies for these energy sources become more advanced.
