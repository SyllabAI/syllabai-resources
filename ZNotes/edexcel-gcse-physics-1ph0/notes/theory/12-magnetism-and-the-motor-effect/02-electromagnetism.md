## Electromagnetism

- When an electric current flows through a wire, it generates a magnetic field around it. This can be demonstrated using iron filings or compasses around the wire.
- The magnetic field forms circles around the wire, with the direction determined by the right-hand rule (thumb in the direction of current, fingers curl in the direction of the field).
- The strength of a magnetic field increases with:
  - Higher current in the wire.
  - Closer proximity to the wire.
- A solenoid (a coil of wire) produces a strong magnetic field inside the coil, resembling a bar magnet, with field lines running from one end to the other.
- Outside the solenoid, the magnetic field is weaker and spreads out.
- The field inside is uniform and strong, while outside, it behaves similarly to the field of a bar magnet, with field lines emerging from the ends of the coil.
