## Magnetic Forces

- A current-carrying wire placed in a magnetic field experiences a force perpendicular to both the current direction and the magnetic field. This is the basis for electric motors.
- The force is caused by the interaction between the magnetic field generated by the current and the external magnetic field. This is described by Fleming’s left-hand rule.
- Force on a conductor in a magnetic field is equal to $\text{F} = \text{B} * \text{I} * \text{L}$
  - F is force
  - B is tesla
  - I is current
  - L is length
- Electric motors work by placing a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field, causing it to rotate due to the forces exerted on different parts of the wire. This rotation is harnessed to do work.
