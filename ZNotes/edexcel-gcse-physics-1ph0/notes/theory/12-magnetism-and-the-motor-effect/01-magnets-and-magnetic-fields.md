# Magnetism

## Magnets and Magnetic Fields

- Magnets have north and south poles. When two magnets are brought close together:
  - Like poles (N-N or S-S) repel each other.
  - Opposite poles (N-S) attract each other.

- The force between magnets weakens with increased distance.
- Permanent magnets always retain their magnetic properties. 
- Induced magnets become magnetic only when placed in a magnetic field. When removed from the field, they lose their magnetism.
- Permanent magnets are used in compasses, fridge magnets, and electric motors.
- Temporary magnets (like electromagnets) are used in cranes for lifting metal in scrapyards, in electric bells, and relays.
- Magnetic field lines are invisible lines that show the direction of magnetic forces.
- The lines are closer together near the poles, indicating stronger magnetic forces, and spread out farther from the magnet where the force weakens.
- A plotting compass has a small magnetized needle that aligns with a magnetic field.
- By placing multiple compasses around a magnet, the direction of the needles can trace the magnetic field lines, revealing their shape.
- A magnetic compass points toward the Earth’s magnetic north pole.
