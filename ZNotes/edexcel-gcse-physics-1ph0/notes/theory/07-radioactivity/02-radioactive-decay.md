## Radioactive Decay

### Types of Radiation:

- Alpha - emits heavy, positively charged particles made of two protons and two neutrons, which are highly ionizing but have low penetration power. Stopped by a sheet of paper or even a few centimeters of air.
- Beta - emits fast-moving electrons (beta-minus) or positrons (beta-plus), which are moderately ionizing and have medium penetration power. Stopped by a few millimeters of aluminum or plastic.
- Gamma - emits high-energy electromagnetic waves that are weakly ionizing but have very high penetration power, able to pass through most materials. Stopped by several centimeters of lead or a few meters of concrete, although it can penetrate most materials to some extent.

### Background Radiation:

- Radioactivity levels are measured using a Geiger-muller tube. 
- When measuring radioactivity you need to take account of the background radiation. So measure the total background radiation in 30 seconds. Then measure your radioactivity then subtract the total background radiation.
- Some sources of background radiation are, food, rocks, cosmic rays and plants.

### Beta Decay:

- Beta-minus decay occurs when a neutron in the nucleus changes into a proton, releasing an electron (beta particle).

- Beta-plus decay occurs when a proton in the nucleus changes into a neutron, releasing a positron (a positively charged beta particle).

 ### Half Life:

-  Half-life is the time taken for half of the radioactive nuclei in a substance to decay, and it helps predict how the activity of the substance decreases over time.
- The unit of activity is the becquerel (Bq), which represents one decay per second.
