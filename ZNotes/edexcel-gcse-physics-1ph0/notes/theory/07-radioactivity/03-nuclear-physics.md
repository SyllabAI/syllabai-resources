## Nuclear Physics

### Nuclear Power:

- Nuclear power produces large amounts of electricity with low greenhouse gas emissions.
- However Nuclear power generates radioactive waste, which is hazardous and requires long-term storage.
- While nuclear power is a low-carbon energy source, concerns about radioactive waste and potential accidents make its use controversial.

### Nuclear Reactions:

- The three types of nuclear reactions are fission, fusion, and radioactive decay.
- Nuclear reactions release large amounts of energy, which can be harnessed for electricity generation.

### Nuclear Fission:

- The fission of U-235 produces two smaller nuclei, additional neutrons, and releases a significant amount of energy.
- A chain reaction occurs when the neutrons released by nuclear fission cause further fission in nearby nuclei, leading to a self-sustaining series of reactions.
- In a nuclear power station, control rods absorb excess neutrons to regulate and control the chain reaction.
- The thermal energy from a chain reaction heats water to produce steam, which drives turbines connected to generators, producing electricity.
- The products of nuclear fission are radioactive and emit radiation, which requires careful handling and disposal.

### Nuclear Fusion:

- In nuclear fusion, two light atomic nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy in the process.
- Nuclear fusion is the process that powers stars, including our Sun, by fusing hydrogen into helium.
- Nuclear fusion combines light nuclei, while nuclear fission splits heavy nuclei into smaller ones, both releasing energy.
- High temperatures and pressures are necessary for fusion to overcome the repulsive forces between atomic nuclei.
