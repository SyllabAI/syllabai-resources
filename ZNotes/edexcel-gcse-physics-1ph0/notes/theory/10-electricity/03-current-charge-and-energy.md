## Current Charge and Energy

### Link Between Potential Difference, Charge, and Energy
- Potential difference (voltage) across a battery or component is the amount of energy transferred per unit charge.
- Charge (Q) is the quantity of electric charge passing through the component, measured in coulombs (C).
- Energy transferred (E) is the work done on the charge as it moves through a potential difference, measured in joules (J).
- The relationship is given by the equation:
- 𝐸=𝑄×𝑉

- E = Energy transferred (Joules, J)
- Q = Charge (Coulombs, C)
- V = Potential difference (Volts, V)
### Understanding Potential Difference (Voltage)
- The unit of potential difference is the volt (V).
- One volt is equivalent to one joule per coulomb of charge:
- 1V = 1J/C
- This means that if there is a potential difference of 1V across a component, 1 joule of energy is transferred for every 1 coulomb of charge passing through it.
### Equation for Energy Transfer, Charge, and Voltage
- The equation:
- 𝐸=𝑄×𝑉
- Energy transferred (E),
- Charge (Q) passing through a component, or
- The potential difference (V) across it.
### Electric Current and Electric Charge
- Electric current (I) is the flow of electric charge through a conductor.
- The unit of current is the ampere (A), where 1 ampere = 1 coulomb of charge passing through a point per second.
- The relationship between charge and current is: 
- 𝑄=𝐼×𝑡
- Q = Charge (Coulombs, C)
- I = Current (Amperes, A)
- t = Time (Seconds, s)
### Electric Current in Metals (Electron Flow)
- In metals, electric current is due to the movement of free electrons.
- Electrons move through the metal conductor when a potential difference is applied, transferring energy as they move from areas of negative charge to positive charge.
