# Astronomy

## Solar System

- The Solar System consists of the Sun, 8 planets, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets and satellites.
- The 8 planets in order (closest to furthest from the sun) are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

## Gravity

- Gravitational Strength is affected by mass and distance.
- Gravity is larger on bigger planets like Jupiter since they have a much larger mass.

## Orbits

- In order to orbit a planet or a star, gravity pulls in the object towards it.
- The gravitational force acting on the orbiting object is always larger, which means gravity acts towards the center of the larger object.
- The gravitational force will then cause the object to orbit around the larger body.
- A planet’s speed varies in its orbit. It moves faster when closer to the Sun and slower when farther due to the gravitational force.
- When the planet is closer to the sun the orbit radius increases and when its further it decreases.

## Life Cycle of a star

- Stars normally follow this life cycle: nebula (gas cloud) → main sequence star (fusion of hydrogen) → red giant → white dwarf.
- Stars larger than the Sun follow a similar cycle but end up as super giants, leading to supernovae. The remnants can form neutron stars or black holes.
- Stars are held together by inward and outward forces.
- The inward force is gravity and the outward force is the pressure caused by thermal expansion.
- If the inward force is bigger the star will contract and if the outward force is bigger the star will expand, else the star will remain in equilibrium.
