## **Ears and Hearing**

### **Parts of the ear:**

* Outer Ear (Pinna): Captures sound waves and aids in determining the direction of sound.
* Ear Canal: Directs sound waves to the eardrum and provides some amplification.
* Eardrum: Converts sound waves into mechanical vibrations.
* Ossicles: Amplify and transmit vibrations from the eardrum to the oval window.
* Oval Window: Transfers vibrations from the ossicles to the cochlea.
* Cochlea: Converts mechanical vibrations into electrical signals through hair cells.
* Auditory Nerve: Sends electrical signals to the brain where they are interpreted as sound.

### **How sound in air is converted to vibrations in solid:**

* Sound waves in the air hit the eardrum, causing it to vibrate.
* These vibrations are passed to the ossicles.
* The ossicles amplify the vibrations and transfer them to the oval window.
* The oval window vibrates, transmitting these vibrations into the fluid of the cochlea

### **Ultrasound:**

* To calculate the water depth using wave speed and time you use the formula $\frac{\text{v x t}}{2}$
* Sounds with frequencies greater than 20,000 Hz are called ultrasound.
* To get sonar ultrasound is used through this process:
  * An ultrasound wave is emitted from a sonar device.
  * The wave travels through water until it hits an object.
  * The wave reflects back to the sonar device.
  * The time taken for the wave to return is measured.
  * Using the wave velocity in water, the distance to the object is calculated.

### **Infrasound:**

* Sounds with frequencies less than 20 Hz are called infrasound.
* Some S waves are infrasound waves.
* Infrasound waves are used to detect natural disasters.
* S waves cannot travel through liquids however P waves can travel through all materials.
* The lack of S waves in the Earth’s core indicate that it is liquid.
