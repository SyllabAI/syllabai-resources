## Sentence Structures

#|
||

Sentence structure

|

Description

|

Usage

|

Example

||
||

Simple sentence

|

One independent clause, one subject + one predicate

|

For clarity, emphasis, and to introduce a point

|

“She loves music.”

||
||

Compound sentence

|

Two (or more) independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or, so, yet, for, nor)

|

To join two related ideas of equal importance

|

“She practices daily, and she also listens to new songs.”

||
||

Complex sentence

|

One independent clause + one or more dependent (subordinate) clauses (using because, although, if, when, etc.)

|

To show cause-effect, contrast, condition, time relationships

|

“Although she was tired, she still completed her homework.”

||
||

Compound‑complex sentence

|

At least two independent clauses + at least one dependent clause

|

To express multiple ideas and relationships in one sentence

|

“She composed a song because she felt inspired, and she shared it with her friends.”

||
||

Periodic / Loose / Inverted / Balanced sentences

|

Varied structures (e.g. placing subordinate clause before main clause, to delay the main clause)

|

For stylistic effect, suspense, emphasis

|

“Despite the rain, we decided to go ahead with the picnic.”
