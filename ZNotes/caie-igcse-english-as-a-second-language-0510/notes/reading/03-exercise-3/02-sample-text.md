## Sample text

Urban planners are rethinking city design through a concept known as the “15-minute city”. This idea proposes that everything a person needs — work, school, shops, healthcare, leisure — should be accessible within a 15-minute walk or cycle from home.

The main goal is to improve quality of life by reducing long commutes, lowering air pollution, and increasing local community interaction. In many cases, roads have been redesigned to favour pedestrians and cyclists over cars. Green spaces, once considered secondary, are now central to these neighbourhoods.

Supporters say the model encourages healthier lifestyles and stronger local economies, as people shop more often at nearby businesses. There's also a benefit for the environment, since it reduces car use and carbon emissions.

However, there is some resistance. Critics argue that the changes limit freedom of movement or could increase housing prices, pushing out lower-income families. Some business owners also worry that reducing road traffic might reduce customer numbers.

For cities adopting this model, experts advise starting small — with pilot zones — and collecting feedback from residents. It’s important to invest in infrastructure like cycle lanes and affordable housing, and to involve local communities in planning decisions from the start.
