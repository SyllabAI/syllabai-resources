## Sample text

### **A. Emilia**

At first, I only signed up for the local animal shelter because my teacher suggested it. I’d never worked with animals before and honestly, I was a bit nervous. On my first day, I had to clean cages and walk some of the dogs, and I thought, "This isn’t for me." But I stuck with it, and after a few weeks, I started feeling more comfortable. I got to know the staff and the animals, and now I look forward to my shifts. Eventually, I was asked to help train new volunteers, which gave me more confidence. I didn’t expect this experience to change me so much, but I’m more patient and responsible now.

### **B. Zach**

I wasn’t planning to volunteer, but a friend invited me to a beach clean-up event, and I decided to go along. We spent the morning collecting litter and sorting it into bags. It was surprisingly satisfying. I liked that I could see the difference we were making immediately. Since then, I’ve joined a monthly clean-up group. I started posting photos on Instagram to show what we were doing, and a few of my classmates said they wanted to join, too. That made me feel like I was doing something that mattered—not just for the environment, but for other people too.

### **C. Farah**

I was very close to my grandparents growing up, and they were always active in the community. So when I heard about a programme that connected teenagers with elderly people who live alone, I joined without hesitation. I visit one woman every Saturday, and we talk for about an hour. She tells me stories from her childhood, and sometimes we do puzzles or listen to music. I’ve come to realise how lonely some people are, and it’s made me appreciate my own family more. I’ve also learned a lot just by listening. It’s not exciting work, but I know it matters.

### **D. Toby**

I’ve always loved working with my hands—building things, repairing stuff—so when my school announced a project where students could fix bikes for local kids, I signed up immediately. We receive broken or old bikes and get them into working condition. Then we give them to families who can’t afford to buy new ones. I enjoy every step of the process, from replacing wheels to testing brakes. It’s not just fun; it’s meaningful. I’ve started to wonder if I could make a career out of this, maybe as a mechanic or engineer. This experience showed me I can use my skills in a useful way.

### **E. Olivia**

I admit I didn’t join the recycling club because I cared deeply about the environment—I just needed something extra for my school application. But once I got involved, I realised how much waste our school produces, and I became more passionate about reducing it. I helped organise a recycling awareness week, and we even convinced the canteen to switch to reusable containers.
