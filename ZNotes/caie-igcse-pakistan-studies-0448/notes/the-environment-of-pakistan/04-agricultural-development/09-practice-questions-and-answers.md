## **Practice questions and answers**

### **1. Distinguish between traditional rice farming and barani wheat farming in terms of inputs, processes, and outputs.**

* **Traditional rice farming inputs:** Small fragmented plots ($<2$ hectares), family labour, bullocks, nursery-germinated seeds, hand tools, manure, and abundant water via perennial canals or monsoon flooding in clay-rich soils.

* **Traditional rice farming processes:** Bullocks ploughing, manual seedling transplantation into flooded fields, manual weeding, and hand-sickle harvesting with animal/wooden-frame threshing.

* **Traditional rice farming outputs:** Rice grains for food/export and crop stalks.

* **Barani wheat farming inputs:** Potwar Plateau rain-fed land, winter monsoon depressions, terraced fields, family labour, limited improved seeds, and minimal chemical fertilizers.

* **Barani wheat farming processes:** Water harvesting via earth embankments, dry-land preparation, and reliance entirely on unpredictable winter rainfall without artificial canal irrigation.

* **Barani wheat farming outputs:** Wheat grain for local consumption and straw (bhusa) for animal fodder.

### **2. Explain the physical factors that favour cotton cultivation in southern Punjab.**

* **Temperature:** Warm growing conditions ranging between $25$°C and $35$°C, which are optimal for plant maturation and growth.

* **Water:** Adequate water supply provided through an extensive network of perennial canals and tube wells meeting the $500$ mm to $1000$ mm crop requirement.

* **Weather:** Completely dry and sunny harvesting weather from September to November, which is crucial because rainfall damages open cotton bolls and ruins lint quality.

* **Soil:** Deep, fertile alluvial soils found across the flat southern plains of Punjab, which provide good nutrient retention and root development.

### **3. What is the difference between waterlogging and salinity? How does each affect crop production?**

* **Waterlogging:** Occurs when seepage from unlined canals raises the underground water table to or near the surface, completely saturating the plant root zone. 

  * Effect on production: Cuts off oxygen supply to roots, causing them to suffocate and destroying the crop.

* **Salinity:** Occurs when standing water in waterlogged areas evaporates under hot, dry conditions, leaving behind a white crust of dissolved alkaline salts on the topsoil. 

  * Effect on production: Alters osmotic pressure, preventing plants from absorbing water and essential nutrients, which stunts growth and renders the land completely barren.

### **4. Why is sugarcane considered an "industrial crop"? Why must it be processed within 24 hours?**

* **Industrial crop classification:** It is grown primarily as a bulk raw material for large-scale industrial manufacturing (producing refined white sugar, gur, bagasse for paper/fuel, and molasses for industrial chemicals) rather than direct family consumption.

* **The 24-hour rule:** Once harvested, the sucrose content inside the cut cane begins to rapidly evaporate and sour upon exposure to air and heat. Processing it within $24$ hours prevents a severe loss in sugar yield and commercial economic value.

### **5. Compare nomadic pastoralism and commercial dairy farming in Pakistan.**

* **Nomadic pastoralism:** Practiced by mobile herders in arid regions (Thar, Balochistan) managing large herds ($50$-$200$) of sheep and goats. It relies entirely on seasonal migration following natural rainfall patterns and seasonal pastures. 

  * Outputs include meat, wool, and hides traded locally. Challenges include land disputes with settled farmers, climate vulnerability, and a lack of veterinary access.

* **Commercial dairy farming:** Intensive, fixed-location farming concentrated near major urban centers (Lahore, Karachi) using high-yielding cattle/buffalo breeds (e.g., Nili-Ravi, Holstein crosses). 

  * It relies on artificial feed, mechanized milking, cooling tanks, and professional veterinary care to supply fresh daily milk to city markets for high profits.
