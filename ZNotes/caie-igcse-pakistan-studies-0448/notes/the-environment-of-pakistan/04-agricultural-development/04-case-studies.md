## **Case studies**

### Case study 1: Traditional rice farming on small holdings

* ### **Context: Practiced on small, fragmented plots ($<2$ hectares) using family labour.**

* System detail: Fields are ploughed using bullocks; seeds are germinated in small nursery beds first; family members manually transplant young seedlings into flooded main fields by hand; fields are inundated using distribution channels from canals; harvested using hand sickles and threshed by beating rice stalks against wooden frames or using bullocks to trample it.

* Factors affecting production

  * Natural factors: Dependence on monsoon rains, flooding risks, pest infestations.

  * Human factors: Fragmented land holdings limit mechanization, family labour dynamics (seasonal shortages during transplanting), lack of capital for HYV seeds and fertilizers, limited market access for fair prices.

### **Case study 2: Barani (rain fed) wheat farming**

* Context: Practiced in the Potwar Plateau and northern hilly areas where canal networks cannot reach.

* System detail: No artificial irrigation; reliance entirely on winter monsoon depressions; farmers practice water harvesting (terracing fields and building small embankments to catch winter rainfall); yields fluctuate drastically depending on annual rainfall variability.

* Factors affecting production

  * Natural factors: Unpredictable rainfall, soil erosion on slopes, frost damage in winter.

  * Human factors: Limited access to improved seeds, inability to afford fertilizers, lack of insurance against crop failure, small landholdings.

### Case study 3: Desert oasis farming via Karez system

* Context: Practiced in arid, mountain-rimmed basins of Balochistan (e.g., Turbat).

* System detail: Underground horizontal tunnels (Karez) tap into water tables at the foot of mountains and bring water to oases via gravity; zero evaporation loss occurs underground; supports high-value cash crops in the middle of extreme deserts: dates, vegetables, and orchards.
