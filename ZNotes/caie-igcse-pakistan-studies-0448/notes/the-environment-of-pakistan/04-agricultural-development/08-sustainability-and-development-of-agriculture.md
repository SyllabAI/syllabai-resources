## **Sustainability and Development of Agriculture**

### **Problems of agricultural development**

* Water scarcity: Groundwater over-extraction and climate change-induced reduced river flows.

* Soil degradation: Waterlogging, salinity, and nutrient depletion from fertilizer overuse.

* Population pressure: Food demand driving over-cultivation and deforestation.

* Climate change: Unpredictable monsoons, pest outbreaks, and melting glaciers.

* Land fragmentation: Inherited divisions making mechanization uneconomical.

* Market volatility: Global price fluctuations on cotton and rice exports.

* Lack of storage: Poor post-harvest facilities causing $30$%- $40$% food wastage.

### Possibilities for future development

* Precision agriculture: Satellite data and sensors to optimize water and fertilizer use.

* Drip irrigation: Saving up to $50$% water compared to flood irrigation.

* High-yielding varieties (HYVs): Drought and pest-resistant seeds.

* Agro-processing: Shifting from raw exports to value-added goods (branded rice, cotton garments).

* Solar-powered tube wells: Reducing diesel costs and carbon footprints.

* Crop diversification: Moving away from water-intensive sugarcane to high-value olives, dates, or vegetables.

### **Sustainability evaluation**

* Positive signs: Thar coal powering fertilizer production more cheaply, solar energy expansion, and drip irrigation promotion.

* Negative trends: Groundwater depleting faster than recharge rates in Punjab, salinity affecting $6$\+ million acres, and climate projections showing a $20$-$30$% reduction in Indus flows by $2050$.

* Overall evaluation: Current agricultural practices are unsustainable, relying on depleting resources and remaining vulnerable to climate shocks. Without massive investments in water conservation, solar energy, and soil remediation, food security will face a crisis within $2$-$3$ decades.
