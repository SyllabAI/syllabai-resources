## Land Reclamation and Government Actions

**Waterlogging and salinity**

* Mechanism: Seepage from unlined canals raises the underground water table to the surface (waterlogging). In hot, dry climates, standing water evaporates, leaving behind dissolved crusts of salt (salinity).

* Solutions and land reclamation:

  * SCARP (Salinity Control and Reclamation Project): Installing deep tube wells to lower the groundwater table and flush salts off fields.

  * Canal lining: Coating dirt channels with concrete to stop seepage at the source.

  * Surface drains: Digging tile drains to carry salty surface water back into rivers.

  * Biological reclamation: Planting salt-tolerant, deep-rooted trees like eucalyptus.

* **Evaluation of prevention strategies:** Canal lining is the most effective long-term solution, but it is expensive and slow (\~$20$% lined). SCARP tube wells help lower water tables but require reliable electricity and risk over-extraction. Surface drains require maintenance and can clog. Biological reclamation is low-cost but takes years. Overall, prevention is better than cure, but government focus has leaned heavily on short-sighted reclamation rather than seepage prevention.

**Government actions to improve agriculture**

* Land reforms: Setting ceilings on maximum land ownership to break up feudal estates and redistribute land.

* Infrastructure: Construction of dams (Tarbela, Mangla) and barrages.

* Inputs and subsidies: Distributing HYV seeds (MaxPak wheat, Basmati $385$ rice), subsidizing chemical fertilizers and pesticides.

* Credit schemes: Soft loans via ZTBL (Zarai Taraqiati Bank Limited) for tractors and tube wells.

* Training: Agricultural extension services educating farmers on modern soil management and crop rotation.
