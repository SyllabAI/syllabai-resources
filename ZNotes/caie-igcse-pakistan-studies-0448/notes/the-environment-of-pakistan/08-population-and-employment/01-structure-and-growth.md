### Population pyramids

**Population Pyramids**

* **What is a population pyramid?** It is a graphical bar chart that displays the distribution of a country's population across different age groups and between males and females.

* **Pakistan's pyramid shape:** Pakistan features a broad-based pyramid, indicating a very high proportion of young people (under $15$ years old) and a narrow top representing a smaller elderly population.

  **Key Trends & Variations in Sex Structure**

  * **Why male-biased at younger ages:** Son preference in rural communities, higher female mortality during childbirth, and male-dominated overseas migration (workers in the Middle East).

  * **Why it reverses at $65$\+:** Women live longer than men (higher life expectancy), while men face more occupational hazards and health risks.

  * **Regional variations:**

    * Urban areas: More balanced sex ratio due to education and employment opportunities for women.

    * Rural areas: More male-biased as women are less visible in workforce statistics.

    * High migration districts (e.g., Mirpur, Gujrat): Feature more female-headed households.

### **Impact of Projected Structures on the Economy**

* **High dependency ratio:** A large youth cohort means a small working-age population must support a massive number of dependents.

* **Strain on public services:** Increased immediate pressure on pediatric healthcare, primary and secondary schooling, and maternal health services.

* **Future workforce pressures:** A large youth bubble means millions enter the job market annually, creating a massive demand for job creation. If trained properly, this becomes a demographic dividend; if ignored, it results in widespread unemployment.

* **Explanation:** The dependency ratio is falling because birth rates are declining slowly and the working-age population is growing. Pakistan has a narrow window (**$2025$–$2040$)** to harness its "youth bulge" before the population ages.

**Key Definitions to Know**

* **Birth rate:** The total number of live births per $1,000$ people in a year.

* **Death rate:** The total number of deaths per $1,000$ people in a year.

* **Overpopulation:** When an area's population is too large to be supported by its available resources (wealth, food, fresh water, and land).

* **Demographic Transition Model:** Graphs suggesting the sequence of change in relationships between birth and death rates over time.

* **Rate of natural increase:** The percentage by which a population grows annually, calculated as the difference between the birth rate and death rate: **Rate of natural increase= Birth rate - Death rate**
