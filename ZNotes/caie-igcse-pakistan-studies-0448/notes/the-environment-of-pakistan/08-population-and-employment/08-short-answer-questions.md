## **Short Answer Questions**

### **1. Distinguish between unemployment and underemployment, with examples from Pakistan.**

* **Unemployment:** Working-age individuals who are actively looking for a job but unable to find one, such as industrial labourers laid off due to factory automation or energy crises.

* **Underemployment:** Individuals who are working but under-utilised or working part-time/seasonally when full-time work is desired, such as university graduates working as taxi drivers or delivery riders due to a scarcity of formal-sector positions.

### **2. Explain how unemployment affects GDP and GNP. Use a concrete example.**

* **Macro Impact:** High unemployment means human resources are idle, directly depressing domestic output (GDP) and limiting national wealth generation (GNP).

* **Concrete Example:** If millions of potential workers are unemployed, the national economy loses billions in unproduced goods and services. If skilled graduates are underemployed in low-skill jobs, their higher productivity potential is wasted, which restricts overall economic growth and suppresses the productive base that complements foreign remittances.

### **3. Describe the sex structure of Pakistan's population. Why is there a slight male bias?**

* **Structure:** Pakistan exhibits a male-biased sex ratio of approximately $105$ males per $100$ females.

* **Causes:** Contributing factors include higher female childbirth mortality risks, cultural son preference in rural communities, and male-dominated rural-to-urban or overseas migration patterns.

### **4. Explain the shift in workforce proportions from agriculture to services in Pakistan.**

* **Shift Dynamics:** Agriculture's workforce share dropped from roughly $50$% in the $1980$s to $35$% to $40$% today due to farm mechanisation (tractors and harvesters), which displaced manual labour and drove rural youth to cities. Consequently, the service sector expanded to $40$% to $45$%, absorbing surplus labour into retail, transport, telecom, and informal urban services.

### **5. What is the dependency ratio? How will it change by $2050$?**

* **Dependency Ratio:** The proportion of dependents ($0$ to $14$ years and $65+$ years) relative to the working-age population ($15$ to $64$ years), currently standing at $70$ to $80$ dependents per $100$ workers.

* **Future Change:** As birth rates decline, the dependency ratio is projected to fall to around $60$ by $2035$ and reach a peak demographic dividend of approximately $50$ dependents per $100$ workers by $2050$.

### **6. Define population density and explain why Punjab has a higher density than Balochistan.**

* **Population Density:** The average number of people living per square kilometre ($\text{km}^2$).

* **Comparison:** Punjab features extensive flat fertile plains, perennial canal irrigation, intensive agriculture, and major industrial cities supporting high densities, Balochistan features vast arid deserts, rugged plateaus, and sparse settlements yielding very low densities.

### **7. What is the difference between push and pull factors in migration? Give two examples of each.**

* **Push Factors:** Negative conditions in rural areas forcing people to leave (e.g., lack of secure farm employment and poor local healthcare/schools).

* **Pull Factors:** Attractive conditions in urban areas drawing people in (e.g., better-paying factory jobs and superior universities/specialized hospitals).
