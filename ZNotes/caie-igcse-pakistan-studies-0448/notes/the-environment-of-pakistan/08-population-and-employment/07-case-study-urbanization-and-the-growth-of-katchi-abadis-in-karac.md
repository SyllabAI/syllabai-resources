## **Case Study: Urbanization and the Growth of Katchi Abadis in Karachi**

* **The Context:** Karachi, Pakistan's largest economic hub, experiences relentless rural-urban migration driven by push factors like farm mechanisation and rural poverty, alongside pull factors like employment and higher education.

* **The Problem:** The city's municipal infrastructure has failed to keep pace with an annual population influx of hundreds of thousands. This has led to the proliferation of katchi abadis (informal settlements/slums) covering large tracts of land without legal titles, proper sanitation, or clean piped water.

* **Socio-Economic Impacts:** Residents in these settlements rely on illegal electrical hookups and contaminated groundwater, leading to high rates of waterborne diseases like typhoid and cholera. Municipal services are severely strained, and traffic congestion and urban crime rates rise.

* **Mitigation Strategies:** Government and community-backed initiatives focus on self-help schemes, providing slum residents with secure land titles and building materials so they can upgrade their own homes, alongside expanding basic sanitation, water lines, and decentralized industrial employment hubs away from primary metropolitan centers.
