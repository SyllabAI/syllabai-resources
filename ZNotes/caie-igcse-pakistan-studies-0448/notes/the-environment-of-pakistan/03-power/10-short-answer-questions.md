## Short Answer Questions

### **1. Distinguish between open-cast, adit, and shaft mining methods. For which type of coal deposit is each used in Pakistan?**

* **Open-cast mining:** A surface mining technique where overlying soil and rock (overburden) are removed to extract coal lying close to the surface. Used in Pakistan for thick, near-surface coal seams, such as at Thar.

* **Adit mining:** A horizontal or gently inclined tunnel driven into the side of a hill or mountain to reach a coal seam. Used in Pakistan for hilly terrains where coal seams outcrop along slopes, such as the Salt Range and Makerwal.

* **Shaft mining:** A deep underground mining method that uses a vertical elevator shaft to reach coal seams buried deep beneath the surface. Used in Pakistan for deeper, steeply inclined coal reserves where surface or adit methods are no longer viable, such as parts of the Lakhra and southern coalfields.

### **2. Why is Pakistan forced to import high-grade coal despite having large reserves at Thar?**

* Pakistan’s domestic coal reserves, particularly at Thar, are predominantly **lignite** (low-grade coal) which has a high moisture content and low calorific value.

* Many industrial sectors; most notably the **cement industry**, require **sub-bituminous or bituminous coal** (high-grade coal) with high burning temperatures and energy output to fuel their kilns efficiently. Because local coal lacks the necessary quality for these specific industrial applications, Pakistan must import high-grade coal.

### **3. Explain the concept of "circular debt" and how it contributes to Pakistan's energy crisis.**

* **Concept:** Circular debt is a cascade of unpaid liabilities within the power sector. It starts when end-consumers and government entities fail to pay their electricity bills to distribution companies (DISCOs). Because DISCOs do not collect enough revenue, they cannot pay power generation companies (like IPPs). In turn, those generation companies cannot pay fuel suppliers (oil, gas, and coal companies).

* **Contribution to the energy crisis:** This financial deadlock drains liquidity from the entire energy chain. Fuel suppliers run out of funds to purchase or import fuel, which forces power plants to shut down or reduce generation capacity, directly leading to severe load shedding and blackouts.

### **4. Why is the Jhimpir Wind Corridor ideal for wind energy generation in Pakistan?**

* The Jhimpir Wind Corridor (located in Thatta district, Sindh) experiences **continuous, high, and reliable wind speeds** (consistently above $5$ meters per second) throughout much of the year.

* It features **wide, flat, unobstructed terrain** that allows wind to flow smoothly without geographical barriers, making it structurally and climatically perfect for installing large-scale commercial wind turbine farms.
