## **Case Study: Sialkot Industrial Cluster**

### Why Sialkot developed these industries

* Sialkot's rise as a premier manufacturing and export hub is driven by specific locational factors and historical advantages.

* Originally, British colonial troops in the region needed cricket bats, saddles, and leather gear repaired locally, which sparked a generational tradition of skilled craftsmanship and metalworking. Over decades, this inherited artisan expertise evolved into specialized clusters for sports goods and surgical instruments.

* Furthermore, the city developed a robust ecosystem of supportive local ancillary industries; such as chemical polishers, die-casting workshops, and specialized toolmakers, eliminating the need to import basic components.

* Proximity to export infrastructure, including the Sialkot Dry Port and Sialkot International Airport, allows manufacturers to quickly dispatch high-value, low-weight goods to international markets in Europe and North America without incurring massive inland transport delays.

### The role of informal home-based workers in these industries

* The informal sector forms the backbone of Sialkot's cost competitiveness through value-added sub-contracting networks.

* Formal, registered factories handle high-end quality control, heavy machinery casting, and direct international client relations, but they routinely outsource labour-intensive stages; such as the hand-stitching of inflatable footballs or the intricate polishing of surgical scissors, to home-based workers.

* Thousands of women and informal artisans working from home absorb excess labour capacity, allowing manufacturers to scale production up or down rapidly based on global seasonal demand without maintaining massive permanent factory payrolls.

* However, these workers often operate without formal legal protections, earning low piece-rates with no health benefits or job security.

### Challenges facing the cluster and potential solutions

* **International Compliance and Labour Standards:** Sialkot faced severe global scrutiny over child labour in football stitching during the 1990s.

  * **Solution**: The establishment of the Sialkot Chamber of Commerce and Industry (SCCI) partnership with international bodies (like the ILO) to create monitored, centralized stitching centers and eliminate child labour.

* **Technological Disruption:** The rise of automated, machine-stitched and thermo-bonded footballs manufactured in China threatens traditional hand-stitched production.

  * **Solution**: Heavy investment in modern thermal-bonding manufacturing technology and diversification into high-tech sports apparel and advanced medical-grade titanium surgical instruments.

* **Input Cost Volatility:** Rising prices for imported raw materials like high-grade stainless steel alloys and synthetic leather compress profit margins.

  * **Solution**: Government provision of subsidized raw material import quotas, tax rebates, and enhanced technical training institutes (like NAVTTC partnerships) to boost productivity and worker efficiency.
