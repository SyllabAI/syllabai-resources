## Practice Questions and Answers

### **1. Distinguish between cottage, small-scale, and large-scale industries in Pakistan, with examples.**

* **Cottage Industry:** Home-based or small village workshops run by unpaid family members using basic hand tools and low capital (e.g., handloom embroidery, pottery).

* **Small-Scale Industry:** Purpose-built urban workshops or industrial estates employing under 10-20 hired workers with power tools and basic electric machinery (e.g., sports goods manufacturing in Sialkot, brick kilns).

* **Large-Scale Industry:** Massive industrial zones utilizing heavy automated machinery, high power use, and hundreds or thousands of hired workers with substantial capital investment (e.g., Pakistan steel mills, cement factories, oil refineries).

### **2. Explain the advantages and disadvantages of the informal sector for Pakistan's development.**

* **Advantages:** Provides vital employment and income for millions of poor, illiterate, or unskilled workers who lack formal education; requires minimal startup capital; supplies cheap, accessible daily services and low-cost goods for low-income citizens.

* **Disadvantages:** The government loses billions in uncollected tax revenue needed for public infrastructure; workers face no employment security, medical benefits, or safety, leading to high risks of exploitation and child labour; unregistered activities distort official economic planning, GDP, and GNP statistics.

### **3. Why is the sugar industry located near sugarcane-growing areas? Explain the "24-Hour Rule".**

* **Locational Reason:** Sugarcane is a bulky, heavy, and weight-losing raw material, making long-distance transport economically prohibitive.

* **The 24-Hour Rule:** Once sugarcane is harvested, the sucrose content inside the cut stalks begins to rapidly evaporate and sour upon exposure to air and heat. Mills must be built directly within agricultural zones so that processing occurs within 24 hours of cutting to prevent severe financial losses in sugar yield.

### **4. What is the significance of Sialkot's industrial cluster for the sports goods and surgical instruments industries?**

* **Significance:** Sialkot has evolved into a world-renowned specialized manufacturing and export hub due to a century-old heritage of inherited craftsmanship and specialized metalworking skills.

* **Support Infrastructure:** The cluster is heavily supported by local ancillary industries, specialized export houses, a local dry port, and an international airport, enabling manufacturers to efficiently export high-value surgical tools and sports equipment to global markets in North America, Europe, and major sporting events like FIFA.

### **5. Describe two natural and two cultural tourist attractions in Pakistan, with specific examples.**

* **Natural Attraction 1 (K2 and Nanga Parbat):** Located in Gilgit-Baltistan, these majestic peaks are the world's 2nd and 9th highest mountains, attracting international mountaineers, trekkers, and adventure tourists.

* **Natural Attraction 2 (Hunza Valley):** Situated in Gilgit-Baltistan, renowned for its stunning mountain scenery, terraced apricot orchards, and unique cultural heritage.

* **Cultural Attraction 1 (Mohenjo-daro):** A 4500-year-old ancient Indus Valley civilization city located in Sindh, featuring the Great Bath and designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

* **Cultural Attraction 2 (Badshahi Mosque):** A magnificent Mughal-era architectural landmark built in Lahore in 1673, capable of holding 100000 worshippers as one of the largest mosques in Pakistan.

### **6. Why did the Hub and Nooriabad industrial estates fail? What feasibility lessons can be learned?**

* **Reasons for Failure:** Both industrial estates were established near Karachi to stimulate regional manufacturing growth but suffered severe economic setbacks because planners failed to secure an adequate, sustainable supply of industrial water.

* **Feasibility Lessons:** A comprehensive feasibility study must rigorously evaluate all physical and human factors (site, water, power, labour, and market access) before investing capital. If basic utilities like water or power are missing, projects must be delayed or scaled down, and if essential resources cannot be provided, the project must be rejected entirely.
