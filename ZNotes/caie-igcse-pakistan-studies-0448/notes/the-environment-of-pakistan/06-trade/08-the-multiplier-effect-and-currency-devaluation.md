## The Multiplier Effect and Currency Devaluation

* ### The Multiplier Effect:

  * Describes how an initial injection of spending creates a larger final increase in national income (e.g, exporting $100$ million creates $200$-$250$ million in total income which is a multiplier of $2$x$2.5$).

  * Pakistan's multiplier remains small due to leakages caused by heavy import dependence and low domestic savings ($10$-$15$% of GDP).

  * **Step-by-Step Example**

    1. Pakistan exports $100$ million worth of textiles to the EU.

    2. Textile factories receive $100$ million and pay workers $60 million in wages.

    3. Workers spend $40$ million on food, transport, and housing.

    4. Food sellers and transport workers receive $40$ million and spend $30$ million on their own goods.

    5. This cycle continues, driving the total increase in national income to $200$-$250$ million (a multiplier of $2$ x $2.5$).

* ### Currency Devaluation:

  * Forcing or allowing the rupee to lose value against foreign currencies.

  * While it makes Pakistani exports cheaper and boosts volumes in the short term, it instantly inflates the cost of imported energy and machinery, worsens the trade deficit, and drives up domestic inflation.

  * **Step-by-Step Effects**

    * **Short-Term (Positive for exports)**

      1. Pakistani goods become cheaper in foreign markets (a $10$ shirt now costs $3.50$ instead of $6.25$).

      2. Export volume increases as foreign buyers purchase more.

      3. Exporters earn more rupees per dollar, boosting their profit margins.

      **Long-Term impacts**

      * Exports may continue to rise if Pakistani goods remain internationally competitive.

      * Imported machinery becomes much more expensive, hurting local industries that rely on foreign equipment.

      * National debt repayments (denominated in foreign currencies) become significantly heavier.
