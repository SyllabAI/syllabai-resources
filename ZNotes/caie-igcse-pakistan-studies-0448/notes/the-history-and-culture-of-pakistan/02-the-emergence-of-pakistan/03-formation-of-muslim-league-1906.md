## Formation of Muslim League (1906)

### Reasons

* Hindu Dominance in Congress
  * Indian National Congress was largely Hindu-dominated, and Muslims felt politically underrepresented.
* Disunity among Muslims
  * Muslims were divided regionally and socially, lacking a unified political voice.
* Fear of Partition of Bengal (POB) Reversal
  * Muslims feared that annulment of Bengal Partition would reduce their political and administrative opportunities.
* Extremist Hindu Nationalist Movements
  * The rise of Swadeshi and extremist nationalist movements made Muslims feel politically threatened.
* To Strengthen Muslim-British Relations
  * Muslims wanted to maintain good relations with the British, who were seen as protectors of their interests.
* To Present Muslim Demands
  * There was a need for a platform to articulate Muslim concerns and rights at the national level.
* Possibility of a New Constitution in India
  * Anticipation of constitutional reforms motivated Muslims to organise and secure safeguards for their community.
* Success of the Simla Delegation (1906)
  * The Simla Delegation, led by prominent Muslims, successfully presented Muslim demands to the Viceroy, showing the need for a formal political organisation.
* Muslims felt Congress was not doing much for rights of Muslims
  * Hindu protests against Partition of Bengal (which was beneficial for Muslims) showed they were a separate force concerned for their own benefit
* Lack of alignment of Muslim and Hindu views/wishes
  * Muslims gained confidence due to acceptance of Simla Delegation, so formed their own party
