## Round Table Conferences (1930-1932)

### Causes

* Demand for constitutional reforms and greater Indian participation in government

* Opposition to British autocracy and dissatisfaction with Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms

* Failure of Simon Commission to include Indian members

* Muslim demand for safeguards and separate electorates

* Growing Indian nationalism and pressure from Congress and Muslim League

* Civil disobedience and protests highlighted the need for negotiation between British and Indian leaders

* To discuss future governance and self-rule in India

### Key Points

* 1st Round Table Conference was not attended by Congress due to their non-cooperation movement
* Congress leaders were arrested so could not take part
* No productive discussion or conclusion reached due to absence of Congress but a federal form of government for India was an established point
* 2nd Round Table Conference involved attendance of Congress
  * Lord Irwin convinced Gandhi to stop non-cooperation
* British demanded for an agreement on the issue of representation otherwise they would form their own system of representation
* British agreed to make Sindh a separate province hence complied to a Muslim demand
* 3rd Round Table Conference was not attended by Congress and Jinnah was not invited
* Lord Wellington replaced Lord Irwin
  * He was far more strict and was less cooperative with the Indians
* No important members attended
  * Congress restarted Non-Cooperation Movement
  * Muslim-Hindu cooperation died down as the 14 points were rejected by Congress
