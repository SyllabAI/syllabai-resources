# The Emergence of Pakistan

## Partition of Bengal (1905)

### Causes

**Administrative Reasons**
* Bengal was the largest province in British India, with a population of over 80 million people.
* It was difficult to administer such a large and heavily populated province from Calcutta.
* The British claimed that partition would lead to better and more efficient administration.

**Economic Reasons**
* Eastern Bengal was economically neglected compared to Western Bengal.
* Most industries, jobs, and educational institutions were located in Western Bengal.
* Partition would allow economic development of Eastern Bengal, especially in agriculture and trade.

**Political Reasons**
* The British wanted to weaken the growing nationalist movement in Bengal.
* Bengal was a centre of anti-British activities led mainly by Hindu elites.
* Partition was intended to break Bengali unity and reduce opposition to British rule.

**Religious Reasons**
* Eastern Bengal had a Muslim majority, while Western Bengal had a Hindu majority.
* The British aimed to divide Hindus and Muslims to prevent united resistance.
* This policy of divide and rule would strengthen British control.

**British Strategic Interests**
* The British believed that Muslims of Eastern Bengal would be more loyal to the British.
* Creating a new Muslim-majority province would help counter Hindu political dominance.

### Reactions to the Partition of Bengal

**Hindu Reaction**
* Strongly opposed the partition; saw it as divide and rule.
* Formation of the Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908):
	* Boycott of British goods and promotion of Indian-made products.
    * Establishment of Indian-owned industries and banks.
* Protests, rallies, and petitions were organised in Bengal and other provinces.
* Growth of extremist nationalist groups (e.g., Jugantar) advocating direct action against the British.
* University and student activism increased; schools and colleges became centres of protest.

**Muslim Reaction**
* Generally welcomed the partition because:
	* Eastern Bengal became a Muslim-majority province, giving Muslims better representation.
    * They gained more political and administrative opportunities under British rule.
* Some Muslims supported the British government, believing it was in their community’s interest.
