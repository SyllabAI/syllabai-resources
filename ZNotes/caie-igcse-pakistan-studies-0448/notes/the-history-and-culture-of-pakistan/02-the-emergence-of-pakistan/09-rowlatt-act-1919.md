## Rowlatt Act (1919)

* Passed by the British Government in India in 1919.
* Also called the “Black Act” due to its repressive nature.
* Allowed the government to imprison any person without trial for up to two years.
* Extended emergency wartime powers of the British in India even after World War I had ended.
* Trial without jury permitted in political cases.
* Aimed to suppress nationalist activities and curb revolutionary movements.
* Led to widespread protests and unrest, including the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (Amritsar, 1919).
* Caused strong opposition from Indian leaders, including Mahatma Gandhi, who launched the Non-Cooperation and Satyagraha movements.
