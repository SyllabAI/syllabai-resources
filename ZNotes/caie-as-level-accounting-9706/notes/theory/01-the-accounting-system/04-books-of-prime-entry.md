## __Books of Prime Entry__

* Books of Prime Entry are used to list all transactions of a kind before being posted on ledgers.
  * For example, all sales transactions will be posted in a ‘Sales Journal’ before being entered into the ledgers.
* There are 6 types of Books of Prime Entry:
  * Sales Journal
  * Sales Returns Journal
  * Purchases Journal
  * Purchases Return Journal
  * Cash Book
  * Journal
* A book of Prime Entry has its functions:
  * Each transaction is recorded chronologically for future reference.
  * Each transaction is analysed using the debit and credit aspects to learn the effect it has on the business financially.

| **Books Of Prime Entry** | **Use** |
|----|----|
| Sales Journal | Record **Credit** Sales from invoices. |
| Sales Returns Journal | Record goods returned from **Credit** Customers. |
| Purchases Journal | Record **Credit** Purchase |
| Purchases Returns journal | Record goods returned to suppliers |
| Cash Book | Record all Cash and Bank Transactions (check payments included) as well as discounts allowed (dr side) and discounts received (cr side ) |
| Journal | Record Transactions for which there isn’t any other entry. This includes correcting errors, adjustments to the accounts, and transfers between accounts. |

* **Limitations of Books of Prime Entry**:
  * Huge and Bulky in size - A single journal for a business would be a lot of work to operate and handle
  * Balance of accounts not available at a glance - As there are numerous transactions in a business, it would take a lot of time to find a particular transaction.
  * Difficulty in Reconciling Cash Book—If a business records all transactions in journal(s) and does not maintain a cash book, reconciling the daily cash balance would be difficult.
  * Repetitive transactions - A business would have similar kinds of transactions every day and to record them would require extra labour.
