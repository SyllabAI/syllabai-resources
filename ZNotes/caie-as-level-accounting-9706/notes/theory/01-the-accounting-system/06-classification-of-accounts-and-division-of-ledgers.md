## Classification of Accounts and Division of Ledgers

* ### Classification of Accounts

  Accounts can be classified in two ways.

  ### 1. Classification by Type of Account
  * **Personal accounts** – Accounts relating to persons or organisations with whom the business has financial dealings.

    * Trade receivables (customers)

    * Trade payables (suppliers)

    * Capital and drawings accounts

  * **Impersonal accounts** – Accounts that are not personal accounts. These can be classified into:

    * **Real accounts** – Accounts relating to assets.

      * Non-current assets (e.g. machinery, buildings)

      * Current assets (e.g. inventory, cash)

    * **Nominal accounts** – Accounts relating to expenses, revenues, gains and losses.

      * Expense accounts (e.g. rent, wages)

      * Revenue accounts (e.g. sales revenue, commission received)

      * Gain accounts

      * Loss accounts

  ### 2. Classification According to Normal Balances

  **Accounts with a normal debit balance**
  * Asset accounts

    * Non-current assets

    * Current assets

  * Expense accounts

  * Loss accounts

  * Drawings account

  **Accounts with a normal credit balance**
  * Liability accounts

  * Capital (owner’s equity) accounts

  * Revenue accounts

  * Gain accounts
* **Types of ledgers.**
  * Sales ledger – accounts of customers
  * Purchase ledger – accounts of suppliers
  * General ledger – impersonal accounts
  * Private ledger – owners’ capital and drawings
  * Cash book
  * Petty cash book
* Division of ledgers is essential as it allows staff to be trained in different aspects of the business, helps detect and prevent errors and fraud, and is referred to as an internal check.
