## Accruals and Prepayments (The Matching Concept)

* Accruals are an expense which is due within an accounting period but which has not been paid, in the statement of financial position, it is known as other payables.
* Prepayments are payments made by the business in advance of the benefits to be derived from them, this is entered as other receivables in the statement of financial position.
* **Accrued expenses –** amount owed to a creditor

* The payments during the year add up by the bank but the income statement has been debited with rent for one year, the amount that relates to the next financial year is subtracted from the total amount and this amount is entered under the Statement Of Financial Position as other receivables under current assets.
* This amount is deducted from the expense amount in the trial balance while including in the Income Statement.
* Inventory of stores as an expense accounts – inventories of consumable stores may be unused at the end of the year, as per matching concept these should not be charged against the profit as they are an asset, this is carried down as a debit balance hence why an expense account may have both debit and credit balances.
* Example: In the year ended 31 Dec 2020, Mr. Ceyard had paid 1200 dollars for stationery, at year end he owed 270 dollars for stationery and had an inventory of unused stationery of 400 dollars.

* Adjusting for income prepayments – incomes received in advance of its due dates is a payable (income prepaid) and is therefore a credit balance and is under current liabilities in the Statement Of Financial Position. The amount prepaid is misused from the amount paid in the Income Statement.
* Adjusting for income accrued – incomes accrued at the date it is due indicates existence of debtors and is therefore a debit balance, this is hence under current assets as other receivables in the Statement Of Financial Position, and it is added to the amount received in the bank in the Income Statement.
