# The Economic Problem

**The basic economic problem** is that resources are finite in relation to the unlimited wants and needs that humans have.

* In essence, this is what the study of economics sets out to study, how people allocate resources despite the finite resources.

* In doing so, choices will have to be made on where to use resources and where to not use them.

* These choices will lead to benefits and drawbacks.

* Evaluating the benefits and drawbacks is crucial for this syllabus. So try to understand the material rather than just memorizing so you can evaluate it.

**Opportunity cost** is the loss of the next best alternative when making a decision

* Opportunity cost is not necessarily a monetary value

* It is something you can’t obtain anymore due to obtaining something else

* e.g. your parents say you can get pizza or burgers, but cannot have both, if you choose pizza, you can no longer have burgers which is the opportunity cost.

**Opportunity costs will affect economic agents differently within an economy**

* Producers may opt for choices that ensure that their business remain profitable, or become more profitable in relation to what is lost when they make the choice.

  * A car rental service may have the decision to choose between a car or a computer for their business which relies heavily on paper work.

  * Both cost $3000

  * Despite them costing the same the computer will allow them to rent more cars per month, and bring in $500 more monthly, whereas the car may only bring $200

  * The computer has the smaller opportunity cost.

* Consumers may opt for choices that ensure that their purchase will give them the most value

* The government may opt for choices that ensure that the most amount of people’s needs are met.

## Production Possibility Curves

**A production possibility** curve shows the maximum output combinations possible within an economy given that all factors of production are used.

* It is normally shown by capital goods and consumer goods, but output combinations can refer to any two good's/services

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/ca5f3f4bbffcc7088037999c.webp =656x)

* Point A - All factors of production within the economy are being used to its potential, and maximum number of goods/services are being produced

* Point B - This level of production is not attainable

* Point C - Factors of production within the economy are not being used to its potential

* Points D and E - All factors of production within the economy are being used efficiently, but to only one type of good

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/0f28bdfa228f5565b4af209f.webp =656x)

**Opportunity cost can be shown on opportunity cost**

* Moving from A to B

* 75 more capital goods can be created

* But there is an opportunity cost of 50 consumer goods

* This is because factors of production are being used for capital goods now

* This is called a movement along the curve

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/b55d4c1da2f553c75d98d997.webp =656x)

From PPC1 to PPC 2

* There is an increase in the productive potential of the economy

* This is because the quality or quantity of factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise) has increased

* Increase in quality of education and/or number of people who get education can shift the PPC outwards as they are more productive and efficient

* There is a decrease in the school leaving age so more people have to join the workforce, this would increase the quantity of labour

From PPC1 to PPC 3

* There is a decrease in the productive potential of the economy

* This is because quality or quantity of factors of production has decreased

* War and conflict could make people flee the country, and destroy infrastructrue, decreasing the quality and quantity of factors of production

* Natural disasters such as floods could destroy crops and infrastructure, decreasing the quality and quantity of factors of production

When the entire curve moves, this is called a shift
