## Factors Affecting Ionisation Energy

* **Nuclear Charge**
  * \+ve charge due to protons in the nucleus
  * Greater nuclear charge means greater ionization energy

* **Shielding Effect**
  * Inner shells of e\<sup\>-\</sup\>s repel outermost e\<sup\>-\</sup\>s, thus shielding them from the \+ve nucleus. The more e\<sup\>-\</sup\> shells, the greater is the shielding effect
  * Greater effect lower I.E because lesser attractive force between
    nucleus & outer e\<sup\>-\</sup\>s
* **Atomic Radius**
  * Distance from the centre of the nucleus to the outermost orbit
  * As the number of electron shells increases, the atomic radius increases
  * As the number of electrons in the outermost shell increases, the atomic radius decreases as the electrostatic attraction between the nucleus and outer electrons increases
  * Greater radius lower I.E; a distance of outermost e\<sup\>-\</sup\> to the nucleus is large $\therefore$ less energy needed to remove
    e\<sup\>-\</sup\>
* **Stable Configuration**
  * High I.E needed to remove e\<sup\>-\</sup\>s from completely or
    half-filled orbitals
* **Spin-pair repulsion:**
  * Electrons in the same atomic orbital in a sub-shell repel each other more than electrons in different atomic orbitals. This increased repulsion makes it easier to remove an electron. So first ionization energy is decreased.
