## Dot and Cross Diagrams

1. For anions, electrons are added to the atom (e.g. Chlorine forms Chloride Cl<sup>-</sup> ions.)

2. For cations, electrons are removed from the atom (e.g. Sodium forms Na<sup>+</sup> ions.)

3. Atoms form the same number of bonds as the number of electrons required to be added/removed for maximum stability. (e.g.: Chlorine will form 1 bond, Sodium will form 1 bond, but Calcium Ca<sup>2+</sup> will form 2 bonds)

4. The central atom may expand its octet to form more bonds if necessary (e.g, S<sub>8</sup>)

5. In ionic compounds, electrons are shown to be completely transferred to each of the ions in the compound.
