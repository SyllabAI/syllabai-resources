## Reacting masses and volumes

### **Reacting Masses and Percentage Yield**

$$\text{Percentage Yield} = \frac{\text{Actual Yield}}{\text{Theoretical Yield}} \times 100\\%$$

Worked Example

$5.60\text{ g}$ of iron reacts with excess sulfur to form iron(II) sulfide:

$$\text{Fe}\text{(s)} + \text{S}\text{(s)} \to \text{FeS}\text{(s)}$$

If $7.48\text{ g}$ of $\text{FeS}$ is isolated, calculate the percentage yield.

*($A_{\text{r}}$: $\text{Fe} = 55.8$, $\text{S} = 32.1$; $M_{\text{r}}(\text{FeS}) = 87.9$)*

1. **Moles of reactant:**
   $$n(\text{Fe}) = \frac{5.60}{55.8} = 0.1004\text{ mol}$$

2. **Theoretical moles and mass of product:**

   * Mole ratio $\text{Fe} : \text{FeS} = 1 : 1 \implies n(\text{FeS})_{\text{theoretical}} = 0.1004\text{ mol}$
     $$\text{Theoretical Mass} = 0.1004\text{ mol} \times 87.9\text{ g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1} = 8.825\text{ g}$$

3. **Percentage yield:**
   $$\text{Yield} = \frac{7.48\text{ g}}{8.825\text{ g}} \times 100\\% = 84.8\\% \quad \text{(3 s.f.)}$$

### **Volumes of Gases (Hydrocarbon Combustion)**

* **Avogadro’s Law:** Equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of moles. Therefore, **gas volume ratios equal stoichiometric mole ratios**.

* **General Combustion Equation:**
  $$\text{C}_x\text{H}_y\text{(g)} + \left\(x + \frac{y}{4}\right\)\text{O}_2\text{(g)} \to x\text{CO}_2\text{(g)} + \frac{y}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)}$$

* **Worked Example:**

* $20\text{ cm}^3$ of a gaseous hydrocarbon $\text{C}_x\text{H}_y$ requires $100\text{ cm}^3$ of oxygen for complete combustion, producing $60\text{ cm}^3$ of carbon dioxide. Determine its molecular formula.

  1. **Establish volume ratios (mole ratios):**
     $$\frac{V(\text{C}_x\text{H}_y)}{20} : \frac{V(\text{O}_2)}{20} : \frac{V(\text{CO}_2)}{20} \implies 1\text{ C}_x\text{H}_y : 5\text{ O}_2 : 3\text{ CO}_2$$

  2. **Determine x (Carbon):**
     $$x = 3 \implies 3\text{ atoms of C}$$

  3. **Determine y (Oxygen):**
     $$x + \frac{y}{4} = 5 \implies 3 + \frac{y}{4} = 5 \implies \frac{y}{4} = 2 \implies y = 8$$

  4. **Molecular Formula:** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$ (Propane)

### **Volumes and Concentrations of Solutions (Titrations)**

* **Worked Example:**
  $25.0\text{ cm}^3$ of $0.0500\text{ mol}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}\text{ H}_2\text{SO}_4$ neutralises $18.50\text{ cm}^3$ of $\text{NaOH}\text{(aq)}$. Calculate the concentration of $\text{NaOH}$ in $\text{mol}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}$ and $\text{g}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}$.
  $$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq)} + 2\text{NaOH}\text{(aq)} \to \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)}$$

  1. **Calculate the moles of standard acid:**
     • • • $$n(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) = c \times V = 0.0500 \times \frac{25.0}{1000} = 1.25 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}$$

  2. **Stoichiometric ratio ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 : \text{NaOH} = 1 : 2$):** $$n(\text{NaOH}) = 2 \times (1.25 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.50 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol}$$

  3. **Calculate molar concentration:**

     $$c(\text{NaOH}) = \frac{n}{V} = \frac{2.50 \times 10^{-3}}{18.50 / 1000} = 0.1351 \approx 0.135\text{ mol}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}$$

  * **Calculate mass concentration ($M_{\text{r}}(\text{NaOH}) = 23.0 + 16.0 + 1.0 = 40.0$):** $$\rho = c \times M_{\text{r}} = 0.1351 \times 40.0 = 5.40\text{ g}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}$$

### **Limiting Reagent and Excess Reagent**

* **Limiting Reagent:** The reactant that is completely consumed first; it determines the maximum theoretical yield of products.

* **Excess Reagent:** The reactant remaining after the limiting reagent is entirely consumed.

*  **To identify:**

  1. Calculate moles of each initial reactant.

  2. Divide each mole amount by its respective stoichiometric coefficient.

  3. The reactant with the **smallest resulting quotient** is the limiting reagent.

* **Worked Example:**
  $4.86\text{ g}$ of $\text{Mg}$ is added to $100\text{ cm}^3$ of $2.00\text{ mol}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}\text{ HCl}$.
  $$\text{Mg}\text{(s)} + 2\text{HCl}\text{(aq)} \to \text{MgCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}$$

  Calculate the volume of $\text{H}*2\text{(g)}$ generated at r.t.p. \*($A*{\text{r}}(\text{Mg}) = 24.3$)\*

  1. **Find moles of each reactant:**

     * $n(\text{Mg}) = \frac{4.86}{24.3} = 0.200\text{ mol}$

     * $n(\text{HCl}) = 2.00 \times \frac{100}{1000} = 0.200\text{ mol}$

  2. **Identify limiting reagent:**

     * Required ratio is $1\text{ Mg} : 2\text{ HCl}$.

     * $0.200\text{ mol of Mg}$ requires $2 \times 0.200 = 0.400\text{ mol of HCl}$.

     * Only $0.200\text{ mol of HCl}$ is present $\implies$ **$\text{HCl}$ is limiting**; $\text{Mg}$ is in excess.

  3. **Calculate product from limiting reagent:**

     * Ratio $\text{HCl} : \text{H}_2 = 2 : 1$
       $$n(\text{H}_2) = \frac{0.200}{2} = 0.100\text{ mol}$$

       $$V(\text{H}_2) = n \times 24.0\text{ dm}^3\cdot\text{mol}^ = 2.40\text{ dm}^3$$

**Deducing Stoichiometric Relationships**

* Reaction coefficients represent the simplest whole-number ratio of moles reacting and products forming.

* **Worked Example:**
  $0.108\text{ g}$ of a metal $\text{M}$ reacts completely with $100\text{ cm}^3$ of $0.0400\text{ mol}\cdot\text{dm}^{-3}\text{ H}_2\text{SO}_4$:
  $$2\text{M} + x\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{M}_2(\text{SO}_4)_x + x\text{H}_2$$

  Given $A_{\text{r}}(\text{M}) = 27.0$, deduce the value of $x$ and the charge on the metal cation.

  1. **Calculate moles:**
     $$n(\text{M}) = \frac{0.108}{27.0} = 0.00400\text{ mol}$$

     $$n(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) = 0.0400 \times \frac{100}{1000} = 0.00400\text{ mol}$$

  2. **Determine mole ratio:**
     $$\frac{n(\text{M})}{n(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4)} = \frac{0.00400}{0.00400} = \frac{1}{1} \implies \text{Ratio } 2\text{M} : 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$$

  3. **Deduce equation and charge:**

     * Since $2 : x = 2 : 2 \implies x = 2$ (simplifying to a $1:1$ reaction ratio, e.g., $\text{M} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).

     * If the formula is $\text{M}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$, the ratio would be $2:3$. For a $1:1$ stoichiometry with sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$), the salt formula is $\text{MSO}_4$, meaning the metal forms an **$\text{M}^{2+}$ ion** (or if written as $2\text{M} + 3\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, the ratio would be $1:1.5$).

     * With an exact $1:1$ reaction ratio, the balanced equation is:
       $$\text{M} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{MSO}_4 + \text{H}_2$$

     * **Cation charge:** $+2$ (since sulfate is $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$).
