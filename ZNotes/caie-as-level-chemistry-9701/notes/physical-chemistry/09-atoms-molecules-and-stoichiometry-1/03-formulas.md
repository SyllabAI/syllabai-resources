### Rules for Constructing Formulas

1. Write down the symbol and charge of the cation, then the anion

2. Criss-cross the numbers of the charges to find the ratio needed to balance charges

### Writing and Balancing Chemical Equations

* The total number of atoms of each element must be identical on both sides of the equation.

### State Symbols:

* (s): Solid / precipitate

* (l): Pure liquid (e.g., H2O)

* (g): Gas

* (aq): Aqueous solution (dissolved in water)

**Spectator Ions**: Ions that do not participate directly in the chemical change (they are not included from the final ionic equation)

* **Only** aqueous ions can be dissociated

Example:

* *Step 1: Write the balanced full equation with state symbols.*
  $$\text{BaCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq)} \to \text{BaSO}_4\text{(s)} + 2\text{NaCl}\text{(aq)}$$

* *Step 2: Split all soluble ionic substances into their constituent ions.*
  $$\text{Ba}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)} + 2\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}\text{(aq)} \to \text{BaSO}_4\text{(s)} + 2\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)} + 2\text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)}$$

* *Step 3: Identify and cancel out spectator ions (2Na\+ and 2Cl-).*
  $$\text{Ba}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}\text{(aq)} \to \text{BaSO}_4\text{(s)}$$

**Empirical Formula**: The simplest whole-number ratio of the atoms of each element present in a compound.

**Molecular Formula**: The actual number of atoms of each element present in a molecule of a compound.

$$\text{Molecular Formula} = (\text{Empirical Formula})_n$$

$$n = \frac{\text{Relative Molecular Mass } (M_r)}{\text{Empirical Formula Mass}}$$

**Water of Crystallisation**: Water molecules chemically bonded into the crystalline structure of a solid ionic compound in a fixed stoichiometric ratio.

**Hydrated**: crystalline substance containing water of crystallisation trapped within its crystal lattice

**Anhydrous**: substance that contains no water of crystallisation (usually obtained by heating a hydrated salt)
