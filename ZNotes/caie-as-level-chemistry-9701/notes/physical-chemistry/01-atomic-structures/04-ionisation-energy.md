## Ionisation Energy

Ionisation Energy : The energy required to remove **one electron** from each atom in **one mole of gaseous atoms** to form **one mole of mono-positive gaseous ions**.

* Ionisation energy is always **endothermic**

### Equations to represent subsequent ionisation energies :

$$\text{X(g)} \to \text{X}^+\text{(g)} + \text{e}^-$$

$$\text{X}^+\text{(g)} \to \text{X}^{2+}\text{(g)} + \text{e}^-$$

$$\text{X}^{2+}\text{(g)} \to \text{X}^{3+}\text{(g)} + \text{e}^-$$

### Trends in 1st Ionisation energy across 3rd Period

![Ionization Energy Trend Across Period](../../../assets/eb814c94ba31-image009.png)

**First ionisation energy of Al is lower than Mg:**

* The outer electron is removed from a **p orbital** instead of an **s orbital**

* The p sub-shell is at a **higher energy level** and is partially shielded by the inner S electrons, making it easier to remove

**First ionisation energy of S is lower than P:**

* In Phosphorous the p sub shell is half filled but in S a pair of electrons occupy the same orbital

* **Spin pair repulsion** between the two electrons in the shared orbital lowers the energy required to remove an electron

### Variation in successive ionisation energies of an element

* Successive ionisation energies for an element **always increase**

### Interpreting successive I.E graphs

* **Gradual Increase:** Electrons being removed from the same sub-shell/shell

* **Minor Jump:** Removal of an electron from an inner sub-shell within the same quantum shell

* **Major/Sharp Jump:** Electron is removed from a new principal quantum shell closer to the nucleus thus significantly reduced shielding and smaller radius.

### Deducing group number from successive I.E graphs

* Number of electrons removed before the first sharp jump = number of valence electrons (group number)

### Ionisation energy

* Ionisation energies are due to the attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron

### Factors influencing the ionisation energies of elements

* Nuclear Charge

  * A greater nuclear charge exerts a stronger electrostatic attraction on the valence electrons

  * Thus, Ionisation energy **increases** as nuclear charge increases

* Atomic / Ionic Radius

  * Electrostatic force weakens as radius increases

  * Thus as  radius increases ionisation energy **decreases**

* Shielding by Inner Shells and Sub-shells:

  * More filled shells and sub shells between the nucleus and the outer electrons result in increased repulsion of the outer shell electrons

  * Thus ionisation energy **decreases**

* Spin-pair repulstion

  * Less energy required to remove one of the paired electrons compared to an electron from a singly occupied orbital

  * Thus Ionisation energy **decreases**

Deduce the group and period of element X, and identify the element. *\[3 marks\]*

* Determine the Group (Number of Valence Electrons)

  * Look for the largest jump in successive ionisation energy values.

  * A massive jump occurs between the $3^{\text{rd}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$ ionisation energies ($2,750 \to 11,600\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$).

  * This indicates that the $4^{\text{th}}$ electron is being removed from a new, inner principal quantum shell closer to the nucleus, which experiences much less shielding.

  * There are 3 valence electrons in the outer shell therefore Element X belongs to Group 13.

* Determine the Period

  * Analyze all the shells by tracking the number of electrons in each energy level based on the jumps:

    * Outer shell (n=3): 3 electrons removed before the first major jump (electrons 1 to 3).

    * Inner shell (n=2): 8 electrons removed before the next major jump (electrons 4 to 11). 

    * Innermost shell (n=1): 2 electrons in the first shell.

  * Count the total number of principal quantum shells that contain electrons. Since electrons occupy up to the third shell:

  *  Element X is in Period 3.

* Identify the element

  * Element in Group 13, Period 3 with 13 total electrons is Aluminium
