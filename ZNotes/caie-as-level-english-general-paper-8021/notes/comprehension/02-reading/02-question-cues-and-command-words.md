## **Question Cues and Command Words**

After your first read, don’t reread the whole thing. Focus on the **questions** and **your annotations**. Use the line numbers or keywords in the question to guide where you look.

* Always have the text *open next to the question*. Flip back and forth constantly - the answer is **always** in the text.

**Question Cues**

Look for these patterns and phrases in your questions to make your reading more efficient:

#|
||

Phrase

|

Line reference, “(lines 10–14)”

|

Read a little before and after to get the full context. Answers are rarely just in the line they give.

||
||

“What does this suggest…”

|

Highlight words loaded with tone/emotion. These often highlight the writer’s intent.

||
||

Definition/context questions

|

Mentally swap the word out with synonyms you already know to see if it feels right. Trust your gut, go for a word that fits the context and tone over just the literal dictionary meaning.

||
||

“In your own words…”

|

Do not use the same key terms, understand the text, then pretend you’re explaining to a younger sibling. Once you have your framework, polish your vocabulary.

||
||

“How does the writer…?”

|

Analyses the words and structure of the text, meaning the choice of vocabulary and how the sentences are constructed.

||
|#
