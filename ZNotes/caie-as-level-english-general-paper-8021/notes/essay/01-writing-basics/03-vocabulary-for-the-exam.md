## **Vocabulary for the Exam**

### **Why Vocabulary Matters**

* In essays: shows precision, maturity, and clarity.

* In comprehension: helps interpret tone, meaning, and intention.

* Using better vocab improves your **style, tone**, and **evaluation quality**.

### **Useful Linking and Signpost Phrases**

**To introduce points:**

* One key argument is

* A major factor to consider is

* It is often argued that

**To contrast:**

* However

* On the other hand

* In contrast

* Nevertheless

**To add:**

* Furthermore

* In addition

* Moreover

* Not only that

**To give examples:**

* For instance

* A relevant example is

* This is illustrated by

**To conclude or evaluate:**

* In summary

* Ultimately

* While some argue that

* The evidence suggests that

### **Topic-Specific Vocabulary to Use in Essays**

**Politics**: governance, authoritarian, populist, democratic values, policy-making
**Law**: justice, legality, human rights, legislation, enforcement
**Environment**: sustainability, biodiversity, carbon emissions, conservation
**Technology**: innovation, automation, cybersecurity, digital divide
**Society**: inequality, marginalised, social norms, cultural values
**Education**: curriculum, pedagogy, literacy, academic achievement
**Economy**: inflation, unemployment, economic growth, wealth disparity
**Ethics**: morality, exploitation, accountability, integrity

### **Vocabulary for Tone and Attitude**

* **Positive**: optimistic, enthusiastic, respectful, celebratory

* **Negative**: critical, sarcastic, dismissive, frustrated

* **Neutral**: objective, analytical, reflective, descriptive

* **Persuasive**: assertive, compelling, emphatic, passionate

* **Humorous/Ironic**: witty, sarcastic, satirical, playful

### **How to Improve Your Vocabulary?**

* Keep a personal word bank of strong words you like and understand

* Read articles.

* When revising a topic, learn 5 topic-specific words with it

* Use synonyms only if you are sure of their meaning and tone
