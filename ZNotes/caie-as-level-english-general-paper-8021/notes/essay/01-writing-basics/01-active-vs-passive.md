# **Writing Basics**

**Active vs Passive Voice**

### **1. What’s the difference?**

* **Active voice**: The subject does the action.

  * *Example:* The journalist wrote the article.

* **Passive voice**: The subject receives the action.

  * *Example:* The article was written by the journalist.

Both are grammatically correct - the choice depends on what you want to **emphasise.**

* **Active structure**:

  * *[Subject] + [verb] + [object]*

  * *Example:* *The manager approved the proposal.*

* **Passive structure**:

  * *[Object] + [form of "to be"] + [past participle]* (+ *by [subject]*)

  * *Example:* *The proposal was approved by the manager.*

### **3. When to choose one over the other?**

Use **active voice** when:

* You want to highlight **who is doing the action**

* The subject’s role is important

Use **passive voice** when:

* The **doer is unknown, obvious, or not important**

* You want to **focus on the result or action**

### **4.  Usage**

* In essays, using a mix of both can show variety and intention.

* Too much passive voice can sound vague if you don’t clarify the subject, and too much active voice makes your writing repetitive.

* Rewriting passive sentences into active is a great editing habit; even if you don’t change them, it helps you understand your writing flow.
