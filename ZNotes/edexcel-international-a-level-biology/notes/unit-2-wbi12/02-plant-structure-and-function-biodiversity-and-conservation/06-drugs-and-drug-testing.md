## Drugs and Drug Testing

* Plants carry many antimicrobial properties which could be used by
  humans to create medicines.
* For bacteria to grow, certain conditions must be present like warmth, moisture, nutrients, oxygen and optimum pH.
* A new drug has to go through several testing stages before being
  available to patients.
* William Withering used a plant called foxgloves that contained the chemical called Digitalis to produce a heart treatment medicine against dropsy (oedema). It has been done through the following process:
  * He extracted the active ingredient from the plant
  * Tested the drug on patients with the disease
  * Side effects were noted
  * He slowly increased the dose of the medicine until patients
    experienced diarrhoea and vomiting then began reducing the dose
    until reaching an ideal dose
  * Recorded and published the results.

Current testing procedures differ from what William used. They are
divided into phases:

* **Preclinical Trials**: Before drugs could be tested on humans,
  they have to undergo testing on other mammals like rats or
  monkeys. Sometimes computer models could be used to see the
  effect of the drug on the cell. If a drug doesn’t pass this
  stage, there is no use of risking human lives on it.
* **Clinical Trials**:
  * **Phase 1**: A small sample of healthy adults are tested for the drug. Healthy adults are used initially because they have a strong immune system and won’t get as much harm if the drug was harmful. This phase is also used to check for side effects.
  * **Phase 2**: A slightly larger sample on patients carrying
    the disease are used. In this stage patients are monitored
    to check for the ideal dosage.
  * **Phase 3**: A large sample size is used. This phase is used to check for the effectiveness and long-term effect of the drugs. The results of the phase and then compared to other groups of people and existing treatments.

Drug testing must have some of these trials conducted in order to see the true effectiveness of the drug.

* **Placebo Trials**
  * Patients may take the placebo drug, which does not contain the drug that is being tested—a sugar pill.
  * Patients are not informed that the pill that they have take does not contain the drug.
  * Patients will experience a placebo effect where they psychologically believe that the drug is making them feel better, so their health starts to improve.
* **Single-blinded Trials**
  * Patients do not know what they will be given—either the drug or the placebo, but the doctors do.
* **Double-blinded Trials**
  * Both the patients and doctors do not know what will be administered—either the drug or the placebo.
  * Usually, a computer program is used to decide this and is kept hiden from both the patients and the doctors.
* **Peer-review**
  * Drug testing is reviewed by other members of the scientific community to ensure that the drug testing has been carried out according to procedures and does not violate any safety standard measures.
