## Adaptations and Gene Pools

**Types of Adaptations**:

* **Anatomical**: A physical change in the structure of an
  organism giving it an advantage
* **Physiological**: A change in the chemical working of the body
  giving an advantage
* **Behavioral**: A change in instinctive behaviors for survival
  benefits

**Hardy-Weinberg’s Principle:**

* Used to calculate allele, genotype and phenotype frequencies
  within a large randomly mating population.
  $p + q = 1$ and
  $p^{2} + 2pq + q^{2} = 1$
  * $p$ represents dominant allele frequency
  * $q$ represents recessive allele frequency
* Hardy-Weinberg’s principle does not apply when there is:
  * Significant selective pressure against a genotype
  * Migration into or out of population
  * Non-random mating
  * Limited population
* **Gene pool**: The total amount of genes in a given population.
* Mutations cause changes in the genetic information of an organism,
  which can lead to a change in the gene frequency in a given
  population. They can also occur as a result of natural selection.
* **Natural selection:** Suitable organisms, who are adapted to their environment survive and are able to reproduce and pass on favourable genes to their offsprings to aid in their survival.
* **Evolution:** The change in the allele frequency in a gene pool over time

**Natural selection leads to evolution:**

* The population of an organism contains diverse genes.
* A catrastrophic event occurs in the environment, causing a change in selection pressure.
  * **Selection pressure:** the factors that affect the survivability of an organism.
* Those who survive the event get to reproduce and pass on their favourable genes to their offsprings
* The allele frequency changes in a gene pool of a population, which lead to evolution.
