## Controlling Gene Expression

* DNA consists of introns (non-coding DNA) and exons.
* **Transcription factors** are proteins that bind to a specific region in the DNA called the **promoter sequence.** Other transcription factors bind to the **enhancer sequence** and work by physically changing the chromatin structure.
* These transcription factors are essential in promoting the transcription process, allowing expression of genes.
* Each transcription factor may either surpress or express certain genes.
* **Post-transcriptional Modification/RNA Splicing:**
  * After the transcription process occurs, the pre-mRNA is produced
  * Spliceosomes separate the introns and exosn and then remove introns from the pre-mRNA.
  * The exons can be arranged in multiple different combinations by the spliceosomes to form mRNA.
  * The exons can be arranged in different lengths by the spliceosomes to form mRNA.
* By this process, multiple different proteins could be produced from 1 gene.
* **Epigenetics**: The study of the factors that affect genes other than the base sequence of the DNA.
* **DNA Methylation**: It’s when a methyl group is added, surpressing a gene.
* **DNA Demethylation**: The removal of the methyl group, causing a gene to be reactivated.
* **Histone acetylation:** Addition of acetyl group, causing transcription of a certain gene.
* **Histone methylation:** This is different to DNA methylation. It depends on the position of the lysine (where methyl is added).
* **Differential Gene Expression causes the specialisation of cells:**
  * The cell receives a chemical stimulus from outside the cell which causes epigenetic modifications in DNA.
  * Certain genes are then switched on and others are switched off.
  * Transcription occurs, producing pre-MRNA.
  * Post-transcription modification/RNA splicing occurs, producing mRNA.
  * The mRNA is then translated into functional proteins.
  * The proteins cause structural and functional changes in the cell.
  * The cell is now permanently specialised.
