## Infection and disease

### The role of barriers in protecting the body

* The skin acts as a physical barrier against infection.

* Stomach acid-high pH denatures bacterial enzymes

* Skin and gut flora provide competition for colonization by pathogens

### **Non-specific immune response**

1. inflammation-characteristics are heat, swelling, pain, and redness which provide more kinetic energy for the body’s enzymes while denaturing bacterial enzymes, increases blood supply to the area by dilation of blood vessels [as damaged cells release chemicals such as histamines which cause dilation], and alert the body that an infection is present
2. lysozyme action causes lysis of infected cells, which releases the pathogen and causes fever
3. interferons-[for viral infections only] infected cells produce proteins to bind to
   neighbouring healthy cells and inhibit viral replication within the cell
4. phagocytosis-phagocytes engulf pathogens, sometimes digesting them, and
   release signalling molecules

B and T lymphocytes also produce antibodies to destroy pathogens.

### Specific immune response

**Humoral**

* The Antigen Presenting Cell [APC] is a macrophage
* The APC activates T helper cells
* Which release cytokines
* Which causes plasma cells to release antibodies
* Agglutination and opsionisation are carried out by antibodies on pathogens [usually viruses as they’re the ones who enter host cells]

**Cell-mediated**

* An infected body cell becomes the APC
* T killer cell binds to the APC [host cell] along with the cytokines from activated T helper [T killer cloning and T killer activated cells]
* Activated T killer cells [which were activated by the cytokines] bind to the host cell and release perforins
* Perforins cause bursting of the cell
* Viruses are released and destroyed by phagocytes through agglutination and opsionisation.
* This method is very effective as viruses can be destroyed before fully forming.

**Immunity**

* active natural getting disease
* active artificial→ vaccination
* passive natural → breastmilk or placenta
* passive artificial → antibody injections
* In active immunity, B memory cells are produced.
* Active immunity antigens are present, and passive immunity-only antibodies are present

Q) Describe how the theory of an ‘evolutionary race’ between pathogens and their hosts is supported by evasion mechanisms shown by pathogens.

* Humans keep developing new drugs [especially antibiotics] but pathogens [especially bacteria] gain resistance much faster than we develop new drugs.

* This is due to bacteria’s fast rate of reproduction and human tendency to provoke antibiotic resistance.

### **Antibiotics**

* Bacteriocidal antibiotics destroy bacteria.

* Bacteriostatic antibiotics stop bacteria from reproducing.

* Codes of conduct related to antibiotic prescription include:

  1. don’t prescribe antibiotics for viral infections
  2. ensure the correct dosage is given and the course of antibiotics is completed

**To**:

1. reduce the possibility of antibiotic resistance through natural selection [in the evolutionary race]
2. prevent the formation of superbugs
