## Answering Questions

The last question will generally consist of 4 parts:

* A 6-marker asking you to plan an experiment and a few short questions on theory. State the independent and dependent variables.

* A general practical question involving both theory and calculations.

* A description of an experiment which tests your ability to use statistical tests. You may be asked to state a null hypothesis, calculate means, draw suitable tables and graphs, calculate values, and compare them to critical values [remember to use 0.05 and round to the nearest value of degrees of freedom] to accept or reject the null hypothesis.

* A question testing your complete knowledge of planning an experiment. The first part may ask you to detail preliminary practical work: finding suitable temperature, pH, concentration, species, method, weather and conditions to experiment. The second part is generally a 10-marker on planning the investigation. Remember the independent and dependent variables, use five different concentrations/temperatures if they are being tested, repeat the experiment thrice, and any calculations needed. The third part asks how the data should be recorded, presented and analysed; you must draw a table or graph [usually a histogram or scatter graph] and state a suitable statistical test to analyse the data. The fourth part will ask about the limitations of your proposed method, so consider what conditions cannot be controlled and what your method cannot test.
