## Core Practical 13

### Investigating the growth rate of microorganisms in liquid culture, considering the safe and ethical use of organisms

**Equipment**:

* eye protection
* disinfectant
* paper towels, stirrer, cotton wool, aluminium foil
* Bunsen burner
* colourimeter set to absorbance with the filter of wavelength 600 nm and cuvettes OR light sensor and datalogger
* yeast cultured for 24 hours
* sterile nutrient broth in a sterile culture vessel, a glass bottle of sterile broth
* six 5cm3 measuring cylinders and 1cm3 automatic
* pipettes

**Procedure**:

* Carry out aseptic techniques to disinfect the work area and apparatus.

Produce a culture containing 250cm3 of 0.5% glucose solution and 1.25g of yeast stirred well with a magnetic stirrer, loosely covered with a cotton wool stopper and incubated at room temperature.

**If using a colourimeter:**

* Fill a cuvette with the glucose culture medium and set the absorbance to zero.
* When the yeast suspension has been mixed, transfer a 3cm3 sample into a cuvette, measure the absorbance and record it in a table along with the time. Return the culture to the stirrer.
* Repeat the above two steps five to eight times over the next 12 hours, taking them at 30-minute intervals after the first 2 hours.

If using a light sensor and datalogger:

* Place a light source and heat shield on one side of the yeast culture and a light sensor on the opposite side.
* Place a cardboard box with a cutout near the light source over the flask and light sensor to prevent ambient light from entering.
* Connect the light sensor to a datalogger and leave it to record continuously for 12 hours.

**Observations and analysis**

* The initial density of cells in the culture can be found using a haemocytometer or graph paper.
* Stain the yeast suspension with a few drops of 0.1% methylene blue, place one drop onto a microscope slide using a pipette cover with a coverslip, and count yeast cells within one field of view under the x40 objective.
* The volume of one pipette drop = volume of 10 drops divided by 10. Divide the area of the x40 field of view by the area of the coverslip, then multiply by the volume of one drop to find the volume under one field of view.
* The initial yeast population cell density can be found by dividing the average cell count in one field of view by the volume of that field of view.
* If many cells overlap when viewed under the x40 objective, the yeast suspension must be diluted for increased accuracy.
