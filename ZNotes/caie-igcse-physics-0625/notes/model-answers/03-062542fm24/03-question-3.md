**b)** 

$P_1 = 2\times10^6$

$P_2 = 1\times10^5$

$V_1 = 0.026 m^3$

$V_2 =,?$

*We need to figure out the volume in order to know the maximum number of balloons that can be filled.*

$$P_1V_1=P_2V_2$$

$$2\times10^6, \times,0.026 m^3, = 1\times10^5, \times, V_2$$

$$V_2 = \frac{2\times10^6, \times,0.026 m^3}{1\times10^5}$$

$$V_2 = 0.52m^3$$

*Each balloon can hold* $0.015m^3$, *so we need to find how many balloons can be filled up.*

*So, we can divide the total volume we have and divide by the volume of one balloon.*

$$\frac{0.52}{0.015}= 34.6$$

*The maximum amount of balloons that can be filled up is 34 (we ignore the decimal).*
