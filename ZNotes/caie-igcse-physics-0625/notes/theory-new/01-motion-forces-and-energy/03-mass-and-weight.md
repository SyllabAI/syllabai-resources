## Mass and weight

* Mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer

* Weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass

* Weight is also the effect of gravitational field on a mass

* $W = mg$

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/0cd0a9ee9891c3a4b3471d3e.webp =300x)

* Mass would be the same for both as it is the identical object, however because the gravitational field strength is different in both planets, there is a difference in weight.

* Gravitational field strength is force per unit mass (this is equivalent to the acceleration of free fall):

* $g=\frac{W}{m}$

* To measure mass or weight, use a balance

* Beam balance or a digital balance for mass

* Spring balance for weight

**Terminal velocity (Example of a parachutist)**

* When the parachutist jumps, initially, there is no drag force, and the only force acting on the parachutist is weight. Acceleration is $9.8m/s^2$

* After some time, the speed increases and the resultant force decreases as Resultant force= Weight-drag force. Acceleration starts to decrease.

* Terminal velocity is reached. Drag force= weight. Resultant force = 0. Acceleration = 0. Constant velocity

* When the parachute is deployed, Surface area increases, drag force increases. Acceleration is upwards and hence the skydiver’s speed decreases. The parachutist decelerates

* When the parachutist reaches the ground, he is at rest
