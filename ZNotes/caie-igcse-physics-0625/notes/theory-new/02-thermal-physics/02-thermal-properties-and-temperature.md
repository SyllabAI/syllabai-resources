## Thermal properties and temperature

### Thermal expansion of solids, liquids and gases

* Thermal expansion is the expanding of matter by the use of heat

* Gases expand the most because they have weak intermolecular forces of attraction between particles and so the distance between particles would be greater compared to solid upon heating.

* When solid, liquid or gas is heated at constant pressure: (the reverse is thermal contraction)

1. Particles gain K.E

2. This causes particles to vibrate/move faster

3. Distance between particles increases

4. Because they are expanding, the volume increases

Examples of thermal expansion in everyday scenarios:

1. Bimetallic strip: It is a strip made up of 2 metals which expand at different rates. When heated, one metal expands more than the other resulting in the bending of the strip to a specific direction. An application of it can be an electric kettle

2. Railway lines: These are made to have tiny gaps so that when the temperature increases during summer, thermal expansion can occur easily.

3. Thermometer: Has either mercury or alcohol that expand and increase in height when temperature increases.

4. Electric cables: Are connected with less tension so that during winter, they don’t break by contracting

### Specific heat capacity

* Rise in the temperature of an object increases its internal energy (sum of all kinetic and potential energies) and increases the average kinetic energies of all particles.

* Specific heat capacity is the energy required per unit mass per unit temperature increase.

* $\boxed{c = \frac{\Delta E}{m \Delta T}}$

* Unit: $$\text{J}/(\text{kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C})$$

**Experiment to measure the specific heat capacity:**

* Measure the mass (of a metal using a balance or if it’s a liquid then measure the difference in mass)

* Turn on the heat and increase it to maximum heat

* Start the stopwatch and record the initial temperature

* Record the voltage and current

* Run the heater for a set amount of time and record the final temperature

### Melting, boiling and evaporation

* Melting is the process where a solid turns to liquid by the input of energy at a specific temperature.

* Boiling is the process where a liquid turns to gas by the input of energy at a specific temperature

* The energy absorbed in these processes is used to break the bonds between molecules rather than increasing the temperature and hence the temperature remains constant until the substance completely changes to liquid/gas.

* Melting point of water = 0 $°C$

* Boiling point of water = 100 $°C$  (at standard atmospheric pressure)

* Solidification/freezing: The process where a liquid changes to solid by releasing thermal energy at a specific temperature (freezing/melting point). Intermolecular forces between particles strengthen which pull particles closer together to an arrangement of a solid.

* Condensation: The process where a gas changes to liquid by releasing thermal energy at a specific temperature (boiling point). Intermolecular forces between particles strengthen which pull particles closer together to an arrangement of a liquid.

* In both of these processes, the temperature and kinetic energy of particles remain constant

* Evaporation is a process where a liquid turns into a gas by the input of heat. The more energetic particles on the surface gain this heat energy and evaporate into gas to escape the liquid

* The average K.E of the remaining particles decreases

* As a result, the temperature of the liquid underneath decreases, and this is what happens during sweating

* Factors that increase the rate of evaporation:

  * Greater temperature: more energetic molecules on the surface that evaporate

  * Larger surface area: more energetic molecules on the surface that evaporate

  * Higher wind speed: removes the water vapour for the vapour pressure gradient to increase and therefore, more water vapour escapes

  * Low humidity: means less water vapour are present which helps more water vapour to escape
