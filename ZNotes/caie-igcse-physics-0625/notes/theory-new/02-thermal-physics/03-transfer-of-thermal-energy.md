## Transfer of thermal energy

### Conduction

* Thermal conduction: Transfer of heat through vibration of atoms in regular lattice with nearby atoms and movement of the delocalised electrons.

* Thermal conduction in solids: transfer of heat through atomic or molecular lattice vibrations.

* Thermal conduction in metals: transfer of heat through atomic/molecular lattice vibrations and by the movement of free/delocalised electrons in metallic conductors.

* For instance, heating a metal rod on one end will increase the kinetic energy of the free electrons that then collide with other electrons and metal atoms which results in thermal energy being passed to the other end of the rod.

* Thermal conduction is poor in gases and liquids as their particles are far from each other and have more spaces between them.

**Experiment to distinguish between good and bad conductors:**

1. Take 3-5 rods of different materials (with same length, thickness, and cross-sectional area)

2. use the same amount of wax to stick a pin on the ends of each rod

3. Take the opposite end of each rod and place them on top of a Bunsen burner

4. Record the time taken for the pin to fall from each rod

5. The rod for which the pin attached to it takes the least time to drop is the best conductor amongst the samples

6. The rod for which the pin attached to it takes most time to drop is the worst conductor amongst the samples

### Convection

* Convection is crucial method of thermal energy transfer in liquids and gases

* Applications of convection:

1. Ocean currents

2. Weather

3. Sea breeze and land breeze

4. Boiling water

* Convection in liquid and gases is the transfer of heat in fluids through the movement of the fluid itself, driven by density changes.

* Heated fluid expands and spreads out (increases the volume, decreases the density).

* The less dense, warmer fluid rises while the cooler, denser fluid sinks.

* This cycle continues till temperature is equal throughout the liquid/gas and establishes an equilibrium.

### Radiation

* Thermal radiation is infrared radiation, and all objects emit this radiation

* Thermal radiation is the transfer of thermal energy from one place to another via electromagnetic waves.

* This doesn’t require a medium to transfer the energy and so it can occur in a vacuum

* Dull black surfaces are the best emitters and absorbers

* Shiny white surfaces are the worst emitters and absorbers

* Rough surfaces absorb and emit more energy than smooth surfaces

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/54726dc720d9aa17686be9ab.webp =x200)

**Greenhouse effect:**

* Thermal radiation from the Sun is emitted and enter the earth’s atmosphere.

* Some radiation is absorbed by the earth’s surface and then emitted into the space

* Some of this emitted radiation gets absorbed and re-emitted by the greenhouse gases in the atmosphere towards the Earth

* This causes the Earth’s surface to heat up leading to global warming

**Factors that increase the rate of emitted energy are:**

* High temperature of the surface of an object

* Greater surface area of an object

**Experiment to distinguish between good and bad emitters of infrared radiation:**

* Fill 2 identical cans with different surfaces (one with dull black and the other silver/white) with hot water

* Insert a thermometer into both cans

* Make sure the initial temperature is the same

* After a set amount of time, record the final temperature shown on both the thermometers

* Calculate the change in temperature by finding the difference between both the values

* The greater decrease in temperature = good emitter

**Experiment to distinguish between good and bad absorbers of infrared radiation:**

* Fill 2 identical cans with different surfaces (one with dull black and the other silver/white) with water

* Insert a thermometer into both cans

* Record the initial temperature

* Place a heater equidistant from both cans and start the stopwatch

* After a set amount of time, record the final temperature shown on both the thermometers

* Calculate the change in temperature by finding the difference between both the values

* The greater change in temperature = good absorber

### Consequences of thermal energy transfer

**Heating objects such as kitchen pans:**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/207685ffedf7f27fc2ace5a1.webp =x400)

* Thermal energy is transferred to the pan from the fire as radiation

* Thermal energy is transferred through the pan by conduction

* Thermal energy is transferred from the pan to the water by conduction

* Thermal energy is transferred through the water by convection

* Thermal energy is transferred through the air above the pan by convection

* The insulators decrease the thermal energy transfer by conduction, so it is not hot the touch when holding the handle

**Heating a room by convection:**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/60a93adcc4c09456b3236df9.webp =x300)

* When the heater is turned on, the air surrounding it gets heated

* This causes the volume to increase, which subsequently decreases the density of the air near the heater.

* The hot air rises and the cool, denser air sinks

* This results in formation of convection currents

**A fire burning wood:**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/e6e180596cde13551f9091b7.webp =x300)

**A radiator in a car:**

* Thermal energy moves from the hot metal engine block into the touching liquid coolant through direct contact (Conduction)

* The pump forces the hot liquid coolant to flow through pipes from the engine to the radiator, carrying the thermal energy along with it​(Convection)

* Thermal energy passes directly from the hot liquid, through the solid metal radiator tubes, and into the thin metal cooling fins ​(Conduction)

* ​Air forced past the fins by the fan or the car's motion heats up, becomes less dense, and carries thermal energy away from the radiator (Convection)
  Little thermal energy is emitted as radiation from the hot metal surfaces into the cooler surroundings ​(Radiation)
