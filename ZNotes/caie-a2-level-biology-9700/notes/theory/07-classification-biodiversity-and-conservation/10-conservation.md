## Conservation

* Ways to protect endangered species:

### National parks:

* Designated areas where ecosystems are protected, in their natural environments

* Human activities such building, hunting, mining, agriculture, industrial work etc. are strictly limited

  * Governments implement conservation laws, and rangers patrol the parks to prevent illegal activity

* Areas that are sensitive to human interference have restricted access

* Alien species (e.g. rats) and invasive plants (e.g. elephant grass) are removed

* Pollution is prevented which protects habitats and breeding sites

* Ecotourism generates funds for maintenance, while educating visitors and raising awareness for public support

* Local people are employed as wardens and rangers, and given incentives

* There are captive breeding and reintroduction programs

* Marine parks are also set up to protect marine ecosystems at risk of overfishing, dredging and pollution

* Example: The Serengeti National Park in Tanzania

**Advantages:**

1. Species are protected from extinction, and biodiversity is maintained

2. Organisms are not moved from their natural habitat

3. It feels closer to wildlife than zoos

**Disadvantages:**

1. Animals are restricted to specific area and cannot migrate

2. Enforcement of conservation laws may be difficult

3. Some species are still so threatened that they are moved to safer places like zoos

### Zoos:

* Prevents extinction by breeding endangered species in captive breeding programs

* The goal is to increase their numbers, and then reintroduce them to their natural habitat to restore wild populations

* Food, healthcare, and protection from predators is provided to the individuals

* Research is done to understand breeding habits, diet requirements and ways to increase genetic diversity to better help the animals

  * It is easier to research animals this way too e.g. their behavior

* Animals are bred with different mates to maintain genetic diversity

* The mother’s health and fetus’s development can be monitored during pregnancy

* Species at high risk in the wild are kept longer in zoos

**Disadvantages:**

1. The animals can simply refuse to mate in captivity

2. Inbreeding may occur due to a small gene pool

3. There are ethical concerns with keeping animals captive

4. Animals may be stressed due to being captive, and show changes in behavior

5. Difficult for animals to readjust and adapt to wildlife after being cared for as they do not have the skills required to survive

* When released into the wild, the animals:

  * Have difficulty in finding food

  * Do not fear humans, who could hunt them

  * May not integrate into other groups

  * Do not know their predators so they do not avoid them

  * Are more susceptible to disease

### Assisted Reproduction:

Solution to inbreeding, infertility, and when animals do not mate

### In vitro fertilization (IVF):

* Genetic material from endangered species is combined in a lab to create embryos

1. The female is given hormone treatment e.g. FSH to stimulate multiple egg production (**superovulation**)

2. A needle is inserted into the female’s ovaries to harvest **oocytes** (eggs)

3. Sperm with high motility, no disease and is genetically different is obtained from males

4. The oocytes mature in a culture medium then mixed with sperm in vitro

5. The resulting embryos are genetically screened before **embryo transfer**:

6. The embryo can be implanted into the same female, but for critically endangered species that cannot be risked with pregnancy, it is implanted into **surrogate** mothers

* The surrogate females are usually closely related species that are not endangered

* The embryos could also be frozen and stored for future use in a frozen zoo

### Artificial insemination (AI):

1. Samples of semen are collected from males and screened to select sperm with high motility, no disease and is genetically different

2. The sperm is put into thin tubes (called straws) and stored as frozen, in liquid nitrogen at -196°C

3. When needed, a straw is withdrawn from the sperm bank and placed into warm water to become active

4. The female is given hormone treatment to super ovulate, then the straw with active sperm is inserted into her uterus using a catheter

5. The resulting embryos may develop into fetuses in the same female, or be transferred to other females (embryo transfer)

### Botanic gardens:

**Roles of botanic gardens:**

1. Protect endangered plant species

2. Conserve or restore habitats

3. Research methods of reproduction, growth, and conservation, cooperating with governments, charities or universities

4. Reintroduce species to natural habitats

5. Educate the public in the roles of plants and their economic value, and spread awareness on conserving them

6. Raise funds

**Conservation methods in botanic gardens:**

1. Seeds or cuttings are collected from species in the wild and cloned

* They are then grown to increase numbers and seeds are collected, so the species can later be reintroduced to the natural habitat

2. Tissue culture & cloning:

* Small samples of cells are taken and grown on agar, where they divide by mitosis to form a mass of identical cells that can be cloned

* The cells are transferred to a mixture containing plant hormones, so they grow stems and roots and can then be transferred to grow in soil

### Seed banks:

* Seeds are stored in seed banks so that if any plant becomes extinct, there would be seeds from which they can be grown

* e.g. Millennium Seed Bank, Svalbard Global Seed Vault etc.

1. Plant seeds are collected from many different sites in the wild from many countries (to maintain an expansive gene pool), especially from areas that are at risk

2. The seeds are planted and grown in botanic gardens

3. Plant numbers and seed numbers are increased by tissue culture and cloning

4. The seeds are dehydrated to protect from ice crystals, then they are stored

* Germination tests are carried out every 5 years to check if stored seeds are still viable

* If not many of the stored seeds germinate successfully, plants are grown from the seeds to collect and store fresh seeds

**Disadvantages:**

1. The gene pool could be narrowed when a few seeds are taken from stored samples to collect fresh seeds (like after unsuccessful germination tests)

2. Some plant seeds are difficult to be dried and frozen (called **recalcitrant** seeds)
