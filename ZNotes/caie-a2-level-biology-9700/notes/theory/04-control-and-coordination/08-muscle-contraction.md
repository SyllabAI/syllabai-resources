## Muscle Contraction

* The movement of thin filaments closer together driven by the action of myosin heads in the thick filaments

* Sarcomere gets shorter as the Z-discs are pulled closer together

* The energy comes from ATP molecules attached to the myosin heads; each myosin head acts as an ATPase enzyme

* Begins at **neuromuscular junction** (synapse between neurone and muscle fiber):

 1. Action potential arrives at presynaptic neurone, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ ion channels open, and Ca²⁺ ions diffuse in

 2. Influx of Ca²⁺ causes vesicles containing ACh to fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release ACh into the cleft

 3. ACh binds to receptors on the sarcolemma, opening Na⁺ ion channels

 4. Na⁺ ions enter, depolarizing the sarcolemma and initiating an action potential

 5. The depolarization spreads along the sarcolemma and T-tubules, to the sarcoplasmic reticulum

 6. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open, and Ca²⁺ ions diffuse out of the SR to the sarcoplasm

 7. Ca²⁺ ions bind to troponin, which changes shape and moves tropomyosin

 8. Tropomyosin moves to expose myosin-binding sites on the actin filaments

 9. Myosin heads form cross-bridges with thin filaments (so the 2 filament types connect)

10. Myosin heads move and pull actin filaments towards the M-line, shortening sarcomere

### Sliding Filament Model:

* As the muscle continues to contract, further steps occur so that the process continues

11. The heads hydrolyze ATP for energy which causes the heads to release the actin filaments

12. The heads then move back to their original position, and bind again to exposed sites, repeating the process and pulling actin filaments further

* The myosin heads hydrolyze more ATP to let go each time

* The process continues as long as the binding sites are open, and the muscle has a supply of ATP

### Muscle Relaxation:

1. Ca²⁺ is actively reabsorbed into the SR

2. Ca²⁺ does not bind to troponin on the actin filament

3. Tropomyosin moves to block myosin-binding sites on actin filament

4. Filaments slide back into original position
