## Sensory Receptor Cells

* These are cells that respond to stimuli by initiating an action potential

* Can be specialized cells or just the end of a sensory neurone

* Found in sense organs e.g. chemoreceptors in the taste buds

* They act as **transducers**, converting the stimulus’ energy into electrical impulses and generating receptor potential

* **Receptor Potential**: A change in normal resting potential across the membrane of a receptor cell specifically, caused by a stimulus

### Chemoreceptors in the Taste Buds:

* Chemoreceptors are receptor cells that respond to electrical stimuli

* The tongue is covered in **papillae** (small bumps) which have many taste buds over its surface, and each taste bud has **50-100** chemoreceptors

* Each chemoreceptor is covered with receptor proteins that are specific to different chemicals (tastes)

* There are 5 tastes: sweet, sour, salt, bitter and umami

* Chemoreceptors that detect salt are involved with Na⁺ ions:

1. Na⁺ ions diffuse into the cell through microvilli, depolarizing the membrane by increasing positive charge, and generates a receptor potential

2. Depolarization stimulates voltage-gates Ca²⁺ ion channels to open, so Ca²⁺ ions enter

3. The diffusion of Ca²⁺ ions stimulates movement of vesicles and release of neurotransmitter by exocytosis

4. The neurotransmitters stimulate receptor potential to become action potential in the sensory neurone if stimulation is above threshold

5. The impulses are sent to the taste center in the cerebral cortex of the brain
