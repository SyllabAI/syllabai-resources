## Sex Linkage

* Linked genes are genes located on the same chromosome and are likely to be inherited together

* Genes that are physically closer on a chromosome are less likely to be separated during crossing over

* They are said to be in the same **linkage group**

* Sex linked genes are located on the sex chromosomes

* They are usually on the X chromosome because the Y chromosome is shorter and carries fewer genes

* The inheritance of these genes depends on the sex of the individual

* To show genotypes for sex-linked genes, write alleles as superscripts

  * e.g. **Xᴿ** (dominant) and **Xʳ** (recessive)

* Males (XY) have only one X chromosome, so they express any allele present on their X chromosome even if it’s recessive

* Females (XX) have two X chromosomes, so they can be homozygous or heterozygous

### Example: Hemophilia

* The X chromosome has a gene that codes for the production of **factor VIII**, which is necessary for blood clotting

  * **Xᴴ** is the normal, dominant allele which produces the factor

  * **Xʰ** is the mutated, recessive allele which causes hemophilia

* In hemophilia, blood does not clot properly due to the lack of factor VIII

* Males have higher chances of hemophilia because there are only 2 possible genotypes for them

* Females have 3 possible genotypes and can be carriers
