## Control of Blood Glucose

* Low glucose concentration: Cells do not have enough glucose for respiration

* High glucose concentration: Excess glucose causes cells to lose water due to the concentration gradient built and become flaccid, disrupting normal cell behavior

* Blood glucose concentration is controlled by the hormones insulin and glucagon, secreted by the **islets of Langerhans** in the pancreas

* Endocrine glands are ductless (like the islets), while exocrine glands have ducts

### The islets contain 2 types of cells:

1. **Alpha cells** that secrete glucagon, which increases blood sugar

2. **Beta cells** that secrete insulin, which decreases blood sugar

* Receptors for protein hormones like insulin are on the cell surface membrane since proteins cannot pass through the lipid bilayer

* Receptors for steroid hormones are inside the cytoplasm because lipids can pass through

### Insulin Action

* Lowers blood glucose by increasing glucose uptake into cells

* Target cells: liver cells, muscle cells and fat cells all have insulin receptors on their membranes

* B-cells secrete insulin into the bloodstream, which binds to the specific receptors

1. Increases glucose uptake by stimulating vesicles in the cytoplasm containing **GLUT** (glucose transporter) proteins to fuse with the membrane

* This allows more facilitated diffusion of glucose into the cell by increasing permeability

* Muscle cells and fat cells have GLUT4 type

* Brain cells GLUT1 and liver cells GLUT2 are always in the cell membrane, insulin has no effect on their distribution

2. It stimulates activation of the enzyme **glucokinase**

* This enzyme phosphorylates glucose so that it cannot leave the cell

3. It stimulates the activation of 2 enzymes, **phosphofructokinase** and **glycogen synthase**

* These enzymes convert glucose to glycogen (**glycogenesis**)

3. Increases rate of respiration of glucose

4. a-cells stop secreting glucagon when blood glucose is high, so glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis are inhibited (explained in action of glucagon)

### Action of Glucagon:

* Increases blood glucose

* Target cells are only liver cells

1. Glucagon binds to a receptor on the liver cell

2. The receptor changes shape, activating a **G-protein** inside the cell

3. The G-protein activates **adenylyl cyclase**, an enzyme in the cell membrane

4. Adenylyl cyclase converts ATP into **cyclic AMP** (**cAMP**), a second messenger that amplifies the signal

5. cAMP binds to and activates **protein kinase A** (**PKA**) enzymes in the cytoplasm

6. PKA phosphorylates (activates by adding a phosphate group) **phosphorylase kinase** enzymes

7. Phosphorylase kinase enzymes activate (phosphorylate) **glycogen phosphorylase**

8. Glycogen phosphorylase breaks down glycogen to glucose (**glycogenolysis**)

9. Glucose molecules diffuse out into the bloodstream through GLUT2 proteins

* Receptor → G-protein → Adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA → Phosphorylase kinase → Glycogen phosphorylase catalyzes glycogenolysis

* Glucagon also stimulates **gluconeogenesis**: formation of glucose from proteins and fats

* The hormone adrenaline also increases blood glucose concentration, by binding to different receptors on liver cells but activating the same enzyme cascade

  * It also stimulates glycogenolysis in muscle cells, but the glucose remains there to respire
