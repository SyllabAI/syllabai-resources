## Hardy-Weinberg Principle

- Used to calculate allele frequencies to determine whether evolution is occurring in a population

- Allele frequencies are constant in a population unless evolutionary forces act on it

- The frequency of a genotype is its proportion of the total population

- Equations:

$$p + q = 1$$

$$p^{2} + 2pq + q^{2} = 1$$

p = dominant allele frequency

q = recessive allele frequency

p² = homozygous dominant frequency

2pq = heterozygous frequency

q² = homozygous recessive frequency

* The total is the whole population (given as 1) and the frequencies are given as decimals

### Conditions:

1\.       Large population size

2\.       Random mating occurs

3\.       No migration

4\.       No selection pressures

5\.       No mutations

### Example calculations:

If 9% of the population has a recessive condition aa, find the frequency of the dominant allele A and heterozygous individuals Aa

* Frequency of aa:

  * q² = 9% = 0.09

* Recessive allele a frequency:

  * q = √0.09 = 0.3

* Dominant allele A frequency:

  * Find p using p + q = 1

  * p = 1 – q

  * p = 1 – 0.3 = 0.7

* Genotype Aa frequency:

  * 2pq = 2 (0.3) (0.7) = 0.42

  * So 42% of the population is heterozygous

* So, in this population, 9% are aa, 42% are Aa, 49% are AA, p = 0.7 and q = 0.3
