## Evolution

* Theory of evolution: the idea that organisms change over time

* New varieties and species are formed over time from pre-existing species due to changes in allele frequencies

* Natural selection is one mechanism by which evolution occurs

  * Overproduction, variation, competition, selection, inheritance

* Species are a group of organisms that:

  * Have the same **morphological** (relating to structure), **physiological** (relating to metabolism), biochemical, and behavioral features

  * Can interbreed to produce fertile offspring

  * Are reproductively isolated from other species

* For example, donkeys and horses are similar but when they interbreed, they produce **infertile** offspring (mules) so they are not part of the same species

### Allopatric speciation:

* This is speciation that occurs when organisms of the same species are physically separated by a geographical barrier

1. Physical barriers such as mountains or rivers divide a population into two or more **reproductively isolated** groups

* Reproductively isolated means the individuals cannot breed with each other, due to geographical barriers, refusal to mate etc.

2. Each isolated group experiences different selection pressures due to different environments

3. New alleles arise by different mutations, and natural selection favors different traits in each group

4. Over time, the groups become so different that even if the barrier is removed, they can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring, meaning they are now separate species

* Example: Darwin’s finches at the Galapagos islands

  * Different islands had different food (small seeds at one, big seeds with tough shells at another)

  * This led to variations in beak sizes, leading to the evolution of different species that each adapted to specific types of food

### Sympatric speciation:

* This is speciation that occurs within the same geographical area, due to **ecological or behavioral isolation**

1. Some individuals in a population use different behaviors or resources, preventing them from breeding with others

* e.g. seeds falling on different soil in the same area, or fish feeding at different depths (bottom vs. open water)

2. Over time, changes in allele frequency occur and different groups stop interbreeding

3. Genetic differences accumulate and different species form despite being in the same habitat

* Example: Palm trees in Lord Howe Island

  * The species used to grow in a place with both neutral and acidic soil, both within the same place

  * Some seeds germinated on the acidic soil which made their flowering times occur earlier so they could not breed with the trees on neutral soil

  * Therefore, they were reproductively and genetically isolated and they developed into 2 different species
