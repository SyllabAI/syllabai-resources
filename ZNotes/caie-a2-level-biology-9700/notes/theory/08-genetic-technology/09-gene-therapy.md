## Gene Therapy

* **Gene Therapy:** Treatment of a genetic disorder by inserting genetically corrected cells, or introducing functioning genes into affected cells

* Procedure:

1. Obtain normal, functional allele

2. Confirm the correct allele using gel electrophoresis/microarrays

3. Amplify DNA using PCR

4. Make recombinant DNA

* Cut DNA and vector with restriction enzyme to create complementary sticky ends

* DNA ligase is used to join the gene to the vector

* Add human promoter upstream of target allele to ensure transcription in host

5. Insert normal allele into vector

6. Inject/spray into host

### Gene therapy for Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID):

* Caused by a **recessive** mutation in the *ADA* gene on chromosome 20, which leads to a deficiency of the enzyme **adenosine deaminase** (**ADA**)

* The enzyme catalyzes the deamination of adenosine and deoxyadenosine

* Deoxyadenosine is toxic to T-lymphocytes, so children born without functioning ADA have a weakened immune system

* They experience recurrent, severe infections and poor growth, and are usually isolated in plastic bubbles

* When diagnosing, blood tests show low levels of immune cells, and genetic testing identifies mutations

* Treated by receiving regular injections of ADA, which is extracted from cattle or made by genetically modified bacteria

* Gene therapy: Some T-lymphocytes are removed from bone marrow, and normal alleles are introduced using a vector, before replacing the cells

* The **adeno-associated virus** (**AAV**) is used as a vector

* Retroviruses were previously used but they caused leukemia as a side effect

### Gene therapy for Leber Congenital Amaurosis (LCA):

* Rare form of inherited blindness, causing retinal cells to die off at an early age

* Caused by mutation in the gene *RPE65*

* *RPE65* codes for a protein that regenerates visual pigment in rod and cone cells in the retina after they have been exposed to light

* *RPE65* is a dominant gene, and the mutated one is **recessive**

* Gene therapy includes delivering a functional copy of the gene to the retinal cells using AAV vectors injected directly into the retina

* The eye is considered a good organ for developing gene therapies because:

  * It’s small and easy to target

  * Genes can be delivered precisely to the retina

  * There is also little activity of immune cells in the eye so low risk of harmful immune response
