## Genetically Modified Plants and Animals

### Herbicide-resistant crops:

* Weeds compete with a crop for space, light, water and nutrients from the soil

* Herbicide resistance allows farmers to spray herbicides to kill weeds while a crop is growing

* The herbicide **glyphosate** inhibits an enzyme involved in the synthesis of 3 amino acids: phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan

  * These amino acids are needed to produce proteins essential for growth

* It is absorbed by a plant’s leaves and transported to the growing tips, inhibiting the production of proteins in these growth areas so the plant dies

* The bacterium *Agrobacterium* has a gene coding for a version of the enzyme that is resistant to glyphosate

  * This gene is transferred into crop plants, providing herbicide resistance

**Disadvantages:**

1. High cost of genetically modified seeds

2. GM plants become an agricultural weed in fields of other crops

3. Pollen transfers the resistance gene to wild relatives of the crop plant, producing hybrid offspring that are invasive weeds

4. If too much of the same herbicide is used, weeds become herbicide-resistant too

### Insect-resistant crops:

* **Bt toxin**, an insecticidal toxin produced by the bacterium *Bacillus thuringiensis*, kills insects that eat it (but is harmless to other animals)

* The gene for Bt toxin is transferred to crop plants to make them insect-resistant

* Different strains of the bacterium produce different toxins that can be used against different insect species

* Maize is protected against the **corn borer** (a caterpillar) which eats leaves and burrows into the stalk, eating its way upwards

* Cotton (*Gossypium hirsutum*) is protected against the **boll worm** which feeds on cotton balls

### GM Atlantic Salmon

* Grows to market weight in half the usual time

* A growth hormone regulating gene from a Pacific Chinook salmon and a promoter from a different fish species (ocean pout) were injected into a fertilised egg of an Atlantic salmon

* By producing growth hormone throughout the year, the modified salmon are able to grow all year instead of just spring & summer

### Ethical and social implications of using GMOs in food production:

1. Modified crop plants may become weeds or invade natural habitats

2. Introduced genes may be transferred by pollen to wild relatives, whose hybrid offspring may become more invasive

3. Introduced genes may also be transferred by pollen to unmodified plants growing on farms that grow organic crops

4. Modified plants may be a hazard to humans or animals by being toxic or causing allergic reactions

5. The herbicides that can now be used, leave toxic residues in the crop which may harm consumers

6. GM seeds are expensive, and their cost may remove any advantage of growing a resistant crop

7. GM seeds need to be bought each season unlike non-GM seeds, so costs are high

8. There is a danger of losing traditional varieties

9. People may avoid or refuse to buy GM food
