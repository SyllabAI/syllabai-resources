## Ethical Guidelines

### Ethical guidelines

Ethical guidelines are general rules that researchers need to follow to protect the rights and wellbeing of participants.

### Consent

**Consent** is the voluntary agreement to participate in research.

Participants should freely agree to take part rather than being forced to participate.

### Informed consent

**Informed consent** means participants agree to take part after being given the relevant information about the research.

* Participants should understand what participation involves

* Participation should be voluntary

**Example:**

* Participants are told what they will be asked to do before deciding whether to take part.

### Debriefing

**Debriefing** involves providing participants with full information about the research once data has been collected.

It is particularly important when deception has been used.

* Explains the true purpose of the research

* Explains any deception that was used

* Helps reduce any negative effects of deception

**Example:**

* Participants are told after an experiment that the researcher gave them incomplete information about the true aim of the study.

### Confidentiality

**Confidentiality** means protecting participants’ personal information by keeping their data anonymous.

* Participants’ names should not be revealed unnecessarily

* Personal information should be protected

**Example:**

* A researcher uses participant numbers instead of names when reporting the results.

### Avoiding deception

* **Deception** means intentionally giving participants wrong or incomplete information about the research.

* Researchers should avoid deception.

**Example:**

* A researcher tells participants that a study is about memory when the real aim is to investigate conformity.

* This is deception because participants have been given incomplete information about the true purpose.

### Right to withdraw

The **right to withdraw** means participants are aware that they can leave the research at any time.

* Participants should not be forced to continue

* They should know that they can stop participating

**Example:**

* A participant decides they no longer want to continue with an experiment and is allowed to leave.

### Privacy

**Privacy** means respecting participants’ personal space and refraining from making them reveal unnecessary personal details.

**Example:**

A researcher should not ask participants to provide personal information that is not necessary for the study.

### Minimising harm

**Minimising harm** means ensuring participants are not exposed to greater physical or psychological risk than they would normally experience in everyday life.

* Researchers should avoid unnecessary physical harm

* Researchers should avoid unnecessary psychological harm

**Example:**

* A researcher avoids exposing participants to unnecessary distress during an experiment.

### Maximising benefit

* Researchers should aim to maximise the benefits of the research while minimising possible harm.

**Example:**

* A study should have a useful purpose that justifies asking participants to take part.

### Application to novel scenarios

**Example:**

* Participants are not told the true purpose of the study and are not informed afterwards.

  * This raises issues with **deception** and **debriefing**.

**Example:**

* Participants are told they cannot leave the study once it begins.

  * This violates the **right to withdraw**.

**Example:**

* A researcher publishes participants’ names alongside their responses.

  * This violates **confidentiality**.

**Example:**

* Participants experience unnecessary psychological distress.

  * This raises an issue with **minimising harm**.
