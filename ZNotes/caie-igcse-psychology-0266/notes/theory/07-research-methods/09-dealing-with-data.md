## Dealing with Data

### Measures of central tendency

**Measures of central tendency** are values that show a typical score in a set of data.

The three measures are:

* Mean

* Median

* Mode

### Mean

The **mean** is calculated by adding all the scores and dividing by the number of scores.

**Example:**

* Scores: $4, 6, 8$

  * $\text{Mean} = \frac{4 + 6 + 8}{ 3} = 6$

### Median

The **median** is the value that appears in the middle of a set of data when the scores are arranged from smallest to largest.

**Example:**

* $3, 5, 7, 9, 11$

  * $\text{Median} = 7$

### Mode

The **mode** is the value that appears most frequently in a set of data.

**Example:**

* $2, 3, 3, 4, 5$

  * $\text{Mode} = 3$

### Selecting a measure of central tendency

The researcher should select the measure that is most appropriate for the data.

* Mean can be affected by extreme scores

* Median is less affected by extreme scores

* Mode shows the most frequently occurring score

**Example:**

* For the scores $2, 3, 3, 4, 20,$ the extreme score of $20$ would increase the mean substantially. The median may therefore provide a better indication of a typical score.

### Measure of spread

A measure of spread shows how similar or different values are in a set of data.

The syllabus requires knowledge of the range.

### Range

The **range** is the difference between the highest value and the lowest value in a set of data.

**Example:**

* $\text{Scores}: 4, 6, 7, 10, 12$

  * $\text{Range} = 12 − 4 = 8$

  * A larger range means the scores are more spread out.

  * A smaller range means the scores are more similar.

### Bar charts

A **bar chart** is used to display categorical data.

Bar charts should include:

* Axes

* Labels

* Equal-width bars

* Units where appropriate

* Spaces between bars

The bars should not touch each other.

**Example:**

A researcher compares students’ preferred study methods.

The categories could be:

* Flashcards

* Practice questions

* Reading notes

* Group study

Each category is represented by a separate bar.

### Application of bar charts

When interpreting a bar chart:

* Identify what each axis represents

* Check the labels and units

* Compare the heights of the bars

* Identify the highest and lowest values

* Identify any patterns in the data

**Example:**

* If the bar for practice questions is the highest, this means practice questions were the most frequently selected study method in the data.

### **Reaching a conclusion**

* A conclusion is an interpretation of the patterns in the data used to make a reasoned decision about whether the results support the research aim.

* When reaching a conclusion, the researcher should identify the pattern in the data and relate it directly to the aim.

**Example:**

**Aim:**

* To investigate whether background music affects memory.

  * If the group exposed to music has a higher average memory score than the group without music, the researcher could conclude that the results support the research aim by suggesting that background music affected memory performance.

**Results:**

* The mean memory score was higher in the no-music condition than in the music condition.

**Conclusion:**

* The results suggest that background music may negatively affect memory because participants had higher memory scores when no music was played.

### **Reaching conclusions from bar charts**

When interpreting a bar chart, the researcher should:

* Identify what the x-axis represents

* Identify what the y-axis represents

* Compare the heights of the bars

* Identify any clear patterns or differences

* Use the data to answer the research aim

The conclusion should be based on the pattern shown by the bars, rather than simply describing the chart.

**Example:**

* A bar chart compares memory scores for participants who studied in silence and participants who studied with music.

* If the bar for the silence condition is higher, this indicates that the silence group had higher memory scores.

* The researcher could conclude that the results support the prediction that studying in silence improves memory performance, **if this was the research aim**.
