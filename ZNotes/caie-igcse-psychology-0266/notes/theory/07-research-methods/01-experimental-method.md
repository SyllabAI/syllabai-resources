# Research Methods

## Experimental Method

### What is an experiment?

* An **experiment** is a method of investigating the effect of one variable on another variable under controlled conditions.

* The researcher manipulates the independent variable (IV) and measures the effect this has on the dependent variable (DV).

* The purpose of an experiment is to investigate a possible cause-and-effect relationship.

**Basic idea:**

IV is manipulated → DV is measured → researcher investigates whether the IV caused changes in the DV

**Example:**

A researcher investigates whether background music affects memory.

* IV = whether background music is played

* DV = memory test score

* The researcher changes the music condition and measures participants' memory performance.

### Independent Variable (IV)

The **independent variable** is the variable that is **manipulated by the researcher**.

It has two or more **conditions or levels**.

The researcher changes the IV to investigate whether it causes a change in the DV.

### Dependent Variable (DV)

* The **dependent variable** is the variable that is **measured by the researcher**.

* It is the outcome that is expected to change as a result of manipulating the IV.

The DV can be measured using:

* Experimental tests or tasks

* Observations

* Questionnaires

* Interviews

### Operationalisation

* **Operationalisation** is the process of clearly defining a variable or behavioural category so that it can be accurately measured or observed.

* This makes the variables precise rather than vague.

* The IV is operationalised by clearly defining how it will be manipulated.

* The DV is operationalised by clearly defining how it will be measured.

Operationalisation is important because it:

* Makes variables clear and specific

* Allows accurate measurement or observation

* Makes the procedure easier to replicate

* Reduces ambiguity about what the researcher actually did

**Example:**

Instead of saying:

“Participants will receive lots of sleep.”

Operationalised:

“Participants will sleep for **8 hours** before completing the memory test.”

Instead of saying:

“Memory will be measured.”

Operationalised:

“Memory will be measured by the number of words correctly recalled from a 20-word list.”

### Experimental conditions

**Experimental conditions** are the different levels of the independent variable that are manipulated in an experiment.

Different conditions allow the researcher to compare participants' responses under different levels of the IV.

**Example:**

A researcher investigates whether background music affects memory.

* Condition 1 = music

* Condition 2 = no music

These are the two experimental conditions because they represent different levels of the IV.

### Experimental groups

**Experimental groups** are participants who experience the experimental condition(s).

They are exposed to the manipulation of the IV.

**Example:**

If the IV is whether students receive a reward:

* Experimental group = students who receive the reward.

### Control groups

A **control group** consists of participants who do not experience the experimental condition and are used as a comparison group.

The control group helps the researcher determine whether changes in the DV are associated with the IV.

**Example:**

A researcher investigates whether a new revision technique improves test scores.

* Experimental group = uses the new revision technique

* Control group = does not use the new revision technique

The researcher can then compare their test scores.

### Experimental designs

An **experimental design** is a way of assigning participants to the different conditions or levels of an experiment.

The syllabus requires knowledge of:

* Independent measures design

* Repeated measures design

### Independent measures design

An independent measures design uses different participants in each condition of the experiment.

Each participant experiences only one level of the IV.

**Example:**

A researcher investigates whether music affects memory.

* Group 1 → music condition

* Group 2 → no-music condition

Different participants are used in each group.

**Random allocation**

* Random allocation means assigning participants to different conditions so that each participant has an equal chance of being assigned to any particular condition.

* Random allocation helps reduce the possibility that participant characteristics systematically differ between groups.

**Example:**

* The researcher randomly assigns participants to either the music or no-music condition rather than allowing participants to choose their group.

### Repeated measures design

* A repeated measures design uses the same participants in every condition of the experiment.

* Therefore, every participant experiences **all levels of the IV**.

**Example:**

A researcher investigates whether music affects memory.

The same participants:

* Complete a memory test with music

* Complete a memory test without music

This allows the researcher to compare each participant with themselves.

### Counterbalancing

* Counterbalancing involves changing the order in which participants experience the different conditions.

* It is used in repeated measures designs to reduce the effects of order.

**Example:**

Half of the participants:

Music → No music

The other half:

No music → Music

This helps reduce the possibility that completing the first condition affects performance in the second condition.

### **Extraneous and confounding variables**

* An extraneous variable is a variable, other than the independent variable (IV), that can have an impact on the dependent variable (DV).

* A confounding variable is an extraneous variable that systematically varies with the IV, meaning it provides an alternative explanation for changes in the DV.

**Example:**

* A researcher investigates whether background music affects memory.

  * IV → background music condition.

  * DV → memory test score.

  * An extraneous variable could be the amount of sleep participants had.

  * If one condition is tested only in the morning and the other only in the afternoon, **time of day could become a confounding variable** because it systematically differs between the conditions.

**Control**

* Control refers to steps taken by the researcher to **reduce or keep the impact of extraneous variables constant**.

Examples include:

* Keeping instructions the same

* Keeping the testing environment the same

* Using the same materials

* Standardising the procedure

* Randomly allocating participants where appropriate

Good control helps the researcher determine whether changes in the DV are actually caused by the IV.

**Why extraneous and confounding variables matter**

* Extraneous variables can make it more difficult to determine whether the IV caused changes in the DV.

* A confounding variable is more serious because it systematically varies with the IV and therefore provides an alternative explanation for the results.

* Therefore, researchers use controls to reduce the influence of extraneous variables and prevent them from becoming confounding variables.

**Exam application:**

When given a scenario, identify:

* IV → what is manipulated

* DV → what is measured

* Extraneous variable → another factor that could affect the DV

* Confounding variable → an extraneous variable that systematically varies with the IV

* Control → what the researcher could do to reduce its influence
