## Non-experimental Method: Questionnaires and Interviews

### Questionnaire

A **questionnaire** is a method of collecting data by asking written questions to participants.

Questionnaires can be used to collect information about:

* Thoughts

* Feelings

* Attitudes

* Opinions

* Behaviours

**Example:**

* A researcher investigates students’ stress levels using a questionnaire containing written questions about how often students experience stress.

### Interview

An **interview** is a method of collecting data by asking verbal question**s** to participants.

The researcher asks questions and records the participants’ responses.

**Example:**

* A researcher investigates students’ experiences of exam stress by verbally asking participants questions about their experiences.

### Open questions

**Open questions** require detailed answers in participants’ own words.

* No answer choices are provided

* Participants can give their own response

* Allows participants to provide detailed information

**Example:**

“What do you think causes stress among university students?”

The participant answers in their own words.

### Closed questions

**Closed questions** have a **fixed set of possible responses**.

* Participants choose from the responses provided

* Responses are limited to the available options

**Example:**

“How often do you feel stressed?”

Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Always

### Application to novel scenarios

**Written questions → Questionnaire**

**Verbal questions → Interview**

**Own words with no fixed responses → Open question**

**Fixed set of responses → Closed question**

**Example:**

* A researcher asks: “Describe your experience of exam stress.” → **Open question**

* A researcher asks: “How often do you experience exam stress?” Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Always → **Closed question**
