## Role of the Researcher, Materials and Procedure

### **The Research Process**

The research process involves several stages.

**Setting an aim**

* The researcher first establishes an aim, which states the purpose of the research.

**Designing the procedure**

The researcher decides how the research will be conducted, including:

* Organising experimental conditions

* Organising the observational setting

* Deciding how participants will be allocated to conditions

* Establishing controls

* Deciding whether a stooge (confederate) is required

**Organising experimental conditions**

* The researcher decides how participants will experience each condition of the IV and keeps relevant aspects of the conditions consistent apart from the IV.

**Example:**

A researcher investigates whether music affects memory:

* Music → memory test with music

* No music → memory test without music

* The same memory test, instructions and testing time should be used in both conditions.

**Organising the observational setting**

* The researcher decides where and when the observation will take place, how long it will last, where the observer will be positioned and how behaviour will be recorded.

**Example:**

* To investigate classroom participation, a researcher could observe the same type of lesson for the same length of time from the same position, using the same recording system.

A stooge (confederate) is a person who plays a role in the research without participants knowing they are part of the research procedure.

**Controls**

Controls are procedures used to reduce the influence of variables other than the IV.

Researchers may:

* Keep instructions and materials the same

* Use the same testing environment

* Keep time limits consistent

* Standardise the procedure

Example:

* When investigating whether music affects memory, the researcher could use the same memory test, instructions, time limit and testing environment for all participants.

* Good control makes the conditions more comparable and helps determine whether changes in the DV are due to the IV.

**Choosing materials**

The researcher chooses materials appropriate to the research method.

These may include:

* Stimuli, such as pictures, sounds, words or objects presented to participants

* Tests or tasks

* Behavioural categories for observations

* Questionnaire questions

* Interview questions

The researcher should ensure that the materials directly relate to the research aim and are presented consistently to participants.

**Considering validity and reliability**

* The researcher considers whether the procedure measures what it intends to measure and whether the procedure or measures are consistent.

**Implementing ethical guidelines**

* The researcher must protect participants' rights and wellbeing throughout the study.

**Briefing participants**

* Participants are given appropriate instructions and information before the research begins.

### Conducting the research and collecting data

Data may be collected using:

* Experimental tests or tasks

* Observations

* Questionnaires

* Interviews

* Case studies

**Analysing data**

* The researcher analyses the results and uses the patterns in the data to reach a conclusion about whether the findings support the research aim.

**Standardisation**

Standardisation means keeping relevant parts of the procedure the same for all participants.

This can include:

* Using the same instructions

* Using the same materials

* Using the same testing conditions

* Using the same time limits

* Following the same procedure

Standardisation helps reduce the influence of extraneous variables and can improve the reliability of the research.
