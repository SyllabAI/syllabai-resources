# Motivation and Needs

## Motivation and Needs- Key Concepts

### Motivation

Motivation is the psychological force that directs behaviour towards certain goals.

In other words, motivation explains why we behave in a particular way and what drives us towards achieving a goal.

**For example:**

* A student studies because they want to achieve a high grade.

* A person exercises because they enjoy it.

* A football player trains because they want to improve their performance.

Motivation can come from **inside the person** or from **external rewards**.

### Types of Motivation

### Extrinsic motivation

Extrinsic motivation is motivation caused by **external benefits or rewards**.

The behaviour is performed because of an outcome that comes from outside the person, rather than because the activity itself is enjoyable.

**Examples:**

* Studying to get a good grade.

* Training to win a trophy.

* Working to earn money.

* Completing homework to receive a reward.

### Intrinsic motivation

Intrinsic motivation is motivation caused by **personal satisfaction, enjoyment or interest**.

The behaviour is performed because the activity itself is rewarding.

**Examples:**

* Studying a subject because you genuinely enjoy learning about it.

* Playing a sport because you find it enjoyable.

* Helping someone because it feels satisfying to help.

* Practising an instrument because you enjoy improving your skills.

**Important:** A person can experience both types at the same time. For example, a student might enjoy studying psychology but also want a high grade.

### Need

A need is a physiological or psychological requirement for survival and wellbeing.

Needs can be physical or psychological.

Physiological needs are requirements related to the body's functioning and survival.

**Examples:**

* Food

* Water

* Sleep

* Maintaining a suitable body temperature

Psychological needs are requirements that contribute to psychological wellbeing.

**Examples:**

* Belonging

* Love

* Feeling competent

* Self-esteem

Needs can create motivation because when a need is not satisfied, it can encourage behaviour aimed at satisfying that need.

**Example:**

* If someone is hungry, their physiological need for food can motivate them to find and eat food.

### Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Maslow proposed that human motivation is driven by a hierarchy of needs.

He argued that people are motivated to satisfy lower-level needs before moving on to higher-level needs.

The hierarchy consists of:

* **Deficiency needs**

  * Physiological

  * Safety

  * Love/Belonging

  * Esteem

* **Growth need**

  * Self-actualisation

### Deficiency needs

Deficiency needs arise when something necessary for wellbeing is lacking. Motivation is directed towards reducing that deficiency.

### Growth Need

**Self-actualisation**

* Self-actualisation is the highest level of Maslow's hierarchy.

* It refers to achieving one's full potential and becoming the best version of oneself.

* Unlike deficiency needs, it is not motivated by a lack of something. Instead, it focuses on personal growth, fulfilment and self-development.

**Examples:**

* Pursuing personal goals

* Developing talents

* Creativity

* Helping others

* Achieving meaningful ambitions

**Example:**

* An athlete may continue training to reach their highest potential even when basic needs have already been met.

### Exam tip

* Maslow argued that lower needs generally take priority over higher needs.

  * **Physiological → Safety → Love/Belonging → Esteem → Self-actualisation**

  * A person struggling to obtain food or shelter is unlikely to focus on self-actualisation until those more basic needs have been satisfied.
