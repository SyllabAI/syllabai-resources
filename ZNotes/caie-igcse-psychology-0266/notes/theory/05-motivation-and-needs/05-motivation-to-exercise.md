## Motivation to Exercise

### Deci and Ryan’s self-determination theory

Deci and Ryan’s self-determination theory (SDT) explains motivation in terms of three basic psychological needs:

* Competence

* Relatedness

* Autonomy

The theory proposes that when these three needs are satisfied, people are more likely to experience self-determined motivation, particularly intrinsic motivation.

### Competence

* Competence is the need to feel capable of performing tasks successfully.

* A person is more likely to feel motivated when they believe they can improve their skills and achieve their goals.

**Example:**
A beginner at the gym learns how to perform an exercise correctly and notices their skills improving. Feeling capable can increase their motivation to continue exercising.

If someone constantly feels unsuccessful or incapable, their sense of competence may be reduced, which can decrease motivation.

### Relatedness

* Relatedness is the need to feel connected to other people and to belong to a community.

* People may be more motivated to participate in activities when they feel socially connected and supported.

**Example:**
Someone may continue attending a sports club because they have developed friendships with other members and feel that they belong to the team.

### Autonomy

* Autonomy is the need to feel in control and able to make one's own choices.

* Autonomy does not mean doing everything independently. It means experiencing a sense of choice and control over one's behaviour.

**Example:**
A person who chooses whether to swim, run or cycle may feel more autonomous than someone who is forced to complete a particular exercise.

### The three needs together

The three needs can work together to support motivation.

**Key idea:**
Greater satisfaction of competence, relatedness and autonomy can support more self-determined motivation.

### Self-determination theory and exercise

Self-determination theory can explain why people continue participating in exercise even when there is no obvious external reward.

For example, someone may exercise because:

* They enjoy the activity → **intrinsic motivation**

* They feel capable of improving → **competence**

* They enjoy spending time with teammates → **relatedness**

* They choose the activity themselves → **autonomy**

When exercise satisfies these psychological needs, participation may be more likely to continue.

### Intrinsic motivation

* Intrinsic motivation occurs when a person participates because the activity itself is enjoyable or personally satisfying.

**Example:**
Someone plays football because they genuinely enjoy playing and being active.

The motivation comes from **within the individual**, rather than from an external reward.

### Extrinsic motivation

* Extrinsic motivation occurs when behaviour is motivated by an external benefit or consequence.

Examples include:

* Receiving a prize

* Winning a competition

* Earning money

* Receiving recognition

* Avoiding an unwanted consequence

**Example:**
A person exercises because they want to win a medal.

### Implications of intrinsic and extrinsic motivation for exercise

* Self-determination theory suggests that the type of motivation can affect persistence.

* Someone who exercises because they genuinely enjoy the activity may be more likely to continue even when there is no external reward.

* Someone who exercises primarily because of an external reward may stop when the reward is removed.

* However, extrinsic motivation is not necessarily ineffective. External rewards can still encourage behaviour, particularly when they support a person's sense of competence or other psychological needs.

### Sports persistence: Calvo et al.

Calvo et al. investigated whether autonomy, competence and relatedness were associated with persistence in sport. Players who continued playing had higher relatedness and autonomy, while competence did not predict whether they persisted.

**Aim:**
To investigate how feelings of autonomy, competence and relatedness are linked to persistence in sport.

**Participants:**
492 male football players aged 13–17 years from a football training programme in Spain.

**Procedure:**
Participants completed questionnaires before their scheduled football practice.

The questionnaires measured self-determined motivation, including whether their motivation was intrinsic or extrinsic.

Participants also rated:

* **Relatedness**- For example, whether some of their best friends were on the team

* **Competence**- Their perceived ability to play football

* **Autonomy**- Whether they felt able to make decisions during training and competition

Participants, their parents/guardians and coaches provided consent.

One year later, the football programme provided information showing which participants were still playing football (persisters) and which had left the programme (dropouts).

### Results

The persisters:

* Had lower levels of external motivation

* Had higher relatedness

* Had higher autonomy

The dropouts:

* Had higher external motivation

* Had lower satisfaction of relatedness

* Had lower satisfaction of autonomy

Importantly, **competence did not predict** whether participants persisted or dropped out.

### Conclusion

* The findings suggest that relatedness and autonomy are associated with persistence in sport.

* The study therefore provides support for self-determination theory in explaining motivation to exercise, although competence was not found to predict persistence in this study.

### Argument for self-determination theory

* Calvo et al. provide support for the theory because participants who continued playing football had greater satisfaction of relatedness and autonomy than those who dropped out.

* This supports the idea that satisfying psychological needs can be associated with continued participation in sport.

### Argument against self-determination theory

* Exercise behaviour is not determined only by psychological motivation.

* There can also be environmental barriers that prevent people from exercising, even when they are motivated.

Examples include:

* Lack of time

* Health issues

* Social limitations

* Lack of access to suitable facilities

Therefore, someone may have high autonomy, competence and relatedness but still find it difficult to exercise because of circumstances outside their control.

### Application to novel scenarios

When applying self-determination theory to a scenario:

1. Identify whether the scenario involves competence, relatedness or autonomy.

2. Explain how that need is being satisfied or frustrated.

3. Identify whether motivation is primarily **intrinsic or extrinsic**.

4. Link this to the person's likelihood of continuing to exercise.

**Example:**
A student joins a football team because they enjoy playing, feel increasingly skilled and have made close friends. Their motivation is likely to be more self-determined because the activity satisfies competence and relatedness, while enjoyment reflects intrinsic motivation.

**Application example involving a barrier:**
A student enjoys running and feels competent and autonomous, but stops exercising because they have no time due to other commitments. This demonstrates that environmental factors can affect exercise behaviour even when psychological needs are satisfied.
