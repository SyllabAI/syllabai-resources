## Psychodynamic Motivation and Marketing

### The psychodynamic approach to motivation

* The psychodynamic approach suggests that behaviour can be influenced by mental processes that are outside conscious awareness.

* In marketing, this means that people may be influenced by motives and desires that they do not fully recognise. Advertisements can therefore appeal to deeper psychological needs, emotions and associations rather than simply providing information about a product.

The unconscious is particularly important to psychodynamic explanations of marketing because advertisements may appeal to desires and associations that consumers are not consciously aware of.

### Structure of personality

Psychodynamic theory also proposes three parts of personality:

* Id

* Ego

* Super-ego

### Id

* The id represents basic desires and wants. It seeks immediate satisfaction.

* For this syllabus, the id should be understood in an age-appropriate way rather than through sexual or violent impulses.

**Marketing example:**

* An advertisement may encourage someone to want a product immediately because it looks enjoyable or satisfying.

### Ego

* The ego helps a person deal with reality and make decisions about how to satisfy desires in realistic ways.

**Marketing example:**

* A person may want an expensive product but consider whether they can realistically afford it before purchasing it.

### Super-ego

* The super-ego represents internalised rules, values and ideas about what is right or acceptable.

**Marketing example:**

* An advertisement that emphasises morality or ethical behaviour may appeal to a person's values and sense of what is right.

### Psychodynamic motivation in marketing

* Psychodynamic motivation can help explain why consumers may respond to products or brands for reasons beyond their practical usefulness.

Marketing can appeal to:

* Unconscious desires

* Identification

* Symbolic meaning

* Defence mechanisms

* Nostalgia

* Morality

### Unconscious desires and identification

* Consumers may have unconscious desires that influence their preferences.

* Advertising can connect a product with desirable feelings, identities or lifestyles.

* Identification occurs when a person connects themselves with a person, group or image represented by a product or advertisement.

**Example:**

* An advertisement may present a brand as representing confidence, success or belonging. A consumer may identify with this image and therefore develop a more positive attitude towards the brand.

### Products and brands with symbolic meaning

* Products can have symbolic meaning, meaning they represent something beyond their practical function.

**For example, a product may represent:**

* Success

* Belonging

* Independence

* Status

* A particular identity

Therefore, someone may want a product partly because of what it **symbolises**, rather than simply because of what it does.

### Defence mechanisms

* Defence mechanisms are psychological processes that can help people deal with thoughts or feelings that are difficult to accept.

### Regression

* Regression involves returning to an earlier stage of development in terms of thoughts, emotions or behaviours.

  * In marketing, an advertisement may encourage consumers to return emotionally to an earlier period of their lives, such as childhood.

  * This can be particularly relevant to **nostalgia**.

### Rationalisation

* Rationalisation involves justifying undesirable thoughts or behaviours using logical explanations so they appear more acceptable.

**Example:**

* A person may buy something they did not really need and then justify the purchase by saying, “I bought it because it was on sale.”

* The person's explanation makes the behaviour seem more reasonable to themselves.

### Advertising appeals

* Psychodynamic marketing can use emotional appeals that connect products with deeper motives or memories.

Two syllabus examples are:

**Nostalgia:**
An emotional longing for the past. An advertisement may remind consumers of positive past experiences and associate those feelings with a product or brand.

**Morality:**
An advertisement may appeal to a person's values and sense of right and wrong.

**Example:**
An advertisement might associate a product with helping others or making an ethical choice, appealing to the consumer's moral values.

### Nostalgic advertising: Merchant et al., study 3 only

Merchant et al. investigated whether nostalgic advertising could influence consumers’ attitudes and behavioural intentions. Stronger feelings of nostalgia were associated with more positive attitudes towards the products and brands and stronger behavioural intentions.

**Aim:**
To investigate whether appeals to nostalgia in advertising could influence behavioural intentions.

**Participants:**
145 adult participants from an online consumer panel, including males and females.

**Procedure:**
Participants were exposed to one of three print advertisements for products or brands designed to create feelings of nostalgia.

The advertisements were for:

* An amusement park

* A food item

* A television channel

* After viewing the advertisement, participants completed a nostalgia scale. They rated how strongly they agreed with statements indicating nostalgic feelings, such as whether they felt that they had relived an event from their past.

* Participants also rated the positive emotions they experienced, such as calmness, and negative emotions, such as sadness.

Finally, they rated:

* Their attitudes towards the advertisement

* Their attitudes towards the brand

* How likely they were to purchase the product

### Results

Advertising that created stronger feelings of nostalgia predicted:

* Stronger attitudes towards the products and brands

* Stronger behavioural intentions towards the products and brands

However, negative emotions produced by the nostalgic advertisements had a negative effect on attitudes towards the advertisement.

### Conclusion

* Feelings of nostalgia caused by advertising can influence people's choices of products and brands.

* The study supports the idea that advertising can influence behaviour by appealing to unconscious desires and emotional longing for the past.

### Argument for psychodynamic motivation in marketing

* Merchant et al. provide support for the role of psychodynamic motivation in marketing.

* The study found that stronger nostalgic feelings were associated with stronger attitudes and behavioural intentions towards the advertised products and brands.

* This suggests that advertising can influence consumers by appealing to emotional and psychological motives, rather than only providing factual information about a product.

### Argument against psychodynamic motivation in marketing

* A limitation is that unconscious motives are difficult to observe and measure.

* Researchers cannot directly observe an unconscious desire in the same way they can observe a person's behaviour. This makes it difficult to establish exactly whether an unconscious motive caused a consumer's response.

Therefore, psychodynamic explanations of marketing can be difficult to test objectively.

### Application to novel scenarios

When applying psychodynamic motivation to a scenario, identify:

1. The unconscious desire or motive

2. How the advertisement connects the product to that motive

3. Whether the product has symbolic meaning

4. Any relevant defence mechanism or advertising appeal

5. How this could influence the consumer's attitudes or behavioural intentions

**Example:**

* An advertisement for a food brand shows a person remembering happy family meals from childhood. This uses nostalgia because it creates longing for positive past experiences and associates those feelings with the brand, potentially increasing positive attitudes and intentions to purchase.
