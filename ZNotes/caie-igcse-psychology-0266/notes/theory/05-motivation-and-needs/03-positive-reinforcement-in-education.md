## Positive Reinforcement in Education

### Positive reinforcement

* Positive reinforcement is adding a pleasant stimulus after a behaviour to increase the chance that the behaviour will happen again.

The key idea is:

**Behaviour → positive consequence → behaviour becomes more likely**

**Example:** 

* If a student answers a question correctly and receives praise, they may be more likely to participate again because the praise acts as a positive reinforcer.

**Important:** Positive reinforcement is not simply “rewarding someone.” The consequence must increase the likelihood of the behaviour occurring again.

### External rewards

External rewards are **rewards provided from outside the individual** following a behaviour.

Examples include:

* Tokens

* Points

* Prizes

* Praise

* Good grades

* Privileges

In education, external rewards can motivate learners to perform behaviours such as:

* Answering questions

* Completing work

* Participating in class

* Following classroom expectations

However, external rewards may not be equally effective for every learner because individual differences and environmental factors can influence how motivating a reward is.

### Shaping behaviour

* Shaping is the process of gradually developing a desired behaviour by reinforcing behaviours that increasingly resemble the target behaviour.

* Instead of expecting the learner to immediately perform the complete behaviour correctly, smaller steps towards the desired behaviour can be reinforced.

**Example:**

A student rarely participates in class.

→ Teacher first praises the student for attempting an answer
→ Then praises the student for raising their hand
→ Eventually reinforces regularly giving appropriate answers

The desired behaviour is gradually strengthened through reinforcement.

### Token economies

* A token economy is a system in which learners receive tokens or points after performing desired behaviours.

* The tokens can later be exchanged for a reward.

The process is:

**Desired behaviour → token → later exchange for reward**

* For example, a student might receive a point for completing their work. After collecting enough points, they might exchange them for a privilege.

* Token economies are based on the principle that a consequence following behaviour can increase the likelihood of that behaviour occurring again.

### Classroom token economy: Boniecki and Moore

Boniecki and Moore investigated whether giving students tokens as rewards would increase classroom participation. Participation increased when tokens were introduced and returned to approximately baseline when the tokens were removed.

### **Aim:**

* To investigate the effectiveness of using a token economy to increase student participation in the classroom.

### **Participants:**

* 63 students from a psychology class at the same university in the USA.

### **Procedure:**

* The study took place across 11 class meetings.

* During each meeting, the teacher asked students questions. Students who wanted to answer raised their hands. The teacher called on students in the order in which they raised their hands until someone answered correctly. If nobody raised a hand, the teacher provided the answer.

The study had three stages:

During the token economy, each token could be exchanged at the end of the meeting for one point towards the student's next exam result.

A stooge/confederate sat in the last row pretending to be a student. The stooge recorded:

* How many students raised their hands

* How quickly students raised their hands

**Results:**

* Student participation doubled during the token economy compared with baseline.

* Students responded more quickly to questions during the token economy.

* When the token economy was removed, participation returned to approximately the baseline level.

**Conclusion:**

* The token economy was effective in increasing classroom participation. This supports the idea that positive reinforcement can increase motivation in education.

### Strength of using positive reinforcement

* Boniecki and Moore provide evidence that positive reinforcement can increase participation.

* The students participated more frequently and responded more quickly when tokens were available. When the tokens were removed, participation returned to baseline levels.

* This suggests that the external reward, rather than simply the passage of time, was responsible for the increase in participation.

### Limitation of using positive reinforcement

* Positive reinforcement is not the only factor affecting learners' motivation and behaviour.

* Individual factors such as interests, personality and existing motivation can influence how a learner responds to rewards. Environmental factors such as classroom relationships, teaching style and the learning environment can also affect participation.

* Therefore, the same reward may not motivate every learner in the same way.

### Application to novel scenarios

When applying positive reinforcement to a scenario:

1. Identify the desired behaviour.

2. Identify the pleasant consequence/reward that follows it.

3. Explain that the consequence is intended to increase the likelihood of the behaviour happening again.

4. If relevant, explain how the reward could be used to shape behaviour gradually.

**Example:**

* A teacher gives students a point every time they contribute an appropriate answer. If participation increases, the points are functioning as **positive reinforcement** because they are a pleasant consequence that increases the likelihood of participation.
