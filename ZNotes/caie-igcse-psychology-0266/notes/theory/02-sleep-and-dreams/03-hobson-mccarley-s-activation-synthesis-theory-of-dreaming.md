### Activation-Synthesis Theory

Hobson and McCarley (1977) proposed that dreams are the brain's attempt to make sense of random neural activity during REM sleep.

### Key Processes

**Random Activation**

* During REM sleep, random nerve impulses are produced in the brainstem.

* These signals have no specific meaning.

**Sensory Input Blockade**

* External sensory information is blocked during sleep.

* The brain relies on internally generated activity instead of information from the environment.

**Motor Output Blockade**

* Signals to skeletal muscles are blocked during REM sleep.

* This prevents the body from acting out dreams.

**Synthesis**

* The cerebral cortex organises the random signals into a meaningful story.

* This creates the dream experienced by the sleeper.

### Williams et al. - Bizarreness of Dream Content

* Williams et al. investigated whether dreams are characterised by bizarre and unusual content. Participants reported their dreams after waking, and researchers analysed the reports.

* Dreams often contained illogical events, sudden changes and unrealistic situations, supporting the activation-synthesis theory that dreams result from the brain interpreting random neural activity.

### Aim

* To investigate the characteristics of dream content.

### Method

* Participants reported their dreams after waking.

* Researchers analysed the content of the dreams.

### Results

* Dreams frequently contained bizarre and unrealistic events.

* Dream content often lacked logical structure.

### Conclusion

* Supports the activation-synthesis theory.

* Dreams are produced by the brain organising random neural activity into a meaningful narrative.

### Evaluation of the Activation-Synthesis Theory

**Strengths**

* Supported by Williams et al., which found dreams are often bizarre and illogical.

* Explains why dreams frequently contain unrealistic events and sudden changes.

* Supported by evidence showing REM sleep is associated with increased brain activity.

**Weaknesses**

* Some dreams are meaningful and relate to real-life experiences.

* Does not explain why recurring dreams often have similar themes.

* Ignores the possible influence of emotions, memories and personal experiences on dream content.

### Application of the Activation-Synthesis Theory

**Scenario:** A person dreams they are flying through space before suddenly finding themselves in a classroom talking to animals.

**Explanation:**

* Random neural activity occurred during REM sleep.

* The cerebral cortex combined unrelated signals into a single dream.

* This produced a bizarre and illogical sequence of events.

**Scenario:** A person dreams about a conversation with a friend, but the setting changes repeatedly without explanation.

**Explanation:**

* The brain is synthesising random neural activity rather than replaying real events.

* The changing locations reflect the brain attempting to organise unrelated signals into a coherent story.

**Scenario:** A sleeper kicks slightly while dreaming but does not physically act out the events of the dream.

**Explanation:**

* During REM sleep, motor output is largely blocked.

* Muscle paralysis prevents the dream from being physically acted out, even though the brain remains highly active.
