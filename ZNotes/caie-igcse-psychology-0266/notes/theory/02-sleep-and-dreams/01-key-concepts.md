### Dreams

Dreams are thoughts, feelings, images and stories experienced during sleep.

* Most dreams occur during REM sleep.

* Dreams often involve emotions, memories and everyday experiences.

* Dream content can range from realistic to unusual.

### Exogenous Cues (Zeitgebers)

Exogenous cues are external factors that regulate biological rhythms.

Light

* The most important zeitgeber.

* Light entering the eyes is detected by the SCN.

* This reduces melatonin production and promotes wakefulness.

* Darkness increases melatonin production, helping us fall asleep.

**Social Cues**

* Daily routines also influence the sleep-wake cycle.

* Examples include:

  * school or work schedules

  * meal times

  * social activities

### Hormones

Hormones are chemical substances released by glands into the bloodstream that affect target cells.

**Melatonin**

* Produced by the pineal gland.

* Promotes sleep by making the body feel sleepy.

* Melatonin levels increase in darkness and decrease in light.

### Biological Rhythms and Everyday Life

### Jet Lag

Jet lag is the psychological and physical effects experienced after travelling rapidly across multiple time zones.

Causes

* Circadian rhythm becomes out of sync with local time.

**Symptoms**

* Tiredness

* Difficulty sleeping

* Poor concentration

* Irritability

### Shift Work

Shift work involves working outside normal daytime hours.

Effects

* Disrupts the circadian rhythm.

* Sleep occurs at biologically unusual times.

**Common Symptoms**

* Tiredness

* Reduced alertness

* Poor concentration

* Increased risk of accidents

### Sleep Disorders

### Insomnia

* Difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep.

* Results in poor-quality or insufficient sleep.

* May be linked to disrupted circadian rhythms or reduced melatonin production.

### Sleep Paralysis

* Temporary inability to move while falling asleep or waking.

* The person remains conscious.

* Occurs when REM muscle paralysis continues after waking.

### Sleepwalking

* Walking or performing activities while asleep.

* Usually occurs during nREM sleep.

* The person often has no memory of the event.

* May result from incomplete transition between sleep stages.

### Application of Biological Processes of Sleep

**Scenario:** A teenager struggles to fall asleep after spending the evening using a tablet in bed.

**Explanation:**

* The bright screen acts as an exogenous cue.

* The SCN detects the light and reduces melatonin production.

* Lower melatonin delays sleep, disrupting the circadian rhythm.

**Scenario:** A flight attendant regularly travels across several time zones and feels exhausted during the day.

**Explanation:**

* Rapid travel disrupts the circadian rhythm.

* The internal biological clock is no longer matched to local time.

* This causes jet lag, leading to tiredness, poor concentration and difficulty sleeping.

**Scenario:** A university student wakes up and is unable to move for a few seconds, even though they are fully awake and aware of their surroundings.

**Explanation:**

* The bright screen acts as an exogenous cue.

* The SCN detects the light and reduces melatonin production.

* Lower melatonin delays sleep, disrupting the circadian rhythm.
