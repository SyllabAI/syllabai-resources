## Gregory's Constructivist Theory of Perception

* Richard Gregory proposed that perception is **constructed** by the brain using sensory information together with past experience and knowledge.

* This is a top-down theory, meaning perception begins with what we already know and expect.

* Gregory believed the information received by the senses is often incomplete or ambiguous, so the brain actively interprets it to create a meaningful perception.

* Perception is therefore strongly influenced by nurture (environment and experience).

### Characteristics of Top-Down Processing

* Perception begins with prior knowledge and experience.

* The brain interprets sensory information rather than simply receiving it.

* Expectations and previous learning influence perception.

* Perception involves active thinking and interpretation.

### Schemas

* Schemas are mental structures that organise and store knowledge about the world.

* They develop through experience and help people quickly recognise familiar objects and situations.

* Schemas allow people to interpret incomplete or unclear sensory information.

**Example:**

After seeing many bicycles, a person develops a schema that helps them recognise different types of bicycles even if part of one is hidden.

### Inferences

* An inference is a conclusion or prediction made using sensory information together with previous knowledge.

* When sensory information is incomplete, the brain fills in the missing details by making inferences.

**Example:**

Seeing only part of a parked car behind a wall allows a person to infer that the rest of the car is hidden.

### Perceptual Hypotheses

* Perceptual hypotheses are educated guesses the brain makes when interpreting sensory information.

* The brain selects the explanation that is most likely based on previous experience.

* As new information becomes available, the perceptual hypothesis may change.

**Example:**

A person seeing an unclear shape in the distance may first think it is a dog, but as they move closer realise it is a rubbish bin.

### Perceptual Set

* Perceptual set is the tendency to notice or interpret sensory information in a particular way because of existing schemas.

* People often notice information that matches what they already expect while overlooking other possible interpretations.

### Culture

* People from different cultures develop different schemas because of their experiences, norms and values.

* This can influence how visual information and depth cues are interpreted.

**Example:**

People raised in environments with many straight buildings may perceive some visual illusions differently from people raised in natural environments.

### Motivation and Emotion

* Needs, emotions and current feelings influence what people are most likely to notice or how they interpret information.

**Example:**

A hungry person may notice food more quickly than someone who has just eaten.

A frightened person may mistake a shadow for a person.

### Expectation

* People are more likely to perceive what they expect to see based on previous experiences or the surrounding context.

**Example:**

If shown several letters before an ambiguous symbol, a person is more likely to interpret it as a letter than a number.

### Haber and Levin (Experiment 2)

Haber and Levin investigated whether familiarity affects the perception of object size.

### Aim

To investigate whether familiar objects are judged more accurately than unfamiliar objects.

### Method

Participants viewed familiar and unfamiliar objects placed at different distances.

They estimated the size of each object.

Researchers compared the accuracy of size estimates for familiar and unfamiliar objects.

### Results

Participants judged the size of familiar objects more accurately than unfamiliar objects.

Previous knowledge improved perceptual judgments.

### Conclusion

Past experience influences perception, supporting Gregory's constructivist theory that perception depends on top-down processing.

### Argument For Gregory's Constructivist Theory

* Supported by Haber and Levin (Experiment 2).

* Participants estimated the size of familiar objects more accurately because they used previous knowledge and experience.

* This supports Gregory's view that schemas and past experience influence perception.

### Argument Against Gregory's Constructivist Theory

* The theory underestimates the role of **nature** in perception.

* Some aspects of perception, such as depth perception in infants, appear to be innate rather than learned.

* Research such as **Gibson and Walk's visual cliff study** suggests not all perception depends on previous experience.

### Application of Gregory's Constructivist Theory

**Scenario:**

* A person mistakes a coat hanging on a door for another person late at night.

**Explanation:**

* The limited sensory information causes the brain to use previous experience to make a perceptual hypothesis, leading to an incorrect interpretation.

**Scenario:**

* A tourist quickly recognises a familiar fast-food restaurant logo in a busy shopping centre.

**Explanation:**

* Schemas developed through previous experience allow familiar objects to be recognised rapidly.

**Scenario:**

* After watching several football matches, a child interprets an unclear shape on television as a football because that is what they expect to see.

**Explanation:**

* Expectation creates a perceptual set, making the child more likely to interpret the ambiguous image as a football.

**Scenario:**

* A hungry shopper notices the bakery before any other stores when entering a shopping centre.

**Explanation:**

* Motivation influences perceptual set, causing food-related stimuli to attract attention more readily.
