## Visual Illusions and Culture

### Types of Two-Dimensional (2D) Visual Illusions

Visual illusions occur when the brain interprets sensory information incorrectly, causing us to perceive something differently from reality.

### Gestalt Principles and Visual Illusions

The Gestalt principles explain how the brain naturally organises visual information into meaningful patterns.

### Ambiguous Figures

**Example:** Leeper's Lady

* The same picture can be seen as either a young woman or an old woman.

* This demonstrates the figure-ground principle because the brain chooses one interpretation as the figure while the other becomes the background.

* The brain cannot usually perceive both interpretations simultaneously.

### Fictions

**Example:** Kanizsa Triangle

* Most people perceive a white triangle even though no triangle is actually drawn.

* This demonstrates the **closure** principle because the brain fills in missing edges to create a complete shape.

* The brain automatically organises incomplete visual information into meaningful objects.

### Influence of Culture on Visual Illusions

* Culture can influence how people perceive visual information because people from different environments learn to focus on different visual cues.

* People living in environments with many straight lines, buildings and corners often rely more on depth cues such as linear perspective.

* People living in more natural environments may rely less on these cues and therefore experience some visual illusions differently.

* This suggests that perception is influenced by both sensory information and past experience.

### Differences in Attention

* Different cultures may attend to different parts of the same visual scene.

* Some cultures focus more on the main object (central attention).

* Others pay greater attention to the surrounding background and relationships between objects (holistic attention).

* These differences in attention can influence how visual illusions are experienced.

### Bremner et al. (Experiment 2) - Culture and Visual Illusions

* Bremner et al. investigated whether cultural background affects susceptibility to visual illusions.

### Aim

* To investigate whether people from different cultures experience visual illusions differently.

### Method

* Experiment:

  * Participants from different cultural backgrounds viewed visual illusions.

  * Researchers compared how accurately participants judged the images.

### Results

* Participants from different cultures experienced some visual illusions differently.

* The extent of the illusion varied depending on cultural background and visual experience.

### Conclusion

* Supports the idea that culture influences perception.

* Experience with different environments affects how visual information is interpreted.

### Evaluation of Culture and Visual Illusions

### Strengths

* Supported by Bremner et al., who found cultural differences in susceptibility to visual illusions.

* Shows that perception is influenced by experience as well as sensory input.

* Helps explain why people from different cultures may interpret the same visual information differently.

### Weaknesses

* Many visual illusions are experienced similarly across cultures.

* Suggests there are universal aspects of human perception that are biologically based.

* Other factors such as age, attention and individual experience may also influence perception.

### Application of Knowledge of Illusions

* **Scenario:** A child looks at the Kanizsa Triangle and says they can clearly see a white triangle even though no triangle has been drawn.

* **Explanation:**

  * The brain applies the Gestalt principle of **closure**.

  * It fills in the missing edges to create a complete triangle.

  * This creates a fictional object that is not physically present.

* **Scenario:** A person looks at the Müller-Lyer illusion and believes one line is longer than the other, even after being told they are equal.

* **Explanation:**

  * The brain misinterprets depth cues.

  * The arrowheads influence perception of distance.

  * This causes equal lines to appear different lengths.

* **Scenario:** A traveller from a rural community and a person raised in a large city view the same visual illusion but report different perceptions.

* **Explanation:**

  * Their cultural experiences influence how visual information is interpreted.

  * Different environments encourage attention to different visual cues.

  * As a result, susceptibility to some visual illusions may differ.

* **Scenario:** A person looking at Leeper's Lady first sees a young woman, but after someone points out the older woman, they can switch between both interpretations.

* **Explanation:**

  * This is an **ambiguous figure**.

  * The brain alternates between two valid interpretations.

  * Only one interpretation is perceived as the figure at a time while the other becomes the background.
