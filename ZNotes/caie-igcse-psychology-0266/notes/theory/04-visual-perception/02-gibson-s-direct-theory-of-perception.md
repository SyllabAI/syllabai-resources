## Gibson's Direct Theory of Perception

* James Gibson proposed that perception is **direct**, meaning the environment provides enough information for us to perceive it accurately without relying on past experience or interpretation.

* This is a bottom-up theory, where perception begins with sensory information received from the environment.

* Gibson believed perception is largely innate (nature) and evolved because it helps organisms survive by responding quickly and accurately to their surroundings.

### Characteristics of Bottom-Up Processing

* Perception starts with information detected by the senses.

* The brain uses the sensory information directly.

* Past experience and expectations are not required.

* Perception is automatic and immediate.

### Motion Parallax

Motion parallax is the apparent difference in the speed at which objects move as an observer moves.

* Nearby objects appear to move quickly.

* Distant objects appear to move more slowly.

This provides information about the relative distance of objects.

**Example:**

When travelling in a car, nearby trees appear to move past rapidly, while distant mountains seem to move very slowly.

### Optic Flow

* Optic flow is the pattern of visual movement created as an observer moves through the environment.

* Objects appear to expand outward from the direction of travel, providing information about movement, direction and speed.

* Optic flow helps people move safely through their environment and avoid obstacles.

### Invariants

Invariants are **features of the environment that remain constant even when the observer moves**.

* They provide reliable information about objects and distances.

### Linear Perspective

* Parallel lines appear to converge as they extend into the distance.

* The greater the convergence, the further away the objects are perceived to be.

**Example:**

Railway tracks appear to meet at the horizon even though they remain parallel.

### Texture Perspective

* Textures appear coarse and detailed when close, but smoother and less detailed as distance increases.

* This helps judge how far away surfaces are.

**Example:**

Individual bricks can be seen nearby, while distant brick walls appear smooth.

### Affordance

Affordances are the **possible actions that the environment offers** to an individual.

People perceive objects according to how they can interact with them.

**Examples:**

* A chair affords sitting.

* A staircase affords climbing.

* A handle affords pulling.

* A doorway affords walking through.

According to Gibson, affordances are perceived directly without conscious thought.

### Gibson and Walk (Visual Cliff)

Gibson and Walk investigated whether depth perception is innate.

### Aim

To investigate whether infants and young animals possess innate depth perception.

### Method

* A visual cliff apparatus was constructed using a thick sheet of glass placed over a patterned surface.

* One side appeared shallow because the pattern was directly beneath the glass.

* The other side appeared deep because the pattern was placed much lower beneath the glass, creating the illusion of a drop.

* Infants were encouraged by their mothers to crawl across the glass.

* Young animals from several species were also tested.

### Results

* Most infants refused to crawl across the apparent drop.

* Most young animals also avoided the deep side.

### Conclusion

* Depth perception develops very early and is largely **innate**, supporting Gibson's direct theory that perception relies on inborn mechanisms rather than learned experience.

### Argument For Gibson's Direct Theory

Supported by **Gibson and Walk's visual cliff study**.

* Both infants and young animals avoided the apparent drop despite little or no previous experience.

* This suggests depth perception is innate and supports Gibson's view that perception develops through nature and direct sensory information.

### Argument Against Gibson's Direct Theory

* The theory ignores the influence of **expectation and culture** on perception.

* People's previous knowledge and cultural experiences can influence how they interpret sensory information, suggesting perception is not always entirely direct.

* Gregory's constructivist theory argues that perception also depends on past experience and learned expectations.

### Application of Gibson's Direct Theory

**Scenario:**

* A cyclist judges that a parked car is much closer than distant buildings while riding down a street.

**Explanation:**

* Motion parallax causes nearby objects to appear to move faster than distant objects, allowing the cyclist to judge distance directly.

**Scenario:**

* A child immediately sits on a chair without needing to think about what it is for.

**Explanation:**

* According to Gibson, the chair's affordance of sitting is perceived directly through sensory information.

**Scenario:**

* A driver judges that a road stretches far into the distance because the edges of the road appear to come closer together.

**Explanation:**

* Linear perspective is an invariant that provides information about distance, allowing direct perception of the environment.

**Scenario:**

* A runner easily avoids a large rock while moving along a forest path.

**Explanation:**

* Optic flow provides continuous information about movement and the surrounding environment, helping the runner navigate safely without relying on prior experience.
