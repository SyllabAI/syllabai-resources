## Biological Explanation of Language Acquisition

* The Biological Explanation argues that humans are biologically prepared to acquire language.

* According to this explanation, language development is largely **innate** (inborn) rather than entirely learned from the environment.

* The most influential biological explanation was proposed by **Noam Chomsky**.

### Language centres in the brain

Specific areas of the brain are specialised for language.

### Broca's Area

**Location:**

* Frontal lobe

**Function:**

* Production of speech

* Formation of words and sentences

Broca's Area is responsible for producing fluent and meaningful speech.

**Example:**

* Speaking during a conversation.

* Reading words aloud.

### Wernicke's Area

**Location:**

* Temporal lobe

**Function:**

* Understanding speech

* Language comprehension

Wernicke's Area allows people to understand spoken and written language.

**Example:**

* Understanding a teacher's instructions.

* Following a conversation.

### Chomsky's Biological Explanation

* Chomsky argued that children are born with an innate ability to acquire language.

* He believed language develops too rapidly and consistently across cultures to be explained entirely by learning.

* His theory includes:

  * Language Acquisition Device (LAD)

  * Universal Grammar

  * Critical Period

### Language Acquisition Device (LAD)

The **Language Acquisition Device (LAD)** is a hypothetical inborn mechanism that helps children learn language.

According to Chomsky, the LAD allows children to:

* Recognise language patterns

* Learn grammatical rules

* Generate new sentences

The LAD helps explain why children acquire language rapidly despite receiving limited language input.

**Example:**

* A child produces a sentence they have never heard before.

### Universal Grammar

* **Universal Grammar** is the idea that all human languages share certain underlying grammatical structures.

* Chomsky argued that children are born with knowledge of these basic language principles.

* This helps explain why children around the world acquire language in similar ways despite learning different languages.

**Example:**

* Children learning English, Spanish and Mandarin still progress through similar developmental stages.

### Critical Period

* The **Critical Period** is an early stage of life when a person is especially ready to acquire language.

  * Language learning occurs most easily during this period.

    * If a child receives little or no language exposure during the Critical Period, normal language development may be difficult.

**Example:**

* Children exposed to language from birth usually acquire language naturally and efficiently.

### Aphasia

**Aphasia** is a language impairment caused by brain damage.

Possible causes include:

* Stroke

* Head injury

* Illness

* Brain damage

Aphasia affects a person's ability to understand and/or produce language.

### Broca's Aphasia

Broca's Aphasia occurs when Broca's Area is damaged.

People generally understand language relatively well but struggle to produce fluent speech.

Characteristics include:

* Slow speech

* Difficulty forming words

* Short sentences

* Missing grammatical words

**Example:**

Instead of saying:

* "I am going to the shop."

A person may say:

* "Go... shop."

### Wernicke's Aphasia

Wernicke's Aphasia occurs when Wernicke's Area is damaged. Speech remains fluent but often lacks meaning. People may also struggle to understand language.

Characteristics include:

* Fluent Speech

* Incorrect Word Use

* Nonsensical Sentences

* Poor Comprehension

**Example:**

* A person produces a grammatically correct sentence that does not make sense.

### Argument for the Biological Explanation

### Named Study: Sign Language Acquisition (Senghas)

* Senghas investigated the development of Nicaraguan Sign Language among deaf children.

* The study examined whether children could naturally develop increasingly complex language structures.

**Key findings**

* Children developed more sophisticated language systems over time.

* New grammatical rules emerged naturally.

* Language became increasingly structured across generations of children.

**Why this supports Chomsky**

* Suggests humans possess an innate capacity for language.

* Supports the existence of biological mechanisms involved in language acquisition.

* Provides evidence for the LAD and Universal Grammar.

### Argument against the Biological Explanation

* Language acquisition is not influenced only by biology.

* Environmental influences also contribute to language development.

**Examples include:**

* Social Interaction

* Cultural Influences

* Exposure to Language

* Communication with Caregivers

Children require interaction with other people in order to fully develop language skills.

Therefore, language acquisition is likely influenced by both biological and environmental factors.

### Application to novel scenarios

When applying the Biological Explanation:

* Identify evidence of innate language ability.

  * Refer to the LAD if a child learns language rapidly.

  * Refer to Universal Grammar if similarities exist across languages.

  * Refer to the Critical Period if age of language exposure is important.

  * Refer to Broca's Area or Wernicke's Area when brain damage affects language.

  * Identify whether symptoms match Broca's Aphasia or Wernicke's Aphasia.
