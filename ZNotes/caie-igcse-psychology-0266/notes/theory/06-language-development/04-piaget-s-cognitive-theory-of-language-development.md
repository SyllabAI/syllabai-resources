## Piaget's Cognitive Theory of Language Development

* Piaget argued that language development depends on cognitive development.

* According to Piaget, children must first develop ways of thinking about the world before they can develop increasingly complex language.

* Language develops alongside a child's understanding of their environment.

* A key concept in Piaget's theory is the **schema**.

## Schemas

A **schema** is a mental structure used to organise and store knowledge about the world.

Schemas help children understand and interpret new experiences.

As children gain new experiences, their schemas develop through:

* Assimilation

* Accommodation

### Assimilation

**Assimilation** occurs when new information is incorporated into an existing schema without changing the schema itself.

The child interprets new experiences using what they already know.

**Example:**

* A child has a schema for "dog".

* They see a wolf and call it a dog.

* The new information is fitted into the existing schema.

### Accommodation

**Accommodation** occurs when an existing schema is changed to incorporate new information.

The child modifies their understanding to better fit reality.

**Example:**

* A child learns that wolves and dogs are different animals.

* The original schema changes.

* Separate schemas are formed.

### Piaget's Stages of Cognitive Development and Language

Piaget believed language develops alongside cognitive development.

### Sensorimotor Stage

**Approximate age:**

* Birth to 2 years

Children learn primarily through physical interaction with the environment.

Language is limited because thinking is largely based on actions and sensory experiences.

Language development involves:

* Repetition of sounds

* Physical exploration

* Early word learning

Piaget linked this stage particularly to **assimilation**, as children begin organising experiences into basic schemas.

**Example:**

* A baby repeatedly says sounds such as "ba-ba".

* A baby learns that shaking a toy produces noise.

### Pre-operational Stage

**Approximate age:**

* 2 to 7 years

Children develop **symbolic thought**.

This means they can use words, images and symbols to represent objects and events.

Language develops rapidly during this stage.

### Symbolic thought

Children understand that words can represent real objects.

**Example:**

* The word "dog" represents an actual dog.

### Egocentric speech

Children often struggle to see situations from another person's perspective.

This is called **egocentrism**.

As a result, their speech may assume that everyone shares their viewpoint.

**Example:**

* A child talks about an event without explaining important details because they assume everyone already knows them.

### Animism

Children may show **animism**.

Animism is the belief that non-living objects can think, feel and behave like living things.

**Example:**

* A child says "The sun is following me."

* A child believes a toy feels sad when left alone.

### Concrete Operational Stage

**Approximate age:**

* 7 to 11 years

Children become less egocentric and begin considering other perspectives.

This is known as **de-centration**.

Language becomes increasingly social and logical.

Speech is used for:

* Asking questions

* Giving answers

* Giving instructions

* Holding conversations

Children become better at adapting language to different situations and audiences.

**Example:**

* A child explains rules of a game to another child.

* A child adjusts their explanation when someone is confused.

### Formal Operational Stage

**Approximate age:**

* 11 years onwards

Children develop the ability to think abstractly and hypothetically.

Language can now be used to discuss:

* Theoretical ideas

* Moral issues

* Future possibilities

* Abstract concepts

Language becomes more sophisticated and flexible.

**Example:**

* Discussing justice, equality or human rights.

* Debating future consequences of a decision.

### Argument for Piaget's Cognitive Theory

### Named Study: Schemas and Language (Hewitt)

Hewitt investigated the relationship between schema development and language acquisition.

The study examined how children's understanding of the world influenced their language development.

**Key findings**

* Children's language reflected their developing understanding of concepts.

* New language skills often appeared alongside cognitive development.

* Changes in schemas influenced language use.

**Why this supports Piaget**

* Suggests language develops alongside thinking.

* Supports the importance of schemas in language development.

* Provides evidence that cognitive development influences language acquisition.

### Argument against Piaget's Cognitive Theory

Piaget may underestimate the influence of environmental factors on language development.

Children's abilities are influenced by:

* Culture

* Upbringing

* Social interaction

* Education

* Opportunities for learning

Children from different environments may develop language differently even when they are the same age.

This suggests language development is influenced by both cognitive and environmental factors.

### Application to novel scenarios

When applying Piaget's theory:

* Identify evidence of schemas.

  * Identify examples of assimilation or accommodation.

  * Match behaviour to the appropriate cognitive stage.

  * Refer to symbolic thought if words represent objects.

  * Refer to egocentrism if a child cannot see another person's perspective.

  * Refer to animism if a child gives human characteristics to objects.

  * Refer to de-centration if a child considers other viewpoints.

  * Refer to formal operational thinking if abstract ideas are discussed.

**Exam tip:** In scenario questions, first identify the child's behaviour, then determine which Piagetian stage best explains that behaviour before applying the theory.
