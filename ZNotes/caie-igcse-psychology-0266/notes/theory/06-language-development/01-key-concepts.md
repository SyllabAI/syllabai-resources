# Language Development

## Key Concepts

Language is a system for communicating thoughts and feelings through both verbal and non-verbal ways.

Language allows people to:

* Share information

* Express emotions

* Communicate needs and wants

* Interact socially

**Verbal communication**

Communication using spoken or written words.

**Example:**

* Saying "I am hungry."

**Non-verbal communication**

Communication without spoken words.

Examples include:

* Gestures

* Facial expressions

* Body language

* Eye contact

**Example:**

* Pointing towards a drink to show you are thirsty.

## Language acquisition

Language acquisition is the process of learning language.

Most children progress through similar stages as their language develops from simple sounds to complex sentences.

### Pre-linguistic stage

The pre-linguistic stage occurs before recognisable words develop.

Communication relies mainly on:

* Crying

* Facial expressions

* Gestures

* Eye contact

Children communicate needs and emotions without using language.

**Example:**

* A baby cries when hungry.

* A baby smiles when happy.

### Babbling stage

Babbling involves producing speech-like sounds that do not yet have meaning.

Children practise sounds that will later be used in language.

Babbling appears in children across many cultures, suggesting a biological basis for language development.

**Example:**

* "Ba-ba-ba"

* "Da-da-da"

### One-word stage

Children begin using individual words to communicate complete ideas.

A single word may represent an entire sentence.

This is sometimes called a **holophrase**.

**Example:**

* "Milk" may mean "I want milk."

* "Up" may mean "Pick me up."

### Two-word stage

Children begin combining two words together.

Speech becomes more meaningful because children can express relationships between objects, actions and people.

**Example:**

* "Daddy go"

* "Want toy"

* "Mummy come"

### Telegraphic stage

Children begin producing short sentences containing mainly important content words.

Grammatical words are often omitted.

Speech resembles a telegram because only essential words are included.

**Example:**

* "Want more juice"

* "Daddy drive car"

### Multi-word (Post-telegraphic) stage

Children begin producing longer and more grammatically correct sentences.

Vocabulary increases rapidly and sentence structure becomes more complex.

Children start using:

* Grammar

* Plurals

* Tenses

* Conjunctions

**Example:**

* "I want to play outside."

* "I went to the park with my friend."
