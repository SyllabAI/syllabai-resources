## Learning Theory of Language Development

The Learning Theory argues that language is learned through interaction with the environment.

Children acquire language through:

* Observation

* Imitation

* Reinforcement

Unlike Chomsky's Biological Explanation, Learning Theory emphasises the role of experience and learning.

### Observation and imitation

Children learn language by observing and copying the speech of people around them.

These people act as **models**.

Children may imitate:

* Parents

* Siblings

* Teachers

* Friends

As children hear language repeatedly, they gradually reproduce words and sentence structures themselves.

**Example:**

* A child hears a parent say "Thank you" and begins using the phrase themselves.

### Positive reinforcement

**Positive Reinforcement** occurs when a pleasant consequence follows a behaviour, increasing the likelihood that the behaviour will occur again.

In language development, adults often reward children's attempts at communication.

Rewards may include:

* Praise

* Attention

* Smiles

* Encouragement

**Example:**

* A child correctly says "Dog."

* A parent responds, "Well done!"

* The child becomes more likely to use the word again.

### Negative reinforcement

**Negative Reinforcement** occurs when an unpleasant stimulus is removed following a behaviour, increasing the likelihood of that behaviour occurring again.

The behaviour increases because it helps remove something unpleasant.

**Example:**

* A child asks for help using words.

* The child's frustration is removed when help is provided.

* The child becomes more likely to use language in future situations.

### Argument for the Learning Theory

### Speech and Social Feedback (Goldstein et al.)

Goldstein et al. investigated whether social feedback from adults influences infants' vocal development. The study examined how adult responses affected babies' speech-like sounds.

**Key findings**

* Babies produced more speech-like sounds when adults responded positively.

* Social interaction encouraged further vocalisations.

* Adult feedback influenced language development.

**Why this supports Learning Theory**

* Demonstrates the importance of environmental influences.

* Supports the role of reinforcement.

* Suggests social interaction contributes to language acquisition.

### Argument against the Learning Theory

* Children often produce completely new sentences that they have never heard before.

* This is known as **novel language**.

* If language were learned only through imitation, children would simply repeat previously heard phrases.

* Instead, children regularly create original sentences.

**Example:**

* A child says "I goed to the park" despite never hearing that phrase before.

  * This suggests children actively learn grammatical rules rather than simply copying others.

### Application to novel scenarios

When applying Learning Theory:

* Identify examples of observation and imitation.

  * Look for evidence of positive reinforcement.

  * Look for evidence of negative reinforcement.

  * Identify adult feedback encouraging language development.

  * Refer to environmental influences on language learning.

  * Explain how reinforcement increases future language use.
