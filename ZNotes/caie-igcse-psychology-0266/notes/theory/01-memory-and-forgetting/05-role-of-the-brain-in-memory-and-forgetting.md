## **Role of the Brain in Memory and Forgetting**

### **Localisation of Function**

Localisation of function is the idea that specific brain areas control specific functions.

* Different brain areas have specialised roles.

* Damage to a brain area can affect the function it controls.

### Limbic System

The limbic system is a network of brain structures involved in:

* Emotion

* Memory

* Learning

**Important structures:**

* Amygdala

  * Involved in emotionally arousing memories.

  * Influences consolidation of emotional memories.

  * Example: Emotional events are often remembered clearly.

  * Damage: May reduce the ability to form strong emotional memories.

* Hippocampus

  * Part of the limbic system involved in memory formation.

  * Consolidates memories from STM into LTM.

  * Important for declarative memory.

  * Damage:

    * Causes anterograde amnesia.

    * Person cannot form new long-term memories.

### Cerebellum

* Involved in balance and coordination.

* Important for procedural memory.

  * Procedural Memory: Memory for skills and actions.

* Examples:

  * Riding a bicycle.

  * Playing an instrument.

* Damage: Causes difficulty learning or performing skilled movements.

### **HM Amnesia (Milner et al.)**

* Milner et al. used a case study of Henry Molaison (HM) to investigate the role of the hippocampus in memory after surgery for severe epilepsy damaged this brain region.

* Researchers used memory tests and observations to assess the effects of the damage.

* HM could not form new long-term declarative memories but retained short-term and procedural memory, demonstrating the hippocampus is essential for consolidating memories from STM into LTM.

**Aim**

* To investigate the role of the hippocampus in memory formation.

**Background**

* HM suffered from severe epilepsy.

* Surgery removed parts of his hippocampus.

* HM developed severe memory problems.

**Method**

* Case study of one individual.

* Memory tests and observations were used.

**Results**

HM showed:

* Anterograde amnesia: Unable to form new long-term memories.

* Preserved STM: Could hold information briefly.

* Preserved procedural memory: Could learn new skills through practice.

**Conclusion**

* The hippocampus is important for transferring STM into LTM.

* Different types of memory are stored in different brain areas.

### **Argument for Brain Role in Memory**

* **Evidence**:

  * The cerebral cortex has specialised areas for functions such as memory, language and reasoning.

  * HM's hippocampal damage impaired new memory formation.

  * The amygdala specialises in emotional memories.

* **Conclusion**:

• This supports localisation of function because different brain areas have different roles.

### **Argument Against Localisation**

* Memory does not depend on one brain area only.

* Other areas, such as the cerebral cortex, are involved in:

  * Reasoning

  * Memory

  * Learning

  * Language

* **Conclusion**: Memory depends on interaction between multiple brain areas.

### **Application of Brain Role in Memory**

* **Scenario:** A person has damage to their hippocampus after a head injury and cannot remember new information, although they can still remember events from childhood.

* **Explanation**:

  * The hippocampus normally transfers STM into LTM.

  * Damage prevents the formation of new declarative memories.

  * This causes **anterograde amnesia**, while many older memories remain intact.

* **Scenario:** After damage to the cerebellum, a musician struggles to relearn how to play the piano but can still remember facts about music theory.

  * The cerebellum stores **procedural memories** for learned skills and movements.

  * Damage makes it difficult to learn or perform motor skills.

  * Declarative memory, involving facts and events, is less affected because it mainly depends on the hippocampus.
