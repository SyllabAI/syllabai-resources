## **Motivated Forgetting**

### **Psychodynamic Theory of Forgetting**

The psychodynamic theory suggests forgetting occurs because memories may be emotionally painful or unacceptable.

* Forgetting is motivated by a desire to reduce anxiety.

* Unpleasant thoughts and memories are pushed away from awareness.

### **Structure of Personality**

**Id**

* The innate part of personality, present from birth.

* Operates according to the pleasure principle, seeking immediate satisfaction of basic needs and desires.

* Is entirely unconscious and does not consider consequences or social rules.

**Ego**

* Develops during childhood as the individual interacts with reality.

* Operates according to the reality principle, balancing the demands of the id, the super-ego and the external world.

* Uses defence mechanisms to reduce anxiety caused by internal conflict.

**Super-Ego**

* Develops through parental guidance and social experiences.
* Represents moral values, conscience and ideals of right and wrong.
* Encourages behaviour that follows social and ethical standards.

### **Defense Mechanisms**

Defense mechanisms are protective behaviours used by the ego to reduce anxiety caused by conflict between parts of personality.

**Repression**

* Unconscious removal of emotionally painful experiences from consciousness.

* The person is unaware the memory has been removed.

* Example: A person cannot remember a traumatic event.

**Suppression**

* Conscious removal of unpleasant thoughts or memories from awareness.

* The person chooses not to think about the information.

* Example: Choosing not to think about a stressful event.

### **Application of Psychodynamic Theory**

* **Scenario:** A person involved in a serious car accident cannot remember the event, even though other people tell them what happened.

* **Explanation:**

  * The memory may have been repressed.

  * The ego unconsciously removes the traumatic memory from conscious awareness.

  * This protects the individual from the anxiety caused by the experience.

* **Scenario:** A student feels anxious before an exam and deliberately chooses not to think about a recent argument with a friend until the exams are over.

  * This is suppression, as the student consciously chooses to avoid thinking about the unpleasant memory.

  * Unlike repression, the memory remains accessible and can be recalled later.

  * The behaviour helps reduce anxiety so the student can focus on the exam.
