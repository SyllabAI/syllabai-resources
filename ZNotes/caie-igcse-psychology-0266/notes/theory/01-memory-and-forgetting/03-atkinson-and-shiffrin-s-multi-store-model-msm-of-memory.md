## **Atkinson and Shiffrin’s Multi-Store Model (MSM) of Memory**

### **Atkinson and Shiffrin’s Multi-Store Model (1968)**

The Multi-Store Model explains memory as three separate stores:

* Sensory register (SR)

* Short-term memory (STM)

* Long-term memory (LTM)

Information moves through the stores in a fixed sequence.

### **Sensory Register (SR)**

* Receives information from the environment through the senses.

* Types of sensory register:

  * Iconic memory – visual information

  * Echoic memory – auditory information

  * Gustatory memory – taste information

  * Olfactory memory – smell information

  * Tactile memory – touch information

**Role of Attention:**

* Attention transfers information from SR to STM.

* Unattended information is lost through decay.

### **Short-Term Memory (STM)**

* Temporary store for information currently being processed.

* **Features**:

  * Input: Information from sensory register through attention.

  * Capacity: 5–9 items.

  * Duration: 15–30 seconds without rehearsal.

  * Encoding: Mainly acoustic.

  * Access: Immediate recall.

**Rehearsal**

* Repeating information to maintain it in STM.

* Allows transfer of information into LTM.

* Example: Repeating a phone number until it is remembered.

### **Long-Term Memory (LTM)**

* Store for information retained over long periods.

* **Features**:

  * Input: Information transferred from STM through rehearsal.

  * Capacity: Unlimited.

  * Duration: Potentially lifelong.

  * Encoding: Mainly semantic.

  * Access: Retrieval of stored information.

### **Serial Position Effect**

The serial position effect is the tendency to remember information depending on its position in a list.

**Primacy Effect**

* Information presented at the beginning of a list is remembered better.

* Early items receive more rehearsal and enter LTM.

**Recency Effect**

* Information presented at the end of a list is remembered better.

* Recent items remain in STM.

### **Murdock (1962)- Serial Position Effect**

* Murdock (1962) conducted a laboratory experiment to test Atkinson and Shiffrin's Multi-Store Model by investigating whether the position of a word in a list affects recall. 

* Participants memorised lists of words and then recalled as many words as possible. 

* The study found that words at the beginning and end of the lists were remembered best, supporting the existence of separate short-term and long-term memory stores through the primacy and recency effects.

**Aim**

* To investigate the serial position effect in memory.

**Procedure**

* Participants were shown lists of words.

* Participants recalled as many words as possible.

**Results**

* Words at the beginning and end of lists were recalled best.

* Words in the middle were recalled least accurately.

**Conclusion**

* Supports the MSM.

* Primacy effect shows LTM storage.

* Recency effect shows STM storage.

### **Forgetting in MSM**

**Displacement**

* Forgetting information in STM because new information replaces old information.

* Occurs due to limited STM capacity.

**Decay**

* Forgetting due to fading of memory traces over time.

* Occurs in SR and STM when information is not attended to or rehearsed.

**Access Problems**

* Forgetting information stored in LTM because it cannot be retrieved.

* Information may still exist but cannot be accessed.

### **Evaluation of MSM**

* Strengths:

  * Supported by Murdock’s serial position effect.

  * Amnesia studies show STM and LTM are separate stores.

* Weaknesses:

  * Overemphasis on rehearsal as the main way information enters LTM.

  * Cannot explain the effect of depth of processing.

  * Treats STM and LTM as single stores despite evidence for different types of memory.

### **Application of MSM**

* Scenario: A student is distracted while their teacher explains a new Psychology concept and cannot remember what was said.

* Explanation:

  * The information entered the **sensory register**, but the student did not pay attention.

  * The information was not transferred to short-term memory (STM).

  * It was lost through decay.
