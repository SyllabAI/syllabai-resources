## **Craik and Lockhart’s Levels of Processing (LoP) Model of Memory**

### **Main Idea**

Craik and Lockhart (1972) proposed that:

* Memory depends on the depth of processing.

* Deeper processing creates stronger and longer-lasting memories.

* Storage is a by-product of processing.

### The Levels of Processing model shows that deeper processing produces stronger and more durable memories.

### **Structural Processing**

* Shallow level of processing.

* Focuses on appearance.

**Example**: Looking at the shape or size of a word.

### **Phonemic Processing**

* Intermediate level of processing.

* Focuses on sound.

**Example**: Deciding whether words rhyme.

### **Semantic Processing**

* Deepest level of processing.

* Focuses on meaning.

**Example**: Understanding the meaning of a sentence.

### **Craik and Tulving (1975)- Levels of Processing**

* Craik and Tulving (1975) conducted a laboratory experiment to test Craik and Lockhart's Levels of Processing theory by investigating whether deeper levels of processing improve memory.

* Participants processed words at structural, phonemic and semantic levels before completing an unexpected recognition test. The study found that semantic processing produced the highest recall, providing strong support for the Levels of Processing model.

**Aim**

* To investigate whether deeper processing improves memory.

**Procedure**

* Participants were shown words and asked questions requiring different levels of processing.

* Structural questions focused on appearance.

* Phonemic questions focused on sound.

* Semantic questions focused on meaning.

* Participants completed an unexpected recognition test.

**Results**

* Semantic processing produced the highest recall.

* Phonemic processing produced better recall than structural processing.

**Conclusion**

* Deeper processing leads to better memory.

* Supports the LoP model.

### **Evaluation of LoP Model**

* Strengths:

  * Supported by Craik and Tulving’s research.

  * Explains why meaningful information is remembered better.

* Weaknesses:

  * Cannot explain the limited capacity of memory.

  * Depth of processing is difficult to measure objectively.

### **LoP Model and Forgetting**

**Encoding Specificity Principle**

* Recall improves when retrieval conditions match encoding conditions.

* Similar contexts and cues improve access to memories.

* Example: Revising in the same environment as the exam may improve recall.

### **Application of LoP Model**

* **Scenario:** A student memorises Psychology definitions by repeatedly copying them without thinking about what they mean.

* **Explanation:**

  * Uses **structural processing** because attention is focused on the appearance of the words rather than their meaning.

  * This is a shallow level of processing, creating a weaker memory trace.

  * Information is less likely to be remembered.

* **Scenario:** A student revises key concepts by explaining them in their own words, linking them to previous knowledge, and practising in the same classroom where they will sit a class test.

* **Explanation:**

  * Uses **semantic processing** because the meaning of the information is actively analysed.

  * Linking new information to existing knowledge creates a stronger, longer-lasting memory trace.

  * Recall is further improved by the encoding specificity principle, as the learning and testing contexts are similar.

  * The student is more likely to retrieve the information accurately during the exam.
