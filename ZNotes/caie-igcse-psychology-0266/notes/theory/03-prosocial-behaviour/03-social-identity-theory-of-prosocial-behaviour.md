## Social Identity Theory of Prosocial Behaviour

### Social Identity

* Social identity is the part of a person's self-concept that comes from belonging to social groups, such as family, nationality, ethnicity, school or sports teams.

* People often define themselves partly by the groups they belong to.

These group memberships influence attitudes, behaviour and interactions with others.

* **Examples include:**

  * Family

  * Cultural or ethnic groups

  * School or university

  * Sports teams

  * Friendship groups

### Tajfel and Turner's Social Identity Theory

* Tajfel and Turner proposed that people classify themselves and others into groups, influencing how they think, feel and behave towards members of different groups.

* The theory explains why people are often more willing to help members of their own group than those outside it.

### Group Membership and Self-Esteem

* People gain part of their self-esteem from the groups they belong to because group membership becomes part of their identity.

  * If an ingroup is viewed positively, individuals may feel increased confidence and belonging because the success of the group reflects positively on themselves.

  * As a result, people may favour and support members of their own group to maintain a positive social identity.

* People generally feel a stronger sense of connection, trust and responsibility towards members of their ingroup because they see them as similar to themselves. This can increase helping behaviour because individuals are more motivated to support people who share their social identity.

* In contrast, people may feel less connected to outgroup members, which can reduce helping behaviour. However, this does not mean people never help outgroups, as empathy and shared goals can also encourage prosocial behaviour.

### Social Identity Theory and Prosocial Behaviour

**Shared Identity**

* People are more likely to help individuals they identify with because they see them as members of the same social group.

* Shared identity increases trust, cooperation and feelings of responsibility.

**Ingroup Favouritism**

* Ingroup favouritism is the tendency to prefer, support or help members of one's own group more than members of an outgroup.

* This does not necessarily involve disliking the outgroup, but simply giving greater support to the ingroup.

**Example**

* Students may be more willing to help classmates from their own school than students from another school because they share a common identity.

### Levine et al. (Study 1) - Helping Behaviour

* Levine et al. investigated whether people are more likely to help someone who belongs to their own social group.

  * Participants encountered a person in need of assistance whose group membership was manipulated.

  * People were more likely to help someone they identified as belonging to their own group, supporting Social Identity Theory.

**Aim**

* To investigate whether shared social identity influences helping behaviour.

**Method**

* Experiment.

* Participants encountered a person who appeared to need help.

* The person's group membership was varied.

* Researchers recorded whether participants offered assistance.

**Results**

* Participants were more likely to help members of their own group.

* Helping behaviour increased when participants shared a social identity with the person needing help.

**Conclusion**

* Shared group membership increases prosocial behaviour.

* People are more likely to help members of their ingroup than members of an outgroup.

### Argument for Social Identity Theory

**Evidence**

* Levine et al. found participants were more likely to help members of their own group.
* Shared identity strengthens feelings of belonging and responsibility.

**Conclusion**

* Social identity influences prosocial behaviour by increasing helping towards members of the ingroup.

### Argument Against Social Identity Theory

* Prosocial behaviour is not influenced by social identity alone.

* Individual differences, empathy, personality and cultural values can all affect whether someone chooses to help another person.

* People may also help strangers who do not belong to any of their social groups.

### Application of Social Identity Theory

* **Scenario:** During a school sports competition, a student quickly helps an injured teammate before offering assistance to players from the opposing school.

* **Explanation:**

  * The teammate belongs to the student's ingroup.

  * Shared identity increases feelings of responsibility and cooperation.

  * This results in ingroup favouritism and a greater likelihood of helping.

* **Scenario:** After a natural disaster, members of a local community organise food, clothing and shelter for other residents in the same neighbourhood.

* **Explanation:**

  * Residents share a common social identity as members of the same community.

  * This shared identity encourages cooperation and prosocial behaviour.

  * People feel responsible for helping members of their ingroup.

* **Scenario:** Two students from different schools meet during a national science competition and discover they both represent New Zealand.

* **Explanation:**

  * Their shared national identity creates a new ingroup.

  * Social identification increases feelings of connection.

  * They become more willing to cooperate and support one another despite attending different schools.
