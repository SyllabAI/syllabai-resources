## Biological Explanation of Prosocial Behaviour

### Biological Explanation

* The biological explanation suggests that prosocial behaviour is partly influenced by inherited biological factors, including neurotransmitters, hormones, brain structures and genetics.

* These biological processes affect empathy, emotional regulation, decision-making and social interactions, increasing or decreasing the likelihood of helping behaviour.

### Neurotransmitters

* Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers released by nerve cells that transmit signals to other nerve cells across synapses.

* They influence communication within the brain and affect behaviour, emotions and social interactions.

### Serotonin

Serotonin is a neurotransmitter involved in regulating mood, emotional control and social behaviour.

Role in Prosocial Behaviour

* Higher serotonin activity is associated with greater cooperation and helping behaviour.

* It helps regulate impulsive and aggressive behaviour.

* Better emotional regulation may increase empathy and willingness to help others.

**Example**

A calm person who remains patient during conflict may be more likely to resolve disagreements peacefully and assist others.

### Hormones

Hormones are chemical substances produced by glands and released into the bloodstream, where they affect specific target cells.

### Oxytocin

Oxytocin is a hormone involved in social bonding, trust and emotional attachment.

Role in Prosocial Behaviour

* Increases trust between individuals.

* Strengthens social bonds and cooperation.

* Promotes caring and nurturing behaviours.

* Increases empathy towards others.

**Example**

Parents caring for their children or friends supporting one another during difficult situations may be influenced by oxytocin.

### Prefrontal Cortex

The prefrontal cortex is part of the cerebral cortex responsible for higher-order thinking and self-control.

**Role**

* Evaluates the consequences of actions.

* Regulates emotional responses.

* Supports thoughtful and socially appropriate decisions.

* Helps individuals choose behaviours that benefit others.

**Damage**

Damage to the prefrontal cortex may reduce self-control, empathy and socially appropriate behaviour.

### Amygdala

The amygdala is part of the limbic system involved in processing emotions.

Role

* Recognises emotional expressions in others.

* Supports empathy and emotional understanding.

* Helps people respond appropriately when others are distressed.

**Damage**

Damage to the amygdala may reduce emotional responsiveness and make it more difficult to recognise another person's distress.

### Heritability

* Heritability is the extent to which differences in a behaviour or characteristic can be explained by genetic differences between individuals.

* Twin studies are commonly used to investigate heritability.

* Researchers measure heritability by comparing how similar monozygotic (MZ) and dizygotic (DZ) twins are in the same behaviour or characteristic. Both twins complete the same measures, such as questionnaires of social responsibility, and researchers compare how closely their results match. If MZ twins show more similar results than DZ twins, this suggests that genetic factors contribute to the behaviour because MZ twins share more genes.

* However, greater similarity does not prove that behaviour is completely genetic, as twins also share environmental influences such as upbringing, culture and experiences. Heritability shows the contribution of genetic differences to variation between individuals.

### Rushton - Heritability of Social Responsibility

* Rushton investigated whether prosocial behaviour is influenced by genetics using twins. By comparing similarities in social responsibility between monozygotic (MZ) and dizygotic (DZ) twins, the study examined the contribution of heredity to prosocial behaviour.

* MZ twins showed greater similarity than DZ twins, supporting the biological explanation.

### Aim

* To investigate the heritability of prosocial behaviour by comparing social responsibility in MZ and DZ twins.

### Method

* Twin study.

  * The questionnaire scores of MZ and DZ twin pairs were compared to determine how similar each pair was in social responsibility.

  * Results from MZ and DZ twins were compared.

### Results

* MZ twins showed greater similarity in social responsibility than DZ twins.

* This suggested a stronger genetic influence on prosocial behaviour.

### Conclusion

* Prosocial behaviour is partly influenced by heredity.

* Genes contribute to individual differences in helping behaviour.

### Argument for the Biological Explanation

**Evidence**

Rushton found MZ twins were more similar in social responsibility than DZ twins.

Research also shows serotonin, oxytocin, the prefrontal cortex and the amygdala influence empathy and helping behaviour.

**Conclusion**

Biological factors contribute to prosocial behaviour by influencing emotions, decision-making and social interactions.

### Argument Against the Biological Explanation

* Prosocial behaviour is not determined by biology alone.

  * Environmental influences such as parenting, friendships, cultural values and social learning also affect whether people choose to help others.

  * Genes may create a tendency towards prosocial behaviour, but experiences influence how that tendency is expressed.

### Application of the Biological Explanation

**Scenario:** A volunteer remains calm during an emergency and quickly comforts an injured child while reassuring the child's family.

**Explanation:**

* The prefrontal cortex helps regulate emotions and make thoughtful decisions.

* The amygdala allows the volunteer to recognise the child's distress and respond empathetically.

* Oxytocin may strengthen caring and supportive behaviour during the interaction.

**Scenario:** Two identical twins, raised in different homes, both regularly volunteer in their local communities and are described as caring and cooperative.

**Explanation:**

* Monozygotic twins share nearly all of their genes.

* Their similar prosocial behaviour suggests heredity may influence helping behaviour.

* However, differences between their environments may also shape how those behaviours are expressed.

**Scenario:** After suffering damage to the prefrontal cortex in an accident, a person becomes impulsive and is less likely to consider how their actions affect others.

**Explanation:**

* The prefrontal cortex regulates decision-making and socially appropriate behaviour.

* Damage reduces self-control and thoughtful decision-making.

* This may decrease prosocial behaviour even though the person understands social rules.
