# Prosocial Behaviour

## Key Concepts of Prosocial Behaviour

### Prosocial Behaviour

Prosocial behaviour refers to voluntary actions intended to benefit another person or group without causing harm.

**Examples include:**

* Helping someone in need.

* Sharing resources or possessions.

* Caring for another person's wellbeing.

* Cooperating with others to achieve a common goal.

Prosocial behaviour benefits both individuals and society by encouraging cooperation, trust and positive social relationships.

### Individual Factors

Individual factors are characteristics unique to a person that influence whether they behave prosocially.

**Personality**

* People who are empathetic, caring or cooperative are generally more likely to help others.

**Age**

* Prosocial behaviour generally increases with age as empathy, perspective-taking and social understanding develop.

**Biological Factors**

* Genes, hormones, neurotransmitters and brain activity can influence how likely a person is to behave prosocially.

These biological influences are explored further in the Biological Explanation of Prosocial Behaviour.

### Situational Factors

* Situational factors are features of the environment that influence whether a person helps others.

**Location**

* People may respond differently depending on where an event occurs because environments influence feelings of responsibility and connection.

* For example, someone may be more willing to help in a quiet neighbourhood where they feel more personally responsible, compared with a busy city where responsibility may be shared among many people.

**Proximity**

* People are generally more likely to help individuals who are physically nearby or who they can easily reach.
* This is because being closer makes it easier to notice someone needing help and respond quickly, increasing the likelihood of helping behaviour.

**Environmental Factors**

The surrounding environment can influence helping behaviour by affecting a person's attention, mood and ability to respond.

**Examples include:**

* Time pressure: People who are in a hurry may be less likely to stop and help because they are focused on reaching their destination.

* Noise levels: Loud environments can make it harder to notice that someone needs assistance.

* Crowded environments: People may assume someone else will help, reducing personal responsibility.

* Weather conditions: Extreme weather may discourage helping if it increases the difficulty or risk of assisting someone.

Stressful or distracting environments may reduce the likelihood of helping behaviour because individuals focus more on their own situation than on the needs of others.

### Interaction Between Individual and Situational Factors

Prosocial behaviour is rarely influenced by one factor alone.

* An empathetic person may still fail to help if they are in a dangerous situation, while someone who is usually less helpful may assist when the situation is safe and assistance is easy to provide.

### Application of Key Concepts

**Scenario:** A secondary school student notices a younger student struggling to carry several heavy books and offers to help.

**Explanation:**

* Helping is a form of prosocial behaviour because it benefits another person.

* The older student's age and greater social understanding may increase their willingness to help.

**Scenario:** During lunchtime, one student shares their lunch with a classmate who forgot theirs.

**Explanation:**

* Sharing is a type of prosocial behaviour.

* The behaviour benefits another person without expecting a reward.

**Scenario:** A commuter walks past an injured cyclist in a crowded train station, assuming someone else will help.

**Explanation:**

* Situational factors can influence prosocial behaviour.

* The crowded location may reduce an individual's likelihood of helping because responsibility is perceived to be shared among many people.
