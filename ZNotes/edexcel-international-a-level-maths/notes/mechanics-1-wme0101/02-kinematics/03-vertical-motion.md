## Vertical Motion


1. Draw a diagram to visualise the problem.
2. Write down the values you know and the values you need to find.
3. Use the appropriate formula.
4. Substitute with the values and solve to find the required variable.

* The magnitude of the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) is 9.8 $ms^{-2}$ (unless otherwise specified).
* Gravity ($g$) always acts downwards.
* Acceleration is equal to -9.8 $ms^{-2}$ when a particle is in free fall (if an answer is needed in terms of $g$ then $a$ =  $-g$).
* Velocity is equal to 0 $ms^{-1}$ at the greatest height reached by the particle.
* When a particle is moving upwards consider velocity and displacement positive.
* When a particle is moving downwards consider velocity and displacement negative.
* When a particle moves upwards then downwards the initial velocity is positive, the final velocity is negative, and the displacement is negative.
* Air resistance is neglected.
* If the motion of two particles is required or not enough values are given, solve simultaneously using two paths.
