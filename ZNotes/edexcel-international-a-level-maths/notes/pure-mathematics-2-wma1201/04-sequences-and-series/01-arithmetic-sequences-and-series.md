# Sequences and Series

## Arithmetic Sequences

* Arithmetic sequences are written as:

$$
a,a+d,a+2d…. 
$$

* They can be simplified and be written in the standard form of: (This is known as the nth term formula)

  $$
  a + (n-1)d
  $$
* This is where:


1. n = the nth term
2. d = common difference
3. a = first term of the sequence

→ **Note: If a, b, and c are consecutive terms of an arithmetic sequence, then:** $b-a = c-b$


---

Example Question:

The first three terms of an arithmetic sequence are 5p, 20, 3p.

Find $t_{20}$


---

Solution:

We can use the relationship of $b-a = c -b$ to get 20 - 5p = 3p - 20. Which, when solving for p, gives us p = 5.

When plugging this back into the sequence provided, we get a common difference of -5.

This can be substituted into the nth-term formula to get

$$
t_{20} = 25 + -5(19) 
$$

Therefore $t_{20} = -70$

## Arithmetic Series

* The standard formula of an arithmetic series is:

$$
S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]
$$

→ The proof of this series can be shown as:


1. Let equation 1 be: \[a\] \ + \ \[a+d\] \ + \  \[a+2d\] \ + \  … \ + \  \[a+(n-2)d\] \ + \ \[a+(n-1)d\] 
2. Let equation 2 be: $[a+(n-1)d] \ + \  [a+(n-2)d] \ + \ … \ + \ [ a+2d] \ + \ [a+d] \ + \ [a]$

   (This is the reverse of equation 1)
3. When you add equations 1 and 2 together, you get $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]$
