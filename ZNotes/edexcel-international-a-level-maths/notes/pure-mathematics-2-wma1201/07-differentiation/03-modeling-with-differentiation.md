## Modeling With Differentiation

* $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is actually defined as $\frac{\text{change in y}}{\text{change in x}}$. It, therefore, represents the rate of change of y concerning x.
* We can, therefore, substitute real-life quantities in replacement of y and x to create models that can help visualize real-life situations.


---

→ For example, we can use differentiation to model the water flow out of a container.

 ![](../../../assets/f01515da0ae9-6553c97bd6cf9a6a1a1e30063da4cb4465f54843e12c4ced.36)→ As the water flows out, we know that the volume will decrease over time. Therefore, We can take our two quantities as V (volume) and t (time) to model the rate of change of volume with respect to time.

→ This is represented as $\frac{dV}{dt}$ and would be in units of liters per second.
