## Solution b

b) Given that $l_{2}$ is perpendicular to  $l_{1}$. Find an equation for $l_{2}$ in the form $ax+by+c=0$, where a, b and c are integers

_____________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________________

Solution:

 Since  $l_{1}$ is perpendicular to $l_{2}$ the product of their gradients is -1, hence $m_{1} \times m_{2}=-1$
Rearrange the equation to get $m_{2}=\frac{-1}{m_{1}}$   , from part a the gradient was found to be 3/2  , substituting that in place of $m_{1}$, gives $m_{2}= -1 ÷ \frac{3}{2}$.

$m_{2}= \frac{-2 }{3}$

Since the point of intersection between  $l_{1}$ and $l_{2}$ has the x-coordinate of 1, $x=1$ , can be substituted into $l_{1}$ 

$2y=3x+5$

$2y=3(1)+5$

$2y=3+5$

$2y=8$

$y=8/2$

$y=4$

Using all this information values can be plugged into the formula $(y-y_{1})=m(x-x_{1} )$,

$(y-4)=-2/3 (x-1)$

$y-4=-2/3 x+2/3$

$y=-2/3 x+2/3+4$
 
$y=-2/3 x+14/3$

$-2/3 x-y+14/3=0$

Multiply both side by -3

$2x+3y-14=0$
