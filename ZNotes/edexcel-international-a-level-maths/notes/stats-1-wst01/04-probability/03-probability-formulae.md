## Probability Formulae

$\boxed {P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)}$

* **Why do we subtract the intersection?**

  * When you calculate the probability of A, you count everything inside circle A, which includes the overlapping section

  * When you calculate the probability of B, you count everything inside circle B, which includes the overlapping section AGAIN

  * As the overlapping section is counted twice, you must subtract to get an accurate value

$\boxed {P(A^{\prime })=1-P(A)}$

* The total probability of something occurring is 1. Therefore, to get the complement of the A, you subtract A from 1
  * **Complement**: everything in the universal set that is not in that specific set

### Example question:

In a sports club, the probability that a randomly chosen member plays tennis ***T*** is 0.6. The probability that they play badminton ***B*** is 0.5. The probability that they play both tennis and badminton is 0.35.

* (a) Find the probability that a member plays tennis or badminton or both, $P(T \cup B)$

* (b) Find the probability that a member doesn’t play tennis

Answer:

(a)

* $P(T\cup B)=P(T)+P(B)-P(T\cap B)$
* $P(T\cup B)=0.6+0.5-0.35$
* $P(T\cup B)=1.1-0.35=\mathbf{0.75}$

(b)

* The probability that a member doesn’t play tennis is 1- 0.6= 0.4
