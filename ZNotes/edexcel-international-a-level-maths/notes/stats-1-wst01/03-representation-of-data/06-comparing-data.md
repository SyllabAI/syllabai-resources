## Comparing data

* When "comparing two data sets," you must comment on two distinct things: a measure of location and a measure of spread. You must also relate your points back to the real-world context of the data

* The usual pairs are:

  * Mean and standard deviation

  * Median and interquartile range

* If the data set contains extreme values, the *median and interquartile range* are more appropriate because they are less affected by unusual values

**Measure of Location (Average)**

* Use mean if the data is symmetrical and contains no extreme outliers.

* Use median if the data is skewed or contains extreme values/outliers.

**Measure of Spread (Variation)**

* Standard Deviation: Use alongside the Mean for symmetrical data.

* Interquartile Range (IQR): Use alongside the Median if the data set contains extreme values, because it ignores data points outside the middle 50%.

**How to compare properly**

Include:

* Which data set has the higher average.

* Which data set is more spread out.

* Whether one set is more consistent.

* Whether outliers may affect the mean.

* **Do not** use the median with the standard deviation or the mean with the interquartile range

Example sentence:

“The mean daily temperature in Leeming is lower than in Heathrow and the spread of temperatures is greater than in Heathrow.”
