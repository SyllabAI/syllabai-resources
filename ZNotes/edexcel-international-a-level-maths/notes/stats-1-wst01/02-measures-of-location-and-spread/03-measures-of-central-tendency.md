## Measures of central tendency

### Definitions

* ### **Mode**: The value that occurs most frequently

* **Modal Class**: The class interval with the highest frequency. It is the class that occurs most often.

* **Median**: Middle value when data sorted; if n odd: middle observation; if n even: average of two middle observations.

* **Median Class**: The class interval containing the median item $\frac{n}{2}$ value) after sorting the values

  * $\frac{n}{2}$ = the total divided by 2

* **Mean**: The statistical average calculated by summing values divided by the total count. $\overline{X} = \frac{\sum X}{n}$

### Choosing the Best Measure of Center

* Mode: Qualitative (Non-numerical) data.

  * This is the only measure that works for words.

  * Simple numerical data with obvious repetitions.

    * **Example**: T-shirt sizes sold: S, M, M, L, M, XL $\rightarrow$ Best measure: mode = M

* Median: Numerical data with extreme values/ outlier

  * Due to outliers skewing the average, this is the only measure that would work

  * Used for quantitative data

    * Example: Staff salaries are 30k, 32k, 35k, 38k, and 300k $\rightarrow$ Best measure: median = 35k

* Mean: Numerical data to calculate for everyone (Affected by extreme values)

  * True mathematical summary

  * Used for quantitative data

    * Example: Heights of plants in a garden: 12 cm, 13 cm, 14 cm, 15 cm, 16 cm $\rightarrow$ Best measure: Mean = 14 cm (No extreme values to affect the average)
