## **Animal adaptations**

**Fast-flowing rivers**

* Tiny remains of plants and animals are washed down into streams and rivers, forming a food supply for any animals adapted to live there.

* The main problem is the current, which can carry the animals away.

* Many invertebrates have solved the problem by developing ways to hold onto the riverbed, presenting as small as a surface possible to the water rushing at them.

* Stonefly and mayfly nymphs have legs adapted to grip rocks.

* They have flat bodies and hold themselves close to the rocks, so the water flows over them.

* Leeches have suckers to hold onto bedrock.

* Freshwater limpets have a foot that acts as a sucker, and a streamlined shell that helps water to flow smoothly over it.

**Tree tops**

* Rainforest canopy is the habitat of many species of monkey.

* Monkeys have small, lightweight bodies that allow them to climb out to slender branches to collect food.

* They have opposable thumbs and a big toe, which allow them to grip the branches firmly.

* Eyes face forward, so that their visions overlap. Allows monkeys to judge distances accurately so that they land safely when leaping through trees.

* Tails help them keep balance as they run and jump.

* Prehensile tails act as a 5th limb, monkeys in South America can use it to grip onto objects or hang from branches.

New/invasive species:

Invasive species: non-native organism introduced to a new environment that causes ecological harm/

* New species may dominate the areas, outcompete native species, prey on native wildlife.

* To adapt, native predators may start eating the invasive species.

* Native species evolve traits to compete better.

* Develop immunity to new toxins introduced by invasive plants.
