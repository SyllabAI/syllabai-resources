## Invasive Species

* **Native species:** A species that lives naturally in a particular area.

* **Introduced species:** A species brought into an area where it does not naturally live, either accidentally or deliberately.

* **Invasive species:** An introduced species that spreads and causes harm to the environment, native species, or human activities. Not every introduced species becomes invasive.

## Effects of invasive species

Invasive species can affect an ecosystem in several ways:

1. **Competition:** They compete with native species for food, water, space, light, and other resources.

2. **Predation:** They may eat native species, reducing their populations.

3. **Spread of disease:** They may introduce diseases or parasites that harm native organisms.

4. **Habitat destruction:** They may damage or change habitats, making them unsuitable for native species.

5. **Reduced biodiversity:** Native populations may decline or become extinct, reducing the variety of species in an ecosystem.

6. **Disruption of food chains:** Changes in the populations of organisms can affect other organisms throughout the food web.

## Example of an invasive species

**Cane toad**

Cane toads were introduced into Australia to control agricultural pests such as cane beetle larvae.

* They became invasive and spread widely.

* They were poisonous to many native predators that attempted to eat them.

* This lead to decline in native predator populations, and disrupted food chains.

## How can invasive species be controlled?

- Monitor ecosystems to detect invasive species early.
- Remove invasive organisms from affected areas.
- Prevent their introduction by checking imported plants, animals, and goods.
- Use biological control carefully, such as introducing a natural enemy after assessing the risks.
