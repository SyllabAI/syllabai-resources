## **Plant adaptations**

**Mangrove swamp**

Occur along the coasts of many countries in tropical climates. Mud in which plants grow is moved by the rising and falling of the tides.

* Mangrove trees have adapted to the tides by growing out many roots from their trunks.

* Roots spread out over a wide area and dip down into the mud to hold the tree in place.

* Have seeds that are adapted to survive in a habitat of moving mud. The formed fruit remains attached to the tree.

* Seed from the fruit germinates using moisture in the humid air and the seedling grows 25cm before it leaves the tree.

* As the seedling falls, it remains vertical so that it the root can hold the plant in place when it hits the mud.

**Tropical rainforest**

* Thick forests, canopy of branches and leaves that shades the ground below.

* Seeds that fall to the ground struggle to germinate, seedlings produced can’t find enough light, and may die.

* Seeds of the **Strangler Fig** are capable are growing in the compost that develops in the forks of tree branches. This means the seed is nearer the canopy, it has a greater chance of surviving cause it receives enough light. Seedling initially uses water in the compost in the fork, but it needs more.

* The root grows down the side of the tree to find the soil on the ground.

* Once the root has reached, it can take up more water and nutrients and the rest of the plant can grow upward toward the canopy.

* Plant continues to send down more roots, they form a basket-like support around the tree.

* Water and minerals provided by the extra roots allow the plant to grow so large that its leaves overshadow the tree on which it’s growing.

* Over time, the roots develop such a rigid hold onto the tree trunk that the tissues beneath the bark, which transport food and water inside the tree - are crushed and the tree dies.

* The strangler fig continues to thrive and takes over the space originally occupied by the tree.

**Keystone species**

* Is a species in a habitat that helps the survival of a large number of other species that are adapted some way to benefit from its presence, often through feeding.

* An example is Strangler Fig, as it is fruit provides for hornbills, monkeys, parrots, pigeons and many insects. The fruit is often produced when other plants are not producing any fruit, so it helps to provide a constant supply to many herbivores.

* If the strangler fig was removed, a great many species would be extinct. When trees are removed for timber, they care must be taken to consider plants like the strangler fig and preserve them.

* This can be particularly difficult when the strangler figs attack the trees that loggers want to cut down.
