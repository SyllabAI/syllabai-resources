## **Movement of molecules** 

**Moving oxygen to the cells**

* Oxygen enters RBCs via the capillaries in the lungs, and then travel along the pulmonary vein to the heart, where the aorta distributes the oxygenated blood to the body. 

* The one-cell thick capillary walls maximise diffusion as it has a large surface area. 

**Moving carbon dioxide to the lungs** 

* CO2 is a product of aerobic respiration.

* It leaves the cells where it is produces and passes through the walls of the capillaries, into the plasma in the blood.

* It travels along the veins which take the blood back to the right side of the heart. 

* From here, the deoxygenated (CO2 containing) blood enters the pulmonary artery and travels to the lungs. 

* Carbon dioxide escapes through the capillary walls, into the air in the alveoli (refer Respiratory System notes) 

**Moving glucose to the cells** 

* Glucose contains the store of energy that is released during respiration. 

* Glucose passes through the walls of the small intestine and into blood plasma in the capillaries. 

* It travels in the plasma along the veins to the right side of the heart, then passes to the lungs where it picks up oxygen. 

* Then the blood passes into the left side of the heart, and into the aorta. 

* From here the body travels along arteries and takes the glucoses to the body again
