## Sexual intercourse

### Copulation

* Before sexual intercourse can take place, the penis must become erect.
* This happens by the action of a muscle at the base of the penis.
* The blood collects in the spongy tissue and makes it expand and harden.
* Prior to intercourse the vagina may also widen to each the passage of the penis into it
* The lining of the vagina may secrete fluid that acts as a lubricant and further helps the penis to enter the vagina.
* When the penis is inside the vagina both partners may make thrusting movements to stimulate the sensitive areas of the penis and clitoris
* This may give each partner a feeling of pleasure called an **orgasm.**
* When the male has an orgasm, it is accompanied by a contraction of the muscles in the **epididymis** and **sperm ducts** which propels the **semen** through the penis into the vagina.
* The action of releasing semen is called **ejaculation.**
* Volume of semen ejected is usually 3-5cm^3.

### Path of the sperm

* Semen ejaculated into the top of the vagina contains over 400 million sperm.
* Sperm cells do not contain a food store to provide them with energy for movement.
* They get nourishment from the seminal fluid which provides each sperm with energy to lash its tail like a whip - this movement drives the sperm forwards.
* Sperm travel through the cervix and up the mucus lining of the uterus wall and into the oviducts
* It takes about 4-6 hours to make their journey.
* Millions dies on the way due to the toxic environment of the uterus, which is unsuitable for sperm to live in. Some may die as they are not fast enough, or not strong enough to travel all the way up to the oviduct.
* Only a few thousands enter the oviduct, and even more die so that only a few hundred reach their destination.
* Sperm may survive for two or three days here before they die.
* During this time if the sperm meets an egg, fertilisation may occur.

### Movement of the egg

* The egg is much larger than a sperm cell because it contains its own food store.
* This is to provide energy and materials for the early development of the embryo upon fertilisation.
* Unlike the sperm, the egg has no means of propulsion.
* It is moved by the action of cilia in the walls of the oviduct.
* They wave to and fro to push the egg cell along.
