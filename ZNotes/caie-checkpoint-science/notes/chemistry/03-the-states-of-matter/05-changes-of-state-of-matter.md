## Changes of State of Matter

* States of matter can be changed physically and chemically
* Physical changes are reversible reactions as they can go forward and backward
* An example of a physical change of state is one brought about **by heating or cooling**.

### **Physical Reactions:**

### Melting

* When a solid is heated enough, it loses its shape and starts to flow. This change is called **melting** and the solid turns into a liquid.
* The temperature at which this takes place is called the **melting point.**
  * When the temperature remains constant or steady for a period of time, the melting point of the solid has been reached and all the heat energy is being used to convert the state of matter, therefore, the temperature of the liquid doesn’t rise.
* **The particle theory:**
  * When a solid is heated, the heat provides the particles with more energy. The energy makes the particles vibrate more strongly and push each other a little further apart.
  * If the solid is heated further, the energy makes the particles vibrate so strongly that they slide over each other and become a liquid.
* Example:
  * Butter melts in a pan during cooking and chocolate can melt in our pocket.

### Freezing

* It is the changing of a liquid into a solid.
* The temperature at which this takes place is called the **freezing point**
  * When the temperature remains constant or steady, the freezing point of the solid has been reached.
* **The particle theory:**
  * If a liquid is cooled sufficiently the particles lose so much energy that they can no longer slide over each other. The only movement possible is the vibration to and from about one position in the lattice.
* Example:
  * When molten wax runs down the side of the candle it freezes and becomes solid before it reaches the bottom.

### Evaporation

* The process by which a liquid changes into a gas over a range of temperatures is called **evaporation**.

* If the temperature of the liquid is higher, it evaporates faster

* If the air above the liquid does not already have a lot of vapour in it, the liquid evaporates faster.

  * The speed at which water evaporates into the air is measured as part of a weather survey.

* **The particle theory:**

  * The particles in a liquid have different amounts of energy. The particles with the most energy will move the fastest. High-energy liquid particles near the surface move so fast that they can break through the surface and escape into the air, forming a gas.

* Example:

  * A drop of water can turn into a gas (known as water vapor) at a room temperature of about 20°C while outside a puddle dries up in the warmth from the Sun.

### Boiling

* When a liquid reaches a certain temperature, it forms a gas inside it. The gas makes bubbles, which rise to the surface and burst into the air. The process is called **boiling.**

* The temperature at which it takes place is called the boiling point.

* If the boiling liquid is heated more strongly, its temperature does not rise but it boils more quickly.

* **The particle theory:**

  * When a liquid is heated, all the particles receive more energy and move more quickly. The fastest-moving particles escape from the liquid surface will form bubbles. The bubbles rise to the surface and burst open into the air. The fast-moving particles are released from the liquid to form a gas.

* Example:

  * Water boils at 100°C and turns into water vapor.

### Condensation

* If a gas is cooled down far enough, it turns into a liquid by a process called **condensation**
* **The particle theory:**
  * The particle in a gas possesses a large amount of energy, which they use to move. If the particles are cooled, they lose some of their energy and slow down. If the gas is cooled sufficiently, the particles lose so much energy that they can no longer bounce off each other when they meet. The particles now slide over each other and form a liquid.
* Example:
  * When water vapor that has evaporated from the sea rises high in the air, it cools and condenses on dust particles to form tiny water droplets (rain).

### Sublimation

* A few substances can change directly from a solid to a gas, or from a gas to a solid, without forming a liquid by **sublimation.**
* Example:
  * Sulphur is released as a gas by volcanoes and as it cools it sublimes to form solid sulphur around the opening of the volcano. This solid sulphur is sometimes called flowers of sulphur.
