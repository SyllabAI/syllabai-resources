## Uses of Neutralisation Reactions

### **Insect stings**

* A bee sting is acidic and may be neutralized by soap, which is an alkali.

* A wasp sting is alkaline and maybe neutralized with vinegar, which is a weak acid.

### **Curing Digestion**

* Sodium bicarbonate is used in some of the tables that are made to cure indigestion.

* Indigestion is caused by the stomach making too many acids as it digests food.

* When a tablet of sodium bicarbonates is swallowed, the chemical dissolves to make an alkaline solution, which neutralizes the acid in the stomach and cures indigestion.

### **Baking  A Cake**

* Baking powder contains a mixture of solid acid and sodium bicarbonate.

* When the baking powder is mixed with water and flour to make a cake, the acid, and the sodium bicarbonate dissolve in the water and take part in a neutralization reaction.

* The carbon dioxide gas forms bubbles in the mixture and makes it rise to give the cake a light mixture

### **A Model Volcano**

* To do this, you may have added a tablespoon of sodium bicarbonate, called baking soda, to an empty plastic drink bottle and then built a mound of sand around the bottle so that it looked like a conical volcano.

* Finally, you should add red dye to half a cup of vinegar, then poured the vinegar into the bottle.

* Moments later a red froth would have emerged from the top of the bottle and flowed down the cone sand, like lava flowing down a volcano.

### **Fighting A Fire**

* The soda-acid fire extinguisher contains a bottle of sulfuric acid and a solution of sodium bicarbonate.

* When the plunger is struck or the extinguisher is turned upside down, the acid mixes with the sodium bicarbonate solution, and a neutralization reaction takes place.

  * The pressure of the carbon dioxide produced in the reaction pushes the water out of the extinguisher and onto the fire
