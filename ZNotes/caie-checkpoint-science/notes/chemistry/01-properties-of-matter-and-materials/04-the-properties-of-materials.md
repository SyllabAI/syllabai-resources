## The Properties of Materials

There are numbers of properties that materials may have. Here is a list of these properties with some examples of materials that possess them.

**Surface appearance**

* You can see based on their appearance. Are they shiny, dull, rough, or smooth?

**Hard or soft flexibility**

* A soft material can be bent or squashed but when the pushing or pulling force is removed, it springs back to its original shape.
* A hard material wont be as flexible and resist deformation under pressure
  * Materials such as sponges feel soft because of their flexibility

**Rigid**

* A rigid material will resist deformation, especially bending, shearing, or compression.
  * Flexible pieces of wood have been used for thousands of years for hunting bows and they are still used today in some parts of the world.

**Malleable**

* A malleable material can be shaped by hammering or by pressing, without the material cracking. It stays in shape after the shaping process has ended.
  * Metals such as gold, silver, and copper are malleable. They can be made into wires and bent to form jewelry such as necklaces, bangles, and earrings.

**Brittle**

* A brittle material breaks suddenly if it is bent or hit.

  * You can snap a biscuit or a chocolate bar because it is brittle.

**Absorbent**

* An absorbent material has holes in its surface through which water can pass and also has spaces inside where the water can collect.

  * Absorbent clothes and papers are used to wipe up spills in the kitchen.

  * Some kinds of rocks, such as sandstone or limestone, are absorbent. There are gaps called **pores** between the rocky and grains and water can pass through them or fill them up to make an underground store of water called an aquifer

**Waterproof**

* A waterproof material does not let water pass through it.

* There are two kinds of waterproof material: water-resistant material and water repellent material.

  * #### Water-resistant material: Made of fibers. There are holes between the fibers through which water could pass. The fibers are coated in **silicones,** which make the water gather up into droplets that cannot pass through the material. These are used to make umbrellas, outdoor jackets and trousers.

  * Water- repellent material: A water repellent material **does not** have any holes in it through which water can pass. It is used to make wellington boots.

**Transparent**

* A transparent material is one that **lets light pass through it without the light rays being scattered**. This means that you can see objects clearly through transparent materials.

  * Examples include air, water, glass, plastic, etc.

**Translucent**

* Translucent material also **lets light pass through it but the light rays are scattered.** This means that you cannot see objects clearly.

  * Translucent glass is used in bathroom windows.

**Opaque**

* Opaque materials **do not** let light pass through them.

  * Most materials are opaque but one material that is used especially for its opaque property is curtain fabric.

  * Opaque materials are also useful in making sunshades such as parasols and canopies

**Heat conductor**

* A material that is a heat conductor **allows heat to pass** through it.

* The particles from which the materials are made pass heat energy along from one particle to the next

**Heat insulator**

* A material that is a heat insulator **does not let heat pass** through it.

* Its particles do not pass heat easily from one to the next. Good insulators are also known as bad conductors.

**Electrical conductor**

* A material that is an electrical conductor **allows an electric current of electricity to flow through it.**

**Electrical insulator**

* A material that is an electrical insulator **does not allow an electric current to flow through it.**
