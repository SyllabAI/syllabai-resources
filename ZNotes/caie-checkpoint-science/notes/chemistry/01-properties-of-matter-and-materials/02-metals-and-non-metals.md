## Metals and Non-metals

* As the number of elements are so large, scientists divide them into two groups: **metals and non-metals**
* The elements that are **metals** can often be recognized by just one of their properties: they **have a shiny surface**
* The elements that are non-metals do not shine. They have a dull surface.
  * Two examples of non-metals are:
    * **Carbon**, which you might see as charcoal in a barbecue
    * **Sulfur**, a yellow substance that is used to make a wide range of substances from car tires to medicines.

### How To Classify Elements?

* The properties that scientists use to classify elements into metals and non-metals are shown in the table below


|Property|Metal|Non-metal|
|:---|:---|:---|
|Surface|Shiny|Dull|
|Physical state at room temperature|Usually solid|Solid, liquid, or gas|
|Strength|Can be shaped by pressing and stretching without breaking easily|Solids are usually soft or brittle (hard but liable to break or shatter easily)|
|Melting point|Usually high|Usually low|
|Boiling point|Usually high|Usually low|
|Density|Usually high|Usually low|
|Conduction of heat|Good|Very poor|
|Conduction of electricity|Good|Very poor|

Nearly all metals and non-metals have the properties shown in the table above

* For instance, one property that *all* **metals** have is that they **conduct electricity**

***However, there are exceptions to the rules described in the table***

* Some metals **do not** have a shiny surface, they are dull
  * **Magnesium** is an example of a metal that has a dull surface
* There is **a non-metal** that has a shiny surface and looks metallic
  * **Iodine** is used with other substances in some portable water-purifying kits.
* One of the **non-metal** conducts electricity
  * **Graphite** is a form of carbon and is used to make pencils. It is a non-metal but it conducts electricity
