# Composition

## Details about Composition

This is the second part, ‘Part B’, of your paper 2 exam. You are provided with four questions, out of which you are supposed to choose one. You will be provided with two descriptives and two narratives, from which you can pick and choose in your best interest. One must remain within the required word limit, which is **350-450 words.**

The candidate should be able to successfully convey the following:

1. W1 articulates experience and expresses what is thought, felt, and imagined.

2. W2 organizes and structures ideas and opinions for deliberate effect.

3. W3 uses a range of vocabulary and sentence structures appropriate to the context.

4. W5 make accurate use of spelling, punctuation, and grammar.

A tip to write a descriptive and narrative is to imagine or create a place that you want and can easily describe in your mind. Something that you can already relate to. Always focus on the 5 main sensory details, which are **sight, smell, taste, touch, and sound.**

The five main sensory details are **sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch**:

* **Sight**: Colour, pattern, and texture affect our senses and can create emotions, which help depict the scene more vividly.

* **Sound**: Mentioning all the different sounds around will help you create a clearer image of what’s going on.

* **Smell**: You should mention different scents around you, such as the aroma of the fresh grass or the musky smell of the rain droplets. You should fully describe all the different smells around you to create a better picture.

* **Taste**: You could either mention this rhetorically or literally. For example, you could write, the bittersweet taste of failure, or the enriching flavour of the dessert had me drooling all around.

* **Touch**: Describing everything that you touch, for example, describing how you feel when you touch something, even if it is a doorknob, for example, shivers went down my spine as I twisted the worn-out, shabby doorknob, with its delicate design, all I felt was rustic.

By mentioning the five senses, one can create a picture-perfect essay. Describing these basic details will allow a writer to write more efficiently and a reader to imagine and relate to the story or the description more effortlessly!

Finally, once you have completed your essay, make sure to go over the following once more:

1. Look for any spelling or grammatical errors.

2. Make sure there is no repetition of any sort.

3. Estimate the word count of your essay by multiplying the number of lines by the average no of words in a line. There is no need to count all of the words: you don’t have to be precise.
