# Directed Writing

## Details about Directed Writing

Directed writing is the first question of the Paper 2 exam. You are provided with two texts - Text A and Text B, both of which are based on similar topics but with different views.

After you read and understand the question, you are expected to go through both the texts whilst highlighting everything you find important, to address the bullet points mentioned in the question. You are required to stay within a word limit, which is around **250-350 words**, with multiple paragraphs all addressing different points and conclusions from both the texts. It is crucial for one to make sure that there is no repetition of points or words; every paragraph should mention a new fact or inference. Every fact or inference you mention should be derived from the two texts. Alongside this, you must evaluate your points.

You will be asked to write a speech, a letter, or an article. When writing your response, you should only follow the format of paragraphing provided to you in the question, along with addressing the relevant person mentioned in the question.

This question consists of 25 marks.

Besides relevance to the question, you will be judged and critiqued on the following aspects:

* W1: Emanate subtle meanings.

* W2: The capability of an individual to structure their paragraphs appropriately.

* W3: Using a broad range of relevant vocabulary.

* W4: Using a set tone and purpose for your essay.

* W5: Making sure that there are no grammatical, spelling, or punctuation errors.

* W6: Being able to successfully evaluate and comprehend ideas, context, and opinions (R1-R3).

* W7: Forming and putting together a well-developed and refined response (R3-R5).

When writing your response to the question, you should make sure to follow the GAP rule.

**G** stands for the general genre of the essay.

**A** stands for the audience of your essay.

**P** stands for the purpose of your essay.

In addition to the **GAP** criteria, make sure you structure your answer properly, including an **introduction, main body, and conclusion.**

### Everything you should be able to do for the ‘Reading’ section of the exam:

* Demonstrate understanding of written texts, and of the words and phrases within them.

* Summarise and use material for a specific context.

* Develop, analyse, and evaluate facts, ideas, and opinions.

* Demonstrate understanding of how writers achieve their effects and influence readers.

* Select appropriate information for specific purposes.

* Recognise and respond to linguistic devices, figurative language, and imagery.

### Everything you should be able to do for the ‘Writing’ section of the exam:

* Express what is thought, felt, and imagined.

* Organise and convey facts, ideas, and opinions effectively.

* Demonstrate a varied vocabulary appropriate to the context.

* Demonstrate an effective use of sentence structures.

* Demonstrate an understanding of audience, purpose, and form.

* Demonstrate accuracy in spelling, punctuation, and grammar.
