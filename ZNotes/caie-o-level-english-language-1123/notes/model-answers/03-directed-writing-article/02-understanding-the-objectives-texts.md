## Understanding The Objectives & Texts

You can earn **up to 10 marks** on this task for how well you analyze and respond to both the implicit and explicit ideas and viewpoints provided in the two texts. Alongside mentioning points from both passages, you must also evaluate the points. Evaluation means to judge how strong or weak the points are and to add any extra details (not present in the texts) to strengthen the points. For example, if you are supporting Text A, you could strengthen your point by explaining its wider impact or adding a personal anecdotes (but do not do this in articles). If you disagree with Text B, then explain why its points are less convincing by giving evidence or reasoning against them. In the end, your essay should make clear which text you find more convincing, based on the arguments you’ve made in your body paragraphs.

**Question 1**

Write a magazine article aimed at young readers discussing the pros and cons of visiting foreign countries.

In your article, you should:

* Examine and respond to the views and arguments presented in both reading texts.

* Share your own perspective on the benefits and drawbacks of travelling abroad, using ideas from the texts as a basis.

Be sure to use your own words when referring to the information in the passages. Cover both points clearly.

Start your article with an appropriate headline.
Your response should be around **250–350 words** long.
Up to **10 marks** will be given for content, and up to **15 marks** for the quality of your writing.

---

Your response to the above question must be based upon the following texts:

### TEXT A: Amira’s thoughts (42 yrs old)

I’ve never really understood why people are so eager to go abroad when there’s already so much to discover in their own country. Most of us barely know our homeland, yet we rush to jump on fuel-hungry planes and fly thousands of miles for holidays in distant places. And what happens when we get there? We pay absurd entry fees for overcrowded tourist spots and end up being overcharged in restaurants, paying double what locals do for the same meal.

I’m a firm supporter of the staycation – taking your holiday closer to home. What better way to learn about your country’s history and traditions than by exploring them yourself? It’s environmentally friendly too, especially if you avoid using a car and instead rely on public transport, bikes, or even your own two feet. If money’s tight, you can stay with friends or relatives.

We get bored so easily and stop appreciating where we live, but think of all the fascinating and beautiful places nearby that you’ve never visited. How many of us can honestly say we truly know our own country? A holiday spent visiting historical sites and important landmarks can help you better understand your own culture. So go for it: enjoy a fantastic break and cut your carbon footprint at the same time.

---

### TEXT B: Li Ping’s thoughts (23 yrs old)

It’s natural for people to want to explore and experience new things. For young people especially, travelling abroad is a powerful way to discover how diverse and fascinating the world can be. It’s also a form of education: you can practise foreign languages, encounter different cultures and ways of thinking, and taste unfamiliar, exciting foods. You have to be bold and leave your comfort zone behind!

After I finished my A Levels, I went travelling myself, and I can honestly say it transformed me. I had very little money to spend, but I still got the chance to do incredible things and visit places of stunning beauty. Along the way, I made many new friends from different countries. We learned a great deal from one another, and I realised that we shared far more in common than I’d imagined.

Travelling also opened my eyes to how obsessed I’d been with having the “right” possessions when, in truth, my needs were actually quite basic. Now, I choose to spend my money on experiences rather than material things. Travelling taught me that you don’t need to pay to watch a sunset, enjoy good company, or walk through a forest, and that you can manage just fine with only what fits in your backpack. It helped me mature and become a much kinder person too. I’d encourage every young person to get out there and explore the world – it’s not as big as it seems!
