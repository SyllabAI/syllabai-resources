## Implicit Questions

### **What are Implicit Questions?**

Implicit questions require candidates to "read between the lines" and make inferences based on the given text. The answer won't be directly stated; however, enough textual evidence will be provided for you to reach accurate conclusions.

Many candidates find these questions more challenging, so to simplify, think about **what the writer is trying to say** rather than what they said.

### **How to attempt these questions?**

- **Read the text carefully** and understand the explicit meaning first before answering the question. 
- After identifying the explicit meaning, **think of its different connotations.**
- Analyse and evaluate the explicit meaning to **develop your thoughts and ideas** about it.
- **Support your answer with evidence from the text** to ensure you reach relevant conclusions.
- **Be selective of the information you pick up** from the text as there is already limited space to write your answer.
- Again, **spend time on questions according to their marks** eg; if it's of 2 marks spend a maximum of 2 minutes on it.
- If any question is challenging or unclear, skip it and come back to it later.
- If the line numbers are mentioned and you still can't figure it out, read the lines before and after it to get the context of that line.
- If the question asks you to write in *your own words*, you **have to paraphrase and use synonyms.**
- When words like **"Describe"**, **"Evaluate"**, **"Explain"** are stated in the question, it's an implicit question.

### **What not to do**

- **Do not copy-paste your answer** from the text.
- When you are told to write in your own words, **never use the words already used in the text.**
- Try not to overwrite and give limited, necessary detail.
