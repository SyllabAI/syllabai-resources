# Speaking (Optional Component)

Speaking is a part of the Lower Secondary Checkpoint English as a Second Language curriculum framework. However, it is not assessed formally in the Checkpoint test at the end of Stage 9 (Grade/Year 8/9).

Your school may test you in this component internally in the form of a speaking test, and if it’s tested, here’s what you need to do to do well in the test.

You should be able to:

* Use both formal and informal language during a talk

* Speak English fluently and accurately, especially during social interactions with other students your age.

* Explain and justify your own or other’s points of view

* Recognize language mistakes that may be made during a talk, and modify them so that it’s correct

* Use a range of appropriate vocabulary during a talk.

It is essential that you should be able to speak with some fluency and accuracy on a range of both general and curricular (subject-related) topics to do well in Checkpoint and beyond at IGCSE level.

During the test, you may be asked to speak about a particular topic assigned to you by the examiner. Here, it will be helpful if you already have experience talking about a range of general topics, as this will help you do well in the test.
