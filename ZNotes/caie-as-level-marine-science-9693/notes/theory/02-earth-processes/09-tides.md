## Tides

* **Tides** → The regular rise and fall of bodies of water as dictated by the gravitational interactions of the Moon, Earth, and Sun.

* Most coastal areas have tides with an interval of 12.5 hours, creating two high tides and two low tides each day.

  * Tidal patterns like this are called semi-diurnal, and the tides are easy to predict.

  * Areas with only one high tide and one low tide each day have a diurnal tidal pattern.

* **Tidal Range** →The difference between the low-water mark and the high-water mark on a coastline.

  * It has many variance due to:

    * Gravitational effects of the Moon, sun, and Earth.

    * Physical features of the coastline where the tide is occurring.

    * If the tidal is entering a narrow channel, the tidal height is increased because the water is being forced into a smaller area.

    * But if it happens along an open beach, the tidal height is much shorter because the same volume of water is more spread out.

    * Environmental factors such as wind, air pressure, and size of the body of water.

    * During a tropical cyclone, the air pressure lowers which allows the body of water to swell.

    * Then there would be wind onshore which pushes more water and increases its height.

* **Spring Tides** → the greatest tidal range for coasts 

  * where the Moon, Sun, and Earth are aligned in a straight line, 

  * amplifying the gravitational effects the Moon and Sun has on the Earth, 

  * creating a larger than average ocean bulge.

  * The result is the highest of high tide and the lowest of low tide.

* **Neap tides** → the smallest tidal range 

  * where the Moon and Sun are perpendicular to Earth, 

  * causing opposing gravitational pull the Moon and Sun has on the Earth, 

  * creating a smaller than average ocean bulge.
