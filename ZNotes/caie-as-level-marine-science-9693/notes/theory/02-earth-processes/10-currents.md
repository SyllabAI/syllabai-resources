## Currents

* **Currents** → Continuous directional movement of seawater.

  * They carry nutrients, dissolved gases, heat, and even organisms with them.

  * Currents are created by different physical forces acting on the water, such as wind, temperature, salinity, density, coriolis effect, and the shape of the seabed.

  * Two major types of currents are Surface currents and Deep currents (Global Conveyor Belt)

* **Surface Currents**

  * Typically driven by the wind, they are steady and dependable as a result of global wind patterns caused by an uneven heating of the Earth’s surface by the Sun.

  * Areas with large amounts of solar radiation (equator) have excess heat energy in the air, causing the air to rise in the atmosphere.

  * As the air rises, it begins to transfer away some of its heat energy until it begins to sink in areas with less radiation and cooler temperatures.

  * This movement of air in convection current forms predictable winds, leading to constant surface sea currents at different latitudes.

  * In the Northern hemisphere, the currents tend to have a clockwise spiral while in the Southern hemisphere, the currents tend to have a counter-clockwise spiral.

  * These patterns are caused by the Earth’s rotation.

* **Deep Currents (Global Conveyor Belt)**

  * Currents driven by differences in density caused by salinity and temperature (thermohaline circulation).

  * These currents happen along the ocean floor and cannot be detected by satellite imagery.

* **Formation of deep surrents**

  * In the arctic ocean, water is colder so it is more dense, thus it sinks, pushing water along it.

  * After that, the current moves North from Antarctica through the Indian and Pacific ocean.

  * As water moves through the equator, it becomes warmer and rises, causing upwelling.

  * Then warmer surface water from the equator moves to take its place.

  * Afterwards, deep currents move south through the equator towards the Southern Ocean.

  * It is important to remember a circumpolar current is moving around Antarctica.

  * Additionally, the warm deep currents then move through the Atlantic, passing the gulf of Mexico.

  * Then it cools and eventually moves back south towards Antarctica.

  * This process is called thermohaline circulation.

* **Importance of deep currents**

  * Allows mixing of seawater, providing nutrients all throughout

  * Allows transport of sediments

  * Regulates Earth’s climate by moving heat energy from the equator to poles.
