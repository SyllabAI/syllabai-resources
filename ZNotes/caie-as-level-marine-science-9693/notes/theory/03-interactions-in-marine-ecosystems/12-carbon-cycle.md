## The Carbon Cycle 

* Carbon is needed by all living things because it is the basis of all organic materials. Carbon enters the biotic phase of the cycle through the fixation of carbon dioxide in photosynthesis and is then released through respiration by all living things.

* The main way CO2 enters the ocean is by dissolving from the atmosphere and it can actually cause ocean acidification if in excess since it can make carbonic acid. Another thing that would happen is also algal bloom is productivity is too high.

* Photosynthesis produces glucose which can be used to make other compounds such as starch as biomass. When eaten, they would assimilate the biomass of the eaten to their own. The process is repeated throughout the food chain.

* When the organisms die, some of the organic matter is broken down by decomposers and returns to the water as dissolved inorganic carbon, but if it isn’t broken down, it falls to the ocean floor as marine snow. This marine snow can then build up, and after high pressure and temperature, it will become sedimentary rock, removing carbon away from the carbon cycle. These rocks can then end up as land mass through  marine uplift, and once exposed, it will get eroded and weathered, giving carbon dioxide back to the carbon cycle.

* Formation of fossil fuels also removes carbon from the carbon cycle as they are made up of living things corpses that are compressed under high pressure and temp after millions of years. This carbon can be given back to the carbon cycle when it gets burned and goes through combustion.

* The ocean is very important for climate control as it is a carbon sink (absorbs more cO2 than giving it out). This does come with risks as again, they are vulnerable to becoming acidic and even triggering algal bloom. If the ocean becomes acidified, corals would struggle to produce their calcium carbonate skeleton or even animals with shells would struggle too. This acid can also erode the skeletons and shells.
