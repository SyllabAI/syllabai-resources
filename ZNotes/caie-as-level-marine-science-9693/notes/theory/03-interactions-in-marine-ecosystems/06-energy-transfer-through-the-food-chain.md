## Energy Transfer Through The Food Chain

* Energy is sourced from the sun’s radiation where only some of it is fixed by the Earth’s producers due to most of the radiation being reflected. 

* In the sea, some of the sun radiation is absorbed, reflected, or scattered by water. The remainder that was absorbed will be used by plants but only some wavelength as the green wavelength is reflected, giving the plant its green colour because the *chlorophyll* only absorbs blue and red light.

* While photosynthesizing, some of the glucose is used by the plant to respire so only the leftover can go to the next trophic level. And additionally, not all parts of the plant are eaten, so the primary consumer (secondary production) overall only gets 10% of the previous energy, and so on the next trophic level only gets 10% energy of the primary consumer. After that, the matter that is left such as corpses are then broken down by decomposers such as bacterias and fungi to obtain the nutrients they need where they’d release it back to the environment.

* *Secondary production* depends on:

  * The biomass available in the producers

  * The amount of energy lost through respiration by the consumers

  * The amount of energy lost in waste products such as urine.

* What’s available to be passed on to the next trophic level is only around 10%, varying depending on:

  * How much of the food is eaten

  * how easy it is  for the consumer to digest and assimilate the nutrients

  * How much energy is used for movement

  * How much is lost in the waste products of metabolism
