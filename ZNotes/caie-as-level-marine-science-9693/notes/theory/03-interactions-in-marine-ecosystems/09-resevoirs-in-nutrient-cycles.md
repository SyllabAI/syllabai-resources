
## Resevoirs In Nutrient Cycles

* The ocean is an important reservoir for many elements which has residence time where the particle has an average time of stay in the system. Usually it is long, especially at the bottom of the ocean since the ocean has a lot of sedimentation. But on the surface it spends a shorter time due to high productivity.

### Processes that Replenish Nutrient Reservoirs

5 main processes that add nutrients to replenish reservoir within the surface layer within the surface layer:

* Dissolving in the Water from the Atmosphere

  * Nitrogen (N2) and Carbon dioxide (CO2) are both present in the atmosphere and therefore both able to dissolve directly into water. The amounts that are dissolved rely on factors such as:

    * Temperature of water

    * Atmospheric concentration of each gas

    * Amount of mixing of water at the surface

* Excretion and Decomposition

  * Excretion → Removal of waste produced by the chemical reactions which take place within living things like respiration (removal of CO2) and urine (removal of N2 in the form of urea)

  * Decomposition →Breaking down of dead organic matter and excretion products to return everything back on to the surface to be used as nutrients.

* Upwelling

  * → Movement of cold, nutrient-rich water from the seabed vertically to the ocean’s surface.

* Run-Off

  * Part of the water cycle where it can carry extra nutrients when it has high concentration (it carries fertilisers or sewage and even just solvable mineral ions from where it passed) or extra water when it has low concentration. Basically if the infiltration rate during the water cycle is high, the lower the rate of surface run-off will be. So the more impermeable the ground is, the more surface run-off there is.

* Tectonic Processes

  * The first way is through hydrothermal vents where the superheated water meets the near-freezing water on the ocean floor, and it begins to cool. As the water cools, minerals including ions of copper, sulfides, zinc, and iron, precipitate out of the solution, forming solids, settling near the fissure, piling on top of each other due to their lack of solubility. This forms a chimney. Then a process called chemosynthesis takes place, where carbon dioxide is turned into usable organic molecules using the energy stored in dissolved chemicals such as hydrogen sulfide by chemoautotrophs. Chemicals dissolve in heated water under sea crust as it makes its way back to the surface to  emerge from the vents. 

  * Or by erosion and weathering where the release of magma from volcanoes or formation of mountains adds fresh rock which can then be eroded and weathered, allowing soluble nutrients to dissolve in rainwater and run-off into water sources. Additionally, ash from eruptions also add nutrients.

### Processes that Removes Nutrient From The Surface Layer

* Nutrients are removed from the surface layer through uptake and assimilation by producers which have fixed inorganic ions to useable organic compounds that are fed on by consumers. When this nutrient has entered the food chain, it can take different paths that removes them from surface layer such as:

  * Marine snow

    * → Particles of organic matter that fall from the surface of the ocean to deeper water where most of it isn't eaten and stays as sediments unless erosion or dissolving helps.

  * Harvesting

    * → Removal of marine species by humans but eventually they find their way back to the ocean like for example from run-offs.
