## Effect of Temperature on Density

![DE-DE KRISHNA BLOG: HALOCLINE, THERMOCLINE DAN PYCNOCLINE](https://3.bp.blogspot.com/-GxvFinbVoz8/VwcXnqOVFdI/AAAAAAAAAac/8YTVUQ33InozmJU4IYIZwMs896AZwmdIQ/s1600/THERMOCLINE.png =424x)

* As the temperature of seawater increases, density decreases.

* Warmer water tends to float near the surface, exposed to the sun,

  * causing that layer to be even warmer and less dense.

* Between these layers, there’s an area of abrupt temperature change, known as the thermocline.

* Water at the surface may reach 25 C or higher in tropical seas but is more likely to be 1 C at depths of 2000m or more.

* In polar seas, the temperature gradient in a thermocline is less drastic, close to freezing and remains fairly constant.

* As salinity increases, the freezing point of water decreases → harder to make ice.

* When water begins to freeze, the individual water molecules begin to arrange into a lattice pattern because of H-Bonds holding them together.

* These bonds hold them at a greater distance, reducing density, making it float on liquid water.

* So, even if the water  freezes at the bottom of the ocean, it would float anyway.

### **H-bond Importance**

* Allows ice to float thus:

  * Prevents the whole ocean from freezing.

  * Acts as a thermal insulator to prevent water beneath from freezing, reducing the rate of further heat loss.

  * Provides habitat for marine organisms on the top of the ice.

  * Provides habitat for marine organisms under the iceberg.
