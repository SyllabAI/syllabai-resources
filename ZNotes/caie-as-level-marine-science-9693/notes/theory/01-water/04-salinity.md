## Salinity

* Salinity is a measure of the concentration of dissolved salts in seawater under the units of parts per thousands (ppt or ‰).

* The average salinity of the open ocean is 35 ppt, but the actual salinity at any given location varies locally due to the water cycle.

* Factors affecting salinity:

  * ++Precipitation++: Lowers the salinity of a body of water as it adds fresh water, thus diluting the seawater.

  * ++Run-off++: These run-offs may contain water with higher or lower salinity than the ocean, thus can either decrease or increase salinity.

  * ++Evaporation++: Increases the salinity of a body of water as water gets taken out of it while the salts are left behind.

  * ++Freezing++: Increases the salinity of a body of water as water gets frozen while the salts are left behind.

  * ++Melting++: Decreases the salinity of a body of water as water gets added from the ice.

* Hypersaline is when a body of water is very concentrated with salt/it has high salinity

### **Mixing Of The Layers**

* The best mixed area of the ocean is from 0 m to around 200 m deep (surface layer).

* This is because wind blows across the surface of the ocean, creating currents and turbulence, making the surface layer fairly uniform in temperature and salinity.

* Mixing of the layers within the ocean is density driven,

* because surface water would cool down and become denser then it’d sink with all its nutrients and dissolve gasses.

* This process can happen in cold oceans also due to salinity levels changing density.

* With this, they created halocline and thermocline which are considered areas of mixing where significant changes in the abiotic factors of the ocean happen.

* while the deep ocean has oxygen and other gasses, but they are limited.

**The pH scale**

* pH → Potential hydrogen (used to measure conc of hydrogen ions in water)

* Important abiotic factor for the survival of aquatic organisms in a marine environment.

* Hydrogen ions are spontaneously created in pure water as they split to form H\+ (hydroxide ion) and OH- (hydroxide ion).

* acidic : High conc of H\+ ions, in the scale it is below 7.0!

* alkaline : High conc of OH- ions, in the scale it is over 7.0. It is also known as basic

* neutral : It is pure water, in the scale is 7.0

* pH can be measured using: Litmus indicator, Universal indicator, and pH probes.

* pH scale isn’t linear, it is logarithmic, where a 0.1 decrease actually indicates 25% rise in acidity, not 1%.

* The ocean is slightly alkaline at an average of 8.2,

* but recently it has dropped to an average of 8.1 due to increasing levels of CO2 in our atmosphere, bringing drastic issues to the environment.
