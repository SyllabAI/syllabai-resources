# Roots of Polynomial Equations

## Quadratics

Let there be a **quadratic** with roots, **$\alpha$** and $\beta$, such that:

$$

ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)
$$

**Dividing** both sides by **a** and then **expanding** the right-hand side gives us:

$$

x^2 + \frac{bx}{a} + \frac{c}{a} = x^2 -(\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha \beta
$$

Upon **comparing coefficients,** we can see that:

$$

\text{Sum of roots} = \sum \alpha = \alpha + \beta = \frac{-b}{a}
$$

$$
\text{Product of roots} = \sum \alpha\beta = \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}
$$

Thus, a **quadratic** can also be written as:

$$

x^2 - \left(\sum \alpha \right)x + \left(\sum \alpha\beta \right)
$$
