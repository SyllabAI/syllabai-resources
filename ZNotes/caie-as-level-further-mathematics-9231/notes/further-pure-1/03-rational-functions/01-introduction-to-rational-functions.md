# Rational functions

## Introduction to Rational Functions

A rational function is a function written in the form.

$$

y = \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} 
$$

The most prominent rational function that we shall be working with is

$$

y = \frac{1}{x}
$$

**Observations:**

* The graph has an asymptote at $x = 0$ (green line). This is because we cannot have a rational fraction, $\frac{p}{q}$, where $q \ \cancel{=} \ 0$. Instead, we take the limit.

$$

\lim_{x \ \rightarrow \ 0} \quad \frac{1}{x} 
$$

As $x$ approaches $0$, the value of $\frac{1}{x}$ will become bigger and bigger (but never reach infinity). Thus, the function approaches infinity ($\infty$) and has an asymptote at $x = 0$.

$$
\phantom{this text is invisible but adds a line}
$$

* The graph also has an asymptote at $y = 0$ (blue line). This is because there are no solutions to $0 = \frac{1}{x}$. We can also take the limit.

$$

\lim_{x \ \rightarrow \ \infty} \quad \frac{1}{x}
$$

As $x$ approaches infinity ($\infty$), the value of x will become smaller and smaller (but never reach 0). Thus, the function approaches $0$ and has an asymptote at $y = 0$.

$$
\phantom{this text is invisible but adds a line}
$$

* There are no intersection points with the x and y axis
* There are no turning points, as $\frac{d}{dx} \frac{1}{x} = 0$ has no solutions. The function is infinitely decreasing from $(0, +\infty)$ and infinitely decreasing from $(-\infty, 0)$
