## Square Root of a Function

The graph of $y^2 =f(x)$ can be thought of as 2 graphs, $y = +\sqrt{f(x)}$ and $y = -\sqrt{f(x)}$ .

To sketch this graph:

* First sketch $y = +\sqrt{f(x)}$, and then reflect it in the $x$ axis to get $y = -\sqrt{f(x)}$ . This completes the sketch for $y^2 = f(x)$
* The $x$-axis acts as a line of symmetry for the graph of $y^2 = f(x)$

It is also helpful to understand this relation:

| Points/lines on $f(x)$ | Points/lines on $y^2 = f(x)$ |
|----|----|
| If $y > 0$. Example:$\ y =16$ | Points are **pulled** **down** towards the $x$-axis. Example:$\ y^2 = 16 \rightarrow y = \sqrt{16} = 4$ |
| If $0 < y < 1$. Example:$\ y = \frac{1}{4}$ | Points are **pulled** **up** from the $x$-axis. Example:$\ y^2 = \frac{1}{4} \rightarrow y = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}} = \frac{1}{2}$ |
| If $y = 1$ | The points stay the same. Example: $y^2 = 1 \rightarrow y = \sqrt{1} = 1$ |
| When $y = 0$ | The points stay the same. |
| Vertical asymptote $x = a$ | Vertical asymptote $x = a$, it stays the same. |
| Horizontal asymptote $y = b$ | Horizontal asymptotes $y = \sqrt{b}$ and $y = -\sqrt{b}$. |
| Nature of turning point | Same nature of turning point. |
| Coordinates of turning point $(a, b)$ | Coordinates of turning points become $(a, \sqrt{b})$. |

y = f(x) = \frac{4x^2 - 1}{x^2 - 4}

To draw the sketch of $y^2 = f(x)$, we first note that the horizontal asymptote becomes $y = 2$ and $y = -2$. The vertical asymptotes stay the same. Next we can sketch the graph of $y = +\sqrt{f(x)}$ and reflect it in the $x$-axis.

$$
