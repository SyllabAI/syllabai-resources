## Parametric Polar Form

The parametric form for a polar curve gives us 2 equations to express the coordinates of a curve in terms of a single parameter, in this case $\theta$.

They can be useful in the following ways:

* Finding the horizontal and vertical asymptotes, if a polar curve has any.
* Finding the maximum horizontal and vertical distance.

Given a function, $r = f(\theta)$, the parametric polar form can be expressed as:

* $x = f(\theta)cos(\theta)$
* $y = f(\theta)sin(\theta)$


---

### <center>Example:</center>

Consider $r = 1 + sin(\theta)$, which is in the form $r = f(\theta)$.

### <center>Solution:</center>

This can be rewritten as:

$y = (1 + sin(\theta))(sin(\theta)) \implies y = sin(\theta) + sin^2(\theta)$

$x = (1 + sin(\theta))(cos(\theta)) \implies x = cos(\theta) + sin(\theta)cos(\theta)$
