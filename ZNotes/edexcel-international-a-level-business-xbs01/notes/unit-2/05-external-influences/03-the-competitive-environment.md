## The Competitive Environment

### **Effects of competition**

* **Numbers:**

  * The level of competition depends on the number of rivals in a market/group.

* **Price:**

  * As the number of rivals increase, the business losses control over pricing

  * Charging high prices often leads to a loss of customers unless the business has a unique selling point (USP).

* **Profit:**

  * Profit margins decline as competition intensifies, but firms can protect or expand profit margins by increasing efficiency to lower costs.

* **Communication with consumers:**

  * Competitive markets encourage businesses to engage more in market research and customer interaction to better meet customer needs.

* **Innovation:**

  * Innovation is encouraged in competitive markets to gain a competitive edge in the market by offering new or improved products.

* **Size:**

  * Large businesses can be difficult to compete with as they have the resources to dominate stakeholders, take risks, recruit best staff, change prices, and influence political decisions.

  * Control over prices will also be low if the firm is large

  * Large firms enjoy high profits and profit margins. Smaller firms cannot do the same.

  * Smaller firms can communicate with customers more effectively (personalised service and interaction), but large firms may use social media.

  * Small firms may try to be more innovative as they are more flexible and can change in response to a change in consumer tastes.

* **Behaviour:**

  * Most consumers are sensitive to prices and will switch to cheaper products, assuming the qualities are the same.

  * Businesses monitor the activities of their rivals, like marketing, to respond in order to regain any lost sales due to the competitor's successful campaigns.

  * Effective product differentiation can pressure rivals to develop a USP or lead to a gain in market share.

  * Improving versions of an existing product can lead to an increase in consumers and sales; hence, firms risk losing market share if they fail to innovate.

  * In some countries, firms may work together and act like a monopoly. Two firms form a cartel and fix prices or share out the market (collusion). Some countries prohibit this.

  * Some markets have low barriers or high barriers, while some firms may create barriers using brand loyalty.

### **Ways for a small firm to compete:**

* **Developing a market niche:** firms meet the needs of a smaller group of customers and avoid competition to survive and charge premium prices.

* **Flexibility:** Due to direct involvement of the owner, Small firms can quickly react to market changes.

* **Personal service:** owners may take a personal interest in consumers to develop a strong relationship. Many buyers would enjoy this service and would pay a higher price.

* **Lower wages:** many workers in small firms do not have access to trade unions which limits their bargaining power and allows the owner to pay the minimum wage. Alternatively, some workers may accept lower pay in recognition tothe firm's limited resources.

* **Better communication:** With fewer employees, communication is open and informal (compared to large firms). This increases the speed of decision-making.

* **Innovation:** Small firms, though resource-constrained, often innovate due to competitive pressure. Their small scale also means that they have less to lose compared to large corporations.
