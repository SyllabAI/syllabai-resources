## Cashflow

* **Cashflow** lists all likely cash inflows and cash outflows over a given period.

* **Cash Solvency** is the degree to which a firm can meet its debts when they fall due.

### **Components of the Cashflow**

* **Net cash flow:** Difference between inflows and outflows for a month.

  * Net cash flow = Total Cash Inflows - Total Cash Outflows.

* **Opening balance:** the amount of cash that a business has at the start of each month.

* **Closing balance:** the amount of cash that a business expects to have at the end of each month.

  * Closing balance = Net Cash Flow + Opening Balance.

### **Use of Cashflow Forecast**

* **Identifying the timing of cash flow shortage and surplus:** The cash balance helps show if and when a bank loan will be needed. Cash flows are important for businesses with seasonal demand, as their cash flows would be irregular.

* **Supporting finance applications:** Lenders insist on seeing documents of performance, outlook, and solvency. Important as it indicates outlook and is useful in planning stages, to convince investors.

* **Planning:** Helps clarify and improve performance. Planning can help reduce mistakes and identify problems.

* **Monitoring cash flow:** Businesses should make a comparison between the figures in the cash flow forecast and those that occurred. This can help identify where problems arose if there was a large difference between the 2 sets.

### **Limitations of Cashflow Forecast**

* Financial information used in the forecast will be based on estimates.

* Difficult to estimate the future cost

* If figures for in-flow and out-flow are inaccurate, the closing balance will be unreliable.

* Business activity is subject to external forces beyond the control of the business and managers.

* Records used in preparing a cash flow. Only focuses on one variable - cash, hence it cannot be used on its own to evaluate business performance.
