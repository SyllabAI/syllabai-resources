## Sales Forecasting

* **Forecasting** is a business process that assesses the probable outcome using assumptions about the future. This can be based on a variety of data, e.g., information from managers.

* **Time series analysis** is predicting future levels from past data. The data used is called time series data. Time series data set of figures arranged based on time of occurrence. This does not explain the cause but estimates what will happen from it.

  * **Trend:** can help identify a pattern from figures

  * **Seasonal fluctuations:** in sales may be larger at particular periods.

  * **Cyclical fluctuations:** periods of highs and lows in sales figures over the years

  * **Random fluctuations:** surprising, unusual figures that stand out from trends.

### **Benefits of a Sales Forecast**

* Help manage finances, as inflows can be predicted

* Plan orders of supplies and components

* Ensure that it has the correct staff levels for predicted sales

* Ensure that the business can meet predicted orders.

* Helps decide if additional equipment is needed or if there is a need to rent/buy additional premises.

### Factors affecting Sales Forecasting

* **Consumer trends:** they are the changing habits and behaviour of consumers. Firms try to anticipate changes in consumer needs to switch production to the product in demand. These changes can be long-term or short-term.

* **Seasonal variations:** knowing seasonal fluctuations allows a business to recognise periods of low sales/revenue. Strategies can be implemented to manage the periods of low cash flow. Time series data is used to identify seasonal fluctuations.

* **Fashion:** this criterion changes frequently, particularly in the clothing sector. A change in fashion leads to a modification in the sales forecast. Unpredictability is a feature in some industries.

* **Long-term trends:** Some changes could be long-term due to factors like increasing population, increased incomes, etc.

### **Economic Variables**

* **Economic growth:** measured using GDP, which is a measure of the total output produced. Generally, customer incomes tend to rise, which leads to an increase in spending. As economic growth increases, sales forecasted will also increase.

* **Interest rates:** When interest rates are high, the cost of loans is high, and demand for loans falls. These loans fund purchases. Therefore, a downward sales forecast is expected.

* **Inflation:** the general rise in consumer prices, which indicates that prices in the economy are rising as well. Firms and consumers often spend less, which implies that sales forecasts are reduced.

* **Unemployment:** the number of people out of work. This leads to reduced sales forecasted as people tend to spend less during times of high unemployment.

* **Exchange rates:** value of one currency in terms of another. If the exchange rate depreciates, sales forecasted increase. The reverse occurs if the exchange rate appreciates.

* **Change in income:** a rise in demand for normal goods if income rises. This leads to an increase in sales forecasted.

* **Actions of competitors:** sales forecast may have to be adjusted downwards if competitors begin attracting more consumers due to a strategy that leads to an increase in market share. The short-term strategy may only have an impact for a few months.

### **Difficulties of Sales Forecasting**

* **Volatile consumer tastes and preferences**

  * Extrapolation is forecasting future trends based on past data.

  * This is a reasonable base to make decisions from, but it is not the best for future predictions.

* **Range of data**

  * May need data like own sales data, wider economic data, unemployment, income growth, commodity prices, exchange rates, etc.

  * The difficulty of accurate sales forecasting lies in the sheer amount of data that exists, which might inform the forecast.

  * The reliability of data determines the reliability of the sales forecast

* **Subjective expert opinion**

  * Final decisions around sales forecasts are made by business experts like sales analysts. They will judge on their deductions and knowledge of the market and wider economic variables.

  * These opinions are subjective and can be wrong.
