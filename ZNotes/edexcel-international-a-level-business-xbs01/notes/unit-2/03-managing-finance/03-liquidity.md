## Liquidity

* **Liquidity** refers to the ease with which assets can be converted to cash.

### **Balance sheet**

* It is used to show a company's financial position. This is given at the end of the year and has a summary of a firm's assets, liabilities, and capital.

* **Current assets**

  * Assets that will be changed to cash in 1 year. They are liquid assets (can easily be converted to cash). Examples include inventories, trade and other receivables, cash, or its equivalent.

* **Current liabilities**

  * Sums owed by a business to be repaid in 1 year. Examples include loans, trade (credit), and other payable items.

* **Non-current liabilities**

  * It is long-term loans and other sums that don't have to be repaid within a year.

* **Intangible assets**

  * Customer lists, franchising agreements, brand names, etc., are also included as intangible assets.

* **Net assets**

  * Subtracting total liabilities from assets. This will be equal to shareholders’ equity.

* **Shareholders’ equity**

  * A summary of what is owed to the owners of the business. Examples are share capital and retained profit.

### **Liquidity ratios**

$$

\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}

$$

* 1:1 implies that for selling, the inventory is held.

* 2:1 implies that cash is held unproductively.

$$

\text{Acid-Test Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventory}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}

$$

* 2:1 implies that current assets - inventories cannot cover current liabilities.

* 1:1 implies that current assets - inventories can cover current liabilities.

* Businesses with a strong cash flow may operate comfortably with a ratio less than 1.

* There is no guarantee that inventories will be sold, and they may be obsolete or deteriorate, which makes this ratio more reliable

### **Improving liquidity**

* Sale of assets/leaseback: unwanted physical assets may be sold. Alternatively, a leaseback contract can raise finance but the agreement can take time to set up, and it is an expensive way to produce in the long term.

* Supplier credit: delaying too long could cause suppliers to withdraw credit options or stop selling to the company.

* Factoring (Debt factoring): a bank or specialist company pays a % of the outstanding amount owed by consumers. The outstanding amount is paid when the consumer repays, less the factoring administrative and service fee. Helps boost available cash.

* JIT inventory: stocks are delivered only when needed. Saves businesses that need to hold a large stock of raw material, saving storage costs.

### **Working capital**

* The amount of money to pay for day-to-day expenses (that have been settled).

* The amount of money left over after all current assets have been settled.

* Low level indicates bad performance

* **Working capital =** Current assets - Current liabilities.

**Managing work capital**

* **Size of business:** A larger business needs a larger amount of working capital.

* **Inventory levels:** the higher the inventory, the more the levels of working capital are needed (assuming others remain the same).

* **Debtors and creditors:** the time between buying inventory financed by trade credit and selling the finished goods can influence the working capital. E.g., a builder needs high levels of working capital (lots of day-to-day expenditure) while a supermarket could operate in negatives.

**Maintaining adequate levels of working capital**

* If liabilities are too high.

  * Unable to fulfill orders or produce due to a lack of stock.

  * May not be able to pay bills without cash.

  * If too much has been borrowed, the firm may struggle to repay with interest

* If current assets are too high

  * The cost of physically storing inventory is very high.

  * Holding too much cash is unlikely to earn a high return on interest, or could instead be used to repay debts or for long-term investment.

### **Importance of cash**

* Most liquid business asset.

* Many businesses fail due to a lack of cash, even though they have the potential to be profitable and are in demand.

* It is different from working capital as working capital components include current assets, which cannot pay bills as well as cash.
