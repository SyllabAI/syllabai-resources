## Business Failure

### **Internal causes of business failure**

* **Poor management of cash**

  * Investing too much in fixed assets.

  * Allowing too much credit (waiting for a long time to gain revenue) causes a need to borrow.

* **Overborrowing (interest rates rise)**

* **Seasonal demand**

* **Unforeseen expenditure**

  * Emergency expense due to insufficient experience or planning.

* **Poor financial management**

  * Solved by up-to-date records, frequent cash flow, and an efficient credit system.

* **Over-estimating sales**

  * A dramatic change in consumer tastes in a short time period can cause demand to fall. Over-optimistic managers may do the same. Over-production due to wrong estimates can also increase storage costs.

* **Over-trading**

  * When a business attempts to fund a large volume of production with inadequate cash. Most likely, if a firm offers generous credit terms, is undercapitalized, or has slim profit margins. One symptom is day-to-day borrowing for expenditure.

* **Poor inventory control**

  * Holding too much, too little, or the wrong stock.

* **Poor marketing**

  * New products may fail to meet customer needs, or invest heavily in inappropriate or wasteful campaigns. A firm may fail to position itself appropriately.

* **Poor quality**

  * News on poor-quality products can travel quickly via social media, resulting in a bad reputation.

---

### **External causes of business failure**

* **Market conditions**

  * Changes in the market can result in sharp fluctuations. Over time, industries decline and get replaced.

* **Competition**

  * A competitor may bring superior products, good market conditions, more effectively, charge lower prices, or use predatory pricing strategies.

* **Economic**

  * General state of the domestic and global economy. Firms that produce non-essential goods and services are likely to feel the effects of an effective economic downturn more severely.

* **Exchange rate**

  * Importing and exporting businesses are affected by exchange rate fluctuations.

* **Interest rate**

  * A sharp increase in interest rates will increase the cost of borrowing, especially for those with large debts.

* **Government regulations**

  * Governments may impose legislation that limits companies’ ability to do business, such as reducing subsidies or cutting government expenditure.

* **Supplier problems**

  * These issues may impact firms using outsourcing or JIT manufacturing more severely.

* **Natural phenomena**

  * Businesses may fail due to natural occurrences such as weather, e.g., farmers.
