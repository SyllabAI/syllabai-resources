## External Finance

External finance is the source of funding outside the business.

### **Sources of Finance**

* **Family and friends:** could be a gift of money or a loan with low or no interest rates. Shares may not be given, but liability to repay can sour relations.

* **Banks:** provide loans, overdrafts, and mortgages. Some banks have areas that deal specially with start-ups and even provide advice. However, a bank account, a formal application, and a business plan will be needed to get a loan.

* **Peer-to-peer lending (P2PL):** lending money to unrelated individuals and avoiding the involvement of a financial institution. Features of P2PL include:

  * No protection for lenders

  * Solely conducted for profit

  * Lenders choose which borrower to lend to

* **Business angels:** individuals who make one or two investments of £10,000 - £100,000 in exchange for a stake in a 3 year period. Best are made in early stages of the start-up or during expansion.

  * Angels like risk of investing, attracted to the cuts, or look for opportunities for investment.

  * Finding the angel is a challenge as the business must have a business plan since they own a stake. Some may only want angels with financial interests.

* **Crowdfunding:** another way to raise money without knowledge of buyer. These fundraisers tend to be firms or groups with social objectives. Transactions are online and administered by specialists like tumblebug.

  * Can display business idea, how much cash needed, and benefit to investor.

  * Some sites may check fundraiser.

* **Other businesses:** subsidiaries, PLC's purchasing shares, joint ventures with shared finance. However, a PLC may plan a take over.

### **Methods of Finance**

* **Loans:** inflexible arrangement where amount borrowed must be returned over fixed time. They could be unsecured (no protection for lender). Interest is also added to total.

  * Bank loans: they may be an unsecured loan (lender has no protection if borrower doesn't return). Long diminished in recent years due to high interest for banks.

  * Mortgage: mortgages are a secured loan where the borrower provides collateral if they fail to repay the loan.

  * Debentures: a specialized loan method where the holder of the debenture is a creditor (someone who the firm owes money to). Holders are entitled to a fixed rate of return but have no voting rights. They must be repaid on a set date. PLC will use this for long time.

* **Share capital:** issued share capital is the money raised from the sale of share. Authorized share capital is the max amount shareholders invest to raise. This instrument cannot be redeemed hence it is known as permanent capital. Inc. shares older can make a capital gain by selling at higher price. Inc. shares are those in stock market while Ltd shares are traded privately (sale of shares in annual meeting to select/replace directors). Types of shares issued:

  * **Ordinary shares (equity):** most common and risky share as there is no guaranteed dividend. Dividend size depends on the profit made, and how much directors decide to return. All holders have voting rights. The share has an original value before it is sold but the price changes once it is sold repeatedly.

  * **Preference *shares:*** holders receive a fixed rate of return when a dividend is declared. Hold less risk as the shareholders get a dividend before ordinary shareholders. However, if the company is sold, their rights to dividends and capital payments are limited to fixed amounts. Some shares allow receiving missed late payments or allow firms to pay back (redeemable).

  * **Deferred shares:** usually held by founders of the company. These shareholders receive a payment after ordinary shareholders have been paid a min. amount. Not often used.

* **Venture capital:** venture capitalists are specialists in the provision of funds for small/medium sized firms. They invest in businesses after initial start-up and prefer tech companies with high growth potential. They take a stake in the company (have control of shares). Venture capitalists raise funds from institutional investors like pension funds, insurance firms, wealthy people etc. Firms usually turn here once they are denied loans.

* **Overdrafts:** when a firm can spend more money than what is in their bank account. The bank and firm agree on an overdraft limit and interest is only charged when an excess amount is withdrawn (flexible source as a certain amount can be drawn). However, the bank has rights to recall money at any point if it thinks that the firm is struggling.

* **Lease:** a contract through which a business acquires the use of resources like machines, property etc for a span of 3 or 3+ years. At the end, the business has the option to buy the asset.

* **Trade credit:** paying for raw materials, fuels, components, etc. at a later date, usually within 30-90 days. This is an interest free way to raise finance. However, many companies offer discounts for early payment thus making late payments more costly. Relations with suppliers may sour if payments are delayed. This is good for firms during inflation.

* **Grants:** A sum of money given by central and local governments to businesses that meet a certain criteria. This criteria could be according to business location, business type etc. The list can be accessed using the government business finance support finder. Most grants do not have to be repaid.
