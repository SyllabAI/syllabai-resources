## Liability

* **Unlimited Liability** means has no legal difference between the owner and the business. They are referred to as unincorporated businesses.

* **Limited Liability** means the business has a separate legal identity from its owners. These are referred to as incorporated businesses.

### **Choosing Appropriate Finance**

* **Short-term or long-term:** external finance is likely to be used for long-term, but methods like trade credit, overdrafts, and leasing are usually short-term.

* **Financial position:** if the position is weak, then lenders will be skeptical about lending. A strong position can help secure a loan due to collateral in huge amounts.

* **Type of expenditure:** it is needed for a long-term source if it is financing a heavy capital expenditure. Revenue expenditure is usually financed using short-term financing.

* **Cost:** prefer less expensive sources and methods in terms of interest rates and administrative costs

* **Legal structure of the business:** decisions on whether a business has limited or unlimited liability.

### **Finance Appropriate for Unlimited Liability**

* **Personal savings**

* **Retained profits:** can only be used once established

* **Mortgage:** a long-term source that usually requires the owner's home as collateral. Has a high risk.

* **Unsecured bank loans:** Established and successful firms with a detailed plan may get these, but it depends on the current financial climate.

* **Peer-to-Peer lending (P2PL):** Raise finance through dedicated websites and avoid banks. Good for small owners. Owners have to check with individual P2PL sites to see if the firm can borrow.

* **Crowdfunding:** once the concept develops and proves to be reliable, small firms can use it for the long term.

* **Overdraft:** Large and established businesses have larger overdrafts.

* **Grants:** lengthy application process to prove that the business qualifies for a grant (free source) usually provided by the government.

### **Finance Appropriate for a Limited Liability**

* **Share capital:** long-term, as money remains in the business once shares are purchased. Firms may use a 'rights issue', which is when existing shareholders can buy new shares at a lower price. This creates free publicity and is cheap and simple.

* **Debentures:** The sale of debentures can be a long-term financing option. The debenture holders don't have control over the business, unlike shareholders.

* **Retained profits:** Large firms tend to have large capital reserves, which can be used at a later date.

* **Venture capitalists:** they tend to invest large amounts in small and medium businesses in exchange for a share and control over decision-making.

* **Business angels:** provide for unlimited and limited firms, and they will normally take a share in the company. Invest smaller and less than venture capitalists. They are difficult to find (opportunity cost of time spent in developing the firm).

* **Other sources:** likely to use overdrafts, mortgages, trade credit, leasing, unsecured loans, and grants in varying combinations. Less likely to use crowdfunding and Peer-to-Peer lending (P2PL).
