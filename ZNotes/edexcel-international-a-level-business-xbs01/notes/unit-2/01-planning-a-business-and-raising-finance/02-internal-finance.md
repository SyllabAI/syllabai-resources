* Capital expenditure: spending on items that can be used over and over again.

* Internal finance: money generated by the business or its current owners.

* Revenue expenditure: payments for goods and services that have been consumed or will be consumed very soon. Also includes maintenance and repair of equipment and buildings.

### **Types of Internal Finance**

* **Retained profit:** Profit that the business has made in the past and has not been distributed to the owners. It is the cheapest source and a flexible (used at a later date) source of finance with no financial charges like interest. However, its opportunity costs are that the money cannot be returned to the owner to fund their lifestyle and/or the shareholders of the company getting lower dividends, which can cause conflicts. This source of finance cannot be used by newly opened businesses.

* **Sales of assets:** Selling unwanted or surplus assets that the business no longer requires. These assets could include machinery, obsolete stock, land etc. A business may also opt for a leaseback agreement, which involves selling an asset to a company that leases it back to the seller. This allows cash to be generated for the seller while allowing maintenance and repair costs to be passed to the new owner. However, this can be more expensive in the long run.

* **Owner's capital:** The money provided by the owner(s) in a business. This is usually from personal savings.
