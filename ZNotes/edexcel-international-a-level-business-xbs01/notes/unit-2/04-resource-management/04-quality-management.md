## Quality Management

**Quality:** Nature of a product that allows it to satisfy customer needs. It also refers to some standard of excellence.

* Features could be:

  * Physical appearance

  * Reliability and durability

  * Special features

  * Suitability

  * Taste

  * Price

  * After sales service

  * Image

  * Reputation

### **Quality circles**

* Consists of workers meeting regularly to solve problems and discuss ideas.

* Intent to motivate and involve workers on a factory floor.

* Both management and employees must support by providing a structure and ensuring employee views are valued.

* To be successful, quality circles should have:

  * Steering committee.

  * A senior manager as chair.

  * One person accountable for the program.

  * Properly trained team leaders.

### **Quality control**

* Quality control in many firms implies that quality controllers or quality inspectors check other

* Objective includes for product include:

  * Satisfy consumer needs.

  * Work under conditions they were likely to face.

  * Operate in the way they should.

  * Could be produced effectively.

  * Could be repaired easily.

* But now it implies finding fault with quality before the product has been sold to the customers

### **Quality assurance**

* A method of working that accounts for customers needs and when standardizing quality. It involves guaranteeing that quality standards are met.

* Commitment to maintaining quality throughout the organisation. Firms try to remove issues rather than discover them at the end.

* Also take account customer's views when planning the process. There may also be a consultation involved at the design and manufacturing stage.

### **Total quality management**

* A managerial approach that focuses on quality and aims to improve effectiveness, flexibility, and competitiveness of the business.

* It is organized so that the business manufacturing process is managed at all stages to prevent errors and poor quality goods.

* **Quality chains:** when employees form a series of links between customers and suppliers in a business, both internally and externally.

  * The chain remains complete if the supplier satisfies a customer.

  * Failure to meet requirements in any stage creates issues like delay in the next stage of production.

* **Company policy, accountability, and empowerment:** A company-wide culture is implemented from the topmost (senior executive) and spread to every employee.

  * Employees must be committed to the job.

* **Control:** customer needs will only be satisfied if the business has control of the factors affecting product quality.

  * These methods can be documented to assess operations. Regular audits should be done.

* **Monitoring the process:** firm relies on monitoring the business process to find possible improvements.

* **Statistical process control (SPC):** involves collecting data related to the performance of process. The info is collected and processed.

  * SPC reduces variability. Variations in products, delivery time, methods, materials, people's attitude, and staff performance.

* **Teamwork:** TQM stresses this; main advantages are:

  * Greater range of skills, knowledge, and experience used to solve the problem.

  * Improved employee motivation.

  * Problems across departments are better dealt with.

  * Greater variety of problems tackled.

  * Team ideas are more likely to be used rather than individual.

* **Consumer views:** must respond to changes in people's needs and expectation.

  * Information must be gathered at an regular basis and there must be clear communication channels for customers to express their views. They are often influential in setting quality standards.

* **Zero defects:** ensures that every product manufactured is free from defects. Eliminating this helps gain a good reputation leading to new clients and improved sales.

### **Kaizen**

* Kaizen is a Japanese concept that involves continuous improvement.

* Eliminating waste: removal of any activity that raises costs without adding value to a product.

* Implementing continuous improvement:

  * **Plan:** must identify areas improvement is needed and develop a plan to get the intended results.

  * **Do:** once the plan is finalized, it has to be implemented (mostly by workers).

  * **Check:** check if improvements are made (usually done by inspectors).

  * **Action:** if plan is successful, it should be done in whole business.

* **Competitive advantage from quality management:**

  * Product quality will be improved, resulting in increased sales.

  * Business costs may be reduced as flaws are unidentified before the product reaches the market.

  * Develop an USP to charge a higher price and increase flexibility in pricing.

  * Overall can help increase market share, raise revenue, and improve profitability.
