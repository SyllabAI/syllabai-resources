## Inventory

* **Raw materials and components** are purchased before production, and can cope with changes in production levels. Production delays can be avoided.

* **Work-in-progress** is partly finished goods.

* **Finished goods are** used to cope with changes in demand. This avoids the step-up production rates quickly.

* **Kanban** is a card or an object that acts as a signal to move or provide resources in a factory.

### **Inventory control**

* **Demand**: stocks should be held to satisfy normal demand, cover growth in sales, and unexpected demand.

* **Stockpiling/gross:** building up stock until times of peak demand.

* **Costs of inventory holding:** only a small quantity is kept due to high costs and uncertainty of sales volume.

* **Amount of working capital available:** In times of working capital shortage, a firm cannot purchase more inventory even if needed.

* **The types of inventory**: a business can only hold a small stock of perishable goods.

* **Lead time:** the time taken for a stock purchase to be ordered, received, inspected, and made ready to use. The longer the lead time, the higher the minimum inventory needed.

* **External factors**: fear of shortages may prompt firms to hold more stock.

* **Buffer stocks:** emergency stock held in case there is a stock item shortage, during a surge in demand, to maximize sales opportunities. Businesses that experience sharp fluctuations hold buffer stocks to prevent a break in production due to a lack of supply of raw material.

### **Implications of poor inventory control**

* **Holding too much inventory:**

  * Storage costs.

  * Opportunity cost of having capital tied up in storage.

  * Spoiled goods.

  * Administrative and financial costs of handling, failing to anticipate price change, placing and processing orders etc.

  * Unsold inventory in low times of demand.

* **Holding too little inventory:**

  * May not be able to cope with an increase in demand.

  * Production halt.

  * May not be able to cope with material shortage.

  * Placing many orders may raise costs, and the firm may miss sales.

* **Waste minimization:**

  * Perishable goods should be stored in the fridge.

  * Forecasting for perishable goods should be done appropriately.

  * Using suitable stock rotation (flow of stock into and out of storage).

  * Ensuring old inventory is first used.

  * Computerized systems are programmed to auto-order inventory once the reorder level is reached.

  * Adjust prices to minimize waste.

  * Transport should be sped up for perishable goods.

  * Find creative uses of expired goods.

* **Competitive advantage of lean production:**

  * Lean production uses fewer resources using techniques like kaizen, cell production, flexible manufacturing, teamworking, empowerment, and multi-skilling.

  * Competitiveness is improved as it:

    * Raises productivity.

    * Reduces costs and cuts lead time.

    * Lowers the number of faulty products.

    * Improves reliability and speeds up design time.

    * With this, businesses can charge lower prices, offer better quality or reliability, and fight off rivals in a market.
