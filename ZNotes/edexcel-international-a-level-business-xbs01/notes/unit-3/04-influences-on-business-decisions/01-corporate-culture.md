# Influences on business decisions

## Corporate Culture

* **Corporate culture** is the values, attitudes, beliefs, norms, and shared meanings that are shared by people and groups within an organization.

**Strong corporate culture:** 

* One that is deeply fixed in the way a business does things.

* **Advantages are:**

  * Provides a sense of identity for the employees, and they feel that they are a part of the business.

  * Flexibility

  * Increase the commitment of employees

  * Motivates workers

  * Help prevent misunderstanding in operations

  * Reinforces values

  * Acts as a control device, which helps when setting the company strategy.

**Weak corporate culture:**

* Exists where it is difficult to identify the factors that form the culture or where a wide range of cultures exist.

* **Disadvantages are:**

  * Behavior, rules, and traditions are not adopted.

  * Need to set up systems for worker alignment (costs)

  * Workers may resist changes

  * Bureaucracy

  * Poor quality of customer service (inflexible)

* **Factors affecting corporate culture strength:**

  * Surface manifestation (appearance)

  * Leaders, myths, rituals, mottoes, and physical layout

* **Core organizational value**: consciously expressed in words and policies, such as the mission statement.

  * Workers at the bottom of the hierarchy may have different values.

### **Effects, Formation, and Difficulties**

* **Effects of organizational culture:**

  * **Motivation**, how achievements are regarded, how colleagues are treated, how competitive an environment is, etc., can impact staff motivation.

* **Organizational structures:**

  * **Person culture** = flat hierarchy

  * **Role culture** = more layers as specialist roles are assigned

* **New management:** The greater the change needed, the more likely it is that the new management will have to challenge the existing organizational culture.

* **Mergers and takeovers:** One way to stop culture clash is to make the senior management in the acquired company redundant. Without powerful advocates, those lower will find it difficult to resist change. However, motivation and morale are likely to be low.

* **Architecture:** Relationships and networks within an organization and those that it develops with its external stakeholders.

  * **Decision-making:**

    * Evidence-based

    * Subjective-based (Intuition)

**How Corporate Culture Is Formed:**

* **Factors are:**

  * **Role of founding members**

  * **Environmental factors** when a business forms (e.g., traditions, norms)

  * **Type of product**. The technical nature and the need for innovation determine the skill, expertise, and need for change, possibly contributing to role culture.

**Difficulties in changing an established culture:**

* Identifying the significance of contributing factors is hard. Subcultures also exist in firms

* Some aspects, like rules, can be easier to change

* Managers try to align workers in a culture. They may do this by training and emphasizing the benefits of the culture whilst training.

* This change is likely to be a lengthy process (culture clash)

### **Classifications/Subcultures**

**Classification/subcultures** (Handy’s 4 Cultures)

**Power culture:** 

* There is a central source of power responsible for decision-making.

* **Pros**: Quick decision-making, control.

* **Cons**: Dependent on 1 individual, limited creativity.

* **Some characteristics are:**

  * Few rules that are rejected by individuals with power if needed.

  * A competitive environment between workers to gain power for their own goals. This creates a political atmosphere.

  * Usually in young, small/medium businesses with a single founder.

**Role culture:** 

* Decisions are made through well-established rules and procedures. Power is associated with a role like marketing director, not people.

* **Pros:** predictability, efficiency, stability, safety.

* **Cons:** inflexible, slow, less innovative, and less motivated.

* **Some characteristics are:**

  * **Tall hierarchy** with a long chain of command.

  * Described as **bureaucratic.**

  * Local government departments for community services are an example.

**Task culture:** 

* Power is given to those who can complete a task (someone with expertise).

* **Pros:** Creativity, Teamwork, adaptability, Good for project-based firms.

* **Cons:** Lack of authority, Conflict between different teams.

* **Characteristics are:**

  * Teamwork is common.

  * Adaptability.

  * Dynamism.

**Person culture:**

*  Where some individuals work together but don't work together particularly closely.

* **Pros:** Motivation, Attracting professionals, Creativity.

* **Cons:** Hard to manage, not for profit-driven/large firms.

* **Characteristics are:**

  * The organization supports the individual.

  * Business is full of people with similar backgrounds, skills, and training.

  * Examples are lawyers, doctors, etc.

  * Suitable for professional partners.
