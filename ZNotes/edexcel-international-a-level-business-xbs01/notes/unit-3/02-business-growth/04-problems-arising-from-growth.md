## **Problems arising from Growth**

### **Diseconomies of Scale**

Diseconomies of Scale occur when a business expands its operations beyond its minimum efficient scale, causing the long-run average cost (LRAC) to rise as output increases.

### **Sources of Diseconomies**

Internal Diseconomies of Scale

* **Poor Communication:** Large size leads to a complex flow of information, causing delays, misinterpretations, and reduced efficiency.

  * **Internal Communication Issues:**

    * Long communication channels can distort messages, leading to arguments and reduced productivity.

    * Technology (ICT) can help, but a system failure can still be disruptive.

    * May lead to resource duplication across different departments.

    * Departments may develop a "Silo Mentality" (reluctant to share information), leading to conflict, missed opportunities, and higher costs.

* **Control and Co-ordination:** It becomes difficult for management to control and coordinate the large amount of resources.

* **Poor Worker Motivation:** Employees can feel disconnected in a massive organisation, leading to lower morale and productivity.

* **Bureaucracy**: Too many layers of management result in long decision-making processes and slow communication.

* **Technical Diseconomies:** Though rare, can occur if massive equipment is less efficient than medium-sized machinery.

* **External Diseconomies of Scale**

  * **Rapid Growth:** High demand for resources (Factors of Production or FOP) from many firms causes the price of FOP to rise.

  * **Geographical Concentration:** Multiple firms growing in the same geographical location compete intensely for the same limited resources (e.g., land, skilled labor), driving up costs.

### **Overtrading**

* Overtrading is a situation where a business experiences rapid growth in production and sales but lacks sufficient working capital (cash) to support that growth. Essentially, the business is growing too fast for its financial resources.

**Causes and Consequences:**

* **Undercapitalisation:** The business doesn't have enough capital, leading to the inability to meet growing orders as quickly as needed.

* **Offering Too Much Trade Credit:** The business sells goods on credit but has to pay its suppliers promptly, creating a cash flow squeeze.

* **Small Profit Margins:** Operating with small margins means the insufficient profit generated cannot cover the increasing volume of operations.

**Impact:**

* **Threatens the survival of the business** due to severe cash flow problems, despite high sales figures.
