## Contingency Planning and Risk Assessment

### **Contingency Planning**

* It is the strategic planning method designed to identify potential crises that a business might experience. It helps:

* Make clear some of the future uncertainties in business.

* Identify risks and apparent opportunities and prepare accordingly.

* Teach managers how crises may occur, develop, and affect the firm.

* Understand the causes and effects of business change and how to manage it.

**Steps include:**

* Identifying the trend and issue (check internal and external environment).

* Identify a possible crisis (imagine a crisis and provide background using the information in previous stages).

* Plan a response.

* Identify the most likely crisis.

* Capitalise on the crisis (applying the planned response).

### **Risk Assessment**

* Identifying and evaluating the potential risks associated with an activity that a business proposes to undertake, and then ensuring compliance with relevant health and safety laws.

  * Identify risks.

  * Identify who might be harmed.

  * Estimate probability.

**Possible Crisis**

* Natural disasters.

* IT system failures.

* Loss of key staff.

**Planning for Risk Mitigation**

* Identify, assess, and prioritise risks, and plan responses to deal with the impact of these risks on the operation of the business. Examples are:

  * Setting up in an area less prone to natural disasters.

  * Take out business interruption cover (a type of insurance that covers the loss of income that a firm suffers after a disaster).

  * Ensure valuable assets are protected.

### **Business Continuity Plan**

* Shows how a business will operate after a serious incident and how it expects to return to normal in the quickest time possible. **Four stages may include:**

**1.** Carry out a **business impact analysis** (identify functions and processes that are essential to run the business). Includes financial consequences.

**2. Formulate recovery strategies** (action to restore the business to a minimum level after an incident). Examples are:

* Agreement to share resources with other firms.

* Maintains high stock levels.

* Shifting production.

**3. Plan development** (Planning to ensure recovery strategies are carried out in an organised way).

**4. Testing and training** (train staff and recovery teams and update the plans according to findings. It has to be regularly updated to account for changes).

### **Succession planning**

* Involves identifying and developing individuals who have the potential to fill key roles within the business in the future.

  * Identify the characteristics a successor should possess.

  * Decide how the successor will be found.

  * Have a vigorous selection process.

  * Make the decision.

  * Communicate the decision.

  * Implement a training and preparation plan.
