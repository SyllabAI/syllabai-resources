## Impact of external influences

### **Structure of Markets**

* **Competitive markets:** a large number of buyers and sellers with close substitutes for each product. The barriers to entry are usually low, and firms have low control over price fluctuations. There is also likely to be a free flow of information about the product's nature, availability, etc.

* **Uncompetitive markets:** A monopoly might exist in this market, which implies that only one business supplies the entire market. It may also be an oligopoly if the market is dominated by a few very large producers.

### **Changing Competitive Environment**

* In the long run, more firms enter a market, which increases the competitiveness, but the government tries to reduce competitiveness by reducing regulations. Some firms look to diversify due to the increase in competition.
* **Impact of a changing competitive environment**
  * **New entrants may** cause a loss in control of price and sales (depending on size).

  * **New products may** force a change in existing products, functionality, or price, plus force the firm to adopt an aggressive advertising campaign.

  * **Consolidation:** when the number of businesses fall but some of the existing businesses grow. Other firms may respond by proposing mergers or takeovers. Firms may look to develop products, diversify, cut costs, or accept lower margins.

### **Porter's Five Forces**

* **Bargaining power of suppliers:** The more power a supplier has, the higher prices it can charge. Limiting the power of suppliers will allow them to grow competitively. It could do so by integrating backwards vertically, seeking new suppliers, minimise info to the supplier, etc.

* **Bargaining power of buyers:** Buyers with lots of power can lower the competitive position. The position could be improved by performing a forward vertical integration.

* **Threat of new entrants:** businesses can protect themselves by erecting barriers to entry—advertising, customer loyalty, etc.

* **Substitutes:** when there are lots of substitutes, competitive pressure is higher, which does not allow a firm to charge higher prices. A business can prevent this by using patents, marketing tactics, and predatory pricing, etc.

* **Rivalry among existing firms:** If rivalry is fierce, it can be reduced by forming cartels or engaging in anti-competitive behavior (but it may be illegal). Businesses may also buy their rivals (horizontal integration). In industries with few businesses, firms may compete by bringing new products, thus creating new brands while charging higher prices.
