## SWOT Analysis

* **Strengths:** Positive aspects of a business outlined from **internal audit**.

  * Many include: leadership, workforce, USP, production facilities, etc.

* **Weaknesses:** Negative aspects of a business highlighted by **internal audit**.

  * It includes what the business lacks or does poorly in terms of layers.

  * Examples could be an organisation with too many layers, out of date products, growing debt, etc.

* **Opportunities:** Options/openings that a business might be able to exploit.

  * This is discovered through **external audit**.

  * Examples can be new markets, fall in costs of raw material, a fall in exchange rate, etc.

* **Threats:** External audits used to highlight potential dangers.

  * Examples include: new competitors, probable recession, change in social attitudes, political or environmental, etc.

* **SWOT analysis** is most helpful before it is documented and is used to decide corporate strategy: increase life (launching new products), launching a new marketing strategy, building workforce to outsource.

### **Internal Audit Areas**

* **Products and costs** - to produce an independent analysis.

* **Finance** - including cash and stock analysis.

* **Production** - including capacity, quality, efficiency, and stock management.

* **Internal organisation**

* **Human Resources** - skills, training, recruitment.

### **External Audit Areas**

* **External audit** - deals with market, competitors, political, social, technological, and environmental issues relevant to the business.

* The factors are:

  * Size and growth potential of the market.

  * Competition.

  * Characteristics of customers.

  * Products on offer.

  * Distribution and promotion of products.

  * Industry practices (government regulation, trade association)
