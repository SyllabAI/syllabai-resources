## Investment Appraisal

* **Investment** is the purchase of capital goods (goods used to produce other goods).

* **Investment appraisal** helps a business objectively evaluate an investment project to determine whether or not it is profitable.

* Allows a business to make comparisons.

* Uses quantitative methods that involve comparing the capital cost of the projects with the net cash flow.

* **Capital cost** is the money spent when setting up a venture.

### **Simple Payback**

* **Payback period:** Time taken for a project to recover or pay back the initial outlay.

* Can be found by calculating the **cumulative net cash flow**.

  * This figure accumulates the net cash flow from the start of the project up to a specific point in time. It shows the total cash balance of the project as of that date.

### **Average (Accounting) Rate of Return (ARR)**

* **Measures the net return each year as a percentage of the capital cost of investment.**

$$

\text{ARR}% = \frac{\text{Net return (Profit) per annum}}{\text{Capital outlay (Cost)}} \times 100

$$

* (Note: The numerator is often the *average annual profit*.)

### **Discounted Cash Flow (Net Present Value / NPV)**

* Payback and ARR do not account for the time value of money.

* Businesses might take into account what cash flow or profit earned in the future is worth at the present.

* Discounted cash flow takes interest rates into account by calculating the present value of future income.

* NPV: Present value of future income from an investment project, minus the cost.

* Does not account for inflation.

* The higher the rate of discount, the lower the present value of cash flow in the future.

**Calculating NPV**

* Rate of return on an investment project, taking into account the effect of interest rate and time.

* NPV = Present value - Costs

**Investment Decision Rule (Notes in margin):**

* **If NPV is negative (-)**: Side with the investment (i.e., reject).

* **If NPV is positive (+)**: Profit from investment (i.e., accept).

* **If NPV is 0:** Same as cost.
