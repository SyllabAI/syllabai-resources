# Assessing competitiveness

## Interpretation of Financial Statements

### **Statements and Concepts**

* A Statement of Financial Position (balance sheet) shows the assets, liabilities, and capital of the business.

* A Statement of comprehensive income (profit and loss account) shows profit/loss for the year and comprehensive income (like gains made on currency transactions).

* Statement of comprehensive income is used by PLCs to show income and expenditure for a time period. It contains:

  * Revenue

  * Cost of sales (production cost, materials & labour)

  * Gross profit (Revenue - Cost of sales)

  * Selling expenses (cost to sell, sale commission, ads)

  * Administrating expense (indirect cost, telephone bills, stationery)

  * Operating profit (Gross Profit - administrative cost)

  * Finance cost (finance cost, such as payments on loans or income from interest)

  * Net profit (Operating Profit - finance cost, Before tax)

  * Net profit after tax

### **Statement of Financial Position Components**

* **Capital**

* **Assets**: capital + liabilities

  * **Non-current assets:** sold after 1 year

    * Goodwill (loyalty), brand names, patents, property, equipment, investment

  * **Current assets**: Liquid assets sold in 1 year

    * Inventories, trade and other receivables, cash

* **Liabilities**

  * **Current liabilities**: debts paid in 1 year

    * Borrowing, trade and payables, dividends payable, and current tax liabilities

* **Non-current liabilities**: long-term

  * Loans, retirement pension obligations, provisions

* **Net assets** = **Shareholders’ Equity**

  * **Equity**: money owed to Shareholders (Asset - Liabilities)

    * Share capital, share premium account (difference between value of new share and its nominal value), reserves, retained earnings

### Profit Margins

* **$$ \text{Gross Profit Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times 100 $$**
  * **Gross Profit = Revenue - Cost of goods sold**

    * Cost of goods sold is the cost of making the product

  * The higher the gross profit margin, the more the Gross profit is made per unit of sales.

  * It is used to analyse the pricing strategy of a product and production efficiency
* **$$ \text{Operating Profit Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Operating Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times 100 $$**
  * **Operating Profit = Gross Profit - Operating expenses**

    * Operating expenses are costs that are not directly tied to production, such as marketing

  * Shows core business performance and operational efficiency
* **$$ \text{Net Profit Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times 100 $$**
  * **Net Profit = Operating Cost - Financial Expenses**
  * Shows the profitability of a business, which indicates the extent to which the business can survive economic downturns.

### Liquidity Ratios

* **$$ \text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} $$**

  * Ideal is between 1.5:1 to 2:!

    * Below 1.5 indicates low working capital, which could be a result of over-trading/over-borrowing

    * Above 2 indicates holding unproductive capital

  * Some businesses, like retailers, tend to have a low Current Ratio (1:1) due to quick selling inventories

* **$$ \text{Acid-Test Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventory}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} $$**

  * Used to assess the liquidity of a business without accounting for inventories.

  * If the ratio is lower than 1:1, it indicates that assets-inventories cannot cover current liabilities, which indicates poor liquidity.

    * This can be more accurate as it is not always confirmed that all inventories will be sold

* **$$ \text{Gearing Ratio} = \left( \frac{\text{Non-Current Liabilities)}}{\text{Capital Employed}} \right) \times 100 $$**

  * A gearing ratio above 50% is regarded as highly geared, which indicates that the majority of finance is through loans, and hence, the business is risky to invest in.

  * Used to analyse capital structure by comparing the amount raised from ordinary shareholders and that raised by loans.

    * This is important because interest payments are fixed while dividends are not.

* **$$ \text{ROCE} = \left( \frac{\text{Operating Profit}}{\text{Capital Employed}} \right) \times 100 $$**

  * **Capital Employed = Total assets - current liabilities**

  * **Capital Employed = Total equity + Non-current liabilities**

  * The ideal is between 20% to 30%

  * Compares the profit made with the amount of money invested

  * Shows the connection between profit and business size

### **Stakeholder Interest in Financial Statements**

* **Shareholders**: analyze asset structure and assess solvency. They check if assets are liquid enough to pay bills. Check if the investment is growing by comparing the value of net assets.

* **Managers and directors**: check financial position and monitor working capital levels to prevent overspending. Consult the current capital structure to choose a source of finance.

  * **Shareholders**: calculate net profit and gross profit margins to assess performance.

  * **Managers and directors**: assess growth.

* **Employees**: wage increase by accessing the Statement of Financial Position.

* **Suppliers**: check creditworthiness to offer trade credit.

* **Government**: integrates and collects economic data for public use.

* **Others**: employees assess their ability to receive a pay raise and job security. Governments may use the balance sheet to compile national statistics.
