# Entrepreneurs and Leaders

## The Role of an Entrepreneur

* **Entrepreneurs:** people who have a business idea and want to make money by working for themselves.

* **Businesses:** Organizing an enterprise means organizing different factors of production (e.g., hiring labor, buying materials, organizing, giving instructions, making arrangements, and setting up new systems).

### Risks and Rewards for Entrepreneurs

* There is a possibility of a large profit
* A chance to implement ideas of their own
* However, it can be difficult to gain investment as starting a business causes a high level of initial costs. The risk of a loss can also demotivate owners from starting their business.
* Obtaining a new idea can be difficult as market conditions and preferences are difficult to interpret, especially for new entrepreneurs.

### Creating and Setting Up a Business

* **Innovations**: They are innovative as they generate more money out of a business idea. Such ideas may come from spotting a gap in the market or new inventions, or market research.

* **Decision making**: They make decisions on financing, product, design, prices, etc., because they are the owners.

* **Risk-taking**: Investing capital into a business involves a risk, as the earnings may or may not succeed. This could lead to profit-related or money loss.

### Entrepreneurship and Business Idea

* **Business experience**: An idea may come from an existing job or from a hobby. Starting a business from an existing job is less risky because the owner or business already has prior knowledge from his or her job.

* **Personal experiences**: Some people may turn their hobbies into a business idea.

* **Skills**: Some entrepreneurs draw on their broad skill base to start a business.

* **Lifestyle choices:** Some businesses attract people who wish to make a change in their lifestyle choices. Which could encourage them to invest.

* **Stages in setting up a business**: A business has to be carefully structured and carefully planned in order to set up a successful business. Good quality workmanship allows a larger potential for business success.

### Planning

* **Idea**: might involve carrying out market research and analyzing the competition to decide if the idea has potential to be successful.

* **Financing**: Finance is needed to set up the business and pay off the initial costs. Entrepreneurs need to decide the amount of finance needed and the source to be used.

* **Location**: The location depends on the nature of the business.

* **Resources**: The business plan contains the required resources to set up and run the business.

* **Launch**: This is the stage the business is officially open. A ceremony could be held to gain a good public reputation.

### Expanding and Developing a Business

* **Financial management**: A business requires funds to operate. This should be done by using cash flow forecasts, deciding on sources of finance, and monitoring cash movements.

* **Administration**: Accurate records must be maintained, including stocks, wages/salaries, tax authorities, and compliance with legislation.

* **Marketing**: Depending on the nature of the business, marketing could involve listing on websites, using email campaigns, distributing leaflets, etc. Customer relationship and market research will also be required.

* **Purchasing**: Businesses purchase commercial services and resources at the lowest possible rate. This requires good bargaining and negotiating skills.

* **Managing people**: If a business is very successful, it requires a staff. This might involve speeding up time on recruitment, selection, and training. Entrepreneurs may need managerial skills to organize and motivate staff.

* **Production**: The entrepreneur will need to monitor product quality and quantity, consider health and safety issues in the factory, and ensure production meets market orders.

### Intrapreneurship

* Intrapreneurs are employees in a large business who use entrepreneurial skills to find and develop innovations that will have financial benefits to their company. If they achieve this, the employee earns an extra reward.

* Intrapreneurs can drive innovation in a business and uncover new commercial opportunities. Intrapreneurs help gain a competitive edge and increase profit significantly.

* Satisfies the self-actualization needs of employees, which is the highest need according to Maslow's hierarchy.

* A business can also win awards for ground-breaking inventions, which could give free PR.

* Individuals get an opportunity for career growth without bearing the costs of failure. This will provide increased job satisfaction.

### Barriers to Entrepreneurship

* **Lack of Finance**: There may not be enough capital because of high failure chances for new businesses.

* **Skills**: Some people may lack the required skills and character to act. Due to a lack of skills, they have less entrepreneurial capacity.

### Becoming an Employer

* **Employers are responsible for worker wages, sick pay, health and safety issues, worker insurance, training, etc.**

* **Employees could turn out to be** **unreliable and damage the firm's reputation.**

* **Legal barriers:** Need to comply with legislation for employment.

* **Environment, consumers, corporate governance, taxation, etc.**

* **Market access barriers:** Could be authorized competition or no access for certain markets.

* **Fear of failure**: The failure rate for startups is high.

* **Aversion to risk**: Some entrepreneurs may not be disciplined to take risks.

* **Corrupt and unsupportive environment**: Some countries could be politically unstable. These have unclear laws, enforce regulations inconsistently.

### Anticipating Risk and Uncertainty

* **Anticipating risk:** Even if entrepreneurs are successful, they have to take risks (investments, new products, etc.). Entrepreneurs could reduce risks by:

  * Testing products in smaller markets.

  * Risk in decision-making can also be reduced by using quantitative techniques.

* **Anticipating uncertainty**: Uncertainty could lead to the cancellation or postponement of projects/investments. These events cannot be predicted, but measures like funds are available.

  * Market uncertainty could be reduced by performing PESTLE analysis and SWOT analysis to prepare for unexpected events and improve the quality of decision-making.
