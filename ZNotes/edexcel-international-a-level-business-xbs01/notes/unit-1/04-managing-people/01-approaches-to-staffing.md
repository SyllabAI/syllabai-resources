# Managing People

## Approaches to staffing

### Motivation

**Staff as an asset**

* Reasonable holidays

* Safe and comfortable working environment

* Training

* Recognition

* Clear and effective leadership

* Chances for promotion

* Allowing employees to use their creative abilities to solve problems and work in teams

* Helps retain employees

**Staff as a Cost**

* Companies will focus on minimizing costs.

* Paying just NMW (National Minimum Wage)

* Using a zero-hours contract policy (No guarantee of minimum work hours)

* Neglecting investment in training

* Use financial incentives to raise productivity

* Provide minimum sick leave and working conditions

* Use cheap recruitment methods

### Flexible Workforce

* **Multiskilling**: the process of enhancing skills of employees. A firm may be able to respond more effectively to problems as individuals gain more abilities.

* **Part-time staff and temporary staff**: part-time workers are those that work fewer than 30 hours a week. These workers are called flexible peripheral workers.

* **Zero-hour Contracts**

  * Zero-hour contracts have some companies concerned with profitability against dismissing an employee.

  * Employers can use workers for a short period and are only paid for the work they do since the employee only has to be called for the required time period.

* **Flexible hours and homeworking**: Flexible hours allows workers to choose working hours to suit their own needs. Homeworking can mean that the worker is more comfortable working at home, which may result in a rise in productivity and also helps businesses reduce costs of work space, amenities, lighting, heating, etc.

* **Outsourcing**: involves getting other people or businesses to carry out tasks which were originally carried out by employees. This allows a firm to focus on its main responsibilities.

  ### Dismissal and Redundancy

  * **Dismissal**: A period of notice is required but the length depends on time worked by the employee. Dismissal can occur because of & unfair reasons.

  * **Redundancy**: legal method of & dismissing an employee. Redundancy can occur because of insufficient work for workers or due to recessions or other external factors.

### Employer/Employee Relationships

* A positive work relationship allows the worker to be more productive at work which assists the business enjoying high levels of skill and output. However, objectives can conflict.

* **Rates of pay**: employers tend to restrain wage growth to control costs.

* **Introduction of technology**: technological machines can be cheaper for firms as they are one-time investments which could replace the jobs of employees.

* **Work conditions**: best work conditions desired by workers could be too expensive.

* Existing relationship between employees and employers can be shaped by two approaches:

  * **Individual approaches**: when disagreements and terms of employment are negotiated by an individual employee and a representative of the employer.

  * **Collective bargaining**: involves determining wages, work conditions, etc. through employers and employee representatives. Conditions required:

    * Employees should be able to join trade unions

    * Employers recognize unions as legal bodies such bodies should be independent of employers and state

    * Negotiated terms should be accepted without a law to enforce them
