## Income Elasticity of Demand (YED)

* **Definition:** YED measures the responsiveness of the quantity demanded for a good to a change in consumer income.

* **Formula of YED:**

$$

\text{YED} = \frac{\%\text{ change in quantity demanded}}{\% \text{change in income}}

$$

* The sign (positive or negative) of the YED value tells us the **type of good**.

  * If the value received is **positive (+)**, the good is called a **normal good**. This means that a rise in income will lead to a rise in demand for the particular good. This can include daily necessities and even luxuries.

  * If the value received is **negative (-)**, the good is called an **inferior good**. This means that a rise in income will lead to a fall in demand for the particular good. This can include public transport, unbranded items, etc.

* The magnitude of the YED value (if positive) tells us the **sensitivity of demand** to income changes.

  * Price inelastic demand (YED < 1) means a change in income results in a less than proportionate change in demand. An example is staple foods like rice, bread, etc.

  * Price elastic demand (YED > 1) means a change in income results in a more than proportionate change in demand. Examples include branded goods, foreign holidays, etc.

### Factors Influencing YED

* **Time**: Over longer periods, consumers are more likely to find substitutes, which can affect elasticity.

* **Competition**: Products with many identical or similar competitors tend to have a high income elasticity of demand.

* **Branding**: Stronger branding leads to lower price elasticity, as consumers are less likely to switch to alternatives.

* **Proportion of Income Spent**: Products that make up a small part of a consumer's income are typically price inelastic.

* **Product Type vs. Individual Business Product**: Overall product categories (like petrol) might have lower elasticity than a specific brand of that product.
