## Pricing

* **Pricing Strategies** is a set of decisions regarding pricing to achieve objectives.

**Cost-plus Pricing**

* It involves the addition of mark-up to unit costs to ensure that a profit is made. However, there may be low sales due to no consideration for market conditions. It may also be difficult to know the exact costs associated with production, especially for multi-product firms.

**Price Skimming**

* Occurs when a high price is charged for a good when it is launched in a market to recover the costs of research and development, and production. This is used when a newly innovated product is launched into a new market to exploit popularity before competitors arrive.

  * High revenue

  * More consumers are drawn as the price starts to decrease

  * Develops a prestigious image

  * Only used if demand is price inelastic

  * However, skimming could attract competition

**Penetration Pricing**

* A strategy used to enter a market by lowering prices for a small time period.

  * Secure initial position

  * Consumers may be attracted to low prices and continue purchasing when price increases.

  * Quick sales growth

  * Puts pressure on rivals to lower prices

**Destroyer/Predatory Pricing**

* It involves charging a very low price until one or more of the firms leave the market. This practice is illegal because it can create a lack of competition for a lengthy period. As a result, if all firms leave the market, the price will be raised to a level higher than the initial one, creating a monopoly.

  * Quick entry into the market

  * Low profit margin

**Competitive Pricing**

* Involves a firm setting a price that is similar to that of a rival, usually decided by studying a competitor's prices.

  * Avoid a price war

  * Firms with low production costs usually dominate the market and are known as “Price leaders.”

**Psychological Pricing**

* A strategy that tricks the consumer into thinking that they benefited from a good bargain. E.g., pricing under a whole number.

* Targets consumers looking for a bargain

* Not used by upmarket products such as cars

**Online Sales and Dynamic Pricing**

* Price changes to reflect social trends

* **Personalised pricing**: uses data of a specific shopper to charge a specific price to that consumer.

* Dynamic pricing is common in airlines and can be used by businesses that oversee many fluctuations in demand.

### Factors Determining Pricing Strategy

* **Differentiation and USP**: High prices for new/additional features.

* **Price Elasticity of Demand**: Scope for price increase if demand is inelastic

* **Competition**: The presence of rivals causes low prices.

* **Strength of Brand**: Strong brands charge higher prices.

* **Stage in Product Lifestyle**: Price is reduced as the maturing stage is reached
