## Market Positioning

* This is concerned with the perception of consumers about a particular product. Consumers tend to categorize products based on price, quality, value for money, etc., to help differentiate them from a large range.

* **Consumers of the product**: A business positions its product in relation to its competitors to become a product leader.

* **Unique selling point**: A USP is the key aspect of the product that differentiates it from its rivals.

### Orientation

* **Product orientation**: The business dedicates its focus on its production process and the product itself. The firm produces and makes products when it thinks they fulfill the consumer's needs and wants. They usually develop a technologically sound product. Examples can be Apple, Boeing, etc.
* **Market orientation**: This type of firm continuously identifies, reviews, and analyzes consumer needs. Thus, it is said to be led by the market. A business that is market-oriented is likely to have very effective marketing and make decisions that revolve around the target customer. Examples include Coca-Cola, Amazon, etc.
* Some advantages include:
  * Anticipate and respond quickly to market changes due to its use of market research.

  * These firms are also stronger in their competition.

  * More confident when launching a product.
* A market-oriented business must:
  * Consult the consumers frequently.

  * Design the product according to the customers' wishes.

  * Produce the product in quantities that consumers want to buy.

  * Distribute the product according to the buying and distributing habits and requirements of the consumers.

  * Set the price at a level that consumers are willing to pay.

### Factors Affecting Business Orientation

* **Nature of the product**: When a firm operates in an industry that is at the edge of innovations (e.g., tech, pharmaceuticals, electronics), it must innovate to survive. The researcher typically does not have a single product in mind when considering customer demands.

* **Policy decisions**: Objectives are defined in terms of technical quality, resulting in product-oriented businesses.

* **Production**: Objectives are in terms of market share and market lead for market-oriented businesses.

* **The views of those in authority**: People have different views. For example, an accountant would emphasize cash flow, a product-oriented business would emphasize technical quality, and a market-oriented business would emphasize the need in the market.

  **The nature and size of markets**: If there are high production costs, then a product needs to be eradicated to ensure that customer needs are met and unsold stock and losses are avoided.

* **Degree of competition**: A company facing little competition is more likely to use resources for market research and concentrate on market share, while businesses with high competition are likely to do the opposite.

### Market Mapping

* A business can use market research to understand how consumers perceive their products in relation to competitors.

* A business wishing to launch a new product also uses market research to define the target market, affect price, and provide promotional support.

* The results can be displayed on market or perceptual maps.

* Market maps can have limitations like being 2-dimensional, which only allows 2 product qualities to be analyzed. They can also be less reliable for corporate firms' image, but good for small individual brands.

* The info needed can also be expensive, as it is obtained from primary market research.

* There could also be a difference between the consumer's perception of the benefits and the actual benefits.

* Perceptual maps may not come from a detailed study, as they are intuitive/judgmental/consensus maps that are made by marketers based on their understanding of the industry.

* These maps give the appearance of reliability to the management's preconceptions.

### Market Segmentation

* Each segment consists of people/consumers who have similar needs. Businesses target particular segments with their products once they recognize this.

* Some businesses focus on producing one product for a particular segment, while some produce products that are targeted at several segments. Some businesses produce for nearly all consumers.

* Dividing markets into segments allows businesses to supply products to meet customer needs.

**Geographic segmentation**

* Different consumer groups living in different areas have different needs.

**Demographic segmentation**

* **Age**: Different age groups, like children, teenagers, and young adults, have different needs due to age differences.

* **Gender**: Men and women have different needs, which is why businesses tend to segment them.

* **Income**: Income varies vastly in most countries, which causes firms to target consumers at certain incomes. This could also help firms charge an appropriate price.

* **Social class**: Socio-economic groups are usually based on occupations.

* **Ethnicity**: Cosmopolitan countries consist of people from different ethnic groups. These ethnically different customers are likely to have different needs due to varied cultures.

* **Religion**: Different religious groups display different tastes.

**Psychographic segmentation**

* Grouping customers according to attitude, opinions, lifestyle, sports, family, AND political views.

**Behavioral segmentation**

* Consumers are categorized according to the quantity and frequency of their purchases.

* **Time and consumption**: Some consumers consume products at a particular time, e.g., breakfast cereals in the morning. This can be used for promotion.

* Loyalty to a particular product or brand

**Benefits of market segmentation**

* Businesses that produce different products for different market segments can increase revenue.

* Customers may be more loyal to businesses that produce products that meet their exact needs.

* Businesses avoid wastage of resources for promotion aimed at consumers who would not want them.

* Some businesses can market a wider range of goods to different customer groups.

### Competitive Advantage of a Product or Service

* A competitive advantage is a set of unique features of a company and its products that are perceived by consumers to be superior to the firm's competitors.

* **Product design**: A business may emphasize the design and superior functionality to appeal to a significant portion of the market.

* **Product quality**: A premium price can be charged for a high-quality product. However, costs can be higher as high-quality materials could be required.

* **Promotion**: The use of persuasive and creative promotion may change consumer perceptions so they are more inclined to buy that product.

* **Customer service**: Some businesses emphasize customer service rather than the product to retain loyal consumers and gain a competitive edge.

* **Delivery time**: If delivery speed is important, prompt usage of delivery could allow the firm to gain an advantage.

* **Economies of scale**: Firms with lower costs can charge lower prices, which allows them to win a bigger market share.

* **Ethical stance**: A competitive edge could be gained by keeping an ethical position.

### Product Differentiation

* Product differentiation is used by businesses to gain a competitive edge over their rivals.

**Purposes of differentiation include:**

* **Flexible pricing**: If businesses can prove superiority in quality and functionality, they might be able to charge a higher price.

* **Recognition**: Consumers find products that tend to stand out as more appealing.

* **Extend product range**: More than one market segment can be served. Businesses can differentiate their products.

* **Brand development**: A long period of differentiation could allow a business to obtain a strong brand image.

* **Overcome competition**: Differentiation of products allows businesses to create real or perceived differences from rivals to increase market share and sales.

### Adding Value

* Value is added when businesses provide additional features for the customer that go beyond their expectations. This is done by:

  * **Bundling**: Value is added by putting together a package of benefits and services to make up for the whole product.

  * **Customer service**: Well-presented, thoughtful, friendly, and professional staff create a good image and allow consumers to feel at ease. After-sales service can also be recognized as added value.

  * **Speed of response to consumers**: Reducing or eliminating wait times can add value, as many customers don't like waiting.

  * **Packaging**: Attractive wrappings and designs can add value to products.

  * **Frequent buyer offers**: Rewarding consumers with repeat purchases with free products, discounts, or great service can add value.

  * **Customization**: Can involve using customized wrapping or adapting the product to customer needs.

**Benefits of Added Value:**

* Can allow a firm to charge higher prices, but a business may want to disguise the value-added element to make it tax-free.

* Adding value to differentiate a product adds a competitive edge

* Adding value may protect itself from competitors charging lower prices to steal customers.

* A business may focus more closely on the target market segment by adding value.
