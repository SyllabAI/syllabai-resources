# Meeting Customer Needs

## The Market

* A market is a place or venue that facilitates the exchange of goods and services. Markets can be physical or virtual.

* Examples of a Market:

  * Consumer goods market (food, cosmetics).

  * Service markets.

  * Housing markets.

  * Commodity markets (Raw materials trading).

  * Financial markets.

**Marketing Involves:**

* Identifying consumer needs.

* Product designing.

* Identifying competitors.

* Spreading awareness.

* Setting prices.

* Persuading consumers to purchase.

* Selling the product in convenient locations.

### Mass Market

* Consists of firms selling homogeneous/generalised products to their large consumer base.

* **Characteristics**: high competition, large-scale production, standardized product, non-segmented customer base

* **Advantages**: Firms can gain high profits by exploiting economies of scale.

* **Drawbacks:** a mass market contains a high level of competition.

### Niche Market

* A niche market is a small segment left untouched by large businesses, where small firms sell goods that meet the exact needs of customers.

* **Characteristics**: low competition, small-scale production leading to high fixed costs, differentiated product, market segmentation to identify target customer.

* **Advantages**: Niche firms can charge a premium price, Small amounts of competition are present.

* **Drawbacks:** operating in a niche market can be risky, as a small firm may not spread its risks across a range of products.

### Market Share

* The proportion of a market that is held by a product, firm, brand, etc. Market share can help identify the market leader.

* The amount of market share can influence the goals and strategies of a business.

* The amount of market share held can also be an indication of failure or success.

$$

\text{Market Share } % = \frac{\text{Total Sales}}{\text{Total Sales in the Market}} \times 100

$$

### Brands

* Companies use brand names to distinguish themselves from rivals. Establishing a brand helps:

  * Differentiate the product.

  * Create customer loyalty.

  * Increase product recognition.

  * Develop an image.

  * Charge a more premium price.

### Dynamic Market

* Markets that get altered/change through shrinking, fragmenting, emerging, disappearing, etc.

* Failure to adapt to these changes can force a business into closure.

**Online Retailing**

* Easier to gather customer information.

* Avoids selling costs like rent, staff, and overhead costs of operating a store.

* Lower marketing costs.

* Larger customer base.

* 24/7 selling.

* Greater flexibility to update product information.

**How Markets Change**

* **Size of markets:** markets are expected to grow as more sellers enter, but some may decline due to the progression of technologies or changes in customer preferences.

* **Nature of markets:** markets are always in a state of flux.

* **New markets:** new markets emerge as countries like Brazil, India, and China continue developing. New markets also emerge as new products are launched.

**Innovation and Market Growth**

* **Economic Growth:** global living standards increase as people earn and spend more (increase in wealth). This allows businesses to increase the supply of their goods/services to growing markets.

* **Innovation:** Businesses create new products and new needs and wants, which cause the growth of the business. Innovation is also linked to the advancement of technologies. Businesses develop new goods by expanding into new locations, using new marketing strategies, etc.

* **Social Changes:** Social changes cause demand for a product or service to rise or fall. This could happen due to an increase in working mothers.

* **Changes in Legislation:** New laws can cause firms to have higher costs. This can happen. Laws passed include environmental laws, advertising laws, etc.

* **Demographic Changes:** Changes in population, like an increase in birth rates or, aging population, cause an increase or decrease in demand for children’s or elderly services.

**Adapting to Change**

* Failure to adapt to changes in the market can cause loss of market share or business closure.

* **Flexibility:** A business has to be prepared for changes by having flexible working practices, machinery, equipment, policies, and trained employees, in case of sudden changes.

* **Market research:** Developments in markets need to be researched to adapt to competition.

* **Communication** with customers and potential customers should also be kept up to date.

* **Business improvement:** Firms have to improve their products over time to achieve a larger market share.

* **Provide an improved sales and marketing strategy:** A business can improve its efficiency and service, which can help a firm achieve a larger market share.

* **Continuous improvement:** Allows a firm to be more adaptable to changes.

* **Develop a niche:** If a business faces decline, it can survive by serving a niche to retain its loyal consumers for profitability.

### How Competition Affects the Market

* Competition is the rivalry that exists between firms.

* **Businesses** have to encourage consumers to buy their products, and this is done by:

  * Lowering prices.

  * Making their products appear different from those of rivals.

  * Offering better quality products.

  * Using more attractive advertising/promotion.

  * Offering 'extras', such as high-quality service.

* These methods generally lower a firm's profit, but these methods have to be used in order to survive. Firms may try to lower competition by taking over competitors or forming strategic alliances. Small firms are not allowed to enter a market.

* **Consumers:** Consumers benefit from competition as competition provides businesses with an incentive to innovate, which allows for more choices. Consumers may also get lower prices as firms wish to attract consumers with various promotions. In a market without any competition, a customer is likely to be exploited and have a smaller amount of choices as the firm does not have any incentive for innovation.

### Difference between Risk and Uncertainty

* **Risk:** Owners take risks while running a business. This may include investing money into a business to expand or purchasing new materials. Both of these investments have a chance of failure, which is why risk-taking is continued after the business starts as well.

* **Uncertainty:** A market is an externally influenced factor, which means the market is out of the control of the business. An external influence, like an unexpected competitor, advances in technology, could cause issues, but could also provide a step to innovations. These influences are hard to predict, which forces a business to act under the air of uncertainty. Businesses often struggle with decision-making due to the uncertainty.
