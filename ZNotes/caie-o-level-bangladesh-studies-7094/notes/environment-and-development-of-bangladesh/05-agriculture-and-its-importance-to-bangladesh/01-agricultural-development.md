# **Agriculture and its Importance to Bangladesh**

## **Agricultural Development**

* Agriculture is a major source of employment in Bangladesh.

* Agriculture provides employment, especially in rural areas.

* Agriculture contributes to Bangladesh’s GDP by providing food, employment, and export earnings.

* Since 1971, the percentage contribution of agriculture to GDP has decreased, even though total agricultural output has increased.

* This decline in share is because the industrial and service sectors have grown faster than agriculture over time.

* Subsistence Farming vs Commercial Farming

  * Subsistence farming: Small-scale farming where crops are grown mainly for family consumption, with little or no surplus for sale.

  * Commercial farming: Large-scale farming where crops are grown primarily for sale in markets to generate income.

  * Subsistence farming is common in rural Bangladesh, while commercial farming contributes more directly to GDP and national income.

  * Food Crops vs. Cash Crops:

    * Food Crops: Grown primarily for local consumption (e.g., rice, wheat, pulses).

    * Cash Crops: Grown for sale in domestic and international markets (e.g., sugar cane, tea, jute).

* Changes in Production (since 1971):

  * Food Crops: Increases in rice, wheat, and pulse production due to better technology and irrigation.

  * Cash Crops: Increases in sugar cane, tea, and jute production, though jute has faced competition from synthetic fibers.
