# **Health and Education**

## Education

* Education is essential for economic growth and personal development.

* Educational programmes in Bangladesh:

  * Financial support programmes to increase enrolment and reduce dropout, especially for girls. 

  * NGO education programmes that provide access for disadvantaged children 

  *  Awareness programmes that reduce child labour and early marriage so students can stay in school.

* Access and equality:

  * Gaps exist between rural and urban areas.

  * Girls still face more barriers to education in some regions.

  * Barriers like poverty, travel distance, safety concerns, early marriage, and fewer schools and teachers in remote areas.

  * Efforts have improved enrolment and reduced dropout rates.

  * Solutions include stipends, school meals, improved rural facilities, and campaigns encouraging girls’ education.

* Progress in education:

  * Literacy and numeracy rates have improved.

  * More students are completing primary and secondary school.

  * School meals, stipends, and awareness campaigns help reduce child labour and early marriage.

* Education and gender equality:

  * Schools empower girls and support equal opportunities.

  * Educated women are more likely to work and make informed decisions.

* Developing skills:

  * Focus needed on vocational and professional training.

  * Helps meet the demands of the job market and reduce unemployment.

* Who provides education?

  * Government builds schools and hires teachers.

  * Private schools offer alternatives, often in urban areas.

  * NGOs support education in poor and remote communities.

* Remaining challenges:

  * Improve teacher training and classroom facilities.

  * Reduce overcrowding and improve quality of teaching.

  * Ensure all children, especially girls and rural students, complete school.
