# **Health and Education**

## Health Care

* A strong health care system is important for national development and a healthy workforce.

* Health care providers in Bangladesh:

  * Government: Runs public hospitals and provides public hospitals, clinics, and national programmes.

  * Private sector: Offers paid medical services, often quicker access in towns and cities

  * NGOs: Support rural health care, maternal health, vaccinations awareness (e.g. BRAC, Grameen).

  * programmes include:

    * Immunisation programmes, for example Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI).

    * Tuberculosis control programmes, run through the National Tuberculosis Control Programme.

    * Maternal and child health support, including antenatal care, safe delivery support, and postnatal care.

    * Nutrition guidance programmes to reduce malnutrition and improve child and maternal health.

    * Family planning services to support parents and reduce health risks linked to closely spaced births.

* Disease control programmes:

  * Focus on reducing diseases like malaria, tuberculosis, diarrhoea, and dengue.

  * Promote vaccination and public awareness.

  * Disease control programmes reduce illness and death, lower pressure on hospitals, and protect the workforce so economic development can continue.

* Health care improves quality of life by:

  * Reducing infant and maternal deaths.

  * Providing nutrition advice and support for mothers.

  * Offering family planning and mental health services.

  * Better health care improves life expectancy, reduces preventable deaths,

  * Increases people’s ability to work, study, and care for their families.

* Challenges and areas for improvement:

  * Unequal access between urban and rural areas.

  * Shortage of doctors and medicines in remote areas.

  * Need for better infrastructure and trained staff.

  * Focus on making care affordable for all.

  * Improve supply chains for essential medicines in remote areas. 

  *  Strengthen clean water, sanitation, and hygiene education because these reduce waterborne disease and improve public health
