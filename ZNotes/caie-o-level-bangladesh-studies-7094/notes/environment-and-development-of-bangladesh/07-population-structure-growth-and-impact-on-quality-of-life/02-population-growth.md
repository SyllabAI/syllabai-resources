# **Population Structure, Growth,Changes and Impact on Quality of Life**

## Population Growth

* Factors Affecting Growth:

  * Birth Rate: Number of live births per 1,000 people. Higher birth rates lead to faster population growth.

  * Death Rate: Number of deaths per 1,000 people. Lower death rates cause population increases.

  * Infant Mortality: Deaths of infants under 1 year per 1,000 live births. A lower rate indicates better healthcare.

  * Dependency Ratio: The ratio of dependents (children, elderly) to the working-age population. A higher ratio puts pressure on resources.

  * Life Expectancy: Average number of years a person is expected to live. A higher life expectancy suggests better health.

* Falling birth rates: Increased education, family planning, and access to contraception reduce the number of children born, narrowing the base of the population pyramid over time.

* Improved healthcare: Better medical facilities, vaccination programs, and disease control reduce infant and child mortality, increasing the proportion of children surviving to adulthood.

* Longer life expectancy: Advances in healthcare, nutrition, and living standards allow people to live longer, increasing the proportion of older adults and gradually widening the top of the population pyramid.

* These factors together shift the population structure from a broad-based, youthful pyramid to a more rectangular shape, indicating slower population growth and a more balanced age distribution.

* Advantages of Population Growth

  * Provides a large labour force, supporting agriculture, industry, and services.

  * Creates economic potential through increased production, consumption, and domestic markets.

  * Can support innovation and entrepreneurship by having a bigger pool of ideas and skills.

  * Helps sustain cultural, social, and community activities with a larger population base.

* Disadvantage

  * Pressure on housing: Overcrowding and growth of informal settlements in urban areas.

  * Education: Schools may be overcrowded, reducing quality of education.

  * Healthcare: Increased demand on hospitals and clinics, making services less accessible.

  * Employment: Competition for jobs increases, leading to unemployment or underemployment.

  * Food supply: Higher demand can lead to shortages and higher prices.

  * Environment: Overuse of natural resources, deforestation, water scarcity, and pollution.
