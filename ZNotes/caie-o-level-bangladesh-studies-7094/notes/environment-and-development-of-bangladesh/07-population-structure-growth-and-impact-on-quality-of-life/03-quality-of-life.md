# **Population Structure, Growth, and Impact on Quality of Life**

## Quality of Life

### Population and Quality of Life Indicators

* Literacy: Ability to read and write; higher literacy indicates better education.

* Numeracy: Ability to work with numbers; reflects basic education and practical skills.

* Life Expectancy: Average lifespan; higher life expectancy shows better health and living standards.

* Per Capita Income: Average income per person; higher values indicate greater prosperity.

* Wider Indicators: Access to clean water, sanitation, healthcare, housing, and employment are key measures of quality of life.

### Changes Since 1971

* Indicators have improved due to better education, healthcare, infrastructure, and economic development.

* Improvements are generally greater in **urban areas**; rural areas often lag because of less access to services and employment opportunities.

### Impact of Population Growth

* Positive: Provides a larger workforce, supporting economic growth and development.

* Negative: Puts pressure on housing, healthcare, education, food supply, and the environment, reducing quality of life if unmanaged.

### Internal Migration

* Rural to urban migration occurs due to push factors (poverty, poor rural services) and pull factors (jobs, better facilities in cities).

* Effects: Increases urban population, contributes to economic activity, but can cause overcrowding and strain on services in cities.

### Inequality in Quality of Life

* Differences exist between rural and urban areas because urban areas have better infrastructure, more employment opportunities, and greater access to education and healthcare.
