# **Natural Resources**

## Energy

* Renewable vs Non-Renewable Energy:

  * Renewable energy sources: Wind, hydroelectric, tidal, solar, and biofuels.

  * Non-renewable energy sources: Coal, oil, and natural gas.

  * Coal is found in Barapukuria in Dinajpur. Natural gas is concentrated in eastern Bangladesh including Sylhet and Chattogram region.

* Non-Renewable Energy Locations:

  * Coal, oil, and natural gas are used for energy generation, with coal being abundant in some regions.

* Pollution from Energy Generation:

  * Includes air, water, noise, light, and visual pollution from energy production, particularly from fossil fuel-based sources.
  * Energy production releases carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide into the air, creates thermal pollution in rivers and produces ash that pollutes land.

* Hydroelectric Power (HEP):

  * Location and generation: Mostly generated in hilly regions where large dams can be built.
  * The main hydroelectric power station is at Kaptai on the Karnafuli River.

* Development of Clean Energy:

  * Focus on making energy affordable and sustainable while reducing reliance on non-renewable sources.
  * The development of renewable energy supports SDG 7 which focuses on affordable and clean energy.

* Case Study: 

  * Name and location: Solar Home Systems (SHS) programme in rural Bangladesh, especially in off-grid villages where national grid electricity is unavailable.

  * Funding sources: Funded through government support, international donors, and organisations such as NGOs and development partners; households often contribute through small instalment payments.

  * How the energy is produced: Solar panels installed on homes convert sunlight into electricity, which is stored in batteries and used for lighting and small electrical appliances.

  * Contribution to national electrification: SHS increases access to electricity in rural areas, reduces dependence on fossil fuels, and supports Bangladesh’s goal of expanding national electrification.

  * Advantages and disadvantages:

    * Advantages include clean and renewable energy, reduced pollution, improved quality of life, and access to electricity in remote areas.

    * Disadvantages include high initial costs, limited power output, and dependence on sunlight and battery maintenance.
