# **Economic Development**

## Sectors of the Economy and Economic Growth

* Primary Sector: Involves natural resource extraction (e.g., agriculture, fishing, mining).

* Secondary Sector: Involves manufacturing and industry (e.g., textiles, construction).

* Tertiary Sector: Involves services (e.g., healthcare, education, transport).

* Quaternary Sector: involves activities related to knowledge, information, and research rather than producing physical goods or providing direct services. It includes activities that generate, process, and share information and knowledge to support decision-making, innovation, and development.

* ++Changing Contribution of Sectors:++

  * Primary sector (agriculture, fishing, forestry, mining)
    * 1971: Dominant sector, employing most people and contributing the largest share to GDP.

    * Since 1971: Contribution to GDP has gradually declined due to mechanisation, urbanisation, and shift toward industry and services.

    * Still important for employment in rural areas and for food production.
  * Secondary sector (manufacturing and industry)
    * 1971: Small contribution to GDP, mainly traditional industries and cottage industries.

    * Since 1971: Contribution increased steadily, especially with growth in textiles, garments, and industrial production.

    * Employment has increased, but still smaller than primary and service sectors.
  * Tertiary sector (services such as transport, retail, banking, education, health)
    * 1971: Relatively small share of GDP.

    * Since 1971: Contribution expanded rapidly, becoming the largest sector in GDP by recent years.

    * Growth driven by urbanisation, trade, education, healthcare, and financial services.
  * Quaternary sector (knowledge-based activities such as information, research, design, and blogging)
    * 1971: Nearly nonexistent.

    * Since 1971: Emerging rapidly, especially in information technology, research, media, and design services.

    * Contribution still smaller than tertiary sector but growing in importance for innovation and modern economy.

* Gross Domestic Product (GDP): GDP measures the total value of all goods and services produced within a country in a given period, usually a year. It reflects the size and health of the economy.

* Trend in Bangladesh since 1971:

  * 1971 (Independence): Extremely low GDP due to the destruction caused by war; economy mainly agrarian and underdeveloped.

  * 1970s–1980s: Slow growth as the country recovered; GDP increased gradually, supported by agriculture and small-scale industries.

  * 1990s–2000s: Faster growth as manufacturing (especially textiles and garments) and services expanded; GDP growth became more stable.

  * 2010s–present: Significant rise in GDP due to industrialisation, export growth, remittances from overseas workers, and expansion of the service sector.

  * Overall trend: Continuous upward trajectory, with periods of accelerated growth linked to industrialisation, export-led growth, and development policies.

* Economic Growth Comparison:

  * Bangladesh has experienced strong economic growth since 1971, despite challenges such as natural disasters, population pressure, and limited resources.
  * Comparator country: India
    * Both countries are in South Asia and started with similar challenges in the 1970s.

    * Bangladesh’s GDP per capita has grown rapidly in recent decades, narrowing the gap with India.

    * Bangladesh has shown faster improvements in human development indicators like health, education, and poverty reduction compared to India in some areas, despite having a smaller economy overall.
  * What the comparison shows:
    * Bangladesh’s growth trajectory demonstrates resilience and effective development policies.

    * Economic growth has been driven by garment exports, remittances, and service sector expansion.

    * The comparison highlights that, although Bangladesh is smaller in total GDP, it has made significant progress in living standards and economic development relative to India.
