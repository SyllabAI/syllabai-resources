# **Migration and Urban Growth**

## Dhaka urban growth case study

* Clean water supply: Rapid population growth increases demand for safe piped water; shortages and unequal access occur; water quality risks increase where sewage systems and waste disposal are inadequate.

* Power supply: Rising demand from homes, businesses, and industry puts pressure on electricity supply; load shedding and power outages occur; some areas depend on generators.

* Traffic: Increased numbers of vehicles and commuters cause severe congestion; longer journey times reduce productivity and limit access to workplaces, schools, and hospitals.

* Pollution: Air pollution from traffic and industry; water pollution from untreated sewage and industrial waste; noise pollution from traffic and construction; light and visual pollution from dense, unplanned development and advertising.

* Employment: Dhaka offers jobs in garments, construction, and services, but many migrants work in informal employment with low wages and insecure conditions; unemployment or underemployment increases when job creation is slower than population growth.

* Housing: High demand leads to rising rents and overcrowding; informal settlements expand when migrants cannot afford formal housing or when housing supply fails to keep pace with population growth.
