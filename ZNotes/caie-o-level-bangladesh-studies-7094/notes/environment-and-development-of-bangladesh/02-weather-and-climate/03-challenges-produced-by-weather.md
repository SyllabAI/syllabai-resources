# **Weather and Climate**

## Challenges Produced by Weather

* **Cyclones**:

  * Caused by low pressure over the Bay of Bengal.

  * Strong winds, heavy rain, and storm surges cause flooding and destruction.

  * Affects homes, lives, transport, and farming.

* **Drought**:

  * Common in north-west Bangladesh (e.g. Rajshahi, Rangpur).

  * Caused by low rainfall and poor water supply.

  * Leads to crop failure, food shortages, and hardship for farmers.

* **Responses**:

  * Build cyclone shelters, improve forecasting, strengthen embankments.

  * Use irrigation systems, store rainwater, grow drought-resistant crops.

**Cyclone Case Study: Cyclone Sidr (2007)**

### **Formation and Path**

* Formed over the **Bay of Bengal** in November 2007.

* Moved northwards and made landfall in **coastal districts such as Barguna, Patuakhali, Bagerhat, and Khulna**.

### **Main Features**

* **Wind speeds:** around **220 km/h** (Category 4 cyclone).

* **Storm surge:** flooded large areas of low-lying coastal land, especially near river mouths.

* Heavy rainfall caused additional flooding and damage.

### **Impact on People**

* About **3,000 people killed**.

* Millions affected; many families lost homes and belongings.

* Widespread injuries and outbreaks of waterborne diseases.

### **Impact on Economy and Environment**

* Hundreds of thousands of homes damaged or destroyed.

* **Crops, rice fields, and fish farms destroyed**, causing severe livelihood loss.

* Roads, embankments and electricity lines damaged.

* Forest destruction, including areas of the **Sundarbans**.

### **Responses**

* Thousands evacuated to **cyclone shelters** before landfall.

* Emergency relief: food, clean water, medicine, and temporary shelters provided by the government, NGOs, and international organisations.

* Post-cyclone reconstruction of homes and embankments.

* **Early warning system improved** after 2007 with better forecasting, signals, and community-based preparedness.

* Expansion of the cyclone shelter network in coastal districts.

**Drought Case Study: Northwest Bangladesh**

### **Causes**

* **Very low rainfall** during the monsoon season.

* High temperatures and evaporation reduced soil moisture.

* Tube wells, ponds, and small rivers **dried up**.

* Over-use of groundwater in agricultural areas worsened the shortage.

### **Impact on People**

* Drinking water scarcity in rural communities.

* Loss of income for farming families.

* Food shortages in poorer households.

### **Impact on Agriculture and Economy**

* **Crop failure** of rice and other staple crops.

* Reduced livestock production due to lack of fodder and water.

* Increased rural poverty and seasonal migration.

### **Responses**

* **Emergency food aid** distributed to affected families.

* Installation of **deep tube wells** to access groundwater.

* Construction of new **irrigation canals** to bring water from nearby rivers.

* Promotion of **drought-resistant rice varieties** (e.g., BRRI-developed varieties).

* Training farmers in water-efficient farming methods.
