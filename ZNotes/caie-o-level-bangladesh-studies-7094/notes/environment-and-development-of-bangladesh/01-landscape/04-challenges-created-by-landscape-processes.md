# **Landscape**

## **Challenges Created by Landscape Processes**

Rivers and plate tectonics pose major natural hazards affecting Bangladesh.

---

## **River Hazards**

---

### **River Bank Erosion**

**Worst-affected rivers in Bangladesh:**

* **Jamuna** (most destructive, highly braided, shifting channels)

* **Ganges/Padma** (strong currents in monsoon)

* **Meghna** (powerful tidal influence in lower reaches)

**Causes:**

* High river flow during monsoon season

* Deforestation along riverbanks

* Sand mining and human activities

* Frequent channel shifting in major rivers

**Effects:**

* Loss of agricultural land and homesteads

* Displacement of thousands of families

* Destruction of crops and livestock

* Loss of infrastructure (roads, schools, markets)

**Responses:**

* Construction of river embankments and revetments

* Afforestation (tree planting to stabilise banks)

* Resettlement programmes for displaced families

* Dredging to maintain stable river courses

---

### **River Floods (Case Study: 1998 Bangladesh Floods)**

**Causes:**

* Exceptionally heavy monsoon rainfall

* Snowmelt from the Himalayas increasing river volume

* Deforestation and poor drainage systems

* Major rivers (Jamuna–Padma–Meghna) overtopping banks

**Impact on People:**

* **30 million people affected**

* **1,000+ deaths**

* Spread of waterborne diseases (cholera, diarrhoea)

* Millions made homeless

**Environmental Impacts:**

* Soil erosion and loss of topsoil in many areas

* Waterlogging lasting weeks to months

* Long-term damage to crops and farmland fertility

* Contamination of drinking water sources

**Impact on Economy:**

* **2 million tonnes of rice lost**

* **US$2 billion in total damage**

* Roads and bridges destroyed

* Transport system severely disrupted

**Responses:**

* International humanitarian aid

* Flood forecasting and warning systems upgraded

* Construction and reinforcement of embankments

* **Long-term response:** Flood Action Plan (FAP)

  * Improved flood prediction

  * Raised roads and embankments

  * Better urban drainage in Dhaka

## **Why is Bangladesh Prone to Earthquakes?**

* Located near the **Indian Plate–Eurasian Plate boundary**

* Stress builds up along faults near Sylhet, Chattogram, and Dhaka

* Soft alluvial soil increases shaking intensity

* High population density and weak buildings increase vulnerability

---

### **Earthquake Case Study: 2017 Bangladesh Earthquake**

**Details:**

* Magnitude: **around 5–6**

* Worst-affected areas: **Dhaka and Sylhet**

**Damage Done:**

* Cracks in buildings; some old structures collapsed

* Panic among residents, temporary evacuation

* Power failures in some neighbourhoods

**Actions to Reduce Impact:**

* Earthquake-resistant building codes

* Public awareness and school drills

* Emergency preparedness training

* Strengthening critical infrastructure
