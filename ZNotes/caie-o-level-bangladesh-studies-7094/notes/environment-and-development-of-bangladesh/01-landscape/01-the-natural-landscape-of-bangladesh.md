# **Landscape**

## The Natural Landscape of Bangladesh

Bangladesh has a diverse landscape made up of floodplains, deltas, hills, and coastal plains, shaped mainly by the country’s major rivers.

---

##  **Main Floodplains of Bangladesh**

### **Brahmaputra–Jamuna Floodplain**

* Very wide and formed by the Brahmaputra (Jamuna) river system.

* Characterised by *braided channels* and *frequent channel shifting*.

* Receives large amounts of sediment; prone to annual flooding.

* Supports agriculture, especially rice and jute.

### **Ganges–Padma Floodplain**

* Low-lying and extremely fertile.

* Formed by the Ganges (Padma) river system.

* Intensively cultivated: rice, wheat, sugarcane, vegetables.

* High population density due to fertile land and access to water.

### **Meghna Floodplain**

* Low-lying areas in eastern Bangladesh.

* Southern parts are *tidal-influenced* due to proximity to the Bay of Bengal.

* Includes the Tippera Surface as an uplifted sub-region.

---

##  **Delta Region**

### **Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna (GBM) Delta**

* World’s largest delta.

* Very low relief and heavily criss-crossed by distributaries and canals.

* Extremely fertile due to continuous sediment deposition.

* Highly vulnerable to floods, cyclones, storm surges, and riverbank erosion.

* Major rivers forming this delta:

  * Brahmaputra

  * Jamuna

  * Padma

  * Meghna

---

##  **Coastal Plains**

### **Southern Districts** (Khulna, Barishal, Noakhali, Cox’s Bazar)

* Include tidal flats, estuaries, chars, and mangrove swamps.

* Regularly flooded by tides, river floods, and storm surges.

* Home to the Sundarbans, the world’s largest mangrove forest.

* Formation of new chars due to continuous sediment deposition.

---

##  **Major Hills of Bangladesh**

Although most of Bangladesh is floodplain, the country also has several upland and hilly regions:

### **Chattogram Hill Tracts (Southeast)**

* Highest hills in Bangladesh.

* Forested and inhabited by indigenous communities.

* Important for forestry, bamboo, fruit cultivation, and hydroelectric power (Kaptai Dam).

### **Madhupur Tract (Central Bangladesh)**

* Elevated plateau with red soil.

* Covered partly with sal forests.

* Lies between the Brahmaputra and old Madhupur jungle region.

### **Barind (Borendra) Tract (Northwest)**

* Old alluvial terrace region.

* Slightly higher than surrounding floodplains.

* Drier climate; important for wheat and mango cultivation.

### **Tippera Surface (East-Central Bangladesh)**

* Slightly uplifted area.

* Part of the greater Meghna floodplain system.

* Supports agriculture and settlement due to higher ground.

---

##  **Main Rivers of Bangladesh**

Bangladesh’s landforms are shaped by its major rivers:

* Jamuna – Wide braided river, major route for transport and agriculture.

* Brahmaputra – Enters from the north; carries huge sediment loads; flood-prone.

* Padma (Ganges) – Vital for irrigation, fisheries, and agriculture; joins Meghna.

* Meghna – Large river system forming the final outlet to the Bay of Bengal.

* Surma – Flows through Sylhet; important for the haor basin.

* Tista – Northern river; used heavily for irrigation.

* Karnaphuli – Southeastern river; crucial for hydroelectric power and Chattogram port.
