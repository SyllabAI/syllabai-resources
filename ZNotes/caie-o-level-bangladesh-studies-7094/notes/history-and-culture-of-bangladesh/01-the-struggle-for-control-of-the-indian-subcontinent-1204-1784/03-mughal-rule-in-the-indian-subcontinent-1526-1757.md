## Mughal Rule in the Indian Subcontinent (1526–1757)

### **Establishment of Mughal Rule (Babur, 1526)**

* Babur was invited by local nobles to overthrow Ibrahim Lodi.

* Battle of Panipat (1526): Babur defeats Ibrahim Lodi.

  * Army sizes: Babur – \~12,000 men, Lodi – \~100,000 men.

  * Why Babur won: Superior tactics, use of gunpowder artillery, mobile cavalry, defensive positioning, and innovative formations like the tulughma.

### **Key Mughal Emperors**

* ### Humayun (1530–1556): Lost to Sher Shah Suri; exiled to Persia. With the help of the Safavid rulers of Persia, he regained control of the empire.

  * had some significant achievments like reorganised the army and introduced better use of artillery after regaining the throne.

  * Re-established central authority and continued Babur’s use of Persian culture at court.

* Akbar (1556–1605): Expanded the empire, annexed Bengal, and promoted religious tolerance.

  * Abolished jizya, introduced the Mansabdari system and Todar Mal system.

  * Built Fatehpur Sikri, promoted Din-i-Ilahi, and implemented Sulh-i-Kul (peace with all religions).

  * Afghan Rulers in Bengal under Akbar 

    These rulers were not Mughal Subahdars — they were Afghan rulers resisting Mughal expansion.

    * Sulaiman Karrani

      * Ruled Bengal as an Afghan Sultan.

      * Extended control to Orissa.

      * Maintained semi-independent rule while acknowledging Mughal supremacy indirectly.

    * Daud Khan Karrani

      * Last Afghan ruler of Bengal.

      * Rebelled against the Mughals.

      * Defeated by Akbar’s general Munim Khan, leading to Bengal becoming a Mughal province (Subah).

* Jahangir (1605–1627): Promoted justice, arts, and trade with the British, giving them a strong foothold.

  * Influenced by Nur Jehan.

  * Introduced Chain of Justice, Dustur ul Amal, and built the Shalimar Garden in Kashmir.

* Shah Jahan (1628–1658): Expanded empire through Deccan campaigns.

  * Notable architecture: Taj Mahal, Moti Masjid.

  * Centralized administration and took steps for famine relief.

* Aurangzeb (1658–1707): Expanded empire but faced revolts and drained resources.

  * Succession after the Battle of Dharmat and Battle of Samugarh.

  * Subjugated Bijapur and Golconda; expanded into Safavid territories.

  * Implemented Fatwa-e-Alamgiri, reimposed jizya, established state monopolies.

  * Architecture: Badshahi Mosque; promoted educational reforms.

### **Bengal’s Role in the Mughal Empire**

### **Important Subahdars (Provincial Governors) of Mughal Bengal**

* Islam Khan

  * First effective Mughal Subahdar of Bengal under Jahangir.

  * Moved the capital to Dhaka.

  * Established firm Mughal authority over Bengal.

* Shaista Khan

  * One of Bengal’s most powerful governors.

  * Drove out the Arakanese and Portuguese from Chittagong.

  * Expanded trade and reduced rice prices significantly.

* Mir Jumla

  * Strengthened Mughal control in the northeast.

  * Led military campaigns in Assam.

* Murshid Quli Khan

  * Reorganised revenue administration.

  * Moved the capital to Murshidabad.

  * Considered the first semi-independent Nawab of Bengal.

---

### **Bengal’s Importance under Mughal Rule**

* Bengal became one of the richest Mughal provinces.

* Famous for textiles, especially muslin.

* Major hub of trade, agriculture, and revenue collection.

* Strategic ports linked Bengal to global trade networks.

### **Decline of the Mughal Empire**

* ### Aurangzeb’s role in decline:

  * Long wars in the Deccan and against Marathas drained treasury.

  * Religious policies caused resentment among Rajputs, Sikhs, and Hindus.

  * Poor provincial control; centralization failed.

* ### Other reasons:

  * Internal court rivalries (e.g., Sayyid Brothers).

  * Economic problems, heavy taxation, mismanagement.

  * Rise of regional powers (Marathas, Nawabs).

  * Foreign invasions (Nader Shah, Ahmed Shah Abdali).

  * Growth of European influence (British East India Company).
