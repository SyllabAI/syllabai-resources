## British Takeover and Rule (1600–1784)

### **Growth of the East India Company (EIC)**

* Founded in 1600 by royal charter from Queen Elizabeth I to trade in spices, silk, and indigo.

* High European demand for spices, textiles, indigo and saltpetre.

* Huge profits due to cheap production costs in Bengal.

* Advanced European naval power gave them an advantage in long-distance

* Emperor Farrukhsiyar granted duty-free trade rights in 1717.

* Battle of Plassey (1757): Robert Clive defeated Nawab Siraj ud Daula, who was betrayed by Mir Jafar. Clive appointed Governor of Bengal.

* Battle of Buxar (1764): Clive defeated combined forces of Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II, Nawab Mir Qasim of Bengal, and Nawab Shuja ud Daula of Oudh.

* Treaty of Allahabad (1765): EIC granted Diwani rights (revenue collection) over Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa.

### **Roles of Nawabs and Diwans**

* Diwans: Managed taxes and finances under Company control. After Buxar, EIC gained full Diwani rights, leading to wealth drain and decline of Bengal’s economy.

* Nawabs: Siraj ud Daula was the last independent Nawab of Bengal. Post-Plassey, Nawabs were puppet rulers under EIC, unable to make independent military or administrative decisions, blamed by the people for misrule.

### **Dual Administration (1765–1772)**

* Naib Diwan and Naib Nazim assisted in administration under Company supervision.

* Consequences: Misuse of Dastaks ruined Indian merchants and domestic industries.

* Land allocations to highest bidders burdened peasants, leading many to leave lands, contributing to the Bengal famine of 1770.

* District divisions prioritized Company revenue over peasant welfare.

### **Direct British Rule (1784)**

* By the 1770s, the EIC was corrupt, bankrupt, and responsible for Bengal famine.

* Warren Hastings (Governor-General) raised concerns in Britain; government intervened.

* India Act (1784): Introduced by Prime Minister Pitt, created a Board of Control to supervise the Company’s political, military, and civil affairs, bringing Bengal under direct British rule.
