## Life, Work, and Culture in Bengal (1204–1784)

### **Social Structure**

* Hindu caste system coexisted with Muslim rule

* zamindars (landlords) held local authority through tax collection.

* Zamindars acted as intermediaries between peasants and rulers.

* Urban elites included wealthy merchants, nobles, and scholars (ulema and pundits).

* Artisans and peasants sustained the agrarian economy.

* Merchants, artisans, and scholars thrived in both urban and rural areas.

### **Daily Life & Customs**

* Religion: Hinduism and Islam coexisted, with Sufis fostering harmony.

* Food & Clothing: Rice, fish, lentils, and spices formed staples; Persian cuisine influenced elites; muslin, dhotis, saris, and turbans were common.

* Women: Central in households; elite women had limited education, while rural women worked in farming and cottage industries.

### **Work & Economy**

* Agriculture (rice, jute, sugarcane, lentils) formed the base, while Dhaka, Sonargaon, and Murshidabad thrived as trade hubs.

* Cottage industries like muslin weaving, pottery, and metalwork supported commerce through weekly haats (markets) with barter and coin trade.

### **Entertainment**

* Music & Festivals: Folk and classical music (baul, bhatiali), religious festivals (Eid, Durga Puja) with fairs, songs, and drama.

* Games & Pastimes: Chess, wrestling, storytelling, and puppet shows.

### **Transport & Infrastructure**

* Rivers (Ganges, Brahmaputra) were vital trade routes; land transport by bullock carts and foot.

* Mughal rule improved roads; later British expanded road and river networks for trade control.

### **Cultural Developments**

* Literature & Language: Persian was the official language, while Bengali poetry and epics flourished.

* Architecture & Religion: Terracotta mosques, forts, and Mughal designs; Sufi saints encouraged Hindu-Muslim unity.
