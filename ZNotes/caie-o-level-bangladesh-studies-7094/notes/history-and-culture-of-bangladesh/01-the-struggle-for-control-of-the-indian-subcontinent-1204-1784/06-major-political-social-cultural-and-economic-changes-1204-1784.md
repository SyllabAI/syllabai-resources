## Major Political, Social, Cultural, and Economic Changes (1204–1784)

### **Political**

Bengal shifted from Hindu kingdoms to Muslim Sultanates, then Mughals, and finally British control.

* ### Key events:

  * 1204: Bakhtiyar Khilji’s invasion

  * 1526: Babur’s victory at Panipat

  * 1757: Battle of Plassey

  * 1765: Treaty of Allahabad – Diwani rights (revenue collection) given to the East India Company

  * 1765–1772: Dual Administration under Company and Nawabs

  * 1784: India Act – Bengal under direct British rule

* Nawabs played central roles before British control, managing administration under Mughal suzerainty and later as figureheads under the EIC.

* 1784 India Act introduced Bengal under complete british rule

* Created a Board of Control in Britain to supervise the Company’s political, military and civil
  affairs.

* Separated the Company’s commercial functions from its governing role.

* Increased government oversight to reduce corruption and mismanagement.

### **Social**

* Islam spread through Sufis while Hindu customs and caste system continued.

* Zamindars remained powerful intermediaries under Sultans, Mughals, and Nawabs, collecting taxes and maintaining local authority.

* Women’s roles were restricted but they supported farming, cottage industries, and occasionally participated in cultural life.

### **Cultural**

* **Language:** Persian became the official language of administration while Bengali literature and poetry
  flourished.

* **Architecture:** Terracotta mosques, Mughal forts, Katra Mosque, Adina Mosque.

* **Literature:** Bengali poetry, Persian chronicles, poets like Alaol.

* **Arts:** music, dance, architecture, literature; Baul music, temple and mosque terracotta, early
  Bengali poetry.

### **Economic / Agriculture**

* **Trade:** Bengal became famous for fine muslin and silk; ports like Sonargaon and Chittagong linked
  Bengal to the wider Indian Ocean trade.

* **Mughal period:** Bengal was called the ‘Paradise of Nations’ due to prosperity.

* **Common crops:** Rice, wheat, sugarcane, jute, cotton.

* **Under Dual Administration (post-1765):** Mismanagement, exploitation, and over-taxation under Diwani rights led to the Bengal Famine (1770), destroying the economy.

* Agriculture remained central throughout, though control passed increasingly into colonial hands.
