## Partition of Bengal (1905–1911)

### Causes & Interpretations of the 1905 Partition:

Background:

* Bengal was the largest province in British India with around 80 million population and difficult to administer.

* Lord Curzon, the Viceroy, decided to partition Bengal into East Bengal and Assam (Muslim majority) and West Bengal (Hindu majority).

British Justifications:

* Claimed the partition would improve administrative efficiency.

Political Motives:

* To weaken Bengal nationalism, largely driven by educated Hindus in Calcutta.

* To control Hindus who were becoming hostile to British rule; the INC was demanding greater Indian participation.

* To divide Hindus and Muslims, whose relations had improved after the 1857 war.

* Hindu revivalist movements were gaining strength; the British sought Muslim loyalty by giving them a majority in East Bengal.

Opposition View:

* Seen as a “Divide and Rule” tactic to weaken Hindu-Muslim unity.

---

### Reactions & Effects:

Hindu Reaction:

* Viewed the partition as a “Divide and Rule” tactic, causing outrage.

* Led to mass protests, boycott of British goods, and the Swadeshi Movement.

Muslim Reaction:

* Supported the partition as it gave them political importance in East Bengal.

* Provided new opportunities in education, employment, and administration.

Simla Deputation (1906):

* Led by Aga Khan; Muslim leaders met Lord Minto to request separate electorates and greater Muslim representation.

Formation of All-India Muslim League (1906):

* Established in Dhaka; led by Nawab Salimullah Khan, Aga Khan, and later Mr. Jinnah.

* Aimed to protect Muslim political rights, promote loyalty to the British, and prevent Hindu domination as INC focused more on Hindu demands.

---

### Opposition to the Partition:

* Growth of nationalist newspapers, slogans, and secret societies.

* Some extremists, like Aurobindo Ghosh and Bal Gangadhar Tilak, supported militant actions.

---

### Reversal of the Partition (1911):

* Partition reversed due to rising unrest and failed attempts to gain Hindu support.

* Strengthened Muslim belief in the need for a separate political identity, increasing support for the Muslim League.

* Marked a turning point in Hindu-Muslim relations, setting the stage for separate communal politics.

* Attempts to promote shared responsibility

* They were effective in ways like:

* Gave some Indians a louder political voice (e.g. Muslim League), but real power still stayed
  with the British.

* Partition and its reversal increased Hindu–Muslim tension instead of creating unity.

* So attempts at ‘shared responsibility’ between 1905–11 were only partly successful.
