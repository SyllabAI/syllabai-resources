# **The years of growing opposition, 1784-1911**

## Life, Work & Culture in Bengal (1784–1911)

### **Economic Changes:**

Agriculture:

* Permanent Settlement Act imposed high land taxes on zamindars and peasants.

* Shift towards cash crops like jute, indigo, and opium caused famines and rural suffering.

* Led to peasant revolts like the Indigo Revolt.

* Decline in traditional village economy; increased dependence on British trade markets.

* Widened the gap between zamindars and peasants.

Industry & Trade:

* Bengal famous for muslin, silk, and handicrafts declined due to cheap machine-made British textiles; led to deindustrialization.

* Skilled artisans forced into manual labor or agriculture.

* Rise of jute mills (Calcutta), tea plantations (Assam, Sylhet), and coal mining (Raniganj).

* Ports of Calcutta and Chittagong expanded for exports.

* Bengal became a hub for raw materials and a center for British imports; local traders lost independence.

* Western-style banking and finance introduced.

Transport & Communication:

* Road and rail networks developed mainly for troop movement.

* Postal and telegraph services introduced.

### Cultural Developments:

Education:

* British established western-style education and English-medium schools.

* Sparked the Bengal Renaissance and rise of reformers like Raja Rammohun Roy, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, and Rabindranath Tagore.

Literature:

* Bankim Chandra Chatterjee wrote *Anandamath*, inspiring nationalism.

* Rabindranath Tagore produced poetry, songs, and plays blending tradition with modern thought.

* Printing press popularized books, newspapers, and journals (e.g., *Tattvabodhini Patrika*).

Music:

* Fusion of classical folk with western melodies.

* Emergence of Rabindra Sangeet and Baul music traditions.

* Music became a tool of social reform and identity.

  Dance:

* Folk dance and theatre (jatra) remained popular, spreading social and political ideas to
  ordinary people.

Architecture:

* British introduced colonial styles.

* Blend of Mughal, Bengali, and European styles.

* Revival of terracotta temple art continued in rural Bengal.

Jobs:

* new urban middle-class jobs emerged like lawyers, teachers, clerks in administration
