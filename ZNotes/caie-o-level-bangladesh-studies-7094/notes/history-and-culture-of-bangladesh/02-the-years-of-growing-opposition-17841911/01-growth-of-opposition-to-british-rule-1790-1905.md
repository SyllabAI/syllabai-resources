# **The years of growing opposition, 1784-1911**

## Growth of Opposition to British Rule (1790–1905)

### Causes of Resistance

### Political & Military Causes

* Doctrine of Lapse (1852): Annexed Indian states without male heirs.

* Expansionist Policies: Indian rulers lost political autonomy under British control.

* Loss of Power of Mughal Emperor: Decline of Mughal authority weakened Indian unity and increased resentment.

* Subsidiary Alliance System: Forced rulers to accept British troops and lose independence.

* Lack of Indian Representation: Indians excluded from higher government and administrative posts.

* Discrimination in Army Ranks: Indian soldiers (sepoys) faced unequal pay and racial bias.

* Cultural Disrespect: Use of Enfield rifles with cow and pig fat insulted Hindu and Muslim soldiers.

* Abolition of Local Forces: Traditional armies of Indian rulers disbanded, creating unemployment and anger.

---

### Economic Causes

* Permanent Settlement Act (1793):

  * Imposed high land taxes on zamindars and peasants.

  * Encouraged exploitation by zamindars.

  * Many peasants lost land due to inability to pay taxes.

  * Focused on revenue collection rather than agricultural development.

* Destruction of Local Industries:

  * Traditional crafts, especially Bengal’s textile and muslin trade, were destroyed.

  * British flooded India with cheap machine-made goods.

* Indigo Planters’ Exploitation:

  * Peasants forced to grow indigo at low rates under harsh European planters.

* Famines:

  * Heavy taxation and cash-crop cultivation led to repeated famines.

* Unfair Trade & Revenue Policies:

  * British policies favored colonial profits, burdening Indian rural classes.

---

### Religious Causes

* Christian Missionary Activity: Forceful conversions caused resentment among Hindus and Muslims.

* Religious Interference: Reforms like abolition of sati and widow remarriage were seen as attacks on religion.

* Western Education: Created fear of cultural loss and religious erosion.

---

### Early Movements of Opposition

### Fakir and Sannyasi Rebellion (1770–1820)

* Leaders: Majnu Shah and other Muslim Fakirs, along with Hindu Sannyasis.

* Causes: Economic hardships, famine, and heavy British taxation.

* Nature: Joint Hindu–Muslim armed resistance against British control.

* Methods: Guerrilla attacks on British officials and tax collectors.

* Outcome: Early symbol of Hindu–Muslim unity; later crushed by British forces.

---

### Titu Mir’s Rebellion (1827–1831)

* Leader: Titu Mir (Mir Nisar Ali).

* Causes: Oppression by indigo planters and Hindu landlords allied with the British (e.g., “beard tax”).

* Nature: Rural Islamic resistance movement.

* Famous For: Bamboo Fort defense at Narkelberia.

* Impact: Symbolized defiance of social and religious oppression.

---

#### Indigo Revolt (1859–1860)

* Location: Nadia and Jessore districts, Bengal.

* Leaders: Local peasants supported by sympathetic landlords.

* Causes: Forced indigo cultivation under exploitative European planters.

* Outcome: Planters fled several areas; British formed the Indigo Commission (1860).

* Legacy: Inspired later peasant uprisings like the Deccan Riots (1875).

---

#### Faraizi Movement (Early–Mid 19th Century)

* Founder: Haji Shariatullah.

* Aims:

  * Religious revival of Islam.

  * Peasant rights in Bengal.

  * Resistance against British rule and Hindu landlord exploitation.

* Beliefs: Condemned un-Islamic practices and corruption.

* Continuation: Led by Dudu Miyan after Shariatullah’s death.

* Impact: Strengthened Muslim identity and influenced later nationalist movements.

---

### Bengal Renaissance & Reform Movements

* Raja Rammohun Roy:

  * Founder of Brahmo Samaj.

  * Promoted monotheism, women’s education, and social reform.

  * Opposed sati and supported modern education.

  * Regarded as the Father of Indian Reform.

* Henry Louis Vivian Derozio:

  * Led the Young Bengal Movement.

  * Advocated free thinking, rationalism, and secularism.

* Haji Muhammad Mohsin:

  * Founded charitable trusts and madrasas for poor students.

  * Promoted Muslim education and social welfare.

* Nawab Abdul Latif:

  * Championed English education for Muslims.

  * Worked for modernization and intellectual reform.

* Syed Ameer Ali:

  * Encouraged Muslim political awareness and self-determination.

  * Promoted unity between education and political activism.

---

### Impact

* Laid the foundation for Indian nationalism.

* Inspired the Revolt of 1857 and later independence movements.

* Sparked intellectual awakening and social reform in Bengal.

* Encouraged unity among Indians across religious and social lines.

* Helped spread anti-British feeling and the idea that Indians should rule themselves.

* Encouraged cooperation between Hindus and Muslims in some movements (e.g. Fakir–
  Sannyasi).

* Prepared the ground for later nationalist organisations and the 1857 revolt.
