# **In search of independence, 1911–1971**

## Opposition to British Rule

### **Reasons for opposition**

* Economic Exploitation

  * Heavy taxation on Indian farmers and businesses enriched the British government and industries.

  * Local industries suffered, particularly Bengal’s jute and textile sectors, as raw materials were exported to Britain.

  * Indian artisans and traders faced declining incomes and market control shifted to British companies.

* Political & Religious Tensions

  * British policies often favored certain religious or social groups, causing divisions among communities.

  * Repressive laws, such as the Rowlatt Act, limited Indian participation in governance, fueling anger and resentment.

  * Lack of representation in legislative councils left Indians without a voice in decisions affecting their own country.

### Methods of Resistance

* Swadeshi Movement (1905–1911)

  * Encouraged boycott of British goods to weaken colonial economic control.

  * Promoted local industry, crafts, and textile production, fostering self-reliance.

  * Led to the revival of Indian entrepreneurship and spread nationalist ideas.

* Armed Resistance in Bengal

  * Secret revolutionary groups such as Anushilan Samiti and Jugantar carried out bombings and assassinations against British officials.

  * Focused on direct action to challenge colonial authority and inspire local support.

  * Though limited in scale, it created fear among British authorities and demonstrated active resistance.

* Lucknow Pact (1916)

  * Agreement between the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League to cooperate for self-rule.

  * Leaders such as Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Muhammad Ali Jinnah played key roles in negotiating the pact.

  * Marked a significant political collaboration between Hindus and Muslims for nationalist goals.

* Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

  * Led by Mahatma Gandhi, promoting mass boycott of British goods, services, schools, and courts.

  * In Bengal, it resulted in strikes, protests, and widespread economic disruption.

  * Strengthened national consciousness and encouraged peaceful civil disobedience.

* 1923 Bengal Pact

  * Aimed at resolving Hindu-Muslim tensions in Bengal.

  * Sought religious harmony and political cooperation between communities.

  * Laid groundwork for future negotiations and efforts to maintain unity in the nationalist movement.
