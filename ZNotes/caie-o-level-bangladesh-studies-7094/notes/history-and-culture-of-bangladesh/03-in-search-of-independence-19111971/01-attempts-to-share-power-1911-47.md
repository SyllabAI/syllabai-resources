# **In search of independence, 1911–1971**

## Attempts to Share Power (1911–194

### **1909 Indian Councils Act (Morley-Minto Reforms)**

* Introduced separate electorates for Muslims

  * Allowed Muslims to elect their own representatives

  * This was intended to give Muslims a distinct political voice and safeguard their interests

* Gave some representation to Indians in legislative councils

  * Indians could now participate in discussions and advise on policy

  * However, real power and decision-making authority **r**emained firmly with the British officials

  * Legislative councils had limited powers; most proposals could be vetoed by British members

### **1919 Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms**

* Introduced Dyarchy (dual government) at the provincial level

  * Division of subjects into “Transferred” and “Reserved” categories

  * Indians could administer “Transferred” subjects while British retained control over “Reserved” subjects

* Indians were given control over:

  * Education policies

  * Health administration

  * Agriculture and local development

* British retained control over:

  * Finance and budgetary matters

  * Law and order, police, and civil administration

* This system was an attempt to gradually introduce self-governance

  * Created tensions due to limited real power for Indians

### **1927 Simon Commission**

* Sent by the British government to review constitutional reforms in India

  * Commission did not include any Indian members, leading to widespread anger

* Sparked mass protests across India

  * Political parties like Congress and the Muslim League boycotted the commission

  * Indians demanded full representation in decision-making

  * The slogan “Simon Go Back” became popular during protests

* Highlighted the growing Indian demand for complete self-rule

### **1935 Government of India Act**

* Gave provinces limited autonomy, allowing Indian ministers to handle provincial affairs

* Maintained British control over central government

  * Defense, foreign affairs, and finance remained under British authority

* Created a federal structure combining central and provincial governments

* Aimed to prepare India for self-rule gradually, but:

  * Did not satisfy Indian political parties

  * Congress and other leaders demanded full independence, not limited autonomy

* Reform Acts increased Indian participation in government, but real authority remained with British officials, making shared responsibility limited and ineffective.

* Political disagreements between Congress and the Muslim League, along with growing Hindu–Muslim tensions, prevented the development of a stable and accepted power-sharing system.

* By 1947, Indian political opinion had shifted towards full independence, showing that attempts at shared responsibility had failed to satisfy nationalist demands.
