# **In search of independence, 1911–1971**

## Rejection & Consequences

### **1919 Rowlatt Act**

* Allowed the British government to imprison anyone suspected of anti-British activities without trial

  * Suspicion alone was sufficient for detention

  * Introduced harsh measures that curtailed civil liberties

* Met with strong protests across India

  * Sparked unrest in multiple cities

  * Fueled nationalist movements and anger against colonial rule

### **1919 Amritsar Massacre (Jallianwala Bagh Massacre)**

* General Dyer’s troops fired on a peaceful crowd gathered for a festival and protest

* Over 1,000 unarmed civilians were killed, thousands injured

* Created widespread outrage across India

  * Unified many Indians in opposition to British rule

  * Strengthened demands for independence and self-governance

### 1928 Nehru Report

* Proposed a united India with a common constitution

* Suggested abolishing separate electorates for Muslims

  * Aimed at national integration

* Was rejected by the Muslim League

  * Muslims feared marginalization and loss of political representation

### **1929 Jinnah’s 14 Points**

* Outlined key demands for Muslim rights

  * Protection of cultural, religious, and educational interests

  * Safeguards against domination by Hindu-majority political parties

* Called for autonomy in provinces with significant Muslim populations

* Became the foundation for Muslim League’s political strategy

### **1940 Lahore Resolution**

* Passed by the Muslim League at its annual session in Lahore

* Called for separate states for Muslim-majority regions

  * Laid the groundwork for the demand for Pakistan

* Emphasized political and religious autonomy for Muslims

### **1946 Delhi Proposals**

* Proposed by the Muslim League as constitutional safeguards for Muslims

  * Aimed to secure autonomy and rights in a future independent India

* Rejected by the Congress

  * Increased political tensions between Hindus and Muslims

  * Contributed to the eventual partition of India
