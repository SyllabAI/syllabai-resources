# In search of independence, 1911–1971

## Life, work & culture, 1911–71

* Life in villages

  * Most people lived in rural areas and depended on agriculture.

  * Many farmers were sharecroppers working on land owned by jotedars.

  * Poverty was common due to low incomes and lack of land ownership.

  * Villages were frequently affected by floods and cyclones.

  * Education facilities were limited, mostly primary schools.

  * Healthcare was poorly developed with few hospitals or trained doctors.

* Life in towns and cities

  * Major urban centres included Calcutta, Dhaka and Chattogram.

  * A growing middle class emerged, including clerks, lawyers, teachers and traders.

  * Industrial employment increased in jute mills, ports and railways.

  * Urban areas had better transport, education and healthcare than villages.

* Education

  * The number of schools and colleges increased during this period.

  * Bengali-medium education expanded and strengthened Bengali identity.

  * Students became politically active in nationalist and independence movements.

* Literature and poetry

  * Bengali literature flourished and reflected social and political change.

  * Rabindranath Tagore promoted humanist and cultural values.

  * Kazi Nazrul Islam wrote revolutionary and anti-colonial poetry.

  * Jasimuddin focused on rural life and folk traditions.

  * Begum Rokeya supported women’s education and rights through her writing.

* Music and performance

  * Popular music included Rabindra Sangeet and Nazrul Geeti.

  * Folk music such as Baul songs remained influential.

  * Jatra theatre was a common form of entertainment.

  * Radio and films later helped spread Bengali culture widely.

* Art and architecture

  * Art developed as a way to express social issues.

  * Zainul Abedin’s famine sketches highlighted poverty and suffering.

  * Urban growth led to government buildings, cinemas, mosques and temples.

  * Architecture showed a mix of colonial and local styles.
