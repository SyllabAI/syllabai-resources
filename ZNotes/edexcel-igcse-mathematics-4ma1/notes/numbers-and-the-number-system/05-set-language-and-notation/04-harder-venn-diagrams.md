## Harder Venn Diagrams

* When working with Venn Diagrams that have $3$ sets that all overlap, the number of members in the intersections between $2$ sets will be the number of members shared between the $2$ sets minus the total number of members shared between the $3$ sets.

  $32$ students were asked whether they had a desktop computer ($D$), a laptop ($L$) or a tablet ($T$).

  $19$ students had a desktop computer.

  $17$ students had a laptop.

  $16$ students had a tablet.

  $9$ students have both a desktop computer and a laptop.

  $11$ students have both a desktop computer and a tablet.

  $7$ students have both a laptop and a tablet.

  $5$ students have all three.

* Subtract the number of members in the overall intersection from each intersection.

  Those who had **both** a desktop computer and a laptop **only** $= 9 - 5 = 4$

  Those who had **both** a desktop computer and a tablet **only** $= 11 - 5 = 6$

  Those who had **both** a laptop and a tablet **only** $= 7 - 5 = 2$

* Subtract the number between each intersection from the total number of members in each set to find the number of members excluding the overlaps.

  Those who had a desktop computer **only** $= 19 - 5 - 4 - 6 = 4$

  Those who had a laptop **only** $= 17 - 5 - 4 - 2 = 6$

  Those who had a tablet **only** $= 16 - 5 - 6 - 2 = 3$

* The number of members in the universal set will be the total number of members given minus the sum of all the members inside of the Venn Diagram.

  $\xi = 32 - 5 - 4 - 6 - 2 - 4 - 6 - 3 = 2$
