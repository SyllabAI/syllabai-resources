## Rationalising the Denominator & Manipulating Surds

*The following notes are part of the higher tier mathematics course. You need not know the following notes if you are taking the foundation tier mathematics course.*

---

### The difference of two squares

* The difference of two squares allows us to quickly factorise or expand algebraic expressions.

  $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$

* You should be able to spot this rule. Look for a square number being subtracted from another square number, or two brackets with different signs and the same terms as shown above.

  $x^2 - 25 = (x + 5)(x - 5)$

  $4x^2 - 9 = (2x + 3)(2x - 3)$

  $(6x + 10)(6x - 10) = 36x^2 - 100$

  $(8x + 9)(8x - 9) = 64x^2 - 81$

### Rationalising the Denominatior

* If the denominator of a fraction is a surd, you can make the denominator a rational number by the following ways:

* If the denominator is only a single surd, multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the surd.

  $\frac{5}{\sqrt{3}} \rightarrow \frac{5}{\sqrt{3}} \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{(\sqrt{3})^2} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}$

  $\frac{4 + 2\sqrt{5}}{6\sqrt{11}} \rightarrow \frac{4 + 2\sqrt{5}}{6\sqrt{11}} \times \frac{6\sqrt{11}}{6\sqrt{11}} = \frac{(4 + 2\sqrt{3})(6\sqrt{11})}{(6\sqrt{11})^2} = \frac{24\sqrt{11} + 12\sqrt{55}}{36(11)} = \frac{24\sqrt{11} + 12\sqrt{55}}{396} = \frac{12(2\sqrt{11} + \sqrt{55})}{396} = \frac{2\sqrt{11} + \sqrt{55}}{33}$

* If the denominator contains multiple terms, multiply both the numerator and the denominator by the denominator **with the sign switched**.

  $\frac{4}{3 + \sqrt{2}} \rightarrow \frac{4}{3 + \sqrt{2}} \times \frac{3 - \sqrt{2}}{3 - \sqrt{2}} = \frac{4(3 - \sqrt{2})}{(3 + \sqrt{2})(3 - \sqrt{2})}$

* Use difference of two squares to easily expand the denominator.

  $\frac{4(3 - \sqrt{2})}{(3 + \sqrt{2})(3 - \sqrt{2})} = \frac{12-4\sqrt{2}}{3^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2} = \frac{12-4\sqrt{2}}{9 - 2} = \frac{12-4\sqrt{2}}{7}$

* Here is a another worked example:

  $\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{6} - 4} \rightarrow \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{5\sqrt{6} - 4} \times \frac{5\sqrt{6} + 4}{5\sqrt{6} + 4} = \frac{(2\sqrt{3})(5\sqrt{6} + 4)}{(5\sqrt{6} - 4)(5\sqrt{6} + 4)} = \frac{30\sqrt{2} + 8\sqrt{3}}{(5\sqrt{6})^2 - 4^2} = \frac{30\sqrt{2} + 8\sqrt{3}}{25(6) - 16} = \frac{30\sqrt{2} + 8\sqrt{3}}{134} = \frac{15\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{3}}{67}$

* Questions often ask you to give your answer in the form $a + b\sqrt{c}$, you will have to rationalise the denominator in such cases.

  $\frac{15\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{3}}{67} \rightarrow \frac{15}{67}\sqrt{2} + \frac{4}{67}\sqrt{3}$

### Manipulating Surds

* To express a surd that has a coefficient as a single root, express the coefficient as the root of its square.

  $5\sqrt{3} \rightarrow \sqrt{25}\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{25 \times 3} = \sqrt{75}$
