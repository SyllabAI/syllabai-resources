## Surds

*Surds are part of the higher tier mathematics course. You need not know the following notes if you are taking the foundation tier mathematics course.*

---

* Irrational numbers cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers. They have no repeating decimal digits, but their decimal digits do not end.

  $\pi = 3.1415926535…$

* Surds are irrational numbers. They are a root of a number that cannot be simplified to a rational number.

  $\sqrt{2} = 1.4142135623…$

* Surds are often written with a whole number integer before them, this means that the surd is a multiple of the root.

  $2\sqrt{3} = 2 \times \sqrt{3} = 2 \times 1.73205… = 3.4641…$

### The Laws of Surds

1. $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$

   When multiplying two surds, multiply the values inside the roots together.

   $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{6} = \sqrt{3 \times 6} = \sqrt{18}$

2. $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = (\sqrt{a})^2 = a$

   Any root multiplied by itself will equal the value inside the root.

   $(\sqrt{3})^2 = \sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 3$

   $(5\sqrt{6})^2 = 5^2 \times (\sqrt{6})^2 = 25 \times 6 = 150$

3. $a\sqrt{b} \times c\sqrt{d} = ac\sqrt{cd}$

   When multiplying two surds with coefficients, multiply the values inside the roots together and the coefficients together.

   $4\sqrt{3} \times 2\sqrt{5} = (4 \times 2)(\sqrt{3 \times 5}) = 8\sqrt{15}$

4. $\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$

   The root of a whole fraction is equal to the root of the numerator over the root of the denominator.

   $\sqrt{\frac{16}{81}} = \frac{\sqrt{16}}{\sqrt{81}} = \frac{4}{9}$

### Simplifying Surds

* To simplify a surd, split the number in the root by its largest square factor(s).

  $\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2}$

* The root of the square factor becomes the coefficient.

  $\sqrt{36 \times 2} = \sqrt{36} \times \sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$

* Some more examples:

  $\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \times 3} = \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3}$

  $\sqrt{128} = \sqrt{64 \times 2} = \sqrt{64} \times \sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2}$

  $3\sqrt{75} = 3\sqrt{25 \times 3} = 3 \times \sqrt{25} \times \sqrt{3} = 15\sqrt{3}$

### Addition & Subtraction with Surds

* You can only add or subtract two surds with the same roots.

  $\sqrt{4} + \sqrt{5}$ cannot be simplified.

* Add or subtract the whole numbers that accompany the roots together.

* Keep the root the same.

* It may be easier to think of it as treating the roots as algebraic variables (ex. $x$) and the whole number integers that accompany the roots as coefficients.

  $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2}$

  $3\sqrt{5} + 4\sqrt{5} = 7\sqrt{5}$

  $6\sqrt{3} - 2\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$

### Division with Surds

* When dividing one surd by another, divide the root by the other root and the coefficient by the other coefficient.

  $\sqrt{12} \div \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{12 \div 3} = \sqrt{4}$

  $36\sqrt{6} \div 4\sqrt{2} = (36 \div 9)(\sqrt{6 \div 2}) = 4\sqrt{3}$
