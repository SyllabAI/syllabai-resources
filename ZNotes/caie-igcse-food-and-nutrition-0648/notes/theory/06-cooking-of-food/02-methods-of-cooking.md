## Methods of Cooking

- <b>Boiling:</b> water is usually heated to a boiling point then heated is lowered.
- <b>Simmering:</b> foods cooked in hot water but require gentler treatment than boiling to prevent toughing.
- <b>Poaching:</b> cooking food in water just below the temperature used for simmering.
- <b>Steaming:</b> food that is steamed without direct contact with water.
- <b>Pressure</b> cooking: at normal a.t.p, water boils at 100 C. If pressure is increased the water will boil at a high temperature forcing steam through food to cook rapidly.
- <b>Braising: </b>a combination of stewing and roasting.
- <b>Stewing: </b>slow method – like boiling but cooked below boiling point.
- <b>Roasting:</b> cooking of meat &amp; vegetables in the oven.
- <b>Baking:</b> cooking foods (like flour mixtures) by convection in the oven.
- <b>Frying:</b> involves high temperatures – solid fats / oils are used.
- <b>Microwave cooking:</b> used to defrost and reheat pre-cooked meals.
- <b>Grilling:</b> involves dry heat being applied to surface of a food.
- <b>Slow cooking:</b> a cooking pot used for cooking foods at low temperatures for a long time.
  - <b>Advantages: </b>Improves flavour of food being cooked; avoids meat to dry and It’s hands-off, so no need for supervision.
