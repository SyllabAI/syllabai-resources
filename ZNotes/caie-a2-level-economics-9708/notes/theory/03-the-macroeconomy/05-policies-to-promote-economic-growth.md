## Policies to Promote Economic Growth

The government will attempt to increase aggregate demand to promote economic growth and reduce a negative output gap. This is done through:

* **Expansionary Fiscal Policy**: e.g., a **cut in tax rate** will increase disposable income, which will aid in increasing consumption, investment,t and aggregate demand.
* **Expansionary Monetary Policy**: e.g., a **reduction in interest rate** will reduce savings and increase consumption and investment, increasing aggregate demand.
* However, there is a risk that these policies can **result in no effect or adverse effect**. E.g. **For example, timing the multiplier** and cutting the tax rate could result in actual economic growth, but it will also **cause demand-pull inflation.**

A government must encourage an **increase in aggregate supply to promote potential economic growth**. This is done through:

* **Supply-side Policies**: e.g. subsidies for training the workforce, creating more productive labour and increasing productive capacity.
* However, there is **no guarantee the policies will have the desired effect**—training workers for a skill that may not be in **demand**. However, government spending increases demand in practice, affecting both potential and actual supply.
