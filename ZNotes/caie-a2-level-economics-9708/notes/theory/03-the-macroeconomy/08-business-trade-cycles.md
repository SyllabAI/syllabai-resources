## Business (Trade) Cycles

- **Business (Trade) Cycle**: fluctuations in economic activity over the years. This causes output gaps.

**The Phases of the Business Cycle:**

- **The Upturn/Growth**. During this phase, the economy expands, **consumption and investment rise** due to household and firm optimism, and **employment rises**. This rapid growth leads to an economic boom.
- **The peak/boom**. This is the peak of the business cycle, where the economy is experiencing a **positive output gap (cd)**, and a **high level of aggregate demand** would potentially result in **inflation and difficulties in the balance of payments**.
- **The downturn**. During this phase, the **economic growth rate falls**, resulting in **negative economic growth**, **savings rises, and investment falls** due to pessimism by households and firms.
- **The trough/Recession**. The lowest point in the economic cycle, with a **negative output gap (ab)**, is due to a lack of **aggregate demand**. If the recession period is too long, it could result in **depression.**

**The Causes of the Business Cycle**:

- **The Fluctuations in Aggregate Demand**:
  - **Business Confidence** affects investment; therefore, with the multiplier, injections have either a positive effect (optimism, higher AD) or a negative effect (pessimism, lower AD) on aggregate demand.
  - **Changes in Money Supply**, effects in investment and consumption.
  - **Political Cycles/Influences** affect government spending. A rise in government spending to help in elections and a fall after the election to quell demand-pull inflation caused.
- **The Fluctuations in Aggregate Supply**
  - **Positive Supply-side shocks**, like technological advancement.
  - **Adverse Supply-side shocks**, like natural disasters. 

**Automatic Stabilisers**

- **Automatic Stabilisers** are used to **offset any fluctuations** in the business cycle.
  - **Reduce the rise in GDP by reducing the growth in aggregate demand** during an upturn and economic boom. E.g. The use of income and corporate tax to reduce the increase in consumption and investment.
  - **Reduce the fall in GDP by increasing aggregate demand** during at least part of a downturn, especially during a recession. E.g. the increase in welfare benefits (increase in government spending) to encourage consumption.
