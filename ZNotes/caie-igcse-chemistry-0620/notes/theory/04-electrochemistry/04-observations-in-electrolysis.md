## Observations in Electrolysis


|**Electrolyte**|**At Cathode**|**At Anode**|
|:---|:---|:---|
|Molten Lead (II) Bromide|Lead|Bromine|
|Concentrated Aqueous Sodium Chloride (Brine)|Hydrogen|Chlorine|
|Dilute Sulfuric Acid|Hydrogen|Oxygen|
|Aqueous Copper (II) Sulfate with Graphite Electrodes|Copper|Oxygen|
|Aqueous Copper (II) Sulfate with Copper Electrodes|Copper|Copper|

* Blue copper (II) sulfate doesn’t change as the concentration of Cu2+ ions remains unchanged.
* **Inert** (Unreactive electrodes) are **Platinum, Graphite or Carbon** Electrodes, So they don’t **react with the ions during electrolysis.**

### Electrolysis of Lead(II) Bromide

* Lead (II) Bromide is heated until molten, so it can act as an electrolyte

* When carbon electrodes are used:

  * At Cathode: Lead is deposited

  * At Anode: Bromine gas is seen as red-orange vapour

* Ionic Equations:

  * At cathode: $Pb^{2+}$ $(l)$ + $2e^-$ $\rightarrow$ $Pb (l)$
  * At anode: $Br^-$ $(l)$ $\rightarrow$ $Br +$ $e^-$

* Lead(II) bromide is a binary compound

  * A binary compound is when two elements are chemically joined together

* When current is passed through molten lead (II) bromide, the compound is broken up into its original elements.
