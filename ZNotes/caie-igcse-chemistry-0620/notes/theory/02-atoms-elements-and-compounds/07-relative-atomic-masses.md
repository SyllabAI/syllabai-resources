## Relative Atomic Masses

* Most elements exist naturally as a mixture of their isotopes. Using the data on the abundance of these naturally occurring isotopes, we can calculate the mass relative atomic mass of the element.

* The formula:

$\text{Relative atomic mass} = \frac{(\%\text{ of isotope 1} \times \text{mass number of isotope 1})+(\%\text{ of isotope 2} \times \text{mass number of isotope 2})}{100}$

An example for calculating the relative mass and abundance:

* * **Question:** Iridium has two isotopes. These isotopes are Iridium - 191 and Iridium - 193. A natural sample consists of 37.3% of Iridium - 191. Calculate the relative atomic mass ($A_{\text{r}}$) of the natural sample of Iridium.
    * $\% \text{of Iridium} - 191 = 37.3\%$
    * $\% \text{of Iridium} - 193 = 100\% - 37.3\% = 62.7\%$
    * $A_{\text{r}}$ = $\frac{(37.3  \times  191)  +  (62.7  \times  193)}{100}$
    * $\therefore$ $A_{\text{r}}$ = 192.2
