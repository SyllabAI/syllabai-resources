## Pressure and Temperature on Gas

Gases are compressible. By changing the pressure acting on them, their volume may be influenced.

1. An increase in external pressure produces a contraction (<b>decrease</b>) in volume. The gas is said to be <b>compressed</b>.
1. A fall in external pressure produces an expansion (<b>increase</b>) in volume. The gas is said to be decompressed.



The volume of gases may also be influenced by temperature. The temperature of a gas affects its internal pressure and, thereby, its volume.

1. When temperature increases, the gas molecules have <b>increased kinetic energy</b> and hit the walls of their container more often and with greater force. This causes an increase in internal pressure and an increase in volume.
1. When the temperature decreases, the gas molecules have decreased kinetic energy and hit the walls of their container less often and with attenuated force. This causes a decrease in internal pressure and a decrease in volume.
