### Apparatus

* Conical flask

* Gas syringe

* Stop-watch

* Measuring cylinder

* Balance

* Water bath

* Thermometer

### Method

* Measure 25 cm³ of 0.5 mol/dm³ HCl into conical flask

* Place in water bath at 25°C

* Add 0.1 g Mg ribbon

* Start stop-watch

* Record volume of gas every 10 s

* Repeat with 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 mol/dm³ HCl

* Repeat each concentration for reliability

### Safety

* Wear goggles (acid is corrosive)

* Handle glassware carefully

* Keep away from flames (H₂ is flammable)

### Processing Results

* Plot volume of gas (y-axis) vs time (x-axis) for each concentration

* Gradient = rate of reaction

* Compare gradients: higher concentration → steeper gradient → faster rate

### Prediction

I predict that increasing concentration will increase the rate of reaction because there are more acid particles per unit volume, leading to more frequent successful collisions with magnesium.
