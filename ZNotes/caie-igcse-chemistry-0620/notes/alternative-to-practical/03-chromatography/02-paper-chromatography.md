### Step-by-Step Method

#|
||

Step

|

Action

||
||

1

|

Draw a **baseline** in **pencil** near the bottom of the chromatography paper

||
||

2

|

Place a **small spot** of the mixture on the baseline using a capillary tube

||
||

3

|

Allow the spot to **dry**

||
||

4

|

Add a **small volume** of solvent to the beaker

||
||

5

|

Suspend the paper in the beaker so the **baseline is above the solvent level**

||
||

6

|

Cover the beaker with a **lid** to prevent solvent evaporation

||
||

7

|

Allow the solvent to **rise** up the paper

||
||

8

|

Remove the paper when the solvent front is **near the top**

||
||

9

|

Mark the **solvent front** immediately in pencil

||
||

10

|

Allow the paper to **dry**

||
||

11

|

If substances are **colourless**, use a **locating agent**

||

### Key Points

#|
||

Point

|

Why

||
||

Use **pencil** for baseline

|

Pen ink would dissolve in solvent

||
||

Baseline **above** solvent level

|

Otherwise spots would dissolve into solvent

||
||

Use **lid**

|

Prevents solvent evaporation

||
||

Mark solvent front **immediately**

|

Solvent evaporates; front disappears

||
||

Use **small spots**

|

Large spots blur and overlap

||
||

Allow spots to **dry** between applications

|

Concentrates the spot

||
|#

### Stationary and Mobile Phase

#|
||

Phase

|

What It Is

|

In Paper Chromatography

||
||

Stationary phase

|

Does not move

|

Water in the paper fibres

||
||

Mobile phase

|

Moves

|

Solvent

||
|#

**Key Idea:**

* Substances that are **more soluble** in the solvent travel **further**.

* Substances that are **more attracted** to the paper travel **less far**.

#|
||

Solvent

**Key Point:** Choose a solvent in which the substances are **soluble**.
