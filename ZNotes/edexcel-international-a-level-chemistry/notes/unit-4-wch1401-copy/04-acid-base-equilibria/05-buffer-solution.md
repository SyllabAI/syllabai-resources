## Buffer Solution

* A buffer solution is a solution that can resist a change in pH when small volumes of acid or base are added. It has an extensive reservoir of $OH^{-}$ ions that can keep the acid-base ratio constant.
* A buffer can be made from a weak acid and its salt or from a weak acid and a weak base.
* Assumptions are that the salt completely dissociates in solution that the equilibrium concentration of the acid is the same as the initial concentration of the acid, and that the only source of $H^{+}$ ions is from the acid and not from water.
* Adding small amounts of acid means that the buffer will become slightly more acidic while adding small amounts of the base means that the buffer will become slightly more basic as it decreases the concentration of the acid.
* Buffers are used to keep the pH of the blood at a reasonable level by the equilibrium of $H_{2}CO_{3}$ + $H_{2}O$ -> $HCO_{3}$ + $H_{3}O^{+}$, these ions neutralize acid that enters the blood by converting them to carbonic acid and water, as well as producing $CO_{2}$ which is exhaled.
* Enzymes require a specific pH to function, which a buffer solution maintains.
