## Enthalpies

* Thermodynamic feasibility and kinetic feasibility are different as a reaction has to have enough energy to occur spontaneously or the kinetic rate could be prolonged, so the reaction would not appear to have happened at all.
* Lattice Dissociation Enthalpy: The enthalpy changes when one mole of a solid ionic compound is completely dissociated into its gaseous constituent ions under standard conditions.
* Enthalpy of Lattice Formation: The enthalpy changes when one mole of a solid ionic compound is completely dissociated into its gaseous constituent ions under standard conditions.
* Atomisation Enthalpy: the energy required to form 1 mole of gaseous atoms from the element in its standard state under standard conditions.
* Enthalpy of Electron Affinity: The enthalpy change when one mole of electrons is added to one mole of gaseous atoms to form one gaseous 1- ions under standard conditions.
* Enthalpy of solution: enthalpy change when one mole of solute is dissolved in water to infinite dilution so that the ions no longer interact under standard conditions.
* Enthalpy of hydration: The enthalpy changes when one mole of gaseous ions is dissolved in water to form one mole of aqueous ions under standard conditions.
* Born Haber Cycles works like Hess’s Law, allowing enthalpy values to be measured indirectly. This value may differ from the theoretical value as it assumes that every ionic compound is in a perfect ionic lattice when some ionic bonds have a covalent character.
* The perfect ionic model assumes that the ions have no covalent character, that the ions are spherical, and that the charge is distributed evenly across the ionic bond.
* Some covalent character happens when the ions are of different sizes and have different charges, as the cation can polarise the anion. The charge distribution is uneven.
* Hess’s Law can be used to find the enthalpy of solution, hydration or lattice dissociation.
* Attractions are larger with ions with a large charge and smaller, so the enthalpy of hydration and lattice dissociation increases.
* The less exothermic (more endothermic) the enthalpy of hydration is, the more soluble it will be.
