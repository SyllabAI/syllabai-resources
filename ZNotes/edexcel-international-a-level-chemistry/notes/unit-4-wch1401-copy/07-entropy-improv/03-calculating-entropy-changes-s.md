## Calculating Entropy Changes ( S)

* A: Entropy of the System ($\Delta S_{\text{system}}$)

  * Calculated using the standard entropy values of the products and reactants:

    $$\Delta S_{\text{system}} = \sum S^\circ(\text{products}) - \sum S^\circ(\text{reactants})$$

* B: Entropy of the Surroundings ($\Delta S_{\text{surroundings}}$)

  * Depends on the enthalpy change of the reaction ($\Delta H$) and the absolute temperature (T in Kelvin):

    $$\Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} = -\frac{\Delta H}{T}$$

  * Calculation Tip:

    * To find the minimum temperature ($T$) at which a reaction becomes feasible:

      $$\Delta S_{\text{total}} = 0 \implies \Delta S_{\text{system}} - \frac{\Delta H}{T} = 0 \implies T = \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S_{\text{system}}}$$

      (Ensure $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S_{\text{system}}$ are converted to matching units, usually Joules).

  * *Important Note*: Always convert $\Delta H$ from $\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ to $\text{J mol}^{-1}$ (by multiplying by $1000$) before calculating as standard entropies are given in Joules.

* C: Total Entropy Change ($\Delta S_{\text{total}}$)

  * $$\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}}$$

  * For a reaction to be thermodynamically **feasible**, **$\Delta S_{\text{total}}$ must be positive ($> 0$)**.
