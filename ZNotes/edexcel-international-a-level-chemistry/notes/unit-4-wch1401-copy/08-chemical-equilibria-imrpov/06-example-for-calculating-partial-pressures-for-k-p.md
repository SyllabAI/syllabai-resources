## Example for calculating Partial Pressures for $K_p$:

* **Reaction:** $\text{N}_{2(g)} + 3\text{H} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}$

* Imagine a mixture is allowed to reach equilibrium at a **total pressure ($P_{\text{total}}$) of $20.0 \text{ MPa}$**. At equilibrium, the mixture contains:

  * $\text{N}_2 = 0.20 \text{ moles}$

  * $\text{H}_2 = 0.60 \text{ moles}$

  * $\text{NH}_3 = 1.20 \text{ moles}$

* Step 1 Find the Total Number of Moles

  Add up the equilibrium moles of all gases in the container:

  * $$\text{Total moles} = 0.20 + 0.60 + 1.20 = 2.00 \text{ moles}$$

* Step 2: Calculate the Mole Fraction for Each Gas

  $$\text{Mole Fraction} = \frac{\text{Moles of gas}}{\text{Total moles}}$$

  * **Mole fraction of $\text{N}_2$** $= \frac{0.20}{2.00} = 0.10$

  * **Mole fraction of $\text{H}_2$** $= \frac{0.60}{2.00} = 0.30$

  * **Mole fraction of $\text{NH}_3$** $= \frac{1.20}{2.00} = 0.60$

  *(Self-check: $0.10 + 0.30 + 0.60 = 1.00$. Your mole fractions must always add up to 1!)*

* Step 3: Calculate the Partial Pressure ($p$) for Each Gas:

  Multiply each mole fraction by the total pressure ($20.0 \text{ MPa}$):

  $$\text{Partial Pressure } (p) = \text{Mole Fraction} \times P_{\text{total}}$$

  * $p(\text{N}_2) = 0.10 \times 20.0 = 2.00 \text{ MPa}$

  * $p(\text{H}_2) = 0.30 \times 20.0 = 6.00 \text{ MPa}$

  * $p(\text{NH}_3) = 0.60 \times 20.0 = 12.00 \text{ MPa}$

* Step 4: Substitute into the $K_p$ Expression and Calculate

  1. Write the expression (products on top, reactants on bottom, powers from balancing numbers):

     $$K_p = \frac{(p_{\text{NH}_3})^2}{(p_{\text{N}_2})(p_{\text{H}_2})^3}$$

  2. Plug in your partial pressure values:

     $$K_p = \frac{(12.00)^2}{(2.00) \times (6.00)^3}$$

  3. Solve:

     $$K_p = \frac{144.0}{2.00 \times 216} = \frac{144.0}{432} = 0.333 \text{ MPa}^{-2}$$

* This type of question might seem confusing at the start but after rereading and doing it multiple times it will get easier so dont worry if you have struggles with this at first
