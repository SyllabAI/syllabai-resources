## **Arrhenius Equation:**

$$ln k = -\frac{E_a}{RT} + \ln A$$

where:

* **A:** Constant

* **$E_a$:** Activation Energy

* **R:** gas constant ($8.31$)

* **T:** Temperature in Kelvin

* **k:** rate constant

If a graph of $\ln k$ is plotted against $\frac{1}{T}$, a straight line is obtained with a gradient of $-\frac{E_a}{R}$.

### Explanation:

* **Arrhenius Equation:**

  $$k = A e^{\left\(-\frac{E_a}{RT}\right\)}$$

$$k = A e^{\left\(-\frac{E_a}{RT}\right\)}$$

$$\ln k = \ln A + \frac{-E_a}{R} \cdot \frac{1}{T}$$

$$\ln A \text{ is Constant}$$

$$\therefore \ln k = \frac{-E_a}{R} \cdot \frac{1}{T} \implies \left\(\frac{\ln k}{1/T}\right\) = -\frac{E_a}{R}$$

$$\downarrow$$

$$\text{Gradient} = -\frac{E_a}{R}$$

$$\therefore E_a = -\text{Gradient} \times R$$
