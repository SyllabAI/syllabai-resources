## Rate equations and mechanisms

Definition: A **reaction mechanism** is the step by step sequence of elementary reactions (1 step reactions) by which an overall chemical change occurs.

* Most chemical reactions do not occur in a single collision because collisions involving more than three particles simultaneously are extremely improbable.

* The overall rate equation reflects **only** the stoichiometry of the **reactants involved up to and including the Rate Determining Step (RDS).**

Example:

* $$\mathrm{Step\ 1\ (Slow\ / \text{RDS}):}\ \mathrm{A + B \rightarrow I_1}$$

* $$\mathrm{Step\ 2\ (Fast):}\ \mathrm{I_1 + C \rightarrow D}$$

* **Intermediate ($\mathrm{I_1}$):** A species formed in an early step and consumed in a subsequent step. Intermediates **do not appear** in the overall equation or the rate equation.

* **Deducing the Rate Equation:** Because $\text{Step 1}$ is the RDS, only reactants $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{B}$ control the rate:

  * $$\text{Rate} = k\mathrm{[A][B]}$$

* **Order with respect to $\mathrm{C}$:** $0$ (because reactant $\mathrm{C}$ reacts only **after** the RDS).
