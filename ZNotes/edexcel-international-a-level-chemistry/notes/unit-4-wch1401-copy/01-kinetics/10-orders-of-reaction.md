## Orders of Reaction ($m$ and $n$)

The order of reaction shows how changing the concentration of a specific reactant affects the overall rate:

* **Zero Order ($m = 0$):** Changing the concentration of $A$ has **no effect** on the rate ($\text{Rate} \propto [A]^0 = 1$).

* **First Order ($m = 1$):** Rate is **directly proportional** to $[A]$ ($\text{Rate} \propto [A]^1$). Doubling $[A]$ doubles the rate.

* **Second Order ($m = 2$):** Rate is proportional to the **square** of $[A]$ ($\text{Rate} \propto [A]^2$). Doubling $[A]$ quadruples ($2^2 = 4$) the rate.

**Overall Order of Reaction:** The sum of individual orders (m \+ n \+ ….).
