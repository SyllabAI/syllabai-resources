# Organic Chemistry I

## Optical Isomers

* A chiral centre is a molecule with four different atoms/groups of atoms bonded around one carbon atom, displaying optical isomerism.
* Optical isomers are also called enantiomers.
* Optical isomers are a type of stereoisomer with the same molecular formula but a different arrangement in space; they are mirror images of each other.
* Each enantiomer can rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions, except for racemic mixtures, which have equal amounts of each enantiomer, so the rotation is cancelled out (this mixture is optically inactive)
* Racemic mixtures can occur via $S_{n}1$ reactions, as the molecule can attack from either side of the enantiomer as there is a planar carbocation intermediate, as well as from nucleophilic addition reactions as the carbonyl group is also planar.
* $S_{n}2$ reactions form a single unstable intermediate; therefore, only one enantiomer is formed.
