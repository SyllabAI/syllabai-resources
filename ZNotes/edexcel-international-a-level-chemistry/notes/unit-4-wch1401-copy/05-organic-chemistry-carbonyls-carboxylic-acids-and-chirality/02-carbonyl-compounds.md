## Carbonyl Compounds

* Carbonyl Compounds have a C=O bond, such as aldehydes and ketones.
* Aldehydes and Ketones are soluble in water as the O in the C=O bond is $\sigma-$ and the H in the water molecules are δ+ therefore, hydrogen bonds can be formed between water and the C=O group. However, aldehydes and ketones cannot form hydrogen bonds with themselves, as they do not have an δ+ H atom, giving them a lower boiling point than carboxylic acids.
* Aldehydes have the functional group R-CHO, with a hydrogen and an oxygen atom attached to a carbon atom. This is found at the end of a carbon chain. This is formed when a primary alcohol is distilled and oxidised with $K_{2}Cr_{2}O_{7}$, and when an aldehyde is heated under reflux, it is oxidised to a carboxylic acid.
* Ketones have the functional group R-CO-R, which is a carbonyl group attached to two carbon groups. It is formed when secondary alcohol is oxidised with $K_{2}Cr_{2}O_{7}$.
