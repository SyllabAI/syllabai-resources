# Chemical Equilibria

## Equilibrium Constant of an Ideal Gaseous Mixture (Kc and Kp)

* There are some equilibrium constants: $K_{c}$ and $K_{p}$ for concentration and pressure, respectively
* $K_{c}$  is equal to the concentration of products divided by the concentration of the reactants, and depending on the number of moles used, the concentrations could be raised to the power of the number of moles used.
* Solids and liquids are not included in the $K_{c}$ calculations. Gaseous and aqueous substances are included in the $K_{c}$ calculations.
* $K_{p}$ is a constant for gaseous equilibria.
* Like the rate constant, $K_{c}$ and $K_{p}$ can also have varying units depending on the powers of the concentrations or of the partial pressures.
* $K_{p}$ is calculated using partial pressures, where the partial pressure equals the mole fraction \* total pressure.
* Mole fraction is calculated by moles of A (at equilibrium)/total moles (at equilibrium)
* Partial pressure is shown by (pA)
* $K_{c}$ and $K_{p}$ are calculated similarly, where the partial pressures of the products are divided by the partial pressure of the reactants, and depending on the number of moles, the partial pressures are raised to the power of the number of moles used.
* $K_{c}$ and $K_{p}$ are unaffected by catalysts, concentration changes or pressure changes as the equilibrium will shift to counteract those changes. The position of the equilibrium will remain the same.
* Temperature does affect $K_{c}$ and $K_{p}$ as it affects the position of the equilibrium, therefore changing the concentration of the products and reactants.
