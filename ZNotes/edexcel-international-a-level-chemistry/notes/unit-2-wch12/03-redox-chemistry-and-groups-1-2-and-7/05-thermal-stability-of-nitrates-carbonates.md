## Thermal Stability of Nitrates & Carbonates

* Thermal stability indicates how stable a compound is when heated, depending on the extent of its decomposition.

**Group 1 nitrates:**

* Metal nitrates (white in colour): metal nitrate  → metal nitrite + oxygen
  * KNO$_3$ → KNO$_2$ + O$_2$  (all group 1 metal nitrates decompose in this way with no visible result)
  * Exception: 4LiNO$_3$ → 2Li$_2$O + 4NO$_2$ + O$_2$ (lithium nitrate decomposes like a group 2 nitrate)

**Group 1 carbonates:**

* Also, white in colour, shows a similar trend as seen in nitrates.
* Group 1 carbonates do not decompose except at very high temperatures
  * Exception: Li$_2$CO$_3$ → Li$_2$O + CO$_2$  (lithium carbonate decomposes at low temperature)

**Group 2 nitrates:**

* Metal nitrates (white in colour): metal nitrate  → metal oxide + nitrogen dioxide + oxygen
  * 2Ca(NO$_3$)$_2$ → 2CaO + 4NO$_2$ + O$_2$
  * The more brown fumes of nitrogen dioxide given off, the greater the decomposition.

**Group 2 carbonates:**

* Also, white in colour, shows a similar trend as seen in nitrates.
  * BaCO$_3$ → BaO + CO$_2$

**Factors affecting these trends:**

* **For group 1 nitrates and carbonates, stability increases down the group because:**
  * the size of the cations increase
  * as a result, the polarising power decreases
  * and the compound is more ionic in nature
* **For group 2 nitrates and carbonates:**
  * Relative to group 1, group 2 metals are smaller and have a greater charge (+2), so these metals are more polarising, which results in greater decomposition.
  * Down the group, the charge density decreases because the ions become larger
