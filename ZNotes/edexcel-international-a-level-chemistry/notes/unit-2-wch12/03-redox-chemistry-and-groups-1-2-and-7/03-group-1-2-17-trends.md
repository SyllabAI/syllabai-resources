## Group 1, 2 & 17: Trends

* The first ionisation energy is the energy required to remove one electron from each atom in 1 mol of gaseous atoms to form 1 mole of gaseous $1^{+}$ ions.
* For any atom M:
  * $M \rightarrow M^{+} + e^{-}$
* The second ionisation energy is the energy required to remove one electron from each singly charged ion to form 1 mole of gaseous 2$^+$ ions.
* For any atom M:
  * $M^{+} \rightarrow M^{2+} + e^{-}$
* The first and second ionisation energy decreases down both groups 1 and 2 because:
  * The number of shells increases with the increase in protons, so the outermost electrons are further away from the nucleus.
  * The increase in inner shells also increases the force of repulsion of the electron being removed increases.
* The solubility of group 2 hydroxides increases down the group
* Solubility of Group 2 sulfates decreases down the group (all group 1 sulfates are soluble)
  * Barium sulfate is insoluble, which is the reason for its use as a test for the presence of sulfate ions.
  * $ Ba(aq)^{2+} + SO^{2-} \rightarrow BaSO_{4} (s)$
  * Barium sulfate forms a white precipitate. This test is carried out with the addition of an acid (dil. HCl or dil. HNO$_3$) to remove other barium compounds that give a similar white precipitate, like BaCO$_3$
* Thermal stability of nitrates and carbonates down groups 1 and 2 increases.

### **Group 17: Trends**

* Melting and boiling temperatures increase down the group.
  * London forces increase down the group as the number of electrons increases, so a greater amount of energy is required to melt or boil the elements.
* Electronegativity decreases down the group (fluorine is the most electronegative)
  * The distance between the nucleus and the outer electrons increases down the group.
  * The shielding effect also increases
* Reactivity decreases down the group
