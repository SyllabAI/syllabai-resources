## Definitions

* **Standard enthalpy change of reaction** $\Delta_{r}H$: the enthalpy changes when the reactants shown in the chemical equation react to give products under standard conditions (the reactants and products are in their standard states).
* **Standard enthalpy change of formation** $\Delta_{f}H$**:** the enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states, under 298K and 100kPa.
* **Standard enthalpy change of combustion** $\Delta_{c}H$**:** the enthalpy changes when 1 mole of a substance is burned in excess oxygen under standard conditions.
* **Standard enthalpy change of neutralisation** $\Delta_{\text{neut}}H$ **:** the enthalpy change when an acid and an alkali react together under standard conditions to form one mole of water.
* **Standard enthalpy change of atomisation** $\Delta_{\text{at}}H$**:** the enthalpy changes when one mole of gaseous atoms is formed from its elements under standard conditions.
  * Example: $\frac{1}{2}H_{2(g)} \rightarrow H_{(g)}$
* **Bond enthalpy:** the amount of energy needed to break one mole of a type of bond in a molecule in the gas phase.
* **Mean bond enthalpy:** the energy needed to break one mole of bond in the gas phase, averaged over many different compounds.
* **Specific heat capacity (c):** amount of energy required to raise the temperature of 1kg of a substance by 1K.
