## Permanent Dipoles

* Arise from uneven electron density within molecules because of the differences in electronegativity.
* Only exist in polar molecules, like in ethanol, $C_{2}H_{5}OH$.
  * The oxygen is more electronegative than carbon and hydrogen and attracts the bonding electrons toward itself. This gives it a partial negative (*δ*−) charge.
* This type of interaction, though usually stronger, can sometimes be weaker than induced dipole forces (London forces).
  * This is because London forces **always** orient the temporary dipoles in a way that promotes attraction between molecules, i.e., the positive end of one atom and the negative end of its neighbour align themselves to maximise the attraction.
  * Permanent dipoles may not always align as favourably, leading to repulsions between molecules. Hence, they can be weaker.
