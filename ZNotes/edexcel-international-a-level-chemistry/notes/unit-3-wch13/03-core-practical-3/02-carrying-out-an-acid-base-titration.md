## Carrying Out An Acid-Base Titration

### **Materials Needed**

* NaOH solution
* HCl solution
* Burette
* Pipette
* Conical flask
* pH indicator

### **Method**

* Rinse the burette and pipette with HCl and NaOH, respectively (the apparatus should be rinsed with the solution they will contain. Water can leave residue behind).
* Pipette 25cm$^{3}$ of NaOH into the conical flask. Place the flask over a white tile to make the colour change clearer.
* Fill the burette with the HCl (make sure no air bubbles form)
* Start titrating by adding the HCl slowly to the conical flask and swirling the flask
* Add an indicator (to the conical flask) to determine the endpoint of the titration.
  * Phenolphthalein will turn pink (alkali) to colourless (neutral) - the colour disappears at the endpoint.
  * Methyl orange will turn yellow (alkali) to orange (neutral) - the colour is orange at the endpoint.
* Record the burette readings before and after adding the acid
* Repeat the titration until a minimum of two **concordant** results are obtained.

### **Analysis**

* The concentration of HCl is known, and by measuring the volume of HCl required to neutralize the NaOH solution, the concentration of NaOH can be calculated.
* It is advised to carry out a rough titration first to identify an approximate volume for the endpoint.
* Concordant results are within 0.2 cm$^{3}$ of each other
* Here is a rough sample data for a titration:


||Rough Titration|Trial 1|Trial 2|Trial 3|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|Initial burette reading/cm^3|0.50|1.50|2.50|0.65|
|Final burette reading/cm^3|26.05|22.9|24.0|25.0|
|Titre/cm^3|25.55|21.4|21.5|24.35|

**Calculating the mean titre:**

* Here, the concordant results are 21.4 and 21.5 because they have a difference of 0.10

* The mean titre will be calculated using the concordant results **only**:

$\frac{21.4+21.5}{2}$ = 21.45

* There can be more than one pair of concordant results in some cases.
