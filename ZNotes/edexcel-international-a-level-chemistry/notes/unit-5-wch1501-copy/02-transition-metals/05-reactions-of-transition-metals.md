## Reactions of Transition Metals

* Ligand Exchange occurs to increase the entropy of $\Delta S_{\text{system}}$ so the complexes become more stable.
* $[Cu(H_{2}O){6}]^{2+} + EDTA^{4-} \rightarrow [Cu(EDTA)]^{2-} + 6HO$ - 6 water ligands are replaced by one $EDTA^{4-}$ ligand so the number of species increases from 2 to 7.
* Haemoglobin also forms dative covalent bonds with oxygen to carry oxygen around the body; this reaction is reversible. $Fe^{2+}$ forms dative bonds with nitrogen to keep it in place. When dative bonds form between haemoglobin and CO, this reaction is not as easily reversible, so it is toxic to the blood.
* Redox: The oxidation number of the transition metal ion changes
* Deprotonation: one or more of the ligands gains or loses a hydrogen ion (a proton)
* Ligand Exchange: one or more of the ligands around the transition metal ion is replaced by a different ligand
* Coordinate Number change: the number of ligands changes

These reactions can change the colour of a complex.
