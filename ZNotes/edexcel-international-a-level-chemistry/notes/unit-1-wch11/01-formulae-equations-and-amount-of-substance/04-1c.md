## Relative Masses

### **Relative Atomic Mass**

$$
A_r
$$

* relative atomic mass (RAM) is the weighted mean (average) mass of an atom compared to $\frac{1}{12}$ the mass of a $^{12}C$ isotope
* the relative atomic mass is used to compare elements of different masses from each other
* Example: $H$ atom has a $A_r$ of 1 as it is 12 times lighter than a $^{12}C$ atom

### **Relative Molecular Mass**

$$

M_r

$$

* the combined $A_r$ in a molecule or compound is called the relative molecular mass

* Example: The $M_r$ of $CO_2$ is $12.0\ +\ 2(16.0)\ =\ 44.0$

### **Relative Formula Mass**

$$

M_r

$$

* while the symbol is the same as relative molecular mass, relative formula mass refer to both molecules and ions, such as when concerning hydrated ionic compounds

Example: $CuSO_4.5\ H_2\ O$ has a relative formula mass of $63.5\ +\ 32.1\ +\ 4(16.0)\ +5[2(1)+16.0]\ =\ 249.6$

### **Molar Mass**

* Molar mass is the mass per mole of any substance
* 1 mole is equal to $6.02\ \times\ 10^{23}$ amount of substance
* the use of the mole instead of atoms allows us to be able to calculate chemical reactions in the scales the humans normally perceived in
* further explanation on the mole is discussed below

### **Moles**

* A mole is defined as the amount of particles that contains the same number of particles as the number of carbon atoms in exactly 12 grams of the $^{12}C$ isotope
* 1 mole of $^{12}C$ atoms will have the mass of 12 grams
* As such, 1 mole will have the number of atoms in exactly 12 grams of $^{12}C$ atoms
* This is known as Avogadro constant which has the value of $6.02\ \times\ 10^{23}$ and is denoted $L$
* thus, $6.02\ \times\ 10^{23}$ $^{12}C$ atoms will have the mass of exactly 12 grams
* mathematically: $amount\ of\ substances\ in\ moles\ =\ \frac{\text{mass in grams}}{\text{molar mass}}\ =\ \frac{m}{M}$ where M is the Molar Mass
* To find number of moles in a solution: $\text{n}$ = $\text{c}$ $\text{(mol dm}^{-3})$ $\times$ $\text{V(dm}^{3})$
* To find number of moles of a gas at room temperature:  $\text{n}= \frac{\text{V (cm}^{3})} {\text{24 000}}$
  * or  $\text{n} = \frac{\text{V (dm}^{3})} {\text{24}}$
* To find moles of a gas at different pressure or temperature: $\text{n} = \frac{\text{p (pa)} \times \text{V (dm}^{3})} {\text{R (8.31J mol} ^{-1} \text{K}^{-1}) \times \text{T(K)}}$

### **Calculations using Reacting Masses**

* using the concept of moles, the amount of substance from a reaction can be calculated from a balanced equation
* Example:
  * $N_2\ +\ 3H_2\ \rightarrow\ 2NH_3$
  * from the equation, 1 mole of $N_2$ reacting with 3 moles of $H_2$ can form 2 moles of $NH_3$
