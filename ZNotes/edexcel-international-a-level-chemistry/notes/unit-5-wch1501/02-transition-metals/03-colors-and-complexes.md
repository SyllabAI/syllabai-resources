## Colors and Complexes

* The colour of a compound depends on the electrons in 3d energy levels - which split when bonded to a ligand.
* Electrons get excited and jump to a higher energy level, so when they drop back down, they release energy that corresponds to a wavelength on the visible light spectrum.
* [$Cu(H_{2}O){6}]^{2+}$ and [$Cu(NH){4}(H_{2}O)]^{2+}$ are different colours because of the different gaps between the split d-orbitals. The ligands can change the colour of a complex.
* Shapes of complexes depend on the electron pair repulsion theory. If there are 6 ligands, there are 6 electron pairs, so the shape is octahedral with a bond angle of 90 degrees. (e.g. [$Co(NH_{3}){6}]^{2+}$) If there are 4 ligands, then there are 4 electron pairs, so the shape is tetrahedral (e.g. [$CuCl]^{2-}$), etc.
* The Charge of a complex depends on the transition metal and the number of negatively charged ligands. Cl molecules are too big, so  [$CuCl_{4}]^{2-}$ only has 4 ligands. [$Ag(NH_{3})_{2}]^{+}$ is linear, and Ag behaves differently as it is in period 5 of the periodic table.
* cis-platin is used to treat cancer as it stops DNA from separating so cancer cells stop dividing. Transplatin is toxic.
* Square planar complexes have a central atom surrounded by 4 atoms in the same plane and with 90-degree bond angles.
