# Redox Equilibria

## Electrochemical Cells

* Reduction is the gain of electrons, the gain of hydrogen, and the loss of oxygen. The oxidation number decreases.
* Oxidation is the loss of electrons, the loss of hydrogen, and the gain of oxygen. The oxidation number increases.
* Electrochemical cells use redox reactions as there is a flow of electrons between the products of the reaction, creating an electrical current between the two electrodes and a potential difference that can be measured using a voltmeter.
* Most electrochemical cells have two electrodes and a salt bridge made from unreactive ions (e.g. KCl, $AgNO_{3}$, etc) that can carry a current. Hence, the flow of electrons is uninterrupted, but the salts do not interfere with the reaction.
* Two half-cells at each electrode make up a full cell - they have a cell potential that determines how they will react (e.g. whether they are oxidised or reduced) - the difference between these half-cells is the potential difference.
