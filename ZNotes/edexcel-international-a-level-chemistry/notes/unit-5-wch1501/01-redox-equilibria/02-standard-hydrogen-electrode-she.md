## Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)

* The SHE (standard hydrogen electrode) can be used to find a numerical value for half-cell potentials, as the cell potential for hydrogen is 0 V (under standard conditions of 100kPa, 1 $mol dm^{-3}$ and 298 K), so the SHE is used as a reference for other cell potentials. The SHE cell has HCl, platinum electrodes, and hydrogen gas.
* Positive cell potentials mean the cell is reduced more easily and will gain electrons.
* Negative cell potentials mean the cell is oxidised more easily and will lose electrons.
* Platinum electrodes are good conductors of electricity and are inert, so they don’t interfere with the reaction.
* The cell has a particular way of being represented: the more negative half cell goes on the left, the most oxidised species from each cell is placed next to the salt bridge, and the salt bridge is depicted using \|\|. Solid lines \| represent a change in state. E.g. $Zn(s) \| Zn^{2+} (aq) \|\| Cu^{2+}(aq) \| Zn (s)
* $Emf(cell) = E(right) - E(left) \| Emf(cell) = E(reduction) + E(oxidation)$
