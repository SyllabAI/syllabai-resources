## Amino Acids

* All amino acids (except glycine) have a chiral carbon centre bonded to four groups (R group, Carboxyl group, amine group and hydrogen). These amino acids can rotate plane-polarized light as they are optically active.
* Amino acids can form zwitterions, which can act as both acid and base depending on the conditions of the solution. Zwitterions are dipolar ions, and it has both $NH_{3}^{+}$ and $COO^{-}$ groups.
* In acidic conditions, low pH, the $COO^{-}$ group accepts hydrogen atoms to become COOH
* In alkaline conditions, high pH, the $NH_{3}^{+}$ group loses a hydrogen atom to become $NH_{2}$
* At an isoelectric point, the $COO^{-}$ and $NH_{3}^{+}$ groups are both present, and the overall charge of the zwitterion is zero.
* Thin layer chromatography and ninhydrin solvent can identify unknown amino acids using a silica plate.
* Proteins are formed by the condensation reaction of multiple amino acids to form peptide bonds between the amino acids, releasing a water molecule. (-CONH-)
