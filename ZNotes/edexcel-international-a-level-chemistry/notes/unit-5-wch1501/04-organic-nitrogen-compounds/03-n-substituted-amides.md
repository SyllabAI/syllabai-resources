## N-substituted Amides

* N-substituted amides are named similarly to esters, where the branch of the amide is mentioned first, and the carbon chain with the carbonyl bond is mentioned later. N- must be at the start of the name. E.g. N-methylbutanamide
* Polyamides are formed through condensation reactions between a carboxylic acid and a diamine, removing a water molecule to make an amide linkage (-CONH-). These polyamides are strong as they are formed from long-chain molecules (e.g. nylon 6,6, Kevlar, etc.). Polyamides can also be formed through addition polymerisation reactions of monomers with an amide group and a C=C bond.
* Polyamides are soluble in water as they can form hydrogen bonds with the water due to the amide functional group.
