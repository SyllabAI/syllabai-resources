## Reactions of Amines

* Aromatic amines can be produced by reducing nitrobenzene with a concentrated HCl and a tin catalyst.
* Benzenediazonium ions are formed when phenylamine reacts with nitrous acid ($HNO_{2}$) at a temperature below 10 degrees Celsius to stop other compounds from forming.
* This ion then reacts with phenol to form an azo compound used in coloured dyes. This reaction is called a coupling reaction, as it joins two compounds together in an alkaline solution. Firstly, a solution of sodium phenoxide is made by dissolving phenol in sodium hydroxide, and when cooled, this solution is added to benzenediazonium chloride to make a yellow-orange solution.
* Amines react with water to form alkaline solutions as they can accept protons from water to release OH- ions into the solution. Amines react with acids to form ammonium salts by accepting a proton.
* Ammonia can react with copper ions to form an octahedral complex, and aliphatic and aromatic compounds react in the same way. Copper aqua ions are formed when copper ions react with water, and then amines will react with the water ligand and accept protons, giving a blue precipitate and an ammonium salt.
* Amines can undergo nucleophilic addition-elimination reactions with acyl chlorides to make amides and N-substituted amides.
* Acid anhydrides can also react this way to form carboxylic acids and amides.
