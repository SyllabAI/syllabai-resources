# Organic Nitrogen Compounds

## Amines

* Amines are made when one or more of the hydrogen atoms in ammonia are replaced with an alkyl group, and, like alcohols, they can be classified into primary, secondary and tertiary amines based on the number of alkyl groups surrounding the nitrogen atom.
* Amines are miscible with water as they can form hydrogen bonds with water due to the polar N-H bond.
* Amines can be produced from the nucleophilic substitution reaction between a halogenoalkane and ammonia, making a primary amine and an ammonium salt. (NH4X). The substitution reactions can occur until all the hydrogens on the ammonia are replaced with amine groups. Following this, a quaternary ammonium salt can be produced.
* A mixture of products is produced, so the efficiency is low. Ammonia can be added in excess, and the product can be distilled off using fractional distillation.
* Amines can be produced by the reduction of nitriles by hydrogenation, either by using a nickel catalyst and hydrogen or using $LiAlH_{4}$ in dry ether.
* Amines are also weak bases as the lone pair on the nitrogen atom can accept protons. The more available the lone pair is, the stronger the base, as it can accept protons more easily. $CH_{3}$ groups are electron pair donors, so secondary amine > primary amine > ammonia > phenylamine in terms of base strength.
* Benzene rings cause the lone pair on the nitrogen atom to interact with it, making the lone pair unavailable to accept protons, therefore, phenylamine is the weakest base.
