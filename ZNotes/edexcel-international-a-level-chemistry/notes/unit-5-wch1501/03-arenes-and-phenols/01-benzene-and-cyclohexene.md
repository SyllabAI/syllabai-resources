# Arenes & Phenols

## Benzene and Cyclohexene

* Arenes are aromatic compounds with a benzene ring in the centre. Benzene rings have the formula $C_{6}H_{6}$ and are non-polar molecules with high melting points but low boiling points.
* Benzene has a sea of delocalized electrons from pi bonds and p-orbital overlap, making benzene very stable.
* Cyclohexatriene and benzene were previously thought to behave similarly but are not. The enthalpy change of cyclohexatriene is -360 KJ/mol, three times the value for cyclohexene, while the enthalpy change of benzene is -208 KJ/mol. This shows that benzene is more stable than cyclohexatriene and that the structures differ.
* X-ray diffraction shows that the bond lengths between carbon atoms in benzene are all equal in length, but if this were cyclohexatriene, then the C-C bond would be longer than the C=C bond. Benzene’s length is somewhere in between the C-C and C=C bonds.
* Benzene also has a different infrared spectrum as it has a peak at the value for aromatics.
