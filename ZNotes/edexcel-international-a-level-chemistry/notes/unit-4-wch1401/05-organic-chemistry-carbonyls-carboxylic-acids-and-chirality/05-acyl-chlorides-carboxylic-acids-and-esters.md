## Acyl Chlorides, Carboxylic acids, and Esters

* Acyl chlorides are derivatives of carboxylic acids that can be formed when carboxylic acids react with $PCl_{5}$, and they contain the functional group -COCl, and the suffix of -oyl chloride, e.g. propanoyl chloride, etc.
* Carboxylic acids can also react with alcohols to form esters in the presence of a sulfuric acid catalyst and when heated under reflux. A water molecule is formed. This is a condensation reaction - also called esterification. This can be reversed by adding water to the ester again.
* Esters have a very nice smell, and they have low boiling points. They are used mainly in perfumes and make good solvents for polar molecules. The functional group is -COO- and they are named after the carboxylic acid and alcohol used to make them, e.g. ethanol and propanoic acid make ethyl propanoate. The carboxylic acid will always have the -oate suffix, while the alcohol has the same suffix as their branch counterparts (e.g. ethyl, methyl, etc)
* Acyl chlorides are very reactive. They can react with water to form a carboxylic acid, they can react with an alcohol to make an ester, they can react with ammonia to make an amide and they can react with a amide to make an N-substituted amide. They react via nucleophilic addition/elimination reactions in aqueous conditions.
* Esters can react with a base to form a carboxylate salt and this is not reversible, which is called saponification, as it makes soaps (since the salts can have hydrophilic/hydrophobic properties). The salt can be converted into a carboxylic acid by adding $H^{+}$
* A dicarboxylic acid and a diol can react to produce condensation polymers. A water molecule is released. An ester linkage is formed (-COO-), making a polyester that can be broken down when water is added, making it biodegradable and used for many plastic objects. They will break down gradually in a landfill.
