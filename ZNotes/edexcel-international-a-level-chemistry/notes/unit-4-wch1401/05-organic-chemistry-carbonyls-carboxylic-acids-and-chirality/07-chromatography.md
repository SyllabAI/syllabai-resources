## Chromatography

* These techniques can be combined to determine the structure of a compound.
* Chromatography involves a mobile phase and a stationary phase to separate and identify different components of a mixture.
* The mobile phase can be either a liquid or gas, and it is the phase that allows molecules to move over or through the stationary phase. More soluble species move faster/further in the mobile phase.
* The stationary phase is a substance more attracted to the molecule being analyzed. The greater the attraction, the slower/shorter distance the molecule will travel
* Rf value can be calculated by the distance moved by the molecule divided by the distance traveled by the solvent, and it is unique to every molecule, so it can identify molecules present in a mixture as the molecules will have different affinities to the mobile/stationary phase. The Rf value will change depending on which solvent is used.
* Thin Layer Chromatography uses a silica-coated metal plate as the stationary phase. The solvent separates the sample, which is then analysed with UV light or iodine (to make it visible to the naked eye)
* For High-Performance Liquid Chromatography, a vertical column is filled with a solid, powdered substance that acts as the stationary phase. The sample moves down a column and is separated by the mobile phase. High pressures are used to force the mobile phase down the column. The time the substance moves down the column is called the retention time.
* Gas chromatography uses a high-pressured gas forced through a tube as a mobile phase, with a solid powdered substance as the stationary phase. It is used to separate volatile liquids (it is fed through the apparatus as a vapour)
* Chromatography can be used in forensics, drug testing in sports, and deduction of compounds. It can also be combined with mass spectrometry to have a more advanced analysis of different compounds (GC-MS - Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry)
