## Techniques of Measuring Reaction Rate

**Titration:**

* **Quenching:** The process of slowing down the reaction in an aliquot (small samples). (e.g. immersing in an ice bath)
* An aliquot of reactions was removed at regular intervals and quenching to stop/slow down the reaction in the aliquot.
* Aliquot is then titrated to determine the concentration of a reactant or product species.
* Plot a graph to determine the rate of reaction by drawing tangents.

**Mass change:**

* Mass of marble chips reacted=mass of flask and contents **before** reaction- mass of flask and contents **after**
  reaction
* All of the masses are measured by digital balance
* Record time dissipated for the complete reaction
* Plot a graph of mass against time.

Most suitable for gases with **high density**.

**Colorimetry:**

* The colourimeter is more **reliable** as it detects changes that human eyes cannot.
* Colorimeter uses the principles of the amount of light absorbed by the light-sensitive device (photocell), and it is recorded as absorbance of light.
* Create a **calibration curve** between different concentrations of reactants and the absorbance of light.
* Record the concentration regularly by recording absorbance and convert it to concentration.
* Find the rate of reaction by using tangent.

**The volume of gas evolved:**

* Measure the change in volume of gas using water over a measuring cylinder or **gas syringe**.

* Record the volume of gases collected at **regular time intervals**.

* Plot a graph for the volume of gases collected (y-axis) against time(x-axis) and draw a **tangent** to determine the reaction rate **at certain times**.

  If gases produced are **soluble** in water, do **not** use water
  collection method.

**Electrical Conductivity:**

* A conductivity meter is used to measure changes in the electrical conductivity of a reaction.
* Like a colourimeter, you need to set a calibration curve with concentration and find the reaction rate.

**Other physical properties:**   change in liquid volume, chirality, refractive index
