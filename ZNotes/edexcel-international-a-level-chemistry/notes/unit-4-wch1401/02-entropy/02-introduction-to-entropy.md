## Introduction to Entropy

* Some reactions can occur spontaneously at room temperature, so enthalpy and entropy are both involved in the feasibility of a reaction.
* Entropy is a measure of disorder. Gas > liquid > solid, as gas has the greatest entropy value as it has the most disorder
* As temperature increases, entropy increases as the particles become more spread apart - more disorder.
* When there are more moles of the product than a reactant, or when a substance is changing state from a solid to a liquid or liquid to a gas, or when a lattice is dissolving (as the ions can move), there is a sudden increase in entropy as there is more disorder.
* If entropy change is positive, then the products are more disordered than the reactants, and if entropy change is negative, then the products are less disordered than the reactants.
* The reaction will naturally want to increase its entropy by increasing its disorder.
* Gibbs Free Energy is measured in KJ $mol^{-1}$, an indicator of reaction feasibility, and can be represented graphically.
