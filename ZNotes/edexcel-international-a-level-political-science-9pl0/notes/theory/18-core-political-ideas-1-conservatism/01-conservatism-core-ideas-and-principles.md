# Core Political Ideas - 1 Conservatism

## Conservatism: core ideas and principles




1\. Pragmatism (Traditional & One-Nation conservatives)

* Solve a problem practically- doing what works
* Opposite of an ideological approach
* Burke: ‘change in order to conserve’- enables a society to adapt to changing circumstances by introducing moderate reforms, rather than reject change completely and risk revolution
* Oakeshott: ‘to be a conservative is to prefer the tried to the untried’
* E.g. conservatives opposed the changes of the labour government 1945-51, but when they were back in power they retained the reforms
* Tension with the New Right: they follow their ideas of a moral sense of society and of individual freedom as an ideology




2. Tradition

* Resist change in order to conserve tradition
* Tradition is the wisdom of the past
* Burke: partnership between ‘those who are living, those who are dead and those who are born’
* ‘a democracy of the dead’
* Tradition generates a sense of identity
* Tension with the New Right: whilst neoconservatives believe in traditional values, neoliberals believe in radical changes




3\. Human imperfection

* Humans are psychologically limited- they prefer security and familiarity
* Humans are morally imperfect- they are selfish and greedy
* Humans are intellectually imperfect- they are suspicious of abstract ideas and prefer tradition
* As a result- humans are incapable of making decisions themselves
* Tension with the New Right: whilst neoconservatives are mistrustful of human beings, neoliberals believe individuals are capable of decision making and can be trusted- as a result they are committed to abstract ideas




4\. Organic society or state & Paternalism

* Humans seek to belong to a society
* ‘Natural aristocratic’- society is naturally hierarchical and unequal, therefore:
* Paternalism- those in power have a responsibility to care for the others
  * Soft paternalism: where this is broad consent
  * Hard paternalism: where paternalism is imposed regardless of consent (the idea that the state knows what is best and others must follow)
  * Tension with the New Right: neoliberals completely reject paternalism- they believe in individualism and free will, and reject state intervention & welfare
* Authority requires strong leader




5\. Libertarianism (Neoliberalism)

* Opposite to paternalism
* It emphasises liberty and minimal state
* State should only protect individual rights
* Libertarian conservatism can be seen as taking over traditional conservatism by classical liberal ideas
