## A wider franchise and debates over suffrage

Suffrage = franchise = the right to vote

### Who can vote in the UK?

You must:

* be registered
* be 18 or above
* be a British, Irish or commonwealth citizen
* be a resident at an address in the UK (or a British citizen living abroad for less than 15 years)
* not be excluded from voting

### The following categories are excluded from voting:

* Members of the House of Lords
* Prisoners
* Those convicted of electoral illegal practices or corruption

### Key milestones in the widening of the franchise

* From the late Middle Ages to the early 19th century, only a small proportion of the population could vote, and women were excluded by custom
* The Great Reform Act of 1832: broaden the franchise to include small landowners, tenant farmers, and shopkeepers
* The Representation of the People Act 1918: broaden the franchise to most adult men, and women over 30 who are either married or property owners or graduates
* The Representation of the People Act 1928: broaden the franchise to all adults over 21
* The Representation of the People Act 1969: lower the voting age to 18

### The work of the suffragists/suffragettes to extend the franchise

* Up until the late 19th century, it was assumed that married women’s views were represented by their husbands
* This began to change with the establishment of the National Union of Women’s Suffrage Societies (NUWSS) in 1897, under the leadership of Millicent Fawcett
* Known as the suffragists, they used non-violent methods to fight for women’s right to vote
* Emmeline Pankhurst, a former suffragist, later left and formed the Women’s Social and Political Union in 1903, also known as the suffragettes
* By adopting more radical methods it helped the suffragettes gain more public attention, but also hostility
* Women were finally given the right to vote in 1918, but the effectiveness of the two movements remains controversial
* Some have argued that the quiet work of the suffragists has not been recognised
* Others have argued that the violent methods used by the suffragettes alienated potential supporters
* Another argument is that the government granted women the right to vote due to their willingness to serve in vital industries during WWI when men were on military service- however, this fails to explain why only women over 30 were given the vote, whilst most women workers during the war were young and unmarried women
