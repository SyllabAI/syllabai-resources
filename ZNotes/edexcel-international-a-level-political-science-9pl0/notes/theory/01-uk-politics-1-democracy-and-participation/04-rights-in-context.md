## Rights in context

### Major milestones in development

* Civil liberties = rights that we have in a democracy
* Unlike many other countries, the UK has an uncodified constitution, which means there is not a single document that sets out all the laws/rules
* Our rights, then, and protected by several ‘acts’
* Magna Carta 1215: first document to put into writing the principle that the king was not above the law- sets out the foundation of ‘equality before the law’
* European Convention on Human Rights 1953 (ECHR): an international treaty that sets out basic human rights- including the right to life, the right to liberty and security, the right to a fair trial, the right to education, the right to property, the freedom of expression, prohibiting torture/slavery/discrimination
  * Brexit has no effect on the ECHR
* Human Rights Act 1998: incorporates the ECHR into UK law
* Equality Act 2010: illegal to discriminate in 9 areas: religion, gender reassignment, age, disability, marriage, pregnancy, belief, race, sexual orientation.

### Civil liberties pressure groups

* One example is Amnesty International (see 1.3)
* Another example is Liberty:
  * Founded in 1934
  * Campaign for everyone in the UK to be treated fairly, with dignity and respect
  * Challenge unjust laws in courts, meet with decision-makers and MPs, uncover injustice and inspire people to stand up to power, provide free legal advice to people on how to protect their rights
  * Recent achievements: banning police use of facial recognition (violates privacy rights and breaks data protection and equality laws)
