# UK Politics - 4 Voting Behaviour and the Media

## Case studies of three key general elections

### Long-term trends affecting voting behaviour

1. Class

   * Upper class (AB) are more likely to vote conservative
   * Working class (CDE) are more likely to vote labour
   * Reasons may include sense of identity and culture (e.g. conservative is often perceived as governing more in the interests of the middle classes, labour more in the interests of the poor)
   * Upper classes are also more likely to turn out to vote
   * In recent years, there has been a decline in voting according to class (class dealignment)
   * This is because fewer people now define themselves according to class
   * In addition, both parties have moved to the centre in order to appeal to a larger audience, and so no longer represent the differences between the classes


2. Gender

   * Not a significant factor in voting
   * Although historically women are more likely to vote conservative than men, and men are more likely to vote labour than women

3. Age

   * Young are more likely to vote labour
   * Old are more likely to vote conservative
   * Young are more likely to vote for third parties
   * Old are more likely to turn out to vote
   * Reasons may include young people tend to hold more left-wing views, and have fewer responsibilities so more likely to vote idealistically, whereas old prefer stability


4. Ethnicity

   * BME votes tend to be more in favour of voting Labour
   * This community is largely poorer than the white community, so more likely to be in the lower classes and have left-wing views


5. Region

   * The more prosperous the area is (rural), the more likely they will vote conservative
   * The poorer the area is (urban), the more likely they will vote labour

### Short-term factors affecting voting behaviour




1. General issues (valence)

   * Including governing competence, popularity of the leader, party unity, economic management
   * Matter of TRUST


2. Specific issues (salience)

   * Salience: when some issues stand out as more important than others (e.g. 2016 = Brexit)
   * Issue voting is when the voter votes based on the issue, and choose the most suitable party that they believe will deliver the best policy
   * Expressive voting = when a voter votes to benefit the society as a whole
   * Instrumental voting = when a vote votes to benefit themselves only


3. Tactical voting

   * In tight elections, some voters may vote tactically
   * Instead of voting for their most-preferred candidate, they vote for another candidate that is more likely to avoid their least-preferred candidate from winning

Turnout

   * Tighter elections tend to have higher turnouts
   * General elections tend to have higher turnouts than local elections
   * In the long term, there is a downward trend in turnout, especially in younger groups
   * This can be due to disillusionment (thinking that no parties represent their interests), as well as apathy (thinking that voting is not going to make a difference)
   * Young people are more interested in other forms of engaging in politics (e.g. petitions and social media)

### Case study 1: The 1979 election

* Turnout: 76%
* Result: Conservative majority of 43
* Class: 59% ABC voted conservative, 49% DE voted labour
* Gender: more men voted labour, more women voted conservative
* Valence: labour was governing as a minority government prior to the election, with a weak image
* Leaders: James Callaghan (labour) vs Margaret Thatcher (conservative)
  * Callaghan had a slightly better image than Thatcher (some saw her as too distant and too ‘posh’)
  * Some may also be reluctant to vote for a women
  * Nevertheless she still won
* Salient issues:
  * 1978 ‘winter of discontent’- various strikes by trade unions- key issue of the election = how to control trade unions
  * The economy was also not in a good state- the conservative responded by promising a return to free markets, which attracted the middle classes

### Case study 2: The 1997 election

* Turnout: 71.4%
* Result: Labour majority of 179
* Class: Labour managed to attract more middle class voters, by adopting centrist ‘Third Way’ politics
* Age: 53% of the 18-24 age group voted for labour (vs 24% conservative), in the 35-54 age group, conservative vote was down by 16% comparing to the previous election
* Gender: the gap narrowed to very limited difference
* Ethnicity: 66% Asian and 82% black voters voted labour
* Valence: conservative’s reputation for economic management was damaged after ‘Black Wednesday’ (1992), as well as a series of scandals
* Leaders: Tony Blair (labour) vs John Major (conservative)
  * Major was seen as weak and unexciting
  * Blair was seen as young with a clear vision
* Salient issues:
  * NHS and education- labour promised to increase investments and standards
  * Labour ‘Third Way’ modernisation- by abandoning nationalisation and tax increases, it attracted more middle class voters

### Case study 3: The 2010 election

* Turnout: 65.1% (continued decline)
* Result: No overall majority, leading to a conservative & lib dem coalition government
* Class: Evidence of dealignment- labour only gained 40% of the DE vote
* Age: Old continued to vote conservative, young continued to vote labour and third parties (30% 18-34 years old voted lib dems)
* Valence: labour mismanagement of the 2008 financial crisis
* Leaders: An Ipsos MORI poll showed 33% people regarded Cameron (conservative) as more capable than Brown (labour)
