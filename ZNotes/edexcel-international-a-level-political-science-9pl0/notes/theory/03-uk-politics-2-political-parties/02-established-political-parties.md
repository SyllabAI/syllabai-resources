## Established political parties

* All parties are made up of members who share broadly the same values or beliefs
* Nevertheless, there remains to be divisions within each party
  * Traditionalists = those who prefer to follow traditional values that the party holds, and oppose to reform
  * Reformists = those who want to make changes to their own party in order to move forward

### The Labour Party

* Labour has traditionally been the party of socialism
* Socialists believe in:
  * Human nature is good - cooperation is the natural inclination of humans
  * Capitalism leads to corruption and creates an unequal society
  * Equality is the goal - there should be no unjustified advantages

### Labour 1945-1994 = OLD LABOUR (traditionalists)

* From 1945-1994, the Labour Party adopted an ideology known as ‘social democracy’
* They pursued the following policies:
  * The state should have a role in managing the economy
  * Nationalisation
  * ‘Cradle to grave’ social welfare
  * Taxing and redistribution
  * State intervention
  * Free and universal education and welfare
* This period was known as **OLD LABOUR** - the policies they adopted mainly matched with their traditional socialist ideology

### Labour 1994-2010 = NEW LABOUR (reformists)

* In 1994, Tony Blair became the leader of the Labour party
* He aimed to modernise the party and move the party towards the centre along the political spectrum
* His policies included:
  * Accepting individual private ownership
  * Reducing taxes
  * Social welfare targeting those most in need
  * Free market - no more nationalisation
* This rebranded, new version of Labour was known as NEW LABOUR

### Labour 2010-2015

* Ed Miliband became the leader of the Labour Party in 2010, after the global financial crisis
* It was crucial for him and the party to appeal to both the middle and working classes in order to win an election
* However, he ultimately failed to balance his left-wing views with a desire to appeal to the centre

### Labour 2015-2020 = BACK TO OLD LABOUR

* During this period, under the leadership of Corbyn, Labour shifted to the left again, seeing a return to Old Labour ideologies
* Corbyn’s policies included:
  * Re-nationalisation of water, railways and the Royal Mail
  * Increase in corporation taxes
  * Abolition of tuition fees for university students
  * A fully state-run NHS

### LABOUR NOW = BACK TO THE CENTRE?

* Due to Corbyn’s unpopularity and failure to win an election, the party is currently on a mission to move back to the centre, in order to win support from a larger population

### The Conservative Party

* Conservative has traditionally been the party of ‘keeping traditions’
* They believe in:
  * Human nature is imperfect and corrupt, therefore:
  * Law and order is important to make sure society is stable and secure
  * Importance of education, church
  * Private ownership - through gaining property it encourages people to work harder and take responsibility
  * Hierarchical view of society - we should be ruled by the elites
  * Pragmatism - prefers to go with what worked in the past, rather than experimenting with new ideas

### Conservative 1945-1979 = ONE NATION CONSERVATISM (reformists)

* During this time, there was a consensus between conservative and labour - conservative moved to the centre between free market and state control
* They adopted policies of mixed economy and welfare provision

### Conservative 1970s-80s = THATCHERISM (aka THE NEW RIGHT) (traditionalists)

* Thatcherism overturned the features of post-war social democracy
* Thatcherism was both NEOLIBERALIST and NEOCONSERVATIVE

### Conservative under Cameron (2005-2016)

* Cameron became the leader of the party in 2005
* He aimed to modernise the party, taking ideas from both One Nation Conservatism and Thatcherism, as well as adding his own new ideas
* His policies included:
  * Reducing taxation and spending on public services and welfare (Thatcherite, neoliberal)
  * Resistance to Scottish independence (Thatcherite, neoconservative)
  * Hardening in punishment and prisons (Thatcherite, neoconservative)
  * ‘Big society’ bank to help with community action (One Nation)
  * Maintaining spending on health, despite cuts to other budgets (One Nation)
  * Endorsing gay marriage (Modernisation)
  * Encouraging more female and ethnic minority MPs (Modernisation)
  * To make the government ‘the greenest government ever’ (modernisation)
* After holding (and losing) the EU referendum in 2016, he resigned as Prime Minister

### Conservative under May (2016-2019)

* May called an early election in 2017, but failed to win a majority, leading to her forming a ‘confidence and supply’ agreement with the DUP
* Her period in office is unlikely to be remembered for much else other than Brexit
* In other areas, there is once again a combination of One Nation and Thatcherism, but overall leaning more towards the One Nation side, evidenced by the following policies:
  * Aiming to set a price cap for energy prices
  * A review of student university fees

### Conservative under Johnson (2019-2022)

* Johnson’s period in office was overcast by the covid pandemic- during this time, he:
  * Increased police spending- hitting the highest in a decade in 2021
  * Pushed for vaccination- a consensus that people were part of a greater society and had a duty to care for themselves as well as others
  * Increased taxation and national insurance to help pay for the NHS
* It could be argued that his policies reflected a ‘one-nation’ approach, especially seen with an increased welfare state during the pandemic

### The Liberal Democrats (Lib Dems)

* Most lib dems believe in the following key values:
  * Positive human nature: humans are rational, as a result:
  * Personal freedom: individuals should be trusted to act freely and make their own decisions
  * Equality of opportunity: everyone should have equal chances in life
  * Civil liberties and rights

= overall quite similar to Labour

* However, they disagree on what the role of the state should be:
  * Classical liberalism believes that in order to maximise personal freedom, there should be a minimal state and free market (= similar to Thatcherism)
  * Modern liberalism believes that in order for individuals to have freedom, it is necessary for the state to intervene and provide welfare, otherwise those who are poor have no real choice but to stay in poverty! (= similar to Old Labour)
* As a result, it is difficult for the lib dems to distinguish itself from the two major parties!
