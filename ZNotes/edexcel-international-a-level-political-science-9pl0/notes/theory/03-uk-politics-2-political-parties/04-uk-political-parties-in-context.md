## UK political parties in context

### Party systems

* Single-party system: a single party dominates, no other parties take part in elections
* Two-party system: two parties dominate and usually one of them gets elected - other parties may exist but have no real chance of winning
* Two-and-a-half-party system: two major parties take turns to form government, with a third party which is much smaller than the ‘big two’ but larger than other minor parties
* Multi-party system: multiple parties compete for office, coalitions are common




### Is the UK a two party system?

* Yes - Labour and conservative are the only two parties with a realistic chance of winning
* No - It could be argued that the UK is a two-and-a-half, with the Lib Dems being the ‘third party’
  * Also, the significance of other minor parties cannot be undermined (e.g. SNP & UKIP), as seen in 2.3




### Factors affecting party success


1. Policies - do they appeal to the public?
2. Unity - is the party united with a common vision in mind?
3. Leadership - charismatic leaders are often favoured more by voters
4. Media - does the media change public opinion, or does it simply reflect public opinion? (see 4.2)
5. Wider context - how well a party handles a particular situation can also affect people’s opinion (e.g. the covid pandemic)
