## The ways and extent to which these institutions address and resolve contemporary global issues, such as those involving conflict, poverty, human rights and the environment

### How the following issues affect global environmental governance from effectively addressing and resolving the issues above

1) Competing views about how to tackle environmental issues

* Shallow green ecology:
  * Humans to live in harmony with nature
  * The environment is necessary for human prosperity, so must be protected
  * Nature’s value is determined by its use to humanity
  * Focus on (weak) sustainable development- balance between the economy and the environment
  * ‘Meet the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs’
  * Three approaches:
    * Green capitalism- let the market resolve environmental problems itself - ethical consumerism and rising cost of resources will force companies to take responsibility in order to keep making profit
    * Managerialism- the state to use regulations and targets to limit environmental impacts
    * Technology- improving technology can create a greener form of development
* Deep green ecology:
  * Nature is more important than humanity- its value should NOT be subject to its use to humans!
  * Nature has its OWN value
  * Human prosperity should be based on the quality of life, not the consumption of material goods
  * Strong sustainability- negative growth for developed economies in the north, until basic needs are met in the south

2) Strengths and weaknesses of international agreements

* Copenhagen and Doha conferences 2009 & 2012
  * Meant to be the first step in dealing with global climate change
  * But no agreements are made and no binding targets are set
* Paris conference 2015
  * Recognised climate change as an urgent and irreversible threat
  * Brought ALL states together to tackle the problem

### ‘Tragedy of the commons’

* Common goal = solve climate change
* But cost is high- no one willing to contribute- all acting in their own self-interest
* Private property leads to selfishness, solution = common ownership???

### The role and significance of the global civil society and non-state actors, including non-governmental organisations (NGOs) in addressing and resolving the issues above

**Human rights**

* Expose human rights abuses and put pressure on states to act
* Leads to international institutions being established, as well as increasing support for intervention
* However, they cannot force governments to act, and have limited impact on international decision-making

**Environment**

* Act as contributors to conferences
* NGOs are accused of exaggerating the impact of climate change in order to further their own agenda
