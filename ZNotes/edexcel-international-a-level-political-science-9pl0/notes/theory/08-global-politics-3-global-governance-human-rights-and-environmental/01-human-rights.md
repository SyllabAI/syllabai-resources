## Human rights

### Origins and development of international law and institutions (International Court of Justice, International Criminal Court, special UN tribunals and European Court of Human Rights (ECHR) in creating the concept of global politics

**International laws**

* Origin: 1648 Treaty of Westphalia- defined the rules and limits for state actions
* International laws to be seen as the ‘mutual will of nations’
* Sources of authority:
  * 1948 UDHR- contains civil and political rights as fundamental rights
  * 1966- economic and social rights were incorporated through the international covenant on economic, social and cultural rights
  * These are now collectively known as the INTERNATIONAL BILL OF HUMAN RIGHTS
  * e.g. right to life, self-determination, all born free and equal

**International Court of Justice**

* 1945
* Principal judicial body of the UN
* Deals with legal disputes between states
* 15 judges- each serves for 9 years
* Make-up of the court is representative of different regions
* Always included a judge from each of the P-5 countries

**International Criminal Court**

* Permanent with global jurisdiction
* Established in 2002

**Humanitarian interventions**

* Military intervention within a state by another state / an international organisation
* In order to prevent crimes against humanity
* Has been rising in recent years because:
  * Instability within one state now has global impacts
  * Global media coverage puts pressure on them to act
* R2P = right to protect
  * States have the primary responsibility to protect its own people
  * When it fails to, it gives way to international responsibility to intervene
  * Intervention must be primarily for humanitarian ends, and only as the last resort, and must have a reasonable prospect of success
  * It must also be approved by the UN security council

### The key issues of these institutions in dealing with human rights

**There are controversies, including the following:**

1. Are human rights ‘universal’, or should we take into account cultural differences?
2. Selective humanitarian intervention- only in some countries but not others?
3. Intervention involves the use of force and violence itself!
4. Violation on state sovereignty and self-determination
5. Western hypocrisy and double standard- the west’s own record on human rights is controversial!
6. Intervene in own self-interest, rather than for humanitarian reasons?
