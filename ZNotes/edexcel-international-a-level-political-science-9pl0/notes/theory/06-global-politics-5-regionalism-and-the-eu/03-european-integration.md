## European integration

* ‘Federal Europe’- sovereignty being shared between European institutions and states, each of them enjoying a sphere of autonomous policy jurisdiction
* The process started in 1945, post WWII, when there was a desire for Europe to cooperate to reconcile, rebuild, as well as build up Europe’s political and economic power against the USSR
* Since the 1980s, new driving forces emerged to keep this going:
  * the need to solve global issues such as climate change, poverty, human rights issues, etc
  * the desire to ensure peace

### The development of the EU / Key treaties

* 1951 - Treaty of Paris- coal and steel common market, establishes the council of ministers, the commission, the European parliament and the court of justice
* 1957 - Treaty of Rome- common market for all goods and economic integration
* 1986 - The Single European Act- to complete a common market by 1992, decision-making process changed, reducing the power of veto
* 1993 - Maastricht Treaty- brings the EU into existence- laid the groundwork for an economic and monetary union with a single currency
* 2007 - Treaty of Lisbon- further expanded the power of the EU, including creating the post of High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy

### Economic and monetary union

* Reduces member states’ economic sovereignty
* Increases the pressure for a closer union

### Debates about the EU

1. Enlargement

* EU membership has been increasing
* This means the EU is less able to effectively make decisions and have a coherent strategy, due to diversity between the member states
* It is difficult to develop a one-size-gits-all policies that suit all of them

2. Brexit

* Domino effect?
* Scotland didn’t want to leave but was forced to?
* Weaken the EU’s global role!

3. Supranational vs Intergovernmental

* Supra = institutions can impose decisions on member states
* Inter = states remain in control, institutions just there to help
* EU = a combination of both?
