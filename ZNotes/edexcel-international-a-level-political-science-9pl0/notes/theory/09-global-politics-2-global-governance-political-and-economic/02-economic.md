### Significance of how global economic governance deals with the issue of poverty

**The North-South divide**

* North: where most of the world’s economy activities take place
* South: marginalised, poverty
* Of course, there are also variations within both the North and the South
* As well as inequalities within states (e.g. women, ethnic minorities)
* Why is there such a divide?

1. Dependency theory

* During colonialism, the South became dependent on the North for exportation of primary goods
* After decolonisation, the South suffered from declining terms of trade and rising price of goods
* As a result, the South is economically drained by the North

2. World-systems theory

* Sees the entire world as ONE single economy
  * core countries = democratic, high welfare and wages, advanced technology
  * semi-periphery countries = authoritarian, limited welfare, low wages
  * periphery countries = limited or no welfare, below-subsistence wages
* Core countries exploit periphery countries for raw materials and cheap labour
* Poverty as a result

**How to measure poverty?**

* Absolute poverty: not having the money to sustain life
* Relative poverty: poorer than a typical person in the country
* Measuring income only? Or take into account other considerations- such as education, healthcare, self-reliance???
* The Human Development Index- measures one’s capability to fulfil his own non-material needs, rather than just economic growth

**Orthodox development theory**

* Unlimited growth is possible under the free market
* Developing countries will eventually become developed as time goes on, with the help of experts and improving technology
  * classical theory: free trade without government intervention will drive growth
  * neoclassical theory: free markets and privatisation will open the state to international trade and trigger growth

**Alternative development theories**

* Development = becoming self-reliant, sustainable, engaging with marginalised groups, improving human well-being
* It relies on local control and participation- state intervention needed
