## The Prime Minister and the Cabinet

### The power of the Prime Minister and the Cabinet

### Power of the PM

* Head of government
* Appoint all ministers
* Lead the country
* Government policies
* Foreign policies

### Factors governing the Prime Minister’s selection of ministers

* They maybe close allies of the PM
* They maybe an ‘enemy’ of the PM
  * by appointing an enemy as a minister, collective ministerial responsibility means he/she is not allowed to criticise the PM publicly
  * it is a way for the PM to ‘silence’ opposite views
* The ability of the minister
* The personal conduct / public image of a minister
* Diversity

### Factors that affect the relationship between the Cabinet and the Prime Minister

* Loyalty may depend on the possibility of winning an election
* Party unity - backbench rebellions
* If the PM is unpopular, there is a risk of leadership challenge (vote of no confidence)
* Majority - a larger majority gives the PM more power and control
* Events and how they are being dealt with - e.g. the covid pandemic
* Media support
* Opposition - how strong?

### Cabinet committees

* Group of cabinet ministers - allows discussions and decisions to be made with fewer ministers than in the full cabinet
* The PM is responsible for selecting members
* The cabinet usually obliges to decisions made by the committees

### The powers of the Prime Minster and the Cabinet to dictate events and determine policy

### Case study - 2010 coalition government

* The PM (Cameron) cannot appoint ministers himself, but has to consult his coalition partner (Clegg)
* The coalition agreement required the conservatives to drop some policies that the Lib Dems did not support, and support some of Lib Dem’s policies
* Both parties’ views had to be considered when formulating policies
* This made the PM more vulnerable to challenges and rebellions
