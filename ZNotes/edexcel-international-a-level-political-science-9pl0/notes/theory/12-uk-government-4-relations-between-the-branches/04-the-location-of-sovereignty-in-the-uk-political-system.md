## The location of sovereignty in the UK political system




### The distinction between legal sovereignty and political sovereignty

* Legal = the body who makes laws that are recognised by courts and enforced by the government
* Political = the body who makes decisions




The extent to which sovereignty has moved between different branches of government




### Where sovereignty can now be said to lie in the UK




In theory, parliament is sovereign in the UK:

* Parliament has the power to make and remove laws
* No parliament can bind its successor - the current government can amend laws made by previous parliaments
* There is no codified constitution- giving parliament a lot of flexibility
* Parliament is also elected by the people, giving it legitimacy

### In practice, there are limits to parliamentary sovereignty:




1. The EU

* EU laws are above UK laws


2. Human Rights Act 1998

* Judges have the power to interpret UK laws and declare laws as unconstitutional if they do no comply with the HRA
* However, parliament can simply ignore the supreme court- it does not have the power to strike down laws


3. Devolution

* Devolution of power to Scotland, Wales and NI reduces the power of the parliament
* 2014 Scottish referendum- nearly led to the break up of the UK


4. The people

* Parliament is subject to public support
* If it does not follow the people’s wishes, it can be voted out during elections


5. The government

* Large majority means government can easily control the parliament
