# UK Government - 4 Relations Between The Branches

## The Supreme Court and its interactions with, and influence over, the legislative and policy-making processes




### The role and composition of the Supreme Court

* As a final court of appeal, for:
  * civil cases in England, Wales, Northern Ireland, Scotland
  * criminal cases in England, Wales, Northern Ireland
* The Supreme Court is independent (in theory), to avoid interference from the government
* The Independent Judicial Appointments commission appoints judges, making sure nominees are political independent
* The Supreme court is made up of 12 members - current members are not allowed to sit in the House of Lords


### The key operating principles of the Supreme Court, including judicial neutrality and judicial independence and their extent




### Judicial neutrality

* The principle that judges should not be politically motivated or active and should show no personal bias / favour towards or against any group
* The Independent Judicial Appointments commission helps to ensure this is enforced and eliminates nominees who may demonstrate a lack of neutrality
* The possibility of appeal restricts the influence of personal views
* Professional training is also provided to judges

**However…**

* Judges are still very unrepresentative- very few women and ethnic minorities

### Judicial independence

* The principle that judges should not be influenced by the parliament / government
* Separation and independence
* Judges cannot be fired unless they break the law - job security ensures judges will not be put under pressure by anyone
* Judges also have a set salary
* Judges are appointed using a procedure free from political influence

**However…**

* Judges may be criticised for their decisions / rulings
* Government still has some say in the final stages of appointing judges




### The degree to which the Supreme Court influences both the Executive and Parliament, including the doctrine of ultra vires and judicial review

* Judicial review = review of government ministers’ actions to ensure they are following the law
* Ultra vires = when ministers are acting beyond their powers - can be declared as unlawful
* The supreme court has the power to stop unlawful actions by ministers, as well as help protect civil liberties

**However…**

* The Supreme court cannot begin a case themselves - they must wait for a case to be brought to them
* The Supreme court can only act within the law
* Judges are unelected and lack legitimacy
* Parliamentary sovereignty means Parliament is on top
