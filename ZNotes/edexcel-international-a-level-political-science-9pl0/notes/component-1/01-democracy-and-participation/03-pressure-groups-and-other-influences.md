**Pressure groups and other influences**

### **Pressure groups**

* Pressure groups are groups of people with similar interests or concerns, who aim to influence public policies

* There are different types of pressure groups

* Insider groups are closely connected with the government and seek influence by directly contacting politicians and key decision-makers

* Outsider groups are not closely connected with the government- they focus on mobilising public support (via e-petitions/marches / civil disobedience*) and hope this will put pressure on the government to respond

  *civil disobedience is to refusing to obey certain laws, which are believed to be unfair or unjust

* Interest groups are concerned about the interests of a particular group within society (e.g. working-class / farmers)

* Cause groups are concerned about a particular issue (e.g. animal rights)

### **What factors contribute to the success/failure of pressure groups?**

* Are their goals realistic/achievable?

* How much wealth and resources are available?

* Do they have public support?

* How many members do they have? Are they well-organised?

* Having celebrity/media support can also increase the chances of success!

### **Other influences: lobbyists, think tanks and corporations**

* Lobbyists = professionals who are paid to gain access to the government for their clients

* Think tanks = privately funded, non-profit organisations that conduct research and investigations, aiming to influence public policies (e.g. The Adam Smith Institute- aim to educate the public about free markets and economic policies)

* Corporations = companies aiming to influence government decisions and public policies
