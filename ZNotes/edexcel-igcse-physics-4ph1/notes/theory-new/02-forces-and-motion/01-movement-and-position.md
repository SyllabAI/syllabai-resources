# Forces and Motion

## **Movement and Position**

### Speed

* **Speed** is the distance travelled by an object per unit time.

* **Average speed** is calculated using:

$$\text{average speed}=\frac{\text{distance moved}}{\text{time taken}}$$

where:

* $v$ = average speed in $\text{m/s}$

* $s$ = distance moved in $\text{m}$

* $t$ = time taken in $\text{s}$

### Distance–Time Graphs

* A **distance–time graph** shows how the distance travelled by an object changes with time.

* **Distance** is plotted on the **vertical (y) axis**.

* **Time** is plotted on the **horizontal (x) axis**.

* The **gradient of a distance–time graph represents speed**:

$$\text{speed}=\frac{\text{change in distance}}{\text{change in time}}$$

![](https://images.znotes.org/edexcel/igcse/physics-4PH1/image002.png =700x)

* **Horizontal line → object is stationary**

* **Straight sloping line → constant speed**

* **Steeper line → greater speed**

* **Curve getting steeper → speed is increasing**

* **Curve becoming less steep → speed is decreasing**

### Acceleration

* **Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity.

* An object accelerates when its velocity changes.

* **Deceleration** is acceleration that causes an object's speed to decrease.

$$\text{acceleration}=\frac{\text{change in velocity}}{\text{time taken}}$$

$$a=\frac{v-u}{t}$$

where:

* $a$ = acceleration in $\text{m/s}^2$

* $u$ = initial velocity in $\text{m/s}$

* $v$ = final velocity in $\text{m/s}$

* $t$ = time taken in $\text{s}$

### Velocity–Time Graphs

* A **velocity–time graph** shows how the velocity of an object changes with time.

* **Velocity** is plotted on the vertical (y) axis.

* **Time** is plotted on the horizontal (x) axis.

* The **gradient of a velocity–time graph represents acceleration**:

$$\text{acceleration}=\frac{\text{change in velocity}}{\text{time taken}}$$

* **Horizontal line** → constant velocity

* **Upward sloping line** → positive acceleration

* **Downward sloping line** → negative acceleration

* **Steeper gradient** → greater magnitude of acceleration

### Distance from a Velocity–Time Graph

**The area between a velocity–time graph and the time axis gives the displacement. If the velocity remains positive, this is equal to the distance travelled.**

For simple graphs, calculate the area using the appropriate shape:

* rectangle: $\text{area}=\text{base}\times\text{height}$

* triangle: $\text{area}=\frac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times\text{height}$

For a more complicated graph, **split the area into rectangles and triangles and add the areas together**.

### Equations of Motion

For an object moving with **constant acceleration**, its initial speed, final speed, acceleration and distance travelled are related by:

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

where:

* $u$ = **initial velocity** in $\text{m/s}$

* $v$ = **final velocity** in $\text{m/s}$

* $a$ = **acceleration** in $\text{m/s}^2$

* $s$ = **distance moved** in $\text{m}$

This equation is especially useful when **time is not given or needed** in the question.

Before using the equation:

* Identify the values of $u$, $v$, $a$ and $s$ given in the question.

* Identify which quantity needs to be calculated.

* Rearrange the equation if the required quantity is not already the subject.

For example, to calculate acceleration:

$$a=\frac{v^2-u^2}{2s}$$

A **negative value of acceleration** means the acceleration acts in the opposite direction to the chosen positive direction; for an object moving forwards and slowing down, this represents deceleration.

### Worked Example

A car travelling at **28 m/s** brakes uniformly. After travelling **160 m**, its speed decreases to **12 m/s**. Calculate its acceleration.

**Step 1: Identify the values**

$$u=28\text{ m/s},\quad v=12\text{ m/s},\quad s=160\text{ m}$$

**Step 2: Choose the equation**

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Step 3: Rearrange for “a”

$$a=\frac{v^2-u^2}{2s}$$

**Step 4: Substitute**

$$a=\frac{12^2-28^2}{2(160)}$$

$$a=\frac{144-784}{320}$$

$$a=-2.0\text{ m/s}^2$$

The **negative sign shows that the car is decelerating**.
