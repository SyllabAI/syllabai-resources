## Forces and Movement

### Force, Mass and Acceleration

An object accelerates when there is an **unbalanced (resultant) force** acting on it.

* For the **same mass**, a greater resultant force produces a greater acceleration.

* For the **same resultant force**, a greater mass produces a smaller acceleration.

* If the resultant force is zero, there is **no acceleration**.

The relationship is:

$$F=ma$$

where **F** = resultant force in N, **m** = mass in kg, and **a** = acceleration in $\text{m/s}^2$

For rearranging:

$$a=\frac{F}{m}$$

$$m=\frac{F}{a}$$

**Example**

A car of mass **1200 kg** has a driving force of **3600 N** and a resistive force of **1200 N**. Calculate its acceleration.

**1. Find the resultant force:**

$$F=3600-1200=2400\text{ N}$$

**2. Use** $$F=ma$$ **and rearrange:**

$$a=\frac{F}{m}$$

**3. Substitute:**

$$a=\frac{2400}{1200}=2\text{ m/s}^2$$

### Weight, Mass and Gravitational Field Strength

* **Weight** is the gravitational force acting on an object. It is measured in **newtons (N)**.

* The weight of an object depends on its **mass** and the **gravitational field strength**:

$$W=mg$$

where:

**W** = weight in N

**m** = mass in kg

**g** = gravitational field strength in N/kg

For rearranging:

$$m=\frac{W}{g}$$                        $$g=\frac{W}{m}$$

**Example**

An object has a mass of **65 kg**. Calculate its weight where the gravitational field strength is **10 N/kg**.

$$W=mg$$

$$W=65\times10=650\text{ N}$$

So the object's **weight is 650 N**.

### Stopping Distance

The **stopping distance** is the total distance travelled from the moment a driver becomes aware of a hazard until the vehicle comes to a complete stop. It consists of:

**Stopping distance = thinking distance \+ braking distance**

* **Thinking distance** — distance travelled during the driver's **reaction time**, before the brakes are applied.

* **Braking distance** — distance travelled **after the brakes are applied until the vehicle stops**.

### Factors Affecting Stopping Distance

* **Speed:** higher speed increases both thinking distance and overall stopping distance.

* **Reaction time:** a longer reaction time increases thinking distance. Tiredness, alcohol/drugs and poor visibility can increase reaction time.

* **Road condition:** wet/slippery roads reduce friction, increasing braking distance.

* **Mass:** the vehicle's mass affects its braking and therefore its stopping distance.

### Falling Objects and Terminal Velocity

A falling object has two main forces acting on it:

* **Weight** acts **downwards** due to gravity.

* **Air resistance (drag)** acts **upwards**, opposing the motion.

### Reaching Terminal Velocity

1. When the object first starts falling, its **weight is greater than air resistance**, so there is a downward resultant force and the object accelerates.

2. As its speed increases, **air resistance increases**, so the resultant force becomes smaller and the acceleration decreases.

3. Eventually, **air resistance equals weight**.

4. The resultant force is now **zero**, so acceleration is zero.

5. The object continues falling at a **constant speed called terminal velocity**.
