## Forces and Shape

### Forces

* A **force** is a push or pull acting on an object.

* Forces can cause an object to:

  * **change speed** — accelerate or decelerate

  * **change direction**

  * **change shape**

* Force is measured in **newtons (N)**.

### Types of Forces

Examples include:

* **Gravitational force (weight)** — attraction between masses.

* **Friction** — opposes motion between surfaces.

* **Air resistance/ drag** — opposes an object moving through a fluid.

* **Normal contact force** — force exerted by a surface on an object.

* **Tension** — pulling force transmitted through a string, rope, or cable.

* **Electrostatic force** — attraction or repulsion between electric charges.

### Scalar and Vector Quantities

* A **scalar quantity** has **magnitude only**.

  * Examples: speed, distance, mass, time.

* A **vector quantity** has **magnitude and direction**.

  * Examples: velocity, displacement, acceleration, and **force**.

* Therefore, **force is a vector quantity** and its direction must be considered when combining forces.

### Resultant Force

* The **resultant force** is the single force that has the same effect as all the forces acting on an object combined.

* Forces acting in the **same direction are added**.

* Forces acting in **opposite directions are subtracted**.

* The resultant force acts in the direction of the **greater force.**

### Example

If a car has a driving force of **800 N forwards** and friction of **300 N backwards**:

$$F_{\text{resultant}}=800-300=500\text{ N forwards}$$

### Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

* **Balanced forces** give a resultant force of zero, so there is no acceleration. The object remains at rest or continues moving at constant velocity.

* **Unbalanced forces:** resultant force is not zero, so the object's motion changes; it accelerates in the direction of the resultant force.

* $$F_{\text{resultant}}=0\text{ N}$$

### Friction

* **Friction is a force that opposes motion.**

* It acts in the direction **opposite to the motion**.

* **Air resistance (drag)** is a frictional force that opposes movement through air.

### Hooke’s Law and Elastic Behaviour

When a force stretches or compresses an object, its **shape or length can change**.

* **Extension** is the increase in length of an object when it is stretched.

* For a spring obeying **Hooke’s law**, the **extension is directly proportional to the applied force**.

* On a **force–extension graph**, this is shown by the **initial straight-line region passing through the origin.**

### Limit of Proportionality

The **limit of proportionality** is the point after which extension is **no longer directly proportional to force**. Beyond this point, the force–extension graph begins to curve. 

### Elastic Behaviour

A material behaves **elastically** if it returns to its **original shape and length** when the deforming force is removed. If a spring is stretched beyond its **elastic limit**, it will **not return to its original length** when the force is removed
