## Ideal Gas Molecules and Gas Laws

### Gas Pressure

* Gas molecules move **rapidly and randomly**.

* They continually collide with the walls of their container.

* Each collision exerts a **force** on the wall.

* These forces produce the **gas pressure**.

![](https://znotes-static.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/edexcel/international-a-level/physics/65c7a6762e6e1f7c1c7698366486bf680ff1590f23637af7.png =300x)

* When a gas is heated, its molecules gain kinetic energy and their **average speed increases**.

* At constant volume, faster molecules collide with the walls **more often and with greater force**.

* Therefore, increasing temperature increases the **pressure**.

### Absolute Zero and Kelvin Scale

* As a gas is cooled, its molecules move more slowly, and its pressure decreases.

* **Gas pressure cannot fall below zero.**

* Extending the pressure–temperature relationship gives zero pressure at about **−273°C**

* The lowest possible temperature is called **absolute zero**.

* Absolute zero is approximately $-273^\circ\mathrm{C}$, which is $0\ \mathrm{K}$.

* The Kelvin scale starts at absolute zero.

### Celsius ↔ Kelvin

$$\text{Temperature in K}=\text{Temperature in }^\circ\text{C}+273$$

$$\text{Temperature in }^\circ\text{C}=\text{Temperature in K}-273$$

Examples:

$20^\circ\mathrm{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{K}$

$$20+273=293\text{ K}$$

$400\ \mathrm{K} \rightarrow ^\circ\mathrm{C}$

$$400-273=127^\circ\text{C}$$

### Temperature and Kinetic Energy

* The **Kelvin temperature** of a gas is proportional to the **average kinetic energy** of its molecules.

* Higher Kelvin temperature → greater average kinetic energy → faster-moving molecules.

$$T\propto\text{average kinetic energy}$$

### Pressure, Volume and Temperature

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/98c9411c087ccc5f59e3b8f0.webp =600x)

This happens because changing the volume changes how often gas molecules collide with the container walls.

* For a **fixed amount of gas at constant volume**:

  * Kelvin temperature increases → pressure increases

  * Kelvin temperature decreases → pressure decreases

* This is because hotter gas molecules move faster, so they collide with the walls more often and with greater force.

### Pressure and Kelvin Temperature

For a **fixed mass of gas at constant volume**:

$$\frac{p_1}{T_1}=\frac{p_2}{T_2}$$

where:

$p_1$ = initial pressure

$p_2$ = final pressure

$T_1$ = initial temperature in $\mathrm{K}$

$T_2$ = final temperature in $\mathrm{K}$

**Always convert °C to K before using this equation.**

### Worked Example

A sealed rigid container has a gas pressure of $100\ \mathrm{kPa}$ at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$. It is heated to $50^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

Convert temperatures:

$$T_1=20+273=293\text{ K}$$

$$T_2=50+273=323\text{ K}$$

use:   $$\frac{100}{293}=\frac{p_2}{323}$$

$$p_2=\frac{100\times323}{293}$$

$\boxed{p_2\approx110\ \mathrm{kPa}}$

### Pressure and Volume

For a **fixed mass of gas at constant temperature**:   $$p_1V_1=p_2V_2$$

where:

$p_1$ = initial pressure

$V_1$ = initial volume

$p_2$ = final pressure

$V_2$ = final volume

Pressure increases when volume decreases, if temperature stays constant.
