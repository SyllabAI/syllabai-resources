## Specific Heat Capacity

* **Specific heat capacity** is the energy needed to raise the temperature of **1 kg** of a substance by **1°C**.

* Unit:  $\mathrm{J/(kg,^\circ C)}$

  $\Delta$Q = mc$\Delta$T

  where:

  * $\Delta Q$ = change in thermal energy, $\mathrm{J}$

  * $m$ = mass, $\mathrm{kg}$

  * $c$ = specific heat capacity, $\mathrm{J/(kg,^\circ C)}$

  * $\Delta T$ = change in temperature, $^\circ\mathrm{C}$

  A substance with a **higher specific heat capacity** needs more energy to produce the same temperature rise.

  Worked Example

  A **1.0 kg** sample of water is heated from **20°C to 100°C** using a **2.4 kW kettle** for **2.5 minutes**. Calculate the specific heat capacity of the water. (Assume all the energy supplied by the kettle is transferred to the water.)

  First find the energy supplied:  $$\Delta Q = Pt$$

  Convert the values:

* $2.4\ \mathrm{kW}=2400\ \mathrm{W}$

* $2.5\ \mathrm{min}=150\ \mathrm{s}$

  $$\Delta Q = 2400 \times 150 = 360000\text{ J}$$

  Find the temperature change: $$\Delta T = 100-20=80^\circ\text{C}$$

  Use:  $$\Delta Q = mc\Delta T$$

  Rearrange for c: $$c=\frac{\Delta Q}{m\Delta T}$$

  Substitute: $$c=\frac{360000}{1.0\times80}$$

  $$\boxed{c=4500\text{ J/kg }^\circ\text{C}}$$
