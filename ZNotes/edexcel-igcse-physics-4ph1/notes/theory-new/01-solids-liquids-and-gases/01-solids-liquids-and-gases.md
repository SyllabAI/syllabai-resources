# Solids, Liquids and Gases

## Density and Pressure

* **Density** describes how much mass is contained in a given volume of a material.

* A material with more mass in the same volume has a **higher density**.

### **$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$**

where:

* $\rho$ = density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

* $m$ = mass in $\mathrm{kg}$

* $V$ = volume in $\mathrm{m^3}$

Density may also be given in **g/cm³**.

**Solved Example:**

An iron block has a mass of $390\ \mathrm{kg}$ and a volume of $0.05\ \mathrm{m^3}$.

$\rho=\frac{390}{0.05}=7800\ \text{kg/m}^3$

### Pressure

* **Pressure** is the force acting on each unit of area.

* For the same force:

  * **smaller area → greater pressure**

  * **larger area → smaller pressure**

$p=\frac{F}{A}$

where:

* $p$ = pressure in pascals, $\mathrm{Pa}$

* $F$ = force in newtons, $\mathrm{N}$

* $A$ = area in square metres, $\mathrm{m^2}$

$$1\ \mathrm{Pa}=1\ \mathrm{N/m^2}$$

### Pressure in liquids and gases

* At any point in a **liquid or gas at rest**, pressure acts **equally in all directions**.

* In a liquid, pressure **increases with depth**.

* Greater **depth** or **density** gives a greater pressure difference.

### Pressure difference with depth

$\Delta p=h\rho g$

where:

* $\Delta p$ = pressure difference in $\mathrm{Pa}$

* $h$ = height/depth difference in $\mathrm{m}$

* $\rho$ = density of the liquid in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$

* $g$ = gravitational field strength in $\mathrm{N/kg}$                                                                                                                                                               

  Greater depth or greater liquid density → greater pressure difference.
