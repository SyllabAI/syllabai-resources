# Electricity

## Mains Electricity

### Electrical Safety

Electrical appliances use several safety features to **prevent electric shocks, overheating and electrical fires**.

**Insulation**

* Electrical wires are covered with insulating materials, such as plastic or rubber.

* These materials **prevent current from passing through to the user**, reducing the risk of electric shock.

**Double Insulation**

* Double-insulated appliances have two levels of protection between the user and live electrical parts.

* They often have an **insulating outer casing**, such as plastic.

* They **do not require an earth wire** because accessible parts are protected from becoming live.

**Earthing**

* Appliances with metal casings are often connected to an **earth wire** for safety.

* If a live wire touches the metal casing:

  * Current flows through the **low-resistance earth wire**.

  * A large fault current causes the **fuse to melt or circuit breaker to trip**.

  * The appliance is disconnected from the supply, reducing the risk of electric shock.

**Fuses**

* A fuse contains a thin wire that **melts when the current becomes too high**.

* This breaks the circuit, preventing overheating and electrical fires.

* Fuses are connected in the **live wire**.

**Circuit Breakers**

* A circuit breaker is an **automatic safety switch** that disconnects the circuit when excessive current flows.

* Unlike a fuse, it **can be reset and reused** after the fault is corrected.

### Mains Plug Safety

* **Live wire:** carries electrical energy from the mains supply to the appliance.

* **Neutral wire:** provides the return path to complete the circuit.

* **Earth wire:** provides a low-resistance safety path if a fault makes the metal casing live.

* The **fuse and main switch** are placed in the live wire so that disconnecting them isolates the appliance from the live supply.

### Heating Effect of Electric Current

* When an **electric current flows through a resistor**, electrical energy is transferred into **thermal energy**, causing the resistor to heat up.

* This happens because moving electrons transfer energy to the metal ions, making them vibrate more and increasing the temperature.

* **Heating elements** have relatively high resistance compared with connecting wires, so electrical energy is transferred as heat.

* Connecting wires have **low resistance** to reduce unwanted heating.

**Domestic Applications**

* **Electric kettles:** Heating elements transfer thermal energy to water.

* **Toasters:** Heating elements become hot and toast bread.

* **Electric heaters:** Resistive elements produce heat to warm a room.

* **Filament lamps:** The filament becomes hot enough to glow, producing light and heat.

### Electrical Power

* **Electrical power** is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred.

* Power is measured in **watts (W)**.

* One watt is equal to one joule of energy transferred per second.

$$1\ \text{W}=1\ \text{J/s}$$

* Appliances with higher power ratings transfer more energy each second.

**Power Equation**

$$P=I\times V$$

Where:

* $P$ = power in watts (W)

* $I$ = current in amperes (A)

* $V$ = voltage in volts (V)

**Rearranging the Equation**

$$I=\frac{P}{V}$$

$$V=\frac{P}{I}$$

### Choosing an Appropriate Fuse

* A fuse must have a **current rating slightly higher than the normal operating current** of an appliance.

* If the rating is too low, the fuse may melt during normal operation.

* If the rating is too high, the fuse may not provide adequate protection against excessive current.

* The operating current is calculated using:

$$I=\frac{P}{V}$$

### Worked Example — Fuse Selection

A 1000 W hairdryer operates at 230 V. Calculate its operating current and select a suitable fuse.

**Step 1: Write the equation.**

$$I=\frac{P}{V}$$

**Step 2: Substitute the values.**

$$I=\frac{1000}{230}$$

$$I=4.35\ \text{A}$$

**Step 3: Select the fuse.**

* A **5 A fuse** is suitable because its rating is above the normal operating current of 4.35 A.

### Energy Transferred

* Electrical energy is transferred when current flows through an appliance.

* The amount of energy transferred depends on:

  * **Current (I):** A greater current transfers more energy in the same time, at a constant voltage.

  * **Voltage (V):** A greater voltage transfers more energy for the same current and time.

  * **Time (t):** The longer the appliance operates, the more energy is transferred, provided current and voltage remain constant.

**Energy Equation**

$$E=I\times V\times t$$

Where:

* $E$ = energy transferred in joules (J)

* $I$ = current in amperes (A)

* $V$ = voltage in volts (V)

* $t$ = time in seconds (s)

Since electrical power is the rate of energy transfer:

$$E=P\times t$$

**Rearranging the Energy Equation**

$$I=\frac{E}{V\times t}$$

$$V=\frac{E}{I\times t}$$

$$t=\frac{E}{I\times V}$$

### Worked Example — Energy Transferred

A lamp operates at a voltage of **12 V** and draws a current of **2 A** for **5 minutes**. Calculate the electrical energy transferred.

**Step 1: Write the equation.**

$$E=I\times V\times t$$

**Step 2: Convert time into seconds.**

$$t=5\times60=300\ \text{s}$$

**Step 3: Substitute the values.**

$$E=2\times12\times300$$

$$E=7200\ \text{J}=7.2\ \text{kJ}$$

**Answer:** The lamp transfers **7200 J (7.2 kJ)** of electrical energy.

### Alternating and Direct Current

**Alternating Current** **(A.C.)**

* Alternating current **repeatedly changes direction**.

* Its magnitude varies with time.

* Mains electricity supplied to homes is A.C.

* An alternating voltage changes polarity, taking both positive and negative values.

**Direct Current (D.C.)**

* Direct current flows in **one direction only**.

* Cells and batteries supply D.C.

* The voltage supplied by a cell has a fixed polarity.

* A steady **D.C.** supply has a constant voltage, although d.c. does not always have a constant magnitude.

**Comparing A.C. and D.C. Graphs**

* **A.C.:** The voltage–time graph alternates above and below the zero line, indicating a change in polarity.

* **D.C.:** The voltage–time graph is a horizontal line, indicating constant voltage and polarity.
