## Experimental Procedure

### Toy Car Method

1. Raise one end of the runway and **securely clamp it**.

2. Measure the vertical height, $h$, with a metre rule. Keep the rule perpendicular to the bench and measure to the **same fiducial mark** each time.

3. Measure a fixed distance, $s=AB$, along the track with a metre rule.

4. Place the toy car at the same starting point, $A$.

5. Release the car **without pushing it**.

6. Measure the time taken to travel from $A$ to $B$ **using a stop clock**.

7. Repeat the timing **at least three times** for the same $h$.

8. Calculate the mean time and average speed.

9. Change $h$ and repeat for several ramp heights.

### Tennis Ball Method

Use the same basic measured-distance method:

1. Mark the same points $A$ and $B$ on the ramp.

2. Place the tennis ball at the same starting point, $A$.

3. Release it **without pushing**.

4. Measure the time taken to travel the fixed distance $s=AB$.

5. Repeat at least three times and calculate a mean.

6. Repeat for different values of ramp height $h$.

### Calculations

Mean time:

$$t_{\text{mean}}=\frac{t_1+t_2+t_3}{3}$$

Average speed:

$$v=\frac{s}{t}$$

where:

* $s$ = distance travelled, $AB$

* $t$ = mean time

### Why These Steps Are Important

* **Same starting point** → keeps the measured journey consistent.

* **Same fiducial mark** → makes measurements of $h$ consistent.

* **Rule perpendicular to the bench** → ensures $h$ is the vertical height.

* **Release without pushing** → avoids giving the object additional starting speed.

* **Repeat at least three times** → reduces the effect of random timing variation.

* An anomalous result should be repeated or excluded when justified.
