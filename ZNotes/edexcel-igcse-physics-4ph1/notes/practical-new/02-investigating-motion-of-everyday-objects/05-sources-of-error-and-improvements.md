## Sources of Error and Improvements

### Sources of Error

* **Human reaction time** affects when you start and stop the stopwatch.

* The object may not be released in the same way each time.

* The starting position may vary slightly.

* $h$ may be measured from a different point if the same mark is not used.

* Distance $s=AB$ may be measured inaccurately.

* An individual timing may be anomalous.

### Improvements

* Repeat each timing at least three times and calculate a mean.

* Always use the same marked starting point.

* Release the object without pushing.

* Measure $h$ from the same fiducial mark.

* Use **electronic timing gates** instead of a hand-operated stop clock.

* Alternatively, use video or photographic methods with a visible measuring scale.

### Experimental Uncertainty

* The metre rule introduces uncertainty into measurements of $h$ and $s$.

* A manual stop clock is affected by both its resolution and **human reaction time**.

* Repeated timings and calculating a mean reduce the effect of random variation.

* Electronic timing gates reduce timing uncertainty caused by the observer.
