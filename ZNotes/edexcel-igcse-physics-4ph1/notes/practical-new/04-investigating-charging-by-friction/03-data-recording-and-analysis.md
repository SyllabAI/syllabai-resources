### Explanation of Results

### **How Does Friction Produce Charge?**

* Rubbing two different insulating materials together can cause **electrons to transfer** from one material to the other.

* The material that gains electrons becomes **negatively charged**.

* The material that loses electrons becomes **positively charged**.

* The two materials become oppositely charged.

* Charge is more likely to remain on insulating materials because it cannot move through them easily.

### **Why Are Neutral Paper Pieces Attracted?**

* The paper is initially **electrically neutral**.

* When a charged rod is brought close, the charges within the paper shift slightly. This is called **polarisation**.

* The side closest to the rod develops an induced charge of the opposite sign.

* The attractive force on the nearer opposite charges is stronger than the repulsive force on the farther like charges.

* This produces a net force that attracts the paper towards the rod.

### Interpreting the Results

* Compare the paper attracted before and after rubbing.

* Greater attraction after rubbing provides evidence that electrostatic charge has built up.

* If both the rod and cloth attract paper, this supports the idea that both materials became charged.

* Attraction alone **cannot identify whether the charge is positive or negative**.

### Conclusion

* Insulating materials can become **electrically charged by friction**.

* This happens when electrons move from one material to another.

* The resulting electrostatic charge can be detected through the attraction of small, neutral paper pieces.

* **Electric charge is conserved:** charge is transferred, not created or destroyed.
