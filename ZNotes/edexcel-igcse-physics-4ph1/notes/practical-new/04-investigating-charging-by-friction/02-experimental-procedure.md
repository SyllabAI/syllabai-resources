## Experimental Procedure

### Method

1. Place **10 small, similar-sized pieces of dry paper** on a flat surface.

2. Bring the uncharged plastic rod close to the paper without touching it. Record whether any pieces move.

3. Rub the plastic rod firmly with the **dry woollen cloth** about 20 times.

4. Bring the rubbed rod close to a fresh set of paper pieces, keeping a similar distance (approximately 1 cm).

5. Observe and record how many pieces of paper move towards the rod.

6. Bring the cloth close to another fresh set of paper pieces and observe whether it also attracts them.

7. Carry out **three trials in total**. Before each trial, check that the rod is not attracting paper. If it is still charged, allow the charge to dissipate or use an uncharged rod and cloth.

### Why Are These Steps Important?

* **Step 2:** Provides a comparison before the rod is charged.

* **Step 3:** Rubbing allows electrons to transfer between the insulating materials.

* **Step 4:** Keeping the distance the same makes the comparison fair.

* **Step 5:** The paper's attraction provides evidence that the rod has become charged.

* **Step 6:** Tests whether the cloth has also gained an electrostatic charge.

* **Step 7:** Repeating the experiment helps check that the results are reliable.

### Expected Observation

* Before rubbing, the rod should attract little or no paper.

* After rubbing, the rod should attract some paper pieces.

* The cloth may also attract paper because it becomes charged during rubbing.
