# Solids, Liquids and Gases

## Density and Pressure

* Solids, liquids and gases have different properties and
  characteristic

**Density** = $\frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{v}
$$

* The units for density are
  * kilograms per cubic metre ($kg/m^3$) or,
  * grams per cubic centimetre ($g/cm^3$).

**Pressure** = $\frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$

$$
P = \frac{F}{A}
$$

* Gases also exert pressure on things around them. The pressure
  exerted by the atmosphere on your body is about 100,000 Pa.

**Mass of water,** $m = (A \times h) \times p$

* This equation can be used for calculating pressure differences in
  other liquids or gases, as long as you know their densities.
* **Density of a liquid:** Place the measuring cylinder on balance. Add liquid. Reading on measuring cylinder = V, change in mass on balance = m. Use formula.
* **The density of solid:**
  * Finding the volume: Use a mathematical formula to find out the volume of a regular object. To find out the volume of an irregular object, put an object into a measuring cylinder with water; the rise of water is the object's volume.
  * Finding the mass: Use balance.
* An object will float in a fluid if its density is lesser than the
  density of the liquid, i.e. The volume of fluid displaced has a
  greater mass than the object itself.

**Pressure difference** = Height × Density × Gravitational field strength

$$

\Delta P = ρ \times h \times g

$$
