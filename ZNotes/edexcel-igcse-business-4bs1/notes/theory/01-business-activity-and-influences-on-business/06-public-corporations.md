## **Public Corporations**

### **Key Vocabulary**

* **Public corporation**: A business or an organization that’s owned and controlled by the government
* **Privatization**: It is the transfer of state-owned assets to the private sector.
* **Subsidies**: Government offering financial benefits for firms in order to make them do something.

### **Features**

* **State owned**: Government is responsible for running and managing the corporation.
* **Created by law**: They are created by an act of Parliament.
* **Incorporation**: They are incorporated businesses and have a separate legal identity.
* **State** **funded**: Government invests capital in these organizations, which is often from the tax revenue.
* **Provides public services**: Their main aim is to provide quality service to the public at a very low price or for free.
* **Public accountability**: These organizations must provide reports of the progress of the business and are accountable to the general public as they are the ones who funded the organization through the taxes.

### **Main reasons for government owning the public corporations**

* **Avoid wasteful duplications**: It would be a waste of resources if the government privatizes these industries because there may be competition and, therefore, many businesses will start to offer the same service, which would cause congestion in the country.
* **Fill the gaps left by the private sector**: The services provided by the private sector businesses are charged at a higher price. So, only those who can pay are able to enjoy the benefits, while the ones who are unable to pay are excluded. Hence, the government wants to ensure that everyone enjoys the benefits.
* **Maintain control of strategic industries**: Governments do not privatize these industries as they fear that outsiders from other countries can purchase and exploit them and produce lower-quality services.
* **Save jobs**: As the main aim of private-sector businesses is to make higher profits, they may lower their costs by laying off workers, which will result in a higher unemployment rate. So, in order to protect workers, the government does not privatize these industries.
* **Serve unprofitable regions.**

### **Disadvantages of the government owning businesses:**

* **Cost to the government**: When public corporations face losses, they have to be funded by taxpayers. Hence, the general public doesn’t support this because the government has to forgo spending on other parts of the economy, which could potentially decrease economic growth in the country.
* **Inefficiency:** As there is no or little competition for these public corporations, they become more inefficient as a result. This increases delays in services such as train operations. Productivity decreases as the corporation has no motivation to be efficient.
* **Political** **interference**: Public corporations are often affected by political interference. This is because when governments change, the policies and management of the corporations also change.
* **Difficulty to control**: As some corporations are extremely huge and employ millions of people, they experience diseconomies of scale. Hence, costs may rise, and eventually, taxes are likely to increase. Also, coordination becomes much more difficult.

### **Different forms of privatizations**

* **Contracting out**: This involves the government allowing the general public to bid for businesses that were previously owned by the public sector and purchase them.
* **The sale of land and properties**: This is when the government sells land or properties that were previously owned by the government while also giving some discounts.
* **Sale of public corporations:** This is when a government sells businesses that are owned by the government to the private sector or general public. One example of this is selling shares.
* **Deregulation**: This involves removing restrictions and encouraging competition in the public sector.

### **Reasons for privatizations**

* **To generate income**: This is because when public corporations are sold, the government can raise a lump sum of money, which could be used to fund huge government projects such as the construction of a bridge.
* **To reduce inefficiencies**: This is because private sector businesses are more efficient since they have to be in order to increase customer satisfaction and generate more profits.
* **As a result of deregulation**: Legal barriers are removed; therefore, more businesses are required to compete with the public corporations.
* **To reduce political interference**: There won’t be frequent changes in policies, and investments can be freely made without government interference.
