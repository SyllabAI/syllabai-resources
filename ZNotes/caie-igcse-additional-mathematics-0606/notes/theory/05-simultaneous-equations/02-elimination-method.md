## Elimination Method

Use when both equations are in standard form and can be aligned to cancel a variable.

### Steps -

1. Rearrange both equations so terms align.

2. Multiply one or both equations (if needed) so that one variable has same or opposite coefficients.

3. Add or subtract equations to eliminate one variable

Solve for the other, then back-substitute

**Example**

Given the following equations, solve for x and y

$2x+3y=16$ (first equation)

$3x-2y=1$ (second equation)

Notice that if you multiply the first equation by 3 and the second equation by 2, you can subtract both the equations and cancel out $x$ !

1. $(2x+3y=10) \cdot 3$ $\implies$ $6x+9y=30$

2. $(3x-2y=2) \cdot 2$ $\implies$ $6x-4y=4$

Subtract equation 2 from equation 1

1. $6x+9y-(6x-4y)=30-4$

2. $13y=26$

3. $y=2, x=8/3$
