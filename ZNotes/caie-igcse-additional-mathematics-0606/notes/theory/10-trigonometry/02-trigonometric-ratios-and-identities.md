
## Trigonometric Ratios And Identities

### **Trigonometric Ratios:**

$
\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta} \qquad
\cosec \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta} \qquad
\cot \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta}
$

**Quick tip**: The 3rd letter of each trig function shows what it is the reciprocal of:

$\sec \theta \rightarrow$ 3rd letter is $c$ $\rightarrow$ reciprocal of $\cos \theta$ $\Rightarrow$ $\sec \theta = \frac{1}{\cos \theta}$

$\cosec \theta \rightarrow$ 3rd letter is $s$ $\rightarrow$ reciprocal of $\sin \theta$ $\Rightarrow$ $\cosec \theta = \frac{1}{\sin \theta}$

$\cot \theta \rightarrow$ 3rd letter is $t$ $\rightarrow$ reciprocal of $\tan \theta$ $\Rightarrow$ $\cot \theta = \frac{1}{\tan \theta}$

### **Trigonometric Identities:**

$$
\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}
$$
$$
\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1
$$

$$
\cot^2 \theta + 1 = \cosec^2 \theta
$$
$$
\tan^2 \theta + 1 = \sec^2 \theta
$$

To **prove** trigonometric identities, show that the Left Hand Side (LHS) equals the Right Hand Side (RHS).

$$
\frac{1 - \cos^2 x}{\sin x} = \sin x
$$

We know:
$$
\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1 \Rightarrow 1 - \cos^2 x = \sin^2 x
$$

$$
\frac{1 - \cos^2 x}{\sin x} = \frac{\sin^2 x}{\sin x}
$$

$$
\frac{\sin^2 x}{\sin x} = \sin x
$$

$$
\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}
$$

Therefore, the identity is proven.

**REMEMBER!**

* Tips for proving trigonometric identities:

* Always convert everything into $\sin \theta$ and $\cos \theta$

* Avoid expanding the denominator unless necessary

* Start by simplifying the more complicated side

* Use identities like $\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$ when helpful

* Multiply top and bottom by a suitable expression if needed

* Aim to make both sides look the same step by step
