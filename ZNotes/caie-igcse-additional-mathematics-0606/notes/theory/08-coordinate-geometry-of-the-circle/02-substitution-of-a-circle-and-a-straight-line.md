## Substitution of a Circle and a Straight Line

Substitute the line into the circle equation and solve the quadratic

Discriminant (D) determines the type of intersection:

$$
D > 0 \Rightarrow \text{Two points (chord)}
$$

$$
D = 0 \Rightarrow \text{One point (tangent)}
$$

$$
D < 0 \Rightarrow \text{No intersection}
$$

**Example:**

Circle equation $\rightarrow (x-2)^2+(y-3)^2=8$

Line equation $\rightarrow y=x+1$

* Step 1: Substitute $x+1$ into the circle equation

  $(x-2)^2+(x+1-3)^2=8$

  $(x-2)^2+(x-2)^2=8$

* Step 2: Solve the resulting quadratic in one variable

  Combine terms like: $2(x-2)^2=8$

  Divide both sides by two and expand

  $(x-2)^2=4$

  $x^2-4x+4=4$

  $x^2-4x=0$

  $x(x-4)=0$

  $x=0$ or $x=4$

* Step 3: Use the discriminant to determine the type of intersection

  The quadratic before factoring is: $x^2-4x=0$

  $x^2-4x+0=0$

  Hence, $a=1, b=-4$ and $c=0$

  Calculate the discriminant: $b^2-4ac=(-4)^2-4*1*0=16$

  Since discriminant $>0$, the line intersects at two distinct points.

* Step 4: Find corresponding y values

  For $x=0$, $y=0+1=1$

  For $x=4$, $y=4+1=5$

  Final intersection points $(0,1)$ and $(4,5)$
