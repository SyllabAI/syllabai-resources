## Quadratic Inequality

Consider the inequality involving the product of two factors:

$
(x - d)(x - \beta) < 0
$

This means the product of $(x - d)$ and $(x - \beta)$ is negative.\
For a product of two real numbers to be negative, one factor must be positive and the other must be negative.\
Assuming $d < \beta$, this implies:
$[
d < x < \beta
]$

Similarly, consider the inequality:

$
(x - d)(x - \beta) > 0
$

Here, the product is positive.\
For the product of two numbers to be positive, both factors must be either positive or negative.\
Thus, $x$ lies outside the interval between $d$ and $\beta$:
$[
x < d \quad \text{or} \quad x > \beta
]$

\
The expression $(x - d)(x - \beta)$ equals zero at $x = d$ and $x = \beta$.\
These points divide the number line into three regions:\
$
x < d, \quad d < x < \beta, \quad x > \beta
$
