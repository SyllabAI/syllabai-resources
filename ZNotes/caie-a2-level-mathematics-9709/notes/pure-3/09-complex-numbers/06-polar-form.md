## Polar Form

  - For a complex number $z$ with magnitude $R$ and argument
    $\theta$:

$$
z = R\left( \cos\theta + i\sin\theta \right) = Re^{\text{iθ}}
$$

$$
\therefore\cos\theta + i\sin\text{θ } = e^{\text{iθ}}
$$

**Polar Form to General Form:**

**<span class="underline">Example:</span>**

Convert from polar to general, $z = 4e^{\frac{\pi}{4}i}$

**<center>Solution:</center>**

$$
R = 4\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \arg z = \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

$$
\therefore z = 4\left( \cos\frac{\pi}{4} + i\sin\frac{\pi}{4} \right)
$$

$$
z = 4\left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}i \right)
$$

$$
z = 2\sqrt{2} + \left( 2\sqrt{2} \right)i
$$

**General Form to Polar Form:**

**<span class="underline">Example:</span>**

Convert from general to polar,
$z = 2\sqrt{2} + \left( 2\sqrt{2} \right)i$

**<center>Solution:</center>**

$$
z = 2\sqrt{2} + \left( 2\sqrt{2} \right)i
$$

$$
R = \sqrt{\left( 2\sqrt{2} \right)^{2} + \left( 2\sqrt{2} \right)^{2}} = 4
$$

$$
\theta = \tan^{- 1}\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

$$
\therefore 4\left( \cos\frac{\pi}{4} + i\sin\frac{\pi}{4} \right) = 4e^{\frac{\pi}{4}i}
$$
