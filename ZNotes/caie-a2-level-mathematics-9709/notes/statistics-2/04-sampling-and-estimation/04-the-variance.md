## The Variance

  - Variance can be calculated/given for either a sample or a population
    and there is a difference between them

**Using the divisor** $\mathbf{n}$

  - This is appropriate to use when
    
      - data is given for the whole population and you are interested in
        the variance of the whole
    
      - data is given for the sample and you are interested in the
        variance of just the sample

$$\sigma^{2} = \frac{1}{n}\left( \Sigma x^{2} - \frac{\left( \Sigma x \right)^{2}}{n} \right)$$

**Using the divisor** $\mathbf{(n - 1)}$

  - Appropriate to use when data is given for a sample and you are
    estimating variance of the whole population

  - The quantity calculated $s^{2}$ is known as the **unbiased
    estimate of the population variance**

$$s^{2} = \frac{1}{n - 1}\left( \Sigma x^{2} - \frac{\left( \Sigma x \right)^{2}}{n} \right)$$
