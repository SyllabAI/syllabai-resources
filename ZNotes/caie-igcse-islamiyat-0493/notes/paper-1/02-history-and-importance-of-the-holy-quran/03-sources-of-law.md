## Sources of Law

Islamic legal thinking relies on a clear hierarchy divided into primary and secondary sources:

1. **Primary Sources:** The Holy Quran and the Sunnah/Hadith. Used together, they identify the foundational principles of morality and action.

2. **Secondary Sources:** Consensus (*Ijma*) and Analogical Reasoning (*Qiyas*). These sources are entirely dependent on the primary texts.

### **1. The Holy Quran (Primary Source)**

**Characteristics and Importance**

* **Supreme Status:** The Holy Quran is the first, ultimate, and supreme source of Islamic law. Its absolute divine authority is accepted by every Muslim without question.

* **Divine Safeguard:** It is unchangeable and unamendable due to its divine nature. Allah has promised to protect it from any form of corruption:

> "Surely we have revealed the reminder, and no doubt we will guard it from corruption." (Surah Al-Hijr)

* **Independence:** It is a completely independent source of law, whereas all other legal sources (*Hadith*, *Ijma*, *Qiyas*) are dependent on it and must align perfectly with its principles.

* **Complete Code:** It serves as a comprehensive code of life guiding both the individual and the collective community.

**Legal Interpretation: Tafsir and Mufassir**

* **Definitions:** *Tafsir* is the structural interpretation and clarification of the Holy Quran, conducted by an expert known as a *Mufassir*.

* **Tafsir al-Quran bil-Quran (Clarification by Quran):** The ultimate explanation occurs when one verse clarifies another.

  *Example:* Surah Al-Ma'idah verse $1$ states: *"Lawful to you are all beasts of cattle, with the exceptions named."* The exact "exceptions named" are not outlined here but are explicitly clarified a little later in verse $3$ of the exact same Surah.

* **Tafsir al-Quran bil-Hadith (Clarification by Hadith):** Occurs when the text is explained via the Prophet's (PBUH) traditions.

  *Example:* The detailed, practical methods of performing mandatory prayers are clarified through Hadith, as the Quran establishes the obligation but not the exact format. The Holy Prophet(PBUH) said “Pray as you see me pray.”

### **2. Hadith and Sunnah (Primary Source)**

**Relationship with the Quran**

The Quran and Hadith do not contradict each other; they are fundamentally complementary and must be evaluated together in legal thinking through three distinct methods:

1. **Explanatory Role:** Where the Quran provides a brief or general command, the Hadiths are referred to for full structural details.

2. **Verifying Role:** The Hadiths expand on the Quran, reinforcing its core moral principles.

3. **Silent Role:** When the text of the Quran is silent on a specific legal point, the Hadiths are utilized as the direct authority.

### **3. Ijma / Consensus (Secondary Source)**

**Definition and Legal Scope**

* **Literal Meaning:** Derived from the terms *Jamun* or *Jamat*, meaning a unanimous agreement.

* **Legal Meaning:** A rule governing the Sharia that is the direct outcome of the unanimous consensus of Muslim jurists on a particular question, operating strictly within the boundaries laid down by the Quran and Sunnah.

* **The Legislative Context:** While revelation provides fixed commandments, day-to-day human occurrences are infinite. If answers cannot be determined using the framework of the primary sources, the perfection of the religion could be questioned. *Ijma* allows the Islamic society to take unified action, saving the *Ummah* from confusion and crippling internal divides.

**Textual Justification for Ijma**

* **From the Holy Quran:**

  > *"And consult them in affairs (of moment)"* (Surah Aal-e-Imran $3$:$159$).

  > *"...who (conduct) their affairs by mutual consultation"* (Surah Al-Shura $42$:$38$).

  > *"O those who believe! Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those charged with authority among you."* (Surah An-Nisa $4$:$59$).

* **From the Sunnah:**

  > The Prophet (PBUH) instructed that if a decision cannot be found in the Quran or Sunnah, look to *"what people unanimously agree upon"* (Abu Daud).

  > *"Gather together the righteous from among my community and decide the matter by their council and do not decide it by any man's opinion."* (Abu Daud).
  >
  > *"My nation will not unite in error."* (Mishkat).

**Framework of Authenticity and Classifications**

**Conditions for Authenticity**

1. Only qualified Muslim jurists are authorized to formulate *Ijma* on delicate, complex Sharia issues.

2. Participating jurists must be true believers who are demonstrably pious, righteous, and God-fearing.

3. It can only be initiated when both the Holy Quran and Sunnah are silent on the specific issue.

4. The resulting law must never contradict the Quran or the Sunnah.

5. The disagreement of a single jurist can completely bar the establishment of *Ijma*, provided they bring a solid, evidence-based argument.

**Types of Ijma**

* **Explicit (*Ijma-e-Qowli*):** A unanimous verbal or written agreement by all eligible jurists of an era regarding a legal ruling.

* **Action-based (*Ijma-e-Amali*):** A unanimous consensus expressed through identical, uncontradicted actions and practices by the jurists.

* **Silent (*Ijma-e-Sukooti*):** Occurs when an opinion is expressed by some jurists, and their contemporaries learn of it but make no comments either in favor or against it.

**Three Levels of Strength**

1. **Highest Level:** The unanimous agreements reached by the Companions of the Prophet (the first Islamic community).

2. **Second Level:** The silent consensus (*Ijma-e-Sukooti*) of the Companions.

3. **Third Level:** The consensus reached by expert scholars of any era following the generation of the Companions.

**Historical Examples of Ijma**

1. The Holy Prophet’s (PBUH) practice of consulting his companions on the best strategies to confront enemies.

2. The selection of the first Caliph, Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA), at Saqifah.

3. The compilation and binding of the Holy Quran into a single written volume under Caliph Abu Bakr (RA).

4. The introduction of the second *Adhan* for the Friday congregational prayer.

### **4. Qiyas / Analogical Reasoning (Secondary Source)**

**Definition and Legal Scope**

* **Literal Meaning:** To measure or estimate the length, weight, or quality of something; to compare or make two things equal.

* **Technical Meaning:** A legal method of deducing an unknown principle from an established one by comparing them together.

* **Operational Rule:** Can only be performed when the Quran, Sunnah, and *Ijma* are completely silent on the issue. It discovers and develops existing principles without creating entirely new laws.

* **Sectarian Exception:** Shiite Muslims do not accept *Qiyas*, replacing its function entirely with human reason (*Aql*).

**Textual Justification for Qiyas**

* **From the Holy Quran:**

  > *"...then if you quarrel about anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger..."* (Surah An-Nisa $4$:$59$).
  >
  > *"We have sent to you the book with the Truth so that you may judge among people by means of what Allah has shown you."* (Surah An-Nisa $4$:$105$).

* **From the Sunnah:**

  * Validated through the famous Hadith of Mu'adh bin Jabal when he was dispatched to serve as a judge in Yemen and confirmed he would use personal reasoning (*Ijtihad*) where texts were silent.

  * The Prophet (PBUH) utilized direct analogy when a woman asked if performing Hajj for her deceased father would benefit him. The Prophet asked: *"Supposing your father had a debt to pay and you paid it on his behalf, would this benefit him?"* When she replied yes, he stated: *"The debt owed to Allah merits even greater consideration."*

**The Four Pillars of Qiyas**

Every exercise of *Qiyas* must strictly contain four structural components:

1. **The Root (*Al-Asl*):** The original case explicitly ruled upon in the primary texts.

2. **The Rule (*Hukm*):** The existing legal ruling governing the original case that is slated for extension.

3. **The Stem (*Fara*):** The new, unprecedented case requiring a legal ruling.

4. **The Effective Cause (*'Illah*):** The core attribute or reason found in common between the original root case and the new stem case.

**Classical Examples of Qiyas**

**Example A: The Prohibition of Narcotics**

* **Original Case (*Asl*):** Wine drinking.

* **Existing Rule (*Hukm*):** Impermissible / *Haram*.

* **New Case (*Fara*):** Modern consumption of drugs like cocaine.

* **Effective Cause (*'Illah*):** The intoxicating effect that damages the human mind.

* **Conclusion:** Cocaine is declared *Haram*.

**Example B: Business Transactions During Friday Prayer**

* **Original Case (*Asl*):** Commercial buying and selling of goods after the final call (*Adhan*) for the Friday prayer.

* **Existing Rule (*Hukm*):** Explicitly prohibited based on Surah Al-Jumu'ah $62$:$9$.

* **New Case (*Fara*):** Other worldly activities such as agriculture, formal administration, or education.

* **Effective Cause (*'Illah*):** Any action causing the active diversion of a Muslim from the Friday prayer.

**Conclusion:** All such transactions are legally prohibited during the prayer window.
