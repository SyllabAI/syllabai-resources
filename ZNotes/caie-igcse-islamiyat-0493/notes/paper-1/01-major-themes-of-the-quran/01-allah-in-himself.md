# Major Themes of the Quran

## Allah in Himself

### **1. Surah Al-Baqarah ($2$:$255$) — Ayat ul-Kursi**

### **Translation:**

In the name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful. Allah! There is no God but He, the Living, the Self-subsisting. Neither slumber nor sleep can seize Him. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth. Who will intercede with Him except by His Leave? He knows what is in front of them and what is behind them, While they encompass nothing of His Knowledge, except what He wills. His Throne extends over the heavens and the earth, And He is never weary of preserving them. And He is the Sublime, the Supreme.

### **(a) Main Theme**

* **Divine Nature & Oneness:** Belongs to Surah al-Baqarah (a Madani Surah) and begins with the *Shahadat* (declaration of the oneness of God), elaborating on His matchless attributes.

* **Absence of Limitations:** God is free from all physical weaknesses, limitations, slumber, or tiredness.

* **Supreme Knowledge & Autonomy:** Possesses perfect, absolute knowledge across all times and enjoys complete independence and autonomy in exercising His supreme authority over the universe.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *“Then He established Himself on the Throne.”* (Surah Yunus $10$:$3$ / Surah Ar-Ra'd $13$:$2$)

  > *“...but God is the One free of all wants.”* (Surah Fatir $35$:$15$)

### **(b) Importance in a Muslim's Life Today**

* **Core Monotheism (*Tawhid*):** Instructs Muslims to firmly believe in *Tawhid* regarding God's uniqueness, power, majesty, and absolute glory.

* **Rejection of Polytheism (*Shirk*):** Reinforces that God alone is worthy of worship, training believers to condemn and reject all forms of *Shirk*.

* **Daily Practice:** Frequently recited by Muslims for protection and to continually reiterate God's supreme authority over any worldly power.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"He is Exalted in Power, Wise. He created the heavens without any pillars..."* (Surah Luqman $31$:$9$-$10$)

### **2. Surah Al-An'am ($6$:$101$–$103$)**

### **Translation:**

101\. To Him is due the primal origin of the heavens and the earth: how can He have a son when He has no consort? 102. He created all things, and He has full knowledge of all things. That is Allah, your Lord! There is no god but He, the Creator of all things: then worship Him: and He has power to dispose of all affairs. 103. No vision can grasp Him, but His grasp is over all visions: He is above all comprehension, yet is acquainted with all things.

### **(a) Main Theme**

* **Originator of the Universe:** Describes God as the sole creator and originator of everything in existence.

* **Negation of Kinship:** Explicitly rejects any misconception of God having a family, spouse, or lineage.

* **Sublime  Nature:** Highlights His infinite knowledge and grasp over creation, emphasizing that human imagination cannot physically encompass His essence, though He understands all mysteries.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"...for God is He Who understands the finest mysteries."* (Surah Al-Hajj $22$:$63$)

### **(b) Importance in a Muslim's Life Today**

* **Deepening Tawhid:** Helps Muslims build a precise, uncorrupted understanding of *Tawhid* regarding God’s nature and absolute sovereignty.

* **Eradication of Shirk:** Categorically refutes the belief of God having children or a wife, striking at the root of *Shirk*—which is the only unpardonable/ unforgivable sin in Islam.

* **Sense of Divine Presence:** Reminds believers that while God cannot be seen physically, He is  closer to them than anything else.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"And He is with you wherever you are."* (Surah Al-Hadid $57$:$4$)

  > *"For We are nearer to him than his jugular vein."* (Surah Qaf $50$:$16$)

### **3. Surah Fussilat ($41$:$37$)**

### **Translation:**

37\. Among His signs are the night and the day, and the sun and the moon. Adore not the sun and the moon, but adore Allah, who created them, if it is Him you wish to serve. 38. But if people are arrogant, in the presence of your Lord are those who celebrate His praises by night and by day. And they never grow tired. 39. And among His signs is this: you see the earth barren and desolate, but when We send down rain to it, it is stirred to life and yields increase. Truly, He who gives life to the earth can surely give life to those who are dead, for He has power over all things.

### **(a) Main Theme**

* **Signs in Nature:** An early Makkan passage that highlights natural cosmic phenomena (the cycles of day, night, sun, and moon) as clear evidence of God's existence.

* **Creator vs. Creation:** Commands humans to direct their worship exclusively to the Creator of these objects, rather than worshipping the natural creations themselves.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"Behold! In the creation of the heavens and the earth; in the alternation of the night and the day... are signs for the people who are wise."* (Surah Al-Baqarah $2$:$164$)

### **(b) Importance in a Muslim's Life Today**

* **Pondering over Creation:** Urges Muslims to observe the natural environment to recognize the invisible Creator through His visible signs.

* **Avoiding Past Mistakes:** Prevents believers from falling into nature-worship or idolatry, a trap that earlier historical communities fell victim to.

* **Gratitude:** Encourages Muslims to use their intellect, express gratitude for divine guidance, and worship one Supreme Being.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"Verily, in the alternation of the night and the day, and in all that God has created in the heavens and the earth, are signs for those who fear Him."* (Surah Ar-Rum $30$:$40$)

### **4. Surah Al-Shura ($42$:$4$–$5$)**

### **Translation:**

4\. To Him belongs all that is in the heavens and on earth: and He is most High, most Great. 5. The heavens are almost rent asunder from above them, and the angels celebrate the praises of their Lord, and pray for forgiveness for beings on earth: Behold! Verily Allah is He, the Oft-Forgiving, the Most Merciful.

### **(a) Main Theme**

* **Dominance:** Focuses on God’s supreme authority, grandeur, and unshared lordship over the entire universe.

* **Angels:** Describes how the heavens almost break apart under the sheer weight of His divine glory, while countless angels continuously glorify Him and seek forgiveness for mankind.

* **Human weakness:** Contrasts God's immense power with human weakness and inclination toward sin.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"His are all things in the heaven and on the earth."* (Surah Al-Baqarah $2$:$255$)

  > *"It is man that wrongs his own soul."* (Surah Yunus $10$:$44$)

### **(b) Importance in a Muslim's Life Today**

* **Dual Articles of Faith:** Reinforces two foundational beliefs—Belief in *Tawhid* and Belief in *Angels*.

* **Hope in Divine Mercy:** Encourages believers not to lose hope despite their human errors, as God's mercy and willingness to forgive are boundless.

* **Prompting Repentance (*Tawbah*):** Realizing that angels pray for human forgiveness motivates Muslims to sincerely repent for their misdeeds.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"Despair not of the mercy of God; for God forgives all sins, for He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful."* (Surah Az-Zumar $39$:$53$)

### **5. Surah Al-Ikhlas ($112$)**

### Translation:

**Translation:**

In the name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.Say: He is Allah, the One;Allah, the Absolute (the Eternal).He does not beget nor is He begotten,And there is none like Him.

### **(a) Main Theme**

* **Purity of Faith:** An early Makkan Surah outlining the most absolute and cardinal definition of *Tawhid*.

* **Absolute Monotheism:** Emphatically declares God's absolute oneness and negates any concepts of lineage, offspring, or ancestry.

* **Timeless & Peerless:** States that God is free from time and space (eternal) and has no equal or counterpart anywhere in existence.

### **(b) Importance in a Muslim's Life Today**

* **Purification of Belief:** Cleanses a Muslim’s concept of God from any subtle or open forms of *Shirk* (hence the title *Al-Ikhlas*).

* **Daily Worship:** Frequently recited during daily prayers (*Salah*) to reaffirm rejection of false deities.

* **Weight in the Quran:** Highlighted by Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) as being equal to one-third of the Holy Quran due to its concentrated theological message.

* **Cross-References:**

  > *"And your God is one God."* (Surah Al-Baqarah $2$:$163$)
