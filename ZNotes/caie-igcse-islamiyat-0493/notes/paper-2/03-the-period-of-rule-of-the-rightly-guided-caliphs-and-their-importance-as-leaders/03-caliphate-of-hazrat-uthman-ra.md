## Caliphate of Hazrat Uthman(RA)

### **Election of Hazrat Uthman**

* **Appointment of Electoral Council:** Before his death, Hazrat Umar appointed a six-member council consisting of Hazrat Uthman, Hazrat Ali, Hazrat Talha, Hazrat Zubair, Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas, and Hazrat Abdur Rahman bin Auf to select the next Caliph.

* **Consultation Process:** Hazrat Abdur Rahman bin Auf withdrew his own candidacy to act as the neutral arbitrator and spent three days consulting the council members, prominent tribal leaders, and the general public in Madinah.

* **Public Pledge (*Bay'ah*):** Upon finding overwhelming consensus in favor of Hazrat Uthman, Hazrat Abdur Rahman bin Auf publicly pledged allegiance to him in the Masjid-e-Nabawi, followed by Hazrat Ali and the rest of the community in $24$ AH ($644$ AD).

### **Administration of Hazrat Uthman**

* **Provincial Structure:** Divided the vast Islamic empire into eight primary administrative provinces, appointing governors (*Walis*) to oversee local administration, revenue collection, and law enforcement.

* **Establishment of the First Naval Fleet:** Authorized Muawiyah bin Abi Sufyan to construct the first Islamic naval fleet to defend Mediterranean coastal territories against Byzantine attacks.

* **Standardization of the Quran:** Formed a specialized committee led by Zaid bin Thabit to produce official, standardized copies of the Quran in the Quraishi dialect to ensure textual unity across the expanding empire.

* **Judicial & Public Vigilance:** Maintained an independent judiciary, preserved separate salaries for judges (*Qadis*), and retained police and vigilance systems to monitor markets and public order.

* **Public Works & Infrastructure:** Funded extensive public welfare projects, including the construction of roads, bridges, guest houses, administrative offices, and the Mahroz dam to safeguard Madinah from floods.

* **Expansion of Holy Mosques:** Extended and renovated both the Masjid-e-Nabawi in Madinah and the Masjid-al-Haram in Makkah using high-quality building materials to accommodate the growing number of pilgrims.

* **Financial Allocations:** Managed the *Bait-ul-Maal* efficiently, increased stipends for citizens, and frequently used his personal wealth to fund state welfare and public projects without drawing a salary.

* **Religious Guidance through Hadith:** Emphasized strict adherence to the Prophetic traditions, aligning state administration with individual and communal Hadith guidance on sincerity, social brotherhood, justice, and kindness.

### **Assassination of Hazrat Uthman ($35$ AH / $656$ AD)**

* **The Siege of His Residence:**

  * Rebels from Egypt, Kufa, and Basra marched to Madinah demanding his resignation.

  * When Hazrat Uthman refused to abdicate the office entrusted to him by God, the rebels laid a $40$-day siege to his residence (*Al-Dar*), cutting off food and water supplies.

* **Refusal to Counter-Attack:**

  * Prominent Companions, including Hazrat Ali, Hazrat Hasan, Hazrat Husayn, and Hazrat Ibn Umar, offered to fight the rebels.

  * Hazrat Uthman strictly forbade any bloodshed in the Prophet’s city, saying: *"I will not be the first to shed the blood of a Muslim."*

* **The Martyrdom:**

  * On the $18$th of Dhul Hijjah, $35$ AH, several assassins scaled the back walls of his residence.

  * They stormed his room while he was seated reciting the Holy Quran.

  * Despite his wife Naila attempting to protect him, the assassins attacked him; his blood spilled onto the Quranic verse: *"Allah will suffice you against them, and He is the All-Hearing, All-Knowing"* (Surah Al-Baqarah $2:137$).

* **Burial:**

  * He was buried at night in *Jannat al-Baqi* in Madinah.

  * His tragic martyrdom triggered intense internal divisions (*Fitnah*), marking a major turning point in Islamic history.

### Modern Application & Contemporary Legacy of Hazrat Uthman (RA)

* **Model for Governance Today:** Demonstrates long-term infrastructure investment (roads, ports, water wells, and public expansion) and standardized state documentation, essential for national stability and growth.

* **Relations with Other States & Non-Muslims:** Developed maritime power and naval diplomacy to protect territorial borders while encouraging cross-border trade and peaceful commerce across North Africa and the Mediterranean.

* **Example for Modern Muslim Communities:** Shows how leaders should preserve scriptural and cultural unity (as done with the Qur'anic standardization) and prioritize civil peace over personal survival by refusing to incite bloodshed among citizens during political turmoil.
