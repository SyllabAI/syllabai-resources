## Caliphate of Hazrat Ali(RA)

### **Election of Hazrat Ali ($656$ AD / $35$ AH)**

* **Post-Martyrdom Chaos:** Following the assassination of Hazrat Uthman, Madinah remained in administrative chaos under the control of rebel factions for five days.

* **Refusal of Office:** Prominent Companions approached Hazrat Ali to assume the Caliphate, but he initially declined, stating he preferred to serve as an advisor (*Vizier*) rather than a ruler (*Amir*).

* **Urgency & Consensus:** With no central authority and increasing instability, senior Companions (including Ansar and Muhajirun) urged him to accept to prevent further division.

* **Public Pledge (*Bay'ah*):** Hazrat Ali agreed on the condition that the pledge be made publicly in the Masjid-e-Nabawi; the general public and prominent Companions took the oath of allegiance in $35$ AH.

### **Battle of Camel (*Jamal*) — $656$ AD / $36$ AH**

* **Core Issue:** Hazrat Aisha, along with Talha and Zubair, demanded immediate retribution (*Qisas*) for Hazrat Uthman's assassins; Hazrat Ali prioritized restoring administrative stability before prosecuting the killers.

* **Negotiations:** Both sides met near Basra and reached a peaceful understanding to avoid military conflict.

* **Sabotage by Instigators:** Saboteurs and extremists (led by Abdullah ibn Saba's network) launched a surprise night attack on both camps to break the peace talks, tricking each side into believing the other had betrayed them.

* **Outcome:**

  * Hazrat Ali’s forces prevailed.

  * Talha and Zubair were martyred during the chaos.

  * Hazrat Aisha's camel was neutralised to end the battle; Hazrat Ali treated her with high honor and escorted her back safely to Madinah.

* **Relocation of Capital:** Hazrat Ali moved the capital of the Caliphate from Madinah to Kufa due to its strategic and central position.

### **Battle of Siffin  — $657$ AD / $37$ AH**

* **Cause:** Muawiyah (Governor of Syria) refused to pledge allegiance to Hazrat Ali until Hazrat Uthman's killers were brought to justice and questioned Hazrat Ali's dismissal of provincial governors.

* **Confrontation:** The two armies met at Siffin (near the Euphrates River); several days of skirmishes ended in a major battle.

* **Turning Point & Quran on Spears:** As Hazrat Ali’s forces (led by Ammar bin Yasir and Malik al-Ashtar) gained the upper hand, Muawiyah’s troops raised copies of the Quran on their spears, appealing for arbitration based on Allah's word.

* **Arbitration (*Tahkeem*):** Hazrat Ali reluctantly agreed to arbitration under pressure from his army; Abu Musa al-Ash'ari was chosen to represent Hazrat Ali, and Amr ibn al-Aas represented Muawiyah.

### **Battle of Nahrawan (Against Kharijites) — $658$ AD / $38$ AH**

* **Rise of Kharijites:** A faction within Hazrat Ali’s army revolted against the decision to arbitrate, claiming *"No judgment but Allah's"* (*La Hukma Illa Lillah*); they deserted his ranks and became known as the Kharijites (Outsliders).

* **Extremism & Violence:** The Kharijites declared all Muslims who accepted arbitration as apostates and began terrorizing and killing civilians (including Abdullah bin Khabbab).

* **Military Action:** Hazrat Ali marched against them after they refused to surrender the murderers; his forces decisively defeated the Kharijites at Nahrawan.

### **Assassination of Hazrat Ali ($661$ AD / $40$ AH)**

* **Kharijite Conspiracy:** A group of surviving Kharijites plotted to simultaneously assassinate Hazrat Ali, Muawiyah, and Amr ibn al-Aas to eliminate all major political leaders.

* **The Attack:** On the $19$th of Ramadan, $40$ AH, Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam struck Hazrat Ali on the head with a poisoned sword while he was leading the morning prayers (*Fajr*) in the Great Mosque of Kufa.

* **Martyrdom:** Hazrat Ali succumbed to his injuries two days later on the $21$st of Ramadan, $40$ AH.

* **Final Instructions:** Before his death, he instructed his family to execute Ibn Muljam strictly in accord with Islamic law (*Qisas*) with a single strike, forbidding any torture or collective punishment. His family worried that his enemies would violate his body, buried him in secret. However today mainstream historical and theological consensus places his resting shrine at **Najaf** (modern-day Iraq).

### **Administration of Hazrat Ali**

* **Centralization at Kufa:** Moved the imperial capital from Madinah to Kufa to secure a strategically central base for political, administrative, and military management.

* **Rigid Accountability & Anti-Corruption:** Replaced ineffective or controversial provincial governors immediately upon taking office to restore integrity and reduce corruption.

* **Judicial Reform & Instructions:** Maintained complete independence of the judiciary (*Qada*) and issued detailed administrative codes (notably his famous letter to Malik al-Ashtar, Governor of Egypt) emphasizing justice, humility, and accessibility for all citizens.

* **Fair Wealth Distribution (*Bait-ul-Maal*):** Restored Hazrat Abu Bakr’s policy of equal distribution of treasury funds among all citizens regardless of tribal status, race, or seniority, eliminating special financial privileges.

* **Taxation & Agricultural Reforms:** Linked land tax (*Kharaj*) to land yield and active cultivation rather than fixed rates, ordering tax collectors to focus on improving infrastructure and supporting farmers over collecting revenue.

* **Market Supervision & Public Oversight:** Personally inspected local markets (*Souks*) in Kufa to enforce fair weights, measures, price control, and honest business practices.

* **Rights of Minorities (*Dhimmis*):** Protected the civil rights, property, and religious freedom of non-Muslim citizens, forbidding collectors from confiscating their basic necessities or tools for tax debts.

* **Public Vigilance & Police (*Shurta*):** Reorganized the police force (*Police / Shurta*) and night patrols to curb urban unrest, prevent sabotage, and preserve law and order across unstable regions.

### Modern Application & Contemporary Legacy of Hazrat Ali (RA)

* **Model for Governance Today:** His famous letter of instruction to Malik al-Ashtar serves as a classic treatise on executive ethics, emphasizing anti-corruption, meritocracy, social safety nets, and total judicial independence.

* **Relations with Other States & Factions:** Established clear frameworks regarding internal conflict resolution, peace negotiations, and civil rights during domestic disputes, emphasizing mediation and maintaining open dialogue with political opponents.

* **Example for Modern Muslim Communities:** Provides an enduring standard for intellectual depth, judicial fairness, and upholding the rule of law—even when legal decisions or political concessions disadvantage the state or leader personally.
