## Hadith in Islamic Legal Thinking and Its Relationship with Other Sources

### How the Qur'an and Hadith Are Used Together

The Qur’an and the Hadith are the two primary sources of Islamic law. They never contradict each other; rather, they are complementary and work together in three main ways:

* **Corroborative (Confirmatory):** The Hadith confirms and reinforces teachings directly stated in the Qur'an, such as the basic obligation to establish prayer (*Salah*), pay the obligatory charity (*Zakah*), or respect parents.

* **Explanatory (Detailed):** Where the Qur'an provides general, brief, or overarching principles, the Hadith provides the practical details and exact operational guidance.

  * *Example:* The Qur'an commands Muslims to "establish prayer", but the Hadith explains the precise timings, number of units (*raka'at*), and physical actions required for prayer.

* **Silent (Legislative/Supplementary):** When the Qur'an is silent on a specific legal matter, the Hadith serves as an independent primary authority to establish rulings.

  * *Example:* The Hadith prohibits wearing silk and gold for Muslim men, as well as the consumption of domesticated donkeys, topics on which the Qur'an does not explicitly legislate.

Together, the Qur'an and Hadith identify the core principles of morality, law, and action in Islam.

### Hadith and *Ijma'* (Consensus of Jurists)

* **Basis of Authority:** *Ijma'* derives its legitimacy directly from the Sunnah. Jurists rely on authentic Hadiths to justify using consensus as a valid legal source.

  * *Hadith Proof:* The Prophet (ﷺ) said: *"My nation will not unite in error"* (Mishkat) and instructed: *"If anything comes to you which is not in the Sunnah of the Prophet, then look to what people unanimously agree upon"* (Abu Daud).

* **Order of Precedence:** *Ijma'* can only be performed when both the Qur'an and the Hadith are silent on a particular issue or require further operational consensus. Any consensus reached by jurists must strictly align with and never contradict the Qur'an or Sunnah.

### Hadith and *Qiyas* (Analogical Reasoning)

* **Basis of Authority:** The practice of *Qiyas* is explicitly validated by the Prophet (ﷺ) through his own actions and teachings.

  * *Hadith Proof:* In the famous Hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal, when sent to Yemen as a judge, he stated he would judge by the Book of Allah, then the Sunnah, and if he found no explicit text, he would exercise his own reasoned judgment (*Ijtihad*)—an approach approved by the Prophet (ﷺ).

  * *Analogical Example from Sunnah:* When a woman asked if she could perform Hajj on behalf of her deceased father, the Prophet (ﷺ) asked her: *"Supposing your father had a debt to pay and you paid it on his behalf, would this benefit him?"* When she agreed, he replied: *"The debt owed to Allah merits even greater consideration"*.

* **Role as the Root (*'Asl*):** In performing *Qiyas*, the original case (*'Asl*) and its governing rule (*Hukm*) are frequently derived directly from an established Hadith. The underlying effective cause (*'Illah*) is then extracted from the Hadith to extend the legal ruling to new, modern cases (*Far'*).
