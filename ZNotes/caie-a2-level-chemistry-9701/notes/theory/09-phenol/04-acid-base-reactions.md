## Reactions of -OH

### **Reaction with Base**

* C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH<sub>(l)</sub> + NaOH<sub>(aq)</sub>
  $\xrightarrow{r.t.p}$
  C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sup>-</sup>Na<sup>+</sup><sub>(aq)</sub> + H<sub>2</sub>O
* Type of reaction:
  * Neutralization
* Reagent:
  * sodium hydroxide
* Condition:
  * r.t.p.
* Regenerating phenol:
  * When strong acid (HCl) added to solution of sodium phenoxide,
    phenol regenerated

    C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sup>-</sup>Na<sup>+</sup><sub>(aq)</sub> + HCl<sub>(aq)­</sub> $\rightarrow$
    C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH<sub>(l)</sub> + NaCl

  * Initially dense white emulsion formed then slowly oily liquid
    layer of phenol separates

### **Reaction with Sodium**

* C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH<sub>(l)</sub> + Na<sub>(s)</sub> $\xrightarrow{r.t.p}$
  C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sup>-</sup>Na<sup>+</sup><sub>(aq)</sub> + ½H<sub>2(g)</sub>
* Type of reaction:
  * (metal and acid) Redox
* Condition:
  * r.t.p.
* Reacts with Na liberating H<sub>2</sub> gas
* Note: phenol does not react with metal carbonate to liberate
  CO<sub>2</sub>, which shows that it’s fairly weaker than other carboxylic
  acids

### **Reaction with acyl chlorides (to form esters)**

* C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>OH<sub>(l)</sub> + R-COCl $\rightarrow$ R-COO-C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub> + HCl
* Type of reaction:
  * Condensation
* Mechanism:
  * Addition-Elimination
* (see “Acyl Chlorides” for more details…)

-> Reactions with phenol apply to other phenolic compounds such as naphthol
