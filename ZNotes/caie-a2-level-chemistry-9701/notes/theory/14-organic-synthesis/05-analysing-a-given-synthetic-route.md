
## **Analysing a Given Synthetic Route**

Exam questions will frequently present a multi-step synthesis and ask you to deconstruct specific steps. You need to identify three key things:

**A. Type of Reaction**

Be precise with terminology. Don't just say "substitution"; say **Nucleophilic Substitution** (e.g., haloalkane to amine) or **Electrophilic Substitution** (e.g., nitration of benzene). Other key types include:

* Electrophilic Addition (alkenes)

* Nucleophilic Addition (carbonyls)

* Oxidation / Reduction

* Hydrolysis (breaking bonds with water/acid/alkali)

* Condensation / Elimination

**B. Reagents and Conditions**

Memorizing the exact conditions is non-negotiable for top marks.

* Is the $NaOH$ *aqueous* (leads to nucleophilic substitution to form an alcohol) or *ethanolic* (leads to elimination to form an alkene)?

* Does the reaction require *reflux*, *room temperature*, or a specific *catalyst* (like $AlCl_3$ for Friedel-Crafts)?

**C. Possible By-Products**

Always account for every atom. When two molecules join, what falls off?

* **Esterification:** Reacting a carboxylic acid + alcohol yields **$H_2O$** as a by-product. Reacting an acyl chloride + alcohol yields **$HCl$** (steamy fumes) as a by-product.

* **Amide formation:** Reacting an acyl chloride + amine yields **$HCl$**.

* **Substitution:** Reacting a haloalkane ($R-Br$) with $NaOH$ leaves a **$NaBr$** salt by-product.
