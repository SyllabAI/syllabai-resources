## Degradable Polymers

- **Disadvantages of plastics:**

  - Non-biodegradable

  - On combustion, give out harmful vapours

**Biodegradable Plastics:**

- Can contain small amounts of starch: bacteria and fungi in moist
  soil can break them down into smaller compounds, increase their surface areas,
  making them easier to decompose

- Can be made from monomers derived from plants e.g. PLA: soil
  microorganism can digest it easily

- Can be hydrolysed in acidic conditions (when moist or if the surface area of contact is great):

  - Polyamides broken down to carboxylic acids & amines

  - Polyesters broken down to carboxylic acids & alcohols

- ### Can be hydrolysed in alkaline conditions (e.g., heating with aqueous NaOH):

  * Polyesters are broken down into **carboxylate salts** (not free carboxylic acids!) and **alcohols**.

    * *Note:* The $OH^-$ ion attacks the ester linkage. Because the environment is basic, the resulting carboxylic acid immediately reacts with the alkali to form a salt (e.g., a sodium carboxylate salt if $NaOH$ is used).

  * Polyamides are broken down into **carboxylate salts** and **amines**.

    * *Note:* Polyamides generally require more prolonged heating with concentrated alkali compared to polyesters, as the amide bond is stronger and more resistant to hydrolysis.

      **NOTE:** Alkaline hydrolysis of polyesters goes to **completion** because it is an irreversible reaction (the formation of the stable carboxylate ion prevents the reverse reaction). In contrast, acidic hydrolysis is a reversible equilibrium reaction. This means alkaline hydrolysis is often faster and more effective for breaking down these plastics.

**Photodegradable Plastics:**

- Carbonyl group (–C$=$O) can absorb energy from ultraviolet waves

- Causes bonds in region of carbonyl group to weaken and break

- Disadvantages:

  - Plastic usually buried under $\therefore$ cannot get sunlight

  - If recycled, products are weaker

- This also increases the surface area of contact, which helps with biodegradation of the of the polymer
