## Chlorine Substituted Acids

![](../../../assets/43c3adc29e53-allimagesd1cdc83700f1a1aa3365b78d.png)
**Acidity of carboxylic acids increases when the C-atom bonded to -COOH is itself bonded to electron-withdrawing atoms (e.g. Cl)**

- The greater the number of Cl-atoms, the greater the polarisation and weaking of the O-H bond is. The acid ionises more as a result

- Conjugate base (the anion formed during ionisation of acid) is more stabilized due to the extended delocalization of the negative charge on the –COO<sup>-</sup>, and so it less likely to bond with a H<sup>+</sup> ion

 

**Remember:**

- If an e<sup>-</sup> donating gp. is present, the molecule less acidic

- If an e<sup>-</sup> withdrawing gp. is present, the molecule more acidic
