## Relative Acidities of Carboxylic Acids

* Carboxylic acids are stronger acids than phenols, water and alcohols because:

  * electron-withdrawing groups further weaken the O-H bond in the carboxylic acid molecule.

  * after the acid dissociates, the electron-withdrawing groups delocalise the negative charge on the $-COO^-$ part of the ion, further increasing the stability of that group, making it less likely to bond with another $H^+$

* Acidity of carboxylic acids decreases as carbon chain length increases
  because:

  - alkyl groups are e<sup>-</sup> donating groups which have a +ve
    inductive effect increasing strength of O–H bond, and making it more
    difficult to donate H<sup>+</sup>

  - alkyl groups increase –ve charge on ion formed when dissociated,
    making it easier to accept the H<sup>+</sup> and reform the molecule

  - alkyl groups destabilise the conjugate base due to positive inductive effect so acidity decreases
