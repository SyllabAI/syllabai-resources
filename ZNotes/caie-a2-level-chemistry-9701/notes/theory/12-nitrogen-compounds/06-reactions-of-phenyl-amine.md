## Reactions of Phenylamine

**Bromination**

![](../../../assets/4f2acc2b5300-image091.png)

- Type of reaction:

  - electrophilic substitution

- Reagent:

  - aq. Bromine

- Conditions:

  - r.t.p. (no catalyst, since the greater electron density in benzene ring makes it a stronger nucleophile)

- Forms 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine (since the 2, 4, and 6 positions around the benzene ring have been activated by the electron-donating $-NH_{2}$)

- Note: White ppt. forms

**Diazotization**

![](../../../assets/9d81a347cef9-image092.png)

(Benzene ring)–N<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup> is called diazonium ion

- Type of reaction:

  - diazotization reaction (it is the 1st step in the making of an azo dye)

- Reagent:

  - nitrous acid (NaNO<sub>2</sub> with excess HCl<sub>(aq)</sub>)

  - *(HNO<sub>2</sub> is a weak, unstable acid, so it has to be produced in a test tube by the reaction of NaNO<sub>2</sub> with excess HCl)*

- Condition:

  - low temp. (less than 10${^\circ}$C)

- Note:

  - Diazonium salts of aryl amine are stabilized by delocalization of
    –N<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup> ion’s e<sup>-</sup>s over the benzene
    ring

  - However, the diazonium ion highly is unstable and can decompose at higher temperatures, giving off N<sub>2</sub> gas

- When diazonium chloride is warmed, phenol is formed along with $N_2$(seen as efferevesence) and $HCl$
