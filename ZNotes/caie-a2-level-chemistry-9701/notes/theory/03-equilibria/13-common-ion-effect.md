## Common Ion Effect

- **Common ion effect**: lowering of solubility of an ionic compound
  by addition of a common ion to the solution

**Calculating solubility with common ion:**

BaSO<sub>4</sub> is a sparingly soluble salt (K<sub>sp</sub>
$= 1.0 \times 10^{- 10}$ mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>). Compare the
solubility at 298K of BaSO<sub>4</sub> in an aq. solution of 0.1 mol
dm<sup>-3</sup> H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> to in pure water

**<center>Solution</center>**

*Calculate conc. of Ba<sup>2+</sup> in pure water*

<center>K<sub>sp</sub> = [Ba<sup>2+</sup>] [So<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>] and both conc. are equal, therefore</center>

$$
\lbrack Ba^{2+}\rbrack= \sqrt{1.0 \times 10^{- 10}} = 1.0 \times 10^{- 5}
$$

*To calculate* Ba<sup>2+</sup>*, first find conc. of common ion,
SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup>*

<center>H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> is a strong acid which dissociates
completely</center>

$$
\therefore \lbrack H_2SO_4\rbrack = \lbrack SO_4^{2-}\rbrack = 0.1
$$

*Use new conc. of SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup> and ignore original as it
is negligible to calculate new $Ba^{2+}$*

$$
\left\lbrack Ba^{2 +} \right\rbrack = \frac{K_{\text{sp}}}{\left\lbrack \text{SO}_{4}^{2 -} \right\rbrack} = \frac{1.0 \times 10^{- 10}}{0.1} = 1.0 \times 10^{- 9}
$$

Hence, solubility has decrease by a factor of $10^{- 4}$
