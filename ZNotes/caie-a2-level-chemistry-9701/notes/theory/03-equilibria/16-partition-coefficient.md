## Partition Coefficient

* **Partition law:** a solutes solubility will be differed in two
  different liquids that are immiscible. The solute will be more
  soluble in one of the two.

  Measured in $\lbrack conc.\rbrack$: g cm<sup>-3</sup> or mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

* When solute added to two immiscible solvents, a dynamic equilibrium
  is set up where particles move between the two solvents but
  concentration in each remains constant.

* Hence, can calculate a constant $k$ for the equilibrium

* **Partition coefficient (K<sub>pc</sub>):** equilibrium constant
  that relates the concentration of solute partition between two
  immiscible solves at a particular temperature.

$$K_{\text{pc}} = \frac{Conc.\ of\ Solute\ in\ Solvent\ 1}{Conc.\ of\ Solute\ in\ Solvent\ 2}$$

Place concentration of the first solvent mentioned on top of the
K<sub>pc</sub> expression

* K<sub>pc</sub> can be useful to work out how much solvent needed to
  extract minimum amount of solute from one solvent to another
* Repeated extractions with smaller volumes of solvent more efficient
  than one extraction with a larger volume.

**Calculating K<sub>pc</sub>**

100cm<sup>3</sup> of a 0.100mol dm<sup>-3</sup> solution of ammonia in
was shaken with 50cm<sup>3</sup> of an organic solvent and left in a
separating funnel for equilibrium to establish

A 20.0cm<sup>3</sup> portion of the aqueous layer was run off and
titrated against 0.200mol dm<sup>-3</sup> dilute hydrochloric acid. The
end-point was found to be 9.40cm<sup>3</sup> of acid.

What is the partition coefficient of ammonia between these two
solvents?

**<center>Solution</center>**

*Calculate conc. of aqueous layer run off (NH<sub>3(aq)</sub>)*

**<center>NH<sub>3(aq)</sub> + HCl<sub>(aq)</sub>
NH<sub>4</sub>Cl<sub>(aq)</sub></center>**

**<center>20.0cm<sup>3</sup> 9.40cm<sup>3</sup></center>**

*Use concentration of acid used to neutralize*

$$0.2 = \frac{\text{mols of }\text{NH}_{3}\text{ in water} }{9.4 \times 10^{- 3}}$$

$$\text{mols of }\text{NH}_3\ in\ water = 1.88 \times 10^{- 3}$$

*Use ratios to find moles of NH<sub>3(aq)</sub> in
100\.0cm<sup>3</sup>*

$$20.0cm^{3}:1.88 \times 10^{- 3}\text{mols}$$

$$100cm^{3}:9.40 \times 10^{- 3}\text{mols}$$

<center> Therefore <b>moles of NH<sub>3</sub> in water in equilibrium</b>
= 9.40 x 10<sup>-3</sup></center>

*To find moles of NH<sub>3</sub> in organic solvent, first find moles
present originally in the ammonia solution*

$$0.1 = \frac{\text{initial mols of }\text{NH}_3\text{ in water}\ }{100 \times 10^-3}$$

<center> Therefore <b>moles of NH<sub>3</sub> in water initially</b>
= 0.01<b>mols</b></center>

*Subtract initial & final moles in water to find no. of mols in organic
solvent* = 0.01 - 9.40 x 10<sup>-3</sup> = 6.0 x 10<sup>-4</sup>

*Calculate the K<sub>pc</sub> value*

$K$<sub>pc</sub> $= \frac{\text{conc. of }\text{NH}{3(org)}}{\text{conc. of }\text{NH}_{3(aq)}} = \frac{\frac{6.0 \times 10^{- 4}}{50 \times 10^{- 3}}}{\frac{9.4 \times 10^{- 3}}{100 \times 10^{- 3}}}$

$$K_{\text{pc}} = 0.1277$$
