## Ionic Product of Water, K<sub>w</sub> 

  - Water molecules can function as both acids and bases; one molecule
    (base) accepts H<sup>+</sup> from second one (acid)

 H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>(l)</sub> $\rightleftharpoons$
 H<sup>+</sup><sub>(aq)</sub> + OH<sup>-</sup><sub>(aq)</sub>

$$K_c = \frac{\left\lbrack H^{+} \right\rbrack\left\lbrack \text{OH}^{-} \right\rbrack}{\left\lbrack H_2O \right\rbrack}$$

  - Conc. of water remains constant $\therefore$ can eliminate from
    equation and find the **ionic product of water**, K<sub>w</sub>

$$K_w = \left\lbrack H^{+} \right\rbrack\left\lbrack \text{OH}^{-} \right\rbrack$$

  - At 25<sup>o</sup>C, the K<sub>w</sub> of water is 1.00 x
    10<sup>-14</sup> mol<sup>2</sup> dm<sup>-6</sup>

  - $- \log\ $throughout equation,
    $- \log{1 \times 10^{- 14} = 14}\therefore$

$$14 = pH + pOH$$

  - pH of an alkali can be calculated using this formula

&nbsp;

**Calculating pH of a strong base:**

Calculate the pH of a solution of sodium hydroxide of concentration
0.05 mol dm<sup>-3</sup>

**<center>Solution</center>**

**It is a strong base and dissociates completely the conc. of ions is
equal to conc. of the solution**

$$\left\lbrack OH^{-} \right\rbrack = \left\lbrack \text{NaOH} \right\rbrack = 0.05$$

**Calculate pOH by logging**

$$pOH = - \log{0.05} = 1.30$$

**Calculate pH by using the following**

$$pH + pOH = 14$$

$$pH = 14 - pOH = 14 - 1.30 = 12.7$$
