## Thin Layer Chromatography

- As solute rises with solvent, they get separated from mobile phase
  and adsorbed to the stationary phase

- **Adsorption:** separation due to different attraction between the
  compound and the stationary phase, relative to their solubility in
  the solvent

- The solid in stationary phase is either Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>
  or SiO<sub>2</sub>; it is spread onto a microscope slide as a slurry
  using water and then dried into a white coating

- Polar molecules have a greater attraction for a polar solid used as
  stationary phase and they are adsorbed more strongly onto its
  surface $\therefore$ travel more slowly

- If dried silica or Al<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> become rehydrated,
  water also acts as a partitioning stationary phase together with the
  adsorbing stationary solid phase

- TLC is quicker than paper chromatography and can be used on smaller
  samples

- **For colourless spots:**

  - Keep plate in closed container with I<sub>2</sub> crystals.
    I<sub>2</sub> vapor accumulate on spots which appear dark brown

  - Place plate under UV light; the glow is reduced by solutes which
    appear as dark spots on the bright plate

## **High $R_f$ vs Low $R_f$**

- **High $R_f$ Value (Spot travels far from the baseline):** The component has a **high relative solubility in the mobile phase** and a **weak interaction with the stationary phase**. It spends more time dissolved in the moving solvent than it does binding to the plate.

- **Low $R_f$ Value (Spot stays near the baseline):** The component has a **low relative solubility in the mobile phase** and a **strong interaction with the stationary phase**. It binds tightly to the polar plate and resists being carried upward by the solvent.
