## Identifying –OH or –NH Signal

* The –OH signal always appears as a single peak because the H atom is
  exchanged rapidly with those in water causing it to appear
  unaffected to spin-spin splitting

 $$ROH + H_2O \rightleftharpoons RO + H^3O$$

* Deuterium is an isotope of hydrogen that behaves chemically like H
  but different magnetic properties to H so don't produce peaks in the
  area of spectrum with H
* Organic compounds are shaken with deuterium oxide (D<sub>2</sub>O)
  and the H-atoms bonded to electronegative atoms (–OH and –NH) are
  replaced; deuterium exchange

$$ROH + D_2>O \rightarrow ROD +DHO$$

When this is done, the NMR signal due to –OH and –NH hydrogen
disappears, confirming that the peaks were caused by those groups
