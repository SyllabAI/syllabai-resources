## Lattice Energy

* **Lattice Energy
  (** $\mathrm{\Delta}H$<sub>${\text{lat}}$</sub>$^{\theta}$ **):**
  Enthalpy change when one mole of an ionic compound is formed from
  its gaseous ions under standard conditions
* It is always exothermic (-ve), as cations attract anions and energy is released when bonds are formed
* e.g: Na<sup>+</sup>(g) + Cl<sup>-</sup>(g) ${\rightarrow}$ NaCl (s)
* The more –ve $\mathrm{\Delta}H_{\text{lat}}^{\ominus}$ is, the stronger the ionic bonding is and the more stable the ionic compound is
* If theoretical and experimental
  $\mathrm{\Delta}H_{\text{lat}}^{\ominus}$ values are similar, bonding is pure ionic (otherwise it is an intermediate between ionic and covalent)

### Factors Affecting Lattice Energy

$$
\mathrm{\Delta}H_{\text{lat}}^{\ominus
} \propto \frac{\text{Charge on Ion}}{\text{Radius of Ion}}
$$

* **Radius of ion:** The smaller the radius/smaller the size of the ion, the greater the charge density. Hence, electrostatic forces of attraction b/w ions are stronger, and $\mathrm{\Delta}H_{\text{lat}}^{\theta}$ is more exo

* **Charge on ion**: The greater the charge, the greater the electrostatic attraction between ions. Hence, $\mathrm{\Delta}H_{\text{lat}}^{\theta}$ more exothermic
