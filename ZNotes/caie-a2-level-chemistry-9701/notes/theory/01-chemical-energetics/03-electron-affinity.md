## ­­­Electron Affinity (E.A.)

* **First Electron Affinity (**$\mathbf{\mathrm{\Delta}}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{\text{ea}}}^{\mathbf{\ominus}}$**):** Enthalpy change when 1 mole of electrons is added to 1 mole of gaseous atoms to form 1 mole of mononegative gaseous anions under standard conditions
  * e.g.: Cl (g) + e<sup>-</sup> $\rightarrow$ Cl<sup>-</sup> (g)
* 1<sup>st</sup> electron affinity is always exothermic, as uncharged atoms attract electrons (due to their nuclear attraction)
* 2<sup>nd</sup>, 3<sup>rd</sup>… electron affinities are endothermic because when an electron is added to –ve ion, repulsion is present and therefore energy is required to force the electron into the ion

### Trends affecting E.A.

* These trends only apply for Groups 16 and 17 (apart from the first elements of either group i.e. O and F)

* After the first element of the groups, electron affinity becomes less exothermic (less negative) down the group

* Factors affecting E.A.:

  * Nuclear charge (the greater the nuclear charge, the greater the E.A., so EA of chlorine is greater than that of sulfur)

  * Number of electron shells (down the groups, no. of shells increases so nuclear attraction decreases, hence E.A. decreases)

  * Number of shielding electrons (down the groups, no. of shielding electrons increases, so nuclear attraction decreases, hence E.A. decreases)
