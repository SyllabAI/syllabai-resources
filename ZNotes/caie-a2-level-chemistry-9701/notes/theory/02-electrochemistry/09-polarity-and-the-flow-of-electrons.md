## Polarity and the Flow of Electrons

### Determining polarity by using E$^{\theta}$ values

* To determine the which half-cell is the negative pole, and which one is the positive one, we must consider the E$^{\theta}$values for either cell 

* The half-cell with the more positive E$^{\theta}$ will be the positive pole 

* The half-cell with the less positive E$^{\theta}$ will be the negative pole

### Determining the direction of electron flow by using E$^{\theta}$ values

* Looking at the E$^{\theta}$ of two half cells, we know that:

  * the half-cell with the more positive E$^{\theta}$ will be easier to reduce and will, hence, gain electrons 

  * the half-cell with the less positive E$^{\theta}$ will be harder to reduce and will, hence, lose electrons

* Electrons move through the wires in the external circuit, not the electrolyte solution

* Hence, the electron flow will be from the half-cell with less positive E$^{\theta}$ to the half-cell with more positive E$^{\theta}$

* In other words, electrons flow from the negative pole to the positive pole (remember that +ve attracts -ve electrons)
