## Product of Electrolysis

**Molten Electrolyte:**

* **At the cathode:** Positive ions are reduced to atoms

Pb<sup>2+</sup><sub>(l)</sub> + 2e<sup>-</sup> $\rightarrow$ Pb<sub>(s)</sub>

* **At the anode:** Negative ions are oxidized to atoms/molecules

2NO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup><sub>(l)</sub> $\rightarrow$ NO<sub>2(g)</sub> +
O<sub>2(g)</sub> + 2e<sup>-</sup>

SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2-</sup><sub>(l)</sub> $\rightarrow$ SO<sub>2(g)</sub> +
O<sub>2(g)</sub> + 2e<sup>-</sup>

**Aqueous Electrolyte:**


||At the cathode|At the anode|
|:---|:---|:---|
|**By $E^\theta$**|If $E^\theta$ > 0,‎ then metal is less reactive than H, so metal is formed|If $E^\theta$ > 0.4‎, then non-metal is less reactive than OH, so non-metal is formed|
||If $E^\theta$ < 0,‎ then metal is more reactive than H, so $H\_{2(g)}$ formed|If $E^\theta$ < 0.4‎, then non-metal is more reactive than OH, so $H\_2O$ is formed|
|**By Conc.**|Following results from above, if the solution is very dilute, this occurs:||
||$2H^+{(aq)}+ 2e­^-$ → $H\_2$|$4OH^-{(aq)}$ → $2H\_2O + O + 4e^{-}$|
