## Variable Oxidation States

- There is a small energy difference between 4s and 3d so electrons from both
  subshells can be removed to form a variety of oxidation states.

- All transition metal exhibit two or more oxidation states

- Most common oxidation state is +2, when the 2e$^{-}$s from 4s lost

- Transition elements show highest oxidation states in either complex ions, or when they combine with O or F (most electronegative)

- When transition elements form compounds with high oxidation states
  (above +4), they form large oxyanions and are covalent (acidic oxides)
  e.g. CrO$_{4}^{2-}$ or MnO$_{4}^{-}$

- When transition elements are in lower oxidation states, they form ionic
  compounds (basic oxides)
