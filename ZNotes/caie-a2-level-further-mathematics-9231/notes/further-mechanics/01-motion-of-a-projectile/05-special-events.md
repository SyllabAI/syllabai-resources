## __Special Events__

* **Time at Maximum Vertical Height**: the time at which the projectile reaches its max height, at a given initial velocity and angle of elevation.
  * This occurs when vertical component of velocity is :

    $U sin\theta - gt = 0$

    $\therefore t = \frac{usine\theta}{g}$
* **Total Flight Time:** knowing the time taken to reach max vertical height, its total flight time is just twice of it. Hence:

  $t = \frac{2u sin\theta}{g}$
* **Horizontal Range**: the horizontal distance the projectile covers at a given initial velocity and angle of elevation.
  * Occurs when the vertical displacement is :

    $ut sin\theta - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 = 0$
  * Find  and substitute into the equation below:

    $x_{max} = utcos\theta$
  * It can also be written in terms of  &  only as:

    $x_{max} = \frac{u^2 sin2\theta}{g}$
* **Maximum Horizontal Range**: the maximum possible horizontal distance the projectile can cover at a given initial velocity.
  * Knowing $x_{max}$ = $\frac{u^2 sin2\theta}{g}$, the maximum distance can be achieved when $\theta = 45\degree$
* **Cartesian Equation of a Trajectory**: For any projectile, the trajectory can be modelled by the equation:

  $y = x \space tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2} sec^2 \theta$

o $\theta$ is the angle of projection.

o $u$ is the initial speed.

Therefore, when given a cartesian equation as:

$y = ax - bx^2$

Then,

$a = tan\theta$ $b = \frac{g}{2u^2}sec^2\theta$

**9231/32/M/J/20 - Question 1**

A particle  is projected with speed u at an angle of 30° above the horizontal from a point $O$ on a horizontal plane and moves freely under gravity. The particle reaches its greatest height at time  $T$ after projection.

Find, in terms of u, the speed of $P$ at time $\frac{2}{3}T$ after projection.

**Solution**

Get the projectile’s horizontal & vertical component:

$v_x = ucos30\degree$ $v_y = usin30\degree - gt$

Simplifying:

$v_x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}u$        $v_y = \frac{1}{2}u - gt$

For $P$ to be in max height, vertical compoment must be equal to 0:

$0 = \frac{1}{2}u - gT$

→ $T = \frac{u}{20}$

We used $g=10$. The above can be instantly found if you remember the formula for the time taken to reach max height.

We can then let $t = \frac{2}{3}T = \frac{u}{30}$ and put it into our components:

$v_x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}u$         $v_y = \frac{1}{2}u - g(\frac{u}{30})$

Simplifying $v_y$:

$v_y = \frac{1}{2}u - \frac{u}{3}$

$= \frac{u}{6}$

We can take the magnitude of these components to find the speed:

$v = \sqrt{(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}u)^2 + (\frac{u}{6})^2}$ \n $ = u \sqrt{{(\sqrt3}{2})^2 + (\frac{u}{6})^2}$

$= \frac{\sqrt{7}}{3}u$
