## Force on a Moving Charge

$$

F = BIl\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ I = \frac{q}{t}

$$

$$

F = Bq\left\( \frac{l}{t} \right\) \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and} \ \ \ \ \ \ v = \frac{l}{t}

$$

$$

\therefore F = Bqv

$$

* $F$: Magnetic force on the moving charge (Newtons, $\text{N}$)

* $B$: Magnetic flux density / magnetic field strength (Tesla, $\text{T}$)

* $q$: Magnitude of the electric charge (Coulombs, $\text{C}$)

* $v$: Velocity of the particle perpendicular to the magnetic field ($\text{m s}^{-1}$)


- If particle moving at an angle $\theta$ to the magnetic field, the
  component of velocity $\bot$ to magnetic field is $v\sin\theta$

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/9d81df458ccfa0fd073213eb.webp =429x)

* For a positive charge, we can again use Fleming’s left hand rule to find the direction of the force

* If the charge was negative, the force would be in the opposite direction

**Question:**

An electron ($q = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$) enters a uniform magnetic field of $0.35\text{ T}$ at right angles with a speed of $4.0 \times 10^6\text{ m s}^{-1}$.

1. Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic force acting on the electron.

**Solution:**

1. **Force:**

   $$F = Bqv = (0.35)(1.60 \times 10^{-19})(4.0 \times 10^6) = 2.24 \times 10^{-13}\text{ N}$$
