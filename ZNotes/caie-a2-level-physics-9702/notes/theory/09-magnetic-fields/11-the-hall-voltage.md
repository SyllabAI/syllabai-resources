## The Hall Voltage

A constant Hall voltage is developed between the faces because:

1. charge carriers are moving normal to the magnetic field

2. charge carriers experience a force normal to the magnetic field

3. charge build up sets up electric field across the slice

4. charge stops building up when magnitude of magnetic force equal magnitude of electric force

**Deriving Hall voltage:**

- An electric field is set up in the probe as there is a difference in
  voltage between a distance $d$ so

$$E = \frac{V}{d}$$

- As a single electron travels with drift velocity $v$, it
  experiences a force to the left due to the magnetic field
  $\text{Bqv}$ and a force to the right due to the electric field
  $\text{Eq}$

- Soon an equilibrium is reached hence forces equated

$$

Eq = Bqv

$$

- Substitute for $E$

$$

\frac{\text{qV}_{H}}{d} = Bqv

$$

- Current is related to mean drift velocity by

$$

I = nAvq

$$

where $A = td$ is cross sectional area and $n$ is number density of
conducting particles

- Substitute for $v$ and rearrange

$$

V_{H} = \frac{\text{BI}}{\text{ntq}}

$$

**Question:**

Mr Jenning is demonstrating the Hall Effect. He uses a semiconductor slice of thickness $0.40\text{ mm}$ which carries a current of $25\text{ mA}$. When placed perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $B$, a Hall voltage of $12\text{ mV}$ is measured across its faces. The number density of charge carriers is $n = 1.2 \times 10^{23}\text{ m}^{-3}$. Calculate the magnetic flux density $B$.

**Solution:**

Using the Hall voltage formula $V_H = \frac{BI}{ntq}$:

$$B = \frac{V_H \cdot n \cdot t \cdot q}{I}$$

Convert units: $t = 0.40 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$, $I = 25 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}$, $V_H = 12 \times 10^{-3}\text{ V}$.

$$B = \frac{(12 \times 10^{-3}) \cdot (1.2 \times 10^{23}) \cdot (0.40 \times 10^{-3}) \cdot (1.60 \times 10^{-19})}{25 \times 10^{-3}} = 3.69\text{ T}$$
