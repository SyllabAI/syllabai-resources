## Gravitational Potential

**The gravitational potential energy** of a unit mass $m$ at a point in the gravitational field of another mass $M$, is the work done in bringing that unit mass $m$ from infinity to that point; where $M$ could be a larger mass like a planet and $m$ could be a smaller mass like a space rock

$$
U = \text{mϕ} = - \frac{\text{GM}}{r}m
$$

The **gravitational potential energy difference** between two points is the work done in moving a mass from one point to another

$$
\mathrm{\Delta}U = m\phi_{\text{final}} - m\phi_{\text{initial}}
$$

Think of Gravitational Potential as simply the Gravitational Potential Energy per single unit of mass

The **gravitational potential** at a point is work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to that point

$$
\phi = - \frac{\text{GM}}{r}
$$

The negative sign is because:

* Gravitational force is always attractive

* Gravitational potential is zero at infinity

* So work is done on the test mass and getting closer loses potential energy

On Earth’s surface, we can use the equation G.P.E $= mgh$ because height change is small relative to the radius of Earth; we can’t use that equation for large displacements from the Earth’s surface
