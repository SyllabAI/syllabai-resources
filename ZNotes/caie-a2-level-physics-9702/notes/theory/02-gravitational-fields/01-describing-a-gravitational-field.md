# Gravitational Fields

## **Describing a Gravitational Field:**

* A gravitational field is defined as a region of space where a mass experiences a gravitational force

* A gravitational field line shows the direction and strength of force on a small test mass

* A higher density of field lines == a region of stronger field

* As gravitational force is strictly an attractive force, the direction of field line will always point inwards towards the centre of mass

* Note that field lines never cross each other

### **Radial Fields:**

* For an isolated point mass, the gravitational field is radial in shape, with the point mass at the centre

* A medium with consistent density throughout is considered to be an isolated point mass

* This field is called a “Non-uniform” field as the closer you are to the mass, the stronger the field is

* When dealing with the Earth or sun, this is considered the field of lines on it:

**Uniform Fields**

* This is a uniform field as the density of the field lines is constant, so field strength is same at all points

* A uniform field is represented by equally spaced parallel lines

* At the surface of the Earth, field lines are parallel so constant field strength so constant acceleration of free fall value of 9.81

* While Earth's field is technically radial, its radius is so massive that the curvature is negligible on a human scale

* Because we are zoomed in so closely on a tiny section of the surface, the field lines do not noticeably converge and can be mathematically treated as perfectly parallel

Note: A gravitational field is the region of space itself, whereas gravitational field strength is the measurable force per unit mass within that region
