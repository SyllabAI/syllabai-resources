## Standard Candle

**Definition:** An astronomical object of known luminosity due to a characteristic property inherent to that class of object.

**Common Examples:**

* **Cepheid Variables:** Pulsating stars whose period of variability directly relates to their luminosity.

  **Type 1a Supernovae:** Stellar explosions of white dwarfs that consistently reach the same peak luminosity.

**Determining Galactic Distances:**

* **Method:**

  1. Identify a standard candle within a target galaxy.

  2. Measure its radiant flux intensity ($F$) on Earth.

  3. Use its known luminosity ($L$) alongside $F$ to calculate distance ($d$) by rearranging the inverse square law:

     $$d = \sqrt{\frac{L}{4\pi F}}$$

* **Cosmic Distance Ladder:** Standard candles allow indirect distance measurements to far-off galaxies where direct methods (like trigonometric parallax) cannot reach.
