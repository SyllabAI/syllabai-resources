## Stefan-Boltzmann Law

* **Defintion:** The total power emitted per unit surface area of a black-body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its thermodynamic temperature.

* **Formula:**

  $$L = 4\pi r^2 \sigma T^4$$

  * $L = \text{Luminosity of the star } (\text{W})$

  * $r = \text{Radius of the star } (\text{m})$

  * $\sigma = \text{Stefan–Boltzmann constant } (5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W m}^{-2} \text{K}^{-4})$

  * $T = \text{Surface temperature of the star } (\text{K})$

* **Surface Area:** Assumes the star is a sphere where $\text{Area} = 4\pi r^2$.

**Estimating Stellar Radii:**

**Method:** Combine both laws when the radius of a star cannot be measured directly.

1. Measure peak wavelength ($\lambda_{\text{max}}$) from the star's spectrum and calculate surface temperature ($T$) using Wien’s Law:

   $$T = \frac{2.9 \times 10^{-3}}{\lambda_{\text{max}}}$$

2. Measure radiant flux intensity ($F$) on Earth and determine luminosity ($L$) using $L = 4\pi d^2 F$.

3. Rearrange the Stefan–Boltzmann equation to solve for radius ($r$):

   $$r = \sqrt{\frac{L}{4\pi \sigma T^4}}$$

*Note 2.9x10\^-3 is the Wien’s Constant*
