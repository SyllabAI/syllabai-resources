## Energy of a Photon

### The Photon

* **Definition:** A **discrete packet** or **quantum** of electromagnetic energy.

* Photons are the fundamental, **massless** particles making up all forms of EM radiation.

* **Energy transfer:** Energy is not supplied continuously but is transferred discretely, entirely **all in one go** by each individual photon.

### Calculating Photon Energy

* Photon energy ($E$) is directly proportional to its frequency ($f$):

  $$E = hf$$

  * *Where $h$ = Planck's constant ($6.63 \times 10^{-34}\text{ Js}$)*.

* Combining this with the wave equation ($c = f\lambda$) relates energy inversely to wavelength ($\lambda$):

  $$E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

  * *Where $c$ = speed of light ($3.0 \times 10^8\text{ ms}^{-1}$)*.

* **Key takeaway:** High frequency (short wavelength) radiation corresponds to higher-energy photons.

### The Electronvolt (eV)

* **Definition:** The energy gained by an electron traveling through a potential difference of exactly one volt.

* Used because quantum energies are far too small to be conveniently expressed in Joules ($J$).

* **Conversion:**

  $$\mathbf{1\text{ eV} = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}}$$

  * $\text{eV} \rightarrow \text{J}$: Multiply by $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$.

  * $\text{J} \rightarrow \text{eV}$: Divide by $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$.

### Relation to Kinetic Energy

* When a charged particle accelerates from rest through a potential difference ($V$), its electrical potential energy converted is equal to the kinetic energy gained:

  $$eV = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

* Rearranging for the specific final velocity ($v$) of the particle:

  $$v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}}$$
