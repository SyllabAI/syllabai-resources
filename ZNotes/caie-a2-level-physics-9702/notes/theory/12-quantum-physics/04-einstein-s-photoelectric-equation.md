## Einstein’s Photoelectric Equation

* **The Photoelectric Effect:** The process where electrons (called photoelectrons) are released from a metal's surface by absorbing electromagnetic radiation. It proves light is quantized because one electron can only absorb a single photon.

* **Threshold Frequency ($f_0$):** The minimum frequency of incident radiation required to liberate a photoelectron from a metal's surface.

* **Threshold Wavelength ($\lambda_0$):** The longest wavelength of incident radiation that can remove a photoelectron. Both threshold frequency and wavelength are unique, constant properties of the specific metal material.

* **Work Function ($\Phi$):** The minimum energy required to release a photoelectron from the surface of a material, measured in Joules ($J$).

* **The Photoelectric Equation:** Expresses the conservation of energy where incident photon energy equals the work function plus the maximum kinetic energy of the electron:

  $$

  E = hf = \Phi + \frac{1}{2}mv^2

  $$

* **Graphical Representation:** A graph of $E_{kmax}$ vs. frequency ($f$) yields a straight line where the gradient is Planck's constant ($h$), the x-intercept is the threshold frequency ($f_0$), and the y-intercept represents the work function ($\Phi$).

* **Kinetic Energy vs. Intensity:** The maximum kinetic energy ($E_{kmax}$) of photoelectrons depends strictly on the frequency of the radiation, not its intensity. Electrons deeper within the metal require extra energy to reach the surface, resulting in lower kinetic energies than surface electrons which achieve $E_{kmax}$.
