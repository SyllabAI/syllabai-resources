### **Laws of Photoelectric Emission:**

* $\textbf{1}^{st}$ **Law**: The number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the intensity of incident radiation

* $\textbf{2}^{nd}$ **Law:** max kinetic energy of photoelectrons is
  directly proportional to the frequency of the incident radiation but
  independent of its intensity

* $\textbf{3}^{rd}$ **Law:** for every metal, there is a minimum
  frequency of incident radiation below which photoelectric emission
  does not take place; threshold frequency

### **Understanding Intensity in Quantum Physics:**

* **Intensity Definition:** Intensity is the total energy arriving per unit area per second ($I = \frac{P}{A}$). For monochromatic light, this corresponds directly to the **number of photons arriving per second**.

* **Rate of Photoelectron Emission:**

  * Increasing intensity increases the **number of photons** per second $\implies$ increases the **rate of photoelectron emission** (and thus the photoelectric current), provided $f > f_0$.

  * $\text{Intensity} \propto \text{Rate of Photoelectron Emission}$

* **Max Kinetic Energy ($E_{k\text{max}}$):**

  * Intensity has **no effect** on the maximum kinetic energy or speed of individual emitted electrons.

  * $E_{k\text{max}}$ depends **only on the frequency ($f$)** of the incident photon ($E_{k\text{max}} = hf - \Phi$).
