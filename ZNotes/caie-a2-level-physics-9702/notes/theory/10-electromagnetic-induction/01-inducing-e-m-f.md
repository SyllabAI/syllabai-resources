# Electromagnetic Induction

## Inducing e.m.f

* **Definition:** Magnetic flux ($\Phi$) is defined as the product of the magnetic flux density normal to the cross-sectional area of a circuit.

* **SI Unit:** Weber ($\text{Wb}$), where $1\text{ Wb} = 1\text{ T}\cdot\text{m}^2$.

* **Equation:**

  $$

  \Phi = B A \cos\theta

  $$

* **Variables:**

  * $B$: Magnetic flux density of the field.

  * $A$: Cross-sectional area of the circuit.

  * $\theta$: Angle between the magnetic field lines ($B$) and the normal to the cross-sectional area.

    ![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/56d64190c363e6b42b9d1a11.webp =658x)

* **Perpendicular Field ($\theta = 0^\circ$):** When magnetic flux density is perpendicular to the surface area, $\cos(0^\circ) = 1$, simplifying the equation to $\Phi = BA$.

* **Angled Field ($0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$):** Only the normal component of the magnetic field contributes to the flux, calculated via $\Phi = BA \cos\theta$.

* **Parallel Field ($\theta = 90^\circ$):** When magnetic field lines run parallel to the plane of the area, $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$, resulting in zero flux ($\Phi = 0$).
