## Charged Panda (Sample Question):

A giant panda of mass **110 kg** slides down a plastic slide inside a dry zoo enclosure, accumulating a uniform net positive static charge of **\+3.2 μC** through friction. A spherical, copper bamboo feeding bowl carrying a net negative charge of **-1.5 μC** is placed such that its centre is exactly **0.45 m** away from the centre of the panda. Modeling both the panda and the bowl as point charges in a vacuum and ignoring any resistive forces, calculate the magnitude and direction of the initial acceleration of the panda.

Solution:

**Step 1: Calculate the electrostatic force using Coulomb's Law**

* Recall and substitutes values into Coulomb's law (converting μC to C):
  $F=\frac{Q_{1}Q_{2}}{4\pi \varepsilon _{0}r^{2}}=\frac{(3.2\times 10^{-6}\text{\ C})\times (1.5\times 10^{-6}\text{\ C})}{4\pi \times (8.85\times 10^{-12}\text{\ F\ m}^)\times (0.45\text{\ m})^{2}}$

* Evaluates the force:
  $F\approx 0.213\text{\ N}$

**Step 2: Calculate the magnitude of the acceleration**

* Recall and links Newton's second law $F = ma$ or $a = \frac{F}{m}$:
  $\\(a=\frac{0.213\text{\ N}}{110\text{\ kg}})$

* Correct numerical answer to 2 or 3 significant figures: $1.9 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2}$ or $1.94 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-2}$.

**Step 3: Determine the direction**

* The direction of acceleration is **towards the copper bowl** because opposite charges attract, pulling the panda forward.
