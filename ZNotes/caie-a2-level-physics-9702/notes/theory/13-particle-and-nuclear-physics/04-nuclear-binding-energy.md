## **Nuclear Binding Energy ($E_b$)**

* **Definition:** The minimum energy required to completely separate all nucleons in a nucleus to infinity (or the energy released when a nucleus is formed from its individual nucleons).

* **Link to Mass Defect:**

  $$E_b = \Delta m \cdot c^2$$

* **Atomic Mass Units Conversion:**

  * $1\text{ u} = 1.66 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg} \approx 931.5\text{ MeV}$

  * If $\Delta m$ is in $\text{u}$: $E_b = \Delta m \times 931.5\text{ MeV}$

* **Binding energy per nucleon definition:** The total binding energy ($E_b$) of a nucleus divided by its total number of nucleons ($A$).

* **Formula:**

  $$\text{Binding energy per nucleon} = \frac{E_b}{A}$$

  * $E_b$ = total binding energy ($\text{J}$ or $\text{MeV}$)

  * $A$ = nucleon number / mass number

* **Significance:** A direct measure of **nuclear stability**.

  * Higher binding energy per nucleon $\rightarrow$ greater stability $\rightarrow$ more energy required to remove a nucleon.

    ![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/85655df68f8dd2532854012e.webp =698x)

  **Features of the graph:**

  * **Peak Stability (Iron-56):**

    * The curve reaches a maximum peak at $A \approx 56$ (Nickel-62 / Iron-56) with a value of approximately **$8.8\text{ MeV per nucleon}$**.

    * Nuclei near this peak are the **most stable**.

  * **Nuclear Fusion Region ($A < 56$):**

    * Light nuclei ($A < 56$) have relatively low binding energy per nucleon.

    * When two light nuclei fuse together to form a heavier nucleus, the product has a **higher binding energy per nucleon** than the reactants.

    * This increase in stability releases large amounts of energy.

  * **Nuclear Fission Region ($A > 56$):**

    * Massive nuclei ($A > 56$) have a lower binding energy per nucleon due to electrostatic repulsion between many protons.

    * When a heavy nucleus splits into smaller fragment nuclei, the daughter products have a **higher binding energy per nucleon** than the original parent nucleus.

    * This increase in stability releases energy.
