## **Centripetal Acceleration:**

**Definition**: acceleration perpendicular to velocity

This is because:

* For an object moving at a constant angular velocity, the speed around the circular path is constant.

* However, since the direction of motion is always changing, the velocity of the object is also constantly changing.

* This means the object is accelerating, even if speed is constant.

* In order for acceleration to not change the magnitude of velocity, the centripetal acceleration must be directed towards the center of rotation, perpendicular to velocity

You can calculate centripetal acceleration using linear velocity ($$v$$) or angular velocity ($$\omega$$):

$$

a = r\omega^2 = \frac{v^2}{r}

$$
