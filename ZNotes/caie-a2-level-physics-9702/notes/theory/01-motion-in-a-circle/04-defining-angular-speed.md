## **Defining Angular Speed:**

The angular velocity ($\omega$) is the rate of change of angular displacement. It is defined as:

$$

\omega = \frac{\Delta \theta}{\Delta t}

$$

The SI unit for angular velocity is radians per second ($\text{rad s}^{-1}$).

For an object moving in a circle, the angular velocity can also be expressed in terms of the period ($$T$$), which is the time taken for one complete revolution (an angular change of $2\pi$ radians), or in terms of frequency ($$f$$):

$$

\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f

$$

where:

* $$T$$ is the period ($s$)

* $$f$$ is the frequency ($\text{Hz}$)

* $f = \frac{1}{T}$

**Note on Terminology:** In circular motion, **angular speed** (scalar) and **angular velocity** (vector magnitude) are often used interchangeably when dealing with magnitude.
