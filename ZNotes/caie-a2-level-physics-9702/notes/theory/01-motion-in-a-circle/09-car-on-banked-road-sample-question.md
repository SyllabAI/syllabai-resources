## **Car on Banked Road (Sample Question):**

Eric is driving his car around a frictionless circular curve of radius \\(r = 150 \\text\{ m\}\\). If the curve is banked at an angle of \\(\\theta = 15.0\^\\circ\\) to the horizontal, at what speed (\\(v\\)) should the car travel so that it does not slide up or down the bank?

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/d09730517292cee8dba810ec.webp =300x300)

**Solution:**

Gravity (\\(mg\\)) acting straight down and Normal force (\\(N\\)) acting perpendicular to the surface.

1. The road is banked at an angle \\(\\theta \\) to the horizontal. We break \\(N\\) into components:

   * Vertical component: $$N \cos\theta\\$$

   * Horizontal component: $$N \sin\theta\\$$

2. The vertical forces must balance:

$$

N\cos \theta =mg\

$$

3. The horizontal component points toward the center of the turn. This component provides the net centripetal force:

$$

N\sin \theta =\frac{mv^{2}}{r}\

$$

4. Divide the horizontal equation by the vertical equation to eliminate the unknown normal force (\\(N\\)):

$$

\frac{\cancel{N} \sin \theta}{\cancel{N} \cos \theta} = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} = \tan \theta

$$

5. Simplify both sides by cancelling out \\(N\\) and the mass (\\(m\\)):

$$

\tan \theta =\frac{v^{2}}{rg}\

$$

6. Take the square root to get the final ideal speed formula:

$$

v=\sqrt{rg\tan \theta }\

$$

Calculate:

* $$v = \sqrt{150 \times 9.8 \times \tan(15.0^\circ)}\\$$

* $$v = \sqrt{150 \times 9.8 \times 0.2679}\\$$

* $$v = \sqrt{393.88} \approx 19.85 \text{ m/s}\\$$

Thus, the velocity the car should travel to not slide up or down the bank is 19.9 m/s to 3 significant figures.
