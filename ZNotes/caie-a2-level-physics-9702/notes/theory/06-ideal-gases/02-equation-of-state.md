## Equation of State

**Ideal gas:** a gas that obeys the equation of state $pV \propto T$ at all pressures, volumes, and temperatures, where $T$ is the thermodynamic (absolute) temperature

Conditions for the equation to be valid:

* fixed amount of gas
* ideal gas
* **Boyle’s Law:** $P \propto \frac{1}{V}$ hence $pV =$ constant
* **Charles’s Law:** $V \propto T$ hence $\frac{V}{T} =$ constant
* **Pressure Law:** $P \propto T$ hence $\frac {P}{T} =$ constant

$$
\therefore\frac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{T_2}
$$

### **Second Form Of Equation:**

If we need to use the equation with the number of particles, and not the number of moles, we can use two ways.

1. Calculate the number of moles using the following formula and then inserting it into the main formula

$$
Moles = \frac{mass}{molar mass}
$$

2. Use the second formula derived from the first one and directly put the values:

$$
\text{pV} = \text{NkT}
$$

Where:

- N is the number of particles (not moles)
- k is the Boltzmann Constant ($\mathrm{k  = \frac{R}{N_A} = 1.38 \times 10^{-23} JK^{-1}}$)

***Key Note: The values of both K and R are given in the exam paper***

### Derivation of $pV = NkT$

* Start with $pV = nRT$

* Substitute the number of moles: $n = \frac{N}{N_A} \implies pV = \left\(\frac{N}{N_A}\right\)RT$

* Substitute Boltzmann’s constant ($k = \frac{R}{N_A} \implies R = k N_A$):

  $$pV = \left\(\frac{N}{N_A}\right\)(k N_A) T \implies pV = NkT$$
