## Hydrogen Handball (Sample Question):

Kevin has a rigid handball of fixed volume $V = 3.20 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}^3$ which holds an ideal gas at an initial temperature of $27.0^\circ\text{C}$ ($300\text{ K}$) and a pressure of $1.50 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$. However in a play session, the handball accidentally rolled onto the bathroom floor. Kevin sanitises the handball by putting it in hot water. The gas in the handball is heated until its pressure increases to $2.25 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$. Calculate the final average translational kinetic energy of a single gas molecule at this new state.

Solution:

**Step 1: Calculate the final temperature ($T_2$)**

* Since volume $V$ is constant ($pV \propto T$):

  $$\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2} \implies T_2 = T_1 \times \frac{P_2}{P_1}$$

* Working:

  $$T_2 = 300\text{ K} \times \frac{2.25 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}}{1.50 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}} = 450\text{ K}$$

**Step 2: Calculate average kinetic energy ($E_k$) at $T_2$**

* Formula: $E_k = \frac{3}{2} k T_2$

* Working:

  $$E_k = \frac{3}{2} \times (1.38 \times 10^{-23}\text{ J K}^{-1}) \times 450\text{ K}$$

* Answer: $E_k = 9.32 \times 10^{-21}\text{ J}$
