## First Law of Thermodynamics

* **The first law of thermodynamics:** the increase in internal energy of a system is equal to the sum of work done on the system and thermal energy supplied to the system

$$
\mathrm{\Delta}U = q + w
$$

* $\mathrm{\Delta}U$: increase in internal energy of the system
* $q$: heat supplied to the system
* $w$: **work done on the system**

**Work Done by the system at constant pressure formula:**

$$
\mathrm w = P{\Delta}V
$$

* $P$ is the pressure of the system, and $\Delta V$ is the change in volume of the gas

* *Note: Work done on the system = negative of work done by the system*

**Example: Pumping up a Bicycle Tire**

When using a manual hand pump to inflate a tire:

* **Work done on the gas ($w > 0$):** You push down on the piston, compressing the trapped air rapidly. Mechanical work is done *on* the gas.

* **Thermal energy transfer ($q \approx 0$ initially):** Because the compression happens quickly, there is little time for heat to transfer to the environment.

* **Result ($\Delta U > 0$):** The mechanical work transfers energy directly into the internal energy of the air. Since internal energy in a gas directly relates to molecular kinetic energy, the temperature of the air rises—which is why the bottom of a bicycle pump barrel feels noticeably warm during rapid pumping

### **Sign Conventions for $q$ and $w$ ($\Delta U = q + w$):**

* **$+q$**: Heat supplied **to** the system (enters system).

* **$-q$**: Heat lost **from** the system to surroundings.

* **$+w$**: Work done **on** the system (compression, volume decreases).

* **$-w$**: Work done **by** the system (expansion, volume increases).

### **Constant Volume Process**

* **No Work Done:** At constant volume ($\Delta V = 0$), $w = 0$.

* Increasing pressure or temperature at constant volume does no work; therefore, the change in internal energy equals heat supplied ($\Delta U = q$).
