Opportunities & Constraints of Natural and Built Environment

|

Natural features (beaches and mountains) and built attractions (hotels and transport) attract tourists and boost income. However, development may be limited due to environmental protection or competing land use.

|

Maldives attracts tourists with beaches but limits construction to protect reefs

||
||

Sustainability

|

Using practices like reduce, reuse, and recycle helps protect the environment and reduces costs (e.g., less need for imports of energy/resources).

|

Eco-hotels using solar power and recycling systems

||
||

Risk and Resilience

|

Natural disasters or extreme weather can limit tourism. However, strong recovery systems and government support help destinations bounce back.

|

Japan continues tourism despite earthquakes (risk) due to strong disaster management (resilience)

||
||

Carrying Capacity

|

The maximum number of tourists a place can handle without damaging the environment or reducing visitor satisfaction.

|

A timed entry system is implemented to protect the city delicate architecture

||
||

Overtourism

|

When too many tourists visit a destination, causing overcrowding, environmental damage, and poor experiences.

|

Venice facing crowding and restrictions on visitors

||
||

Seasonality

|

Tourist numbers vary by season (peak and off-peak), making it difficult to manage income, staffing, and resources year-round.

|

Ski resorts busy in winter but quiet in summer

||
||

Government objectives

|

* Economic: increase income and create jobs

* Political: improve global image and reputation

* Environmental: protect and conserve the environment

* Sociocultural: preserve culture and improve tourist–local relationships

|

Promoting tourism for jobs, protecting heritage sites, and hosting global events like the Olympics
