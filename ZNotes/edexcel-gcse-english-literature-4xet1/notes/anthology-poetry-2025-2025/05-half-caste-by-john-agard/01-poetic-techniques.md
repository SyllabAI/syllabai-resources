# "Half-Caste" by John Agard

## Poetic Techniques

* Colloquial language and phonetic spelling ("wha yu mean") create an authentic, conversational tone

* Direct address challenges the reader's assumptions and prejudices

* Repetition of “explain yuself” emphasizes frustration and demand for recognition

* Irony and sarcasm are used to ridicule the absurdity of the term "half-caste"

* Metaphor: compares mixed identity to “half a symphony,” “half a canvas” — showing the absurdity of dividing art or people

## **Themes**

* Identity and racial heritage

* Challenging stereotypes and labels

* Cultural pride and defiance

* Social inequality and prejudice

## **Context**

* Agard is of Guyanese and British heritage; he experienced racism after moving to the UK

* The poem critiques how society labels people of mixed race

* Written to confront readers with their unconscious bias and force reflection

## **Structure & Form**

* **Free verse**: no regular rhythm or rhyme reflects non-conformity and independence

* **Irregular stanza length** and **enjambment** mimic natural speech

* The poem builds in intensity until it ends with a defiant challenge to the reader

* No punctuation — shows refusal to follow "rules" of standard English, symbolic of breaking societal norms

## **Language Note**

* The poem uses phonetic spellings and Caribbean Creole dialect (e.g., “wha yu mean”, “de whole of yu”) instead of Standard English

* This is intentional and serves several important purposes:

  * Reflects Agard’s **c**ultural and linguistic identity

  * Challenges the idea that **Standard English = superior or correct**

  * Makes the reader slow down and reflect on how language and identity are judged

  * Emphasises the poem’s message: labels and standards are limiting and unfair

* The “unusual” spellings are not mistakes — they are part of the poem’s authentic voice and resistance to linguistic conformity
