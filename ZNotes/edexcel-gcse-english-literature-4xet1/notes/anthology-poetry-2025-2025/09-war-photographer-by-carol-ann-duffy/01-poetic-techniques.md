# "War Photographer" by Carol Ann Duffy

## Poetic Techniques

* Juxtaposition: contrasts the chaos of war with the calm of rural England

* Alliteration: “spools of suffering” evokes both sound and emotion

* Imagery: vivid and disturbing war zone descriptions create empathy and discomfort

* Symbolism: The darkroom represents emotional and moral isolation

* Ambiguity: moral conflict is left unresolved, provoking the reader to reflect

## Themes

* War and its emotional aftermath

* Detachment vs responsibility

* Moral conflict and guilt

* Media portrayal of suffering

## Context

* Carol Ann Duffy is a former UK Poet Laureate known for dramatic monologues

* Inspired by real war photographers (e.g., Don McCullin, Philip Jones Griffiths)

* Reflects on how the Western world consumes images of suffering from a distance

## Structure & Form

* Four regular stanzas of six lines each reflect an attempt to impose order on chaos

* Consistent rhyme scheme (ABBCDD): contrasts with the emotional chaos described

* Enjambment mirrors the flow of memory and the photographer's emotional turmoil

* Shifts between the photographer’s thoughts and his experiences in war zones
