# **"If—" by Rudyard Kipling**

## **Poetic Techniques**

*  Extended conditional sentences ("If...") create rhythm and cumulative moral build-up

*  Personification: Triumph and Disaster are treated as "impostors"

*  Repetition of "If" structures each stanza logically and emphatically

*  Didactic tone: a father teaching life values

*  The second-person narrative voice gives direct advice to the reader

## 
**Themes**

* Stoicism and resilience

* Manhood and maturity

* Moral integrity and composure

* Time and self-mastery

## 
 **Context**

• Written during the British imperial era, reflecting Victorian ideals of self-control and strength

• Kipling had complex views on empire, duty, and personal responsibility

• Inspired partly by the fall of British officer Leander Starr Jameson

## 
**Structure & Form**

* 4 stanzas of 8 lines each

* Consistent rhyme scheme: ABABCDCD

* Iambic pentameter throughout (echoes formal speech/advice)

* The final couplet acts as a climax and a summary of the poem’s message
