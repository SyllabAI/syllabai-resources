## Question 2(d): Writer’s Effect

### **Overview**

This sub-question tests your ability to explain how the writer uses language to convey meaning, create effects, and provoke thought for the reader. You are asked in the question to select **three examples of words or phrases from each paragraph**, including imagery, to support and elaborate on your analysis.

### **Step-by-Step Answering Method**

* Read the question thoroughly. Take note of the command words given (i.e., explain, analyse), the paragraphs you are asked to re-read, and the focus of the question.

* Highlight three precise words or short phrases per target paragraph; focus on what you can analyse fully. Include a range of techniques and imagery such as visual, auditory, and take note of the literary devices in use. **Do not** pick phrases you can’t elaborate fully on.

  * *Explicit meaning* = the direct, surface meaning of the words

  * *Implicit meaning* = what the words suggest, hint at, or imply beyond the surface

* Ask 3 key questions for each words/phrase: What has the writer written? How have they written it (techniques, word choice, imagery)? What thought is it provoking the way the text is written (effect/impact on reader)? Elaborate on these points throughout your first paragraph that consists of the 3 words/phrases.

* End each paragraph with a concluding ‘overall statement’.

* **Tip**: Copy quotes directly into your sentences and explain the choice of each word/phrase in context.

* Follow and repeat this pattern per word/phrase: literal meaning → connotations/implicit meaning → elaborate on characterisation, tone, formality and impact → conclusion.

* Repeat the same for the second paragraph, try not to exceed the word count.

### **Worked Example**

The example below showcases a concise way to structure your ideas when answering a writer’s effect question:


**Paragraph given**: The corridor stretched endlessly before her, its narrow walls swallowing the faint light. Her footsteps echoed through the empty passageway as she crept forward. Shadows twisted along the walls, and a wall of silence seemed to surround her. She hesitated, listening for any sign of movement, but nothing answered.

***Overview:*** *In paragraph 1, the writer conveys a sense of isolation and foreboding.*

***Word 1:*** *‘Echoing’: literally repeating sound, connotes emptiness and loneliness, creating a feeling that the character is alone and vulnerable.*

***Word 2:*** *‘Twisting’: suggests unnatural, contorted movement, evoking unease and tension.*

***Phrase:*** *‘A wall of silence’: pin-drop silence, connotes suffocation and emotional barrier, giving the impression that communication is impossible, which intensifies the sense of dread.*

***Effect:*** *These language choices collectively create an atmosphere of isolation and tension, making the reader feel on edge and fully aware of the character’s precarious situation.*
