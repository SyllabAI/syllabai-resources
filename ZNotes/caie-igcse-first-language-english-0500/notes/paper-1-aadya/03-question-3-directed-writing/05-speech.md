## Speech

### **Format & Structure**

* **Introduction:**

  * Begin with a **salutation**: “*Good afternoon everyone*” / “*Hello everyone*”

  * Introduce yourself and explain why you are giving the speech/talk

  * Include a **quotation, rhetorical question, or anecdote** to capture attention

  * State your **purpose**

  **Body (Paragraphs 2–4):**

  * Develop ideas fully using examples, explanations, or anecdotes

  * Use **strong vocabulary and emotive language**

  * For speeches, aim to **persuade or convince**; for talks, **inform clearly**

  * In the **final bullet**, include opinions, predictions, or reflections based on the ending of the passage

* **Conclusion (Paragraph 5):**

  * Summarise main points briefly

  * End with a **closing line**, such as *“I appreciate your attention and hope these ideas stay with you.” / “Thank you for your time and attention.“* (adapt phrasing naturally)

### **Language & Style**

* **Rhetorical devices:**

  * **Rhetorical questions** to engage the audience

  * **Anecdotes** for relatability

  * **Imperatives** to encourage action (speeches only)

  * **Collective pronouns** like *we, us, our*

  * **Repetition** of main points for emphasis

* **Tone:**

  * **Speech:** conversational, emotive, engaging, persuasive or informative

  * **Talk:** conversational, factual, concise, informative only

* **Audience awareness:** Always imagine speaking to a live audience; language should sound **natural and convincing** for speeches, or **precise and informative** for talks

* Make an informed decision as to whether your piece is **persuasive** (speech) or **informative** (talk), so you can adapt your writing style to it.
