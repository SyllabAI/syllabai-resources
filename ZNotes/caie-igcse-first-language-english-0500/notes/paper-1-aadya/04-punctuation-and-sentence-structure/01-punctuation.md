# Punctuation & Sentence Structure

## Punctuation

This table shows the key punctuation marks you need to know, what they do, and how to use them. Knowing these helps make your writing clearer, more precise, and easier to read.

#|
||

**Type**

|

**Usage**

|

**Example**

||
||

Em Dash (—)

|

Adds emphasis, interrupts, or inserts extra information

|

"The garden—a wild, overgrown mess—seemed like a forgotten paradise."

||
||

Semicolon (;)

|

Connects closely related independent clauses; separates complex list items

|

"She loved the forest; it was her escape from the city's chaos."

||
||

Colon (:)

|

Introduces lists, explanations, or elaborations

|

"Three things defined her childhood: the garden, the oak tree, and summer days."

||
||

Ellipsis (…)

|

Indicates trailing off or unfinished thought

|

"As the sun set, the sky turned orange… it was perfect."

||
||

Quotation Marks (" ")

|

Encloses direct speech or quotations

|

"He said, 'The stars are brighter here.'"

||
||

Parentheses ( )

|

Adds extra or clarifying information

|

"The book was set in the 19th century (a period she loved)."

||
||

Exclamation (!)

|

Shows strong emotion or emphasis

|

"The discovery was astonishing!"
