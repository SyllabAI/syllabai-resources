## Sentence Structure

This table covers different types of sentences and how they’re built. Using a mix of sentence structures can make your writing more interesting, expressive, and polished.

#|
||

**Type**

|

**Definition / Function**

|

**Example**

||
||

Simple Sentence

|

Contains one independent clause

|

"The sun set behind the mountains."

||
||

Compound Sentence

|

Two independent clauses joined by conjunction or semicolon

|

"She wanted to leave, but the meeting ran long."

||
||

Complex Sentence

|

One independent clause $+$ one or more dependent clauses

|

"Although the town was small, it had unmatched charm."

||
||

Compound-Complex

|

Multiple independent clauses $+$ at least one dependent clause

|

"She wanted to start, but since the resources were delayed, she had to wait."

||
||

Declarative

|

Makes a statement or expresses fact

|

"The mansion had been abandoned."

||
||

Interrogative

|

Asks a question

|

"What secrets did the diary hold?"

||
||

Imperative

|

Gives a command or request

|

"Please close the door."

||
||

Exclamatory

|

Shows strong emotion

|

"What a magnificent view!"

||
||

Periodic Sentence

|

Delays main clause for emphasis

|

"Despite the rain and wind, the hikers reached the summit safely."

||
