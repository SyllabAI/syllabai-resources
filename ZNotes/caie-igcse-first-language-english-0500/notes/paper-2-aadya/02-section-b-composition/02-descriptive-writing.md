## Descriptive Writing

### Purpose

The aim of descriptive writing is to create a vivid picture of a person, place, object, or moment through emphasised details. The focus should be on **observation**, allowing the reader to visualise, hear, smell, feel, or even taste the scene. The writing should capture an atmosphere or mood instead of telling a story. You will be marked on content, structure, style and accuracy.

### Recommended Structure

#|
||

**Paragraph**

|

**Purpose**

||
||

Panoramic View

|

Begin with a broad description of the setting. Establish the time, atmosphere, and overall mood to orientate the reader.

||
||

Zoom In

|

Narrow the focus to one specific feature of the scene. Explore smaller, carefully chosen details using imagery.

||
||

Central Impression

|

Highlight the major mood or emotion of the scene. This paragraph should contain your most striking imagery or a memorable line that reinforces the atmosphere.

||
||

Shift Focus

|

Move your attention to another part of the setting. This change in perspective keeps the description dynamic while maintaining the same mood.

||
||

Final Focus

|

Draw attention to a final detail, movement, or object that adds depth to the scene. This may be something in the background or just beyond the main focus.

||
||

Panoramic Ending

|

Zoom out to the wider scene once more. End by showing how the atmosphere and mood has changed, leaving the reader with a lasting impression.

||
|#

### Language & Style

Use **precise vocabulary** instead of generic words (such as *nice*, *beautiful*, or *amazing)* while appealing to multiple **senses** (sight, sound, smell, touch, and taste where appropriate).

* Include figurative language naturally, such as:

  * Similes, metaphors, personification, symbolism

* Vary sentence lengths:

  * Short sentences for tension and emphasis.
  * Longer sentences to convey chaos or movement.
