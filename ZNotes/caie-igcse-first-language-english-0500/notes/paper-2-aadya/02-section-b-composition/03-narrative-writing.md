## Narrative Writing

### Purpose

The aim of narrative writing is to write a **convincing and engaging story** with a clear plot, believable characters, and an effective resolution. All events should contribute to the overall storyline and message.

### Characterisation

* Limit the story to **one or two well-developed characters**.

* Develop characters through:

  * thoughts and emotions, body language and actions, dialogue (used **sparingly** and only when it develops the plot or reveals character) and interactions with others.
  * Allow your protagonist to **grow** over the course of the story. A clear character arc makes the narrative more realistic and meaningful.
    * **Example**: a character who is **selfish at the beginning** may gradually learn the importance of **kindness or sacrifice** by the end. This change should be shown naturally through their decisions, actions, and experiences rather than simply stated.

    * **Effect:** Character development adds depth, strengthens the story's message, and creates a more satisfying and convincing ending.

* Apply the principle of **show, don't tell** through actions, thoughts, body language, and sensory details.

  * **Example**:
    **Tell:** *Kyle was nervous.*
    **Show:** *Kyle’s hands trembled as he reached for the door handle, his heartbeat thudding louder with every step.*
