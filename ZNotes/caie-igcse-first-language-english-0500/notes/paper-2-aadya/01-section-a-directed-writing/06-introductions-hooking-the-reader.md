## Introductions & Hooking the Reader

### Purpose of an Introduction

An introduction establishes the tone, voice, and direction of a piece of writing. Its purpose is to engage the reader immediately, introduce the topic clearly, and signal the writer’s viewpoint or main argument. Effective introductions are concise, purposeful, and attention-grabbing.

### Common Hooking Techniques

Bold statement

|

Grabs attention immediately by presenting a strong, provocative claim.

|

*Schools should ban smartphones entirely before they do irreversible damage to students' education and wellbeing.*

||
||

Rhetorical question

|

Encourages the reader to think about the topic and become engaged.

|

*How many opportunities have been missed because people were afraid to take a risk?*

||
||

Vivid scene or anecdote

|

Creates interest by placing the reader in a memorable moment.

|

*The rain hammered against the windows as hundreds of passengers waited anxiously for news of their delayed flight.*

||
||

Statistic or fact

|

Establishes credibility, highlighting the importance of a certain issue.

|

*Over 8 million tonnes of plastic enter the oceans every year.*

||
||

Direct address

|

Involves the reader personally by speaking to them directly.

|

*Imagine waking up tomorrow without access to the internet.*

||
||

Contrast or paradox

|

Creates curiosity by presenting two opposing ideas.

|

*We are more connected than ever before, yet many people feel increasingly isolated.*
