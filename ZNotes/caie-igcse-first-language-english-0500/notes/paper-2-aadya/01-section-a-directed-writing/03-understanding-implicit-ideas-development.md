## Understanding, Implicit Ideas & Development

You must use ideas from **both texts** and show clear understanding of their arguments. Strong answers go further by identifying and developing **implicit ideas**.

* **Explicit ideas** → clearly stated in the text

* **Implicit ideas** → inferred from text

You should aim to identify **4–6 implicit ideas from each text**, such as:

* Hidden assumptions, weaknesses in arguments, consequences of ideas & alternative viewpoints

Once identified, these ideas must be **developed**. This means:

* Evaluating the writer’s argument, challenging points made, adding counter-arguments & suggesting solutions or improvements

### Developing Implicit Ideas into Arguments

Do not simply identify what the writer says, instead, explore the **hidden assumptions, weaknesses, or overlooked perspectives** behind their ideas.

**For example:**

* **Text’s point:**

  *"Schools should remove homework because students are already overwhelmed by lessons."*

* **Implicit idea:**

  “The writer assumes that homework is the main cause of student stress and that removing it will automatically improve wellbeing.”

* **Development / Evaluation:**

  Challenge this assumption by considering limitations or alternative viewpoints:

  “Although excessive homework can increase stress, removing all homework may reduce opportunities for students to practise skills independently. The issue is not homework itself, but whether tasks are meaningful and appropriately balanced.”
