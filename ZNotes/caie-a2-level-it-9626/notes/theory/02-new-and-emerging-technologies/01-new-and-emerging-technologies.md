# New and Emerging Technologies

## Types of New and Emerging Technologies

### Artificial Intelligence (AI)

* **Definition**

  * AI refers to the ability of computer systems to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence (problem-solving, learning, decision-making).

  * AI analyses its environment using sensors, takes actions based on what it senses, and learns from experience to adapt behaviour without human intervention.

* **Applications**

  * Used in robotics, medicine (AI-assisted surgery, patient monitoring), translation, and autonomous systems.

### Augmented Reality (AR)

* **Definition**

  * AR overlays computer-generated elements onto the real world in real time, enhancing the user’s perception of reality.

* **Examples**

  * Firefighting masks showing navigation and thermal data.

  * Medicine: projecting surgeons’ hands into real operations; locating veins using handheld scanners.

  * Education: interactive skeletal models for medical training.

### Holographic and 4th Generation Optical Data Storage

* **Definition**

  * Uses laser interference patterns (green and red lasers) to store data holographically in multiple layers of an optical disc.

* **Features**

  * Stores around 60,000 bits per laser pulse.
  * Capacity of 100 GB–1 TB.
  * The green laser reads data; the red laser aligns the read heads using reference data.

### Impact of New and Emerging Technologies

* **On** **Individuals**

  * Enhanced convenience and personalisation.

  * Access to better healthcare and entertainment.

  * Risk of unemployment due to automation.

  * Privacy and data security concerns.

* **On Organisations**

  * Increased productivity, efficiency, and innovation.

  * Improved communication and decision-making.

  * High implementation and maintenance costs.

  * Need for staff retraining and data protection.

* **On** **Medicine and Healthcare**

  * **Applications**

    * AI, VR, and AR: training, diagnostics, and surgery.

    * Wearables: patient monitoring, fitness tracking.

    * 3D printing: prosthetics, implants, tissue and organ creation.

    * Tissue engineering: customised and faster recovery.

    * Holographic imaging: 3D anatomy visualisation.

    * Artificial blood vessels: reduce waiting time and rejection risk.

  * **Advantages**

    * Non-invasive procedures.

    * Remote monitoring and faster recovery.

    * Customised patient treatment.

  * **Disadvantages**

    * Ethical concerns, cost, and training requirements.

* **On** **Scientific Research**

  * CAT improves collaboration through multilingual translation.

  * AI assists in data analysis.

  * Robotics and holography enhance experiment precision.

  * Cloud and molecular storage improve data accessibility.

  * **Disadvantages**

    * Risk of contextual translation errors.

    * Data confidentiality issues.

* **On** **the Environment**

  * **Positive Impacts**

    * Robots reduce waste and optimise resource use.

    * IoT aids environmental monitoring.

    * Precision agriculture reduces chemical use.

  * **Negative Impacts**

    * E-waste pollution (toxic metals, plastics).

    * High energy use of AI, robotics, and blockchain.

    * Rare metal extraction for devices damages ecosystems.

  * **Solutions**

    * Recycling programmes, sustainable materials, energy-efficient systems.
