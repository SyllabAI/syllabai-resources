## **Mobile Communication Systems**

### Structure of a Cellular Network

* A geographical area is divided into cells.

* Each cell is served by a base station (cell tower).

* Mobile devices communicate with the nearest base station using radio waves.

* Base stations connect to the wider telecommunications network.

* As users move between cells, a handover transfers the connection to another base station.

### 3G, 4G and 5G Mobile Communication Systems

* **3G**

  * Supports voice, text and mobile internet access.

  * Lower bandwidth and slower data transfer rates than 4G and 5G.

  * Higher latency than newer systems.

* **4G**

  * Higher bandwidth and faster download/upload speeds.

  * Reduced buffering and faster web browsing.

  * Uses packet switching for all communications.

  * Improved handovers between base stations.

  * Better support for video streaming and internet services.

* **5G**

  * Very high bandwidth.

  * Very low latency (near-instant communication).

  * Supports up to millions of connected devices.

  * Beam-forming improves signal quality.

  * Better coverage in densely populated areas.

  * Supports IoT and machine-to-machine communication.

### Satellite Communication Systems

* **Sending Data**

  * Data is converted into radio signals.

  * Signals are transmitted from an Earth station to a satellite (uplink).

  * The satellite receives and amplifies the signal.

  * The satellite retransmits the signal back to Earth (downlink).

  * Receiving stations decode the signal back into data.

### Global Positioning System

* **How GPS Works**

  * GPS consists of satellites orbiting Earth.

  * Satellites continuously transmit:

    * Their position.

    * Accurate atomic clock time.

* **Determining Position**

  * Receiver receives signals from at least 3 satellites.

  * Calculates signal travel times.

  * Determines distance from each satellite.

  * Uses trilateration to calculate latitude and longitude.

  * Four satellites improve accuracy and time synchronisation.

* **Causes of inaccuracy**

  * Buildings or tunnels block signals.

  * Poor weather conditions.

  * Insufficient visible satellites.

  * Signal reflection (multipath errors).

  * Weak battery power.

* **Uses**

  * Vehicle satnav systems.

  * Tracking vehicles, goods and people.

  * Mobile phone location services.

  * Mapping and surveying.

  * Geotagging photographs.

  * Tourism and location-based apps.

  * Geological and earthquake monitoring.

### Global Mapping System

* **Purpose**

  * Combine GPS coordinates with digital maps.

  * Display the user's location visually.

  * Provide route planning and navigation.

  * Calculate distances and estimated travel times.

  * Used in satnavs, smartphones, and mapping applications.

### **Telecommunication Applications of Satellites**

* Television Broadcasting

  * Satellite TV transmission over large areas.

  * Supports high-definition and UHD broadcasting.

* Radio Broadcasting

  * Distributes radio signals over large regions.

* Telephone Communications

  * Enables voice and data communication over long distances.

  * Useful where terrestrial infrastructure is unavailable.

* Internet and Data Transfer

  * Broadband access in remote areas.

  * Communications for ships, aircraft and isolated communities.

### Ultra-High Definition Television (UHDTV)

* Why Satellites are Suitable

  * Very high bandwidth available.

  * Large geographical coverage.

  * Reliable distribution of large video files.

  * Supports 4K and 8K broadcasting services.

* Limitation

  * High bandwidth requirements increase transmission costs.
