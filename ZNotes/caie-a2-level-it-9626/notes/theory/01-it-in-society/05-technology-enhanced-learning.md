## Technology Enhanced Learning

### Methods of Delivery of Technology Enhanced Learning

1. **Computer-based Training**

   * **Definition**

     * CBT is an interactive, computer-delivered learning method that uses digital devices to deliver educational content.

     * It replaces the constant presence of a teacher with automated systems that guide, assess, and provide feedback to learners.

   * **Accessibility and Delivery**

     * Learners can access CBT at any time and from any location, allowing flexible and self-paced study

     * It can be delivered through CD-ROMs, web-based training, or local area network (LAN) systems within institutions.

   * **Core Features**

     * Step-by-step lessons that prompt learners to respond and take regular short tests.

     * Continuous feedback on progress and prerequisites before moving to the next topic.

     * Interactive tutorials combining text, images, audio, and video.

     * Simulations and games that reinforce problem-solving and practical understanding.

     * Assessment tools that record results, track progress, and report performance to both learners and teachers.

     * The ability to repeat or review lessons for better retention and understanding.

   * **Teaching and Learning Approach**

     * CBT delivers structured content designed for independent learning

     * It incorporates drill-and-practice exercises, tutorials, and simulations to enhance comprehension and retention

     * Recorded lectures and real-world examples are often used to demonstrate techniques or concepts.

   * **Academic Impact**

     * CBT supports asynchronous learning, allowing students to learn without being bound by time or location

     * It increases engagement through multimedia interaction, encourages active participation, and provides measurable data on learning outcomes

     * The approach also standardises instruction across all learners, ensuring consistency in delivery and content quality.

2. **Online Tutorials**

   * **Definition and Nature**

     * Online tutorials are self-study educational activities delivered via the internet, designed to achieve specific learning outcomes

     * They enable students to engage in learning without physically attending classes and can involve individuals or groups interacting with teachers or peers in real time or asynchronously.

   * **Types of Online Tutorials**

     * Recorded Tutorials: Linear presentations using video or audio that progress in a fixed sequence, often including narration or demonstrations of expert ideas.

     * Interactive Tutorials: Structured collections of web pages with navigable routes chosen by the learner, often combining text, images, video, audio, and animation. Some routes depend on answers to questions, enabling branching learning paths.

     * Many online tutorials combine both recorded and interactive elements to enhance engagement and personalisation.

   * **Structure and Delivery**

     * Tutorials are delivered through web-based platforms or browser-based interfaces accessible via any internet-enabled device.

     * They may be part of formal academic programmes or business training systems and can be free or subscription-based.

     * Common tools include document sharing, screen sharing, and live discussions to emulate real classroom experiences.

     * Tutorials can be recorded for later review by students or teachers to ensure topic coverage and understanding.

   * **Learning Modes**

     * Synchronous Tutorials: Real-time sessions where teachers and students interact directly, similar to a live classroom environment.

     * Asynchronous Tutorials: Flexible sessions allowing students to access materials and communicate with teachers at different times, promoting independent, self-paced learning.

   * **Educational Purpose and Design**

     * Designed to provide structured, topic-based instruction that moves from general concepts to specific knowledge areas.

     * Serve to reinforce class learning, support revision, or substitute in-person lessons to free up classroom space.

     * Used for teaching factual content, guiding advanced study, and providing vocational or industrial training.

     * Allow note-taking, annotations, and integrated testing for immediate feedback.

   * **Access and Communication**

     * Provide access to teachers and experts who may be geographically distant.

     * Facilitate group collaboration and learning communities without physical proximity.

     * Allow customised courses suited to individual needs and learning levels.

   * **Academic Impact**

     * Encourage independent and self-directed learning.

     * Support flexible study schedules and personalised pacing.

     * Expand educational reach beyond geographical limitations.

     * Provide mechanisms for regular assessment and progress monitoring.

3. **Networked Courses**

   * Networked courses are a form of computer-based training delivered online, commonly used in schools, colleges, and businesses.

   * Course materials and assessments are provided digitally, typically stored on an intranet for restricted access within an organisation, though some may be available over the internet via login accounts.

   * They enable collaborative learning by creating support communities where students can ask questions, share knowledge, and seek advice from peers.

   * Course materials are centrally stored, allowing for regular updates and easy management by educators or administrators.

   * Assignments and assessments can be submitted electronically – some are automatically marked by computers to provide instant feedback.

   * Teachers can track and monitor student progress and adjust their involvement based on each learner’s needs.

   * Students may be able to set personal learning goals and track their progress, enhancing self-motivation and responsibility.

   * Networked courses can follow either a linear structure (same path for all students) or a non-linear structure (customised or branched paths depending on performance or preference).

   * Some systems allow pre-assessment before the course begins to create customised learning routes, adapting to individual abilities and prior knowledge.

   * Non-linear courses offer personalised learning experiences, whereas linear courses provide a uniform progression similar to traditional classroom teaching.

4. **Massive Open Online Courses (MOOC)**

   * MOOCs are large-scale, internet-based courses with open access and no formal entry requirements.

   * They allow global participation, enabling learners from anywhere with internet connectivity to enrol.

   * Courses can be organised and accessed anytime, offering flexibility and self-paced study.

   * They use multimedia tools such as videos, audio, graphics, simulations, and interactive activities.

   * MOOCs often include quizzes, assessments, and instant feedback features to monitor progress.

   * Interactive forums and social media platforms are used to promote collaboration and peer learning.

   * Course content can evolve dynamically based on learner interaction and participation.

   * They can be provided in multiple languages to accommodate diverse learners.

   * MOOCs are typically offered by universities, businesses, and international organisations.

   * They may be integrated into wider learning programmes or used independently for self-development.

   * While many are free, some charge for advanced features such as certification or teacher feedback.

5. **Video Conferencing**

   * Enables real-time visual and audio communication between students and teachers in different locations.

   * Allows learners from around the world to connect, interact, and collaborate on shared projects.

   * Facilitates resource and idea sharing between students across various classrooms or institutions.

   * Provides access to education for students in remote or rural areas without requiring travel.

   * Supports inclusion of home-based learners, such as those unable to attend school due to illness.

   * Allows participation in virtual field trips to locations that may be inaccessible or unsafe in person.

   * Encourages diverse learning experiences by exposing students to multiple perspectives globally.

   * Complements independent study with interactive and engaging face-to-face sessions online.

   * Saves time by reducing the need for physical travel, increasing the time available for learning.

### Impacts of Technology Enhanced Learning

* **Motivation**

  * Use of multimedia (video, audio, graphics, simulations, and games) in CBT and MOOCs increases engagement and prevents boredom.

  * Online tutorials and video conferencing provide real-time interaction and feedback, helping maintain interest and accountability.

  * Repetition and instant feedback through digital assessments reinforce learning and motivate progress.

  * Collaborative tools in networked courses and video conferencing promote peer interaction and social motivation.

  * However, lack of direct teacher presence (especially in CBT and MOOCs) may lead to reduced motivation and disengagement for some students.

* **Achievement**

  * Students can revisit topics, take quizzes, and receive immediate feedback, improving retention and performance.

  * Continuous tracking of progress in CBT, online tutorials, and MOOCs helps both learners and teachers identify strengths and weaknesses.

  * Interactive and structured materials ensure consistency and high-quality delivery across large groups.

  * Virtual collaboration through video conferencing and networked courses enhances understanding through discussion and shared problem-solving.

  * Self-paced learning enables students to master concepts before advancing, supporting differentiated achievement levels.

* **Autonomy**

  * All forms of tech-enhanced learning promote independent study and self-regulation.

  * Students can choose when and where to study, giving flexibility and ownership over their learning.

  * Online tutorials, MOOCs, and CBT allow learners to control the pace and sequence of learning materials.

  * Non-linear networked courses adapt routes based on student performance or choice, promoting personalised learning.

  * Increased autonomy can be challenging for students who struggle with self-motivation or time management.
