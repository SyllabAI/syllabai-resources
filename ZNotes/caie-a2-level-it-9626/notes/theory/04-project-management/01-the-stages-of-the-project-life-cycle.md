# Project Management

## **The Stages of the Project Life Cycle**

* Every project follows a structured sequence of stages known as the **Project Life Cycle**.

* The purpose of the life cycle is to ensure that projects are properly planned, controlled, monitored and reviewed.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/191f4be3897c44ca69c12553.webp =400x)

1. **Project Initiation**

   * The initiation stage determines whether a project should go ahead and establishes its overall direction.
   * **Table for all aspects of the stage and their purposes**
   * A project cannot be planned effectively until stakeholder requirements have been identified. Stakeholders include
     * Clients

     * End users

     * Project manager

     * Developers

     * Shareholders

     * Senior management

2. **Project Planning**

   * Once approval is given, detailed planning begins.

   * The project manager breaks the project into smaller tasks and allocates resources.

   * **Table for all activities in this stage and their purposes**

   * Project management software is frequently used to create schedules and calculate timings.

3. **Project Execution and Monitoring**

   * This is the stage where actual work is completed.

   * **Table for listing all the monitoring areas and what is monitored**

   * Managers compare actual progress against planned progress.

   * Reports must be created for stakeholders at multiple steps in the cycle to ensure they're kept updated and to ensure any new changes can be made. These reports may contain

     * Progress summaries

     * Cost information

     * Delays encountered

     * Risk updates

     * Revised completion dates

4. **Project Close**

   * In this stage

     * Final deliverables are handed over

     * Stakeholder approval is obtained

     * Documentation is completed

     * Resources are released

   * Project reviews are necessary because they allow organisations to

     * Identify strengths

     * Identify weaknesses

     * Improve future projects

     * Record lessons learned

     * Assess whether objectives were achieved
