# **Demands For Political Reform, Including The Chartists**

### **Chartism**

* The Reform Act failed to improve the working class
* A new movement emerged in the 1830s called Chartism
* Other factors like bad harvests in 1837-38 and 1847-48 led to high food prices
* Severe unemployment in 1842 also contributed
* Trade unions also failed to improve wages or working conditions
* The New Poor Law was also despised
* In 1838, the People’s Charter was drawn up
* It had six key points:
  * Universal male suffrage
  * No property qualifications for MPs (allowing the working class to become MPs)
  * Annual elections
  * Equal representation
  * Payment for MPs (again allowing the working class to become MPs)
  * Voting to be done in secret
* The main periods of Chartist activity were 1837-38, 1842 and 1847-48
* These were linked to high food prices or high unemployment
* Petitions were sent to parliament each time, containing millions of signatures
* Parliament accepted none
* Rising employment, increased wages and drops in food prices meant Chartism ceased to be a national movement after 1848

### **Failure Of Chartism**

* Chartism was largely divided and fuelled by hunger and unemployment
* When conditions improved, the movement declined
* The movement itself faced divisions over:
  * The use of force or peaceful protest
  * A revolution or moderate change
  * Collaboration with the middle class or to stay separate
  * Religious or not
* Given that there were many different objectives and the changing economic conditions, it perhaps was not surprising that the movement achieved little
