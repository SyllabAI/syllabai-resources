# **Origins Of Organised Labour**

### **Trade Unions**

* Trade unions had limited impact in this period
* Although they were illegal, they did exist
* The ruling class did not like the idea of the working people coming together
* Groups of skilled men did organise together, called ‘combinations’
* They were conservative and defensive
* They wanted to preserve the interests of their members
* During the French Revolution, the illegality of these unions was reinforced in 1799 by new Combination Laws
* The government was afraid of revolutionary activity so they made unions punishable by imprisonment
* During the period of wars, membership of these unions boomed
* However, economic depression and government repression after 1815 reduced union activity
* In 1824, the Combination Laws were repealed as part of a general relaxation of repressive laws
* Trade unions were now legal, which created a sudden outburst of strikes
* This led to the modification of the law in 1825 where unions were still legal but they could only negotiate hours and wages and could not strike
* The most famous anti-union action occurred in 1834
* George Loveless tried to form a union to improve conditions for agricultural workers
* Members swore an oath of secrecy, but they were still discovered and Loveless was transported to Australia for his punishment
* This stopped any further activity among agricultural workers
* Attempts at forming national unions by carpenters in 1827 failed
* The Grand National Consolidated Trade Union was formed to gain an 8-hour day, but also failed
* Overall trade unions achieved little
