# **Reactions To Mechanisation And Economic Change**

### **Rural Unrest**

* Before the agricultural revolution, there was little unrest in rural areas
* However, rapid population growth in rural areas meant less work and the domestic industry was being phased out
* The enclosure movement lead to the loss of common land for grazing and small plots of land for agricultural workers
* Landowners did not need full-time workers and so regular paid work was less available
* When food prices rose, there would be small-scale riots which were easily suppressed
* However, the years after 1815 saw an increase in protest in rural areas
* Mass unemployment and poverty led to more food riots and attacks on livestock in the 1820s
* The welfare system was badly maintained as many landowners refused to contribute more to the system, giving many no choice

### **The Captain Swing Riots**

* The riots lasted from 1830 to 1831
* They highlighted several issues:
  * Lack of regular employment and welfare support
  * Low wages
  * Bad housing and working conditions
  * Hunger
* The main areas of protest were in southern England and in East Anglia where there was little alternative employment
* The government reacted quickly and sent in troops to keep order
* The protests achieved little

### **The Luddite Riots**

* Despite the huge impact of industrialisation on the working class, there were surprisingly few protests against the factory system
* There were occasional attacks on machines before 1830, but fewer later
* There were only two significant attempts at rioting
* The first was in Lancashire in the 1770s caused by the arrival of machinery that improved the output and quality of the finished cloth
* The men who made textiles by hand found themselves without work
* They smashed new machinery but lacked popular support and they were only confined to a small area
* The other main outburst was known as the Luddite movement
* It occurred between 1811 and 1816 in the north of England and the Midlands
* Mobs broke into factories and smashed machineries like the spinning jennies and water frames
* Most of the men involved were skilled workers whose income and independent status was threatened by the new machines
* The government passed the Frame Breaking Act of 1812, making machine breaking a capital crime
* This riot was again firmly suppressed by troops

### **The Political And Constitutional Consequences Of Industrialisation**

* For the first 50 years of this period, the political landscape was barely changed
* The government was also sympathetic towards the Revolution, putting up few obstacles
* Radical groups in the government did form calling for reform
* However, they were only a real threat when the working class seemed discontent
* For example, in 1815 there was an exceptionally bad harvest
* The Corn Laws were passed in 1815, banning the import of cheap corn to help landowners maintain their profits
* This created more hunger and unemployment, which many radicals tried to take advantage of
* However, they were easily repressed
* Even peaceful protest was sometimes dealt with brutally
* In 1819, over 60,000 people went to listen to a speech by the radical political speaker Henry Hunt
* An armed cavalry charged the crowd, killing 11 people
* This became known as “Peterloo”
* Reform finally started to seem likely from 1830
* In 1830, the Conservatives lost, bringing in a new liberal government
* The years 1830 to 1832 were filled with campaigning for parliamentary reform
* The working class and middle class also formed an alliance to pressure the government
* Organisations were set up to demand political change
* In 1831, the National Union of the Working Class was set up in London to demand the vote for all adult males
* This mass protest forced the government into major constitutional and political reform

### **The Great Reform Act**

* In 1832, parliament was finally reformed
* The middle class was given the vote
* The new industrial towns could send MPs to parliament
* Yet the working class gained little
* This disappointment led to further demands for change, notably the Chartist movement

### **The New Poor Law**

* The government knew the working class was angry over the changes
* Therefore, they wanted to deal with the problem of poverty
* The government set up a Royal Commission in 1832 to investigate and recommend actions for poverty
* The Poor Law Amendment Act was passed in 1834
* It aimed to give as little welfare as possible to cut the cost for landowners
* Instead, those who needed help would be sent to workhouses
* The workhouses would be intentionally worse than the lowest wage jobs
* This would save money for the middle-class men who ran these
* This law was passed in the interests of the middle class, again disappointing the working class
