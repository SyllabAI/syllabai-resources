# Why was there a rapid growth in the industrialisation after 1780

## Development Of The Factory System

### **Steam Power**

* Arguably, the development of Newcomen’s steam engine was a vital spark
* James Watt adapted Newcomen’s great invention into a multipurpose source of power
* He made the powered mechanisation of the industry possible
* By 1776, Watt’s steam engines were successful in pumping water out of coal mines more efficiently and cheaply than Newcomen’s
* It also powered blast furnaces to achieve higher temperatures and make better-quality iron
* This also meant less coal was needed
* With better-quality iron available, more efficient engines could be made
* By 1800, variants of Watt’s engines were providing power for hundreds of different enterprises, ranging from cotton and woollen mills to powerful steam hammers at iron foundries and corn grinders in flour mills
* These machines were also being sold abroad for substantial profits
* Richard Trevithick was the first to develop the railway steam locomotive by 1804
* Demand and vast profit have led to remarkable innovations

### **Textiles**

* Textiles, mostly wool and cotton, were central to the industrialisation process in Britain.
* They were the second biggest industry after the agricultural industry
* Innovations such as those of Kay in weaving and Hargreaves in spinning came before the period of rapid growth
* The first major development came in 1771 when Richard Arkwright patented his water frame
* This invention revolutionised the spinning process, which made raw cotton into useable thread
* Now, one worker with a machine could produce 128 threads at the same time
* Also, the threads were stronger and of higher quality
* Therefore, the price of cotton cloth dropped substantially, which increased its demand
* Eli Whitney's invention of the cotton gin (a machine which removed the unwanted seeds from raw cotton quickly and efficiently) in the US made raw cotton cheaper and increased its availability
* Further innovations in spinning were made by Samuel Crompton, who patented his spinning mule
* This again increased the quality of the threads and the speed at which threads were made
* By 1820, more than 4 million of these spinning mules were operating in British factories
* Once Wattʻs steam engines replaced water power or hand-driven power to drive these new machines, output soared further
* Between 1779 and 1830, the price of finished cotton dropped by 93%

### **The Factory System**

* Owners saw that large profits could be made by making quality textiles
* With this, factories were made
* The first modern factory was built by Richard Arkwright in 1769 in Derbyshire.
* He chose the site partly because it was easy to access raw materials but also because of the availability of good water power.
* He built the first cotton mill on five floors
* It could operate 24 hours a day
* Arkwright also built housing for his workforce to attract more workers
* Between 1770 and 1835, about 1200 cotton factories and 1300 woollen factories were built in Britain
