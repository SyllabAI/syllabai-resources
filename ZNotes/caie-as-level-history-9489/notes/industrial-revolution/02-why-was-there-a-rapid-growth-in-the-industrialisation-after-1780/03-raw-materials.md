# **Raw Materials**

### **Coal**

* Coal was available in unlimited quantities at a reasonable price once transport costs dropped
* Britain also had access to different types of coal required for different processes
* Coal was mined in many parts of Britain, which made getting it to where it was needed easier

### **Iron**

* To produce iron, there needed to be iron ore
* Fortunately, Britain had large supplies of it and it was quite easy to dig out
* The development of canals made transporting the ore to refineries straightforward
* In 1783 and 1784, Henry Cort took out two patents on ‘puddling’ and rolling iron
* This led to the production of better-quality and cheaper iron in Britain
* One iron foundry in South Wales produced 500 tonnes of quality iron in 1785
* After adapting the new techniques, it produced 10,000 tonnes in 1812
* Britain now had a surplus of good-quality iron
* The key causes of this change are:
  * The capital needed to fund his research
  * Cheap coal and means of transporting it
  * Iron ore was available
  * There was a huge demand
  * There was entrepreneurship and a willingness to take risks

### **Textiles**

* Cotton was not grown in Britain, so it was imported
* Britain had colonies which could produce large amounts of raw cotton
* It also had a large merchant navy and the Royal Navy to protect it
* Even though political relations between Britain and America were not ideal after the War of Independence, cotton growers in America ensured that their trading was not affected
* The other vital raw material was wool
* The wool trade had existed in Britain for centuries before cotton began to arrive on a large-scale
* There was more than enough raw wool before the mechanisation of industry
* Mechanisation did increase the quality and supply, reducing its prices which also increased demand
