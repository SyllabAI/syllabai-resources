## Developments In Transport

### **Canals**

* For the period between 1780 and 1830, canals were essential to industrial growth.
* By the end of this period, there were over 6000 km of navigable rivers and canals in Britain.
* Large sums of money were invested in canals in what was known as ʻcanal maniaʻ
* Canals enabled industrial development and new towns to be created
* Small towns flourished because canals enabled a steady supply of coal and raw materials
* For example, St Helens in Lancashire developed large-scale industries in copper, glass and iron and could ship out their finished products using the canal system
* Canals also provided work for thousands and developed engineers

### **Railways**

* Engineers Richard Trevithick and George Stephenson and his son Robert had been experimenting with mobile steam engines since about 1800
* As with many inventions, a combination of demand, profit potential and willingness by practical men to invest led to a breakthrough.
* In 1825, a railway line opened between Stockton and Darlington, with trucks of coal being pulled by a mobile steam engine.
* The following year, a line opened between Manchester, one of the great urban manufacturing centres of Britain and Liverpool, one of Britainʻs major ports
* This radically reduced the cost of transport, increasing the demand
* Rail was faster, more reliable and cheaper than canals
* Railways did not freeze in the winter or suffer from severe droughts
* By 1844, there were 3000 km of railway and 6000 km by 1852
* While canals went into decline, railways generated significant employment opportunities
* Thousands of men worked to build tracks, railway stations, bridges and tunnels
* Rail transport also meant families could move around quicker in search of work
* Demand for iron and steel soared

### **Steamships**

* Between 1800 and 1850, there was a lot of experimentation and innovation with steam-driven ships at sea and canal barges.
* The first steamship to cross the Atlantic was the Savannah in 1819
* In 1838, the Great Western completed its first transatlantic voyage in 14 days
* The first regular transatlantic cargo and passenger service began in 1840
* The first wrought-iron ship, the Great Britain, was built and launched in 1843
* Competition between companies formed as they competed for speed and efficiency
* It was not until the latter part of the century that steam-driven ships made entirely out of iron and steel became Britain's main way of trading with India, North America and the Far East
