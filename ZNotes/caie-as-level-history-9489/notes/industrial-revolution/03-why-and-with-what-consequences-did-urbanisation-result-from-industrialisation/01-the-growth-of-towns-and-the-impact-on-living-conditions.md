# **The Growth Of Towns And The Impact on Living Conditions**

### **Urbanisation**

* Although Britain was the most developed and urbanised nation in the world in 1750, only 15% of the population lived in towns
* In 1775, just 7 towns had a population of over 30,000 and 5 of them were ports
* The growth in commerce and industrialization led to a steady growth of urban areas
* By 1800, 25% of the population lived in town and by 1850, it was over 50%
* This migration from country to town was a key part of the industrialisation process

### **The Cause Of Rapid Growth In Towns**

* One of the most important factors in the growth of towns was the rise in Britain’s overall population
* Population increased more than twice between 1750 and 1800
* The national census showed that it went from 15.7 million in 1801 to 27.3 million in 1851
* This stemmed from a combination of ʻpushʻ and ʻpullʻ factors
* A push came from agriculture as fewer workers were needed in the countryside because of the increase in efficiency and productivity
* Therefore, a pull came from towns which provided jobs
* The establishment of textile factories in Leeds and cotton factories in Manchester draw people in
* The population of Leeds grew from 53,000 in 1801 to 172,000 in 1851, and Manchester’s rose from 75,000 in 1801 to 303,000 in 1851
* Changes in the agricultural sector led to more and better-quality food being produced, providing for the growing population
* Their merchant navy could also import any food they lacked
* People were also no longer tied to their land
* They could move easily to another town for work
* Transport changes allowed food and materials to come in to supply the towns

### **Urban Conditions, House And Health**

* With the influx of workers, there was a large demand for housing
* Building entrepreneurs bought land near factories and tried to fit as many houses as possible for quick profits
* There was no attention paid to sewage or water supplies
* Heating and ventilation were non-existent
* This led to rampant diseases like tuberculosis and pneumonia during winter
* During summer, contaminated water supplies caused cholera, killing 50,000 people
* Part of the problem was the ill-suited system of local government
* The government passed the Municipal Corporations Act in 1835 to help
* This set up a new system of local government where local property owners elected local officials to manage the town
* However, this excluded many in the lower classes
* Local taxes would be raised and used to improve the town
* However, many did not want to pay the increase in taxes, so towns were not improved
* Therefore, the situation deteriorated until the Public Health Act of 1848
* This had created a Board of Health in London which encouraged the creation of local health boards that had the power to make improvements
* Still, there was no national pressure for improved housing
