# **Government Responses To The Consequences Of Industrialisation**

### **Controlling Working Conditions**

* Traditionally, the government would not interfere in the relationship between an employer and their employees
* Yet the exploitation of women and children was obvious
* Some attempts were made to control child labour
* An act was passed in 1802 which prevented child labour at night and limited their working hours to 12
* Another act in 1819 banned children under 9 from working in cotton mills and ages 9-16 could only work 12 hours a day
* Both these acts were difficult to enforce so there was no real improvement

### **Political Breakthrough**

* The two main political parties were the Whigs and the Tories
* The Tories tended to be conservative
* The Whigs favoured political reform and preferred a laissez-faire approach in other areas
* Two prominent leaders of the Tory party, Richard Oastler and Michael Sadler, were gaining support from many workers hoping to see a reduction in working hours for all
* They tried to pass a 10-hour limit for workers under 18
* The bill did not pass but it did achieve national publicity
* Sadler was unfortunately voted out as he was strongly opposed by factory owners and those who believed in a policy of laissez-faire
* However, Lord Ashley continued his work and only just failed to pass the 10 hour bill by one vote
* This prompted the government to set up a Royal Commission to examine the subject
* This resulted in the 1833 Factory Act:
  * No child under 9 could work
  * Ages under 18 had limited working hours
  * Children over 9 and under 13 had to receive 2 hours of education a day
* Inspectors were appointed by the government to make sure these laws were being obeyed
* However, this only covered textile factories
* Coal mines were still dangerous
* The failings of the act were reviewed by a select committee, leading to the 1842 Mines Act
* The Mines Act prohibited children under 10 and women from underground work along with inspectors to enforce these rules
* They also introduced the 1844 and 1847 Factory Acts which reduced the working hours of 8 to 13-year-olds and increased the minimum amount of education required to 3 hours a day
* A maximum of 12 hours a day was also laid down for women which was reduced to 10 hours in the 1847 act
