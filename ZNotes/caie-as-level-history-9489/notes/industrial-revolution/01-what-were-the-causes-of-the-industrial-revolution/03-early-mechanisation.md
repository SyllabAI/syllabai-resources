## Early Mechanisation

### **Iron And Coal**

* Good quality iron was required for all machines
* Britain used charcoal to burn iron, but they were running out of wood to make charcoal.
* In 1709, Abraham Darby of Coalbrookdale developed a technique for using coke (formed from coal) to melt iron ore.
* There was already a vast supply of coal, and it eliminated impurities from the iron ore.
* Now, better quality and cheaper iron could be produced
* This iron proved to be essential in developing the machinery needed

### **Steam Power**

* Coal was in huge demand, but mines kept getting flooded
* In 1712, Thomas Newcomen invented a steam engine powered by coal, which could pump water out of deep mines at a low cost
* This reduced the price of coal and doubled the quantity produced (from 2.5 million tons in 1700 to about 5 million in 1750 and about 15 million by 1800)
* Newcomen’s invention was later developed to drive machines in factories and railway engines

### **Textiles**

* Two other inventions played a major part in assisting industrialisation
* Britain produced two main types of textiles: wool and cotton
* Both were in high demand globally
* The first major invention was John Kay’s ‘flying shuttle’ in 1733
* This greatly improved the weaving speed and quality of the cloth
* It also meant fewer people were needed for production
* The second development came in 1765 when James Hargreaves invented the ‘Spinning Jenny.’
* This sped up the spinning process
* It could do the work of 120 spinners by itself
* In 1775, Britain’s cotton cloth production totalled 57,000 yards
* By 1783, it was 3.5 million yards
* The quality was better, and the price rapidly decreased, creating further demand.
* Cotton went from a luxury to an everyday material.
