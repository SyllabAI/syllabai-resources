## Early Developments In Transport

### **Rivers And Ports**

* Manufacturing industries needed to bring in supplies and export their goods.
* Therefore, they needed a solid transportation network
* Britain had natural advantages like navigable rivers like the Severn, Humber, Trent, Mersey, Clyde and Thames
* In response to demand, substantial engineering projects were carried out to improve them so bigger cargoes could be carried further

### **Roads**

* In 1700, Britain’s roads were generally in poor condition
* A major barrier to industrialisation was the inability to move goods quickly and cheaply
* The solution was a system called ‘Turnpike Trusts’
* A company could be formed, backed by an Act of Parliament, and would have substantial powers to acquire the land needed
* In return for radically improving and maintaining the road, they could charge a fee to all who travelled on it
* Between 1750 and 1770, Parliament passed over 500 acts creating Turnpike Trusts covering over 24,000 km of road
* This was in response to the major demand from both farming and manufacturing businesses
* Now, the whole of England and Wales was connected
* Local citizens also invested in these companies, which created large profits and encouraged future investments.

### **Canals**

* In the years before rapid industrialisation, canals were perhaps the most important improvement in transport.
* Coal was expensive to transport on horseback.
* The solution came in 1761 when the Duke of Bridgewater built a canal from his coal mines in Worsley to the centre of the growing industrial city of Manchester.
* The cost of transportation went down significantly
* Between 1759 and 1774, 52 Acts of Parliament were passed to allow canals to be built, mainly in the Midlands and the north
* By 1800, nearly 3,000 km of canals had doubled the length of navigable rivers
* Skills learnt in building canals were to be used in the development of the railway network
* Cheap capital and good support from the government helped to achieve this

### **The Growth Of The Population**

* People were essential to any process
* The rapid growth of the population was an important factor behind industrial growth.
* People were needed to work in factories and build railways
* More people also meant a higher demand for textiles, as domestic demand was important
* The population tripled from 1700 to 1850
* This provided labour and essential domestic demand
* The falling infant mortality rates caused this growth due to better diets
