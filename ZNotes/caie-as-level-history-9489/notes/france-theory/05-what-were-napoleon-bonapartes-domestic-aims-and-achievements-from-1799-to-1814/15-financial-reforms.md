## **Financial Reforms**

* Possibly the most pressing issue was the need to establish financial stability
* As Napoleon lacked the skills to implement financial reforms, he appointed a number of very able ministers to undertake this task
* The early financial reforms introduced were:
  * Clearer division of roles between the ministry of finance (which oversaw collection of taxes and revenues) and the treasury (which dealt with government expenditure)
  * Reorganisation of both direct and indirect tax collection
  * The first steps in establishing a public banking system
* Napoleon centralised the collection of duties by creating a Central Excise Office in 1804
* Revenue from indirect taxes increase by over 400% between 1806 and 1812
* One of the most important reforms introduced by Napoleon was the creation of the Bank of France on the 6 January 1800
* The Bank of France had the sole right to issue paper notes
* His aim was to improve the efficiency of the state’s finances
* On the 28 March 1803, Napoleon introduced the *franc de germinal*, which became the basis of his monetary system
* This reform gave France the most stable currency in Europe at that time
* It would remain the basis of France’s currency for the next 120 years
* He began by bringing tax collection under the control of the Ministry of Finance, developing a hierarchy of paid tax collectors and building up a comprehensive tax register
* He ensured the flow of credit at reasonable rates of interest by guaranteeing the payment of interest on government debt
* Military victory in 1801 also brought plunder while peace in 1802 reduced costs
* Similarly, economic policies such as the offer of credit to businesses, the buying up of foreign grain stocks to ensure bread-price stability and then a series of good harvests all contributed to the healthy economic climate of the Consulate
