## **The Civil Constitution Of The Clergy**

* The Assembly wanted the Church to be free from absenteeism and plurality
* They wanted it to be independent of Rome and closely linked to the State
* They also wanted it to be democratic but did not want to interfere with the doctrines of the Church or with its spiritual functions
* The Civil Constitution of the Clergy was approved on 12 July 1790
* The key terms of the Constitution were:
  * All priests and bishops were to be elected
  * Priests were to be paid by the State
  * There was to be no absenteeism
* Most of the Clergy were opposed to the principle of election
* However, most of them also wanted to find a compromise
* The Assembly did not want to compromise as this would give the Church a privileged position, something they were trying to avoid
* Instead of agreeing, the Clergy waited for the Pope to give his verdict
* The Assembly grew tired of waiting and on 27 November 1790, decreed that the Clergy must take an oath to the constitution
* This split the Clergy as they would be choosing between their faith or the State
* Only a third of the Clergy took the oath
* The Pope then condemned the Civil Constitution in March and April 1791
* Many then retracted their oath
* There were now two Churches in France
* One was the Constitutional Church that accepted the Revolution
* The other was a nonjuring Church of refractory priests
* This was a huge area of counter-revolution
* Refractory priests were expelled and peasants felt a sense of betrayal as they were very close with their local parish priests
