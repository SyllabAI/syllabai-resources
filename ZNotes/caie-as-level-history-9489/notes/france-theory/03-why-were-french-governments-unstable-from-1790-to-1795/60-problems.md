## **Problems**

* Robespierre’s popularity among the sans-culottes was falling because of: 
  * The execution of Hebert and his supporters 
  * The dissolution of the popular societies 
  * The end of direct democracy in the Sections 
  * The raising of the Maximum prices in March, resulting in inflation and a fall in the assignat 
  * The imposing of the Maximum on wages 
* On 23 July, the Commune, now filled with Robespierre’s supporters, decided to apply the Maximum to wages 
* This led to a fall in wages by as much as half 
* This greatly affected the sans-culottes who were wage-earners 
* By the spring of 1794, the revolutionary armies had driven all foreign troops from French soil 
* It seemed that the Terror was not needed anymore
* The dictatorship of the two committees remained unchallengeable until they fell out between themselves
* In April, the CPS set up its own police bureau with Robespierre in charge 
* It was meant to prosecute dishonest officials, but the CGS felt that it interfered with its own control of internal security 
* This made them rivals 
* There were also conflicts within the CPS 
* Some members disliked the Saint-Just’s Laws of Ventose (26 February 1794) 
* Many on the CPS became suspicious of Robespierre particularly because of the Cult of the Supreme Being
