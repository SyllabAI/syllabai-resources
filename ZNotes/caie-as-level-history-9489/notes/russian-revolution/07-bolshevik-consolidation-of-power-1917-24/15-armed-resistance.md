## **Armed Resistance**

* 40,000 Czechoslovak troops in Russia had volunteered to fight on Russia’s side in the war
* After the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, they were isolated
* Their aim was to travel to Vladivostok to re-join the Allies
* The Bolsheviks resented this foreign army, so local Soviets began to challenge the Czech Legion
* All of this encouraged the Whites and all anti-Bolshevik groups to come out openly against Lenin’s regime
* The SRs organised a number of uprisings in central Russia and established an anti-Bolshevik Volga ‘republic’ at Samara
* A White ‘Volunteer Army’, led by General Denikin, had formed in the Caucasus region and was made up of tsarist loyalists and outlawed Kadets
* In Siberia, the presence of the Czech legion encouraged the formation of a White army under Admiral Kolchak, the self-proclaimed ‘Supreme Ruler of the Russia State’
* White units appeared in many regions elsewhere
* The speed at which they formed indicated the limitations of Bolshevik control outside the cities of western Russia
* The White generals, Denikin, Kolchak, Wrangel and Yudenich, did not coordinate their strategy
* This allowed the Red Army to fight them individually and overcome them
* The Bolsheviks were able to maintain control over the railways to keep themselves supplied while denying the Whites the same benefit
