## Food And Transport

* Initially, many of the peasant farmers benefited from the war since the demand for food increased.
* Russia’s grain yields were high, but they started to fall in 1916
* Inflation made trading unprofitable, so peasants started hoarding their stocks
* The requisitioning of horses and fertilisers for the war effort made it difficult for peasants to sustain agricultural output
* The military had priority in the use of the transport system, so food supplies could not be distributed effectively
* Shortages were worse in towns and cities
* Petrograd suffered particularly badly because it was far away from food-producing regions and had many refugees.
* It was the disruption of the transport system that caused Russia’s wartime shortages.
* Less than 2 years after the war began, the Russian railway system had already virtually collapsed.
* By 1916, Petrograd and Moscow were receiving a third of their food and fuel requirements.
