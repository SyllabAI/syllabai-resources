## The Russian Economy

* Russia was slow in economic development.
* The low number of urban workers was a sign that Russia had not achieved the major industrial growth that had taken place in countries like Germany, Britain, and the USA in the nineteenth century.
* However, Russia was not entirely without industry.
* The Urals region produced considerable amounts of iron, and the chief western cities, Moscow and St. Petersburg, had extensive textile factories.
* Most villages had smelting works, which enabled them to produce iron goods, and most peasant homes produced wooden, flaxen or woollen goods to supplement their incomes.
* However, these activities were all small-scale.
* The sheer size of Russia and its undeveloped transport system had limited the chance for industrial expansion.
* A further restriction had been the absence of an effective banking system.
* It was hard for Russia to borrow and invest money, which explains why its expansion has been so slow.
