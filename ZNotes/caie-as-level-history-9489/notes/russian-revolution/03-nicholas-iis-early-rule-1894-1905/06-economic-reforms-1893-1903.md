## Economic Reforms 1893-1903

* For some time, it seemed that Russia would become a modern industrial nation.
* This was largely due to Count Sergei Witte and Peter Stolypin
* In the 1890s, Russian industry grew so rapidly that the term ‘great spurt’ was used to describe the period.
* A major reason for the growth was the increase in coal output in Ukraine and the increase in oil production in the Caucasus.
* Although this sudden acceleration was the result of private enterprise, it was sustained by deliberate government policy.
* Witte was brought into government in 1892 as a minister of finance
* He achieved a series of major financial and economic reforms that led to the ‘great spurt.’
* However, after 11 years, the tsar dismissed him as he believed a fabricated story that Witte was involved in a Jewish conspiracy to undermine the Russian state.
* Despite this, the tsar recalled him in 1905 to negotiate peace in the war against Japan, which he did brilliantly.
* Witte also drafted the October Manifesto, which enabled the tsar to emerge from the 1905 Revolution with his powers largely intact.
* However, Witte’s effort was unappreciated, and many officials disliked him.
* Therefore, in 1906, Nicholas II dismissed him, thus depriving the government of its most capable minister.
* His reforms showed that Russia could have achieved a lot more had he remained in office for longer.
