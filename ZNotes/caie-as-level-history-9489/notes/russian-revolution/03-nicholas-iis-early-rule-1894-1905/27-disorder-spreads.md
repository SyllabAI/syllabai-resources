## **Disorder Spreads**

* The immediate reaction to Bloody Sunday was widespread strikes and terrorism against government officials
* Much of it was organised by the Social Revolutionaries and it spread to the countryside
* The situation was made much worse by the outcomes of the war against Japan
* Buildings were attacked and seized by the peasants
* The peasants feared the government was going to repossess the homes of those families who had failed to pay off their mortgages in the post-emancipation years
* The unrest and the government’s failings to continue it encouraged non-Russian minorities to assert their demands
* Georgia and the Poles wanted independence
* The Jews demanded equality
* In May, Pavel Milyukov, leader of the Union of Liberation, persuaded the majority of the liberal groups to form a ‘Union of Unions’
* They aimed to organise a board-based alliance that would include the peasants and factory workers
* It was from this group that the Kadet party would be formed in 1905, also led by Milyukov
