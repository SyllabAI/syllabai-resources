## Quote Bank

For your IGCSE English Literature course, it is recommended to memorize some quotes from the texts you have studied along with the specific literary devices used in each quote to create a certain effect (authorial choice). The following is a list (non-exhaustive) of quotes you may consider studying.

### Act 1

- "Four days will quickly steep themselves in night/Four nights will quickly dream away the time" - Hippolyta
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Dreams
  - Characters: Hippolyta (sp), Theseus
  - Analysis: The wedding establishes the **theme of love**. It is also evident that dreams are more than mere figments of imagination
   
- "Hippolyta, I wooed thee with my sword/ And won thy love doing thee injuries" - Theseus
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Gender tensions & power, Love
  - Characters: Hippolyta, Theseus (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote raises the question of whether Hippolyta has any power as Theseus conquered her. Also, the sword is **symbolic** as it hints that the relationship is not ideal (swords are meant to kill)
 
- "As she is mine, I may dispose of her— Which shall be either to this gentleman/Or to her death" - Egeus
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Gender tensions & power, Love
  - Characters: Eqeus (sp), Theseus
  - Analysis: It is sad that Egeus is willing to watch Hermia die (even though she is his own daughter!) if she won't obey him. Here, love is seen as an external power that takes over and destroys reason. 

- "The course of true love never did run smooth." - Lysander
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love
  - Characters: Lysander (sp), Hermia
  - Analysis: This quote **foreshadows** the obstacles for the rest of the play. It also states Lysander's blind love for Hermia.

- "Marry, our play is The most lamentable comedy and most cruel death of Pyramus and Thisbe." - Quince
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Play within Play
  - Characters: Quince (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote **foreshadows** that these men are not meant to be great actors - what is a lamentable comedy?!

- "Nay, faith, let me not play a woman. I have a beard coming." - Flute
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Play within Play, Theatre
  - Characters: Flute (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote mocks Elizabethan theatre, suggesting how it might not be effective that men play all the roles. 
 
### Act 2

- "The juice of it on sleeping eyelids laid/Will make or man or woman madly dote/Upon the next live creature that it sees." - Oberon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Fairies, Conflict, Illusion, Deception & Trickery
  - Characters: Oberon (sp), Puck
  - Analysis: Here we see how the love juice acts as a **symbol** indicating how love is an external and sometimes overwhelming force, and can cut across boundaries of beauty, status and power.

- "I am your spaniel…The more you beat me I will fawn on you." - Helena
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Gender tensions
  - Characters: Helena (sp), Demetrius
  - Analysis: This quote reveals a dark side of unequal love; how it has enslaved Helena to be a dog for Demetrius.

- "Nay, good Lysander. For my sake, my dear/Lie further off yet. Do not lie so near." - Hermia
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Gender power
  - Characters: Hermia (sp), Lysander
  - Analysis: This quote reveals another aspect of love; how it could become a threat. Hermia could potentially face social ruin if she is not careful.
 
- "Methought a serpent eat my heart away/And you sat smiling at his cruel pray." - Hermia
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Supernatural forces, Transformation
  - Characters: Hermia (sp), Lysander
  - Analysis: This quote is highly **symbolic** and **foreshadows** a lot of occurrences in the play. Hermia dreams of a snake, which is a symbol of betrayal, stealing her heart which is a symbol of love. Overall, this quote comments on how the her 'love' has been stolen by magic. 

### Act 3

- "There are things in this comedy of Pyramus and Thisbe that will never please" - Bottom
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Theatre, Play Within Play
  - Characters: Bottom (sp)
  - Analysis: Here we see the true magic of theater - the audience laughs at Bottom for his dimwittedness (as a person not an actor). He is incredibly simpleminded and literal about the play and does not trust the audience to find any hidden meanings in their act. 

- "What hempen homespuns have we swaggering here?" - Puck
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Play Within Play
  - Characters: Puck (sp)
  - Analysis: This is probably one of the most known quotes in the play. The use of **alliteration** with 'h sound' suggests the comical insult. The hempen homespuns is a reference to a poor-quality cloth using rough hemp fibers that is spun at home. It shows how unsophisticated the Mechanicals are. 

- "O Bottom, thou art changed/What do I see on thee?
What do you see?/You see an ass head of your own, do you?" - Snout/Bottom
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Play Within Play, Supernatural forces, Transformation, Appearance vs Reality
  - Characters: Snout (sp), Bottom (sp), Puck
  - Analysis: We see how Puck's 'play' blurs the line between fiction and reality. Wild things happen that the laborers can't control or comprehend, and this leads to the comic relief of the play. We also see Bottom's utterly comical **irony**.

- "Out of this wood do not desire to go. Thou shalt remain here, whether thou wilt or no.  Tie up my lover's tongue" - Titania
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Order vs Disorder, Supernatural forces, Appearance vs Reality
  - Characters: Titania (sp), Bottom
  - Analysis: This quote shows Titania's foolishness due to the potion. It reveals yet another less-pretty side effect of love - jealousy. Under the impact of the potion, Titania tries to control Bottom.

- "Methinks mistress, you should have little reason for that. And yet to say the truth, reason and love keep little company nowadays" - Bottom
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Order vs Disorder, Supernatural forces, Transformation, Appearance vs Reality
  - Characters: Bottom (sp), Titania
  - Analysis: Even the self-centered Bottom realizes that Titania's love makes no sense, but there seems to be a glimmer of hope. After all, love is irrational…

- "If thou hast slain Lysander in his sleep. Bring o'ver shoes in blood, plunge in deep. And kill me too." - Hermia
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love
  - Characters: Hermia (sp), Demetrius
  - Analysis: This quote reveals the depth of Hermia's love for Lysander - she'd rather die than be separated from him. This mirrors the Thisbe and Pyramus in the Mechanicals' play.

- "Lord, what fools these mortals be!" - Puck
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Supernatural forces, Order vs Disorder, Reversal
  - Characters: Puck (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote is quite **ironic** because Oberon and Titania have also been fools, but they are not mortals. It shows how the whole situation is all fun and games to Puck, revealing how the irrational nature of love makes it a show for spectators. 

- "And those things do best please me...That befall preposterously." - Puck
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Supernatural forces, Order vs Disorder, Reversal
  - Characters: Puck (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote reveals a stark difference between Puck and Oberon. Puck enjoys the mischief he has caused, while Oberon seems to sort of resent interfering with the lovers in the first place.

- "I am not yet so low But that my nails can reach unto thine eyes...You juggler! You canker-blossom! You thief of love!" - Hermia
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Conflict
  - Characters: Hermia (sp), Helena
  - Analysis: This quote reveals yet another darker side of love - about how it can expose a person's insecurities. Fear begins to eat Hermia about how she looks and causes a strife between her and her childhood friend Helena. 

- "When thou wak'st thou tak'st / True delight in the sight / of thy former lover's eye" - Puck
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Supernatural forces, Reversal
  - Characters: Puck (sp), Lysander
  - Analysis: Once again, the lovers' lives have been written by the fairies. Note that this reversal continually happens at night as they sleep (a dream?)

### Act 4

- "And now I have the boy, I will undo this hateful imperfection of her eyes." - Oberon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Reversal, Supernatural
  - Characters: Oberon (sp), Titania
  - Analysis: This quote shows how true to his words, Oberon asserts his power and then undoes the spell.

- "…but by some power it did happen—my love for Hermia melted away like snow" - Demetrius
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Transformation, Supernatural forces, Magic
  - Characters: Demetrius (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote is **symbolic** - the 'some power' is actually the potion. It shows how Demetrius is under the love potion - and only then the lover tangle is solved. 
 
- "Methought I was—there is no man can tell what." - Bottom
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Reversal
  - Characters: Bottom (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote shows **parallelism** between love and dreams - there is nothing to understand in either of them. 

### Act 5

- "*A tedious brief scene of young Pyramus and his love Thisbe. Very tragical mirth* “Merry” and “tragical?” “Tedious” and “brief?" - Theseus
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Play within Play
  - Characters: Theseus (sp)
  - Analysis: The Mechanicals are unintentionally funny to their audience (like the lovers were to the fairies)
 
- "For never anything can be amiss/When simpleness and duty tender it." - Theseus
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Play within Play
  - Characters: Theseus (sp)
  - Analysis: The Mechanicals fear that the audience will take their play literally - Theseus understands the underlying intentions and implies that life is full of plays within plays.

- "This is the silliest stuff that ever I heard." - Theseus
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Love, Play within Play
  - Characters: Hippolyta (sp)
  - Analysis: The play mirrors Lysander and Hermia's situation at the beginning very closely, hence indicating that love makes everyone silly.

- "No epilogue, I pray you. For your play needs no excuse" - Theseus
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Dreams, Play within Play
  - Characters: Theseus (sp)
  - Analysis: The epilogue would destroy the play by revealing the unreality, just like explaining a dream would destroy the irrationality.
 
- "If we shadows have offended/Think but this, and all is mended—/That you have but slumbered here/While these visions did appear." - Puck
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Dreams, Play within Play, Supernatural forces
  - Characters: Puck (sp)
  - Analysis: Puck says to think of the play as a false reality without any power to offend. Then, how much of what the audience has witnessed was actually real?
