## Themes

### Love

* The play is about love, but overall, it is a comedy since the audience is encouraged to laugh at the lovers’ trials and tribulations.
* Power and passion of love can make people blind and foolish, throw nature into turmoil, destroy friendships, etc.
* However, the ending reaffirms love’s importance, relevance, and beauty
* **Quotes**: “The course of true love never did run smoothly.” (I.I) - Lysander

### Dreams

* The word ‘dream’ is used to describe everyone’s experience in the forest$\implies$used because otherwise they wouldn’t be able to comprehend the bizarre and irrational things they experienced
* Bottom and the lovers allow the experiences to exist as they are without clarification
* Play seems to suggest that dreams and imagination are useful as reason and could create truths that go past the limits of reasoning.
* A fantastical world was in their ‘dreams’, with fairies and magic
* **Quotes**: “I have had a dream, past the wit of man to say what dream it was. Man is but an ass if he go about to expound this dream.” (IV.I) - Bottom

### Men and Women

* Echoes across both fairy and mortal worlds, as both show attempts to control women
* Even one of the play’s happier relationships was built upon a man asserting power over a woman$\implies$Theseus won Hippolyta’s love by defeating her in battle.
* Though the play ends happily, all displays of love show the women accepting a subservient role to their husbands$\implies$not accepted by modern-day audiences, but was generally accepted by an Elizabethan audience.
* **Quotes**: “But earthlier happy is the rose distilled - Than that which, withering on the virgin thorn - Grow, lives, and dies in single blessedness.” (I.I) - Theseus
