## Symbolism

### **Music**

* Soyinka describes the European music being played during the play as that of poor quality, and weak
  * Can allude to how British colonialism in the play is seen as rather out of place and inappropriate in Nigeria
* In contrast, the drumming and singing from the Yoruba people is strong and constant
  * Refers to how the native music, as well as the local culture, is what is appropriate and welcomed in Nigeria
* The drumming heard when Elesin’s suicide is about to take place is loud
  * Strength and continuity of the music shows the need for the event to take place


  * Underscores the sacredness and solemnity of the ritual
* British characters often dismiss or misunderstand the significance of Yoruba music, showing themes of cultural superiority and colonialism 


### **Chains**

* Elesin is chained and locked in a cellar that once held slaves
  * Shows the dehumanization of African people by the British, where they are still viewed as inhumane 
* British colonial rulers in Nigeria attempt to restrict the Yoruba people with chains
  * Shows how colonialism removes any traces of tradition and culture in occupied lands
* Elesin strangles himself at the end of the play with his chains
  * A reference to how colonialism has claimed the lives of multiple people
  * Chains can also show the inevitability of fate and death, as Elesin still managed to complete his duty of dying 
* However, breaking free of chains in the play can also refer to freedom
