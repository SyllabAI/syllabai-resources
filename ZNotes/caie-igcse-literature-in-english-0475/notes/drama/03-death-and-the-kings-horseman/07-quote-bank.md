## Quote Bank

For your IGCSE English Literature course, it is recommended to memorize some quotes from the texts you have studied along with the specific literary devices used in each quote to create a certain effect (authorial choice). The following is a list (non-exhaustive) of quotes you may consider studying.

### **Scene 1**

- "This market is my roost" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Women, Marketplace
  - Characters: Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote shows how Elesin loves life...the market acts as a **metaphor** for the centre of world and women symbolise the creation of life

- "They love to spoil you but beware. The hands of women also weaken the unwary." - Praise Singer
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Women, Circle of Life, Difficulties of a hedonistic love of life
  - Characters: Praise Singer (sp), Elesin 
  - Analysis: The continuous warnings **foreshadow** the ultimate tragic flaw - the market women and the Praise singer are a reminder that Elesin needs to cross over, but he is too attached to let them go.

- "I am a chicken with a hundred mothers…a monarch whose palace is built with tenderness and beauty" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Earthly power, Circle of Life  
  - Characters: Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote uses **olfactory imagery** to show how Elesin is a very sensual man. This is highly paradoxical and raises the question of how his death will occur. 
 
- "The world sip its honey from your lips" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Significant individual  
  - Characters: Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote is highly **metaphorical** because Elesin seems to be so sure that all his deeds were good.
 
- "I unrolled my mat for him [Not I bird] to see" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Life and death, Yoruba proverbs, Not-I-Bird  
  - Characters: Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: Elesin claims boastfully that he is ready to die; according to him, everyone else was afraid of Not-I bird, but this raises the question of whether he is really ready.
 
- "For a while we truly feared our hands had wrenched the world adrift in emptiness" - Women
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Transition and connection between worlds  
  - Characters: Women (sp), Elesin
  - Analysis: The little joke that Elesin makes shows the patriarchal nature of the society, and the belief in culture and traditions.
  
- "Elesin: (exasperated) Must you be so blunt? (Recovers)" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: The difficulties of a hedonistic love of life, Sacrifice  
  - Characters: Elesin (sp), Iyaloja
  - Analysis: The **stage directions** show how Elesin is not as keen on dying as he pretended to be earlier. His reactions show that he is more interested in the pleasures of life than an honourable death.
  
### Scene 2

- "Mista Pirikin...Come on Amusa, you don't believe in all this nonsense do you? I thought you were a good Moslem" - Simon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism, Spirituality and politics, Egungun
  - Characters: Simon (sp), Amusa, Jane
  - Analysis: This quote uses **caesura** to indicate shock - Amusa has not forgotten the power of the Egungun. It is also suggestive of how colonisers have political power but cannot replace the local culture.

  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism, Spirituality and politics, Egungun
  - Characters: Simon (sp), Amusa, Jane
  - Analysis: This quote uses **caesura** to indicate shock - Amusa has not forgotten the power of the Egungun. It is also suggestive of how colonisers have political power but cannot replace the local culture.

- "How can man talk against death to person in uniform of death?" - Amusa
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Life and death, History and culture, Egungun
  - Characters: Amusa (sp), Simon, Jane
  - Analysis: This quote uses **circumloco speech** to indicate the importance of the Egungun. However, we see Simon being very exploitative and disrespectful.

- "Jane: Did I hear you say commit death? Simon: Obviously he means murder" - Jane/Simon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Life and death, Colonialism
  - Characters: Simon, Jane
  - Analysis: The quote suggests that Simon jumps to the conclusion that it must be a ritual murder...idea of suicide is inconceivable to them.

- "It's only two years since your conversion. Don't tell me all that holy water nonsense wiped your tribal memory." - Simon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Spirituality and politics, History and culture
  - Characters: Simon, Joseph
  - Analysis: The quote suggests how Simon rubs it on Joseph that he is not truly Christian. For Simon, all religion is silly, and he is only interested in Christianity as it is a way to control the natives.

- "Jane: Simon, it's obvious we have to miss the ball Simon: Nonsense." - Jane/Simon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism, Duty
  - Characters: Simon, Jane
  - Analysis: This is a typical scenario of the white man's burden. He has to prove his supremacy even if it means jeopardizing his own desire - but Simon doesn't seem to accept that.
  
- "Official business you white man's eunuch?" - Women
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism, Duty, Women and power
  - Characters: Simon, Jane
  - Analysis: The Women insult Amusa because he doesn't seem to fit in on either side. The Women are able to regain agency under colonization by laughing at the colonisers.
 
- "Amusa: The chief who call himself Elesin Oba...Woman: You ignorant man. It is not he who calls himself Elesin Oba, it is his blood that says it." - Women/Amusa
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: The significant individual, Women and power, Honour, History and culture
  - Characters: Amusa, Women
  - Analysis: Women succeed to protect Elesin from arrest...they demonstrate power by invoking custom. This **foreshadows** that Olunde will come suggesting the possibility die...blood begets blood.
  
- "Amusa (bewildered, looks at Women and at the entrance to the hut) Iyaloja, is it wedding you call dis kin' ting?" - Amusa
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonisation, History and culture, Drums, Music and Dance
  - Characters: Amusa (sp), Iyaloja, Women
  - Analysis: This quote shows how Amusa has become more Western culturally than any other characters...he cannot identify the drumbeats anymore (the reason why he thoroughly insulted).
 
- "No no Iyaloja, leave us to deal with him [put on a play-act in a British accent]" - Girls
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Women and power, Colonialism
  - Characters: Girls, Women, Amusa
  - Analysis: The tone of mockery shows that the young girls are future of the Yoruba but also tune into how the English behave...their understanding gives them an edge.
 
- "This [market place] is where I have chosen to do my leave-taking, in this heart of life…where I have known love and laughter…in the market, nothing ever cloys" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Market place, The difficulties of a hedonistic love of life, The significant individual, Duty and will
  - Characters: Elesin (sp), Praise Singer, Women
  - Analysis: Elesin wishes to die in the centre of attention, in the middle of what constitutes his earthly existence. He turns the death into a communal act rather than an individual one, as if he wants everyone to note how his dying is a tribal affair benefiting them all.
 
- "It takes an Elesin to die the death of death…Only Elesin… dies the unknowable death of death" - Iyaloja
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Life and death, Duty and Will
  - Characters: Iyaloja (sp), Elesin
  - Analysis: In theory, Elesin knows where he is going, but really, they all are curious about what death entails.
  
### Scene 4

- "The band plays 'Rule Britannia' badly, beginning long before he [prince] is visible…the orchestra's waltz rendition is not of the highest musical standard" - Simon
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism, Power and corruption, Spirituality and Politics, Music and Dance
  - Characters: Simon (sp), Amusa, Jane
  - Analysis: This quote uses **caesura** to indicate shock - Amusa has not forgotten the power of the Egungun. It is also suggestive of how colonisers have political power but cannot replace the local culture. 
  
- "Resident:  If we all let these little things slip past us where would the empire be eh?...Simon: Sleeping peacefully at home I bet"
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism, Power and corruption, Duty and Will
  - Characters: Resident, Simon
  - Analysis: This quote shows how Elesin's suicide is a mere annoyance to Simon - he only wants to have a good time and receive recognition but doesn't want to put in the effort. 

- "And that is the good cause for which you desecrate an ancestral mask?"
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Life and Death, Responsibility
  - Characters: Olunde (sp), Jane
  - Analysis: Olunde can reach across cultures, giving readers insight to Yoruba culture and traditions. 

- "Olunde: I find it rather inspiring. It is an affirmative commentary on life...Jane: Nonsense. Life should never be thrown deliberately away."
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Life and death, Sacrifice, Colonialism, Duty and Will
  - Characters: Olunde, Jane
  - Analysis: Olunde's insistence shows that he still adheres to Yoruba beliefs - it is acceptable to sacrifice one person for the greater good **(foreshadowing)** Olunde can make connections across cultures. 
  
- "...I don't want him to incur the enmity of our people. Especially over nothing"
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Duty and Will, Responsibility
  - Characters: Olunde (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote shows how Olunde is still connected to his roots...complex character, has acquired knowledge from the West to use it for his own culture. 
 
- "These natives put on a suit and they get high opinions of themselves" -Aide-de-camp
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Colonialism
  - Characters: Aide-de-camp (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote shows the belief that colonised people can never really enter the society of the colonisers. They are inferior and will always be. 
  
- "I have no father, eater of leftovers" - Olunde
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Duty and Will, The significant individual
  - Characters: Olunde (sp), Elesin
  - Analysis: This quote shows how Elesin's inability to die has made him unimportant and dishonourable (it is a callback to women's insults).
 
### Scene 5

- "You did not save my life District Officer. You destroyed it…The end of the night's work is not over" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Duty and Will, Responsibility
  - Characters: Simon, Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote illustrates how both Elesin and Simon have two very different conceptions of duty. Elesin tries to explain his duty, but Pilkings insists he is doing his duty. Elesin's inability to perform his duty has far-reaching consequences. 
 
- "Did you think it all out before, this plan to push our world from its course and sever the cord that links us to the great origin?" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Spirituality and Politics, History and circle, Earthly power
  - Characters: Simon, Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote refers to the bigger meaning of the whole situation - Olunde and Elesin's bond was severed, Elesin couldn't perform his duty, and hence, he was reduced to rubble. All in all, this **foreshadows** how Olunde will do something to take away Elesin's shame. 
 
- "My young bride…you sit and sob in your silent heart but say nothing to all this" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Women and power, Responsibility, Earthly power
  - Characters: Elesin (sp), Bride
  - Analysis: A **rhetorical question** is posed here - was The Bride ever given a chance to voice her opinions? We are left to wonder about the African culture - it is a society that values women of power but also raises women who just do their duty quietly.
 
- "For I confess to you…my weakness came not merely from the abomination of the white man…there was also a weight of longing on my earth-held limbs" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Duty and Will, Significant individual, Responsibility, Circle of Life
  - Characters: Elesin (sp)
  - Analysis: This quote shows Elesin admitting that colonialism acts as a catalyst. It is quite **paradoxical** because blame is a strange peace, and Elesin here seems to have a spiritual enlightenment. However, he is still too attached to earthly pleasures to let go (really, he is to blame, not others).
 
- "That is my wife sitting down there. You notice how still and silent she sits?" - Elesin
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Women and Power
  - Characters: Elesin (sp), Jane
  - Analysis: This quote illustrates the underlying concept of patriarchy - Jane has no right to question these things and make Elesin understand what is going on.
 
- "Not yet. It drags behind me on the slow, weary feet of women. Slow as it is, Elesin, it has long overtaken you." - Iyaloja
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Women and Power, Duty and Will
  - Characters: Iyaloja (sp), Elesin
  - Analysis: The women coming over with Olunde's body suggests how the women make sure the men follow the rules; they carry the burden when men mess up.
 
- "Is it the parent shoot which withers to give sap to the younger or, does your wisdom see it running the other way?" - Iyaloja
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Responsibility, Yoruba proverbs, Duty, and will
  - Characters: Iyaloja (sp), Elesin
  - Analysis: This quote suggests a connection to Olunde's death. There is a reversal of roles—Olunde came to perform the rites for Elesin, not to have his father perform them on him.
 
- "The gods demanded only the old expired plantain but you cut down the sap-laden shoot to feed your pride." - Iyaloja
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Duty and Will, Colonialism
  - Characters: Iyaloja (sp), Pilkings
  - Analysis: This is a reference to how Iyaloja's wrath turns to Simon - he wore clothes worn by the dead and sought to control both life and death.
 
- "Now forget the dead, forget even the living. Turn your mind only to the unborn." - Iyaloja
  - Themes, Motifs, Symbols: Responsibility, Circle of Life
  - Characters: Iyaloja (sp), Elesin
  - Analysis: This quote shows how society needs their children to save them. In the long run, they will need to deal with the consequences of Elesin's failure and colonialism.
