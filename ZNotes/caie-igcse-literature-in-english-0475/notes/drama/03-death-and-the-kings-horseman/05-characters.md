## Characters

### 1. **Elesin Oba**

* Protagonist of the play, father of Olunde 
* King’s horseman that is duty-bound to perform a ritualistic suicide 
* Does as he pleases and is slightly selfish and arrogant
  * Marries Iyaloja’s soon to be daughter-in-law a day before his death
  * Enjoys the attention he is given by the women of the market
* Loves life and is spiritual
  * Dances and sings with the women of the market
  * Is hesitant to leave earthly pleasures
  * Makes multiple comments about his love for women
* A traditionalist, who knows about his duty and does not actively attempt to evade it
* Highly emotional and dramatic in multiple scenes of the play
  * Shamed by Olunde’s disgust
  * Curses the British officers for their actions
  * Strangles himself when his son commits suicide
* Respected and honored by the women of the market
  * Granted his final wish of marrying the already engaged girl
  * Given fine clothes to be dressed in 


### 2. **Simon Pilkings**

* Portrayed as the antagonist of the play, as he attempts to prevent Elesin’s suicide
* A British district officer, married to Jane Pilkings
* Authoritative and condescending 
  * Job is to control the local Oyo area, and ensure laws are obeyed by the people
  * Represents the Christian religion with his job
  * Represents the British colonial rule within the play
* Arrogant and self-important 
  * Dismissive towards his wife, as well as rude
  * Primarily interested in meeting the Prince at the ball, rather than attending to pressing matters
* Disrespectful and apathetic towards local customs, or rather any non-Christian customs
  * Calls the natives “devious bastards”
  * Keeps referring to the Yoruba ritual as “nonsense” and “mumbo-jumbo”
  * Wears the local Egungun customs, despite being informed of their significance
  * Ignores Elesin’s pleas to keep Olunde in Nigeria, according to Yoruba tradition
* Stubborn with all he interacts with, such as when he insists on isolating Elesin, or dismisses local advice telling him not to charge Amusa with arresting Elesin




### 3. **Olunde Oba**

* Eldest son of Elesin Oba, therefore next in line to become the King’s horseman
* Trained as a doctor in England with Simon Pilkings’ help
* Both a traditionalist and a modernist
  * **Traditionalist**: Says that time away has cemented his love for his culture, proud of his father’s impending sacrifice, sacrifices himself for the village 
  * **Modernist**: Friends with the Pilkings’, travels to England for his education, questions certain aspects of Yoruba customs
* Wise and honorable
  * Reasons with Jane by providing examples of similarities between English and Yoruba traditions
  * A critical thinker, who uses a British navel captain’s sacrifices as an example to Jane
* Loyal to his duties as the heir to the title of the King’s horseman
  * Travels to Nigeria once he hears of his father’s impending sacrificial suicide
  * Disowns his father when he learns he has failed to complete the Yoruba custom
  * Commits the ritualistic suicide in place of his father


### 4. **Iyaloja**

* A mother figure to the women of the market, and is therefore labelled as the “mother of the market”
* Nurturing and kind to her people
  * Helps Elesin prepare for his sacrifice, almost like a guide
  * Asks the women to dress Elesin in fine clothes
* Presented as selfless and loyal to her community
  * Permits Elesin to marry her son’s fiancée, though she knows her son will not be able to do the same once Elesin dies
  * Personal sacrifice is emphasized heavily by Soyinka in the play
  * States that her reason for this action is to not disrupt the upcoming events (Elesin’s suicide), showing her loyalty to the Yoruba community
* Strict and resilient, and is furious with Elesin for failing his community
  * Tells him that he has disrupted the order of the universe
  * Emphasizes the above by asking Elesin “Is it the parent shoot which withers to give sap to the younger or, does your wisdom see it running the other way?”
* Also admonishes Simon Pilkings for preventing Elesin’s ritual, and concludes the play
  * Shows her significance as an omnipresent character in the play




### 5. **Jane Pilkings**

* Wife to Simon Pilkings
* More thoughtful and sympathetic than her husband
  * Worried about their servant, Joseph, taking offense to Simon’s harsh words
  * Questions the Egungun costumes when noticing Amusa’s reaction to them
  * Tells Simon that the word “bastard” has more serious connotations in Nigeria, than in England
* Emotional and difficult to reason with
  * Becomes visibly distressed and confused when Elesin is arrested by British authorities
  * Shows empathy towards Iyaloja and the Yoruba community, and feels guilt for disrupting and interfering in their customs
  * Becomes emotional and stubborn when talking with Olunde about his father’s impending duty to the Yoruba people
* Acts as a warning voice in the play, almost like a foreshadowing element
  * Attempts to stop Simon from his impulsive actions and tells him to first talk to Elesin before his arrest
  * Asks Simon to attend to the matters at hand, rather than stay at the party
