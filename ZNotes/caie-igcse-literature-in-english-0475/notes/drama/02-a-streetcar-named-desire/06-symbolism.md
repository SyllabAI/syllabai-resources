## Symbolism

### **Streetcar**

* Literal: The method of transport used by Blanche to go to New Orleans
* Symbolic: Can represent the power of desire, almost like a driving force behind actions
* Can refer to the inevitable movement of time and the loss of innocence
* Can also be seen as a vehicle of progress and change, that threatens Blanche's traditional values
* Represents Blanche’s pursuit of desires and pleasures, which eventually lead to her mental and emotional destruction


### **Polka**

* Associated with Blanche’s young husband
* Played when Blanche remembers the past: can show her retreatment into the past
* Represents a world of nostalgia and dreams for her
* A reminder of happier times and a way to momentarily forget her troubles
* Often associated with tradition and old-fashioned values, reflects Blanche's longing for the past and her inability to adapt to the present in New Orleans
* Contrasts sharply with the tense and often dramatic scenes unfolding in the play



### **Bathing**

* A form of escaping reality for Blanche, but is temporary
* Can act as a form of the Fountain of Youth, which Blanche may use to convince herself of her supposed youthfulness 
* Often associated with both physical and spiritual cleansing: Blanche may bathe often to ‘cleanse’ herself of the past, as well as feelings of guilt and shame
* Bathing scenes also highlight Blanche's vulnerability and the fragility of her mental state, as it often occurs at pivotal moments in the play
* Can also carry undertones of desire and sensuality, symbolizing her attempts to allure and seduce



### **Paper Lantern**

* Acts as Blanche’s attempt to mask both the past and present, yet is still temporary as it can be ripped off
* Serves as a metaphor for the gradual unraveling of her carefully constructed facade
* A symbol of how Blanche creates illusions about her past and identity, as paper lanterns give out a soft light that creates a dreamlike atmosphere
* Symbolizes the shattering of Blanche’s facade when Stanley destroys it towards the end of the play
* Mirrors Blanche’s fragility as it can be damaged and torn, like her visage



### **Alcohol**

* Used by Blanche as a form of escape from reality, to numb herself from the harsh nature of her reality$\implies$ties into bathing
* Blanche often drinks cocktails and whiskey, showing her love for refined tastes, as well as a need to maintain a facade of delicacy and sophistication
* Stanley’s beer drinking shows a rough and blunt demeanor
* Also serves as a representation of destructive forces within the play, as it contributes to Blanche’s downfall and also fuels Stanley’s aggressiveness
* Can also represent the median between truth and deception, as the characters often drink to either avoid truth (Blanche) or manipulate (Stanley)
