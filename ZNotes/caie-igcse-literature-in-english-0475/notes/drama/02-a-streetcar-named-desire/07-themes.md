## Themes

### **Power of Desire**

* Blanche views herself as an **object of male desire**
  * Uses flirtation to navigate interactions \[ex. Mitch and the Young Man\]
  * Comes from a fear of losing her identity, after the revelation of her husband’s homosexuality
* Stella is drawn towards Stanley, and vice-verse
  * **Stella**: Drawn to Stanley’s brute, animal-like demeanor, pulling her from Belle Reve
  * **Stanley**: Drawn to Stella’s domestic and feminine persona
* Blanche’s pursuit of desire lead to the loss of her past, while Stanley’s pursuit leads to tragic outcomes
* Desire is linked to destruction; examples include the play’s violent relationships (Eunice and Steve, Stanley and Stella) and the loss of Belle Reve
* Stanley’s brute persona asserts itself over both Stella and Blanche, culminating in Blanche’s assault
* Blanche’s husband’s “unacceptable” homosexuality leads to his suicide
* **Quotes**:
  * Scene 4: “There are things that happen between a man and woman in the dark - that sort of make everything else seem - unimportant.” - Stella
  * Scene 6: “It isn’t enough to be soft. You’ve got to be soft and attractive. And I’m fading now!” - Blanche


### **Fantasy and Delusion**

* Blanche’s desires have led her down paths of **promiscuity** and **alcoholism**
  * By coming to stay with her sister, she has reached the end of the line
  * Her desire to escape causes her to lose touch with the world around her
  * By the end, she can no longer distinguish between fantasy and reality
* Blanche hopes to hide her troubled self by maintaining an illusory exterior
  * She only appears in semi-darkness and shadows, keeping herself out of the harsh glare of reality
  * By using **satin robes** and **paper lanterns**, she hopes to keep up her innocent image, rather than face her tainted past and destitute present
* **Paper Moon**: A song about a make-believe world that becomes reality with love
  * Blanche sings this while bathing, while her past is exposed to Stella by Stanley
  * Her make-believe world however, doesn’t overtake reality, and her fantasy crumbles
* Blanche creates a facade of refinement and elegance, often retreating into illusions of her past glory as a Southern belle$\implies$seen by her euphemisms and form of speech
  * Represented by her belief in the kindness of strangers, and her romantic yearning for protection and security
* However, the presence and characters of Stella and Stanley contrast strongly with Blanche’s delusions, adding another layer to her make-believe world and showcasing how her mental state has resulted in such fantasies
* **Quotes**:
  * Scene 6: “We are going to pretend that we are sitting in a little artists’ cafe on the Left Bank in Paris!” - Blanche
  * Scene 7: “It’s only a paper moon, just as phony as it can be - but it won’t be make-believe if you believed in me!” - Blanche



## **Femininity vs. Masculinity**

### **Femininity**:

* Both Stella and Blanche define themselves in terms of the men in their lives
* They see relationships with men as the path to happiness and fulfillment
* **Blanche**:
  * Embodies the archetype of the **Southern belle**, with her refined accent, elegant fashion, and flirtatious mannerisms
  * Presents herself as delicate, as she believes this makes her most attractive to men
  * Insists that Stella gets away from Stanley, but her alternate solution still depends on another man
  * Portrayed as highly sensitive and vulnerable, with frequent bouts of hysteria, anxiety, and despair$\implies$a classic representation of women in literature
  * By using charm, flirtation, and wit to navigate her interactions with men, she showcases the survival mechanisms and societal expectations of women back then
* **Stella**:
  * Femininity is based on **reality**, unlike Blanche
  * Her pregnancy shows the physical, unmasked version of femininity
  * Takes on the traditional domestic work of women during that time period, in which she maintains the household, cooks meals, and more
  * Femininity is sometimes expressed through her sexuality and desire for Stanley, which is seen in their intensely passionate relationship
  * Her submissiveness towards Stanley is also a representation of past societal expectations
  * Often forgives Stanley’s outbursts and reconciles with him after conflicts, reflecting a nurturing and forgiving aspect of femininity
* **Quotes**:
  * Scene 1: “I can't stand a naked light bulb, any more than I can a rude remark or a vulgar action." - Blanche
  * Scene 4: “You didn't know Blanche as a girl. Nobody, nobody was as tender and trusting as she was.” - Stella

### **Masculinity**:

* Linked to the idea of lust and a brute, aggressive form of force
* The play showcases its implications in personal relationships and societal dynamics
* **Stanley**:
  * Physical appearance itself implies his masculinity, with his bright clothing and body
  * Asserts dominance through loud actions and violence, with the women of the play
  * Asserts his masculinity both physically \[calling out for Stella\] and psychologically \[airing Blanche’s dirty laundry\]
  * Masculinity asserts itself in response to femininity \[coming from Stella\]
  * Embodies traditional masculine roles and expectations of the time period, as he is the provider and protector of his household
  * His actions often reflect his desire to maintain control and power over others, as he challenges authority figures and feels threatened by Blanche’s influence over his wife
* **Mitch**:
  * Showcases masculinity as a trait of comfort and refuge, and is still exploring it in the play
  * His longing for a meaningful connection with Blanche demonstrates a different aspect of masculinity
  * Masculinity is more **emotionally driven**, contrasting heavily with Stanley
  * Still finds his power through physical assertion (ex. almost assaulting Blanche)
  * Feels betrayed and emasculated by Blanche’s betrayal, showing the fragility of traditional masculine ideals
  * His emotional depth, in contrast to Stanley, shows another feature of masculinity that is often suppressed or overshadowed by more aggressive traits
* **Quotes**:
  * Scene 8: "I am the king around here, so don’t forget it!" - Stanley
  * Scene 9: "You need somebody. And I need somebody, too. Could it be – you and me, Blanche?" - Mitch
