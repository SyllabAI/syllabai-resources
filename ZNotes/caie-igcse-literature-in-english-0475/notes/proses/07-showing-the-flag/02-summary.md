## Summary

The narrative begins at an English harbor where Philip’s mother, **Gwen**, and her stoic friend, Miss Pym, see him off. Gwen is quietly devastated by her son’s departure, weeping as the ship departs, though her friend reminds her that sending young children away is the accepted English "custom" that requires "renunciation". Philip, waving back from the upper deck, misinterprets his mother’s forced laugh-crying facade as a sign of emotional indifference

Once alone at sea, Philip checks his small brass tin containing the paper Union Jack flag. When he lifts it out, a gust of wind instantly blows the flag into the ocean. Left with "no Union Jack," Philip experiences an existential crisis, convincing himself that his mother hates him and actively wanted him to get lost or cast away. He becomes Hyper-vigilant, successfully guarding his immense suitcases against thieves on the French train while plotting a fantasy of independent survival in France. Exhausted and hungry, Philip finally opens the parcel of sandwiches his mother packed for him. Hidden deep inside the package, he finds a **spare paper Union Jack** and a deeply affectionate note reading, *"Oh Philip, my darling, don't hate me for fussing, but I do so love you."* The narrative closes on his sudden emotional relief and the humbling realization that his mother had anticipated his needs and loved him deeply all along.
