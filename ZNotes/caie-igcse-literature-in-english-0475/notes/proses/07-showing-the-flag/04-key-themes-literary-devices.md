## **Key Themes & Literary Devices**

* **Childhood Anxiety and the Culture of Reserve:** The text critiques how the traditional English middle-class habit of keeping "private their secret thoughts" creates massive emotional barriers. Because adults hide their pain behind a stoic facade, children grow up misinterpreting parental restraint as genuine apathy or rejection.

* **Maternal Love vs. Perceived Abandonment:** Explores the tragic disconnect between a parent’s protective sacrifices and a child's internal insecurity. The story proves that love is often expressed through unseen, quiet actions (such as expert packing) rather than overt emotional displays.

* **Third-Person Limited Narration & Stream of Consciousness:** Gardam fluidly shifts the narrative focalization into Philip's immediate stream of consciousness. The narrator mimics a child’s erratic mental patterns—oscillating rapidly between mundane sensory distractions (seagulls, hunger) and crushing existential dread.

* **Situational Irony:** The foundational irony relies on Philip labeling his mother as "stupid" and "wicked" for not providing a backup flag, only for the climax to reveal that her meticulous maternal love had completely neutralized his mistake before he even made it.
