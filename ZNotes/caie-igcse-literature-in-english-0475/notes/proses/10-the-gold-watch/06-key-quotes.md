## Key Quotes 

* ***‘Mr. Sharma,’ Mr. Acton said... ‘I have brought a special gift for you from London... A gold watch.’ And he smiled with the grand, paternalistic benevolence of the master conferring a favor on a loyal slave."***

  * Captures the deep irony and paternalistic dynamic of the moment. The formal presentation hides the underlying cruelty of the dismissal under the guise of an executive reward, exploiting the submissive colonial hierarchy.

* ***"He looked at it. It was a beautiful watch, with a gold case... But it was a token of his retirement, the instrument of his execution... Five years before his time! He was being thrown on the scrap-heap."***

  *  Highlights Srijut Sharma's sudden realization of his predicament. The watch transitions instantly from a superficial object of pride to a symbol of catastrophic economic loss and life-altering eviction from his life's routine.

* **"*He had given twenty-five years of his life to this firm... And now they were casting him off like an old shoe... The watch was ticking, marking away the moments of his remaining days in the office."***

  *  Encapsulates the core critique of the story: corporate and colonial systems reduce human lives and decades of absolute dedication to empty, replaceable units of time, leaving the worker economically vulnerable.
