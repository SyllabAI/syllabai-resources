## **Key Themes**

1. **Fear and the Fragility of Order:** The narrative illustrates how quickly social structures can erode. Fear transforms daily life into a quiet battleground long before physical violence arrives.

2. **Survival, Gender, and Self-Reliance:** Traditional gender roles position men as protectors, yet Mrs. Burridge realizes Frank cannot save her. True survival relies on her own practical, domestic skills, emotional endurance, and willingness to take up arms.

3. **Isolation and Communication Breakdown:** A profound lack of real communication exists between Mrs. Burridge and Frank, as well as between her and the townspeople. This emotional isolation heightens her vulnerability and drives her inward.

4. **Illusion vs. Reality:** Atwood leaves the nature of "it" ambiguous. The tension lies in whether the impending disaster is a tangible external crisis or an internal manifestation of hyper-vigilance and aging.

## **Literary Devices & Symbolism**

* ## **Symbolism:**

  * **Green Tomatoes & Pickles:** Symbolize preservation, continuity, and human resourcefulness in the face of scarcity. Canning food is an attempt to store security for an uncertain future.

  * **The Shotgun:** Symbolizes self-defense, power, and the loss of domestic peace. It represents her transition from passive homemaker to active survivor.

  * **Smoke on the Horizon:** Symbolizes looming destruction, chaos, and the approaching catastrophe.

  * **The Calendar:** Symbolizes the relentless passage of time, routine, and the inevitability of change and mortality.

  * **The Hole in the Stairs:** Symbolizes neglect, domestic decay, and her dependence on a husband who fails to address impending structural flaws.

* **Juxtaposition:** Contrast between domestic contentment (canning) and violent survival scenarios (holding a rifle).

* **Foreboding Tone:** Created through passive news media, subtle changes in community behavior, and cold internal monologues.
