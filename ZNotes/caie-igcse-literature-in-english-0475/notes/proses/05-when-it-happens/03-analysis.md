## Literary Analysis

* **Genre & Form:** A tense short story that merges **dystopian/post-apocalyptic fiction with psychological realism**. Rather than focusing on grand action or overt catastrophe, Atwood explores how geopolitical dread infiltrates ordinary, domestic spaces.

* **Narrative Perspective:** Third-person limited, closely aligned with Mrs. Burridge’s inner thoughts and anxieties. Atwood uses subtle stylistic shifts and short, blunt sentences to blur the boundary between present reality and hypothetical future scenarios, creating a disorienting effect for the reader.

* **Juxtaposition of Mundane and Catastrophic:** The core structural drive of the text relies on placing quiet, routine tasks (canning pickles, making lemon meringue pie) directly alongside cold, clinical preparations for violent survival (learning to shoot, stealing supplies).

* **Historical Grounding:** Written during the Cold War era (published in 1983), the text captures widespread fears of nuclear war, economic recession, and social vulnerability, reflecting how past traumas shape present paranoia.
