## Summary 

Set on a quiet farm, "When It Happens" follows Mrs. Burridge, a 51-year-old rural woman navigating her daily domestic routines—such as preserving green tomato pickles—while carrying a deep, quiet dread of an impending, unspecified societal collapse or apocalypse. Having lived through historical crises like the Great Depression and World War II, Mrs. Burridge reads subtle signs of breakdown in the world around her: rising prices, shortages at the grocery store, blank or non-committal news broadcasts, and an unspoken anxiety among local townspeople.

As the story progresses, the narrative seamlessly shifts between everyday reality and her internal, imagined scenarios of what will happen "when it comes". She realizes that her husband, Frank, views life through a simpler lens and will not be able to protect her when disaster strikes. In her vivid vision of the collapse, men come to mobilize Frank, leaving her alone. Left isolated, Mrs. Burridge methodically prepares: packing essential supplies, taking Frank’s shotgun, and stepping out onto the road alone to face approaching strangers. The story closes on an ambiguous note, blurring the line between external reality and psychological anticipation.
