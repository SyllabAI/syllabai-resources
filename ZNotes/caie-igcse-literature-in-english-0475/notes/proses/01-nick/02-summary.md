## Summary 

"Nick" is a Victorian moral tale written by Christina Rossetti. Set in a small village near Fairyland, the story follows Nick, a prosperous, healthy farmer who has everything he needs. Despite his good fortune, he suffers from deep discontent and bitter envy, obsessing over his neighbours' successes—such as Giles Hodge's fine cherry trees.

One day, while wishing ill upon Giles’s crop, Nick is visited by a fairy ("a little rosy woman"). She grants him a magical curse-like gift: **whatever Nick wishes to become, he will transform into, but he must remain in that shape for at least one full hour**.

Nick misuses this gift out of malice and spite, initiating a chaotic chain of shape-shifting transformations:

* **Sparrows:** To ruin Giles’s cherries. Giles shoots at them with a gun, leaving Nick limping and injured.

* **A Dog:** To attack a cat that targeted him as a bird. The townspeople think he is a "mad dog" and hunt him down.

* **A Stick / Bludgeon:** To escape the crowd. Instead of being useful, he is tossed into a fireplace.

* **Fire:** To avoid burning as wood. As fire, he nearly burns down a house, only to be doused with cold water until reduced to a weak blue flame.

* **A Rich Old Man:** Wishing for power and status, he becomes a wealthy elder. However, robbers break in that very night to murder the old man.

Having suffered physical pain, humiliation, and near-death in every single form, Nick finally realizes that his spite brings harm back onto himself. He wishes to be himself again, scares off the robbers, returns to his village, and makes amends to his neighbours. He learns his lesson and never wishes for anything again.
