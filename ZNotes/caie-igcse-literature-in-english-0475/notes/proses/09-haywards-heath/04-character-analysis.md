## **Character Analysis**

**Attila**

* **Role:** The central protagonist; a successful West African trauma doctor and consultant visiting England.

* **Traits:** Successful, observant, compassionate, patient, emotionally resilient, and dignified.

* **Development & Function:** Attila represents growth, resilience, and success despite the subtle racial biases of his student days. While his career trained him to treat physical trauma, visiting Rosie forces him to confront an incurable psychological loss. His arc culminates in selflessness: he prioritizes Rosie’s happiness over his own desire for emotional validation.

**Rosie**

* **Role:** Attila's former university lover, now living in a care home in Haywards Heath.

* **Traits:** Formerly playful, witty, and strong-willed; currently vulnerable, forgetful, and living in a fragmented reality.

* **Development & Function:** Rosie serves as the emotional anchor of the narrative and a symbol of human fragility. Her dementia dismantles the shared history between her and Attila, demonstrating how illness can strip away identity while leaving basic emotional needs—like warmth, dance, and affection—intact.

**The Young African Care Worker**

* **Role:** A carer at the retirement home who looks after Rosie.

* **Traits:** Gentle, patient, attentive, and kind.

* **Function:** Functions as a mirror to Attila’s younger self. Rosie mistakes him for Attila due to his skin tone and features. He provides the daily care and gentleness that Attila can no longer offer, symbolizing the passing of responsibility and allowing Attila to find peace.
