## Summary

Set during the Jim Crow era of racial segregation in America, "The Black Ball" follows John, an African American single father working as a janitor in a luxury white apartment building, where he lives in a small room above the garage with his young son. John works tirelessly—polishing brass, mopping floors, and sweeping—to maintain his job and secure a living for his son.

The story opens with John's son asking if he is "black," having been teased by an older neighborhood child. John gently counters this prejudice, reassuring his son that he is "brown" and, above all, an American. Later, while working, John is approached by a white union organizer with severe burn scars on his hands. The man describes how he was burned by a white supremacist mob for defending a Black man and urges John to join a multiracial workers' union to fight for economic rights and dignity. Suspicious and cautious of putting his job at risk, John accepts the organizer's card but remains hesitant.

During the afternoon, while John is actively working out front, a white boy takes the young child's toy ball and throws it through the window of Mr. Berry, the harsh white building manager. Mr. Berry immediately blames John's son and threatens John, warning him that one more mistake will put him "behind the black ball" (a threat of being fired). Realizing that individual deference and hard work will never protect him or his son from systemic prejudice, John looks at his sleeping child, reflects on the metaphor of the "black ball" game, and decides to retrieve the union card to attend the meeting.
