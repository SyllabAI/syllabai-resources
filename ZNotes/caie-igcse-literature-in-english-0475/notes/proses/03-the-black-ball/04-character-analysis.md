## Character Analysis

* **John (The Protagonist & Narrator):**

  * **Role:** A hardworking Black janitor and single father trying to protect his son while surviving under Jim Crow.

  * **Traits:** Cautious, observant, resilient, dignified, and protective.

  * **Development & Function:** Serves as the moral anchor of the story. Initially, he relies on compliance and stoicism to keep his job. His arc progresses from quiet individual endurance to an awakening of political consciousness when he realizes he must fight for a better future for his son.

* **John’s Son:**

  * **Role:** John's young, innocent four-year-old child.

  * **Traits:** Curious, trusting, playful, and observant.

  * **Function:** Represents childhood innocence on the precipice of racial awareness. His questions about skin color and his interaction with the toy ball prompt John to confront the harsh realities of societal prejudice.

* **Mr. Berry:**

  * **Role:** The white building manager and John's employer.

  * **Traits:** Authoritative, prejudiced, cynical, and demanding.

  * **Function:** Embodies systemic racial and economic exploitation. He values property and brass over human dignity and uses the threat of dismissal to keep Black workers subordinate.

* **The Union Organizer:**

  * **Role:** A white activist with scarred hands who tries to recruit John into a labor union.

  * **Traits:** Courageous, persistent, empathetic, and resilient.

  * **Function:** Acts as a catalyst for John’s political transformation, showing John that cross-racial labor solidarity is a viable path toward equality.
