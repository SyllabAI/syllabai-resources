## Key Quotes 

* ***"Brown's much nicer than white, isn't it, Daddy?" / "Some people think so. But American is better than both, son."***

  *  Demonstrates John's effort to protect his child's self-esteem and shield him from internalizing hatred, asserting their rightful place as equal citizens in society.

* ***"I didn't look at him. I didn't look any white man in the face unless I had to."***

  * Captures the conditioned self-preservation mechanisms Black individuals had to adopt under Jim Crow, highlighting the psychological toll of suppressing one's ego to avoid conflict. Reflecting on his survival strategy in a deeply prejudiced environment, John reveals his cautious approach to white authority:

* ***"Will I play with the black ball, Daddy?" / "In time, son... In time." He had already played with the ball; that he would discover later. He was learning the rules of the game already...***

  * At the conclusion of the story, as John reflects on his son's future and the unavoidable reality of racial prejudice, he observes. This serves as the central emotional and thematic climax. John realizes that his son cannot remain innocent forever and will inevitably have to navigate the oppressive "game" of societal racism, prompting John to finally take a stand by joining the union.
