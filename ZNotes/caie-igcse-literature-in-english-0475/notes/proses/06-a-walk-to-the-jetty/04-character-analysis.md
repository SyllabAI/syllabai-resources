## Character Analysis

**Annie John (The Protagonist)**

* **Role:** A seventeen-year-old Antiguan girl leaving her homeland.

* **Traits:** Hypersensitive, observant, emotionally conflicted, rebellious, and fiercely independent.

* **Development & Function:** Annie represents the painful transition from childhood to adulthood. She struggles with an intense **ambivalence**—a desire to escape her family's expectations while simultaneously mourning the loss of childhood intimacy. She recognizes that to become her own person, she must physically distance herself from her mother.

**Annie's Mother**

* **Role:** Annie’s mother and primary emotional anchor/foil.

* **Traits:** Protective, nurturing, traditional, and emotionally present.

* **Development & Function:** Represents both ultimate love and overwhelming constraint. Historically, she shared a near-symbiotic relationship with Annie, but as Annie hit adolescence, their bond fractured. During the walk, her tenderness serves as a reminder of the unconditional safety Annie is choosing to abandon.

**Annie's Father**

* **Role:** Annie’s elderly father.

* **Traits:** Quiet, steady, practical, and gentle.

* **Development & Function:** Serves as a grounding, quiet background presence during the journey. His advanced age highlights the generational gap and the passing of the old way of life on the island.
