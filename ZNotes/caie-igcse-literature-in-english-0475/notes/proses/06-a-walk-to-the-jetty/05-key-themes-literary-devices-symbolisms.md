## **Key Themes**

* **The Pain of Maturation and Independence:** True independence cannot be achieved without sacrifice. Growing up requires severing the safety of the maternal bond, which causes deep emotional grief.

* **Memory, Nostalgia, and Space:** Physical geography serves as a repository for memory. Every building, street, and corner in St. John's carries a specific emotional history that Annie must confront before leaving.

* **Colonial Migration and Displacement:** Leaving Antigua for England highlights the post-colonial condition of displacement—moving to a foreign country, while leaving behind an island that no longer feels like enough.

## **Literary Devices**

* **First-Person Stream of Consciousness:** Allows the reader direct access to Annie’s rapid shifts between present physical observations and deep emotional memory recall.

* **Juxtaposition:** The calm, mundane routine of the island morning is sharply contrasted against Annie’s internal turmoil and panic about leaving.

* **Sensory Imagery:** Rich descriptions of sound, taste, and sight anchor Annie's emotional grief in physical reality.

## **Symbolisms**

* **The Jetty:** Symbolizes a liminal space—the threshold between childhood and adulthood, security and the unknown, Antigua and the wider world.

* **The Sea:** Symbolizes both liberation and frightening vastness; an unpredictable expanse separating Annie from everything she has ever known.

* **Annie's Shoes:** Symbolizes her outgrown childhood phases, domestic conflicts with her mother, and the physical footprints of her past left behind on the island.

* **The Blue and White Travel Clothes:** Freshly ironed by her mother, these garments symbolize her complex maternal inheritance—literal artifacts of her mother's care and identity that she wears out into the independent world.
