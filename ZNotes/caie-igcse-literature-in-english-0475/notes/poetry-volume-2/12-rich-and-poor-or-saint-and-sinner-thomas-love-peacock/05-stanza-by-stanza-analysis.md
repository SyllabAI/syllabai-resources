## Stanza-by-stanza analysis

#|
||

Stanza

|

Points

||
||

1

|

* The poor man’s actions are judged openly

* Sunday morning, shows strict religious expectations

* Buying basic food is treated as a sin

* Society watches the poor more closely than the rich

* Introduces theme of unfair moral judgement

* Tone is critical

* There is social hypocrisy

||
||

2

|

* The rich man’s sins are hidden by his wealth and status

* The Children of light conveys religious authority as ignoring injustice

* Suggests society protects the rich from judgement

* Introduces contrast between visibility and invisibility

* Moral rules depend on class, not behaviour

||
||

3

|

* The rich man has privacy

* The poor must act publicly

* Activities done to survive are labelled as wrongdoing

* Shows lack of independence among the poor

* Hence this reinforces idea that poverty removes dignity

||
||

4

|

* The rich can drink privately (cellar)

* The poor are watched even during leisure ( drinking his pint of beer)

* This suggests there is  constant surveillance of the poor

* Religious figures judge the poor’s harmless behaviours

* This shows double standards of morality

||
||

5

|

* The rich hide entertainment behind thier painted windows

* The poor share simple public music (crack’d fiddle)

* Society accepts rich pleasure 

* Yet condemns poor pleasure

* This highlights unfair judgement

* Hence reinforces status bases hypocrisy

||
||

6

|

* The rich remain unnoticed in society crowds

* Yet the poor man’s happiness becomes offensive to others

* This depicts that society rejects the visible working class’s joy

* This highlights the prejudice against poor people’s lifestyle

* There are strong criticism of social attitudes

||
||

7

|

* The rich travel privately (carriage)

* The poor travel publicly (third class train)

* Wealth protects reputation

* Poverty brings public judgement

||
||

8

|

* The rich escape judgement through luxury (yachting)

* The poor are criticised even during their cheap recreations

* Religious authority overpowers the poor again

* This conveys religion to support social inequality

* Reinforcement of the idea of unfair moral standards dominates the poem
