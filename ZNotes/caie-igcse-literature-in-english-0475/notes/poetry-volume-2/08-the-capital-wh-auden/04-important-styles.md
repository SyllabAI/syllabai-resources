Apostrophe

|

5-8, 9-10, 13 & 17-20

|

* Auden addresses the city directly as “you”

* This makes the Capital feel like a conscious, malicious force

* Rather than just a collection of buildings

* It’s not just a place

* The word “apparatus” combined with “charm”

* This suggests the city uses a manufactured, mechanical beauty

* This abolishes nature

* By talking to the city, the persona sounds like a disillusioned observer

||
||

Simile & metaphor

|

14-16, 19 & 3-4

|

* The metaphor of lovers who “ eat each other”  in a restaurant illustrates a world of predatory selfishness

* There is no romance here

* Only consumption of wealth, beauty, or status

* In the factories, lives are made for “temporary use”

* This simile is brutal and dehumaniztion

* A human being has no more value than a mass produced object

* The City life doesn't kill you instantly

* It wears the persona down over time until his unique "shape" is gone

||
||

Juxtaposition

|

1-4, 7-8 & 13-20

|

* The "exiles" create a “malicious village”

* This suggests that even those who flee oppression end up recreating the same snobbish, gossipy, and cruel environments they left behind

* There is “no winning” between rural and urban life

||
||

Assonance

|

3-4, 6, 8 & 15-19

|

* The repetition of the short “ih” sound

* This creates a thin, whispering sound 

* It mimics the "sing-song" gossip of the malicious village

* This also mimics the quiet, seductive "whisper" of the wicked uncle luring children

* Using "mere" and "here" in the rural stanza creates a repetitive, "dull" sound that mirrors the “dull” sound that mirrors in the countryside
