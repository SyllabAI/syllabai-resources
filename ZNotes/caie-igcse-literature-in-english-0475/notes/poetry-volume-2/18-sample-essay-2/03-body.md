## Body

The writer portrays strangeness through the symbol of strangers. Towards the first stanza, the people of the city welcome them without any hesitation and treat them with lights and food. The following day causes suspicion of whether they’re under attack and questioning them as the ‘strangers’ never spoke a word. Elizabeth creates an atmosphere of tension as the city’s is troubled by suspicion

The poet illustrates strangeness in lines 14 saying ‘man meets man speaking cautiously.’ This phrase highlights the fact that the people can’t help themselves to resolve the issue even though they know that they might be in harm. This alliteration powerfully depicts a sense of thought that the strangers are much more stronger than them. Elizabeth conveys that the city is troubled with suspicion so much that the people are talking in low tones to avoid the strangers overhearing them.

Strangeness is further spotted in the theme of the fear of the unknown. Much as strangers are warmly welcomed by the town wives this lights and food, this only lasts for a night as the following day, the town’s people are anxious, unsettled and uncomfortable as they ‘speak cautiously’ among themselves. This demonstrates how these strangers can be a possible threat to the city and as the jittery citizens show themselves away from the new immigrants. Their unsettled cautions nature further reveals that the strangers are invading their minds as if domination them.

Elizabeth further portrays strangeness through imagery. In the opening stanza, the strangers are welcomed with “lights and food,” imagery that creates a warm and hospitable atmosphere. However, this warmth is short-lived as the mood quickly changes into one of fear and uncertainty. The contrast between kindness and suspicion highlights how quickly human attitudes can change when faced with the unfamiliar. Elizabeth suggests that people often fear what they do not understand, transforming strangers into perceived enemies despite there being no evidence that they pose a threat.

The poet also uses contrast to emphasise the strange atmosphere throughout the poem. At first, the townspeople treat the strangers with generosity and compassion, yet by the following day they are filled with doubt and suspicion. This dramatic shift in behaviour illustrates how easily trust can be replaced by fear. The contrast reveals that the real threat may not be the strangers themselves but the fear and paranoia that grow within the community. Elizabeth therefore suggests that prejudice often develops from uncertainty rather than fact.

Finally, Elizabeth presents strangeness through the absence of communication. The strangers “never spoke a word,” creating an unsettling silence throughout the poem. Their silence prevents the townspeople from understanding their intentions, allowing fear and imagination to replace reason. Instead of asking questions directly, the people speculate among themselves, increasing tension within the community. Elizabeth implies that a lack of communication often leads to misunderstanding, making people view those who are different as enemies rather than fellow human beings.
