# Boxes: Sampurna Chattarji

## The poem

### Stanza 1

1. *Her balcony bears an orchid smuggled in a duffle bag*

2. *from Singapore. It’s roots cling to air. For two hours*

3. *every morning the harsh October sun turns tender*

4. *at its leaves. Nine steps from door to balcony and*

5. *already she is a giant insect fretting in a jar.*

### Stanza 2

 6. *On one side of her one-room home, a stove, where she*

 7. *cooks dal in an iron pan. The smell of food is good.*

 8. *Through the window bars the sing-song of voices high*

 9. *then low in steady arcs. With his back to the wall,*

10. *a husband, and a giant stack of quilts, threatening to fall.*

### Stanza 3

11. *Sleeping room only, a note on the door should have read,*

12. *readying you for cramp. Fall in and kick off your shoes.*

13. *right-angled to this corridor with a bed, trains make tracks*

14. *to unfamiliar sounding places. Unhidden by her curtains,*

15. *two giant black pigs lie dead, or sleeping, on a dump.*

### Stanza 4

16. *Every day the city grows taller, trampling underfoot*

17. *students wives lovers babies. The boxes grow smaller.*

18. *The sea becomes a distant memory of lashing wave*

19. *and neon, siren to seven islands, once. The sky strides*

20. *inland on giant stilts, unstoppable, shutting out the light.*
