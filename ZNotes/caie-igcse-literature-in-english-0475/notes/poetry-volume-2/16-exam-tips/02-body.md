## Body

* Use PEEL to construct each paragraph:

  * **P**oint (any style of theme),

  * **E**vidence (exact lines from the poem to back up the point),

  * **E**valuate (talk about what the author is trying to convey),

  * **L**ink (connect it back to the question, answer the question in literal terms).

* Every paragraph should answer these 5 questions:

  * What is the specific theme, emotion, or message being conveyed in this part of the poem?

  * What literary devices or word choices is the author using to get that point across? (You must provide evidence).

  * Why did the author choose this specific word or technique instead of another?

  * What is the effect on the reader?

  * How does it relate to the bigger picture of the poem?

* If you are running out of words, its advised to add one more device in the same paragraph to create a decently sized paragraph.

* Include the reader’s feelings, (*the reader is likely of feel …* ), this earns you marks in the A03 and A02 criteria.

* When analyzing a style, talk about its literal meaning and then what the author is trying to convey, this is known as evaluations and effect.

* Every poem has a turning point, here it is the split strands collaborating to form a muscle, make sure to talk about this as this earns high marks.

* Never mix up contrast and juxtaposition: Contrast = difference between ideas; Juxtaposition = contrast placed very close together.

* By focusing on the speaker's shifting observation, you prove that you are analyzing the poem's structural arc rather than just providing a play-by-play description.

* Always focus your argument on one word per quotation not the whole phrase (Ex of what not to do: the phrase shows oppression …; Ex of what to do: the word “weight” conveys the fact that oppression…).

* Examiners award precision not a summary
