## Body

The poet creates a setting of the stage in this phrase “ everything remains just as it was” said in line 2 of stanza 1. This portrays the sort of silence that had filled the hall prior to the performance. We can tell from the poet’s tone that the audience are looking forward to the show. The quote gives us an insight of what runs in the character’s brain creating tension in the reader.

George Szirtes illustrates his use of rhyme in the quote “of nothing happening until something does” that is a repetition of the first line. Line 4 of stanza 1 was placed to exert emphasis on the state of the grand hall. This illustrates the calm and relaxed setting that was in the hall and demonstrates the excitement and expectations seen from the audience who were looking forward to a good performance.

“A lot of small hands in a monstrous hall can make the air vibrate” creates a visual imagery in line 5-6 in stanza 1. This presents a picture of what the stage view was like right form the character’s first point of view. The metaphor “monstrous” creates a detailed description and a sense of scale for the hall, it was incredibly huge with so many people waiting to witness the show.

The phrase, “can make the air vibrate and even shake the wall” is an auditory imagery that depicts the sort of loud clapping that’s seen. It’s so loud that it could shake the walls suggests just how powerful the claps were. There is use of kinaesthetic imagery when George says “ shake the walls suggests” that is quite impossible which creates dramatic irony of the loud, ear breaking sound that filled the hall

The phrase “ a lot of small hands in a monstrous hall” not only creates visual imagery but an oxymoron,two things that mean two different things are placed together. The juxtaposition creates the mood and description of the grand hall.

George creates a sense of awareness that the reader must have said in line 3-4 in stanza 2 “a voice can break a plate or glass” that shows the destructive power that the persons voice can have. The poet has powerfully converted how experienced the character is by demonstrating a visual imagination in this phrase. There is use of alliteration in the “tip” and “ship.”

The poet creates an understanding that this song hand been sung before by many singer but have failed in impress the audience said in line 1 of stanza 3. “It’s the very same tune that’s been sung time and again by those whose heavy fate has hung.” This illustrates just how many times and how often its been sung. This phrase also foreshadows the character’s fate as we are quiet unaware of what had happened to the other singers.

George pushes the reader to the peak of tension as the reader is sat at the edge of the seat, waiting for the character to begin singing. The poets is teasing the reader’s patience as he pauses down a couple of stanzas with unrelated facts.

The phrase “even earth may be made to move” in line 6 of stanza 4 demonstrates the hardships that previous singers had on stage. They were struggling to sing the song as the “weight” lowered or crushed them. The “weight” here symbolises the anxiety and trouble that comes with presenting on stage.

George creates a visual imagery when he says “ even earth may be made to move.” The kinaesthetic imagery creates a dramatic irony as no one can really move the earth. George creates a metaphor that elegantly describes just how much of a difficulty it is to sing on stage amidst the tension and stress.
