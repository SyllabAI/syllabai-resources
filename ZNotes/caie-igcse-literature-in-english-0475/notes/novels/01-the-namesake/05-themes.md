## Themes

### **Immigration**:

* **Ashoke** - amused and fascinated by the USA, prospers as first a student then a professor, remains attached to Bengali traditions but has always liked travelling
* **Ashima** - misses her life in Calcutta immensely, finds difficulty in understanding American customs, clings to her family in both USA and India. works actively to maintain the family’s Bengali traditions, represents the difficulty of fitting into a new culture while retaining one’s own culture
* Ashoke and Ashima represent 2 sides of the immigrant experience
* **Gogol, Sonia, and Moushumi** - represent the first American-born generation, assimilation comes more naturally to them, grow up speaking English natively, India is more foreign to them, divided loyalties often lead to an internal struggle for a unified identity

### **Identity**:

* Gogol changes his name to Nikhil$\implies$shows his desire to control his identity
* Main tension driving Gogol’s identity confusion is the divide between his family’s Indian heritage, and his own desire for a more modern American lifestyle
* Many of Gogol’s choices seem motivated by a desire to live life as a “normal American”, to escape his family’s influence \[ex. relationship with Maxine\]
* At certain moments however, Gogol seems to reach back towards his roots, possibly as he accepts that his background is an inevitably vital component of his identity \[ex. marriage to Moushumi\]
* Naming, as well as nicknames, are a symbol of the bonds shared by the characters
  * Ashima and Ashoke become **Monu** and **Mithu** in Calcutta with their families
  * Maxine is **Max** to Gogol
  * Moushumi is **Mouse** to Dimitri
  * Sonia calls Gogol **Goggles**

### **Family and Tradition**:

* For **Ashoke and Ashima**, the concept of a family life is inherited from India
  * Families share the same home for generations
  * Everyone is deeply invested in each other’s lives
  * Connections and bonds are reinforced through traditions and rituals (naming, marriage, death, holidays)
* Though Ashoke and Ashima are cut off from their relatives in India, their fellow Bengali immigrant friends help to maintain such traditions; for them, family is a constant force that naturally defines one’s identity
* However, for **Gogol and Sonia**, growing up in America, family becomes a symbol of foreignism, something that pulls their identity away from what they learn in school/from American society
* To them, Bengali rituals and traditions seem like empty ceremonies, as they grow up in a culture that seen them as alien
* Nevertheless, family is a defining force in the novel: Ashoke and Ashima are a solid foundation their children rely on, and inevitably return to for comfort, as throughout their accidents the only constant is their connection

### **Love and Marriage**:

* **Ashoke and Ashima** -
  * Concept of love is characterized by Bengali ideals \[e. defined gender roles, less openly displayed affection\], yet they have a deep sense of loyalty and companionship
  * They are one another’s sole partners in life, as their marriage was an arranged affair, which further emphasizes their traditional Indian lifestyle
  * Though they have a traditional gap, the intimacy and care they have together is made clear throughout the novel
* **Gogol** -
  * He has intense, openly sexual relations with multiple women, both outside of and briefly within a marriage
  * He uses love as a form of rebelling, as well as a way to develop his own identity since love is to be found independently for him
  * For both Gogol and Moushumi, their marriage was driven by a desire to conform to Bengali ideals, and an attempt to find their identity in one another$\implies$their need for independence is greater than their commitment to a family identity
