## Synopses

### **Chapter 1:**

* Ashoke is moving to the USA for his graduate studies.
* When visiting his grandfather, he experiences a train accident, which becomes a pivotal moment in his life, in which he is saved by Russian literature.
* Ashoke marries Ashima and they move to Cambridge, Massachusetts.
* Ashima, after 9 months of pregnancy, is now in the hospital starting contractions while reminiscing on her life with Ashoke, who waits in the hospital’s hall.

### **Chapter 2:**

* Ashima gives birth to a healthy baby boy, and both parents send a telegram to Calcutta, awaiting a letter from Ashima’s grandmother.
* Mr. Wilcox, a staff at the hospital, asks for a name for their baby, and the couple decide on Gogol as a daknam (calling name), until the letter arrives with his bhalonam (good name).
* Ashima, over a period of several months, becomes comfortable with raising Gogol and running errands in her Cambridge neighborhood, though the letter with Gogol’s bhalonam hasn’t arrives, as her grandmother is sick.
* The Gangulis become acquainted with Bengalis in the Boston area, and have Gogol’s annaprasan (rice ceremony).
* While preparing to visit Calcutta, the family receives a call from Ashima’s brother, informing them that Ashima’s father has passed away.

### **Chapter 3:**

* The 3 Gangulis move to the suburbs when Ashoke receives an offer for an assistant professor, and Gogol starts nursery school.
* After 2 years, the family buys a house at 67 Pemberton Road.
* Ashima becomes pregnant again when Gogol is 5, and he starts kindergarten, where his refuses to be identified by his bhalonam Nikhil; this is respected by the principal and teacher of the kindergarten.
* Gogol’s sister is born, and her daknam and bhalonam are combined calling her Sonali, though the family refers to her as Sonia.
* 10 years pass and the family adopts more American customs.
* Gogol gets made fun of for his name occasionally, and he begins to feel self-conscious.

### **Chapter 4:**

* Gogol celebrates his 14th birthday twice, once with his school friends and once with his Bengali friends; at the birthday with Bengali friends, he meets a girl named Moushumi and receives a copy of Nikolai Gogol’s short stories from his father.
* When Gogol is in 10th grade, Ashoke receives a sabbatical, and the family moves to Calcutta for 8 months.
* Gogol and Sonia feel out of place in the city, however Ashoke and Ashima are confident; they travel to multiple aunts’ and uncles’ houses for months, eating and catching up.
* When summer arrives in Calcutta, the Gangulis go on a sightseeing trip, where Gogol is taken by the Taj Mahal.
* In 11th grade, Gogol’s English teacher talks of the author Nikolai Gogol, and he is anguished with shame, though the other students don’t notice.
* One night, Gogol goes to a party at Ashoke’s college, where he kisses a girl named Kim and introduces himself as Nikhil, rather than Gogol.

### **Chapter 5:**

* Gogol changes his name to Nikhil the summer before he heads to Yale, and introduces himself to his roommates as Nikhil.
* He participates in typical college activities, but remains a disciplined son and goes home regularly.
* On his way to Boston for Thanksgiving, he meets a girl named Ruth; they date for over a year, and break up after she returns from Oxford after a semester abroad when they fall out of love.
* In his senior year, Gogol again travels for Thanksgiving, when his train is delayed; at the station in Boston, Gogol finds his father anxiously waiting for him, and Ashok reveals the story of his train accident in India to Gogol.

### **Chapter 6:**

* After a time skip, Gogol has graduated from Yale and Columbia Architecture School, and now works for a firm in Manhattan.
* At a party, Gogol meets Maxine Ratliff and is attracted to her; the day after the party, she invites him to dinner at her parents’.
* Gogol falls for Maxine and becomes a part of the Ratliff’s house.
* Gogol communicates less frequently with his family, but in August 1994 the family meets, along with Maxine, before Ashok leaves for a professorship in Cleveland, while Gogol and Maxine are on their way to New Hampshire.
* At Maxine’s parents’ family cabin, Gogol falls for the Ratliff’s way of life, spending his 2 weeks with Maxine, Gerald and Lydia (Maxine’s parents).

### **Chapter 7:**

* Ashima is 48 now, and has begun to appreciate her solitude, though she still enjoys Ashok’s visits every 3 weeks.
* Ashoke calls one night to inform her that he has checked into a hospital, but to not worry as it is only an upset stomach.
* Several hours pass and Ashima calls the hospital, and is placed on hold; the hospital official tells her that they had been trying to reach her as Ashoke had died of a heart attack, in the hospital.
* Gogol learns of his father’s passing after a night out with Maxine and flies to Cleveland alone, though Maxine offers to come.
* He takes a taxi to the hospital and identifies his father’s body; he then drives his father’s car to his apartment complex and, on his mother’s orders, throws away all that his father owned.
* Gogol returns to Boston and observes a period of family mourning with Sonia and Ashima for 10 days.
* Ashima then holds a gathering to celebrate Ashoke’s life, which Maxine attends; Gogol declines to join her for the new year.
* In the weeks that follow, Ashima takes care of logistical details; Sonia decides to remain in Boston permanently instead of returning to California; Gogol takes the Amtrak to Penn Station, where Maxine will be waiting for him, though he has mixed feelings about seeing her again.

### **Chapter 8:**

* A year goes by, with Gogol still in NYC working for a large architecture firm, studying, and visiting his family most weekends; he has ended things with Maxine after growing apart from her mentally.
* Ashima constantly bothers Gogol about a girlfriend, and eventually he agrees to go on a date with a family friend - Moushumi; they have a drink at a bar downtown, and Gogol learns more about her.
* A week later they go out for lunch; Gogol then comes over to Moushumi’s place for a third date and their romance quickly follows.
* The chapter ends with a description of Moushumi’s life before Gogol, where she went to Brown, fell in love with literature, got engaged and split with an American banker, and so on.

### **Chapter 9:**

* After a year of dating, Moushumi and Gogol get married in a traditional and long Bengali ceremony; after it ends they remark on how glad they are to be alone, and make love.
* They rend an apartment on Third Avenue in the Twenties, downtown; Gogol enjoys the patterns of their married life, despite occasionally feeling as though he was a “back-up” to Graham, the banker.
* In the spring of that year the couple goes to Paris, where Moushumi gives a talk; after delivering her paper, she meets Gogol in a cafe, where she remarks that she misses Paris, upset.
* Back in America, at a party hosted by Moushumi’s friends, Gogol feels both drawn to, and frustrated over Moushumi not telling him things, as well as jealous of her closeness to her friends; she reveals Gogol’s original name and he remarks that it was because his father “was a fan”
* Gogol then realized that his life with Moushumi. despite seemingly satisfying, has in fact left him angered and lonely.

### **Chapter 10:**

* It is the couple’s first wedding anniversary: Moushumi has now passed exams for her doctorate, settled more into married life, and has won a fellowship in Paris, though Gogol doesn’t know.
* They prepare to go out for their anniversary dinner, however it goes poorly and they leave vaguely sad
* Soon after, Moushumi begins teaching Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays at NYU; while sorting some mail after an administrator passed, she sees a return address for a man named Dimitri Desjardins.
* Back in high school, Moushumi and Dimitri met during a shared bus ride to Washington, DC and though they flirted, they never pursued a relationship; they encountered each other once more but Dimitri, being older older, was planning to study literature in Europe, so their romantic connection did not progress any further; in the present Moushumi copies down Dimitri’s number.
* She calls his one day and they begin an affair, though Moushumi knows it is wrong; Gogol continues to be oblivious and they drift apart, as Moushumi spends time after each class with Dimitri, feeling that they have an intellectual connection.

### **Chapter 11:**

* Gogol wakes up alone, since Moushumi is away attending a conference.
* He recognizes that their marriage is now strained, and among his work finds an old picture of his wife, reminding him of the initial intensity of their love; he remembers Thanksgiving and, though he enjoyed it, begins to wonder if Moushumi still loves him, as she has seemed unhappy for a while.
* Back in the office, he goes out for lunch and realizes that he hadn’t any idea what to buy Moushumi for Christmas; he continues walking and gets inspired to give the two of them a trip to Italy as a gift.
* He then walks back to their apartment, feeling better yet still gloomy; the doorman tells Gogol that Moushumi has returned, and he imagines her in the apartment, bathing and drinking wine.

### **Chapter 12:**

* It is Christmas Eve, and the family’s final holiday celebration in Massachusetts before Ashima moves; she plans to divide her time between India and USA, with 6 months in each location.
* Ashima remembers her children’s relationships, feeling happy for Sonia and Ben (her fiancé), and relieved for Gogol; after cooking she goes upstairs and realizes that she now considers Pemberton Road to be home.
* For the final time in the novel, Gogol rides the train from NYC to Boston, knowing that Ashoke won’t be there to pick him up from the station.
* He thinks to himself how fair his parents travelled from, yet how he has always called Northeast home; he also remembers the conversation with Moushumi about a year ago on the train to Boston, where he found out about her affair with Dimitri and they swiftly separated.
* Gogol spends time at the party, taking family pictures and setting up the fake tree; it is joyous, but Gogol goes upstairs for some time alone, and finds the copy of Nikolai Gogol’s works that his father gifted him years ago.
* Gogol returns to the party but thinks on his life, then of the night ahead where he will read Nikolai Gogol’s stories; he realizes that it is finally his opportunity to connect more fully with his father’s life, and to learn more deeply about what the name “Gogol” meant to Ashoke.
