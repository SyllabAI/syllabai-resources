## Context

**Heart of Darkness:** In 1899, Joseph Conrad wrote and published Heart of Darkness. It tells a story of a European man visiting the Congo River.

* The longest sentence an African said is “Catch him, give him to us, eat him".

  * “Us”. It collectively assumes how the community acts as a whole.

  * “Eat him”. This emphasizes the cannibalism and inhumanity. The stereotype is that African people are animalistic and unevolved.

* The novel dehumanizes African people and gives them animalistic characteristics. Conrad depicts the people as having lack of emotion and empathy when seeing others suffering.

* Achebe wrote Things Fall Apart to **rebuke the barbaric stereotype.** In the novel, Achebe reveals the complexity within the culture of the Igbo people to the Western audience using language and customs.

Achebe opens Things Fall Apart with a quotation from **W.B. Yeats’ poem “The Second Coming”.**

* *“Things fall apart; the centre cannot hold;*
  *Mere anarchy is loosed upon the world...”*

* This directly echoed back to Chinua Achebe’s title for the novel. “Things Fall Apart” describes the disintegration of Igbo society.

* “The centre” represents the culture, beliefs and independence of Igbo, which collapsed after the arrival of colonialism.

* “Mere anarchy is loosed”, which depicts chaos and lack of order. This links to the colonialists’ act of undermining Igbo culture, taking over the justice system, and disrupting the tribal unity.

**Written one year before Nigeria gained independence (1959),** the tone of the novel is negative, uncertain of the future, and striving for identity.

* Explores **pre-colonial Igbo society** and the **changes brought by the British** colonialists. Achebe aims to highlight the cultural richness and challenge the uncivilized stereotype.

* Achebe used literature as a way to deliver because literature can shape people’s perspectives. It’s effective in reflecting the brutal reality of colonialism. It **gives a voice to those who have been historically marginalized.**

* Things Fall Apart **challenged historical documents** on colonialism in the past.

* Achebe’s education was influenced by the legacy of colonial Christian missionaries in Nigeria.

* Achebe was **raised in a household that had both** traditional Igbo culture and Christian values so he has a unique authority to mediate perspectives of both traditions.

* The ending was purposefully written to emphasize how colonial records were **twisted and fabricated.**

* Achebe wrote this novel in Lagos, Nigeria in between 1958 to 1959.
