## Key Quotations

### Characters:

### **Okonkwo:**

* Heavy hand (Chapter 4-5) - highlights his tragic flaw and refusal to communicate when needed.

* Cannot get out words, he would ‘use his fists’. (Chapter 4-5) - depicts Okonkwo’s default response to emotion as physical force.

* Springy walk. (Chapter 1) - emphasizes his arrogance and rigid masculinity through every motion.

* Pulls at the ‘loose end of cloth’ of Ekwefi when she leaves her husband for him . (Chapter 12)

* He was a man of action, a man of war… first to bring home a human head. (Chapter 2) - emphasizes his strong belief in using violence to exert and reinforce his masculinity.

* Okonkwo knew how to kill a man’s spirit. (Chapter 4) - highlights how he looks down on men without status. He sees them as weak and feminine.

* He ‘trembled with the desire to conquer and subdue’ (about drums for the wrestling match)  . (Chapter 5; during New Yam festival and wrestling contest.) - emphasizes his desire to always be on top and masculine.

* ‘He was afraid of being thought weak’ . (Across part one, Chapter 4-5) - his tragic flaw, which influences him to act irrationally in lots of events.

* ‘The law of the land must be obeyed’. (Chapter 7; he participates in communal practices and imposes strict discipline.) - emphasizes how he blindly follows his belief; he does not question or critically think about it. This contributes to his fatal downfall because he is unable to adapt to the new changes after the colonialists arrive; he is too fixed in one belief and not flexible.

* Living fire begets cold, impotent ash . (Chapter 17 - bitter about Nwoye) - highlights the generational shift of Igbo society and the breakdown of Okonkwo’s family/legacy.

### **Ikemefuna:**

**Chapter 7**: Refer to Ikemefuna’s tragic death and events leading to his execution.

* Ill-fated boy. (Chapter 7) - refers to the reason why he was chosen to be killed; it highlights how the spirits/oracle dictate every event happening in this society.

* Doomed lad. (Chapter 7)

* ‘poor child’ – could be for Ikemefuna or Nwoye. (Chapter 7)

* ‘My father, they have killed me. ’ (Chapter 7) - highlights the brutality of rigid tradition; reinforces how toxic masculinity has taken over Okonkwo for him to ignore the paternal relationship with Ikemefuna just to prove his power.

* Full of the sap of life. (Chapter 7-8) - emphasizes the complex personality of Ikemefuna as a character. Justify why he has such a big influence on Okonkwo and Nwoye.

* Kindled a new fire in the younger boy. (Chapter 7-8; Ikemefuna’s effect on Nwoye. Ikemefuna revitalizes Nwoye’s spirit yet deepens Nwoye’s disillusionment.) - Ikemefuna opens up a new interpretation for masculinity where it is not forced and rigid; it contrasts with Okonkwo.

### **Ezinma:**

* I wish she were a boy. (Chapter 8; Okonkwo’s thought when he saw Ezinma’s courage and intelligence.) - highlights how Okonkwo appreciates femininity but refuses to admit it; unfolds Okonkwo’s affectionate side.

* She was wiser than her years . (Chapter 5; Ezinma demonstrates insight and perceptiveness in her conversations with Ekwefi.)

* ‘Ogbanje, one of those wicked children who, when they died, entered their mother’s wombs to be born again’ . (Chapter 9; Explanation for the tight relationship between Ekwefi and Ezinma as well as the beliefs of Igbo on certain things.)

* ‘Most beautiful girls in Mbanta… crystal beauty’ . (Chapter 14-15; emphasizes her beauty.)

### **Chielo:**

**Chapter 11:** Chielo and Ezinma’s night journey.

* Chielo. She was the priestess of Agbala, the Oracle of the Hills and the Caves - explains more about how the spirits are present in Igbo’s life.

* Her voice was as clear as metal.

* Chielo was not a woman that night - emphasizes the power of spirits and oracle over people.

### **Obierika:**

* If I were you, I would have stayed at home . (Chapter 19-20; reflects Okonkwo’s sentiment of regret.) - emphasizes how fate has dictated Okonkwo’s life.

* He has put a knife on the things that held us together and we have fallen apart – Obierika. (Chapter 20; responds to the disintegration of the clan under colonial influence.) - emphasizes the breakdown of Igbo society.

**Chapters 20-23:** Reflects his growing unease with colonial law - emphasizes the flaws that exist within Igbo society.

* I do not know how we got that law.

* Who knows what may happen tomorrow?

* Perhaps green men will come to our clan and shoot us .

### **Uchendu:**

**Chapter 14:** Emphasizes communal resilience, fear of the future and generational insight.

* I know more about the world than any of you.

* You are still a child, my child.

* We are better than animals because we have kinsmen.

* I fear for the younger generation.

* I fear for you; I fear for the clan.

### **Unoka:**

**Chapter 2-3:** Unoka’s failings and childlike traits - highlights the reason why Okonkwo acts irrationally and violently.

* Flue.

* Kite sailing.

* Loafer.

* Failure.

### **Society:**

* Let the kite perch and the eagle too. (Chapter 1)

* Proverbs are the palm oil with which words are eaten. (Chapter 1) - reveals the complexity of Igbo culture and the importance of oral tradition.

* If a child washed his hands, he could eat with kings. (Chapter 1-2)

* Yam, the king of crops, was a very exacting king. (Chapter 3) - emphasizes the significant role of yams in society.

* When a man says yes, his chi says yes also. (Chapter 4) - reinforces the concept of fate vs free will.

* Egwugwu – the most powerful and the most secret cult in the clan. (Chapter 10) - introduces more about Igbo social structure.

* Nine masked spirits who administer justice in the clan. (Chapter 10) - introduces the vital role of spirits in people’s lives.

* Tortoise, who calls himself ‘all of you’ – the power of language to manipulate. (Chapter 11; Tortoise tale.) - emphasizes the importance of oral tradition and storytelling; highlights the complexity of Igbo culture.
