## Characters

### **Okonkwo**

* A clan leader of Umuofia.

* Embarrassed about his effeminate father, Unoka.

* Hard working, ambitious provider, resilient, hypermasculinity.

* He behaves irrationally because he fears being weak, which leads to lots of trouble for himself and the people around him.

* Isolated, heavy-handed, rigid, patriarchal.

* Tragic downfall. Tragic flaw: Fear of weakness. His inability to adapt as the power of the Igbo weakened contributes to his downfall.

* **Themes related:** Interpretation of masculinity, fate vs free will, gender roles, hybridity, family and kinship, consequences of colonialism.

### **Nwoye**

* Okonkwo’s oldest son.

* Struggles with masculinity, search for identity.

* Being called lazy and harshly beaten by Okonkwo.

* Significantly influenced by Ikemefuna and his death.

* His skepticism towards his culture led him to convert to Christianity. Symbol of change.

* Sensitive.

* Impacted by colonialism.

* Symbol of the Igbo kinship falling apart. He is the representation of a generational shift. As Igbo lose their next generation, the tradition breaks down because no one is there to continue the legacy.

* **Themes related**: Interpretation of masculinity, gender roles, hybridity, family and kinship, and consequences of colonialism.

### **Ezinma**

* Only child of Ekwefi.

* Unexpectedly survived infancy.

* Okonkwo’s favourite child: Okonkwo’s affectionate side, which Okonkwo denies, is revealed when Ezinma is present.

* Ezinma is the emotional foil for Okonkwo. She unfolds and reveals hidden side of the protagonist.

* **Themes related:** Interpretation of masculinity, family and kinship, gender roles, and the importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions).

### **Ikemefuna**

* He was given to Okonkwo as a bargaining tool. He was killed by Okonkwo as a sacrificial pawn for Okonkwo’s fear of weakness and rigid masculinity.

* Loyal, obedient, innocent, courageous, charismatic.

* Victim of rigid tradition.

* Became popular among Okonkwo’s children, especially Nwoye.

* He was ordered to be killed; Okonkwo joined because he did not want to look weak.

* His death is the turning point for the novel because he changed Nwoye’s view of Igbo culture and influenced the detachment within the family. On a bigger picture, he influenced the generational shift after he died.

* **Themes related:** Interpretations of masculinity, family and kinship, fate vs free will, gender roles.

### **Ekwefi**

* Okonkwo’s second wife.

* Ran away from her first husband to be with Okonkwo when she saw Okonkwo defeat Amalinze the Cat.

* She treated Ezinma as an equal since Ezinma survived after nine past infancy.

* Good friend with Chielo.

* She embodies resilience and challenges patriarchal norms by leaving her first husband. She also represents the kinship and maternal love that exists in the Igbo society.

* **Themes related:** Family and kinship, gender roles, and the importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions).

### **Mr.Brown**

* First white missionary in Umuofia.

* Middle ground between two extremes, non-aggression, respectful of the tribe’s beliefs. “Soft power” approach. He represents a spectrum of colonial strategies

  * His name can be associated with white and black mixed together; his mindset is in the grey area.

* Became friends with the clansmen: built a school and hospital to build goodwill.

* Reveals the gentle approach and benefits of colonialism.

* **Themes related:** Hybridity, consequences of colonialism, the importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions).

### **Reverend James Smith**

* Replaced Mr.Brown when his health declined.

* Strict, stereotypical white colonialist, disrespectful.

* Encourages violence from the converts towards the indigenous customs.

* He represents another spectrum of colonial strategies (hard power).

* **Themes related:** Hybridity, consequences of colonialism, the importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions).

### **Unoka**

* Okonkwo’s father.

* Cowardly, title-less musician, never repays his debts.

* Artistic, gentle.

* Died of an illness.

* He influenced Okonkwo’s interpretation of masculinity and rigid behavior. He influenced Okonkwo’s personality and it became Okonkwo’s tragic flaw.

* **Themes related:** Gender roles, interpretations of masculinity, the importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions).

### **Obierika**

* Okonkwo’s friend.

* Loyal, reliable, supportive, wise, reflective.

* Sells yams for Okonkwo and comforts him (Okonkwo’s foil).

* Obierika embodies the mission of a messenger to Okonkwo and the audience.

* Obierika does question the tribe’s belief but still follows it.

* Voice of reason/critical commentator for the audience, moral compass for Okonkwo.

* He acts rationally when Okonkwo is irrational.

* **Themes related:** Family and Kinship, interpretations of masculinity.

### **Uchendu**

* Okonkwo’s uncle (the younger brother of his mother)

* Supports Okonkwo during the exile in Mbanta.

* His compromising personality greatly contrasts with Okonkwo’s.

* He represents the maternal refuge during Okonkwo’s weak moments.

* **Themes related:** Family and Kinship, the importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions).

### **The District Commissioner**

* Represents the colonial government in Nigeria.

* Racist, ignorant, abrasive, disrespectful.

* Embodies the reductive and dehumanizing attitude of colonialists.

* **Themes related**: Hybridity, consequences of colonialism, fate vs free will.

### **Enoch**

* A violent convert who unmasks the Egwugwu.
* He emphasizes that the coexistence of cultures is impossible.
* **Themes related:** Hybridity, consequences of colonialism, family and kinship.

### **Chielo**

* Priestess of the Oracle of the goddess Agbala.

* Calls Ezinma “my daughter” and carries her around the village.

* She highlights the significance of spiritual guidance to every Igbo person.

* **Themes related:** The importance of cultural identity (evident through language and traditions), fate vs free will, gender roles.

### **Mr.Kiaga**

* Converts to Christianity.

* Comes to Mbanta and helps with communication, which converts Nwoye and many others.

* He explains the tactics of colonialists in approaching Igbo culture.

* **Themes related:** Hybridity, consequences of colonialism, family and kinship.

### **Ojiugo**

* Okonkwo’s third wife.

* Okonkwo beats her during the Week of Peace for leaving the hut without cooking.

* She reinforces the gender role of men have power over women in Igbo society.

* **Themes related:** Interpretations of masculinity, family and kinship, gender roles.
