## How to write a literature essay

The question paper consists of different passages and questions from different novels. Find the page that has Things Fall Apart. There will be **two** questions.

For the first question, you are required to use the given passage (random) to answer. For the second question, you will use your knowledge of the whole text/novel to answer.

### **Plan:**

* Identify & highlight quotations that you will use to answer the question.

* Group chosen quotations into ideas.

* Write down adverbs that are relevant to the questions:

  * For example: How does Achebe powerfully portray… in this passage?

  * Adverbs to use (vary adverbs to emphasize the same ideas without too much repetition): powerfully, strikingly, compellingly, vividly, emphatically…

**Thesis statement (Introduction):**

* Sentence 1 (what): Answer the question clearly, directly and instantly. Outline your 2 ideas.

* Sentence 2 (why): Link your 2 ideas to the big idea. Your essay should be connected like a golden thread.

***Tip:*** *Balance analysis of Achebe’s technique/choice of language with the context of Igbo culture and colonial impact. This will help you meet the requirements for AO1 and AO3. Link the writer’s choice to cultural meaning to reinforce your understanding with the examiner.*

* **Overview:** Provide a brief overview of the passage, considering the question.

***Tip:*** *Each paragraph must focus on a new, unique idea. Avoid repeating ideas or using the same quotations. Choose new evidence/quotations to explain every time you introduce a new point.*

### **Paragraph 1:**

1. Topic sentence: Introduce what you will be covering in the whole paragraph.

2. Embedded Quotations: Introduce the groups of quotations you will use.

3. Analysis: Analyzes the use of quotations. This should be linked closely to the question and supported with references.

4. Link back to the question: Answer the question.

5. More quotations and analysis.

6. Link to the big idea and the question: At the end of every paragraph, explicitly connect the analysis to the question. This will show the examiner that you’re on task, your essay has a tight structure and you can maintain the golden thread.

**Paragraph 2**: Repeats the steps for paragraph 1.

**Conclusion** (optional): Summarizes your 2 ideas.

---

### **For analyzing:**

Use this chain of adverbs:

1. Literally: The literal meaning/definition of the single word or quotation.

2. Metaphorically: The implicit imagery/meaning of the single word or quotation.

3. Symbolically: Link back to Achebe’s purpose in using that specific word/quotation and not any other words.

Analyze imagery, symbolism, irony and their effects on the reader.

### **PEEL structure:**

* Point (what is being conveyed).

* Evidence (quotation that proves the point).

* Explanation (link between point and evidence).![](https://www.google.com/url?sa=i&url=https%3A%2F%2Fschoolsfinders.com%2Fhow-to-structure-paragraphs-using-the-peel-method%2F&psig=AOvVaw0TWotTDBWCettMpDDR_gNg&ust=1755614762639000&source=images&cd=vfe&opi=89978449&ved=0CBUQjRxqFwoTCNjfpOzMlI8DFQAAAAAdAAAAABAK)![](https://www.google.com/url?sa=i&url=https%3A%2F%2Fschoolsfinders.com%2Fhow-to-structure-paragraphs-using-the-peel-method%2F&psig=AOvVaw0TWotTDBWCettMpDDR_gNg&ust=1755614762639000&source=images&cd=vfe&opi=89978449&ved=0CBUQjRxqFwoTCNjfpOzMlI8DFQAAAAAdAAAAABAK)

* Link (link the point with the question).

### **Things to pay attention to:**

* Your chosen quotations should be closely linked to the question being asked.

* Always try to seamlessly embed your quotations, avoid explicitly quoting them or dropping them in without explanation.

* Always link back to the question after analyzing.

* AO3: Focus on the interpretation of the writer’s effect on the reader

* Vary the use of adverbs to avoid repetition, but still be able to emphasize your ideas. (e.g, replace powerfully with strikingly, compellingly, emphatically, explicitly, vividly….)

### **Time management:**

Spend **at least 45 minutes** on prose (move on from poetry if needed and vice versa)

1. 5 minutes: Read the question, underline keywords from the question, and brief plan on what to include in the paragraphs.

2. 5-10 minutes: Analyse quotations, plan the structure of the essay, and create a thesis statement.

3. 25-30 minutes: Write your essay.

4. 5 minutes: Check and review.
