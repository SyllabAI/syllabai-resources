## Themes

### **Clash of cultures/Hybridity**

* The arrival of Christianity undermines the traditional practices.

* The differences in beliefs, lifestyle, and religion lead to conflicts.

* Okonkwo’s suicide is a sign of desperation, impotence in stopping the disruptive force of colonial rule. It symbolises a bigger image of the downfall of the old order.

* Colonizers question Igbo gods.

### **Interpretations of masculinity**

* Okonkwo views it with strength, aggression, and dominance.

* Yams symbolize manliness and high productivity.

* Nwoye rejects Okonkwo’s interpretation of masculinity.

* Okonkwo’s rigid interpretation is his fatal, tragic flaw.

### **Fate vs Free Will**

* Okonkwo’s downfall is a clash between personal choice and destiny.

* Ikemefuna’s death is decided by the Oracle.

### **Consequences of colonialism (change vs tradition)**

* Okonkwo resists change, which highlights his fear of losing cultural identity.

* Okonkwo’s loss of Nwoye to Christianity represents a wider generational shift to Christianity.

* The introduction of Christianity challenges the clan’s religious practices.

* Achebe emphasizes how colonialism is a double-edged sword. It brought benefits such as education and medicine; some characters benefit from it materially and mentally. The presence of Mr. Brown benefited Igbo society under his soft power. However, there was the risk of cultural destruction, especially under Reverand James Smith’s hard power.

### **The importance of cultural identity is evident through language and traditions**

* Proverbs are used in Igbo communication.

* Lack of communication leads to conflict.

* Colonizers are unwilling to learn the language, which exposes how ignorant they are.

* The District Commissioner’s attitude at the end shows how African voices are silenced and ignored.

### **Gender roles**

* Patriarchal.

* Male-dominant and men are providers.

* Women cook, take care of children and serve men. They have some important religious and market roles, yet their status are lower.

### **Family and Kinship**

* Significant within the culture.

* A key aspect that helps build up the Igbo society.

* Rebuke the stereotype of Igbo people being barbaric and apathetic.
