## Symbolism

### **Locusts**

* People had a lack of knowledge about it but chose to embrace it. Same with the colonialists’ arrival. Both seem harmless at first but then become destructive. People ate the locusts without knowing that they came to harm their crops.

* Rare; it appeared then disappeared for a long time. This mimics the time gap between the first and second arrival of the colonialists.

* A warning for something unknown/unfamiliar arriving in the village.

* Arrived one by one, then the number expands. This epitomizes the approach of the commissioners, having one man arrive first, then more over time.

* Destroyed and ate away crops. Crops embody the moral, cultural values of the Nigerian people and the colonialists will destroy them like locusts.

### **Kola nuts**

* Proverbs: Reveals the depth of Nigerian culture; it is not as primitive and barbaric as the stereotype.

* Educates the audience about the unknown side of Nigeria, especially the Western audience.

* Symbolizes hospitality/respect, social customs, importance of community bonding.

* Offered when conflict is about to happen, which depicts how the culture values the willingness to communicate instead of using violence to maintain peace.

* Welcoming and bonding.

* Examples: Unoka welcomes Okoye with kola nuts when Okoye asks for repayment to show respect and avoid conflict; Nwakibie shares kola nuts with Okonkwo when Okonkwo asks for yam seeds to show mutual respect in important conversations; kola nuts were presented in farewell feasts.

### **Fire**

* Crucial for survival. Builds up the society.

* Double-edged sword: This ‘fire’ personality did Okonkwo good in the past but destroys him in the future. It’s not good if it’s excessive.

* Used to purify land and sin; Okonkwo’s property was burnt when he was exiled.

* Used to burn down the church, represents the climax of the novel.

* Symbolises rage and rebellion.

* Okonkwo’s trait is a “Roaring Flame”: he lets it control him until it exhausts and destroys him. He has a fiery personality but it also leads to uncontrollable fury and impulsive actions.

### **Yams**

* Survival, the foundation of Ibo culture, blessing from god. People eat yams as the main source of food.

* Igbo’s calendar (New Yam Feast, the Week of Peace…); everything is based on the season of the yams.

* Represents merit and social status, property and value; Okonkwo tries to grow yams when he works his way up the social hierarchy.

* It’s the “King of Crops” because it can be grown anywhere.

* Okonkwo’s pride. Yams reflect his hard work and rigid adherence to traditional values.

* People’s lives and mentality rely on it.

* People share it with each other to show hospitality. It was present at feasts.

* Ikemefuna is forced to eat yams. This represents forced masculinity. Yams are directly tied to masculinity.

* It was mentioned less later in the novel: Colonialists introduce new values and meritocracy is replaced with capitalism.

### **Mbanta**

* Okonkwo’s motherland where he stayed during his exile: maternal connection. This highlights how society contains more than toxic masculinity, familial love and protection.

* Okonkwo spent 7 years here to rebuild his life.

* Reveals how society is not centralized only in Umuofia. It unfolds more about the complex community and reveals varied Igbo social dynamics.

### **Drums**

* Sense of community: unite people together. Present in a meeting or a wrestling contest.

* Used in celebration and artistic traditions.

* After the colonialists came, there were no more drums. This foreshadows the lack of union and lack of happiness to be celebrated. This links directly to *loss of communal voice*.

* Used when elders show leadership and power in ceremonies and festivals. There is no more freedom and authority after colonialism, as the culture is silenced.

### **Umuofia**

* Where Okonkwo builds up his life from zero.

* Subsistent society.

* The meaning of this word is “People of the Forest.”. People have a strong connection with nature; their lifestyle relies on agriculture.

* Has a high reputation for war and strength. This was established at the beginning of the novel.

* Value language and communication to resolve conflicts instead of violence. Trade Ikemefuna and a young woman to keep the peace.

* Strongest, well-established village, but still cannot escape colonialism. This highlights how abrasive, inescapable colonialism is and how fragile the community is.

### **The Evil Forest**

* Achebe reveals the myths and beliefs of the Igbo, emphasizing that the Igbo are not primitive and that it has a complex literature.

  * Examples: Twins are abandoned, people with shameful diseases are buried, those who commit immoral acts are buried…

* The first territory of commissioners where Christianity fosters (gained converts).

* Where untalented people go (osu, efulefu).

* Revived by Christians, which proves all the past fears of the Igbo are misguided.

  * It raised doubts about Igbo beliefs. For example, Nwoye became skeptical because of the twins.

* Achebe reveals how the Igbo have flaws in their beliefs. People hide from the mystery of the forest and leave it unanswered.

### **Sacred Cotton Tree**

* Spiritual beliefs.

* Connection with ancestors; during the public trial conducted by the Egwugwu, it symbolises justice.

* Contrasts with the colonizers’ disregard for Igbo traditions.

### **The Oracle**

* Voice of spiritual authority, guiding the clan.

  * Example: When Chielo said Agbala wanted to see Ezinma, Ekwefi and Okonkwo still had to obey.

* Represents the reliance on fate rather than personal agency.

* Symbolizes cultural conflict.

### **The Church**

* Arrival and expansion of Christianity. They built the church in the Evil Forest to mark their territory.

* A space for people to question and reject traditional Igbo practices.

* Disrupts the union of the Igbo society.

### **Chi**

* Spiritual guide.

* Balance between individual effort and fate.

  * Unoka had a bad chi so he had a bad fate.

  * Okonkwo’s chi aligns with his hard work; that explains why he is successful.

### **Egwugwu Masks**

* Represents the spirits of the ancestors.

* Serves as a bridge between the living and the dead.

* Justice, fairness, authority.

* Lost the authority to the colonialists.

  * For example, Enoch unmasked an Egwugwu under the encouragement of Reverend James Smith.
