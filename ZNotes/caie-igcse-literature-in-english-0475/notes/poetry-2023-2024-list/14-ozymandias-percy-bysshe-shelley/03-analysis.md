## Analysis

* The poem begins with the speaker telling the reader a story.
* The first line is mysterious and ambiguous. The speaker states that they ‘*met a traveller from an antique land*’. This is a highly confusing and ambiguous statement, and it leaves many questions to be answered.
* For example, has the traveller just journeyed through an ‘*antique land*’, or do they originate from such a place”? The ambiguity of this statement creates an air of intrigue.
* The speaker then retells what this traveller told him. He states that ‘*two vast and trunkless legs of stone stand in the desert*’. This is a slightly confusing line itself. Are the ‘*legs of stone*’ here metaphorical, or are they actual statues of legs?
* The word ‘*trunkless*’ means that the torso and body of the statue was missing, and can be indicative of how the speaker finds Ozymandias to be heartless and cruel in nature.
* However, the speaker clears up the antiquity by saying that ‘*Near them, on the sand, half sunk a shattered visage lies*’.
* The words ‘*half sunk*’ introduces one of the most important themes of the poem, which is legacy and time. The face is half-buried in the sand, and it is implied that given more time, it will disappear completely, much like the rest of the statue must have.
* The word ‘*shattered*’ is a metaphorical representation of how Ozymandias’ legacy and image has been destroyed, and how none of it still stands today.
* The speaker then describes the ‘*visage*’, saying that it has a ‘frown’, a ‘*wrinkled lip*’, and a ‘*sneer of cold command*’. The fact that these details are still visible, despite all the memory of Ozymandias and his empire being lost, is Shelley’s way of commenting on the eternal nature of art, and the way in which the negative qualities of one’s being tend to last longer than the positives.
* The description gives the image that Ozymandias prided himself on being a man with absolute power, and gives the image that he held a lot of pride as the leader of his civilisation.
* The traveler’s attention, and consequently the speaker’s attention, turns to the sculptor of the statue. He says that the ‘*sculptor well those passions read, which yet survive, stamped on these lifeless things*’. It shows how the sculptor was able to accurately identify the core values of who Ozymandias was as a person, and consequently, was able to imbue them onto the state.
* The usage of the word ‘*survive*’ is contrasted with the destruction of Ozymandias’ empire, and it shows how, in a way, the sculptor is a greater man than Ozymandias, for he was the one that was responsible for defining his legacy.
* The speaker states that Ozymandias had a ‘*hand that mocked them*’ and a ‘*heart that fed*’. This shows the duality of his nature, but also serves as a reminder that only one’s negative qualities are remembered as one’s legacy, as indicated by the fact that all that can be made out about Ozymandias from the statue is that he is a cold tyrannic ruler.
* Ozymandias’ egotistical nature is elaborated on further, through the speaker informing the reader of what the traveler claimed was written on the pedestal of the statue.
* On the pedestal, Ozymandias declared himself the ‘*king of kings*’ and said, ‘*Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair*’. However, these ‘*works*’ that are referenced here no longer exist, as indicated by the speaker stating that ‘*Nothing beside remains*’.
* This shows how it never serves well to be proud and egotistical, as all the reasons one has to be proud eventually fade away as time goes on.
* The speaker concludes by stating that ‘*Round the decay of that colossal wreck, boundless and bare the lone and level sands stretch far away*’. The word ‘*decay*’ emphasises the state of ruin of the statue, and the word ‘*colossal*’ emphasises the scale of the statue.
* The final statement is ominous, and shows how Ozymandias’ empire is all but gone, and that all that remains is just sand.
