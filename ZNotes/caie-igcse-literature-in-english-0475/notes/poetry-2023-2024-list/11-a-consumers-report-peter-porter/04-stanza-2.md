## Stanza 2

* The second stanza is where the review of Life begins. The speaker states that they ‘*had it as a gift*’. This is a metaphorical reference to how one does not pick to be born - it is not a choice.
* The word ‘*gift*’ also has connotations of celebration and happiness, and it is an indication of how life is something which should be cherished and not taken for granted.
* The speaker continues, saying that thay ‘*didn’t feel much while using it*’. This is a reference to how life, for the most part, is dull and lacking any excitement.
* The speaker says that they would have ‘l*iked to me more excited*’, and this echoes the views of many people, all of whom believe that there should be more excitement in life.
* The speaker states that it ‘*seemed gentle on the hands*’. This is a reference to how life seems gentle and simple in one’s childhood.
* The word ‘*seemed*’ implies that this is not actually a case, and acts as a reference to the fact that this simplicity of life fades away as one enters adulthood.
* The speaker explains that life has ‘*left an embarrassing deposit behind*’. The ‘*embarrassing deposit*’ being referred to here is a metaphor for all of the embarrassing memories that one makes as they live through life.
* These being described as a ‘*deposit*’ is representative of the human tendency to take longer to forget very embarrassing moments in life.
* They continue by saying that life is not ‘*economical*’, and follow this up by saying that they ‘*have used up much more than \[they\] thought*’. This is once again a reminder to the reader to not take life for granted, as it is very difficult to tell how much of one’s life one ends up using up in the pursuit of useless activities.
* The speaker here also estimates how much time they have left to use, by saying that they suppose they have ‘*about half left, but it’s difficult to tell*’, which is a reference to how one can never know when they will die, and that it is important to enjoy life as much as possible in the short span of time that one does have.
* The speaker says that the ‘*instructions*’ for life are ‘*fairly large*’ and there are ‘*so many of them*’. This shows how there are so many rules in life, both written and unwritten. It is a reference to the complicated social structure and rules of conduct while have been built up over generations.
* The speaker explains that they ‘*don’t know which to follow, especially as they seem to contradict each other*’. This shows the confusing nature of life, and how it is important for one to figure out what morals and rules are important to follow for oneself.
* The speaker’s next statement is ironic, as they say that they do not believe that life should ‘*be put in the way of children*’. This can also be interpreted as a reference to how children should not be made to have to deal with the serious aspects of life, and implies that children should be allowed to enjoy themselves without excess pressure.
* They say that it is ‘*difficult to think of a purpose*’, which represents how it is often difficult for one to understand what one wishes to achieve in life.
* The speaker states that the ‘*price*’ of life is ‘*much too high*’, which is a reference to how life comes with its problems and issues.
* The speaker states that the ‘*world got by for a thousand million years*’ without life, and questions whether it truly is necessary now. This can be interpreted as a reference to the negative impact which us humans have had on the planet, along with a sarcastic way for the speaker to say that the world was better off without life.
* The speaker talks about the lack of uniformity of life, by saying that there are a ‘*lot of different labels*’. This once again reiterates one of the major themes of this poem, which is that life is very confusing and difficult to understand.
* The speaker calls life’s shape ‘*awkward*’, saying that life is not smooth and normal.
* He says that it is waterproof, which is an ironic way of talking about how it does not go away very easily.
* This is reinforced by him saying that it is ‘*very difficult to get rid off*’.
* He talks about how the value of life seems to be going down with time.
* He says that life is ‘*delivered anyway*’ even if one does not want it, once again re-iterating that life is not a choice.
* He says that people ‘say they’re on the side’ of life, which talks about how people often opt to do evil things in the name of sustaining their own life. 
* They say that it’s ‘*overdone*’ and that people ‘*should take it for granted*’. This shows how he believes that people should just enjoy life, instead of trying to fuss about sustaining their lives for as long as possible.
* The speaker says that the opinions of ‘*philosophers or market researchers or historians*’ are irrelevant, and that each and every person has the right to live life in whatever way they want to.
* He concludes his review by saying that he would ‘*buy it*’, which is his way of getting across that life is worth it, and should not be discarded.
* The poem ends on a humorous note, with the speaker saying that he would leave the ‘*question of a best buy"‘* until he gets a ‘*competitive product*’.
