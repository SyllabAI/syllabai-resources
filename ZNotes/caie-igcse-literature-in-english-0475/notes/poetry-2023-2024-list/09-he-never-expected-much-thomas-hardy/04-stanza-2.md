## Stanza 2

* The second octet of the poem begins with the speaker saying, ‘*’Twas then you said, and since have said*’. The depth of the speaker’s use of apostrophes increases, as now they are not just conversing with the ‘*World*’ but are also saying that the ‘*World*’ used to speak to them when they were a child.
* This can be interpreted as the speaker referring to their own mind and subconsciousness communicating with them as a child and using the ‘*World*’ apostrophe to communicate their mental conversations with himself regarding life.
* The repetition of the word ‘*said*’ shows how this was not a one-time event and how it has been repeated multiple times across the speaker’s life.
* This is further supported by the second line of the stanza, which is ‘*Times since have said*’. The word ‘times’ indicates the frequency of these thoughts about life in the speaker’s mind.
* The speaker describes the ‘*World*’ as having a ‘*mysterious voice*’ which seemed to emanate ‘*From clouds and hills around*’. This shows how the speaker felt at one with nature and how, when lying there on the ground looking at the sky, it felt as if their surroundings were speaking to them.
* The speaker then properly personifies the ‘*World*’ for the first time by having it actually speak aloud to him, saying, ‘*Many have loved me desperately, / Many with smooth serenity, / While some have shown contempt of me / Till they dropped underground*’.
* Each of these lines is relevant, with each one showing a different outlook of people on life.
* The first is people who love life ‘*desperately*’. These are those who are desperate to cling to whatever life they have and are representative of the portion of society that spends most of their life trying to stay alive rather than enjoying it.
* The second is people who love life ‘*with smooth serenity*’. These are those who live life in peace and inaction, without taking any steps to better their lives, but just enjoying them as they are.
* The last category described here are those that show ‘*contempt*’ of life. This describes the portion of society that hates their lives and who holds a hostile outlook on life.
* The final line of this stanza, ‘*Till they dropped underground*’, shows how no matter which category a person falls into, all of society is united by one defining factor - irrespective of their opinion, all people die the same.
* This is the speaker providing a commentary on society and the different ways in which people choose to live their lives while also addressing the futility of living life with very extreme opinions, as no matter what your opinion is, everyone dies the same.
