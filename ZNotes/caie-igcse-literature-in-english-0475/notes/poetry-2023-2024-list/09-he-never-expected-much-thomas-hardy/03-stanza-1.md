## Stanza 1

* The first stanza of the poem begins with the speaker saying, ‘*Well, World, you have kept faith with me*’. This is an example of apostrophe, which is a literary device where a character or the speaker addresses and communicates with someone or something that is either not present, or does not exist in a physical form at all.
* The way in which the speaker addresses the ‘*World*’ throughout this poem seems to exude a feeling of familiarity and understanding. This shows how the speaker feels like they know the ‘*World*’ well, and how they feel like they know what their place in the world is.
* The calm and friendly way in which they converse with the world is a metaphorical reflection of their peaceful and friendly nature.
* The phrase *‘you have kept faith with me*’ gives a sign of a deep connection that the speaker hold with the world, and it sets the tone for the rest of the poem going forward.
* The second line is a reiteration of the same phrase, as it once again reads ‘*Kept faith with me*’. This repetition can be interpreted as a reference to how the speaker’s life has been consistent, and provides a metaphorical insight into the neutrality of their nature.
* The speaker continues by saying that the world has ‘*proved to be / much as \[it\] said \[it was\]*’. The speaker is not actually communicating with the ‘*World*’, so this line can be interpreted as the speaker attempting to say that life has been much like what they expected it to be from a young age.
* This shows how the speaker was intelligent from a young age, and once again re-iterates the message that the speaker feels as if they know what their place is in the world.
* The poem continues with the speaker describing a pastime of theirs from their childhood. This pastime of the speaker’s is unlike what most children do at a young age - instead of running around and playing, the speaker preferred to lie down, watch the sky, and think about life. This shows their wise and intelligent nature. 
* The fact that they were thinking about life is indicated by the last two lines of this stanza, which reads, ‘*Never, I own, expected I / That life would all be fair*’. Even at a young age, the speaker expected that life would not be all smooth sailing, and they expected to have to undergo some hardships in life.
