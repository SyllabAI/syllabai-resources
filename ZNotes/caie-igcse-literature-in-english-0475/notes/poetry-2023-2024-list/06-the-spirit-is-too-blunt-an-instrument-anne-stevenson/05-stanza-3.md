## Stanza 3

* The final stanza shifts its focus to the body’s creation, and how it is, in any way, linked with human emotions.
* The speaker directly challenges the reader with a sort of rhetorical requisition, demand that they ‘*name any passion of sentiment possessed of the simplest accuracy*’.
* As the reader would know, it is impossible to name any such element - emotions are not accurate, they are not precise, and they do not follow any particular trend. They are too inaccurate, and lack the precision, to create something such as the body, which the speaker states by saying ‘*no, no desire or affection could have done with practice what habit has done perfectly*’.
* The speaker acknowledges that it is impossible for one to practice one’s emotions to produce such a perfect result, and that it is simply habit that has led to the creation of the human body.
* This habit has carried out this task ‘*indifferently*’ through the ‘*body’s ignorant precision*’, by ignoring the imperfections of the spirit in order to create a perfect final product.
* The speaker ends on the note that the body initially has no pain or problems, it is ‘*left to the vagaries of the mind to invent love and despair and anxiety and their pain*’.
