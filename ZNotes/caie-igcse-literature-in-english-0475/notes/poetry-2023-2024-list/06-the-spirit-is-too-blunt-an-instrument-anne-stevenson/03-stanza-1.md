## Stanza 1

* The poem begins by re-iterating the title of the poem, by stating, ‘*The spirit is too blunt an instrument / to have made this baby*’.
* The speaker is admiring a baby, and upon examining it, the speaker believes that it is impossible for the human soul to have created something as intricate and perfect.
* The words ‘*too blunt*’ makes it clear that the speaker believes that the spirit is not precise enough to have created something like another human body. This introduces, from the very first line, the theme of this poem, which is the perfection of the body in contrast to the imperfection of the spirit.
* The speaker also refers to the spirit as being an ‘*instrument*’, displaying their opinion that the human soul is a tool to be used for one’s own benefit.
* By calling ‘*human passions’ ‘unskilful*’, the speakers displays their disdain for human passions and emotions. This shows the speaker’s mindset and contextualises everything they say for the rest of the poem.
* The speaker goes on to describe the ‘*tiny blind bones*’ of the baby. The speaker is awed that despite being unable to see around themselves, the bones of the baby are all arranged perfectly in the right way.
* The next part of the baby which the speaker points out is the ‘*manipulating tendons*’. The word ‘*manipulating*’ here creates the image in the reader’s mind of the tendons weaving themselves around the bones in intricate patterns. This supports the speaker’s argument that the spirit is too imperfect to design something as complicated as this.
* The speaker then names various other bones and parts of the body, such as the ‘*knee*’, ‘*knucklebones*’, ‘*ganglia*’, ‘*vertebrae*’, and ‘*spine*’.
* The speaker ends the first stanza at this point, after having described many different body parts, and why they believe that it is impossible for the soul to make them.
