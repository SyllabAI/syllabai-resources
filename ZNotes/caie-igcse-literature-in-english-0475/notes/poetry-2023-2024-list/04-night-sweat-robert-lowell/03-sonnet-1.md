## Sonnet 1

* The poem begins with the speaker listing various items that he can see around him in his room.
* The varied list of items shows how the speaker is unable to focus their thoughts on one direction, and is unable to write or come up with anything productive.
* Instead, in an attempt to possibly gain inspiration, or to simply calm his thoughts, he is looking around and listing everything he sees.
* The word ‘*stalled*’ being used to describe the ‘*equipment*’ shows how it is not in use, and is indicative of the fact that the speaker is unable to get any work done at the moment.
* The speaker describes the room that he is in as being a ‘*tidied room*’, which is in contrast with the description of items in the room which was given in the first two lines.
* This once again shows the lack of clarity of the speaker’s thoughts, and how he feels mentally lost at the moment. 
* The usage of the word ‘*nights*’ instead of ‘*days*’ is interesting, as it shows how the speaker has lost track of time, and shows how he has been unable to accomplish anything during the days, as he does not find them significant enough to even mention them.
* The phrase ‘*creeping damp*’ is a veiled reference to the speaker’s titular night sweat, and the presence of this sweat indicates just how much effort he has put into attempting to write.
* The word ‘*float*’ being used to describe the sweat shows how it seems detached from his body, as if it is not something physical but is more like a feeling, thus showing the impact which the sweat has on him and his thinking. 
* The usage of ‘*wilted*’ to describe the colour of his pyjamas is also significant, as it can be interpreted as a metaphorical reference to how flowers wilt and droop when they lose water.
* The speaker continues by describing the sensations that he experiences as he is drenched in sweat.
* He initially refers to the sweat as ‘*Sweet salt*’, which is an oxymoron.
* He states that the sweat ‘*embalms*’ him. This is a reference to the process of embalming, which is when a dead body is treated in order to preserve it and prevent it from decaying.
* In this way, the speaker is metaphorically likening himself to a corpse, which shows the lethargic state that he is in.
* He is also stating that the sweat is keeping him in this corpse-like state, preventing the lethargy from decaying and preventing him from being able to write.
* He states that his ‘*head is wet*’, which can be interpreted as a reference to how the sweat has not only impacted his body, but also his mind, since the head is often associated with the mind and one’s thought process.
* He finally, for the first time, states that the issue he is, in fact the titular night sweats, by saying ‘*my life’s fever is soaking in night sweat*’.
* He exclaims, ‘*one life, one writing!*’, which shows the speaker’s intentions with writing - he wishes to create one great work, only one, but one that will make him famous for eternity.
* Going on, the speaker states the ‘*downward glide and bias of existing wrings us dry*’, a statement through which he communicates that living itself tends to strip a person of all creativity.
* The word ‘*dry*’ here is juxtaposed with all of the references to being ‘*wet*’ earlier on in the poem, and signifies a shift in narrative away from the significance of the night sweat.
* The speaker says, ‘*always inside me is the child who died, / always inside me is his will to die*’. The child, here, refers to his hope and child-like spirit, which he claims has ‘*died*’.
* He also claims that this child’s ‘*will to die’* is always inside him, which is indicative of his depression and how he constantly feels like a part of him wishes to die.
* ‘*Always inside me*’ is repeated twice for emphasis.
* The speaker continues, stating, ‘*one universe, one body… in this urn, the animal night sweats of the spirit burn*’. Him saying ‘one universe, one body’ here is time expressing his belief that he only has one life, and one chance to create his one great literary piece.
* He refers to his body as an ‘*urn*’, showing his morbid approach to life and how he is fixated on the idea that he will, someday, die.
* The idea of heat from ‘*wrings us dry*’ is once again re-iterated here, as he said ‘*night sweats of the spirit burn*’.
