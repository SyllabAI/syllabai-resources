## Sonnet 2

* While the larger focus of the first sonnet was on the speaker’s night sweat and inability to get work done, along with his morbid opinions on reality and death, the second sonnet brings with it an uplift in the tone.
* This sonnet focuses more on the impact of the speaker’s wife (addressed in the second person) on him, and how being around her allows him to be creative.
* He starts this section of the poem by exclaiming ‘*Behind me! You!*’, which is representative of the moment that he sees his wife.
* He states that ‘*Again \[he\] feel\[s\] the light lighten \[his\] leaded eyelids*’. The word ‘*again*’ indicates that this is not the first time that his wife’s presence has invigorated him and allowed his creativity to come back to life again.
* The word ‘*light*’ is representative of his wife’s positive impact on him, and how to him, it feels as if her presence brightens the surroundings. 
* He continues by stating that ‘t*he grey skulled horses whinny for the soot of the night*’. The ‘*grey skulled horses*’ here represent the negative parts of his mental state, and are a general metaphor for all things evil.
* By having them ‘*whinny for the soot of the night*’, the speaker is attempting to convey that his wife’s ‘*light*’ makes it too bright for them to be comfortable, that is, she drives away all things evil with her very presence.
* The speaker’s admiration and love for his wife is quite clear, and it is also clear how much he depends on her. He describes himself as ‘*a heap of wet clothes*’, but it is interesting to note that he only describes his clothes as sweat-soaked, and not himself.
* This can be interpreted as his wife’s presence having made him shed the sweat and embrace his creativity.
* He says that he sees his ‘*child exploding into dynamite*’, through which he means that his child-like emotions, such as hope, have come back to him and have no longer ‘*died*’ as described in the first sonnet.
* He states that his wife’s ‘*lightness alters everything*’, showing the profound impact she has on his mental state and creativity.
* He describes and states that his wife ‘*tears the black web from the spider’s sack*’. Here, the ‘*spider’s sack*’ is a metaphor for his mind, and the ‘*black web*’ is a metaphor for his negative and morbid thoughts.
* By tearing the ‘*black web*’ from the ’*spider’s sack*’, the speaker means that his wife’s presence causes all negative emotions and thoughts to leave his mind, leaving it only full of positive emotions and creative ideas.
* He states that his wife’s ‘*heart hops and flutters like a hare*’. The hare is often used as a symbol of energy and vigour, and here, he is saying that his wife’s heart, which is representative of her spirit and psyche, is full of energy, and is not lethargic like his was a few moments ago.
* He also refers to his wife as ‘*Poor turtle, tortoise*’. Here, the usage of the word ‘*poor*’ shows that he possible feels bad for his wife, who has to take the burden of maintaining his mental state, and, as he later states, bearing ‘*this world’s dead wright and cycle on \[her\] back*’.
* The usage of ‘turtle, tortoise’ here ties back to two different myths, both of which play into the wife’s character.
* First (and this one is supported by him calling her heart a ‘*hare*’ in the previous line), it could be interpreted as a reference to the class fable of the turtle and the hare. By comparing her to both the turtle and the hare at two different points in the poem, the speaker is saying that his wife has the best qualities of both of the main characters of the story.
* The second myth which he may be referencing is the once-popular myth that the earth was actually carried by a massive turtle, and he has implied the same by saying that his wife carries the ‘*world’s dead weight*’ on her back, much like the turtle in the story.
