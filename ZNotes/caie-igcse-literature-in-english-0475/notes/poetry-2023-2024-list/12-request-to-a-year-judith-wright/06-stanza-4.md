## Stanza 4

* The fourth stanza also continues the same sentence that was started in the very first line of the poem, using enjambement.
* The speaker goes on to finish the story, by saying that the great-great-grandmother’s second daughter, ‘*impeded, no doubt, by the petticoats of the day*’, extended a ‘last-hope alpenstock’ to her brother.The fact that the daughter was ‘*impeded*’ by ‘*petticoats*’ shows how women were shackled and restricted in what they could do in society at that time.
* An alpenstock is something which is typically used to allow people to balance themselves, and the fact that it was used to help the son is ironic, as it did, in fact, allow him to regain his balance.
* The speaker concludes this story, and this sentence, by saying, ‘*(which luckily later caught him on his way)*’. The usage of brackets shows how the emphasis of the story is not on the actual events, but on what the grandmother saw.
