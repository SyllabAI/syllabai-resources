## Analysis

* The poem begins with the titular phrase ‘*Away melancholy*’, and this is follow up with ‘*Away with it, let it go*’. These lines are repeated all throughout the poem, and form the backbone of its structure. They are repeated in order to show how the speaker wants to get rid of their sadness and ‘*melancholy*’, and they help to reiterate the themes of the poem in each stanza, which are, despair and hope.
* The poem then truly begins, with the speaker asking, ‘*Are not the trees green, / The art as green?*’. This rhetorical question can be interpreted as a reference to environmental damage, and how humans have had an impact on the greenness of the earth.
* The speaker continues by saying, ‘*Does the wind not blow, / Fire leap and the rivers flow?*’. All of these different references to the many different characteristics of nature act as a way for the speaker to address that nature is constant in life, and how nature is a reminder that life has some normality to it.
* This stanza ends with the titular statement being reiterated.
* The next stanza focuses on the metaphor of an ant, and the ant is compared to man, and is used as another way to describe the repetitive and simple way in which the natural world operates.
* The speaker states that ‘*The ant is busy*’. This is a reference to how ants are symbolic of working, and this is shown due to the fact that being ‘*busy*’ is something often associated with ants.
* We are told that the ant carries ‘*his meat, all things hurry*’. This shows the hurried yet repetitive lifestyle of the ant.
* The speaker then says, ‘*To be eaten or eat*’. This is a reference to the metaphorical circle of life, and how in this world, nature keeps moving. One must either eat, or be eaten oneself.
* This explains all of the references to hurry and business, as the speaker is attempting to say here that there is no point in staying upset and still when the world and nature is going to keep moving on anyways.
* The stanza ends with a reiteration of ‘*Away, melancholy*’.
* The next stanza compares man to an ant, by saying that ‘*Man, too, hurries, eats, couples, buries*’. This shows the depression and despair of the speaker - she sees to be disturbed by this, and by comparing humans to ants, seems to be attempting to stay that humans are meaningless and their importance is miniscule.
* The speaker continues by saying that man ‘*is an animal also*’, showing their belief that humans, too, follow the basic repetitiveness and simplicity of the natural lifestyle of an animal.
* The speaker says, ‘*with a hey ho melancholy*’, which is the speaker saying that man possesses more melancholy than other animals, which sets them apart.
* Repetition of one of the key two repeated lines occurs again at the end of this stanza.
* The speaker continues to say that ‘*Man of all creatures is superlative*’, which is in contrast to the way in which humans are portrayed as meaningless through their comparison to ants earlier on in the poem.
* This stanza is different from the ones that came before it. Every two lines are interposed with ‘*(Away melancholy)*’, which shows how the topic of depression and despair is on the speaker’s mind, and which also shows how she do not wish to think negative thoughts.
* This stanza comes off as the speaker trying to convince herself to put away her negativity and embrace the many positives of being a human.
* The speaker states that ‘*He of all creatures alone raiseth a stone*’, where ‘*stone*’ is a metaphor for what man is capable of building and creating.
* The speaker states, ‘*Into the stone, the god pours what he knows of good calling good, God*’. The utilisation of both lowercase and uppercase for the word ‘*god*’ shows the transition of the poem from one that is generic, to one that has a highly religiously and spiritually motivated tone, and shows how the speaker feels like God helps lift her out of her despair and depression.
* They call God ‘*good*’, reiterating this idea.
* The speaker asks the reader to ‘*speak not to me of tears, tyranny, pox, war*’, showing how she does not wish to believe that God could ever do anything wrong.
* The speaker holds the belief that God is good, and does not want this to be questioned.
* The usage of first person in this shows how the speaker is finally voicing her own ideals and beliefs in the poem.
* The speaker continues by saying that even though God is ‘*stone of man’s thought*’, he is good, and that is what she wants to believe.
* The speaker says that humanity was made good, by saying that god is the ‘*stone of man’s good*’, and that humanity made a good god, saying, ‘*man’s called god*’.
* The speaker states that ‘*Man aspires to good, to love*’, showing the speaker’s belief that humans are pure at heart, and how they were created to be good.
* The speaker sighs, showing how they feel like are at peace.
* They conclude the poem by saying that even when humans are ‘*beaten, corrupted, dying in his own blood lying*’, they cry ‘*love, love*’. The usage of highly negative words, which are contrasted with the hopeful tone, shows the darkness of life.
* Despite all of the negativity of life, humanity lives on, stays hopeful, and stays good.
