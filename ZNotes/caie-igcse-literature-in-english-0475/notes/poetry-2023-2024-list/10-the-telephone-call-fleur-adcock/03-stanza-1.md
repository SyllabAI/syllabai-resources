## Stanza 1

* The first stanza of the poem begins on an abrupt note, without any preamble. The poem straight launches into the story of the telephone call, with ‘They asked me *‘Are you sitting down?’*.
* The abrupt way in which this begins without describing any of the events leading up to the call, shows how unexpected and out of place the call was. It also shows how rushed the speaker’s emotions were without the call, and how the call was so stunning to them that they forgot everything that happened before it.
* The usage of the word ‘*they*’ exudes a sense of mystery. It sets an ominous tone for the rest of the poem, and creates the image in the reader’s mind that all is not as it seems.
* The caller asking the speaker to sit down sets up a sense of anticipation for what will come next.
* The caller then seems to introduce themselves, by saying that they are ‘*Universal Lotteries*’. This name is very ambiguous, and this is probably intentional.
* The caller declares that the speaker has won their ‘*top prize, / the Ultra-super Global Special*’. The extremely over-the-top name of this prize, along with how ridiculous it seems to sound, acts as the first indicator that the prize is not actually real.
* The caller’s deceptive techniques continue. They do not outright state what the speaker has won, instead asking them what they would ‘*do with a million pounds*’. The phrasing of this sentence comes back to haunt the speaker as the poem goes on, when it is eventually revealed that all of this is fake.
* The caller saying ‘*Or, actually, with more than a million*’ is also foreshadowing the fact that this is all a trick - an actual lottery company would state the actual amount, especially if it was a very large amount.
* They say that it does not make ‘*a lot of difference once you’re a millionaire*’. This can be interpreted as Adcock commenting on how after a certain point, people start losing interest in their money, and how money cannot translate directly to happiness.
* The stanza ends with the speaker informing the reader that the caller ‘*laughed*’, which foreshadows the eventual reveal of the poem.
