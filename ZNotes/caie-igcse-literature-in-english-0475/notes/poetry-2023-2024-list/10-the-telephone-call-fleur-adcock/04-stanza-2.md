## Stanza 2

* The speaker does not say anything to respond to what the caller says, indicating the state of shock and disbelief that they are in. The caller reacts to this silence by asking the speaker if they are ‘*OK*’ and if they are ‘*still there*’.
* Despite the presence of words of concern, the delivery of these words seems aggressive, as if they are mocking the speaker.
* Their patronising attitude continues with the next dialogue of theirs, which reads, ‘*Come on, no, tell us, how does it feel?*’. The caller’s emphasis on emotions, and how the speaker feels upon learning of having ‘won’ the lottery, is a recurring theme throughout the poem.
* The speaker finally responds to the caller, saying, ‘*I just… I can’t believe it!*’. This phrase is ironic in the context of the entire poem - the speaker’s initial instincts were, in fact, correct.
* The poem acts as a depiction of how emotions can lead to clouded judgement.
* The caller responds to this by saying, ‘*That’s what they all say. / What else? Go on, tell us about it*’. The first part of this response is extremely suspicious - how has the caller given away the ‘*top prize*’ so many times?
* The caller’s insistent tone shows their impatience, and their constant stress on asking the speaker how they feel shows how their ultimate objective here is to understand what emotions the speaker is going through.
* The speaker responds by saying that they feel as if ‘*the top of \[their\] head has floated off, out through the window, revolving like a flying saucer*’. This can interpreted as a metaphorical reference to how the speaker has lost all sense of rational thinking due to the surprise and shock that they are going through - it is as if their capacity to think with reason and ration has ‘*floated off, out through the window*’.
* The phrase ‘*revolving like a flying saucer*’ uses a simile to create a whimsical image in the speaker’s mind, and it maintains the overall humorous tone of the poem.
