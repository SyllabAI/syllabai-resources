## Stanza 4

* The final stanza shifts tense from the past to the present. While the first three stanzas were describing events from the past, this one now discusses what the speaker is doing today, and what his situation is.
* The first line of this stanza is ‘*I believe life ends with death, and that is all*’. This is an intriguing statement  - it shows the rational way in which the speaker likes to think of things, and how he believes in the finality of death.
* This is reinforced by him ending the sentence with ‘*and that is all*’, which gives it a final and concluding tone to it.
* The next sentence, ‘*You haven’t both gone shopping; just the same*’, provides the first indication that both of the speaker’s parents have now passed away, and even though it is not explicitly stated in the following lines, it is certain that this is the case.
* The subtle and veiled way in which the speaker implies his father’s death shows how raw the grief that he is feeling is, and how he does not wish to directly state it, unlike the direct and straightforward way in which he addresses his mother’s death in the first stanza.
* The poem ends with the speaker stating that he still has his father’s name in his ‘*new black leather phone book*’, and he sometimes still calls the ‘*disconnected number*’.
* The word ‘*new*’ being used to describe the phone book shows how despite the speaker having gotten a new book, he has still copied his father’s name across, and the fact that he still calls the number directly contrasts with his statement in the first line of this stanza.
* This shows how grief can truly affect a person, and no matter what their ideals are, it can change the way in which one acts, much like the speaker in this poem.
