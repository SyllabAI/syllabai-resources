## Stanza 3

* The third stanza begins with ‘*He couldn’t risk my blight of disbelief*’. The phrase ‘*couldn’t risk*’ shows how the father is afraid of being judged for his coping mechanisms. Even though the reader is aware that the son would not judge his father, and does not judge him, the father is unsure of this.
* As elaborated on earlier, he is insecure and ashamed, and does not want others to know of what he does to grieve. The irrationality of his actions is not lost on him.
* The word ‘*blight*’ indicates that the father believes that his son’s disbelief would be like a disease, and that it could lead to the destruction or weakening of their relationship; something which he does not wish to risk.
* However, the father is ‘*sure that very soon he’d hear her key / scrape in the rusted lock and end his grief*’. The word ‘*sure*’ indicates the strength of the father’s love and grief.
* The usage of ‘*key*’ shows how to the father, his wife is locked away, just out of reach, but very much present.
* This is reinforced by ‘*rusted lock*’. The adjective ‘*rusted*’ being used to describe the lock shows how it has not been used for a very long time, indicating just how long it has been since the speaker’s mother has died
*  This stanza ends with the line ‘*He* knew *she’d just popped out to get the tea*’. The italicisation of ‘*knew*’ shows the strength of the father’s belief. The phrase ‘*popped out*’ makes it seem like the mother has only been gone for a very short time.
