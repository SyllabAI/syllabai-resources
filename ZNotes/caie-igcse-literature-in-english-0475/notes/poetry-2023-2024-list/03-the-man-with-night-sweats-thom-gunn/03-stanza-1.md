## Stanza 1

* The word ‘*cold*’ clues the reader in on the fact that the speaker has fallen victim to the titular ‘*night sweats*’ but does not obviously state this.
* The word ‘*heat*’ is juxtaposed with the word ‘*cold*’ from the poem's first line. The word ‘*prospered*’ indicates success, but the wealth and success the speaker feels like he attains in his ‘*dreams of heat*’ are all not actually real and are just - dreams.
* Here, ‘*dreams of heat*’ is a metaphorical reference to dreams of passion and love, which is a reference to how AIDS, the disease which the speaker suffers from in the poem, is transmitted.
* The speaker states that he wakes to the ‘*residue*’ of these dreams, with the ‘*residue*’ here being ‘*sweat, and a clinging sheet*’. The word ‘*residue*’ describes the sweat and how these dreams have a longer-lasting impact on his mental state and strength.
* The reader’s possible suspicions from the first line are finally confirmed here, with the reason for the speaker’s coldness being revealed to be sweat. The sweat links together the ‘*cold*’ and ‘*heat*’ from the first two lines, showing how heat can lead to feeling cold, contextualising those lines and making their meaning clearer to the reader.
* The words ‘*clinging sheet*’ are also relevant. As the speaker later discusses in the poem, their flesh once acted as a shield, but now, they require a sheet to shield themselves from external stimuli. This shows the impact of the disease.
