## Stanza 1

* The word ‘*cruising*’ gives the idea of a peaceful and calm motion and one which is not rushed in any manner. This image of calmness is reinforced by the usage of the word ‘*Sunday*’, which is often indicative of rest and thankfulness in many cultures.
* This line seems to give the impression that the speaker is carrying out a very ordinary and everyday activity.
* The juxtaposition of the words ‘*offends*’ and ‘*sanities*’ sets up what is a recurring theme throughout the rest of the poem - the speaker’s disdain for the overly rectified and mathematical way in which the suburbs have been constructed.
* The word ‘*pedantic*’ typically has a more negative connotation than similar words such as ‘*meticulous*’, and Atwood reinforces that the speaker disapproves of the ordered way the houses are placed.
* The word ‘*planted*’ works as a double entendre here - it can both be interpreted as just a simple reference to how the trees have been planted there or can be interpreted as a reference to the alternative meaning of plant as a person placed in a group as a spy or informer.
* The fact that even the trees have been stripped of their natural randomness and all made ‘*sanitary*’ shows the extent to which humanity is willing to go in order to attain order and control.
* The word ‘*assert*’ gives the trees and rows of houses an almost hostile impression in the reader’s mind. The fact that they are asserting the ‘*levelness of surface*’ is significant, as perfectly level surfaces do not typically exist in the natural world, and the fact that the houses and trees are all perfectly levels gives an insight into just how many of their features have been changed and ‘perfected’.
* The word ‘*rebuke*’ shows how the dent in the car door seems to stand out in the highly ordered landscape that is the suburbs. The dent in question acts as a marker of reality, and a reminder to the reader that nothing is perfect.
* The speaker then talks about the absence of all kinds of natural sounds and noises in the suburbs, how there is no ‘*shouting*’ or ‘*shatter of glass*’. The only sound to be heard is the ‘*rational whine of a power mower / cutting a straight swath in the discouraged grass*’. The word rational is a reminder to the reader that the lawn mower is also man-made, just like the rows of houses.
* The words ‘*straight swath*’ shows how even the lawn mower is being operated in a very straightforward and controlled manner, the word ‘*discouraged*’ being used to describe the grass shows natural features and tendencies have been repressed.
