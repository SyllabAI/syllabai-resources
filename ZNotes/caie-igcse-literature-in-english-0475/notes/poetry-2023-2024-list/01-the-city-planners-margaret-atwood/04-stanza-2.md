## Stanza 2

* The second stanza begins with the word ‘*But*’, which indicates that not everything is as it seems in the suburbs.
* The words ‘*neatly*’ and ‘*even*’ being used to describe the driveways and the roofs once again re-iterate the mathematical precision with which the suburbs have been constructed.
* The word ‘*hysteria*’ in the phrase ‘*driveways neatly / sidestep hysteria*’ seems to show the unstable mental state of the speaker.
* The phrase ‘*slant of avoidance to the hot sky*’ has two points of note - first, the words ‘*slant of avoidance*’, which act as a reference to how the suburbs’ construction is an attempt to avoid the reality of life, and second, the word ‘*hot*’, which has a very harsh and negative connotation.
* The speaker follows up this description by providing a short list of all of the subtle imperfections that they have been able to make out in this highly controlled and ‘perfect’ landscape. This list is juxtaposed with the list of mathematical perfections which was given through the first stanza and the beginning of the second stanza.
* The word ‘*sickness*’ being used here shows how the smell of oil seems to be almost foreign.
* The speaker also compares a ‘*splash of paint on brick*’ as being as ‘*surprising as a bruise*’ using a simile. It uses the image of a bruise standing out on fair skin to show how prominent the paint stain seems.
* The word ‘*vicious*’ describing the way in which a ‘*plastic hose*’ is ‘*poised*’ contrasts the winding pattern of the hose with the perfection of the houses.
