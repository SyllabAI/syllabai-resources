## Analysis

* The phrase *‘remembering again’* implies that this is not the first time that the speaker has spent time pondering his own mortality and the topic of his own inevitable death.
* The blunt way in which he simply states that he *‘shall die’* shows how the war has stripped him of all subtlety, and shows how being in the war has forced him to confront the fact that he will likely die soon.
* By saying *‘And neither hear the rain nor give it thanks / For washing me cleaner than I have been’*, the speaker makes it clear that his thoughts are significant enough to block out the sound of the rain; may also be interpreted as a reference to him possibly being shellshocked.
* The speaker saying that he is thankful for the rain ‘*washing \[him\] cleaner than \[he has\] been*’ can be interpreted both literally and figuratively - literally, the rain washes the dirt off him, and figuratively, *‘washing me cleaner’* can be interpreted as a reference to baptism and one’s sins being washed away. 
* The word *‘born’* in line 6 shows how the speaker believes that becoming a part of the war has changed him into an entirely new individual, as if he has been born again. It shows how the world around him, the world of the war, is different from anything else he had ever seen before in the past, and how everything is new for him.
* The next line reads, ‘*Blessed are the dead that the rain rains upon*’. This once again brings back the theme of death, which is a recurring element of this poem. Just like how the speaker believes that the rain has washed him clean of his sins, he also believes that it will do the same for the dead.
* The way in which this line is placed right after him stating that he was ‘*born*’ into this new world creates an example of juxtaposition, and makes it seem like the speaker is referring to himself as one of the ‘*dead*’ that are blessed by the rain, showing how for him and others like him, death feels like salvation.
* Line 8 begins with the word ‘*But*’, as the speaker shares a wish that is juxtaposed with him stating that he should be thankful for the rain, and that those under it are blessed. He states that he wishes to ‘*pray that none whom once \[he\] loved / Is dying to-night or lying still awake*’. The word ‘*pray*’ brings back the theme of religion and God, which was introduced when the speaker likened the rain to baptism.
* The usage of the word ‘*once*’ implies that this love for these people existed only in the past. This seems to give the idea that the speaker has become desensitised and incapable of love after joining the war, and, once again, shows the adverse impact which his surroundings have had on him.
* The speaker states that he wishes that no one is ‘*Solitary, listening to the rain*’. This, combined with his wish that no one is ‘*lying still awake*’, makes it clear that he does not want anyone to be in the position that he is in. The pity that he is showing the people whom he is referring to can also be applied to him, and he understands, and knows, that the situation he is in is one that he would not wish on anyone he loves.
* He does not wish for his loved ones to be ‘*Either in pain or thus in sympathy*’. This makes it clear that the speaker is in pain, and he does not wish for others to be in such a situation.
* Even though he has implied earlier that he does not love people anymore, he is clearly still a compassionate and sympathetic individual. He does not wish for his loved ones to be (like him) ‘*Helpless among the living and the dead*’. This shows the extent of the destruction which the war has brought with it, and how the speaker does not believe himself to be either completely alive or completely dead.
* In line 13, the speaker compares himself to one of the many ‘*broken reeds all still and stiff*’ in cold water. He believes himself to be ‘*broken*’, showing the impact the war has had on him and his psyche.
* The phrase ‘*still and stiff*’ shows how he feels stuck in place, unable to leave, and unable to do anything to soothe his pain.
* He says that those like him ‘*have no love which this wild rain / Has not dissolved except the love of death*’. This is an example of irony, as it is not possible for him to have no love if he just showed sympathy for others.
* The speaker claims that the ‘*wild rain*’ (which is repeated from the first line) has washed away all of his love, except for ‘*the love of death*’. He explains that the reason which he loves death is that death ‘*Cannot, the tempest tells \[him\], disappoint*’. This ties back to the theme of death being inevitable.
