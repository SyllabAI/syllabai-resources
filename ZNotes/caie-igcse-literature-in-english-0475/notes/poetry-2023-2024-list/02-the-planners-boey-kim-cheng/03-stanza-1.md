## Stanza 1

* The first stanza begins with a sequence of short sentences, one after another - ‘*They plan. They build.*’. The short phrases highlight how this planning and building seems to happen very quickly and show the monotonous manner in which the process constantly repeats itself.
* The next sentence spans lines 1 and 2 using enjambement and reads, ‘*All spaces are gridded, / filled with permutations of possibilities*’. The word ‘*gridded*’ gives the first insight into the mathematical precision of the planning and building that will be described later throughout the poem.
* The phrase ‘*permutations of possibilities*’ is especially impactful. Both ‘*possibilities*’ and ‘*permutations*’, when utilised in the correct context, can act as synonyms, so ‘*permutations of possibilities*’ seem to show how the different ways grids can be constructed are enclosed.
* Moving on, the speaker describes the mathematical precision which was hinted at earlier. They state that the ‘*buildings are in alignment with the roads*’, which shows how everything has been constructed to be as perfect as possible.
* The roads ‘*meet at desired points*’, which refers to how everything they build follows the titular Planners’ desires.
* The reader is informed that the points are ‘*linked by bridges*’ that ‘*hang / in the grace of mathematics*’. The references to ‘*bridges*’ are relevant, as they often symbolise connection and change.
* The phrase ‘*grace* *of* *mathematics*’ shows how mathematics is the ‘God’ of this world and how everything in existence exists because of it.
* Until this point in the stanza, it seems like the reader approves of what the Planners are doing, and they even seem to be showing some admiration for the perfection that is created. However, this changes with the last three lines of the stanza, which begin with ‘*They* *build* *and* *will* *not* *stop*’.
* This shows the relentless pursuit of the Planners, and the phrase ‘*will* *not* *stop*’ indicates how they refuse to acknowledge the negatives of this pursuit and how they simply keep going.
* The last two lines of this stanza read, ‘*the* *sea* *draws* *back* *and* *the* *skies* *surrender*’. This shows how nature seems wounded and negatively impacted by the Planners’ actions.
* ‘*Draws* *back*’ shows how nature has been repressed, and ‘*surrender*’ gives the impression that the skies are under attack.
