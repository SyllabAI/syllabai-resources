## Stanza 3

* The concluding stanza of the poem is only four short lines in length.
* The speaker says that their ‘heart would not bleed / poetry’ and that they would not want a ‘single drop to stain the blueprint / of our past’s tomorrow’. This statement is ironic, as, in a poem about the Planners, the speaker is stating that they would not write any poetry about the Planners.
* The word ‘stain the blueprint’ seems to imply that poetry would have some form of negative impact on the Planners’ plans for the future, and here, the speaker seems to be, in a veiled manner, trying to encourage any poets reading the poem to follow suit and also write poetry about the future the Planners are trying to create.
* The poem’s final words, ‘past’s tomorrow’, are an oxymoron, and once again reiterate the Planners’ wish to erase the ‘past’ in order to create ‘tomorrow’ in their image.
