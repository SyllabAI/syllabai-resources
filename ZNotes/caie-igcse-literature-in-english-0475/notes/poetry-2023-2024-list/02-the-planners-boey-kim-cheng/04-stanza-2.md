## Stanza 2

* The second stanza uses an extended metaphor to compare the work of the Planners with that of a dentist.
* The highly clinical and cold way in which the Planners’ actions are described shows their emotionless and dry nature, and how they have trivialised the destruction that comes with perfection.
* The phrase ‘*erase the flaws*’ show the almost unnatural way in which the Planners simply eliminate all ‘*flaws*’, which are the imperfections of nature.
* The phrase ‘*blemishes of the past*’ seems to refer to the history and legacy of those who lived in the past, but the Planners wish to ‘*erase*’ that as well, showing their complete disregard for their history. The reader is informed that the Planners ‘*knock off useless blocks*’.
* The phrase ‘*knock off*’ seems crude, and shows how despite their pursuit of perfection, the Planners are not as sophisticated as the world they are trying to build.
* This action is described as being done with ‘*dental dexterity*’, and this marks the beginning of the dental metaphor.
* The speaker goes on to say that ‘*All gaps are plugged / with gleaming gold*’. Here, gaps is a metaphorical comparison of imperfections to gaps between teeth. The ‘*gold*’ here is the artificial perfection created by the Planners to get rid of this imperfection.
* The word ‘*gleaming*’ seems to be a reference to how the perfection of the Planners seems to be a good thing initially, but upon closer inspection, it reveals itself to be more than what meets the eye.
* The speaker compares the rows of houses to *‘perfect rows of / shining teeth*’, with the ‘*perfect rows*’ being the suburbs and rows of houses, and the ‘shining teeth’ are the houses.
* In the next line, the speaker lists some of the methods that the Planners have of getting what they want done: ‘*Anaesthesia, amnesia, hypnosis*’. Anaesthesia refers to the feeling of numbness and shock, amnesia refers to forgetfulness, and hypnosis refers to the Planners’ ability to manipulate and control the way in which people think.
* The speaker follows up this list with the statements, ‘*They have the means. / They have it all so it will not hurt*’. These declarations are ominous, and makes the Planners out to be villainous and authoritarian. The Planners seem to be trying to play God in the new world that they are building.
* The speaker explains that the Planners wish to make it so ‘*history is new again*’. This is an oxymoron - ‘*history*’ and ‘*new*’ are opposites, and this shows how the Planners are willing to completely erase history and replace it with the present.
* The stanza ends with ‘*The drilling goes right through / the fossils of last century*’. The word ‘*drilling*’ ties back to the dental imagery. The phrase ‘*fossils of last century*’ is a reference to how the Planners are willing to erase the past to create a ‘*perfect*’ future.
