## Stanza 1

* The first stanza begins with the words ‘*stop all of the clocks*.’ The speaker’s grief and wish to be in peace is evident from the first statement - they wish to grieve without the pressure of time.
* It may also be a metaphorical reference to how time seems to pass excruciatingly slow when one is in mourning.
* The next demand of the speakers is to ‘*cut off the telephone*’, which shows how they do not wish to communicate with anyone, along with how they do not wish to be disturbed by the sounds of the phone ringing.
* The speaker’s wish to ‘*Prevent the dog from barking*’ is an early manifestation of their wish to manipulate and control nature, something that becomes more apparent later on in the play.
* They provide a reasonable way to silence the dog - giving it a ‘*juicy bone*.’
* They also state that they wish to ‘*silence the pianos*.’ Music is typically associated with feelings of positivity and celebration, and the speaker does not wish to indulge in the joys of life at the moment
* This shows how grief is all-encompassing and how it tends to make people wish they did not enjoy other things in life.
* This stanza concludes with another demand of the speaker - ‘*with muffled drum / Bring out the coffin, let the mourners come*’. The phrase ‘*muffled drum*’ can be interpreted in two ways - first, as the actual beating of a drum at a funeral, and two, the sound made by the feet of the pallbearers.
* The usage of ‘*muffled*’ shows the speaker’s wish to subdue the sound of an instrument that is as loud as the drum, showing, once again, their aversion to music and distractions and their will to grieve in peace.
* The ‘*coffin*’ and the ‘*mourners*’ serve as the first indications in the poem that the reason for the speaker’s fractured mental state is that someone close to them has died.
