## Stanza 3

* The third stanza veers away from the discussion of the speaker’s many wishes, and instead, focuses on what the deceased meant to the speaker.
* This stanza’s main themes are the themes of love, loss and grief. The stanza is representative of the speaker learning to open up more, and to allow themselves to grieve properly.
* The stanza opens with the words, ‘*He was my North, my South, my East and West*’. This can be interpreted two ways.
* One, no matter what direction the speaker looks in, all they can remember and think of is their dead love one. This shows just how much the deceased meant to them, and the significance they held in their life.
* The second interpretation is that the mentioning the four directions is a metaphorical way for the speaker to say that the deceased represented their purpose and direction in life, and how now, they feel like they have lost all sense of direction, but literally and figuratively.
* The speaker’s metaphoric declarations continue, with ‘*My working week and my Sunday rest*’. The working week, combined with Sunday, make up all of the days of the week.
* Here, the speaker is trying to say that their love for the deceased was perennial and that it penetrated into their lives everyday.
* This is further emphasised by the next metaphorical comparison, with the speaker saying that the deceased was the speaker’s ’*midnight*’ and their ’*noon*’.
* The speaker goes on to say that the deceased was their ’*talk*’ and their ’*song*’. Through this, what the speaker is trying to convey is that the deceased was a very large part of their personality (‘*talk*’) and energy (‘*song*’).
* The speaker concludes this stanza by stating, ‘*I thought that love would last for ever: I was wrong*’, which sets the melancholy and despair-filled tone of the last stanza, and adds depth to the speaker’s grief.
