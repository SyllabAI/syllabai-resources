2b. ‘Death and the King’s Horseman’, by Wole Soyinka, is a play narrating the last day of a horseman in Nigeria. It makes use of multiple characters to add to the plot, including Olunde, the son of the late King’s horseman. He is used in multiple scenes of the play to create dramatic atmospheres.

One of the ways in which this is done is with Olunde’s character. Being the son of the lively and erratic Elesin Oba, one would expect him to have somewhat of a similar character to his father. However, as the play progresses, we find that this is not the case. Instead, Olunde proves to be one of the wiser characters of the play, which may have been due to his time in England as a doctor An example of this is how instead of arguing with Simon Pilkings for locking up his father, he thanks him for his efforts and proceeds to fix matters himself. Moreover, though his time in England makes other characters believe that he is a more modern character, he is instead seen as more of a traditionalist, with great respect for the Yoruba people’s culture. Olunde is a wise, observant man who proves to be someone completely different to both the characters and readers. By creating such a complex, yet thought provoking character, Soyinka adds to the play’s dramatic atmosphere, as Olunde serves as a more grounded character, in the midst of the multiple happenings of the play.

In addition to this, his personality contrasts greatly with that of the other characters, further highlighting his differences, and hence assisting in delivering an impactful drama, For instance, Olunde’s contrast with Simon Pilkings is seen through how he remans composed throughout all the conflicts of the play, whereas Simon tends to get riled up at almost any form of disturbance he encounters. Furthermore, Olunde’s character also contrasts with that of Jane Pilkings, as she is much more sensitive than Olunde in many regards. For instance, while discussing the topic of death, Olunde seems to be unbothered, whereas Jane gets emotional to the point that the aide-de-camp steps in. Olunde also contrasts greatly with his own father Elesin; he is more calm and grounded, while Elesin is a character with intense emotions, that constantly overpower him. An example of this is the scene where Olunde states, almost emotionless, that Elesin is no longer a father to him; Elesin is then seen to have a breakdown. Soyinka makes use of Olunde’s contrast, in terms of characterization within the play, in order to heighten the impact of the scenes, for example by keeping his dialogues short and simple when compared to the emotion-packed words of the other characters in the drama.

Finally, the plot twist at the end of the play, involving Olunde is one of the main ways in which Soyinka uses Olunde’s portrayal, to create an impactful scene for the reader. The plot twist of the play is when Olunde performs his father’s ritualistic suicide, as he believed that Elesin would not be able to do so; this results in Elesin losing his composure and committing suicide as well. This scene is one of the most impactful within the drama, as it had not provided the reader with any form of foreshadowing prior to it, unlike most of the drama’s scenes. Moreover, Olunde had been portrayed as someone that respected Yoruba traditions, but would not partake in them. He was also displayed as someone that was grounded, and would not resort to any extreme means in a difficult situation. However, Soyinka reverses such expectations of Olunde by involving him in the ritualistic suicide Elesin was meant to perform. By creating such dramatic and revealing moments involving a character perceived as being anything but, Soyinka successfully delivers a final, impactful blow to the reader of the play, further sealing its status as an impactful, thought-provoking drama.

In conclusion, by making use of Olunde’s character in the play’s climax, as well as constructing a thoughtful personality for him that contrasts greatly with the rest of the drama’s characters, Soyinka successfully conveys an impactful drama for the reader, with multiple scenes in hand.
