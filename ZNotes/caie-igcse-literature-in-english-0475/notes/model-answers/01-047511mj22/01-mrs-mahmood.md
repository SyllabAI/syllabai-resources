## Section B: Prose


22. ‘Mrs. Mahmood’, by Segun Afolabi, is a short story in which a shop assistant of a London sports shop attempts to catch a burglar, but instead fails. The narrator of the story is the shop assistant himself, and his character is portrayed through multiple writing methods within the story.

    \
    To begin with, one way that Afolabi portrays the narrator, Mr. Mahmood, is through the complex relationships he finds himself in, particularly with his wife, Isobel, who is also the title’s namesake. Throughout the story, it is clear that he cares for, and admires, his wife. He constantly brings up details about her that he has observed, and uses their interactions to convey parts of himself. For instance, while telling the reader that he is someone with a cold exterior, he brings up Isobel, in order to show who exactly brought this to his attention. Moreover, as he converses with his wife, he makes note of certain details about her, in a tone of awe, such as what she wore and her voice. Such details in the writing help to show that though initially monotone, Mr. Mahmood did have certain people with whom he let down his guard. The narrator’s issues with Isobel also contribute to his portrayal: her dislike for crowds, the way she held herself, and so on. However, such issues do show the more determined side of Mr. Mahmood, as he says that he’d fought for Isobel, though she and her family had not liked him at first. Their opposing views on children also convey the idea of Mr. Mahmood being an impatient person. These dynamics within the Mahmood couple help to offer insight into Mr. Mahmood’s complex and intriguing character that appears to be everchanging. In addition to this, Afolabi also uses Mr. Mahmood’s occasional vulnerability to portray his character. As mentioned earlier, most of his vulnerability in the story comes up when with his wife, though even this is only a small display of it. However, most of this is shown through his inner monologue. For instance, he worries about people judging him for being old as he chases the teenager.

    \
    Moreover, his insecurity over not following his dreams is a large part of this, as he is boastful of the last time he had won a competition in his younger days. His regretful tone in such parts of the story shows that though he was proud, the resulting overconfidence led to him not considering failure as a real possibility, and therefore leading him to give up on his dreams. Mr. Mahmood also appears to dislike himself; for example, when he almost strikes the teenager, he seems to be angrier at himself for being so affected, unlike the boy in front of himself. These moments in the story show that Mr. Mahmood was, and is, afraid of failure, as he dropped running due to one singular event. Moreover, his envious tone while describing the youth around him also help to convey his insecurity, as he thinks of himself as someone that could’ve been the same, had he not given up so easily. The teenager is an important part to this feature of Mr. Mahmood, since it is due to him that his vulnerability really comes to rise.

    \
    Finally, Afolabi highlights Mr. Mahmood’s identity crisis in order to portray him as well. This internal conflict within himself mainly arose due to the failure he went through in his last competition. Prior to it, he was proud and overconfident, thinking that he could take on any challenge given to him. However, the failure to win the race led to drastic changes in his character: he turned passive, and began avoiding any form of risks. As he gave up on his dream of being an athlete, Afolabi allows the narrator to project his old dream in other parts of his life. For example, Mr. Mahmood says that he is someone that was particular about order, and liked things to be ‘running in straight lines‘. He struggles to let go of his past as the competition was unpredictable, leading him to project his desire for predictability onto the shop he works in. Moreover, he works at a sports shop, showing that he still carries a part of his past achievements within him. His identity crisis is also seen within his marriage to Isobel. He still struggles with providing a life she is satisfied with, and appears to be hung up on what her parents used to think of him. Mr. Mahmood’s struggle with self-acceptance helps to convey him as someone relatable and multidimensional.

    \
    In conclusion, Afolabi makes use of multiple pieces of Mr. Mahmood’s personality, as well as his interactions with other people, to portray him as a relatable and complex narrator within the story.
