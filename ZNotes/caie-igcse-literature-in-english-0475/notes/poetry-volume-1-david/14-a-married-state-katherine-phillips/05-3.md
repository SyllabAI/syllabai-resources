## Themes

### Marriage as a Source of Hardship

The main idea of the poem is that marriage brings many responsibilities and difficulties rather than happiness. From the opening line, Philips immediately challenges the romantic view of marriage by claiming that it "affords but little ease." She suggests that even the "best of husbands" can be demanding, making married life stressful for women. Instead of presenting marriage as a desirable goal, the poem warns women about its emotional and physical burdens.

### Freedom and Peace in Single Life

Philips contrasts marriage with virginity, presenting unmarried life as peaceful, innocent, and free from worry. She argues that single women are not burdened by the responsibilities of husbands, children, or household duties. The repeated use of "No" highlights everything an unmarried woman avoids, making her life seem calmer and happier.

### The Burdens of Womanhood

The poem explores the many challenges women face after marriage. Philips mentions difficult pregnancies, childbirth, raising children, and trying to satisfy a husband. These examples show that women often carry the greatest responsibilities within marriage, suggesting that society places unfair expectations on them.
