## Important Ideas / Quotes to Remember

* **"A married state affords but little ease;"**

  * **Core Idea:** Immediate challenge to societal romanticization of marriage.

  * **Analysis:** Opens the poem with a stark, realistic declaration that married life brings constant discomfort and hardship rather than fulfillment.

* **"The best of husbands are so hard to please"**

  * **Core Idea:** Inherent strain and emotional burden on wives.

  * **Analysis:** Uses hyperbole to emphasize that even "ideal" marriages demand continuous effort and sacrifice from women without fair return.

* **"This in wives' careful faces you may spell, / Though they dissemble their misfortunes well."**

  * **Core Idea:** Appearance vs. reality; hidden female suffering.

  * **Analysis:** Highlights how women are forced to hide (*dissemble*) their domestic misery behind controlled expressions, exposing the private trauma behind public expectations.

* **"A virgin state is crowned with much content,"**

  * **Core Idea:** Autonomy, peace, and honor in singlehood.

  * **Analysis:** Uses the metaphor *"crowned"* to elevate remaining unmarried to a noble, rewarding achievement rather than a social failure.

* **"No blustering husbands... / No pangs of childbirth... / No children's cries..."**

  * **Core Idea:** Exemption from domestic and physical suffering.

  * **Analysis:** Uses **anaphora** (repetition of *"No"*) to systematically list the anxieties, physical pain, and responsibilities that single women avoid.

* **"Thus, Madam, be advised by me: / Turn, turn apostate to love's levity."**

  * **Core Idea:** Direct instruction and rejection of societal romance.

  * **Analysis:** Employs imperative language (*"Turn, turn"*) and direct address to command the reader to renounce shallow romantic temptations (*"love's levity"*).

* **"There's no such thing as leading apes in hell"**

  * **Core Idea:** Subversion of patriarchal threats and myths.

  * **Analysis:** Directly refutes the contemporary superstition that unmarried women were doomed in the afterlife, dismantling societal scare tactics used to force women into marriage.
