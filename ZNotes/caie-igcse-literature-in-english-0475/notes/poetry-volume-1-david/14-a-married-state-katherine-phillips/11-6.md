## Conclusion

*A Married State* is a powerful warning against the unequal realities of marriage in seventeenth-century society. Rather than celebrating marriage as a source of happiness, Katherine Philips exposes its emotional, physical, and social burdens, particularly for women. By contrasting the peace of single life with the responsibilities of married life, she challenges traditional expectations and encourages women to value their independence. Through persuasive language, vivid imagery, repetition, and direct advice, Philips delivers a message that remains relevant today, making the poem an important early critique of gender inequality and societal pressure.
