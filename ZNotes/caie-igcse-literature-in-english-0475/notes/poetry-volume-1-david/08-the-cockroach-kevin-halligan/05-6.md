## Model Questions with Answers

### Model Question 1:

### How does Halligan present the cockroach as a symbol of human behaviour?

### Model Answer:

Halligan presents the cockroach as a symbol of human behaviour by describing its uncertain and irregular movement. Instead of moving in a straight line, the cockroach frequently changes direction, which reflects how humans often hesitate when making decisions. The poet also uses personification and similes to make the cockroach appear thoughtful and confused. This encourages readers to see the insect as representing a human mind struggling with uncertainty. Through this comparison, the poet suggests that people can sometimes feel lost or unsure about their path in life.

### Model Question 2:

### How does the poet use language to show confusion in the poem?

### Model Answer:

The poet uses descriptive verbs and similes to show confusion in the cockroach’s movement. The insect’s behaviour is compared to someone who is disoriented, which suggests mental uncertainty. The language emphasises hesitation and unpredictability, making the cockroach appear unsure about where it is going. These techniques help readers connect the insect’s movement to human confusion and inner conflict.

### Model Question 3:

### How does the poem turn a simple observation into a deeper meaning?

### Model Answer:

At the beginning of the poem, the speaker simply observes a cockroach moving across the floor. However, as the poem progresses, the speaker begins to interpret the insect’s behaviour as similar to that of a human who is thinking deeply or feeling uncertain. This shift from observation to interpretation creates a deeper meaning. Halligan shows that even small and ordinary moments can reveal important ideas about human psychology and behaviour.
