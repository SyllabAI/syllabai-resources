## **Stanza One**

Love is presented as sitting in “fantastic triumph“ while **bleeding hearts surround him,** showing his enjoyment of human suffering. He creates new pains and displays “tyrannic power,“ suggesting that love rules like a dictator. Love takes **fire from the beloved’s eyes** (suggesting beauty and attraction), but from the speaker he takes **desire so strong it could “undo the amorous world.“**

**This shows that the speaker feels love more intensely and painfully than the beloved.**

## **Stanza Two**

The speaker explains how love is formed:

* From the speaker: **sighs, tears, fear, weakness**

* From the beloved: **pride, cruelty, tyranny**

Together, they have “armed“ Love and turned him into a god. However the ending is bitter: **only the speaker’s heart is damaged**, while the beloved remains free and victorious.

This highlights the **inequality of love** and emotional imbalance.

## **Themes**

1. **Love as Violence and Tyranny**

Love is repeatedly described using **military and sort of political imagery:**

* “arm’d“

* “kills“

* “tyranny”

* “triumph“

Love is not gentle or healing; it **conquers and wounds,** behaving like a ruthless ruler.

2. **Unequal Power in Relationships**

   The speaker gives love vulnerability and emotion, while the beloved gives love dominance and cruelty. This reflects how one person can love deeply while the other remains distant and unaffected.

3. **Suffering and Desire**

Desire is shown as overwhelming and destructive. The speaker’s longing fuels Love’s power but also becomes the cause of their pain.

4. **Human Responsibility**

   The poem suggests that humans creates Love’s power themselves. By giving in to desire and pride, people “arm“ Love and allow it to dominate them.
