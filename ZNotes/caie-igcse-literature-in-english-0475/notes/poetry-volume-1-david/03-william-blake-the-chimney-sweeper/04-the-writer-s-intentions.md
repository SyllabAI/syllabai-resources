**Blake’s Message/ Effect**

||
||

**Child Exploitation**

|

The speaker is sold into labour and forced to work in dangerous conditions.

|

*“my father sold me”* / *“in soot I sleep”*

|

Children are treated as property. Blake exposes how society normalises cruelty through poverty.

||
||

**Loss of Innocence**

|

Childhood is replaced by labour and suffering. Hair (symbol of purity) is shaved.

|

*“curled like a lamb’s back”*

|

Innocence is physically and symbolically stripped away.

||
||

**Religion & False Hope**

|

An angel promises salvation if Tom is obedient.

|

*“if he’d be a good boy”*

|

Religion comforts the oppressed instead of challenging injustice.

||
||

**Obedience vs Reality**

|

Children believe doing their duty will protect them.

|

*“they need not fear harm*”

|

Dramatic irony — they are harmed despite obedience. Blake criticises blind acceptance.

||
||

**Death & Entrapment**

|

Chimneys are described as coffins; children are trapped.

|

*“coffins of black”*

|

Work is a form of living death. Society sacrifices children for economic gain.

||
||

**Innocence as Manipulation**

|

The speaker comforts Tom instead of questioning injustice.

|

*“Hush, Tom! never mind it”*

|

Innocence becomes a coping mechanism that prevents resistance.

||
||

**Societal & Parental Failure**

|

Father sells him; no protection from adults.

|

*“my father sold me”*

|

Authority figures fail their moral responsibility.
