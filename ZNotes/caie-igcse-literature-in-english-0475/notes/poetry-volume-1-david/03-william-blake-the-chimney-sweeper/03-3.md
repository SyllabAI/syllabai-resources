## **Stanza 1: Childhood stolen**

*“When my mother died I was very young, / And my father sold me…”*

* The child is **orphaned and sold,** showing how poverty turns children into commodities.

* The verb “sold“ is shocking as it implies slavery, not care.

* The child’s inability to pronounce “sweep“ properly (*“weep! weep!’'*) highlights his extreme youth and is a hidden pun on **“weep“**, suggesting unspoken suffering

* Sleeping “in soot“ symbolises how innocence is literally blackened by labour

It’s It’s important to note that Blake criticises parents and society for **normalising child exploitation.**

## Stanza 2: Comfort through innocence

*“That curled like a lamb’s back, was shaved”*

* Tom Dacre is compared to a **“lamb“**, a biblical symbol of **innocence and sacrifice.**

* Shaving his hair removes his last sign of purity.

* The speaker comforts Tom by saying soot can’t spoil his hair anymore, this unfortunately shows how children are forced to **rationalise abuse** and the idea of innocence being used as a coping mechanism.

* Suffering has now been disguised as something practical or even helpful.

**Stanzas 3-5: The dream (false hope)**

> “coffins of black“

* The chimneys are metaphorically described as **coffins,** suggesting:

* **Living death**

* **Entrapment**

* **The inevitability of early death**

* The Angel freeing the children introduces **religious imagery:**

* **Heaven**

* **Light**

* **Freedom**

* Washing in the river and shining in the sun symbolises **spiritual cleansing and rebirth.**

This suggests the idea the religion offers **comfort,** but it’s only apparent in dreams, not reality.

## **Stanza 6: Religious manipulation**

> “if he’d be a good boy“, / He’d have God for his father””

* God replaces the absent, neglectful earthly father.

* The promise of joy is **conditional:** obedience now, reward later.

* Blake criticises how religion teaches children to **accept suffering** instead of resisting injustice.

Faith becomes a tool of **control** and not salvation.

## **Final stanza: The bitter irony**

> “So if all do their duty, they need not fear harm.“

* Tom wakes up and goes straight back to work, nothing has changed.

* The warmth he feels is only **emotional, not physical**, showing self-deception.

* The final line is deeply ironic:

* The children do their duty

* But they are harmed anyway

Blake carefully uses that line to expose the lie that obedience guarantees safety.
