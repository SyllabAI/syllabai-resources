## **About the Poet**

William Blake was a **Romantic poet, artist, and engraver** born in **1757** in London. He was largely unrecognised in his lifetime but is now considered one of the most important Romantic writers to have lived.

He wrote both poetry and illustrated his own books. It is notable that Blake strongly believed that society corrupted natural innocence and institutions like the **Church** and **government** are oppressive.

## **Context**

* Written in **1794** during the Industrial Revolution.

* Chimney sweeping was a dangerous job done by **poor children**, often sold by their parents.

* William Blake strongly criticised:

* Child labour

* The Church

* The government and monarchy

* This poem is part of **Songs of Experience,** showing the world through the lens of **harsh reality rather than childhood innocence.**

## **Overview**

This poem presents a chimney sweeper who understands he has been been **abandoned and exploited.** Unlike the hopeful dream in the *Innocence* version, this child sees the **hypocrisy of adults and institutions.** Blake shows how society creates suffering and then hides behind religion and authority.

## **Structure & Form**

* Three quatrains (four-line stanzas)

* Regular rhyme scheme (AABB)- nursery rhyme style contrasts with dark subject

* Simple structure reflects a **child’s voice,** but the message is deeply political

* Dialogue format (question and answer) makes the child’s suffering direct and personal

**Effect:** The innocent rhythm clashes with the grim reality, highlighting how society disguises cruelty.
