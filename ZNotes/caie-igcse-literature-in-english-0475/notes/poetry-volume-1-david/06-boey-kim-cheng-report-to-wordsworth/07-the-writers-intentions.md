## Form

The poem is written in **fourteen lines**, giving it the appearance of a sonnet. However, its subject matter challenges the traditional celebration of nature associated with Romantic poetry.

## Structure

Boey organizes the poem to move from a direct appeal to Wordsworth towards increasingly severe descriptions of environmental destruction, ending with a religious and apocalyptic warning.

* **Opening appeal:** “You should be here, Nature has need of you.” The poem begins with a direct statement that establishes urgency. Addressing Wordsworth suggests that the modern world needs the values associated with Romanticism to restore humanity's relationship with nature.

* **Progression of destruction:** The descriptions of smog, silent flowers and declining bird populations establish the damage caused by pollution. The poem then develops this destruction through images of helpless mythological figures and humanity's continuing exploitation of nature.

* **Increasing sense of helplessness:** Proteus, Triton and Neptune are presented as unable to protect the natural world. Their progression reinforces the idea that the destruction has reached a point at which even powerful symbolic figures are powerless.

* **Shift towards humanity's responsibility:** “insatiate man moves in for the kill” places responsibility directly on human beings. Rather than presenting environmental destruction as an unavoidable natural process, Boey identifies humanity's greed and exploitation as major causes.

* **Climactic ending:** “God is labouring to utter his last cry” brings the poem to a disturbing conclusion. The reference to God suggests that environmental destruction threatens not only the natural world but also the spiritual values that should guide humanity.

* **Enjambment:** Ideas continue across several line breaks, allowing descriptions and images to flow into one another. This creates continuity while helping the poem develop its increasingly urgent warning.

The structure moves from an appeal for help to a depiction of widespread destruction and finally to an apocalyptic warning, intensifying the poem's sense of crisis.

## Tone

The tone is primarily urgent, mournful, accusatory and despairing, reflecting the speaker’s concern about humanity’s destruction of nature.
