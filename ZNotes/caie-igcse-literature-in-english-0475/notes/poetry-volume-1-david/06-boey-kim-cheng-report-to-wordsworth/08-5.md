## Model Questions & Answers

### **How does Boey Kim Cheng present nature in the poem?**

**Answer:**
Boey Kim Cheng presents nature as a victim of modern human activity. The poem contrasts the natural landscapes celebrated by Wordsworth with the present reality of environmental destruction caused by industrialisation and urbanisation. Images of concrete, pollution, and machinery replace rivers, forests, and open land, showing a world dominated by human interference. Nature is no longer a source of peace or spiritual reflection; it has been exploited and reduced to a resource for human convenience.

The direct address to Wordsworth emphasises the loss: it is as if Boey is apologising for the failure of modern society to protect what Wordsworth once revered. The poem conveys a deep sense of regret, making readers reflect on the consequences of neglecting the environment. By presenting nature as fragile and overwhelmed, Boey warns that humanity’s “progress” comes at the cost of irreversible damage, both physically and spiritually.

### **How does the poem explore the contrast between Romanticism and modern reality?**

**Answer:**
A key feature of the poem is the contrast between the ideals of Romanticism and the harsh reality of the contemporary world. Wordsworth believed that nature was pure, restorative, and morally guiding, offering humans spiritual and emotional nourishment. Boey, however, shows that industrialisation has destroyed this harmony. Nature is no longer a place for contemplation; it is consumed by urban sprawl and machinery.

By addressing Wordsworth directly, Boey creates a dialogue across time, which emphasises how much modern society has strayed from Romantic ideals. The poem’s tone is partly apologetic, suggesting that humanity has failed to uphold the values that Wordsworth celebrated. This contrast is reinforced through imagery, such as polluted landscapes and invasive urban development, highlighting a shift from emotional and spiritual connection with nature to disillusionment and environmental degradation.

### **In what ways does Boey Kim Cheng criticise industrialisation?**

**Answer:**
Boey Kim Cheng critiques industrialisation by portraying it as relentless, mechanical, and indifferent to the natural world. The poem suggests that modern development prioritises profit, efficiency, and expansion over preservation and harmony. Nature is treated as something to be exploited, commodified, and controlled rather than cherished or protected.

The imagery of urban sprawl, machinery, and destruction reinforces the poem’s critical stance. Industrialisation is depicted as aggressive and invasive, consuming what was once untouched and sacred. By contrasting this with Wordsworth’s vision of harmonious, restorative nature, Boey highlights the moral and spiritual consequences of unchecked industrial growth. The poem ultimately questions whether material progress can ever justify the destruction it causes, suggesting a societal failure to respect the environment and maintain a balance between human advancement and natural preservation.
