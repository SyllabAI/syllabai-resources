
**Themes**

### **Love and Admiration**

* The poem is fundamentally an expression of **love and admiration**.

* The speaker begins by considering whether the beloved can be compared to summer:
  **“Shall I compare thee to a summer’s day?”**

* However, the beloved is described as:
  **“more lovely and more temperate”**

* This immediately places the beloved above the natural beauty of summer.

* The speaker presents the beloved’s beauty as unusually stable and perfect compared with the changing natural world.

* Rather than simply praising physical appearance, the poem creates an idealised image of the beloved that will survive beyond ordinary human experience.

**Meaning:** Shakespeare presents love as something powerful enough to inspire the creation of lasting art.

### **Beauty and Its Impermanence**

* Shakespeare initially compares beauty with summer, but quickly shows that **summer itself is imperfect**.

* **“Rough winds”** can damage the flowers, while summer’s duration is described as:
  **“all too short a date”**

* The sun can also become:
  **“too hot”**
  or have its appearance **“dimm’d.”**

* These imperfections demonstrate that natural beauty is **temporary and unreliable**.

* Shakespeare then broadens the idea:
  **“every fair from fair sometime declines”**

* **“Every fair”** suggests that all beautiful things eventually lose their beauty.

**Meaning:** Physical beauty naturally fades, but Shakespeare argues that the beloved’s beauty can escape this process through poetry.

### **Time**

* Time is presented as a powerful force that changes and destroys beauty.

* Summer has:
  **“all too short a date”**

* The word **“date”** suggests an expiry point, making summer seem temporary.

* Shakespeare later refers to:
  **“nature’s changing course”**

* Nature is constantly changing, meaning that nothing physical can remain exactly the same forever.

* However, the speaker challenges time by claiming that the beloved’s beauty will survive in his poem.

**Meaning:** Time can destroy physical beauty, but Shakespeare believes poetry can preserve beauty beyond the limits of time.
