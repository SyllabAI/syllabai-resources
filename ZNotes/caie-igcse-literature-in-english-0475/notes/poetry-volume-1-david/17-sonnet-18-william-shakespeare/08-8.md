## **Important Ideas / Quotes to Remember**

* **“Shall I compare thee to a summer’s day?”** — introduces the central comparison.

* **“more lovely and more temperate”** — establishes the beloved as superior to summer.

* **“Rough winds do shake the darling buds of May”** — shows nature’s instability and fragility.

* **“summer’s lease hath all too short a date”** — summer is temporary.

* **“every fair from fair sometime declines”** — all physical beauty eventually fades.

* **“But thy eternal summer shall not fade”** — the volta introduces the beloved’s lasting beauty.

* **“Nor shall death brag”** — personifies death as an opponent the poet intends to defeat.

* **“in eternal lines to time thou grow’st”** — poetry gives the beloved permanence.

* **“So long lives this”** — the poem itself will survive.

* **“this gives life to thee”** — poetry creates literary immortality.

## Main Message

Shakespeare initially compares the beloved to summer, but ultimately argues that the beloved is superior to summer because natural beauty is temporary and imperfect.

Summer fades, flowers die, the sun changes and all physical beauty eventually declines.

However, the beloved’s beauty can survive through poetry.

The poem suggests that while nature and human life are temporary, art and poetry can preserve beauty and memory beyond the limits of time and death.
