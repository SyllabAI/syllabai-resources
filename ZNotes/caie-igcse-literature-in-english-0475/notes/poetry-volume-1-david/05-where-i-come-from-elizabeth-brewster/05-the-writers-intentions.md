## Form

The poem is written in **free verse**, without a regular rhyme scheme or a consistently structured meter.

* **Absence of a fixed rhyme scheme:** This gives the poem a natural, conversational quality, allowing the speaker's reflections to develop without strict formal constraints.

* **Reflective poetic voice:** The first-person perspective makes the poem personal, as the speaker considers the lasting influence of her homeland.

* **Descriptive style:** The poem combines observations about different environments with memories of the speaker's own origins, allowing the reader to understand how geography and experience contribute to identity.

### 3. Structure

Brewster organises the poem to move from general observations about people and their environments to a more personal exploration of her own origins.

* **Opening statement:** “People are made of places.” The poem begins with a broad, declarative statement that introduces its central theme. Its directness presents the relationship between place and identity as an important truth.

* **Shift in focus:** The opening section describes different environments, including cities and natural landscapes. The poem then becomes more personal as the speaker describes the woods, farmhouses and schoolhouses associated with her homeland.

* **Accumulation of images:** The lists of landscape features and everyday details build a vivid picture of the speaker's background. These accumulated images suggest that identity develops through numerous experiences rather than one defining event.

* **Movement towards memory:** The final lines shift from physical surroundings to the inner world of the mind. The image of a door opening and a “frosty wind” entering suggests that memories of home remain alive within the speaker.

* **Enjambment:** Sentences and ideas flow across line breaks, creating continuity between the descriptions. This flowing movement reflects how memories and associations connect different aspects of the speaker's identity.

The structure therefore moves from a general observation about humanity towards an intimate representation of personal memory, reinforcing the idea that a person's origins remain psychologically present.

## Message

### 4. Tone

The tone is primarily **reflective, descriptive and quietly confident**, although the descriptions of the speaker's homeland also suggest nostalgia.

* **Reflective:** The speaker considers how environments shape people's identities, encouraging readers to think about their own origins.

* **Descriptive:** Detailed images of cities, woods, farms and seasonal changes create a clear impression of the different places discussed.

* **Nostalgic:** The speaker's detailed recollection of her homeland suggests an emotional attachment to familiar surroundings and memories of the past.

* **Quietly confident:** The opening declaration, “People are made of places,” expresses the speaker's belief in the lasting influence of one's origins.

* **Contemplative:** The ending focuses on the mind and the return of memories, leaving the reader to consider how the past continues to influence the present.

The controlled, thoughtful tone allows Brewster to explore identity without relying on dramatic emotional language.

### 5. Message

Brewster's central message is that **where we come from remains an important part of who we are**. Our surroundings, culture, experiences and memories influence our personalities and perspectives, even when we leave our original homes.
