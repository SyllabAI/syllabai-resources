## Model Questions and Answers

## Question 1:

**How does Brewster present the theme of identity in the poem?**

### Model Essay Answer

Brewster presents identity as something deeply rooted in origin and environment, suggesting that who we are is shaped long before we are consciously aware of it. The repeated phrase “Where I come from” acts as a structural anchor throughout the poem, reinforcing the idea that identity begins with place. This repetition creates a declarative tone, almost as if the speaker is defining herself through her origins. It suggests certainty and confidence, showing that the speaker understands and accepts the influence of her homeland.

Furthermore, Brewster uses imagery of the landscape and natural surroundings to symbolise the shaping of personality. The land is not simply background description; it becomes a metaphor for the speaker’s internal characteristics. By linking her identity to physical geography, Brewster implies that environment moulds behaviour, values, and perspective. The permanence of nature mirrors the permanence of identity, reinforcing the idea that origins cannot be erased.

The reflective tone of the poem also strengthens the theme of identity. The speaker does not express conflict or confusion about who she is. Instead, there is calm acceptance. This suggests maturity and self-awareness. Brewster ultimately presents identity as inseparable from memory and place, arguing that even if one leaves their homeland, its influence remains embedded within them.

## Question 2:

**How does Brewster use structure and repetition to develop meaning?**

### Model Essay Answer

Brewster uses repetition as a structural device to emphasise the central idea that identity is rooted in origin. The recurring phrase “Where I come from” provides a rhythmic pattern that unifies the poem. This repetition mirrors the concept of roots, just as roots repeatedly anchor a tree into the soil, the repeated line anchors the poem’s meaning in the speaker’s homeland.

Structurally, the poem progresses through descriptions of landscape and environment, gradually building a layered understanding of identity. Each repeated phrase introduces another element of the speaker’s background, suggesting that identity is constructed piece by piece. This layered development reflects how identity forms over time through accumulated experiences.

The use of free verse further supports this idea. Without a rigid rhyme scheme, the poem flows naturally, resembling personal reflection rather than formal declaration. This structural freedom symbolises how identity grows organically rather than being artificially imposed. Through repetition and fluid structure, Brewster reinforces the idea that identity is both deeply rooted and naturally formed.

## Question 3:

**Explore how Brewster presents the relationship between place and personality.**

### Model Essay Answer

Brewster presents place as a powerful shaping force that directly influences personality and values. Through vivid imagery of landscape and natural elements, she suggests that the physical environment becomes internalised within the individual. The homeland is not portrayed as distant scenery but as something embedded within the speaker’s character.

By repeatedly referring to where she comes from, Brewster implies that personality is not created in isolation. Instead, it develops in response to surroundings. The stability and permanence of nature symbolise emotional grounding and resilience. The environment acts almost like a silent teacher, shaping attitudes and worldview.

Additionally, the calm and confident tone reflects someone secure in their identity. This security stems from a strong connection to place. Brewster implies that understanding one’s origins provides clarity and stability in life. Therefore, the poem ultimately argues that personality is not separate from geography, it grows out of it.
