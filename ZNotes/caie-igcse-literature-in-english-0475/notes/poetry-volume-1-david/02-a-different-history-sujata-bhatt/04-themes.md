## **Themes**

1. ### **Spirituality and Nature**

* The poems opens with the claim that **Pan, the Greek god of nature, emigrated to India.**

* This suggests India is a place where **ancient spirituality still survives.**

* Nature is portrays as sacred and alive, not something to control.

**Meaning:** Indian culture is shown as spiritually rich and deeply connected to the natural world.

2. ### **Reverence for Knowledge**

* Books are treated with **religious respect:**

  > “It is a sin to shove a book
  >
  > aside“

* The act of touching books with your feet is considered disrespectful in Indian culture, Bhatt reflects this belief.

* Learning is not just intellectual, but **moral and spiritual**

**Meaning:** Knowledge is sacred: education is tied to cultural identity and tradition.

3. ### **Language and Colonialism**

* The poem shifts from cultural pride to political questioning

  > “Which language has not been the oppressor’s tongue?“

* Bhatt suggest many global languages spread through violence, empire, and domination.

* English, though now a global language, carries the **memory of colonial rule** in countries like India.

**Meaning:** Language is not just neutral, it holds historical power.

5. ### **Identity and Generational Change**

* Bhatt suggests future generations may:

* Speak the coloniser’s language

* Love it

* Use it as their own

* This creates tension between heritage and modern identity.

**Meaning:** Identity is shaped by history, and people must live with cultural contradictions.
