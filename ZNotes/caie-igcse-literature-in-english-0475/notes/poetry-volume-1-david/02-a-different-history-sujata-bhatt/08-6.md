## Model Answers

**Question 1**

**How does Sujata Bhatt present the importance of culture and learning in *A Different History*?**

Bhatt presents culture and learning as sacred and deeply valued in Indian society. In the first half of the poem, she suggests that India is a place where spirituality is still alive by saying the Greek god Pan “emigrated to India.” This implies that ancient beliefs continue to exist there, showing cultural continuity and richness.

She reinforces this respect for knowledge through religious imagery linked to books. The speaker says, “It is a sin to shove a book aside,” which uses the language of religion to elevate learning to a moral and spiritual level. Calling it a “sin” suggests that knowledge is holy and must be treated with reverence. This reflects Indian cultural traditions where education is deeply respected.

Bhatt also connects knowledge with the divine, suggesting that learning is not only intellectual but spiritual. This shows that culture is rooted in values that honour both nature and education.

Overall, Bhatt presents culture and learning as sacred forces that shape identity and moral behaviour.

## **Question 2**

**Explore how Bhatt presents the theme of language and power in the poem.**

In the second half of the poem, Bhatt shifts from cultural admiration to political reflection, focusing on language as a tool of power. She asks, “Which language has not been the oppressor’s tongue?” This rhetorical question suggests that many widely spoken languages spread through conquest and colonisation. It challenges the reader to see language as something shaped by history and conflict rather than as neutral.

The phrase “oppressor’s tongue” links language directly to control and domination. This is especially significant in a post-colonial context, where English was imposed on countries like India during British rule. Bhatt suggests that even though people may now speak English comfortably, it still carries the memory of oppression.

She also implies that future generations may grow up loving this language, despite its violent history. This creates tension between the past and present, showing how identity becomes complicated when shaped by colonial influence.

Bhatt ultimately presents language as powerful, political, and deeply connected to history.

## **Question 3**

**How does the structure of the poem help to convey its message?**

The structure of the poem reflects the shift in focus from cultural pride to historical complexity. The poem is written in free verse, which creates a natural, reflective tone, as though the speaker is thinking aloud. This allows ideas to flow and mirrors the complexity of identity and history.

The poem is divided into two main parts. The first part focuses on spirituality, nature, and respect for learning, creating a calm and admiring tone. The second part shifts to questioning language and colonialism, creating a more serious and reflective mood. This contrast highlights the tension between cultural richness and the painful history associated with language.

The shift in subject matter mirrors the poem’s main message: while culture preserves sacred traditions, history — especially colonial history  complicates identity. The structural change therefore reinforces the thematic shift from celebration to critical reflection.
