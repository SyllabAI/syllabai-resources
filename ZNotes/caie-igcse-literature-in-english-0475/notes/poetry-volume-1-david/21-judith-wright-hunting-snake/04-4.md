## Themes

### Nature's Power

* The snake is presented as a powerful creature that immediately changes the humans' behaviour.

* Before seeing it, the speakers are simply walking through the landscape.

* When the snake appears:

  > **"we froze half-through a pace."**

* The verb **"froze"** suggests that the snake has the power to stop them physically.

* The snake itself remains completely focused on its own purpose, moving confidently through the grass.

* The humans do not control or interfere with it; instead, they simply **watch**.

* This gives the snake a sense of dominance over the scene.

**Meaning:** Wright presents nature as a powerful force that can suddenly interrupt human activity and demand our attention.

### Beauty and Danger

* One of the poem's most important contrasts is that the snake is both **dangerous and beautiful**.

* The speaker describes its:

  > **"diamond scale"**

* The adjective **"diamond"** creates an image of something precious, brilliant and beautiful.

* However, the snake is also described as having:

  > **"fierce intent"**

* This reminds us that the snake is a predator hunting another creature.

* The humans initially react with fear, but their fear gradually becomes fascination.

* The final description:

  > **"Cold, dark and splendid"**
  > combines threatening qualities with admiration.

* **"Cold"** and **"dark"** create an ominous image, while **"splendid"** expresses admiration.

**Meaning:** Wright shows that nature cannot always be classified as simply beautiful or frightening; it can be both at the same time.

### Human Fear

* The appearance of the snake immediately creates fear and tension.

* The speakers:

  > **"froze half-through a pace"**

* The verb **"froze"** suggests a physical response to danger.

* Wright also writes:

  > **"we lost breath to watch him pass."**

* This could suggest fear, astonishment or intense concentration.

* The snake's size and movement make the humans aware of their vulnerability.

* However, the fear does not cause them to run away immediately.

* Instead, they remain still and observe it.

**Meaning:** Wright presents fear as a natural human response to danger, but also shows that fear can exist alongside curiosity and admiration.
