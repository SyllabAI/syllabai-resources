## About the Poet

Alexander Pope was an English poet of the 18th century and one of the major writers of the Neoclassical/Augustan period. He is famous for his use of satire, reason, balance, and heroic couplets.

His poetry often explores human nature, morality, reason, society, and humanity's relationship with God.

## Overview

*An Essay on Man* is a philosophical poem in which Pope examines humanity's place in the universe and argues that humans should recognise their limitations rather than trying to understand everything about God.

## Structure & Forms

* Philosophical / didactic poem- Pope presents an argument and teaches the reader about human nature.

* Written mainly in heroic couplets- pairs of rhyming lines in iambic petameter.

* The regular rhythm and rhyme create a sense of order and balance.

* The poem frequently uses contrasts with the “Chaos of thought and passion” Pope describes.

## Key structural idea:

**Order in poem** `↔` **confusion within humanity.**

Pope’s highly controlled form reflects the Neoclassical belief in **reason and order,** while his subject shows how complicated human beings actually are.
