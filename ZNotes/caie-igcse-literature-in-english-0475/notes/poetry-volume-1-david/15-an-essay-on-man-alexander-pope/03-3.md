## Themes

### Human Nature

* Pope presents human beings as **complex and contradictory creatures**.

* Humans possess intelligence and greatness, but these qualities are always limited by weakness and ignorance.

* This is shown in:

  > **"A being darkly wise, and rudely great"**

* The combination of **"wise"** and **"darkly"** suggests that human knowledge exists, but it is incomplete or uncertain.

* Similarly, **"rudely great"** suggests that humans can achieve greatness without being perfect or refined.

Pope presents human nature as inherently contradictory: humans have the ability to achieve greatness, but their knowledge, reason and existence remain limited.

### Human Limitations and Knowledge

### Pope argues that humans should recognise the **limits of their own understanding**.

* The poem begins with the command:

  > **"Know then thyself, presume not God to scan"**

* The imperative **"Know"** suggests that self-knowledge should be humanity's priority.

* However, **"presume"** suggests arrogance: humans should not assume that they are capable of understanding everything.

* The phrase **"presume not God to scan"** establishes a boundary between **human knowledge and divine knowledge**.

* Pope reinforces this through:

  > **"The proper study of mankind is man."**

* Rather than attempting to understand God's entire plan, humans should focus on understanding their own nature and limitations.

Pope suggests that true wisdom comes from recognising the boundaries of human knowledge rather than trying to achieve knowledge that belongs to God.

### Reason and Error

* Pope recognises that **reason is one of humanity's greatest abilities**, but he also questions its reliability.

* Humans are capable of thinking, analysing and reasoning, yet they remain capable of making mistakes.

* This is captured in:

  > **"Born but to die, and reasoning but to err"**

* The parallel structure connects two unavoidable human limitations:

  * Humans are **born to die**

  * Humans are **capable of reasoning but destined to make errors**

* Pope therefore challenges the idea that human reason is completely reliable.

* This connects to:

  > **"Whether he thinks too little or too much"**

* Too little thought can lead to ignorance, while too much thought can lead to confusion.

Human reason is valuable, but it is imperfect. Pope encourages intellectual humility rather than complete confidence in human understanding.
