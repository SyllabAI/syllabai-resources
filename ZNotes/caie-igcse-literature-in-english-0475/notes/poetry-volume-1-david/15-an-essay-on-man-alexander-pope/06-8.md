## **Question 1**

### **How does Pope present human nature in the poem?**

Pope presents human nature as complicated and contradictory. He describes humanity as **"A being darkly wise, and rudely great"**, combining ideas of intelligence and greatness with the suggestion that these qualities are imperfect. The phrase **"darkly wise"** suggests that humans possess knowledge but cannot fully understand the world around them.

Pope develops this contradiction through the phrase **"He hangs between"**. The verb **"hangs"** suggests that humanity is suspended between opposing states, which is reinforced by the contrast between **"god or beast"**. Humans are therefore neither completely divine nor completely animal.

Pope ultimately presents humanity as both powerful and weak. He describes humans as the **"Great Lord of all things, but a prey to all"**, showing that humans can dominate the world while remaining vulnerable themselves.

Overall, Pope presents human nature as a mixture of **greatness, weakness, intelligence and limitation**.

## **Question 2**

### **How does Pope present the limitations of human knowledge?**

Pope immediately establishes the limitations of human knowledge through the command **"Know then thyself, presume not God to scan."** The imperative **"Know"** suggests that humans should focus on understanding themselves, while **"presume"** suggests that attempting to understand God completely would be arrogant.

This idea is reinforced by the famous statement **"The proper study of mankind is man."** Pope suggests that humanity should focus on what it can reasonably understand rather than attempting to uncover every mystery of the universe.

Pope also admits that human reason itself is imperfect through the phrase **"reasoning but to err."** The contrast between reasoning and error suggests that even humanity's greatest intellectual ability has limitations.

Therefore, Pope's message is that **recognising the limits of human knowledge is itself a form of wisdom**.
