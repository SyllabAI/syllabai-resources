## Conclusion

Rumens presents the carpet weaving as both beautiful and disturbing, using the children's work to explore the loss of childhood, the passage of time and the hidden human cost behind cultural traditions. The vivid colours and religious imagery create a sense of beauty, while phrases such as "hard at work" remind us that this beauty is produced through children's labour. Ultimately, the carpet becomes a symbol of the children's lives: their creativity creates something lasting, but their own childhood passes quickly into "all-that-was." The poem therefore encourages us to look beyond the beauty of the finished carpet and consider the human story behind it.
