## Themes

### Children and Loss of Innocence

* The children are introduced as young and visually striking:

  > **"Their assorted heights"**

* Their different heights emphasise that they are **children of different ages**.

* However, instead of playing or attending school in the usual sense, they are working at a loom.

* The phrase:

  > **"The children are hard at work"**
  > immediately reminds us of their labour.

* Rumens creates a contrast between the children's **youth and the seriousness of their work**.

* Their childhood becomes something shaped by labour rather than freedom.

**Meaning:** Rumens presents childhood as something precious that is being restricted by the demands of work.

### Child Labour and Exploitation

* The children are physically involved in producing something that will eventually be sold.

* The carpet:

  > **"will travel in the merchant's truck"**

* The word **"merchant"** introduces the commercial world surrounding their labour.

* The children create the carpet, but they do not appear to benefit from its eventual journey.

* The carpet will eventually be:

  > **"spread by the servants of the mosque"**

* This creates a contrast between the children's difficult labour and the carpet's eventual use in a religious setting.

* The poem therefore encourages us to think about **who produces valuable objects and who ultimately gets to use them**.

**Meaning:** Rumens draws attention to the inequality behind the beautiful carpets, reminding us that they are created through the labour of children.

### Time

* Time is one of the poem's most important ideas.

* The children are described as being in the:

  > **"school of days"**

* This metaphor suggests that their lives are being shaped and taught by the passage of time.

* The final lines become especially important:

  > **"all-that-will-be"**
  > and
  > **"all-that-was"**

* These phrases contrast the **future and the past**.

* The children's work creates something that will survive, while their own childhood passes quickly.

* The carpet therefore becomes a physical record of time.

**Meaning:** The poem suggests that childhood is temporary, while the consequences and memories of childhood may last much longer.
