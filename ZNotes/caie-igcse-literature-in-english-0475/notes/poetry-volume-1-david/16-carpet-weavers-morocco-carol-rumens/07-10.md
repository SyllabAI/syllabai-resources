## Model Answers

### How does Rumens present the children in *Carpet Weavers, Morocco*?

Rumens presents the children as both **creative and vulnerable**. At the beginning of the poem, she focuses on their appearance, describing their **"braids"** as **"oiled and black"** and their **"dresses bright."** The colour imagery creates a vivid and attractive image of the children, emphasising their youth and individuality.

However, this beauty is contrasted with the fact that they are **"hard at work"**. The phrase reminds the reader that despite their young age, the children are involved in labour. Rumens therefore prevents the reader from seeing the scene as simply beautiful or exotic.

The children are also presented as creative through the description of **"the colours of all-that-will-be"** flying from their fingers. The verb **"fly"** gives their work energy and suggests that they possess imagination and skill.

Overall, Rumens presents the children as **young and creative but restricted by their labour**, creating sympathy for the childhood they may be losing.

## **Question 2**

### How does Rumens explore the theme of time?

Rumens uses the process of weaving as a metaphor for the passage of time. The children are described as being in the **"school of days"**, suggesting that their lives are being shaped by the experiences they go through.

The strongest presentation of time appears at the end of the poem in **"all-that-will-be"** and **"all-that-was."** These phrases create a direct contrast between the future and the past. The children are creating something connected to the future, yet their own childhood is simultaneously becoming part of the past.

The verbs **"fly"** and **"freeze"** are also significant. **"Fly"** suggests movement and possibility, while **"freeze"** suggests something becoming permanently fixed. This could suggest that although the children's lives continue forward, their lost childhood cannot be recovered.

Therefore, Rumens presents time as **powerful and irreversible**, showing that childhood passes even while the children remain occupied with their work.
