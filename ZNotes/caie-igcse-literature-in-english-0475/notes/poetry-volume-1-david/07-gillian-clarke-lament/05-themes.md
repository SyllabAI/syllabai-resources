## Themes

### 1. Environmental Destruction

Environmental destruction is one of the central themes of the poem. Clarke explores how warfare damages ecosystems, contaminates the oceans and threatens wildlife.

* Oil pollution contaminates the sea and beaches.

* Marine animals and birds suffer from the destruction of their habitats.

* Natural environments are transformed into scenes of death and devastation.

The image of “the ocean’s lap with its mortal stain” presents the sea as a nurturing force that has been fatally harmed. The word “mortal” emphasises the potentially deadly consequences of pollution.

Clarke suggests that environmental destruction is one of the most serious and lasting consequences of war, affecting species that have no responsibility for the conflict.

### 2. The Human Cost of War

Clarke demonstrates that war affects civilians and soldiers as well as the natural world.

* “For Ahmed at the closed border” highlights the suffering of people affected by conflict and restricted movement.

* “For the soldier in his uniform of fire” presents the dangers faced by those involved in warfare.

* References to the soldier, gunsmith, armourer and young recruits reveal the different people involved in the machinery of war.

By including both human and animal victims, Clarke broadens the reader's understanding of suffering. The poem suggests that the consequences of war extend far beyond military casualties.

### 3. Death and Suffering

Death is a recurring theme, presented through images of dying animals, contaminated habitats and destroyed landscapes.

* “the cormorant in his funeral silk” associates wildlife with mourning and death.

* “the long migrations and the slow dying” suggests prolonged suffering and the destruction of natural life.

* “the whale truck dumb by the missile’s thunder” presents an image of wildlife overwhelmed by the violence of warfare.

These images create an elegiac atmosphere, encouraging readers to mourn the victims of war. Clarke shows that suffering is not limited to immediate casualties; it can continue through environmental damage and the loss of entire habitats.
