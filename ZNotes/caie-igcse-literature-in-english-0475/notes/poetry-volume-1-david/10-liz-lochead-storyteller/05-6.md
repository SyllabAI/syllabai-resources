## Model Questions & Answers

## **How does the poet present the power of storytelling?**

In *Storyteller*, Liz Lochhead presents storytelling as a powerful force that transforms ordinary experience into something imaginative and meaningful. Although the audience already knows the endings of the stories, they remain engaged, suggesting that the significance of storytelling lies not in plot development but in the act of telling itself. This emphasises how stories create emotional engagement rather than suspense.

Lochhead also shows how storytelling reshapes reality by turning a simple domestic kitchen into a space filled with imagination. Within this setting, the storyteller is able to momentarily transport her audience beyond their everyday lives, suggesting that storytelling has the ability to alter perception and enrich ordinary environments. In this way, the poem highlights storytelling as an active and powerful creative force rather than passive entertainment.

## **How is the storyteller’s role significant in the poem?**

The storyteller’s role is significant because she represents creativity and imagination within an otherwise ordinary domestic setting. While other women are occupied with traditional household tasks, she is engaged in a different kind of work that involves shaping narratives and engaging the imagination of her listeners. This contrast highlights her uniqueness within the community.

Although she does not openly challenge social expectations, her role subtly disrupts traditional ideas about women’s work. By creating stories that captivate and influence others, she holds a quiet form of authority. Lochhead, therefore, presents the storyteller as an important figure whose influence lies in creativity rather than physical labour, suggesting that imagination can be a powerful and meaningful form of contribution.
