## Imagery

* ### **Visual imagery**:

  * “cobbled street”, “Castle Keep”, “rain-slick paving slabs” $\rightarrow$ creates a vivid image of Newcastle (the area's gritty, historic charm) and gives the setting a historic, atmospheric quality.

* ### Pathetic fallacy (Attributes human emotions and feelings to nature or weather):

  * “rain-slick”, “turbid mist” create a gloomy, reflective atmosphere and mirror the speaker's thoughts about the past $\rightarrow$ cinematic scene conveying both the beauty and melancholy of the place
  * The mist could represent more than sadness. Its obscurity may also reflect the difficulty of understanding a place fully when its history contains both meaningful memories and uncomfortable truths.

* ### **Water imagery/motif**:

  * “rain”, “mist”, “Tyne”, “river” $\rightarrow$ water becomes a recurring motif representing movement, change and connection.

* ### **Simile**:

  * “like this river, drawn from two sources” $\rightarrow$ the river represents two different people coming together, suggesting that their differences can form a meaningful connection. 
  * This image is particularly meaningful because the two sources merge without necessarily losing their origins, suggesting that genuine connection does not require people to abandon their individuality.

* ### **Extended metaphor**:

  * “drawn from two sources ... our flaws ... our sedimental selves” $\rightarrow$ continues the river imagery to suggest that both people carry different layers of history and experience, willing to share and accept them, allowing them to form a deeper connection.
