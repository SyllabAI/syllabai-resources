## Essay practice

### What is PEEL?

- P – Point: State the main idea of your paragraph.
- E – Evidence: Support your point with a quotation from the poem.
- E – Explanation: Analyse the poet’s techniques, word choices, and their deeper meanings.
- L – Link: Link your analysis back to the question and the poet’s overall message.

### Model essay

Through the poem Baltic Mill, Kayo Chingonyi has explored how people’s identities are shaped by their relationships to the places they belong to and the history of their communities. He presents how people can connect through backgrounds, how a place can hold memories, and how the past can influence a person’s sense of belonging.

Firstly, Chingonyi has presented how people from different backgrounds can form meaningful connections. The poet wrote, "like this river, drawn from two sources”. This is a simile that compares two people to a river formed from two sources. The two sources represent their backgrounds and experiences, while the river symbolises how they can come together. This shows that people do not need to have the same experiences to form a strong connection. Furthermore, the phrase "ourselves" uses a metaphor to compare people’s identities to layers of sediment in a river. Just as sediment builds up over time, people’s identities are shaped by their experiences and memories. The word "our" also creates a sense of togetherness, suggesting that both individuals are willing to share their experiences. Through these images, Chingonyi shows that meaningful relationships are built on trust and the acceptance of each other’s differences.

Another way Chingonyi explores identity is through the relationship between people and the places they belong to. Chingonyi was born in Zambia. Grew up in Newcastle, making the city an important part of his personal history. The phrase " / slick paving slabs" uses visual imagery to describe the city’s streets. This allows the reader to picture the setting and creates an image of Newcastle. Furthermore, the enjambment across " / slick" creates a continuous flow reflecting the speaker’s movement through the city. It may also represent how his thoughts and memories continue to develop as he walks. This suggests that a place is more than a physical location as it can hold important memories and experiences. Therefore, Chingonyi shows how the places people grow up in can influence their identity and sense of belonging.

Lastly, Chingonyi explores how a place’s history can influence the identity of its community. The poet refers to "blackface minstrels," which's a historical allusion to racist performances in which people darkened their faces to portray Black people through offensive stereotypes. This reference exposes the racism that existed within Britain’s history. It also challenges the idea that a place’s past should only be celebrated, as its history may contain uncomfortable experiences. This is particularly significant because Chingonyi was born in Zambia and grew up in Newcastle, connecting his identity to different places and cultures. By including this reference, the poet shows that belonging to a place does not mean ignoring its mistakes. Instead, people should recognise both the negative parts of their community’s history. This highlights how cultural history can affect the way people understand their identity and the places they call home.

In conclusion, Kayo Chingonyi has used language, imagery and structure to explore the relationship between people, place and identity. He shows how relationships connect people with backgrounds, how places preserve personal memories, and how cultural history influences a community. Ultimately, the poem suggests that identity is shaped by different experiences, and understanding ourselves and others requires us to accept the different histories that make us who we are.
