## Language

* ### **Word choice:**

  * “Sly” suggests secrecy, cunning, and deception. Unlike words associated with open aggression, it implies that the N-word can operate indirectly, slipping into conversations where its significance may be disguised or dismissed.

  * Chingonyi may be criticising the way people minimise racist language by treating it as a joke or an innocent expression. The word's deceptive quality suggests that its harmful implications can remain present even when speakers do not openly acknowledge them.

  * “Smuggled” usually refers to secretly transporting something prohibited or unwanted across a boundary. By applying this verb to language, Chingonyi presents the N-word as something that crosses the boundaries of acceptable speech.

  * The phrase suggests deliberate secrecy, as the word enters a space where it is considered unacceptable. This exposes the contradiction between rejecting racism publicly and allowing racist language to persist in everyday conversations.

  * “Poisoned” carries connotations of contamination, danger, and lasting harm. Rather than describing the word as merely offensive, Chingonyi associates it with something capable of damaging whoever encounters it.

  * The phrase “poisoned on his lips” also places the word physically at the point of speech. This connects the harm directly to the act of speaking, emphasising that language can become a means through which prejudice is communicated, and suggests that words can carry the effects of historical racism into present-day relationships. It challenges the idea that racist language becomes harmless simply because it is spoken casually, showing that its historical associations can remain damaging.

  * “signifyin(g)” introduces the idea that language can communicate meanings beyond its literal definition. A word's significance may depend on who uses it, whom it addresses, and the social context surrounding it. $\rightarrow$ complicates the poem's exploration of the N-word because language does not have a single, fixed meaning in every situation. Chingonyi draws attention to the importance of context while suggesting that the word's historical associations cannot simply be ignored.

* ### **Metaphor:**

  * “Bad blood” suggests hostility, resentment, and conflict between people or groups. Its association with history connects these feelings to the long legacy of racial oppression.

  * In contrast, “wine” suggests something produced through a process of transformation. The metaphor therefore presents the past as material from which something new can be created. $\rightarrow$ This may suggest that contemporary uses and interpretations of racial language have developed out of historical oppression.

  * However, the metaphor also raises a question: can something harmful from the past be transformed into something different, or does its history continue to influence its meaning? Chingonyi presents language as something that evolves, while reminding readers that its origins remain significant.

* ### **Direct address:**

  * “You sly devil.” The poem begins by directly addressing the N-word as “you”. This makes the word appear like a character with its own behaviour and influence. $\rightarrow$ By speaking to the word rather than simply describing it, Chingonyi makes it the central subject of the stanza. This allows him to examine its power, almost as if confronting the word itself and questioning the harm it carries.
