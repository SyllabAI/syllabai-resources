## Imagery

* ### **Visual imagery:**

  * “my shadow on this wall.” The image of a shadow projected onto a wall creates a distinction between the speaker himself and the outline visible to others. A shadow represents only a partial image of a person, lacking the complexity of their thoughts, personality, and experiences.

  * The possessive pronoun “my” establishes the speaker's personal connection to the image, while “shadow” suggests something that follows him and cannot easily be separated from him.$\rightarrow$ The shadow may symbolise how racial identity shapes the way others perceive the speaker. Rather than seeing him as a complete individual, society may reduce him to the racial associations attached to his appearance. This explores the conflict between personal identity and an identity imposed by others.

* ### **Violent imagery:**

  * “knocking me about the head with your dark hands.” The phrase presents the N-word as though it possesses hands and can physically attack the speaker. The use of personification transforms an abstract word into an aggressor, making the emotional harm associated with racist language feel immediate and tangible.

  * The verb “knocking” suggests repeated, forceful impact rather than a single gentle touch. Meanwhile, “about the head” directs attention towards the speaker's head, which may symbolically connect the attack to his thoughts, self-perception, and sense of identity.

  * The possessive phrase “your dark hands” gives the word an imagined physical presence, making it seem capable of pursuing and harming the speaker. $\rightarrow$ implying that racist language can feel like an act of aggression, even when the harm is inflicted through speech rather than physical contact. By giving the word the ability to attack, Chingonyi challenges the assumption that language is separate from real-world consequences.

* ### **Metaphorical imagery:**

  * “poisoned on his lips.” The image of poison associated with someone's lips combines the physical act of speaking with the idea of contamination. Lips are normally associated with communication, intimacy, and expression, but the reference to poison transforms speech into a potential source of harm.

  * The contrast suggests that language can appear ordinary at the point of delivery while carrying damaging historical associations. $\rightarrow$ Highlighting the hidden danger of racist language: its harm does not necessarily disappear when the speaker uses a casual tone. Instead, the word can carry the weight of historical prejudice into present-day interactions.

* ### **Natural imagery:**

  * “leaves strewn as I had never seen them strewn.” The scattered leaves create a visual image of a natural environment that appears unusual to the speaker. The repetition of “strewn” draws attention to the arrangement of the leaves, making the scene feel distinctive within his memory.

  * The phrase “as I had never seen them” emphasises the speaker's unfamiliarity with the scene. This makes the memory feel unusually vivid, as though the experience has become fixed in his mind. $\rightarrow$ The imagery may suggest how a particular encounter can become inseparable from a person's memory of a place or moment. Within the stanza, the vivid recollection contrasts with the broader discussion of language, reminding readers that experiences of racism are not merely abstract social issues but can become lasting personal memories.
