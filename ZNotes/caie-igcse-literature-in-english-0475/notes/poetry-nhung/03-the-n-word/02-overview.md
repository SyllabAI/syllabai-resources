## Overview

* ### **Structure:**

  * 11 lines, 1 stanza

* ### Themes:

  - Identity

  - Racism

  - Historical oppression

* ### Background

  * The N-word has a long history of being used as a racial slur against Black people, particularly in connection with slavery, segregation, and racial discrimination in the United States.

  * The word carries historical associations of racism and dehumanisation. However, its meaning can become more complicated when considered in different social and cultural contexts, including discussions of reclamation and who has the power to use it.

  * The poem explores how racial language exists within everyday interactions, showing that racism is not confined to openly hostile situations.

  * As a Black British poet, Kayo Chingonyi explores how language and racial attitudes can influence how Black people understand themselves and how they are perceived by others.

* ### Poet's purpose

  * Chingonyi presents the N-word as more than an ordinary word, highlighting how its historical associations can cause emotional harm and reinforce racial prejudice.

  * By placing offensive language within an everyday conversation, he encourages readers to question how racism can be disguised as humour or casual speech.

  * The poem examines how racial language can influence a person's sense of self, particularly when society defines them through racial stereotypes.

  * The reference to “the bad blood of history” links present-day language to a longer history of racial hostility, suggesting that the past continues to influence contemporary society.

  * hingonyi invites readers to consider how context, intention and historical background influence the meaning of words, while recognising that seemingly casual language can still carry harmful associations.
