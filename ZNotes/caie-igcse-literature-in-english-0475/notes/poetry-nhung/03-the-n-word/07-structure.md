## Structure

* ### **Enjambment:** 

  * “though you been/smuggled from polite conversation.” The sentence continues across the line break, delaying the completion of the thought, creating a sense of movement as the word passes from one part of the sentence to the next.

  * The line break after “been” briefly leaves the reader waiting for the action to be completed, which may reflect the way offensive language crosses social boundaries, entering conversations where it is supposedly unwelcome. The continuation also mirrors the poem's wider argument that racist language cannot always be neatly contained or excluded.

* ### **Personification:** 

  * “knocking me about the head with your dark hands.” The word is given the ability to attack the speaker physically. This transforms language from an abstract concept into an active force within the stanza.

  * Structurally, the movement from describing the word to imagining it attacking the speaker intensifies its apparent power. This makes the consequences of racist language increasingly difficult to dismiss as harmless.

* ### **Shift from personal experience to historical reflection:** 

  * “the bad blood of history”. The stanza moves from a particular interaction towards a broader reflection on history. This widens the focus from the speaker's immediate experience to the historical context that gives the word its power.

  * This shift suggests that an individual's encounter with racist language is connected to a much longer history of racial discrimination. $\rightarrow$ Encourages the understanding of personal experiences of racism within their wider social and historical context.
