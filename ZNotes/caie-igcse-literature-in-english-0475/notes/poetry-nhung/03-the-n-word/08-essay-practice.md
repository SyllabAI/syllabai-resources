## Essay practice

### What is PEEL?

- P – Point: State the main idea of your paragraph.
- E – Evidence: Support your point with a quotation from the poem.
- E – Explanation: Analyse the poet’s techniques, word choices, and their deeper meanings.
- L – Link: Link your analysis back to the question and the poet’s overall message.

### Model Essay:

Through the poem The N Word, Kayo Chingonyi explores how racist language can affect people's experiences and identities. He shows how racism can remain present even when society claims to reject it, how language can be reclaimed despite its painful history, and how experiences of racism can leave a lasting emotional impact.

Firstly, Chingonyi explores how racist language can remain present even in places where it is considered unacceptable. The poet describes the word as “my shadow on this unlit road”. The metaphor of a “shadow” suggests that the word follows the speaker, making it difficult for him to escape its presence. Furthermore, the phrase “unlit road” creates a sense of darkness and uncertainty, possibly reflecting the speaker's discomfort when confronting racism. Chingonyi also describes the word as being “smuggled from polite conversation”. The verb “smuggled” suggests secrecy and deliberate action, as the word enters a space where it would normally be excluded. This exposes the contradiction between rejecting racism publicly and allowing racist language to persist in everyday conversations. Therefore, Chingonyi suggests that racism can remain present beneath the surface, even when society appears to reject it.

Another way Chingonyi explores identity is through the relationship between language and history. The phrase “he’s only signifyin(g) on the sign” suggests that the speaker recognises a difference between the word's original meaning and how another person uses it. The term “signifyin(g)” draws attention to how language can be used in different ways depending on its context and intention. Furthermore, the metaphor “making wine from the bad blood of history” suggests that something harmful can be transformed into something different. The word “wine” contrasts with “bad blood”, presenting the possibility of creating something new from a painful past. However, the reference to “history” reminds readers that the word remains connected to the suffering caused by racism. Through this, Chingonyi explores how language can be reclaimed, while showing that its historical significance cannot simply be erased.

Lastly, Chingonyi explores how experiences of racism can leave a lasting emotional impact. The phrase “Think of how you came into my life that day” uses direct address, as the speaker speaks to the word as though it were a person. This makes the word seem like an unwanted presence in his life and suggests that his encounter with it has remained in his memory. Furthermore, the phrase “knocking me about the head with your dark hands” uses violent imagery to convey the force of this experience. The verb “knocking” suggests that the encounter has a powerful impact on the speaker, while the personification of the word through “your dark hands” makes the word seem capable of causing harm. This suggests that racist language is not simply a collection of words, but something that can affect how people experience the world and understand their identities. Therefore, Chingonyi highlights the lasting personal consequences of racism.

In conclusion, Kayo Chingonyi uses metaphor, imagery and word choice to explore the relationship between language, history and identity. He shows how racism can continue beneath the surface of polite society, how words can be reclaimed despite their painful origins, and how racist experiences can remain with a person over time. Ultimately, the poem encourages readers to consider not only how racist language is used, but also the history and personal impact behind it.
