## Mood

* ### **Playful but unsettling mood:**

  * “You sly devil.” The phrase initially creates a teasing, conversational tone. “You” directly addresses the word, while “sly devil” resembles a familiar expression used between people who know each other.

  * However, the word “devil” introduces darker associations of evil and temptation beneath the playful surface. The contrast creates an unsettling effect because the language sounds familiar even though its subject carries a painful history. Chingonyi may be encouraging readers to question how easily offensive language can be treated casually, even when its wider implications remain serious.

* ### **Threatening mood:**

  * “poisoned on his lips”. The association with poison creates a sense of danger and contamination. The image suggests that the word can cause harm through the simple act of being spoken.

  * The mood becomes threatening because the source of harm is not necessarily an obvious physical danger. Instead, the threat comes through language, highlighting how racism can affect people within ordinary social situations.

* ### **Aggressive mood:**

  * “knocking me about the head with your dark hands.” The violent verb “knocking” creates an aggressive tone, while the imagined hands make the word seem physically powerful.

  * Emboldening readers to recognise the seriousness of verbal racism. By presenting the word as an attacker, Chingonyi suggests that its impact can extend beyond the moment of speech and affect the speaker's emotional experience.

* ### **Reflective mood:**

  * “the bad blood of history” The history reference creates a serious, reflective tone. “Bad blood” suggests that racial hostility has developed over time rather than appearing without cause. The poem moves beyond an individual encounter to examine the wider forces that continue to influence how racial language is understood.
