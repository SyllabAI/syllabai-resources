## Structure

* ### Narrative progression:

  - The poem progresses from the popularity of waves to the effort required to maintain them, before revealing the boys' shame about their natural hair $\rightarrow$ the shift exposes the hidden emotional cost of conformity, encouraging readers to reconsider the seemingly harmless pursuit of fashion.

* ### Enjambment:

  - “each follicle / knew” continues across the line break, creating a flowing movement that reflects the continuous growth of natural hair. The continuation also delays the completion of the thought, mirroring how the boys' attempts to control their appearance cannot prevent their natural features from returning.

* ### Contrast:

  - “without shame” / “ashamed” contrasts confidence with insecurity. This shift implies that the boys' relationship with their natural hair deteriorates as they become more influenced by beauty standards, highlighting how social pressure can reshape self-perception.

* ### Collective pronouns:

  - “we”, “our”, “ourselves” establish a collective voice, presenting the experience as shared rather than purely individual $\rightarrow$ emphasises how peer pressure and cultural expectations influence an entire group, while also suggesting that the boys collectively participate in the standards affecting them.

* ### Volta:

  - “But for all we tried” marks a turning point through the contrasting conjunction “But”. It challenges the boys' earlier efforts to control their hair, revealing the limitations of their attempts to conform and shifting the poem towards a more critical reflection on identity.

* ### Juxtaposition:

  - “to set ourselves apart, betray our roots” juxtaposes individuality with betrayal. Although the boys want to distinguish themselves through their appearance, they risk distancing themselves from their heritage. The irony evoke the pursuit of social recognition can undermine the very individuality they hope to achieve, leaving readers to question the cost of conformity.
