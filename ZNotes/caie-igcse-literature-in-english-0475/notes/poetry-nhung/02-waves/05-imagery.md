## Imagery

* ### Visual imagery:

  - “pepper grains” creates an image of tightly curled hair, emphasising its natural texture. The comparison makes each curl appear small and distinct $\rightarrow$ highlighting the physical features that the boys later feel pressured to change.

* ### Metaphor:

  - “the facade in place” presents fashionable appearance as a mask hiding natural identity. “In place” suggests that this image must be carefully maintained, implying that fitting in requires continuous effort to conceal one's natural features.

  - “the hair’s natural grain” symbolises natural identity and individuality. The boys' shame suggests that society's beauty standards can make people view an inherent part of themselves as a flaw $\rightarrow$ creating conflict between self-acceptance and social approval.
    Personification:

* ### Personification:

  - “each follicle / knew” gives hair human awareness, as if it anticipates the boys' desire to alter their appearance $\rightarrow$ suggesting that natural hair continues to exist beneath their carefully constructed image, highlighting the difficulty of suppressing their natural identity.

* ### Symbolism:

  - “our roots” symbolises ancestry and cultural heritage. By connecting hair to roots, Chingonyi links physical appearance to personal origins, suggesting that the pressure to change one's appearance can also affect one's relationship with their heritage.

* ### Juxtaposition:

  - “slick” contrasts with “natural grain”, highlighting the difference between controlled, fashionable hair and natural texture. Therefore, highlighting how dominant beauty standards can encourage people to reject their natural features in exchange for social acceptance.
