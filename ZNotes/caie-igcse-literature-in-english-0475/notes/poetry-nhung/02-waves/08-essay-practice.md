## Essay practice

### What is PEEL?

- P – Point: State the main idea of your paragraph.
- E – Evidence: Support your point with a quotation from the poem.
- E – Explanation: Analyse the poet’s techniques, word choices, and their deeper meanings.
- L – Link: Link your analysis back to the question and the poet’s overall message.

### Model Essay:

Through the poem Waves, Kayo Chingonyi explores how social pressure and popular culture can affect people's natural identity. He shows how following trends can help people feel included, how beauty standards can make people feel insecure, and how trying to fit in can cause people to lose touch with their cultural background.

Firstly, Chingonyi explores how social expectations affect the way people see their natural appearance. The poet writes, “the facade in place”. The metaphor of a “facade” suggests that the boys create a false appearance to hide their natural features. The word “place” suggests that they have to put effort into maintaining this appearance. This shows that fitting in is not always easy, as people may feel the need to change themselves to meet society’s expectations. Furthermore, the phrase “the hair’s natural grain” highlights their natural hair texture. The contrast between their natural hair and the appearance they want suggests that beauty standards can make people see their natural features as flaws. From that, it shows how social pressure can affect people's confidence and make them hide parts of themselves to gain acceptance.

Another way Chingonyi explores identity is through popular culture and shared experiences. The phrase “you're sweet like chocolate, boy” uses a simile to create a playful and affectionate tone. The reference suggests that the hairstyle is connected to popular culture and something the boys can share with others. Furthermore, the phrase “everyone had a method” uses the word “everyone” to show how popular the hairstyle has become. It suggests that many boys are following the same trend, which allows them to feel connected to their peers. However, it also creates pressure to look similar to others, implying that although following trends can help people feel like they belong, it can also make it harder for them to express their individuality. Therefore, Chingonyi suggests that popular culture can bring people together, but it can also encourage them to conform.

Lastly, Chingonyi explores how trying to fit in can create conflict with cultural identity. The phrase “ashamed of the hair’s natural grain” uses emotive language to show how embarrassed the boys feel about their natural hair. The word “ashamed” suggests that society's beauty standards have affected the way they see themselves, making them feel insecure about a natural part of their identity. Furthermore, the phrase “betray our roots” uses symbolism, as “roots” represent their cultural background and origins. The word “betray” suggests disloyalty, showing that changing their natural appearance may feel like rejecting an important part of who they are. The pronoun “our” also shows that this is a shared experience among the boys. Through that, Chingonyi shows that trying to fit in can come at an emotional cost, as people may feel pressured to reject parts of their cultural identity to gain acceptance.

In conclusion, Kayo Chingonyi uses language and imagery to explore how social pressure shapes people's identities. He shows how popular trends can help people feel included, but beauty standards can also cause insecurity and make people feel ashamed of their natural features. Ultimately, the poem suggests that people should recognise the effects of social pressure and learn to accept their natural identity instead of changing themselves just to fit in.
