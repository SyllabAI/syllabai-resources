## **Subject**

* ### Metaphor and symbolism:

  * “The year waves came in, when we sang.” The phrase “waves came in” refers to the popular hairstyle, but “waves” can also suggest a wave of a cultural trend spreading through society. The verb “came” presents the hairstyle as something that arrives and gains popularity, suggesting how changing trends influence young people.

  * The collective pronoun “we” establishes a shared experience, suggesting that the speaker and his peers participate in the same cultural movement. The phrase “when we sang” links the hairstyle to music, particularly R&B culture, showing how popular music influences fashion and identity. $\rightarrow$ Connects to identity, belonging, and social influence, showing how trends can shape the way young people present themselves.

* ### Semantic field of appearance and fashion:

  * “slick”, “crazy-paved”, “clippers”, “edges”, “barbers”, “trim”, “beauty”, “shaved”, “sideburns”, “eyebrows”. The repeated references to grooming and physical appearance establish a semantic field of fashion and beauty. $\rightarrow$ making hair the poem's central focus, showing how much effort the boys invest in controlling their appearance.

  * The detailed references to grooming suggest that appearance is not simply personal preference; it becomes an important part of social acceptance. $\rightarrow$ Connects to beauty standards and fitting in, revealing how social expectations influence personal choices.

* ### Allusion:

  * “R&B stars”: The reference to R&B stars establishes the influence of popular music and celebrity culture on the speaker's generation. The stars become role models whose hairstyles and appearance are copied by young fans.

  * Ultimately suggesting that identity is partly constructed through admiration and imitation, as young people adopt the appearance of figures they consider desirable. $\rightarrow$ Connecting to identity and social pressure, showing how popular culture can influence the way people understand beauty.

* ### Contrast:

  * “transcendent beauty” contrasted with “ashamed of the hair’s natural grain”. “Transcendent beauty” presents the desired hairstyle as something extraordinary, almost beyond ordinary physical attractiveness. In contrast, “ashamed” reveals the negative feelings associated with the speaker's natural hair. $\rightarrow$ The contrast suggests that the boys have learnt to admire an altered appearance while feeling dissatisfied with their natural features. $\rightarrow$ Creating an underlying criticism of beauty standards that encourage people to view their natural appearance as inferior.
