## Essay practice

### What is PEEL?

- P – Point: State the main idea of your paragraph.
- E – Evidence: Support your point with a quotation from the poem.
- E – Explanation: Analyse the poet’s techniques, word choices, and their deeper meanings.
- L – Link: Link your analysis back to the question and the poet’s overall message.

### Model Essay

Through the poem *Broomhall*, Kayo Chingonyi explores how people's identities are shaped by their cultural backgrounds, relationships with others, and connections to places. He shows how people can be identified through their appearance, how cultural differences can affect their sense of belonging, and how familiar places can provide comfort when their identities feel complicated.

Firstly, Chingonyi explores how a person's identity can be shaped by their cultural background and the way others perceive them. The poet describes “the Arabic texture of my hair”. The word “texture” creates a sensory image of the speaker's hair, drawing attention to a physical feature associated with his cultural identity. However, the phrase “what my aunt calls” suggests that this description comes from another person's perspective, showing how identity can be influenced by the way others interpret our appearance. Furthermore, the speaker states, “I'm Abdi outside the only shop”. The name “Abdi” suggests that people associate him with a particular cultural background. The word “outside” also establishes his connection to the shop, a familiar place within his community. Through this, Chingonyi explores how identity is shaped by both cultural heritage and the places where people are recognised by others.

Another way Chingonyi explores identity is through cultural differences and the desire to belong. The phrase “blue shameless blackness” uses an oxymoron, combining “shameless” with “blackness” to draw attention to the speaker's racial identity. The word “shameless” suggests confidence and a refusal to feel embarrassed about who he is. However, it may also imply that Black identity is something society has encouraged people to feel ashamed of. Furthermore, Chingonyi contrasts the speaker's experiences with those of “middle-class white boys in reggae-bands”. The contrast between their social backgrounds and racial identities raises questions about whether people can fully understand a culture simply by participating in its music. The word “love” in “who love roots and can love as if their love is enough” suggests that admiration alone may not provide a complete understanding of another person's experiences. Through this, Chingonyi questions the difference between appreciating a culture and understanding the history and identity behind it.

Lastly, Chingonyi explores how familiar places can provide a sense of belonging. The speaker states, “I can be Abdi and this shop / can be all the home I need.” The phrase “I can be Abdi” suggests that the speaker is able to express a particular version of his identity in this place. The extended metaphor of the shop as “home” presents it as a source of comfort and acceptance. Rather than referring to a traditional home, the speaker finds belonging in an everyday location where he can interact with others. However, the phrase “all the home I need” may also suggest that his sense of belonging is limited to this particular place. The word “today” earlier in the stanza reinforces this idea, as it may imply that his feelings of belonging are temporary rather than permanent. Through this, Chingonyi suggests that places can provide emotional security, even when a person's identity and sense of belonging remain uncertain.

In conclusion, Kayo Chingonyi uses imagery, contrast and metaphor to explore the relationship between people, place and identity. He shows how cultural background influences the way people are perceived, how differences can complicate relationships between communities, and how familiar places can provide a sense of belonging. Ultimately, the poem suggests that identity is complex and that feeling at home may depend not only on where a person comes from, but also on where they feel accepted.
