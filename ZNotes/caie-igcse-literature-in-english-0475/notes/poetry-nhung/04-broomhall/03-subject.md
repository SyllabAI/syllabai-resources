## Subject

* ### Sensory imagery:

  * “the Arabic texture of my hair” and “Texture” appeal to the sense of touch, making the speaker's physical appearance more vivid.

  * The imagery highlights how cultural identity can be tied to visible features.

  * “the smell of cassava roasted over open coals”. This smell imagery appeals to the sense of smell, creating a vivid impression of the food and its cultural associations.

  * It connects the speaker's surroundings with familiar cultural experiences.

* ### Oxymoron:

  * “blue shameless blackness” $\rightarrow$ “Shameless” suggests confidence and a refusal to feel embarrassed, while “blackness” emphasises racial identity.

  * The unusual combination encourages readers to consider how the speaker views and expresses his identity.

* ### Contrast:

  * “middle-class white boys in reggae bands”. The contrast between the speaker and the white boys highlights differences in race and social background.

  * It raises the question of whether appreciating another culture means fully understanding its experiences and history.

* ### Third-person self-reference:

  * “At least these boys who call me / Abdi seem to be fond of Abdi.” Referring to himself as “Abdi” in the third person creates distance between the speaker and the identity others recognise.

  * From that, it may suggest uncertainty about whether the identity others assign to him fully represents who he is.

* ### Metaphor:

  * “this shop / can be all the home I need” The shop is presented as a form of home rather than simply a physical location, implying that a place can provide emotional comfort and belonging.

* ### Enjambment:

  * “I can be Abdi and this shop / can be all the home I need.” The sentence continues across the line break, creating a flowing rhythm.

  * The separation briefly draws attention to the connection between the speaker's identity and his sense of home.
