## Overview

### Structure:

* 27 lines, 5 stanzas.

### Themes:

* Identity

* Cultural heritage

* Assimilation

* Belonging

* Cultural appropriation

* Detachment and displacement

### Background:

* Broomhall is a district in Sheffield, South Yorkshire, England.

* The area has a diverse population, including communities with African and Caribbean heritage.

* The poem refers to cultural foods such as “tamarind balls”, “Irish Moss” and “Supermalt”, which are associated with Caribbean culture.

* The speaker's references to Arabic hair texture, the name “Abdi” and African food suggest the presence of different cultural identities within the community.

* Reggae music and “roots” introduce questions about cultural heritage and whether people who appreciate a culture can fully understand its history and experiences.

* The shop becomes an important place where the speaker experiences familiarity and a sense of belonging.

### Poet's purpose:

* Identity: To explore how cultural background, appearance, and other people's perceptions influence a person's identity.

* Assimilation: To examine how people navigate different cultural identities and expectations.

* Belonging: To show how familiar places and everyday interactions can provide a sense of home.

* Cultural understanding: To question whether appreciating another culture means genuinely understanding its history and experiences.

* Detachment: To explore the uncertainty of belonging, suggesting that a person may feel accepted in one place without feeling completely at home everywhere.
