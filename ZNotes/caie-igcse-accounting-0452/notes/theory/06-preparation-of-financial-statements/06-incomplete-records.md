## Incomplete Records

* Certain businesses do not maintain a complete set of double-entry bookkeeping records (for example, small businesses). No trial balance can be drawn up for them, and some preparatory calculations are required before they can start preparing their financial statements.
* Under a single entry system, none of the aspects (Dr. or Cr.) of the financial transaction are recorded, one of the aspects is recorded, or both are recorded.
* When a list of assets and liabilities is prepared without double-entry bookkeeping, it is known as a statement of affairs, like a balance sheet.

### **Why is the Single-Entry System Used?**

* Usually, small businesses don’t maintain complete accounting book records as traders’ partnerships.
* Businesses are not supposed to maintain financial statements by law compulsorily and generally adopt an entry system.
* Since the maintenance of books of accounts involves a lot of accounting knowledge and manual work, businesses prefer not to maintain complete books.
* It is cheaper as the number of books is less, and an accountant/clerk/cashier need not be appointed.

* To calculate profit, a capital A/C can be made to account for any changes in capital (for example, drawings). Then, the difference in capital is taken as either the profit or loss. Doing this, however, will not give any information about the gross profit, sales, etc. No analysis can occur (Analysis will help make informed future decisions). So, this is one drawback involved.
* **Profit = Closing Capital + Drawings - Additional Capital - Opening Capital**
* Sometimes, a business can provide information about money received, paid, etc. In addition to assets and liabilities, it is possible to calculate sales, purchases, and expenses. Therefore, a complete set of financial statements can be made after some calculations.
* The amount paid to the creditors is not always going to be equal to the purchases figure, and the same applies to the sales figure, which is relative to the amount received from debtors for obvious reasons such as goods being bought from the previous year, good that has not yet been paid for, etc.
* Credit Sales = Total Sales - Cash Sales or Prepare a sales ledger control A/C
* Credit purchases = Total Purchases - Cash Purchases or Prepare a purchases ledger control A/C
* **Mark-up**: $\frac{Gross\ Profit}{Cost\ Of\ Sales}$ \* 100
* **Margin**: $\frac{Gross\ Profit}{Revenue}$ \* 100
* The rate of inventory turnover is the number of times a business replaces its inventory in a given period of time and is also given by the formula: $\frac{Cost\ of\ Sale}{Average\ Inventory} \times 100$ where Average Inventory is $\frac{Opening\ Inventory\ +\ Closing\ Inventory}{2}$
* **Stolen Cash = Prepare a cash book**
* **Depreciation = Apply revaluation method**
