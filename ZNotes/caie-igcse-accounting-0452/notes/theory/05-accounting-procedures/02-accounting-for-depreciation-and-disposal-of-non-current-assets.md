## Accounting for Depreciation and Disposal of Non-Current Assets

* **Depreciation** is a year-end adjustment that reduces the value of non-current assets with time, i.e., it estimates the loss in value of a non-current asset over its expected working life.
* Non-current/fixed assets depreciate with time as they are used, and depreciation is the loss in their value; this is an application of the principle of prudence, as showing these assets at their cost prices can be misleading and lead to overstating a business’s profit which can lead to more being drawn by the owner than the business can afford.
* Land does not generally depreciate unless it is a mine or a well wherein something is drawn from the land (it’s then called Depletion); buildings do depreciate.
* The purchase of a non-current asset is a part of capital expenditure, so the entire cost of the non-current asset is not charged as an expense the year it is purchased, as it helps the business for several years. The capital expenditure is thus matched (by estimating) against the sales, i.e. the cost of the non-current asset is spread over the years, which benefits from using that asset.
* The principle of prudence is also applied where the non-current asset is shown at its net book value in the SOFP/BS, thus overriding the principle of historical cost.
* Depreciation can be caused by Physical deterioration and/or Economic or other reasons and/or the passage of time.
* There are several depreciation methods, but the most appropriate one (which spreads cost fairly) must be used consistently. Refer to TABLE 4 for the different methods of depreciation.

| **Method:** | **Formulae/ Additional info** | **Notes:** |
|----|----|----|
| Straight line method of depreciation/ fixed instalment method. | ‎$\frac{Cost of asset - Residual value}{Number of expected years of use‎}$ | The formula gives us a constant value that must be deducted from the asset annually. The depreciation value and percentage stay constant. The asset's value can fall to nil if no estimated residual value exists. |
| Reducing Balance method of depreciation/ diminishing balance method of depreciation | This method is used when the greater benefits from using the asset have been gained in the earlier years of its life (the depreciation amount reduced). This method is used when assets become obsolete quickly. Any residual value is taken into consideration when the depreciation percentage is decided. The depreciation amounts are expressed in whole dollars and thus are rounded off. | The amount of depreciation reduces each year, but the percentage stays constant as depreciation is calculated on the net book value/ written down value. The asset's value can never fall to nil as the depreciation is always calculated from the NBV. |
| Revaluation method of depreciation | The assets are valued at the end of each financial year, and the amount is compared with the NBV of the previous financial year (or cost- if the first year of ownership). The amount by which the asset's value falls is the depreciation for that financial year. | This depreciation method is used when it is impractical/difficult to keep detailed records regarding certain non-current assets, e.g., Loose tools. |

* **Factors to be considered when choosing a method of depreciation are:**
  * Life expectancy
  * Residual value
  * Estimated benefit from the use of the asset.
* If the straight-line method of depreciation has been used, the dep. Amt. Stays constant.
* It is a business’ choice whether to start depreciating the asset from the date of purchase; for example, only a proportion of the dep. May be charged (annual dep. /12\* months of ownership till financial year end) OR it may be decided to ignore the date of purchase and record a full year of depreciation, or not depreciate it in the year of the purchase at all. The same applies to the sale of a non-current asset.
* When the revaluation method of depreciation is used, there is no provision for dep. A/C is created, and the same (asset) A/C is used to calculate dep.
* Depreciation reduces the year's net profit when debited into the IS from the provision for dep.
* In the SOFP, the dep. to date (the accumulated dep. up till this financial year + depreciation for this year) is subtracted from the cost price to become the NBV (which thus reduces year by year).
