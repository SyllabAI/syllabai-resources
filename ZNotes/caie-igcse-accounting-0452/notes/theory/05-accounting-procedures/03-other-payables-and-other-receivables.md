## Other Payables and Other Receivables

* Often, adjustments are made in the financial statements to present a more accurate view of the profit/loss of a business; these adjustments are known as year-end adjustments.
* In practice, it is expected to find expenses or income paid in one financial year but related to other financial years.
* Only items relating to the specified time period should be included in the income statement; the timing of the actual receipts and payments is irrelevant. This is a practical application of the accruals principle.
* It is necessary to adjust the income statement about amounts prepaid or accrued. This results in a more accurate profit/loss figure, and therefore, more meaningful comparisons can be made between financial statements of different years and businesses.

### **Accrued**:

* An amount due in an accounting period which remains unpaid till the end of that period

* When an expense is accrued, some benefit or service has been received during the accounting period, but this benefit or service has not been paid for by the end of the period.

### **Prepaid Expenses**:

* A prepayment is an amount paid in advance. When an expense is prepaid, a payment has been made during the financial year for some benefits or services to be received in a future accounting period.

* The outstanding amount is added to the respective expense, and prepaid is subtracted.

* Wherever several expenses are accrued, a collective figure is often shown in place of posting individual figures.

---

* **For Expenses to be paid (due)/ Accrued expenses:** Total expenses incurred in the trading period (including ones not going to be paid for in this year but have been incurred)- expenses to be paid for (opening Cr balance) = Amount To be transferred to IS.

* **Accrued Income:** Where an item of income is accrued, a person receiving a benefit or service from the business during the accounting period has not paid for it by the end.

* **Prepaid Income**: Where an item of income is prepaid, a person has paid for a benefit or service from the business, but the business has not provided this at the end of the financial year. The accruals principle applies to prepaid income; thus, any amount for which the legal title of the goods or services has not been passed on to the buyer has to be deducted from the total income transferred to the IS and shown as a liability in the SOFP/BS.
