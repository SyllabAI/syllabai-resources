# Accounting Procedures

## Capital and Revenue Expenditure and Receipts

### **Capital and Revenue Expenditure**

* Purchasing a non-current asset is regarded as capital expenditure; thus, its cost is not recorded in the purchases ledger but in the non-current assets A/C. The entire cost of the non-current asset is not charged as an expense the year it is purchased, as it helps the business for several years. The capital expenditure is thus matched (by estimating) against the sales, i.e. the cost of the non-current asset is spread over the years, which benefits from using that asset. This is known as depreciation and is revenue expenditure.
* **The capital expenditure includes** The cost of the NCA, legal costs incurred for the purchase of the NCA, carriage on the NCA and installation fees.
* Because the income statement includes revenue expenditure, recording capital expenditure as revenue expenditure will understate the profit and the NCA (thus affecting the accounting equation) and vice versa.
* Revenue expenditure is the cost incurred by running the business on a day-to-day basis. E.g. repairs on any non-current asset, general expenses, cost of public transport, etc. These are matched against the revenue receipt in the income statement.

### **Capital and Revenue Receipt**

* Capital receipt is the income from sales of NCA. This should not be entered in the IS, but any profit or loss incurred from the sale of an NCA should be included in the Income statement.
* The receipt from the disposal of non-current assets is regarded as a capital receipt; it is recorded in the disposal of a non-current asset(s) A/C, not the sales A/C.
* **Revenue receipt:** The income from the running of the business, such as commission received, rent received, sales, etc.
