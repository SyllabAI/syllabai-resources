## Bond Energies

* **Bond energy**: The energy needed to break one mole of a particular covalent bond. It is measured in $kJ/mol$.
* Bond energies can be used to calculate the enthalpy change of a reaction:

$$
\Delta H = \text{energy needed to break bonds (reactants)} - \text{energy released making bonds (products)}
$$

### Methane + Chlorine Reaction

$$
CH_4(g) + Cl_2(g) \rightarrow CH_3Cl(g) + HCl(g)
$$

![](https://znotes-static.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/edexcel/igcse/chemistry-4ch1/6685198e57d2ba2bb52e1dd530c57d04c2f989d00c9bf1ea.png =550x)

*Displayed formulae showing the bonds in the reactants and products of the reaction between methane and chlorine.*

**Bonds broken (reactants)**

$$
4 \ C-H: \ 4 \times 413 = 1652
$$

$$
1 \ Cl-Cl: \ 1 \times 243 = 243
$$

$$
\text{Total for reactants} = 1652 + 243 = 1895 \ kJ
$$

**Bonds made (products)**

$$
3 \ C-H: \ 3 \times 413 = 1239
$$

$$
1 \ C-Cl: \ 1 \times 346 = 346
$$

$$
1 \ H-Cl: \ 1 \times 432 = 432
$$

$$
\text{Total for products} = 1239 + 346 + 432 = 2017 \ kJ
$$

$$
\Delta H = 1895 - 2017 = -122 \ kJ/mol
$$

* $\Delta H$ is negative, so the reaction is exothermic: more energy is released making bonds than is needed to break bonds.
