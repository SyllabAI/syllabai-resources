# Energetics

## Endothermic & Exothermic Reactions

### Exothermic Reactions

* Exothermic reactions are reactions that give out heat to the surroundings.
* This causes the test tube with the reactants and products of the reaction to get warmer.
* The products have less chemical energy than the reactants, so $\Delta H$ is **negative**.
* Chemical energy is transferred to thermal energy that warms the surroundings up.
* Making bonds is exothermic (MEXO).
* Examples: combustion, neutralisation, displacement reactions

### Endothermic Reactions

* Endothermic reactions are reactions that absorb heat from the surroundings.
* This causes the test tube with the reactants and products of the reaction to get colder.
* The products have more chemical energy than the reactants, so $\Delta H$ is **positive**.
* Heat energy from the surroundings is transferred to chemical energy, which cools the surroundings down.
* Breaking bonds is endothermic (BENDO).
* Examples: thermal decomposition

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/b009a903d77478ca7e2dd529.webp =600x)

*Energy level diagram for an endothermic reaction: the products are at a higher energy level than the reactants, so energy is taken in from the surroundings and $\Delta H$ is positive.*

### Features of an Energy Level Diagram

* **Reactant and product levels**: Horizontal lines showing the energy stored in the reactants and in the products.
* **Energy difference ($\Delta H$)**: The vertical gap between the reactant and product levels. This is the overall energy change of the reaction.
  * Products **below** reactants: energy is released, so the reaction is exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$).
  * Products **above** reactants: energy is absorbed, so the reaction is endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$).
* **Activation energy ($E_a$)**: The minimum energy needed for a reaction to start. It is shown as the "hump" rising from the reactant level to the highest point of the curve on a reaction profile.

### Why Bond Breaking and Bond Making Involve Energy

* **Breaking bonds requires energy (endothermic)**: Atoms in a bond are held together by strong forces of attraction. Energy must be put in to overcome these forces and pull the atoms apart.
* **Making bonds releases energy (exothermic)**: When new bonds form, atoms are pulled together by forces of attraction and move into a more stable, lower energy arrangement. The extra energy is released to the surroundings.
* In a reaction, bonds in the reactants are broken and new bonds in the products are made:
  * If **more** energy is released making bonds than is needed to break bonds, the reaction is **exothermic**.
  * If **more** energy is needed breaking bonds than is released making bonds, the reaction is **endothermic**.

$$
\Delta H = \text{energy needed to break bonds} - \text{energy released making bonds}
$$
