## Enthalpy Change

* **Specific heat capacity**: The amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of $1 \ g$ of a substance by $1 \ ^\circ C$. **Units for SHC**: $J/g/^\circ C$
* The amount of heat energy is directly proportional to the mass and the temperature change.

$$Q = mc\Delta T
$$

* Heat energy change = mass $\times$ specific heat capacity $\times$ temperature change
* $100 \ cm^3$ of water $= 100 \ g$ of water

### Simple Calorimetry: General Principles

* **Calorimetry** is used to measure the heat energy given out or taken in by a reaction, by measuring the temperature change of a known mass of water (or solution).
* The energy released or absorbed by the reaction is assumed to be equal to the energy gained or lost by the water.
* **Apparatus for combustion**: A spirit burner containing the fuel, a copper or metal calorimeter (can) containing a measured volume of water, a thermometer, a clamp stand and a balance.
* **Apparatus for reactions in solution** (dissolving, displacement, neutralisation): A polystyrene cup (with a lid) placed in a beaker for support, a thermometer and a measuring cylinder. Polystyrene is a good insulator, so less heat is lost to the surroundings.
* **General method**:
  1. Measure a known mass or volume of water (or solution) and record its starting temperature.
  2. Carry out the reaction (burn the fuel, or add the second reactant) and stir.
  3. Record the highest (or lowest) temperature reached.
  4. Calculate $\Delta T$, then $Q$ using $Q = mc\Delta T$.
  5. Calculate the number of moles that reacted, then $\Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n}$.
* $\Delta H$ is **negative** if the temperature rises (exothermic) and **positive** if the temperature falls (endothermic).
* Results are usually less exothermic than the true value because of **heat loss** to the surroundings and incomplete combustion.

*Results from burning ethanol in a spirit burner to heat water.*

$$m = 100 \ g \qquad c = 4.18 \ J/g/^\circ C
$$

$$\Delta T = 62.8 - 21.5 = 41.3 \ ^\circ C
$$

$$Q = mc\Delta T = 100 \times 4.18 \times 41.3 = 17 \ 260 \ J = 17.26 \ kJ
$$

$$\text{mass of ethanol burned} = 137.36 - 136.58 = 0.78 \ g
$$

$$n = \frac{m}{M_r} = \frac{0.78}{46} = 0.01696 \ mol
$$

$$\Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n} = -\frac{17.26}{0.01696} = -1020 \ kJ/mol
$$
