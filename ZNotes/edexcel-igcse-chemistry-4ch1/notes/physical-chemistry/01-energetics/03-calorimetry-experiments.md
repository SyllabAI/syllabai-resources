## Calorimetry Experiments

### Combustion Reactions

* Measure $100 \ cm^3$ of cold water using a measuring cylinder and transfer the water to a copper can.
* Record the initial temperature of the water.
* Weigh a spirit burner containing ethanol with its lid on. The lid should be kept on when the wick is not lit to prevent the alcohol from evaporating.
* Arrange the apparatus as shown.
* Light the wick to heat the water and stop when there is a reasonable temperature rise.
* Stir the water thoroughly and record the final temperature.
* Weigh the spirit burner again.
* Repeat to check for reliability.

*Results from burning ethanol to heat water.*

$$Q = mc\Delta T \qquad m = 100 \ g \qquad c = 4.18 \ J/g/^\circ C
$$

$$\Delta T = 62.8 - 21.5 = 41.3 \ ^\circ C
$$

$$Q = 100 \times 4.18 \times 41.3 = 17 \ 260 \ J = 17.26 \ kJ
$$

$$\text{mass of ethanol} = 137.36 - 136.58 = 0.78 \ g \qquad M_r = 46
$$

$$n = \frac{m}{M_r} = \frac{0.78}{46} = 0.01696 \ mol
$$

$$\Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n} = -\frac{17.26}{0.01696} = -1020 \ kJ/mol
$$

### Displacement Reactions

* Place a polystyrene cup in a $250 \ cm^3$ glass beaker.
* Transfer $50 \ cm^3$ of $0.2 \ mol/dm^3$ copper(II) sulfate solution into the polystyrene cup using a measuring cylinder.
* Weigh $1.2 \ g$ of zinc using a weighing boat on a balance.
* Record the initial temperature of the solution.
* Add the zinc.
* Stir the solution as quickly as possible.
* Record the maximum temperature reached.

$$Zn(s) + CuSO_4(aq) \rightarrow ZnSO_4(aq) + Cu(s)
$$

### Data for Displacement Reaction


#|
|| **Measurement** | **Value** ||
|| Initial temperature of copper(II) sulfate solution ($^\circ C$) | $17.0$ ||
|| Maximum temperature of reaction mixture ($^\circ C$) | $27.3$ ||
|#

*Results from the displacement reaction between zinc and copper(II) sulfate solution.*

$$\Delta T = 27.3 - 17.0 = 10.3 \ ^\circ C
$$

$$Q = mc\Delta T = 50 \times 4.18 \times 10.3 = 2152.7 \ J = 2.1527 \ kJ
$$

$$n(Zn) = \frac{1.2}{65} = 0.0185 \ mol \qquad n(CuSO_4) = cV = 0.2 \times 0.05 = 0.01 \ mol
$$

* Zinc is in excess, so copper(II) sulfate is the limiting reactant ($0.01 \ mol$).

$$\Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n} = -\frac{2.1527}{0.01} = -215 \ kJ/mol
$$

### Salts Dissolving in Water

* Place a polystyrene cup in a $250 \ cm^3$ glass beaker.
* Transfer $100 \ cm^3$ of water into the polystyrene cup using a measuring cylinder.
* Record the initial temperature of the water.
* Weigh $5.2 \ g$ of ammonium chloride using a weighing boat on a balance.
* Add the ammonium chloride to the water and stir vigorously until all of it has dissolved.
* Record the minimum temperature reached.

### Data for Dissolving


#|
|| **Measurement** | **Value** ||
|| Initial temperature of water ($^\circ C$) | $18.3$ ||
|| Minimum temperature of water ($^\circ C$) | $15.1$ ||
|#

*Results from dissolving ammonium chloride in water.*

$$m = 100 \ g \qquad c = 4.18 \ J/g/^\circ C \qquad \Delta T = 18.3 - 15.1 = 3.2 \ ^\circ C
$$

$$Q = 100 \times 4.18 \times 3.2 = 1337.6 \ J = 1.3376 \ kJ
$$

$$n(NH_4Cl) = \frac{5.2}{53.5} = 0.0972 \ mol
$$

* The temperature fell, so the process is endothermic and $\Delta H$ is positive.

$$\Delta H = +\frac{1.3376}{0.0972} = +13.8 \ kJ/mol
$$

### Neutralisation Reactions

* Place a polystyrene cup in a $250 \ cm^3$ glass beaker.
* Transfer $25 \ cm^3$ of $2 \ mol/dm^3$ potassium hydroxide solution into the polystyrene cup using a measuring cylinder.
* Record the initial temperature.
* Fill a burette with $50 \ cm^3$ of dilute hydrochloric acid.
* Use the burette to add $5 \ cm^3$ of dilute hydrochloric acid to the potassium hydroxide.
* Stir vigorously.
* Record the maximum temperature reached.
* Continue adding $5 \ cm^3$ of dilute hydrochloric acid in portions and recording the maximum temperature reached each time, until a total volume of $50 \ cm^3$ has been added.
* Plot a graph of temperature against volume of acid added. The highest temperature on the graph is reached when the alkali has been exactly neutralised.

$$KOH(aq) + HCl(aq) \rightarrow KCl(aq) + H_2O(l)
$$

### Data for Neutralisation Reaction


#|
|| **Measurement** | **Value** ||
|| Initial temperature ($^\circ C$) | $19.3$ ||
|| Maximum temperature ($^\circ C$) | $31.8$ ||
|| Volume of acid added at maximum temperature ($cm^3$) | $28$ ||
|#

*Results from the neutralisation of potassium hydroxide with hydrochloric acid.*

* The mass of solution used is the **total volume** of liquid in the cup at the maximum temperature: $25 \ cm^3$ of potassium hydroxide $+ 28 \ cm^3$ of acid $= 53 \ cm^3$, so $m = 53 \ g$ (assuming the solution has the same density as water).

$$\Delta T = 31.8 - 19.3 = 12.5 \ ^\circ C
$$

$$Q = mc\Delta T = 53 \times 4.18 \times 12.5 = 2769.3 \ J = 2.7693 \ kJ
$$

$$n(KOH) = cV = 2 \times 0.025 = 0.05 \ mol
$$

$$\Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n} = -\frac{2.7693}{0.05} = -55.4 \ kJ/mol
$$
