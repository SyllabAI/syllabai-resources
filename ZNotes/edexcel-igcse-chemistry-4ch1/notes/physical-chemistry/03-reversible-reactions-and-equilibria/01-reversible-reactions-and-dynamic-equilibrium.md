# Reversible Reactions and Equilibria

## Reversible Reactions and Dynamic Equilibrium

* **Reversible reaction**: A reaction in which the products can react to form the reactants again. The symbol $\rightleftharpoons$ shows that a reaction is reversible.
* **Dynamic equilibrium**: In a closed system, a reversible reaction reaches dynamic equilibrium. The forward and backward reactions are still happening, but at the **same rate**, so the reactants are formed just as fast as the products. As a result, the **concentrations of the reactants and products stay constant**.
* A dynamic equilibrium can only be reached in a *closed system* (nothing can enter or leave).
* **Le Chatelier’s principle**: If a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing the conditions (e.g. temperature or pressure), the position of equilibrium shifts to counteract the change.

### Dehydration of Copper(II) Sulfate Crystals

* If you heat blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals, the crystals turn white and water is given off (they lose their water of crystallisation).
* If water is added to white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, it turns blue again and gets hot.
* We can write two equations, one for the dehydration and one for the hydration of copper(II) sulfate:

$$
CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O(s) \rightarrow CuSO_4(s) + 5H_2O(l) \qquad \text{(dehydration)}
$$

$$
CuSO_4(s) + 5H_2O(l) \rightarrow CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O(s) \qquad \text{(hydration)}
$$

* Written as a reversible reaction:

$$
CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O(s) \rightleftharpoons CuSO_4(s) + 5H_2O(l)
$$

### Heating Ammonium Chloride

* When ammonium chloride is heated at the bottom of a test tube, it undergoes **thermal decomposition**: the white solid breaks down into two colourless gases, ammonia and hydrogen chloride.

$$
NH_4Cl(s) \rightarrow NH_3(g) + HCl(g)
$$

* The gases rise up the tube to the cooler part near the top. On **cooling**, the reaction reverses: ammonia and hydrogen chloride recombine to form white ammonium chloride again.

$$
NH_3(g) + HCl(g) \rightarrow NH_4Cl(s)
$$

* This is why the white solid disappears from the bottom of the tube and reappears further up.

**Linking this to Le Chatelier's principle**

* The forward reaction (decomposition) is **endothermic**, so the backward reaction (recombination) is **exothermic**.
* At the hot bottom of the tube, the higher temperature makes the position of equilibrium shift in the **endothermic** direction (to the right), so ammonium chloride decomposes into ammonia and hydrogen chloride.
* At the cooler top of the tube, the lower temperature makes the position of equilibrium shift in the **exothermic** direction (to the left), so ammonia and hydrogen chloride recombine to form ammonium chloride.
* This shows that the same reaction can go in either direction depending on the conditions, which is what makes it reversible.
* Written as a reversible reaction:

$$
NH_4Cl(s) \rightleftharpoons NH_3(g) + HCl(g)
$$
