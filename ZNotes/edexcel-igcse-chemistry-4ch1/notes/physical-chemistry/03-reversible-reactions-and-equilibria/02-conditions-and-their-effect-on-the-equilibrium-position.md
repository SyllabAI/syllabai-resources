## Conditions and Their Effect on the Equilibrium Position

* You need to know how changing the **temperature** and the **pressure** affects the position of a dynamic equilibrium.
* Le Chatelier's principle: when a condition is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts to **oppose** the change.

### Temperature

* If a reaction is exothermic in the forward direction, it is endothermic in the backward direction (and the other way round).
* If the **temperature is increased**, the position of equilibrium shifts in the **endothermic** direction, as this takes in heat energy and opposes the rise in temperature.
* If the **temperature is decreased**, the position of equilibrium shifts in the **exothermic** direction, as this gives out heat energy and opposes the fall in temperature.

**Worked example**

$$
H_2(g) + I_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HI(g) \qquad \Delta H = -10\ kJ/mol
$$

* $\Delta H$ is **negative**, so the forward reaction is **exothermic** and the backward reaction is **endothermic**.
* If the temperature is increased, the position of equilibrium shifts to the **left** (endothermic direction), so less hydrogen iodide is formed.
* If the temperature is decreased, the position of equilibrium shifts to the **right** (exothermic direction), so more hydrogen iodide is formed.

### Pressure

* Pressure only affects equilibria that involve **gases**.
* The pressure of a gas depends on the number of gas molecules, so you need to count the **moles of gas** on each side of the balanced equation.
* If the **pressure is increased**, the position of equilibrium shifts to the side with **fewer moles of gas**, as this reduces the pressure.
* If the **pressure is decreased**, the position of equilibrium shifts to the side with **more moles of gas**, as this increases the pressure.
* If both sides have the **same number of moles of gas**, changing the pressure has **no effect** on the position of equilibrium. For example, in $H_2(g) + I_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2HI(g)$ there are $2$ moles of gas on each side.

**Worked example**

$$
N_2O_4(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NO_2(g)
$$

* Left side: $1$ mole of gas. Right side: $2$ moles of gas.
* If the pressure is increased, the position of equilibrium shifts to the **left** (fewer moles of gas), so more $N_2O_4$ forms.
* If the pressure is decreased, the position of equilibrium shifts to the **right** (more moles of gas), so more $NO_2$ forms.

**Worked example**

$$
N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)
$$

* Left side: $1 + 3 = 4$ moles of gas. Right side: $2$ moles of gas.
* Increasing the pressure shifts the position of equilibrium to the **right** (fewer moles of gas), so more ammonia is formed.

### Catalyst

* A catalyst speeds up the forward and backward reactions by the **same amount**.
* It does **not** change the position of equilibrium, but equilibrium is reached more quickly.

#|
|| **Change in condition** | **Effect on the position of equilibrium** ||
|| Increase temperature | Shifts in the endothermic direction ||
|| Decrease temperature | Shifts in the exothermic direction ||
|| Increase pressure | Shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas ||
|| Decrease pressure | Shifts to the side with more moles of gas ||
|| Add a catalyst | No change (equilibrium is reached faster) ||
|#

*Summary of how changing the conditions affects the position of equilibrium.*

**Practice questions**

1. $2SO_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2SO_3(g) \quad \Delta H = -196\ kJ/mol$. State and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the amount of $SO_3$ formed.
2. For the same reaction, state and explain the effect of increasing the pressure.

**Answers**

1. The amount of $SO_3$ decreases. The forward reaction is exothermic, so increasing the temperature shifts the position of equilibrium in the endothermic direction, to the left.
2. The amount of $SO_3$ increases. There are $3$ moles of gas on the left and $2$ on the right, so increasing the pressure shifts the position of equilibrium to the side with fewer moles of gas, to the right.
