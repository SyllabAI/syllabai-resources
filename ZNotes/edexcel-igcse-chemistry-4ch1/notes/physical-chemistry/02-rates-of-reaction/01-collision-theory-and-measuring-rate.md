# Rates of Reaction

## Collision Theory and Measuring Rate

### Key Terms

* **Collision theory**: For a reaction to happen, particles must collide with each other with enough energy.
* **Activation energy**: The minimum amount of energy that colliding particles need in order to react.
* **Successful collisions**: Collisions with energy greater than or equal to the activation energy. These lead to a reaction.
* The rate of reaction depends on the **frequency of successful collisions**: the more successful collisions per second, the faster the reaction.

### Calculating the Rate of Reaction

$$
\text{rate of reaction} = \frac{\text{change in amount of reactant or product}}{\text{time taken}}
$$

* The change measured can be a change in mass, a volume of gas produced, or a change in temperature or colour.
* Units depend on what is measured, e.g. $g/s$ for mass or $cm^3/s$ for volume of gas.

**Worked example 1**

* $60 \ cm^3$ of hydrogen gas is collected in $30 \ s$ when magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid. Calculate the mean rate of reaction.

$$
\text{rate} = \frac{60}{30} = 2 \ cm^3/s
$$

**Worked example 2**

* A flask of marble chips and hydrochloric acid loses $1.8 \ g$ in mass over $2$ minutes as carbon dioxide escapes. Calculate the mean rate of reaction in $g/s$.

$$
2 \ \text{minutes} = 120 \ s \qquad \text{rate} = \frac{1.8}{120} = 0.015 \ g/s
$$

### Practice Questions

1. $45 \ cm^3$ of oxygen is produced in $90 \ s$ when hydrogen peroxide decomposes. Calculate the mean rate of reaction.
2. A reaction mixture loses $0.60 \ g$ in $40 \ s$. Calculate the mean rate of reaction.
3. Explain, using collision theory, why not every collision between particles leads to a reaction.

**Answers**

1. $\frac{45}{90} = 0.5 \ cm^3/s$
2. $\frac{0.60}{40} = 0.015 \ g/s$
3. Only collisions with energy greater than or equal to the activation energy are successful. Collisions with less energy than this do not lead to a reaction.
