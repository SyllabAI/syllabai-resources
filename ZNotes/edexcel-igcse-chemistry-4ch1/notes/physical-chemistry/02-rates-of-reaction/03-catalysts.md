## Catalysts

* **Catalysts** speed up the rate of a reaction without being used up in the process (they are chemically unchanged at the end).
* They provide an alternative route (pathway) for the reaction with a **lower activation energy**.
* With a lower activation energy, more colliding particles have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy, so the frequency of successful collisions increases.
* A catalyst does **not** change the enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) of the reaction, or the amount of product made.
* Enzymes are an example of biological catalysts in the human body.

### Showing That a Substance Is a Catalyst

* Weigh the substance before adding it to the reactants, and weigh it again after the reaction has taken place.
* If the mass of the substance has changed, it isn't a catalyst. If it increases the rate of the reaction (the reaction finishes faster) and there is no change in its mass, it is a catalyst.

**Experiment: effect of a catalyst**

* Carry out a reaction that produces a gas (e.g. zinc with dilute sulfuric acid) in a conical flask connected to a gas syringe, and record the volume of gas every $10 \ s$.
* Repeat with the same amounts of reactants, but add a small amount of the possible catalyst (e.g. a few drops of copper(II) sulfate solution).
* **Result**: With the catalyst, gas is produced faster (steeper curve), but the same total volume of gas is produced.

### Reaction Profile Diagrams

* **Reactants and products**: The horizontal lines at the start and end show the energy of the reactants and products.
* **Activation energy ($E_a$)**: The energy difference between the reactants and the peak (highest point) of the curve. This is the minimum energy the particles need to react.
* **Enthalpy change ($\Delta H$)**: The energy difference between the reactants and the products. If the products are lower than the reactants, $\Delta H$ is negative (exothermic).
* **With a catalyst**: The peak of the curve is lower, showing a lower activation energy. The reactant and product levels stay the same, so $\Delta H$ does not change.
