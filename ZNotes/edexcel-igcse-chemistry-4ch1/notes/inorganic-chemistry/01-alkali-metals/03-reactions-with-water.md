## Reactions With Water

$$
\text{alkali metal} + \text{water} \rightarrow \text{alkali metal hydroxide} + \text{hydrogen}
$$

$$
2Na(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2NaOH(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

### Reaction of Sodium With Water

* Sodium floats

* Melts into a ball

* Fizzing as hydrogen gas is produced

* Moves around on the surface of the water

* The piece of sodium gets smaller and disappears

* The solution formed is alkaline (it turns universal indicator purple), because sodium hydroxide contains $OH^-$ ions.

### Other Alkali Metals

* The reaction of water with **lithium** is less vigorous, so the lithium doesn’t melt, but the rest of the observations are similar.

  $2Li(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2LiOH(aq) + H_2(g)$

* The reaction of water with **potassium** is more vigorous. The hydrogen ignites with a lilac flame, and the potassium may spit around and explode.

  $2K(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2KOH(aq) + H_2(g)$

* The reactions of water with **rubidium** and **caesium** are extremely violent.

* All of these reactions are similar (they all produce hydrogen and an alkaline hydroxide solution), which is evidence that the alkali metals belong to the same family. The reactions get more vigorous going down the group, showing that reactivity increases down the group.
