*The alkali metals. Every alkali metal atom has 1 electron in its outer shell.*

### Why Reactivity Increases Down the Group

* All the alkali metals have **1 electron in their outer shell**. When they react, they lose this electron to form a $1+$ ion, e.g. $Na \rightarrow Na^+ + e^-$.

* Going down the group:

  * **Atomic radius increases**: The atoms have more electron shells, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus.

  * **Shielding increases**: There are more inner shells of electrons between the nucleus and the outer electron. These inner electrons shield (block) the outer electron from the positive charge of the nucleus.

  * **Attraction decreases**: Because the outer electron is further away and more shielded, the electrostatic attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron gets weaker.

* So the outer electron is **lost more easily** going down the group, which means the alkali metals become **more reactive** going down the group.

### Evidence That the Alkali Metals Are a Family

* All the alkali metals react with water in the **same way**: they float, fizz, produce **hydrogen gas** and form an **alkaline solution** of the metal hydroxide.

* They all react with oxygen to form oxides and form $1+$ ions in their compounds.

* These similar reactions happen because they all have **1 outer electron**, which is why they are grouped together as a family in Group 1.
