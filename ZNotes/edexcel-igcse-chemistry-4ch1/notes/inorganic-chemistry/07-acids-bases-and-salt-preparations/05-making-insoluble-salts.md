## Making Insoluble Salts

* Insoluble salts are made by **precipitation**: two solutions are mixed and the insoluble salt forms as a solid (precipitate).

**Reactants**:

* The *nitrate* of the metal part (all nitrates are soluble)

* The *sodium/potassium* salt of the non-metal part (all sodium and potassium salts are soluble)

### Making Lead(II) Sulfate (Precipitation Method)

$$
Pb(NO_3)_2(aq) + Na_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow PbSO_4(s) + 2NaNO_3(aq)
$$

* Measure $25 \ cm^3$ of lead(II) nitrate solution into a beaker.
* Add $25 \ cm^3$ of sodium sulfate solution to the beaker and stir.
* A white precipitate of lead(II) sulfate forms.
* Remove the precipitate by filtration.
* Wash the residue with distilled water to remove any soluble impurities.
* Dry the solid by blotting it with a paper towel or leaving it in a warm place.

* The ionic equation shows only the ions that form the precipitate:

$$
Pb^{2+}(aq) + SO_4^{2-}(aq) \rightarrow PbSO_4(s)
$$
