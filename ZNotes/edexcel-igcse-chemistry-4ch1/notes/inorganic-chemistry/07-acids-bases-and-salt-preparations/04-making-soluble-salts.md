## Making Soluble Salts

* The method used to make a soluble salt depends on whether the other reactant is soluble or insoluble.

* **SAP salts** are salts of **S**odium, **A**mmonium and **P**otassium. To make these, both reactants (the acid and the alkali or carbonate) are soluble, so the **titration method** is used.

* **Non-SAP salts** (e.g. copper(II) sulfate, zinc chloride, magnesium sulfate) are made by reacting an acid with an **insoluble** reactant (a metal, metal oxide, metal hydroxide or metal carbonate), using the **excess insoluble reactant method**.

### Preparing a Soluble Salt from an Insoluble Reactant

* An excess of the insoluble solid is added so that **all** of the acid reacts. The leftover solid does not dissolve, so it can be removed easily by filtration, leaving a pure solution of the salt.

**Making copper(II) sulfate crystals (non-SAP, crystallisation method)**

$$CuO(s) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow CuSO_4(aq) + H_2O(l)
$$

* Measure $50 \ cm^3$ of dilute sulfuric acid and warm it on a tripod and gauze using a Bunsen burner.
* Add copper(II) oxide (insoluble) a little at a time, stirring, and continue until the copper(II) oxide is in excess (some black solid remains undissolved). This shows that all the acid has reacted.
* Filter off the excess copper(II) oxide and transfer the solution (filtrate) to an evaporating basin.
* Heat the filtrate to boil off some of the water and concentrate the solution.
* Keep heating until a saturated solution is formed. This can be tested by dipping a glass rod into the solution and seeing if crystals form on the rod when it is removed.
* Stop heating and allow the solution to cool to room temperature.
* Remove the blue crystals by filtration.
* Dry the crystals by blotting them with a paper towel or leaving them in a warm place.

*Preparing zinc chloride from an insoluble reactant: excess zinc is added to hydrochloric acid, the excess zinc is filtered off, and the filtrate is evaporated to form crystals.*

$$Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow ZnCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

### Preparing a Soluble Salt by Titration

**Making sodium sulfate crystals (SAP, titration method)**

$$2NaOH(aq) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow Na_2SO_4(aq) + 2H_2O(l)
$$

* Measure $25 \ cm^3$ of sodium hydroxide solution into a conical flask using a pipette.
* Add methyl orange as the indicator.
* Add dilute sulfuric acid from the burette until the indicator changes from yellow to orange.
* Note the volume of acid added.
* Mix the same volumes of the acid and alkali, without the indicator, in a clean flask. (The indicator would contaminate the salt.)
* Heat the solution to evaporate off some of the water and concentrate the solution.
* Stop heating and allow the solution to cool to room temperature.
* Remove the crystals by filtration.
* Dry the crystals by blotting them with a paper towel or leaving them in a warm place.
