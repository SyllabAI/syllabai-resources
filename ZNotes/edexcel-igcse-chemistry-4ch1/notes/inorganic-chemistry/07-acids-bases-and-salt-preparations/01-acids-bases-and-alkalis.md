# Acids, Bases and Salt Preparations

## Acids, Bases and Alkalis

* An **acid** is a *proton donor* (it donates $H^+$ ions).

* A **base** is a *proton acceptor* (it accepts $H^+$ ions).

* **Metal oxides**, **metal hydroxides** and **ammonia** can all act as bases, e.g. $CuO$, $Mg(OH)_2$, $NH_3$.

* An **alkali** is a base that is **soluble in water**. Alkalis release $OH^-$ ions in solution, e.g. $NaOH$, $KOH$, $NH_3(aq)$.

* All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis. For example, copper(II) oxide is a base but it is insoluble in water, so it is not an alkali.

*General rules for predicting the solubility of ionic compounds in water.*

* **SNAP** is a mnemonic for the ions whose salts are always soluble: **S**odium ($Na^+$), **N**itrate ($NO_3^-$), **A**mmonium ($NH_4^+$), **P**otassium ($K^+$).

*Summary of insoluble salts: carbonates and hydroxides, except those of sodium, ammonium and potassium.*

**Worked example**

* Is barium sulfate soluble? No, as barium sulfate is one of the exceptions to the sulfate rule.
* Is copper(II) nitrate soluble? Yes, as all nitrates are soluble.
* Is calcium carbonate soluble? No, as only sodium, potassium and ammonium carbonates are soluble.
