## Reactions with Acids

### Naming Salts

* The salt formed depends on the acid used:

#|
|| **Acid** | **Formula** | **Salt formed** ||
|| Hydrochloric acid | $HCl$ | Chloride ||
|| Sulfuric acid | $H_2SO_4$ | Sulfate ||
|| Nitric acid | $HNO_3$ | Nitrate ||
|#

*The type of salt formed by each of the three common acids.*

### Acids and Metals

* Metals above hydrogen react to produce hydrogen gas (metal + acid $\rightarrow$ salt + hydrogen).
* Metals below hydrogen don’t react with the dilute acids.
* The higher the metal in the reactivity series, the more violent and vigorous the reaction.

$$
Mg(s) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow MgSO_4(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

* In this reaction, there is rapid fizzing as a gas is produced. Heat is produced, which makes the reaction warm. A colourless solution of magnesium sulfate is produced.

$$
Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow MgCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

* This reaction is similar to the previous reaction above, only here a solution of magnesium chloride is formed.

* Reactions between zinc and the acids are slower, as zinc is lower down the reactivity series than magnesium.

$$
Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow ZnCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

### Acids and Metal Carbonates

* Metal carbonates react with acids to produce a salt, water and carbon dioxide:

$$
\text{metal carbonate} + \text{acid} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water} + \text{carbon dioxide}
$$

* **Observations**: Fizzing (effervescence) as carbon dioxide gas is given off, and the solid carbonate disappears. The gas turns limewater milky.

$$
CaCO_3(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow CaCl_2(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)
$$

$$
CuCO_3(s) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow CuSO_4(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)
$$

$$
Na_2CO_3(aq) + 2HNO_3(aq) \rightarrow 2NaNO_3(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)
$$

* With copper(II) carbonate, the green solid disappears and a blue solution of copper(II) sulfate forms.
