# Extraction and Uses of Metals

## Extraction of Metals

* Most metals are found in the **Earth's crust** combined with other elements, usually as oxides, sulfides or carbonates. These compounds are found in rocks called ores.

* **Ore**: A rock containing enough of a metal compound (mineral) to make it worth extracting the metal.

* **Native**: An element that exists naturally in its uncombined state.

* **Very unreactive metals**, such as gold and silver, are found **native** (as the uncombined element) in the Earth's crust. This is because they are so unreactive that they do not easily react with oxygen, water or other substances in the environment to form compounds.

* **More reactive metals** have reacted with other elements over time, so they are only found combined in compounds in ores. These metals have to be extracted from their ores by chemical reactions.

* The method of extraction depends on the **position of the metal in the reactivity series**:

#|
|| **Position in reactivity series** | **Extraction method** | **Examples** ||
|| Above carbon | Electrolysis of the molten compound | Potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium ||
|| Below carbon | Heating the oxide with carbon (reduction) | Zinc, iron, copper ||
|| Very unreactive | Found native, so no chemical extraction needed | Silver, gold ||
|#

*How the position of a metal in the reactivity series determines its method of extraction.*

* **Roasting**: Converting sulfide ores into oxides by heating them in air, so that they can then be reduced with carbon.

  e.g. $2ZnS(s) + 3O_2(g) \rightarrow 2ZnO(s) + 2SO_2(g)$

### Evaluating a Metal Extraction Process

When given information about an extraction method, consider:

* **Energy cost**: Electrolysis needs a lot of electricity and high temperatures to melt the ore, so it is much more expensive than heating with carbon.

* **Cost of raw materials**: Carbon (coke) is cheap and readily available.

* **Purity of the metal**: Some methods produce a purer metal than others.

* **Environmental impact**: Heating with carbon produces carbon dioxide (a greenhouse gas), and roasting sulfide ores produces sulfur dioxide (which causes acid rain). Electricity may also come from burning fossil fuels. Mining ores damages habitats and produces waste.

* **Worked example**: Explain why aluminium is extracted by electrolysis and not by heating with carbon. Aluminium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot remove the oxygen from aluminium oxide. Electrolysis must be used, even though it is expensive, as it needs a large amount of electricity.
