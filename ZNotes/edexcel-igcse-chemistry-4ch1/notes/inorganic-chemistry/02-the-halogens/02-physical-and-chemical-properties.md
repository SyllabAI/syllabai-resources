## Physical and Chemical Properties

#|
|| **Element** | **Physical state at room temperature** | **Colour** ||
|| Fluorine, $F_2$ | Gas | Pale yellow ||
|| Chlorine, $Cl_2$ | Gas | Pale green ||
|| Bromine, $Br_2$ | Liquid | Red-brown liquid, orange-brown vapour ||
|| Iodine, $I_2$ | Solid | Grey-black solid, purple vapour ||
|| Astatine, $At_2$ | Solid | Very dark grey or black solid ||
|#

*Physical state and colour of the halogens at room temperature. The colour gets darker going down the group.*

* The melting and boiling points increase going down the group, as the relative molecular mass increases. This increases the strength of the intermolecular forces of attraction, so more thermal energy is needed to overcome them.

* Halogens are poor conductors of heat and electricity, as they are non-metals.

* Halogens are extremely corrosive and dangerous, so they have to be used in a fume cupboard. Fluorine and chlorine are extremely toxic and reactive, and liquid bromine is corrosive, so great care has to be taken to keep it off the skin.

### Predicting the Properties of Other Halogens

* We can use the trends in Group 7 to predict the properties of halogens that are not normally studied, such as **astatine** (below iodine).

* **Worked example**: Predict the state, colour and reactivity of astatine.

  * **State**: Melting and boiling points increase down the group, and iodine is already a solid, so astatine will be a **solid** at room temperature.

  * **Colour**: The colour gets darker down the group (pale green, red-brown, grey-black), so astatine will be **very dark grey or black**.

  * **Reactivity**: Reactivity decreases down the group, so astatine will be **less reactive than iodine**. It will **not** be able to displace iodine from potassium iodide solution, but iodine **would** displace astatine from a solution of potassium astatide.

  * **Compounds**: Like the other halogens, astatine will react with hydrogen to form hydrogen astatide ($HAt$), which is acidic when dissolved in water, and react with sodium to form sodium astatide ($NaAt$).

* **Worked example**: Predict the state and colour of fluorine from the trends. Fluorine is above chlorine, so it should have a lower boiling point (a **gas**) and a paler colour (**pale yellow**), which matches the observed properties.
