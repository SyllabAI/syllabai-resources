# The Halogens

## Group 7 Elements

* The halogens are the elements in Group 7 of the Periodic Table.

* They react with metals to produce a wide range of salts.

* All the halogens are non-metals and exist as diatomic molecules, e.g. $F_2$, $Cl_2$, $Br_2$, $I_2$.

*All halogen atoms have 7 electrons in their outer shell.*

### Why Reactivity Decreases Down the Group

* All halogen atoms have **7 electrons in their outer shell**. When they react, they **gain 1 electron** to form a $1-$ ion (a halide ion), e.g. $Cl_2 + 2e^- \rightarrow 2Cl^-$.

* Going down the group:

  * **Atomic radius increases**: The atoms have more electron shells, so the outer shell is further from the nucleus.

  * **Shielding increases**: There are more inner shells of electrons between the nucleus and the outer shell.

  * **Attraction decreases**: The extra electron being gained is less strongly attracted to the nucleus.

* So it becomes **harder to gain an electron** going down the group, which means the halogens become **less reactive** going down the group.

* For example, chlorine is more reactive than bromine because the electron gained by a chlorine atom goes into a shell that is closer to the nucleus and less shielded, so it is attracted more strongly.
