## Carbon Dioxide and Global Warming

### Formation of Carbon Dioxide by Thermal Decomposition

* **Thermal decomposition** is the breaking down of a substance by heating it.

* Many metal carbonates break down when heated to form a **metal oxide** and **carbon dioxide**.

$$\text{metal carbonate} \rightarrow \text{metal oxide} + \text{carbon dioxide}
$$

* **Example: copper(II) carbonate**

$$CuCO_3(s) \rightarrow CuO(s) + CO_2(g)
$$

* When copper(II) carbonate is heated, the **green** solid turns **black** (copper(II) oxide), and the gas given off turns **limewater milky**, showing that carbon dioxide is produced.

* Other examples: $CaCO_3(s) \rightarrow CaO(s) + CO_2(g)$ and $ZnCO_3(s) \rightarrow ZnO(s) + CO_2(g)$

### Carbon Dioxide as a Greenhouse Gas

* Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas that contributes to the enhanced greenhouse effect and global warming.

* Carbon dioxide is produced by burning vast quantities of fossil fuels, such as coal, oil and natural gas.

* **How the greenhouse effect works:**

  1. Radiation from the Sun (mainly visible light) passes through the atmosphere and is absorbed by the Earth's surface, warming it up.
  2. The warm Earth gives out (emits) energy as **infrared (IR) radiation**.
  3. Greenhouse gases, such as carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour, **absorb** some of this infrared radiation and then re-emit it in all directions, including back towards the Earth.
  4. This keeps the Earth's surface and lower atmosphere warmer than they would otherwise be.

* Greenhouse gases absorb **infrared** radiation given out by the Earth. They do not absorb the incoming visible light from the Sun, which is why sunlight can pass through the atmosphere.

* Burning fossil fuels increases the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, so more infrared radiation is absorbed. This is the **enhanced greenhouse effect**, which causes **global warming**.

* Global warming can cause climate change, leading to the melting of ice caps and glaciers. This causes a rise in sea levels, leading to flooding and other extreme weather conditions.
