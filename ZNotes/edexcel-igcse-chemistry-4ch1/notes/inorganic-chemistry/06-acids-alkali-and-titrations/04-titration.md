## Titration

### What is Titration?

* **Titration** is a technique used to find the exact volume of one solution that reacts completely with a known volume of another solution.
* An acid-alkali titration is used to find the volume of acid needed to exactly neutralise a given volume of alkali (or vice versa).
* The results can then be used to calculate the unknown concentration of one of the solutions.
* The solution of **known** concentration is usually placed in the **burette**, so the volume added can be measured accurately.
* The solution being analysed is measured accurately using a **pipette** and placed in the **conical flask**, together with a few drops of indicator.

### Method

*“Using HCl and NaOH solutions with phenolphthalein indicator”*

1. Measure $25 \ cm^3$ of hydrochloric acid using a pipette.
2. Transfer it to a conical flask.
3. Fill the burette with sodium hydroxide solution.
4. Take the initial reading on the burette and record it to $2$ d.p.
5. Add a few drops of phenolphthalein indicator to the acid in the conical flask.
6. Add sodium hydroxide from the burette, swirling the flask, until the indicator changes colour. Add the alkali drop by drop near the end point.
7. Take the final reading on the burette when the indicator turns a pale pink colour.
8. Find the volume of alkali added to the acid to neutralise it.

### Calculating the Titre and Concordant Results

* The **titre** is the volume of solution added from the burette:

$$\text{titre} = \text{final burette reading} - \text{initial burette reading}
$$

* The first titration is a rough (trial) titration to find the approximate end point.
* The titration is then **repeated** carefully until **concordant results** are obtained. These are titres within $0.10 \ cm^3$ of each other.
* The **mean titre** is calculated using only the concordant results (the rough titre is ignored).
