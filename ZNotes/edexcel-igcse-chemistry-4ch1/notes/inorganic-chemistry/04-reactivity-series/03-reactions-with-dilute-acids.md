## Reactions With Dilute Acids

$$
\text{metal} + \text{acid} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{hydrogen}
$$

* Metals such as **copper, gold and silver** (below hydrogen in the reactivity series) **don’t react** with dilute acids.

* **Potassium, sodium, lithium and calcium** react very violently with dilute acids, so it is unsafe to react them.

### Magnesium

* Reacts vigorously with cold dilute acids, producing heat and making the mixture hot.

$$
Mg(s) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow MgSO_4(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

### Aluminium

* Reacts slowly when cold, but when heated, the acid is able to break through the strong layer of aluminium oxide on the surface, allowing the aluminium to react properly with the dilute acid.

$$
2Al(s) + 6HCl(aq) \rightarrow 2AlCl_3(aq) + 3H_2(g)
$$

### Zinc and Iron

* Slow to react with cold acids, but react more rapidly when heated.

* These reactions are less vigorous than the reaction of aluminium, as aluminium is higher in the reactivity series than zinc and iron.

* **Zinc**:

$$
Zn(s) + H_2SO_4(aq) \rightarrow ZnSO_4(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

* **Iron**:

$$
Fe(s) + 2HCl(aq) \rightarrow FeCl_2(aq) + H_2(g)
$$
