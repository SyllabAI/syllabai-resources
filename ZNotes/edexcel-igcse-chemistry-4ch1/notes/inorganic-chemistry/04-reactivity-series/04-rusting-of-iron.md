## Rusting of Iron

* Iron rusts in the presence of **both oxygen and water**.

* Many metals corrode, but only iron (and steel, which is mostly iron) rusts.

* During rusting, iron is oxidised to iron(III) ions, $Fe^{3+}$.

* **Rust**: Hydrated iron(III) oxide, $Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$

$$
4Fe(s) + 3O_2(g) + 2xH_2O(l) \rightarrow 2Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O(s)
$$

*Investigating rusting: damp iron filings in a test tube of air. As the iron rusts, it uses up the oxygen in the air, so the water level rises up the tube.*

### Ways of Preventing Rusting

**Barrier method**: Coating the iron with a metal below it in the reactivity series

* Cheap ways of preventing rusting

* The iron will rust if any part of the coating is broken

* **Examples**: Painting, coating with oil or grease, covering with plastic

**Galvanised iron**: Iron that’s coated with a layer of zinc

* The iron doesn’t rust even if it’s exposed to oxygen, as the zinc reacts and forms a layer of zinc oxide, protecting the iron beneath it

* **Examples**: Cars, iron nails and tools

**Sacrificial protection**: Using a metal more reactive than iron, which reacts and protects the iron beneath it

* It has to be replaced occasionally, when all the metal has reacted and formed an oxide

* Used on large structures

* **Examples**: Ships and tankers, underground pipelines
