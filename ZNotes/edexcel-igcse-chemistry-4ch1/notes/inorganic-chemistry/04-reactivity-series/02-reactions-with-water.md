## Reactions With Water

* Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series react with water (liquid or steam), producing hydrogen.

$$
\text{metal} + \text{cold water} \rightarrow \text{metal hydroxide} + \text{hydrogen}
$$

$$
\text{metal} + \text{steam} \rightarrow \text{metal oxide} + \text{hydrogen}
$$

* Metals below hydrogen in the reactivity series don’t react with water or steam.

### Calcium

* Reacts gently with cold water, and the mixture heats up as heat is produced.

* Calcium hydroxide forms, which isn’t very soluble in water.

* Most of the $Ca(OH)_2$ is left as a white solid, although some dissolves and forms a colourless solution.

$$
Ca(s) + 2H_2O(l) \rightarrow Ca(OH)_2(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

### Magnesium

**Cold water:**

* Magnesium doesn’t react with cold water, as the magnesium gets coated with insoluble magnesium hydroxide, which prevents any more magnesium from reacting with the water.

**Steam:**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/6c01d43c0f3c4030ce4bb184.webp =603x)

*Magnesium reacting with steam: water in the mineral wool is heated to produce steam, which passes over the heated magnesium ribbon. The hydrogen produced is collected over water.*

* In this method, the mineral wool soaked in water is heated, and the steam produced moves towards the magnesium ribbon.

* Magnesium burns with a bright white flame.

* White magnesium oxide is formed.

$$
Mg(s) + H_2O(g) \rightarrow MgO(s) + H_2(g)
$$

### Zinc

* With **steam**, zinc oxide is formed, which is yellow when hot and white when cool.

$$
Zn(s) + H_2O(g) \rightarrow ZnO(s) + H_2(g)
$$

### Iron

* With **steam**, iron slowly forms a dark layer of iron(II,III) oxide ($Fe_3O_4$).

$$
3Fe(s) + 4H_2O(g) \rightarrow Fe_3O_4(s) + 4H_2(g)
$$
