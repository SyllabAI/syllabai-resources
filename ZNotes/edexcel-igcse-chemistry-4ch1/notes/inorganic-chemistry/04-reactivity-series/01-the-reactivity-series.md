* **Carbon** (even though it’s a non-metal) is included in the reactivity series as a marker. If a metal is less reactive than carbon, it can be easily and cheaply extracted by heating its oxide with carbon.

### Oxidation and Reduction

* **Reducing agent**: A substance that reduces something else and gets oxidised in the process.

* **Oxidising agent**: A substance that oxidises something else and gets reduced in the process.

* **Oxidation**: Loss of electrons, gain of oxygen.

* **Reduction**: Gain of electrons, loss of oxygen.

* **Redox**: When oxidation and reduction happen at the same time in a reaction.

### Displacement Reactions: Metals and Metal Oxides

* A more reactive metal will remove oxygen from the oxide of a less reactive metal when they are heated together.

* e.g. magnesium is more reactive than copper:

  $Mg(s) + CuO(s) \rightarrow MgO(s) + Cu(s)$

* e.g. the thermite reaction, where aluminium is more reactive than iron:

  $2Al(s) + Fe_2O_3(s) \rightarrow Al_2O_3(s) + 2Fe(l)$

* The more reactive metal is **oxidised** (gains oxygen) and acts as the reducing agent. The metal oxide is **reduced** (loses oxygen).

* If the metal is less reactive than the metal in the oxide, there is no reaction, e.g. $Cu + MgO \rightarrow$ no reaction.

### Displacement Reactions: Metals and Aqueous Solutions of Metal Salts

* A more reactive metal will displace a less reactive metal from a solution of its salt.

* e.g. zinc in copper(II) sulfate solution:

  $Zn(s) + CuSO_4(aq) \rightarrow ZnSO_4(aq) + Cu(s)$

  Ionic equation: $Zn(s) + Cu^{2+}(aq) \rightarrow Zn^{2+}(aq) + Cu(s)$

* **Observations**: A pink-brown coating of copper forms on the zinc, the blue colour of the solution fades, and the mixture gets warmer.

* e.g. copper in silver nitrate solution:

  $Cu(s) + 2AgNO_3(aq) \rightarrow Cu(NO_3)_2(aq) + 2Ag(s)$

  **Observations**: Silver crystals form on the copper, and the solution turns blue.

* If the metal is less reactive than the metal in the salt, there is no reaction, e.g. $Cu + ZnSO_4 \rightarrow$ no reaction.

* These are redox reactions: the more reactive metal loses electrons (oxidation), and the less reactive metal ion gains electrons (reduction).

### Practice Questions

1. Predict whether a reaction happens, and write a word equation if it does: (a) iron + copper(II) sulfate solution (b) silver + zinc nitrate solution (c) zinc + iron(II) oxide (heated)

2. In the reaction $Mg + CuO \rightarrow MgO + Cu$, which substance is oxidised and which is the reducing agent?

3. Four metals W, X, Y and Z are added to solutions of each other's salts. W displaces X and Y, but not Z. X displaces Y. Put the metals in order of reactivity, starting with the most reactive.

**Answers:**

1. (a) Reaction: iron + copper(II) sulfate $\rightarrow$ iron(II) sulfate + copper (b) No reaction, as silver is less reactive than zinc (c) Reaction: zinc + iron(II) oxide $\rightarrow$ zinc oxide + iron

2. Magnesium is oxidised (it gains oxygen), so magnesium is the reducing agent.

3. Z, W, X, Y
