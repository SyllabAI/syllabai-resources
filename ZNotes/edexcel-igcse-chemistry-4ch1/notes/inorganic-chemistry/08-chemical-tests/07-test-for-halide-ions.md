## Test for Halide Ions

* Make a solution of your suspected halide and add enough dilute nitric acid to make it acidic, then add silver nitrate solution.
* The nitric acid removes other ions (such as carbonate ions) that would also form a precipitate.
* The colour of the precipitate shows which halide ion is present.

#|
|| **Colour of precipitate formed** | **Name of compound formed** | **Ion present** ||
|| White | Silver chloride ($AgCl$) | $Cl^-$ ||
|| Cream | Silver bromide ($AgBr$) | $Br^-$ ||
|| Yellow | Silver iodide ($AgI$) | $I^-$ ||
|#

*Results of the silver nitrate test for halide ions.*

$$
Ag^+(aq) + Cl^-(aq) \rightarrow AgCl(s)
$$

### Practice Questions

1. A gas relights a glowing splint. Identify the gas.
2. A solid gives a lilac flame in a flame test. Name the metal ion present.
3. Sodium hydroxide solution is added to a solution of an unknown salt and a green precipitate forms. Which ion is present?
4. Dilute hydrochloric acid and barium chloride solution are added to a solution. A white precipitate forms. Name the precipitate and the ion it shows is present.
5. A solid fizzes when dilute hydrochloric acid is added, and the gas turns limewater milky. Which ion is present in the solid?
6. Describe how you would show that a solution contains ammonium ions.
7. A student adds dilute nitric acid and then silver nitrate solution to a solution and a cream precipitate forms. Identify the ion present and write the ionic equation.

**Answers**

1. Oxygen
2. Potassium ($K^+$)
3. Iron(II) ($Fe^{2+}$)
4. Barium sulfate; sulfate ion ($SO_4^{2-}$)
5. Carbonate ion ($CO_3^{2-}$)
6. Add sodium hydroxide solution and warm gently. Hold damp red litmus paper at the mouth of the test tube. It turns blue, showing ammonia gas is given off.
7. Bromide ion ($Br^-$): $Ag^+(aq) + Br^-(aq) \rightarrow AgBr(s)$
