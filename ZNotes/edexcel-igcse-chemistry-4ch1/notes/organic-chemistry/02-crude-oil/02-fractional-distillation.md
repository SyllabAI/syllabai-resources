## Fractional Distillation

* **Fractional distillation** is used to separate crude oil into fractions, based on their different boiling points.

### How It Works

1. Crude oil is heated until most of it vaporises and is passed into the bottom of the fractionating column.
2. The column is **hottest at the bottom** (about $350 \ ^\circ C$) and **coolest at the top** (about $25 \ ^\circ C$).
3. The vapours rise up the column and cool.
4. Each fraction condenses when it reaches a level in the column where the temperature is below its boiling point. The liquid is then drawn off at that level.
5. Larger hydrocarbons, with **higher** boiling points, condense **lower** down the column. Smaller hydrocarbons, with **lower** boiling points, travel further up before condensing.
6. Refinery gases do not condense, so they leave from the top of the column as gases.
7. Bitumen is removed as a residue from the bottom of the column, as the temperatures aren’t high enough for it to vaporise.
