## Structural Isomerism

* **Structural isomers**: Compounds that have the **same molecular formula** but a **different structural (displayed) formula**.
* Isomers can form when:
  * The carbon chain is **branched** in different ways (alkanes and alkenes).
  * The position of the $C=C$ bond changes (alkenes).

### Isomers of $C_4H_{10}$

* Butane, $C_4H_{10}$, has two structural isomers:
  * **Butane**: a straight chain, $CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_3$
  * **2-methylpropane**: a branched chain, $CH_3CH(CH_3)CH_3$
* To draw isomers, rearrange the carbon chain (e.g. move a carbon to a branch), keeping the same number of each type of atom.

### Isomers of $C_4H_8$

* But-1-ene, $CH_2=CHCH_2CH_3$
* But-2-ene, $CH_3CH=CHCH_3$ (the $C=C$ is in a different position)
* 2-methylpropene, $CH_2=C(CH_3)CH_3$ (branched chain)

### Other Points

* Methane, ethane, propane and ethene don’t have any structural isomers.
* Propene ($C_3H_6$) doesn’t have a structural isomer that is still an alkene.

**Practice question**

* Name the two structural isomers of $C_4H_{10}$ and state why they are isomers.

**Answer**

* Butane and 2-methylpropane. They have the same molecular formula ($C_4H_{10}$) but different structural formulae.
