## Substitution Reactions with Halogens

* Alkanes react with halogens (e.g. chlorine, bromine) in the presence of **ultraviolet (UV) light**.
* This is a **substitution reaction**: a hydrogen atom in the alkane is replaced by a halogen atom, and a hydrogen halide is also formed.
* The UV light provides the energy needed to start the reaction.

### Methane and Bromine

$$
CH_4(g) + Br_2(g) \xrightarrow{UV} CH_3Br(g) + HBr(g)
$$

* methane + bromine → bromomethane + hydrogen bromide
* A mixture of methane and bromine is orange-brown because of the bromine. When exposed to UV light (sunlight), the colour fades as the bromine reacts.

### Methane and Chlorine

$$
CH_4(g) + Cl_2(g) \xrightarrow{UV} CH_3Cl(g) + HCl(g)
$$

* methane + chlorine → chloromethane + hydrogen chloride

### Mono-substitution

* **Mono-substitution**: only **one** hydrogen atom in the alkane is replaced by a halogen atom.
* The organic products are called **halogenoalkanes**. They are named by the position of the halogen on the longest carbon chain, e.g. 1-bromopropane.
* With longer alkanes, more than one product is possible. For example, propane and bromine can form two isomers:
  * **1-bromopropane**, $CH_3CH_2CH_2Br$ (bromine on the end carbon)
  * **2-bromopropane**, $CH_3CHBrCH_3$ (bromine on the middle carbon)
* These products have the same molecular formula ($C_3H_7Br$) but different structural formulae, so they are isomers.
