# Synthetic Polymers

## Addition Polymerisation

* **Polymerisation**: Joining up lots of small molecules called ***monomers*** to make a very large molecule called a ***polymer***.
* **Addition polymerisation**: Monomers containing a $C=C$ double bond join together to form a polymer, with **no other product** formed.
* Alkenes (and substituted alkenes such as chloroethene) can act as monomers because of their $C=C$ bond.

### How It Works

* One of the two bonds in each $C=C$ double bond breaks, leaving a $C-C$ single bond.
* The electrons from the broken bond are used to join the carbon atom to a neighbouring monomer, forming a long chain.
* The polymer is **saturated** (it has no $C=C$ bonds).

$$nCH_2=CH_2 \rightarrow -[CH_2-CH_2]_n-
$$

* ethene (monomer) → poly(ethene) (polymer)
* $n$ is a very large number, and the section in brackets is the **repeating unit**.
