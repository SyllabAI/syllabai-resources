## Production of Ethanol

### Fermentation (Biological Method)

* Yeast is added to a solution of sugar (e.g. glucose) in **anaerobic conditions** (without air). Enzymes in the yeast convert the sugar into ethanol and carbon dioxide.

$$
C_6H_{12}O_6(aq) \xrightarrow{\text{yeast}} 2C_2H_5OH(aq) + 2CO_2(g)
$$

* **Conditions**:
  * ***Anaerobic conditions***: if air (oxygen) is present, the yeast respires aerobically and produces carbon dioxide and water instead of ethanol. Air could also allow microbes to oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid.
  * ***Temperature of about*** $30 \ ^\circ C$: if the temperature is too low, the reaction is too slow; if it is too high, the enzymes in the yeast are **denatured**.
* Fermentation stops when the ethanol concentration reaches about $15\%$, as the ethanol kills the yeast. So pure ethanol cannot be made directly by fermentation.
* The ethanol is then purified by **fractional distillation**.

### Hydration of Ethene (Chemical Method)

* Ethanol can also be made by reacting ethene (from cracking crude oil) with steam.

$$
C_2H_4(g) + H_2O(g) \rightleftharpoons C_2H_5OH(g)
$$

* **Conditions**:
  * Temperature: $300 \ ^\circ C$
  * Pressure: $60-70$ atmospheres
  * Catalyst: phosphoric acid
* This is an addition (hydration) reaction.

### Evaluating the Two Methods

#|
|| **Factor** | **Fermentation** | **Hydration of ethene** ||
|| Raw material | Sugar from crops (e.g. sugar cane): **renewable** | Ethene from crude oil: **finite** ||
|| Rate of reaction | Slow | Fast ||
|| Purity of product | Impure (needs fractional distillation) | Pure ethanol produced ||
|| Type of process | Batch process (one batch at a time) | Continuous process ||
|| Conditions and energy | Low temperature and normal pressure: less energy needed | High temperature and pressure: more energy needed ||
|| Equipment cost | Cheap | Expensive ||
|#

*Comparison of the two methods of producing ethanol.*

* Fermentation is often used in countries with lots of land to grow sugar cane but little crude oil. Hydration is used where crude oil is easily available and large amounts of pure ethanol are needed quickly.

**Practice question**

* Give two advantages of making ethanol by hydration of ethene rather than fermentation, and one disadvantage.

**Answer**

* Advantages: faster reaction; produces pure ethanol; continuous process. Disadvantage: uses ethene from crude oil, which is a finite resource (or: high energy costs from the high temperature and pressure).
