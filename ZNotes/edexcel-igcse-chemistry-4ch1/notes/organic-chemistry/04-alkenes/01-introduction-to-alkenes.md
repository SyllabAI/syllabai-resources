# Alkenes

## Introduction to Alkenes

* Alkenes are hydrocarbons that contain a carbon–carbon **double bond** ($C=C$).
* The $C=C$ bond is the **functional group** of alkenes.
* General formula: $C_nH_{2n}$
* Alkenes are **unsaturated** hydrocarbons, as they contain a $C=C$ double bond, so more atoms can be added across it.
* They have similar physical properties to alkanes: small alkenes (up to $C_4H_8$) are gases at room temperature.
* Alkenes are **more reactive** than alkanes because of the $C=C$ bond.

### Naming Alkenes

* The number in the name shows the position of the $C=C$ bond. The chain is numbered from the end that gives the $C=C$ the **lowest** number.
* **Example**: In $CH_2=CHCH_2CH_3$, the longest chain has $4$ carbons (but-) and the $C=C$ is between carbons $1$ and $2$, so the name is **but-1-ene**.

### Isomers of Alkenes

* Alkenes can have structural isomers due to:
  * A different **position of the $C=C$ bond**, e.g. but-1-ene ($CH_2=CHCH_2CH_3$) and but-2-ene ($CH_3CH=CHCH_3$)
  * A **branched chain**, e.g. 2-methylpropene, $CH_2=C(CH_3)CH_3$
* All three have the molecular formula $C_4H_8$.
