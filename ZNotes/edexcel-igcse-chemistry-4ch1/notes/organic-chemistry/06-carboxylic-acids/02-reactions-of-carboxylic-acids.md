## Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

### Weak Acids

* Carboxylic acids are **weak acids**: in aqueous solution they only **partially ionise** (only some molecules release $H^+$ ions).

$$
CH_3COOH(aq) \rightleftharpoons CH_3COO^-(aq) + H^+(aq)
$$

* Their solutions have a pH of about $3-5$, which is higher than a strong acid (e.g. hydrochloric acid) of the same concentration.
* They show the typical reactions of acids, but react more **slowly** than strong acids.
* The salts of ethanoic acid are called **ethanoates**, e.g. sodium ethanoate, $CH_3COONa$. The ethanoate ion is $CH_3COO^-$ (charge $-1$).

### Reaction with Metals

$$
\text{metal} + \text{carboxylic acid} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{hydrogen}
$$

$$
Mg(s) + 2CH_3COOH(aq) \rightarrow (CH_3COO)_2Mg(aq) + H_2(g)
$$

* magnesium + ethanoic acid → magnesium ethanoate + hydrogen
* **Observations**: fizzing (bubbles of hydrogen) and the metal dissolves. The reaction is slower than with dilute hydrochloric acid of the same concentration, e.g. zinc produces bubbles of hydrogen more slowly.
* Metals below hydrogen in the reactivity series (e.g. copper) don’t react.

### Reaction with Metal Carbonates

$$
\text{metal carbonate} + \text{carboxylic acid} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water} + \text{carbon dioxide}
$$

$$
Na_2CO_3(s) + 2CH_3COOH(aq) \rightarrow 2CH_3COONa(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)
$$

$$
CaCO_3(s) + 2CH_3COOH(aq) \rightarrow (CH_3COO)_2Ca(aq) + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)
$$

* **Observations**: fizzing, as carbon dioxide is given off (it turns limewater milky), and the solid carbonate disappears.

**Practice questions**

1. Name the salt formed when zinc reacts with ethanoic acid.
2. Write a balanced equation for the reaction between potassium carbonate and ethanoic acid.
3. Explain why ethanoic acid reacts more slowly with magnesium than hydrochloric acid of the same concentration.

**Answers**

1. Zinc ethanoate, $(CH_3COO)_2Zn$
2. $K_2CO_3 + 2CH_3COOH \rightarrow 2CH_3COOK + H_2O + CO_2$
3. Ethanoic acid is a weak acid and only partially ionises, so there is a lower concentration of $H^+$ ions, giving fewer successful collisions per second.
