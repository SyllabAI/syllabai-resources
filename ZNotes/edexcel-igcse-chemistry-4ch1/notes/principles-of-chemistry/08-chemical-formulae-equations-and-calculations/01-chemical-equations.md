# Chemical Formulae, Equations and Calculations

## Chemical Equations

### Word Equations

* Show reactants (left) and products (right) using full chemical names.

* Reactants $\rightarrow$ products. The arrow means "to form" / "produces".

* Conditions or catalysts are written above the arrow.

e.g. sodium hydroxide + hydrochloric acid $\rightarrow$ sodium chloride + water

### Symbol Equations

* Use formulae instead of names.

* Write these non-metals as **molecules**: $H_2$, $N_2$, $O_2$, $F_2$, $Cl_2$, $Br_2$, $I_2$

* **State symbols:** $(s)$ solid · $(l)$ liquid · $(g)$ gas · $(aq)$ aqueous (dissolved in water)

### Balancing Equations

* Required by the law of conservation of mass: atoms cannot be created or destroyed, so **total mass of reactants = total mass of products**.

* Balance by changing the **big numbers (coefficients)** in front of formulae. Never change the small subscript numbers, as that changes the substance itself ($H_2O$ and $H_2O_2$ are different substances).

* Work left to right, one element at a time. Balance elements that appear on their own last.

* If a group (e.g. $NO_3^{-}$, $SO_4^{2-}$) is unchanged on both sides, count it as one unit.

**Worked example:**

$Al + CuO \rightarrow Al_2O_3 + Cu$

* 2 $Al$ needed on the left: $2Al + CuO \rightarrow Al_2O_3 + Cu$

* 3 $O$ on the right: $2Al + 3CuO \rightarrow Al_2O_3 + Cu$

* 3 $Cu$ now on the left: $2Al + 3CuO \rightarrow Al_2O_3 + 3Cu$ ✓
