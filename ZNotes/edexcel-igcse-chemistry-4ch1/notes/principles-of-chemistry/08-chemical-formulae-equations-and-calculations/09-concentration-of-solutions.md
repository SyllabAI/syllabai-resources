## Concentration of Solutions

* **Solute** - The solid that dissolves.

* **Solvent** - The liquid it dissolves in.

* **Solution** - The mixture formed.

* **Concentration** = The amount of solute in a specific volume of solution.

### Key Formulae

$$
\text{concentration (mol/dm}^3) = \frac{\text{moles}}{\text{volume (dm}^3)}
$$

$$
\text{moles} = \text{concentration (mol/dm}^3) \times \text{volume (dm}^3)
$$

$$
\text{concentration (g/dm}^3) = \frac{\text{mass (g)}}{\text{volume (dm}^3)}
$$

$$
\text{concentration (g/dm}^3) = \text{concentration (mol/dm}^3) \times M_r
$$

### Unit Conversion

* $cm^3 \div 1000 \rightarrow dm^3$

* $dm^3 \times 1000 \rightarrow cm^3$

* $1\ dm^3 = 1000\ cm^3 = 1\ L$ and $1\ cm^3 = 1\ ml$

* Volume must be in $dm^3$ before using these formulae.

### Titration Calculations

* **Method**: Start by calculating the number of moles of the solution whose concentration you know. Then use the mole ratio from the balanced equation to find the number of moles of the unknown solution. Finally, divide that by the volume of the unknown solution (in $dm^3$) to give its concentration.

**Worked example:**

25.0 $cm^3$ of $Na_2CO_3$ with a concentration of 0.050 mol/$dm^3$ is neutralised by 20.0 $cm^3$ of $HCl$. What is the concentration of the $HCl$?

$$
Na_2CO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow 2NaCl + H_2O + CO_2
$$

* Moles of $Na_2CO_3 = 0.050 \times \frac{25.0}{1000} = 0.00125$ mol

* Ratio $Na_2CO_3 : HCl = 1 : 2$, so moles of $HCl = 2 \times 0.00125 = 0.00250$ mol

* Volume of $HCl = \frac{20.0}{1000} = 0.0200\ dm^3$

* Concentration of $HCl = \frac{0.00250}{0.0200} = 0.125$ mol/$dm^3$
