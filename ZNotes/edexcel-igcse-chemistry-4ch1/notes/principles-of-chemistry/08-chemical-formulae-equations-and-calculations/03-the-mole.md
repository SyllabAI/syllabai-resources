## The Mole

* **Law of constant proportion**: Compounds contain elements in a fixed ratio by mass.

* **1 mole** = The amount of substance containing as many particles as there are in 12 grams of carbon-12 = $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ particles (Avogadro's constant).

* **Molar mass** = Mass of 1 mole = the $A_r$ or $M_r$ in grams (units: g/mol).

e.g.

* 1 mol of $He$ = 4 g

* 1 mol of $H_2O$ = 18 g

* 1 mol of $C$ = 12 g

### Key Formulae

$$
\text{moles} = \frac{\text{mass}}{M_r}
$$

$$
\text{mass} = \text{moles} \times M_r
$$

$$
\text{moles} = \frac{\text{number of particles}}{6.02 \times 10^{23}}
$$

**Worked example:**

How many moles are in 2.64 g of sucrose ($M_r = 342.3$)?

$$
\text{moles} = \frac{2.64}{342.3} = 7.71 \times 10^{-3} \text{ mol}
$$
