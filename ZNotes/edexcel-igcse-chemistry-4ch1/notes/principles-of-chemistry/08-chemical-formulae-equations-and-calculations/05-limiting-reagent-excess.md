## Limiting Reagent & Excess

* The **limiting reagent** is used up first and determines how much product forms.

* Any reactant left over is **in excess**.

* **Method**: Convert both reactants to moles, compare with the mole ratio, and whichever runs out first is limiting.

**Worked example:**

3.2 g of $Cu$ reacts with 0.4 mol of $HNO_3$.

$$
Cu + 4HNO_3 \rightarrow Cu(NO_3)_2 + 2H_2O + 2NO_2
$$

* Moles of $Cu = \frac{3.2}{63.5} = 0.05$ mol

* Ratio $Cu : HNO_3 = 1 : 4$, so 0.05 mol of $Cu$ needs $0.05 \times 4 = 0.2$ mol of $HNO_3$

* 0.4 mol available > 0.2 mol needed, so $HNO_3$ is in excess and $Cu$ is the limiting reagent
