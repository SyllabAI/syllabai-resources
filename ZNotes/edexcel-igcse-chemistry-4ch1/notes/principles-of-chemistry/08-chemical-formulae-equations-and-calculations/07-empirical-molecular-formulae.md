## Empirical & Molecular Formulae

* **Empirical formula** - The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element.

* **Molecular formula** - The actual number and type of each atom in one molecule.

* The formula of an ionic compound is always empirical.

* e.g. ethanoic acid: molecular formula $C_2H_4O_2$, empirical formula $CH_2O$

### Calculating Empirical Formula

#|
|| **Step** | **What to do** ||
|| 1 | Write each element ||
|| 2 | Write its mass (g) or percentage ||
|| 3 | Write the $A_r$ of each element ||
|| 4 | $\text{moles} = \text{mass} \div A_r$ ||
|| 5 | Divide all by the smallest number of moles ||
|| 6 | Multiply by 2 or 3 if you get decimal/fractional values, then write the formula ||
|#

*Steps for calculating an empirical formula.*

**Worked example:** Find the empirical formula of a compound containing 10 g of hydrogen and 80 g of oxygen.

#|
|| **Step** | **$H$** | **$O$** ||
|| Mass (g) | $10$ | $80$ ||
|| $A_r$ | $1$ | $16$ ||
|| Moles | $\frac{10}{1} = 10$ | $\frac{80}{16} = 5$ ||
|| Divide by smallest | $\frac{10}{5} = 2$ | $\frac{5}{5} = 1$ ||
|#

*Empirical formula calculation: the ratio $H : O = 2 : 1$.*

Empirical formula = $H_2O$

### Calculating Molecular Formula

$$
\text{multiplier} = \frac{M_r \text{ of molecular formula}}{M_r \text{ of empirical formula}}
$$

* Multiply every subscript in the empirical formula by the multiplier.

**Worked example:** The empirical formula is $C_4H_{10}S$ and the $M_r$ of the molecular formula is 180. What is the molecular formula?

* $M_r$ of empirical formula $= (12 \times 4) + (1 \times 10) + 32 = 90$

* Multiplier $= \frac{180}{90} = 2$

* Molecular formula $= C_8H_{20}S_2$

### Finding the Formula of a Metal Oxide by Experiment

**Method 1: Combustion (e.g. magnesium oxide)**

1. Weigh an empty crucible with its lid. Add a coil of magnesium ribbon and weigh again.

2. Heat the crucible strongly. Lift the lid slightly from time to time with tongs, to let oxygen in without letting magnesium oxide smoke escape.

3. When the magnesium stops glowing, remove the lid and heat strongly for a few more minutes.

4. Let it cool, then weigh the crucible, lid and contents.

5. Repeat the heating, cooling and weighing until the mass stays the same (constant mass), so all the magnesium has reacted.

6. Mass of magnesium = (crucible + lid + magnesium) − (crucible + lid). Mass of oxygen = (crucible + lid + magnesium oxide) − (crucible + lid + magnesium).

$$
2Mg(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2MgO(s)
$$

**Worked example:** 0.24 g of magnesium forms 0.40 g of magnesium oxide.

#|
|| **Step** | **$Mg$** | **$O$** ||
|| Mass (g) | $0.24$ | $0.40 - 0.24 = 0.16$ ||
|| $A_r$ | $24$ | $16$ ||
|| Moles | $\frac{0.24}{24} = 0.01$ | $\frac{0.16}{16} = 0.01$ ||
|| Divide by smallest | $1$ | $1$ ||
|#

*The ratio $Mg : O = 1 : 1$, so the formula is $MgO$.*

**Method 2: Reduction (e.g. copper(II) oxide)**

1. Weigh an empty reduction tube. Add copper(II) oxide and weigh again.

2. Pass a stream of hydrogen (or methane) gas through the tube to push out the air, then light the excess gas at the end of the tube.

3. Heat the copper(II) oxide strongly. The hydrogen removes the oxygen and the black solid turns pink-brown (copper).

4. Stop heating but keep the gas flowing while the tube cools, so the hot copper does not react with oxygen in the air again.

5. Weigh the tube and copper. Repeat heating, cooling and weighing until the mass is constant.

6. Mass of oxygen = mass of copper(II) oxide − mass of copper.

$$
CuO(s) + H_2(g) \rightarrow Cu(s) + H_2O(l)
$$

**Worked example:** 1.59 g of copper oxide is reduced to 1.27 g of copper.

#|
|| **Step** | **$Cu$** | **$O$** ||
|| Mass (g) | $1.27$ | $1.59 - 1.27 = 0.32$ ||
|| $A_r$ | $63.5$ | $16$ ||
|| Moles | $\frac{1.27}{63.5} = 0.02$ | $\frac{0.32}{16} = 0.02$ ||
|| Divide by smallest | $1$ | $1$ ||
|#

*The ratio $Cu : O = 1 : 1$, so the formula is $CuO$.*

* **Safety**: Hydrogen is flammable, so all the air must be removed from the tube before heating to prevent an explosion.
