## Water of Crystallisation

* A **hydrated salt** is a crystallised salt containing water molecules in its structure, e.g. $CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$. The dot shows the water of crystallisation.

* **Anhydrous** = The salt with all water removed.

* Found by heating to a constant mass and re-weighing.

* **Method**: Same as empirical formula, but the two "elements" are the salt and water, and you use $M_r$ instead of $A_r$. The ratio comes out as 1 salt : $x$ water.

**Worked example:**

11.25 g of hydrated copper(II) sulfate, $CuSO_4 \cdot xH_2O$, is heated until all the water of crystallisation is driven off, leaving 7.19 g of anhydrous salt. Find $x$.

* Mass of water lost $= 11.25 - 7.19 = 4.06$ g

#|
|| **Step** | **$CuSO_4$** | **$H_2O$** ||
|| Mass (g) | $7.19$ | $4.06$ ||
|| $M_r$ | $159.5$ | $18$ ||
|| Moles | $\frac{7.19}{159.5} = 0.045$ | $\frac{4.06}{18} = 0.226$ ||
|| Divide by smallest | $\frac{0.045}{0.045} = 1$ | $\frac{0.226}{0.045} = 5$ ||
|#

*Calculating the ratio of anhydrous salt to water.*

* Formula $= CuSO_4 \cdot 5H_2O$, so $x = 5$
