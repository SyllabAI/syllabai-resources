## Solutes, Solvents and Solutions

* **Solute**: The substance that dissolves in the liquid
* **Solvent**: The liquid the solid dissolves in
* **Solution**: The liquid formed
* **Saturated solution**: A solution that contains the **maximum concentration of solute dissolved** into the solvent at a particular temperature
* **Solubility**: The mass of solid that must dissolve in 100g of solution at that temperature to form a saturated solution

### Solubility for Solids and Gases

* **Solid**: Temperature increases --\> more soluble.

* **Gases**: Temperature increases --\> less soluble.

* **Gases**: Pressure increases --\> more soluble.

### Calculating Solubility

$$
\text{Solubility (g per 100 g of water)} = \frac{\text{mass of solute}}{\text{mass of solvent}} \times 100
$$

**Worked example 1**

25 g of potassium nitrate dissolves in 50 g of water at 40 °C to form a saturated solution. Calculate the solubility of potassium nitrate at 40 °C.

$$
\text{Solubility} = \frac{25}{50} \times 100 = 50 \text{ g per 100 g of water}
$$

**Worked example 2**

The solubility of sodium chloride at 20 °C is 36 g per 100 g of water. What is the maximum mass of sodium chloride that dissolves in 250 g of water at 20 °C?

$$
\text{Mass} = \frac{36}{100} \times 250 = 90 \text{ g}
$$
