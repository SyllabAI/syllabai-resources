## Electrolysis of Aqueous Solutions

![](https://znotes-static.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/edexcel/igcse/chemistry-4ch1/667050c327b5386e6cf0c429067cacb6cdb0f1bce31247ce.png =650x)

*Flowchart for predicting the products of electrolysis of aqueous solutions, based on the reactivity of the metal and the type of negative ion.*

* Aqueous solutions always contain water. A small number of water molecules split up into hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions ($H^+$ and $OH^-$), and these ions can also take part in electrolysis.

* **At the cathode (negative electrode)**: Either the metal ions or the $H^+$ ions are discharged. Only one of them gains electrons (**reduction**).

* **At the anode (positive electrode)**: Either the non-metal ions or the $OH^-$ ions are discharged. Only one of them loses electrons (**oxidation**).

### Sodium Chloride Solution

* Aqueous sodium chloride contains $Na^+$ and $H^+$ ions, which are both attracted to the cathode.

* Hydrogen is less reactive than sodium, so it is easier to add electrons to hydrogen ions. Hydrogen gas is produced at the cathode.

* At the anode, $Cl^-$ and $OH^-$ ions are present. As there are many more chloride ions, these are discharged and chlorine gas is produced.

#|
|| **Electrode** | **Half-equation** | **Type of reaction** ||
|| Cathode ($-$) | $2H^+(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2(g)$ | **Reduction** ||
|| Anode ($+$) | $2Cl^-(aq) \rightarrow Cl_2(g) + 2e^-$ | **Oxidation** ||
|#

*Half-equations for the electrolysis of sodium chloride solution.*

* The remaining solution contains $Na^+$ and $OH^-$ ions, so sodium hydroxide ($NaOH$) is left. The hydroxide ions make the solution alkaline.

* Less chlorine is collected than expected, as some chlorine dissolves in the water.

### Dilute Sulfuric Acid

* Dilute sulfuric acid contains $H^+$, $SO_4^{2-}$ and $OH^-$ ions.

* Hydrogen is produced at the cathode. Sulfate ions are not discharged, so $OH^-$ ions are discharged at the anode and oxygen is produced.

#|
|| **Electrode** | **Half-equation** | **Type of reaction** ||
|| Cathode ($-$) | $2H^+(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2(g)$ | **Reduction** ||
|| Anode ($+$) | $4OH^-(aq) \rightarrow 2H_2O(l) + O_2(g) + 4e^-$ | **Oxidation** ||
|#

*Half-equations for the electrolysis of dilute sulfuric acid.*

* In the first equation, 2 electrons produce 1 mol of hydrogen, so 4 electrons produce 2 mol of hydrogen.

* In the second equation, 4 electrons produce 1 mol of oxygen.

* This means that **twice as much hydrogen** is produced as oxygen (by volume).

### Copper(II) Sulfate Solution (Inert Electrodes)

* Copper(II) sulfate solution contains $Cu^{2+}$, $H^+$, $SO_4^{2-}$ and $OH^-$ ions.

* Copper is less reactive than hydrogen, so copper ions are discharged at the cathode and copper metal is deposited.

* Sulfate ions are not discharged, so $OH^-$ ions are discharged at the anode and oxygen is produced.

#|
|| **Electrode** | **Half-equation** | **Type of reaction** ||
|| Cathode ($-$) | $Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu(s)$ | **Reduction** ||
|| Anode ($+$) | $4OH^-(aq) \rightarrow 2H_2O(l) + O_2(g) + 4e^-$ | **Oxidation** ||
|#

*Half-equations for the electrolysis of copper(II) sulfate solution with inert electrodes.*

* **Observations**: A pink-brown layer of copper forms on the cathode, and bubbles of oxygen are given off at the anode. The blue colour of the solution fades, as copper ions are removed.

* The solution left behind contains $H^+$ and $SO_4^{2-}$ ions, so it becomes sulfuric acid.
