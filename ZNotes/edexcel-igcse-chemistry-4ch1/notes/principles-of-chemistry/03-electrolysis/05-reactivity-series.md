## Reactivity Series

* The products of electrolysis of aqueous solutions depend on the **reactivity** of the elements present.

### Products at the Cathode

* If the metal is **more reactive than hydrogen** (above hydrogen in the reactivity series), hydrogen is produced at the cathode. e.g. potassium, sodium or calcium compounds produce hydrogen.

* If the metal is **less reactive than hydrogen** (below hydrogen in the reactivity series), the metal is produced at the cathode. e.g. copper, silver or gold compounds produce the metal.

* **Why**: The ions of a less reactive element gain electrons more easily. Hydrogen ions gain electrons more easily than the ions of very reactive metals, so hydrogen is discharged instead. But ions of metals less reactive than hydrogen gain electrons more easily than $H^+$, so the metal is discharged.

### Products at the Anode

* If the solution contains **halide ions** ($Cl^-$, $Br^-$, $I^-$), the halogen is produced at the anode (chlorine, bromine or iodine).

* If there are **no halide ions** (e.g. the solution contains sulfate or nitrate ions), oxygen is produced at the anode from the $OH^-$ ions.

### Worked Examples

#|
|| **Solution** | **Cathode product** | **Reason** | **Anode product** | **Reason** ||
|| Potassium nitrate, $KNO_3(aq)$ | Hydrogen | $K$ is more reactive than $H$ | Oxygen | $NO_3^-$ is not a halide ||
|| Copper(II) chloride, $CuCl_2(aq)$ | Copper | $Cu$ is less reactive than $H$ | Chlorine | $Cl^-$ is a halide ||
|| Sodium bromide, $NaBr(aq)$ | Hydrogen | $Na$ is more reactive than $H$ | Bromine | $Br^-$ is a halide ||
|| Silver nitrate, $AgNO_3(aq)$ | Silver | $Ag$ is less reactive than $H$ | Oxygen | $NO_3^-$ is not a halide ||
