# Electrolysis

## Introduction to Electrolysis

* For a compound to undergo electrolysis, it must be able to conduct electricity. This happens when it contains charged particles (ions) that are free to move.

### Ionic Compounds

* These don’t conduct electricity when solid, but do conduct electricity in the molten or aqueous state (when they are dissolved in water), as the ions are free to move.

* Ionic compounds consist of cations, which are positive, and anions, which are negative.

*Formation of an ionic compound: sodium transfers its outer electron to chlorine, forming $Na^{+}$ and $Cl^{-}$ ions.*

### Covalent Compounds

* These **don’t conduct electricity** in any state, as they consist of molecules that have no overall electrical charge, so there are no charged particles to move.

* They also have **covalent bonds which are very strong**, so the electrons are held firmly in place in the atoms.

![Image from GeeksforGeeks](https://znotes-static.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/edexcel/igcse/chemistry-4ch1/667050c327b5386e6cf0c429a1460246c41b3ca081d2133b.png =767x)

*Formation of a covalent bond: two non-metal atoms share a pair of electrons to form a covalent molecule.*

### Key Terms

* **Electrolysis**: A chemical change caused by passing an electric current through a compound that is molten or in solution.

* **Electrolyte**: A liquid or solution that undergoes electrolysis. It contains ions that are free to move.

* **Electrodes**: Electrical conductors that carry the current into the electrolyte. They are usually made of carbon (graphite) or platinum, which are inert, so they don’t react.

* **Anode**: The positive electrode.

* **Cathode**: The negative electrode.

### The Electrolytic Cell

![Electrolysis Cell Diagram](https://images.znotes.org/ib/myp/chemistry/image024.png =600x)

*An electrolytic cell: two inert electrodes dip into the electrolyte and are connected to a d.c. power supply. Positive ions move to the cathode and negative ions move to the anode.*

* Electrons flow around the external circuit from the anode (positive electrode) to the cathode (negative electrode).

* Positive ions (cations) move towards the cathode, while negative ions (anions) move towards the anode.

### Mnemonics

* **PANIC**: Positive is Anode, Negative Is Cathode

* **ANOX**: ANode = OXidation

* **REDCAT**: REDuction at the CAThode
