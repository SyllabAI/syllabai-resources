## Electrolysis of Ionic Compounds

![](https://znotes-static.s3.ap-southeast-1.amazonaws.com/edexcel/igcse/chemistry-4ch1/667050c327b5386e6cf0c429288d1ff0a19f750d432f84c8.png =500x)

*Electrolysis of a molten ionic compound: the metal is produced at the cathode and the non-metal is produced at the anode.*

* Molten ionic compounds undergo electrolysis and always produce their elements.

* **Discharge** of ions means that the ions lose their charge at the electrodes and become atoms or molecules.

* Half-equations (ionic half-equations) are used to show what happens at each electrode.

### Electrolysis of Molten Lead(II) Bromide

* Lead(II) bromide ($PbBr_2$) is an ionic compound made of lead(II) ions ($Pb^{2+}$) and bromide ions ($Br^-$) packed together in a lattice. When it is heated, it melts and the ions become free to move.

* The lead(II) ions are attracted to the cathode. Each one gains two electrons and becomes a lead atom. Molten lead collects at the bottom of the crucible.

* The bromide ions are attracted to the anode. Each one loses an electron, forming a bromine atom. Two bromine atoms join to form a bromine molecule (bromine is diatomic).

#|
|| **Electrode** | **Half-equation** | **Type of reaction** ||
|| Cathode ($-$) | $Pb^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Pb$ | **Reduction** (gain of electrons) ||
|| Anode ($+$) | $2Br^- \rightarrow Br_2 + 2e^-$ | **Oxidation** (loss of electrons) ||
|#

*Half-equations for the electrolysis of molten lead(II) bromide.*

* Overall equation: $PbBr_2(l) \rightarrow Pb(l) + Br_2(g)$

### Experiment: Electrolysis of Molten Lead(II) Bromide

**Apparatus**: Crucible, two graphite (carbon) electrodes, d.c. power supply, bulb or ammeter, connecting wires, pipeclay triangle, tripod and Bunsen burner.

**Method**:

1. Put solid lead(II) bromide into a crucible and place two graphite electrodes into it, making sure the electrodes do not touch.

2. Connect the electrodes to a d.c. power supply with a bulb (or ammeter) in the circuit.

3. Switch on. The bulb does **not** light, because the ions in solid lead(II) bromide cannot move.

4. Heat the crucible strongly with a Bunsen burner until the lead(II) bromide melts. The bulb now **lights**, because the ions are free to move and carry the charge.

5. Continue the electrolysis for a few minutes, then switch off and allow the crucible to cool.

**Observations**:

* At the anode, **brown fumes** of bromine gas are given off.

* At the cathode, **silvery molten lead** forms and collects at the bottom of the crucible.

**Safety precautions**:

* Carry out the experiment in a **fume cupboard**, as bromine vapour is toxic and corrosive.

* Lead and lead compounds are toxic, so avoid contact with skin and wash hands afterwards.

* Wear eye protection, and use tongs because the crucible is very hot.
