# Ionic Bonding

## Formation of Ions

* Ionic bonding is the **electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions (positive and negative ions).**

* Elements in Groups 1, 2 and 3 lose electrons to form $1+$, $2+$ and $3+$ ions. Elements in Groups 5, 6 and 7 gain electrons to form $3-$, $2-$ and $1-$ ions. In both cases, the ions have a full outer shell, so they are stable.

* Ionic compounds usually contain a metal.

* Positive ions are called **cations**.

* Negative ions are called **anions**.

* Metal atoms lose electrons to form positive ions (cations).

* Non-metal atoms gain electrons to form negative ions (anions).

### Dot-and-Cross Diagrams: Electron Transfer

* In a dot-and-cross diagram, the electrons of one atom are drawn as dots and the electrons of the other atom as crosses.

* The ions formed are drawn in square brackets, with the charge written outside the bracket at the top right.

*Dot-and-cross diagram for sodium chloride: the sodium atom transfers its one outer electron to the chlorine atom, forming $Na^{+}$ and $Cl^{-}$ ions.*

**Examples of electron transfer between Groups 1, 2, 3 and Groups 5, 6, 7:**

#|
|| **Groups** | **Compound** | **Electron transfer** | **Ions formed** ||
|| 1 and 7 | Sodium chloride, $NaCl$ | $Na$ (2.8.1) transfers 1 electron to $Cl$ (2.8.7) | $Na^{+}$ (2.8) and $Cl^{-}$ (2.8.8) ||
|| 1 and 6 | Sodium oxide, $Na_2O$ | Two $Na$ atoms each transfer 1 electron to one $O$ atom (2.6) | $2Na^{+}$ (2.8) and $O^{2-}$ (2.8) ||
|| 2 and 7 | Magnesium chloride, $MgCl_2$ | $Mg$ (2.8.2) transfers 1 electron to each of two $Cl$ atoms | $Mg^{2+}$ (2.8) and $2Cl^{-}$ (2.8.8) ||
|| 2 and 6 | Magnesium oxide, $MgO$ | $Mg$ (2.8.2) transfers 2 electrons to $O$ (2.6) | $Mg^{2+}$ (2.8) and $O^{2-}$ (2.8) ||
|| 3 and 6 | Aluminium oxide, $Al_2O_3$ | Two $Al$ atoms (2.8.3) each transfer 3 electrons, and these 6 electrons are gained by three $O$ atoms (2 each) | $2Al^{3+}$ (2.8) and $3O^{2-}$ (2.8) ||
|| 3 and 5 | Aluminium nitride, $AlN$ | $Al$ (2.8.3) transfers 3 electrons to $N$ (2.5) | $Al^{3+}$ (2.8) and $N^{3-}$ (2.8) ||
|#

*How electrons are transferred when ionic compounds form. The number of electrons lost by the metal always equals the number gained by the non-metal.*
