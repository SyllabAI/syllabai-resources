## Giant Ionic Structures

* A **lattice** is a regular arrangement of particles. Each lattice is held together by the electrostatic force of attraction between the oppositely charged ions. The positive and negative charges cancel out, so the lattice is neutral overall.

### Comparing the Strength of Ionic Lattices

* The strength of the electrostatic attraction depends on the **charges on the ions** and the **size of the ions**.

* **Higher charges** give stronger attraction. In magnesium oxide, the ions are $Mg^{2+}$ and $O^{2-}$, whereas in sodium chloride they are $Na^{+}$ and $Cl^{-}$. The attraction between $2+$ and $2-$ ions is much stronger than between $1+$ and $1-$ ions.

* **Smaller ions** can get closer together, so the attraction between them is stronger.

* Stronger attraction means more energy is needed to break the lattice, so the melting point is higher. For example, magnesium oxide melts at about 2850 °C, while sodium chloride melts at about 801 °C.

### Electrical Conductivity of Ionic Compounds

* To conduct electricity, a substance must contain **charged particles that are free to move**.

* **Solid**: Ionic compounds **do not conduct**. The ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice by strong electrostatic forces, so they cannot move.

* **Molten (melted)**: Ionic compounds **conduct**. The lattice breaks down and the ions are free to move and carry the charge.

* **Aqueous (dissolved in water)**: Ionic compounds **conduct**. The ions separate and are free to move through the solution.
