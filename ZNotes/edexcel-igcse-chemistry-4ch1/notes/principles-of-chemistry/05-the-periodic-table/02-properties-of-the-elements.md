*Comparison of the properties of metals and non-metals.*

### Identifying Metals and Non-metals from the Periodic Table

* A zig-zag (staircase) line runs from boron ($B$) down to astatine ($At$) in the Periodic Table.

* Elements to the **left** of the staircase line are **metals**. Most elements are metals.

* Elements to the **right** of the staircase line are **non-metals**.

* Elements next to the line, such as silicon ($Si$), show properties of both and are called metalloids.

* **Worked example 1**: An element has the electronic configuration 2.8.1. It has 3 shells, so it is in Period 3, and 1 outer electron, so it is in Group 1. Group 1 is on the left of the staircase line, so the element (sodium) is a **metal**.

* **Worked example 2**: An element has the electronic configuration 2.8.7. It is in Period 3 and Group 7, which is on the right of the staircase line, so the element (chlorine) is a **non-metal**.

### Using Oxides to Classify Elements

* **Metal oxides are basic.** They react with acids to form a salt and water. Metal oxides that dissolve in water form alkaline solutions (pH above 7).

  e.g. $Na_2O(s) + H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2NaOH(aq)$

* **Non-metal oxides are acidic.** They react with bases, and those that dissolve in water form acidic solutions (pH below 7).

  e.g. $CO_2(g) + H_2O(l) \rightarrow H_2CO_3(aq)$

  e.g. $SO_2(g) + H_2O(l) \rightarrow H_2SO_3(aq)$

* So, if an element burns in oxygen to form an oxide that gives an **alkaline** solution (or reacts with acids), the element is a **metal**. If the oxide gives an **acidic** solution, the element is a **non-metal**.

### Linking Properties to the Periodic Table

* Metals are on the left of the Periodic Table because they have **1–3 outer electrons**. They lose these electrons in reactions to form **positive ions**, and their delocalised electrons allow them to conduct electricity.

* Non-metals are on the right because they have **4–7 outer electrons**. They gain electrons to form **negative ions**, or share electrons to form covalent bonds, so they have no delocalised electrons and do not conduct electricity (except graphite).

* Moving **across a period** from left to right, elements change from metals to non-metals. Electrical conductivity decreases and the oxides change from basic to acidic. This repeating pattern in every period is an example of **periodicity**.
