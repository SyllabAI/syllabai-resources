# Metallic Bonding

## Structure of Metals

### What Is Metallic Bonding?

* Metallic bonding is the **electrostatic force of attraction between the lattice of positive metal ions and the sea of delocalised electrons**.

* When metal atoms lose their outer (valence) electrons, they become positively charged ions.

* These electrons are no longer attached to any one atom. They are **delocalised**, which means they are free to move throughout the whole structure.

* The positive ions are arranged in a regular pattern (a giant lattice), and the delocalised electrons move freely between them.

* Metallic bonding is weaker than ionic bonding.

### Labelling the Metallic Structure

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/9210fc5ec0cba112c34ab6e7.webp =463x)

*Metallic bonding: a lattice of positive metal ions (large circles labelled +) surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons (small dots).*

* **Positive metal ions**: Shown as the large circles with a $+$ sign. They are arranged in regular layers and stay in fixed positions in the lattice.

* **Delocalised electrons**: Shown as the small dots between the ions. They came from the outer shells of the metal atoms and can move freely through the whole lattice.

* **Metallic bond**: The attraction between the positive ions and the negative delocalised electrons. It acts in all directions throughout the lattice, which holds the structure together.
