*Properties of metals and how they are explained by metallic bonding.*

### Malleability and Ductility

* **Malleable**: Can be hammered into different shapes.

* **Ductile**: Can be drawn out into wires.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/35db7fe3bedcff8e76ee7f39.webp =601x)

*When a force is applied, the layers of positive ions slide over each other and the shape changes, but the metallic bonding is not broken.*

* **Why sliding doesn't break the bonding**: The delocalised electrons don't belong to any specific metal atom, so they move along with the layers of positive ions and keep holding them together. The metallic bonds aren't broken; they are strong and flexible.

### Practice Questions

1. Describe the structure of a metal. [2 marks]

2. Explain why metals have high melting points. [2 marks]

3. Explain why copper is a good conductor of electricity. [2 marks]

4. Explain why metals can be bent into shape without breaking. [2 marks]

**Answers:**

1. A giant lattice of positive metal ions (1) surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons (1).

2. There is a strong electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons (1), so a lot of energy is needed to overcome it (1).

3. Copper has delocalised electrons (1) that are free to move through the structure and carry the charge (1).

4. The layers of positive ions can slide over each other (1), while the delocalised electrons move with them, so the metallic bonding is not broken (1).
