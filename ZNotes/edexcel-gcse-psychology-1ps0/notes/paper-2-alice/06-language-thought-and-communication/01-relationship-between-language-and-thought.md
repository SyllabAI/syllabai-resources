# Language, Thought & Communication

## Relationship Between Language and Thought

* **Language:** symbols (words or signs) others understand and use to communicate.
* **Cognitive development:** the way children develop their thinking abilities.
* **Representational thinking:** thinking/mental processing about objects in the world.

### **Piaget’s (1950) Explanation that Representational Thinking Precedes Language**

* Piaget (1950) proposed that children's thinking develops in stages through mental images of the world.
* Representational thinking involves mentally processing objects as things in the world, occurring before naming those objects with language.

### **Stages of Cognitive Development Linked to Language:**

* **Sensorimotor Stage (Birth to 2 years):**
  * Inborn responses and complex movements develop.
  * Infants build schemas to understand the world and grasp the concept of objects (representational thinking).
  * Language is not prominent in this stage, suggesting that thought precedes language.
* **Pre-Operational Thinking Stage (2–7 years):**
  * The child builds on mental imagery and develops symbols for objects, including language.
  * Other symbols emerge, such as symbolic play and mental imagery, indicating that thinking extends beyond language.
  * Piaget's stages demonstrate that thinking evolves first, understanding objects in the world, followed by the development of language use.

### **Vygotsky’s (1981) Explanation that Language and Thinking are Separate**

1. **Mental imagery:** seeing and thinking without having real objects present.
2. **Symbols:** anything used to represent an object; a word, a thought or a picture can be a symbol.
3. **External language:** social language used for interacting with others.
4. **Pre-intellectual language:** a child uses words, such as repeating a word or phrase, without underlying thought about the meaning.
5. **Pre-linguistic thought:** a child's thinking and developing of ideas without using symbols such as words to organise thinking.
6. **Inner speech:** use of language internally when thinking.
7. **Egocentric speech:** use of external things or talking aloud to aid thinking.

* Vygotsky's theory, developed in the 1920s and 1930s but translated in 1981, explores the influence of social and cultural experiences on development.
* Like Piaget, Vygotsky sees children as active learners in their development.
* **Pre-intellectual Language:**
  * **Speech as Social:**
    * Vygotsky emphasizes the social nature of speech, stating that children develop through talking with adults.
    * Initially, words are used in a social context to discuss objects and engage with others, termed as external language.
  * **Development Progression:**
    * Vygotsky's theory includes pre-intellectual language, where a child's speech, initially focused on copying, doesn't involve active thinking.
* **Pre-linguistic Thought:**
  * **Separate Development:**
    * Initially, thought and language develop independently, known as pre-linguistic thought.
    * Babies build their understanding of the world without using language actively.
  * **Example:**
    * Prelinguistic thought is observed when an infant intentionally throws food on the floor.
* **Language and Thought Integration:**
  * **Internalization of Speech:**
    * Language and thought converge when speech is internalized, typically occurring at ages 2–3.
    * Before this stage, a language primarily involves labelling objects rather than thoughtful reflection.
  * **Development of Reasoning:**
    * With internal speech, thought involves reasoning, marking a shift from externalized, label-based language to more complex thinking processes.
* **Egocentric Speech:**
  * **Use of External Aids:**
    * Before internalising speech, children may use egocentric speech, where objects and external elements aid in their thinking process.
    * Examples include using fingers to count.
