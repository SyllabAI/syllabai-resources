## Validity and Reliability

**Reliability and Validity:**

* **Reliability:** Refers to the consistency or repeatability of research. Ensuring reliability involves controlling variables, including extraneous factors, using standardized procedures, and employing scientific measures that yield quantitative data for easier comparison. Inter-rater reliability occurs when two or more researchers agree on observations, enhancing overall reliability.
* **Validity:** Reflects the accuracy of a study in representing the truth. Achieving validity includes conducting research in a natural environment and designing tasks to minimize participant biases such as demand characteristics and socially desirable responses. Ecological validity assesses whether the setting is natural, with participant familiarity enhancing ecological validity, while unfamiliar lab experiments may lack it.

**Ethics in Observations:**

* The most ethical form of observation is **overt observation,** where participants are aware of being observed, allowing them to choose whether to participate.
* The least ethical form is **covert observation** because participants are unaware of being observed and cannot provide informed consent.

**Reliability and Validity in Observations:**

* The most reliable form of observation is **structured observation**, involving planned and staged events, providing greater control over variables.
* The least reliable is **natural observation**, occurring in a natural setting, making it harder to control variables, especially extraneous ones.
* The most valid observations are **natural and covert observations as they occur** in a familiar setting, and participants are unaware of being observed, fostering truthful behaviour. This enhances ecological validity.
* The least valid observations are **structured and overt observations** due to participant awareness, potentially leading to demand characteristics. Artificial settings in structured observations reduce ecological validity.
