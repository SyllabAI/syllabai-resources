## Van Houtte and Jarvis (1995) The Role of Pets in Preadolescent Psychosocial Development

### **Background:**

* Children perceive pets as warm and empathetic due to unconditional positive regard.
* Children aged 9-12 develop identity and autonomy, impacting self-image and self-esteem.
* The study aimed to investigate with controlled variables, responding to prior questionnaire-based evidence lacking controls.

### **Key Terms:**

* *Unconditional Positive Regard:* Affection without expecting anything in return (e.g., pets to children).
* *Identity:* Individual sense of self.
* *Autonomy:* Ability to make personal decisions.
* *Self-Image:* Personal perception based on beliefs and external viewpoints.
* *Self-Esteem:* Valuation of oneself.

### **Aim:**

To examine the impact of pets on children's development, specifically in terms of self-esteem, self-concept, sense of autonomy, and attachment to animals.

### **Sample:**

* There were 130 white American school children (71 boys, 59 girls).
* 65 pet owners (cats or dogs) and 65 non-pet owners.
* Matched for family, economic, and social status.
* Participants provided informed consent and could opt-out anytime.

### **Procedure**

1. Conducted interviews for background information, including family details.
2. Questionnaires (Likert-type scales) on autonomy, self-concept, self-esteem, and animal attachment.

### **Results**

1. **Autonomy:**
   * Pet owners are more likely to perceive parents as individuals, suggesting autonomy.
2. **Self-Concept:**
   * 11-year-old pet owners exhibited higher self-concept (94.2) than non-pet owners (83.2).
3. **Self-Esteem:**
   * 9-year-old pet owners had lower self-esteem, while 10- and 11-year-olds had higher self-esteem.
4. **Animal Attachment:**
   * No significant difference; attachment is not related to pet ownership.

### **Conclusions**

1. Limited support for pet owners showing higher autonomy, focusing on parental perception.
2. 11-year-old pet owners demonstrated higher self-concept and self-esteem.
3. Pet attachment did not correlate with pet ownership.
4. Older pet owners had higher self-esteem, suggesting pets had a positive impact before adolescence.
