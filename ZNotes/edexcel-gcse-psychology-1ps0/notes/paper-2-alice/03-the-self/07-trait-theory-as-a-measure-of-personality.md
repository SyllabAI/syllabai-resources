## Trait Theory as a Measure of Personality

**Personality Assessment: Trait Theory and Factor Analysis**

* **Factor Analysis:**
  * Utilizing statistical analysis to identify overarching patterns.
  * **Cardinal Traits:** Uncommon, dominant traits that strongly influence our lives.
  * **Central Traits:** Common and moderate traits, like friendliness.
  * **Secondary Traits:** Traits present but not always displayed, such as anger.
  * Factor analysis uncovers underlying characteristics predicting behaviour from observed traits.
* **Trait Theory Overview:**
  * **Definition Debate:** Psychologists debate how to define personality.
  * **Trait Theory Concept:** Human personality consists of broad traits or dispositions.
  * **Cattell's Personality Factors:** Incorporated into the Sixteen Personality Factor Questionnaire (16PF).
  * **Continuum Concept:** Personality traits exist on a continuum; individuals possess all 16 traits to varying degrees.
* **Cattell's Approach:**
  * Analyzed Allport's list, reduced it to 171 characteristics, and applied factor analysis.
  * Identified 16 key personality factors through statistical techniques.
  * **Measurement Methods:**
    * Life data: Everyday behaviours.
    * Experimental data: Reactions to standardized situations.
    * Questionnaire data: Self-ratings of behaviour and feelings.
* **Strengths of Trait Theory:**
  * **Observability:** Allport selected observable traits.
  * **Comparability:** Allows comparison of people's scores and labelling of personality.
  * **Validity:** Self-rating scales eliminate external interpretation.
  * **Real Data Element:** Personality measured using individual criteria.
  * **Theoretical Support:** Backed by various theorists, enhancing its credibility.
* **Weaknesses of Trait Theory:**
  * **Behaviour Prediction Challenge:** Difficulty predicting behaviour across different situations.
  * **Self-Awareness Requirement:** This relies on individuals being self-aware for accurate self-ratings.
  * **Subjectivity Issue:** Discrepancies between self-perception and others' perceptions.
  * **Descriptive Focus:** Describes personality but lacks explanatory power for trait origins.
  * **Limited in Developmental Insights:** Falls short in explaining personality development and facilitating changes based on traits.
