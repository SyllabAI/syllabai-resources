## How Personality Can Be Measured

### **Key Concepts:**

1. **Measurement Components:**
   * **Scales:** Classification of information through numerical representation of characteristics.
   * **Items:** Statements or questions in questionnaires, including those on personality tests.
   * **Dimensions:** Measurable aspects, like 'openness' in personality.
2. **Personality Measurement:**
   * **Conscientiousness:** Organization, efficiency, and carefulness driven by a desire to excel.
   * **Neuroticism:** Inclination towards heightened feelings of depression, anger, and anxiety.
   * **Likert-type Scale enables** individuals to rate their agreement level with a statement.
   * **Objective Measurement:** Scores from scales offer an objective personality assessment, minimizing interpretative biases.
3. **Scales in Personality Measurement:**
   * Measurement involves scales providing numerical values to characteristics.
   * Example: Scoring someone's 'shyness' to 'outgoing.'
   * Totalled scores contribute to a personality summary, offering an overall characteristic score.
4. **Personality Tests:**
   * Utilize Likert scales for standard scoring.
   * Incorporate dimensions for self-assessment.
   * Example: "On a scale from happy to sad, where 'happy' is 10 and 'sad' is 1, rate yourself."
5. **Big 5 Personality Dimensions:**
   * **Likert Scale:** A close-ended, forced-choice questionnaire scale with various answers.
   * Focus on the Big 5 Personality Dimensions in items on personality tests.
6. **Personality Theories:**
   * **Type Theory:**
     * Categorizes personality into types (e.g., introvert or extrovert).
     * Assigns a named characteristic to an individual.
   * **Trait Theory:**
     * Views personality characteristics on a continuum (e.g., extraversion scale).
     * Examines qualitative differences among individuals.
7. **Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI):**
   * Developed by Isabel Myers and Katharine Briggs (1956).
   * Based on Carl Jung's theory, incorporating extraversion/introversion, sensing/intuition, thinking/feeling, judging/perceiving.
   * Identifies four overall ways individuals experience the world.
   * Aims to help individuals understand their unique approaches and assist couples in navigating differences.
