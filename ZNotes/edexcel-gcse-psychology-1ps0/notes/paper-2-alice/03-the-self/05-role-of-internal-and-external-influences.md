## Role of Internal and External Influences

### **Key Concepts:**

* **Internal Factors:** Pertaining to the individual, such as character traits.
* **External Factors:** Relating to elements outside the individual, such as situational influences.
* **Temperament:** The innate nature with which an individual is born, influencing behaviour.
* **Introvert:** An individual inclined to be reserved and less likely to seek social interaction.
* **Effortful Control:** Utilizing reasoned effort to manage actions and thoughts.

### **Internal Influence on Self and Self-Esteem**

1. **Temperament:**
   * A biological aspect is unique to each person shapes emotions and self-control.
   * Temperament plays a role in influencing self-concept.
   * Example: A person with low self-esteem and a shy temperament might tend towards introversion, potentially leading to depressive tendencies.
2. **Effortful Control:**
   * A facet of temperament involves self-regulation.
   * Involves decision-making regarding priorities, such as maintaining focus in a distracting classroom.
   * Individuals with effective self-regulation often exhibit higher self-esteem due to improved interactions with others.
3. **Negative Emotions:**
   * Individuals experiencing negative emotions are prone to anxiety, contributing to lower self-esteem.
4. **Persistence:**
   * Another temperament aspect.
   * Research by Windle et al. (1986) indicates a correlation between persistence and high self-esteem.
   * Similar findings are observed for individuals with low distraction levels, another trait related to temperament.

### **External Influence on Self and Self-Esteem**

1. **Self-Efficacy:**
   * The strength of belief in one's ability to accomplish a specific task.
   * Varies across different goals, influenced by experiences and interactions.
   * Highly dependent on the situation, emphasizing the external aspect and the impact of experiences.
2. **Factors Affecting Self-Image:**
   * Proposed by Michael Argyle (2008).
   * A positive self-image results from praise and attention, while criticism contributes to a negative self-image.
   * Social comparisons play a role, with better-performing peers leading to a negative self-image and vice versa.
   * Experiences in society provide an understanding of acceptable social roles, influencing self-image.
3. **Perceptions of Others:**
   * Others' perceptions can alter how we view ourselves, subsequently impacting self-esteem.
   * Positive achievements may enhance self-image, while negative perceptions can have adverse effects.

### **Evidence of Others' Perceptions of Self-Esteem:**

1. Rina Bajaj (2008) explored the impact of the 2005 London bombings on young Asian men, noting shifts in perceptions that affected identity and self-esteem.
2. Instances of bullying in school are well-documented, showcasing the severe negative consequences on an individual's self-esteem.
