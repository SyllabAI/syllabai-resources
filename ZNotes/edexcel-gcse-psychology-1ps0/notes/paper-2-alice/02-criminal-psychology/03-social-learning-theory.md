## Social Learning Theory

* *Role Models:* Individuals observed and identified with in some way.
* *Modelling:* Imitation of observed behaviour.
* *Identification:* Observer's connection with the role model.
* *Observational Learning:* Learning through observation.
* *Vicarious Reinforcement:* Motivation for reproducing behaviours seen in others.

### **Identification and Modeling Process**

* **Modelling Process:**
  * **Attention:** Focus on observing the behaviour.
  * **Retention:** Remember the information and behaviour witnessed.
  * **Reproduction:** Need an opportunity to replicate the observed behaviour.
  * **Motivation:** More likely to reproduce behaviour if motivated, often driven by a reward.
  * **Identification:** Successful processing may lead to adopting the beliefs of role models.
  * **Vicarious Reinforcement:** Motivation to copy behaviour due to gaining pleasure.

### **Strengths and Weaknesses of Social Learning Theory**

* *Strengths:*
  * Research supports theory (e.g., Bandura's bobo doll study).
  * Explains why some individuals become criminals.
  * Not overly reductionist, considering both environment and thoughts.
* *Weaknesses:*
  * Ignores genetic factors.
  * Limited to short-term aggression.
  * Doesn't explain all criminal behaviours.

### **Studies Supporting Social Learning Theory**

* *Bandura, Ross and Ross (1961):*
  * Aggressive role models influence children's behaviour.
* *Tony Charlton et al. (2000):*
  * TV introduction had little impact on pro-social and anti-social behaviour.
