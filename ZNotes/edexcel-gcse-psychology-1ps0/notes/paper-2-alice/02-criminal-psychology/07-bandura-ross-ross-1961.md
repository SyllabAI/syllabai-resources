## Bandura, Ross & Ross (1961)

**Aim(s):**

* Investigate if children imitate aggressive and non-aggressive role model behaviour without receiving rewards.
* Explore whether children are more inclined to copy same-sex role models compared to opposite-sex role models.
* Examine if boys exhibit more aggression than girls, especially when exposed to aggressive male role models.

**Procedure:**

* Sample: 72 children (36 boys, 36 girls), aged 37-69 months, from Stanford University nursery school.
* Two adult role models (one male, one female).
* Divided into eight sub-groups (six participants each) and a control group (24 participants).
* Experimental conditions:
  * Aggressive role model: Boys (male and female), Girls (male and female).
  * Non-aggressive role models: Boys (male and female), Girls (male and female).
  * The control group observed no role model behaviour.
* Participants were rated for aggression based on nursery school interactions, ensuring groups were matched.
* Participants played with familiar toys in experimental conditions while the role model engaged differently.
* Non-aggressive role model played with other toys, avoiding the Bobo doll.
* The aggressive role model displayed novel aggressive behaviours toward the Bobo doll, accompanied by aggressive comments.
* After 10 minutes, the experimenter moved the participant to another room with more toys.
* Participant's behaviour was observed through a one-way mirror at 5-second intervals for 20 minutes, focusing on imitative aggression.

**Results:**

* Aggressive groups exhibited more physical and verbal aggression than non-aggressive groups.
* Boys imitated more physical aggression than girls, with no significant sex difference in verbal aggression.
* Boys imitated more aggression after exposure to male-aggressive role models than female-aggressive role models.
* Non-aggressive conditions saw more nonaggressive play activities or quiet behaviour.

**Conclusions:**

* Exposure to aggressive adult role models may weaken inhibitory responses in children, increasing the likelihood of aggressive reactions.
* Children tend to learn through imitation, with a higher likelihood if the adult role model is male, irrespective of the child's gender.


|**Strengths**|**Weaknesses**|
|:---|:---|
|**Controlled Experimental Design:** Facilitates manipulation of variables and establishes cause-and-effect relationships.|**Limited Generalizability:** A sample from a specific nursery school limits generalizability to a broader population.|
|**Standardised Procedures:** Ensures consistency in presenting aggressive and non-aggressive behaviours.|**Demand Characteristics:** Participants may have guessed research aims, affecting behaviour.|
|**Matched Groups:** Participants rated for aggression beforehand, minimizing pre-existing group differences.|**Ethical Concerns:** Mild aggression arousal and exposure may cause distress to participants.|
|**Behavioural Observations:** Objective and measurable data obtained through direct observations.|**Limited Long-Term Effects:** Focuses on short-term effects and lacks insights into long-term consequences.|
||**Stereotyped Gender Roles:** Assumes a significant role for gender without considering variations within genders.|
||**Artificial Setting:** A controlled lab setting may not fully capture real-world dynamics.|
||**Ethnocentric Bias:** Primarily features a Western context, limiting universal applicability.|
