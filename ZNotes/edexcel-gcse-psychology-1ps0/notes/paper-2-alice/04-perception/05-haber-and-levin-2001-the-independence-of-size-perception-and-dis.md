## Haber and Levin (2001) The Independence of Size Perception and Distance Perception

### **Aims:**

* Investigate if objects can be categorized as token invariant or token variable.
* Examine participants' accuracy in estimating the size of token invariant and token variable objects from memory.
* Assess whether familiarity and size knowledge influence participants' judgments of distance.

### **Procedure:**

* Size Estimation from Memory (Experiment 1):
  * List of 50 familiar items.
  * 109 male university students estimated the size (feet & inches) of 30 items categorized as token invariant or token variable.
  * Researchers independently rated drawings for accuracy on a 5-point scale.
* Distance Judgment (Experiment 2):
  * 9 male university students not in Experiment 1.
  * Large field with 3 sections containing token invariant, token variable, and unfamiliar objects.
  * Participants estimated the size and distance of objects.
  * Questionnaires on familiarity and size knowledge.
  * Analysis of accuracy in distance perception.

### **Results:**

* Experiment 1: Token invariant items had more accurate size estimates.
  * 74% resemblance in Group 1, 73% in Group 2, and 45% in the control group.
  * Average viewings: Group 1 & 2 (3 times), control group (4 times).
* Experiment 2: Familiar invariant objects aided distance cues more than variant/unfamiliar objects.
  * Accuracy is higher for token invariant objects (0-50 meters).
  * Size estimation accuracy is similar to Experiment 1.

### **Conclusions:**

* Familiarity with objects influences accurate size and distance perception.
* Token invariant items contribute to more accurate estimations, emphasizing the role of experience.

### **Strengths:**

* Controlled Vision Screening: Participants underwent vision screening, ensuring consistent visual ability.
* Independent Familiarity Judgment: Including only highly familiar objects, independently judged, strengthens the study's focus.

### **Weaknesses:**

* Small Sample Size: Experiment 2 utilized only 9 male participants, limiting generalization and potential gender bias.
* Uncertain Familiarity Levels: Participants' varying familiarity levels with each object could impact results, though efforts were made to ensure familiarity.
