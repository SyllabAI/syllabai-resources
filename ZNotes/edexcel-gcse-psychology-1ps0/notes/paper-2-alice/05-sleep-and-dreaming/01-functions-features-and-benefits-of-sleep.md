# Sleep & Dreaming

## Functions, Features and Benefits of Sleep

* Sleep is a recurring condition involving a temporary suspension of consciousness. Also defined as a periodic, natural, reversible, and near-total loss of consciousness.

### **Biological Rhythms:**

* Biological rhythms involve regular contrasts in biological activity.
* Examples include changes in brain chemicals, bodily temperature regulation, and the shift between sleeping and waking.
  * Circadian Rhythms: A 24-hour biological rhythm.
    * The Hypothalamus regulates the circadian rhythm.
  * Ultradian Rhythms: Biological rhythms with more than one cycle in 24 hours.

### **Four Stages of Sleep and REM Sleep:**

* Stage 1:
  * Light sleep, easily awakened.
  * Muscle activity decreases, and eye movements slow.
  * Presence of alpha and theta brain waves.
* Stage 2:
  * Late-night sleep stage.
  * Slower brain waves, mainly theta.
  * Spindles of brain activity, drop in body temperature and heart rate.
* Stage 3:
  * Deep sleep stage with slow delta waves.
  * The transition between light and deep sleep.
* Stage 4:
  * The final deep sleep stage is with slow delta waves.
  * Harder to wake up with no eye movement.
  * Associated with sleepwalking and night terrors.

### **REM Sleep:**

* Rapid Eye Movement stage.
* Flickering eyelids, vivid dreaming.
* Approximately 2 hours of sleep are spent on dreaming.
* Sensory blockade during REM prevents incoming information from the senses.
* Characteristics include shallow, rapid, irregular breathing, eye jerking, and muscle paralysis.

### **NREM Sleep (Non-Rapid Eye Movement):**

* One of the two distinctive types of sleep in mammals.
* Stages 3 and 4 are referred to as slow-wave sleep (SWS).
* Differentiated from REM sleep by EEG waves.

### **Other Sleep Concepts:**

* Oneirology: The study of dreams.
* Recuperation: Allowing neurons and cells to rest and repair during sleep.
* Sleep Deprivation: Not having enough sleep.
* Sleep Debt: Accumulated lack of sleep affecting physical and mental functioning.

### **Sleep Disorders:**

* Insomnia: Difficulty falling or staying asleep.
* Narcolepsy: Uncontrollable sleep attacks.
* Sleep Apnea: Temporary cessation of breathing during sleep.

### **Sleep Cycle vs. Sleep-Wake Cycle:**

* Sleep Cycle:
  * Lasts about 90 minutes, ultradian rhythm.
  * Involves stages 1 to 4 and REM sleep.
  * Five cycles occur during a night's sleep.
* Sleep-Wake Cycle:
  * A circadian rhythm lasts around 24 hours.
  * Encompasses periods of wakefulness and sleep.
  * Includes the sleep cycle within the sleep period.
