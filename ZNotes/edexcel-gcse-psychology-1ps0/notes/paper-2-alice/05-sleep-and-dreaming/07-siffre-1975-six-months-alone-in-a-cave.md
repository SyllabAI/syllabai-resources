## Siffre (1975) Six Months Alone in a Cave

### Aims

1. To find out what it would be like for astronauts in space, where there were no exogenous zeitgebers such as daylight to affect our biological rhythms
2. To see what his natural sleep-wake cycle was when deprived of external cues

### **Sample**

* Siffre, who was 33 years old at the time of the study

### **Procedure**

* Siffre stayed in an underground cave in Texas, USA, where he lived for the following six months (February 14th to September 5th)
* Electrodes were attached to him to measure his heart, brain and muscle activity.
* Each time he would wake up, he’d call someone in order to tell them that he had awakened and to inform them if they should turn on the lights or off.
* To ensure it was not clear what time of day it was, telephone calls were short
* He had a table, tent, lamp, and a notebook with him where he recorded his experiences
* While awake, he conducted a few tests on himself, such as:
  * recording his blood pressure
  * tasks to measure mental activity
  * measuring memory
  * and physical ability
    * cycle machine to cycle 3 miles
    * firing practice to test coordination
    * threading bead on a string
* He cleaned the cave daily in order to avoid pulmonary diseases

### **Findings**

* Siffre’s mental wellbeing deteriorated progressively, getting depressed with his lack of freedom, trapping a mouse as he was seeking companionship.
  * His eyesight also worsened, thoughts of suicide arise with his resources getting damaged (books wet & record player broken)
* As he wanted to withdraw from the experiment, his short-term memory worsened.
  * Compared to the effect that is compared to those astronauts experience in space
* His sleep cycle would be irregular, ranging from 18-52 hours, with his first sleep cycle being more than 24 hours long.

### **Conclusions**

* Siffre experienced effects during his time in his caves with
  * memory lapses
  * weakened eyesight
* Emphasising the importance of external cues and bodily rhythms
* In the absence of external cues, there’s a tendency for sleep cycles to shift to a 48-hour sleep cycle
  * furthermore, raising concerns for NASA in relation to long-range space travel
