## Caspi et al (2003)

### **Background:**

* Depression linked to stressful life experiences.
* Diathesis–stress theories propose genetic vulnerability as a factor.
* Investigation focused on serotonin-targeting antidepressants (SSRIs).
* Role of 5-HTT gene in serotonin efficiency explored.

### **Aim:**

* Examine why some experience depression after stressful events.
* Investigate the contribution of the 5-HTT gene to depression.

### **Procedure:**

* Longitudinal study from age 3 to 26 (96% follow-up).
* Participants from the Dunedin Multidisciplinary Health and Development Study.
* Divided into three groups based on 5-HTTLPR genotype.
* Stressful life events recorded (employment, financial, housing, health, relationships).
* Depression assessed at age 26 using Diagnostic Interview Schedule.
* Informants questioned about participants' depressive symptoms.

### **Results:**

*Measure 1 – Stressful Life Events:*

* No differences in experienced stressful life events across groups.

*Measure 2 – Depression:*

* 17% experienced DSM-IV depressive episode in the past year.
* 3% reported a past-year suicide attempt.

### **Results**

1. (s) allele carriers showed a stronger interaction between life events and depression.
2. (s) allele carriers had increased depressive symptoms from 21 to 26 years old.
3. Life events after age 21 predicted new depression cases at 26 for (s) allele carriers.
4. Stressful life events predicted major depression among (s) allele carriers.
5. Stressful life events predicted informant reports of depression among (s) allele carriers.
6. Stressful life events predicted suicide ideation among (s) allele carriers.
7. 5-HTT gene moderated the impact of stressful life events, reducing their effects.

### **Conclusions:**

* 5-HTT gene interacts with life events to predict depressive symptoms and related outcomes.
* (l) allele carriers less likely to develop depression or suicidality.
* (l) allele moderates effects of childhood maltreatment on depression.
