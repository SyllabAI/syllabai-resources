## Genetic Explanation for Depression

* **Genetic Influence on Depression:**
  * Depression is hereditary, carried in our DNA, and more likely to occur in families. **Craig Hyde et al. (2016)** identified 17 gene variations linked to depression development.
* **Genetic Predisposition:**
  * Refers to the tendency to become depressed due to one's genes.
* **Diathesis-stress Model:**
  * Suggests that individuals possess a gene predisposing them to depression, which is triggered by a stressful event.
  * *Genetic predisposition \+ environmental factors = depression*

![](https://znotes-static.s3.amazonaws.com/edexcel/igcse/psychology/659d9ec3e9444d77a00c70f47a5b4e6ab32af1cd8bcfb066.jpeg =667x)

* **Twin Studies:**
  * **Peter McGuffin et al. (1996)** found that if one genetically identical (monozygotic) twin became depressed, there was a 46% chance the other would too. In contrast, if a dizygotic twin became depressed, there was only a 20% chance their twin would follow suit. This supports both genetic predisposition and the diathesis-stress model.
