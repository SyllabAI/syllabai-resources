## **Sperry (1968) Hemisphere Deconnection**

### **Aims:**

* Investigate the independent and cooperative functioning of the two hemispheres in split-brain patients.
* Determine the roles of the left and right hemispheres in processing different types of information and assess their independent capabilities.

### **Background**

* Built upon previous research suggesting distinct functions for the left and right hemispheres of the brain.
* Aims to gain insights into brain lateralization and functional specialization by studying split-brain patients.

### **Sample**

* Included 11 participants who had undergone corpus callosum surgical cutting, crucial for investigating split-brain functioning.

### **Procedure**

* Utilised various tests and tasks to evaluate participants' abilities, such as presenting images or words to specific visual fields and assessing responses.
* For example, words presented to the right visual field allowed participants to speak about what they saw, as the left hemisphere controls speech.

### **Results**

* Sperry's findings demonstrated the independent and distinct abilities of the two brain hemispheres.
* The right hemisphere excelled in spatial awareness and creativity, while the left was responsible for language and logical thinking.

### **Conclusion**

* Confirmed the concept of brain lateralization, highlighting specialized functions of the left and right hemispheres.
* Deepened understanding of cognitive abilities in split-brain patients and their information processing mechanisms.
* Significant implications for neuropsychology and cognitive science emerged from Sperry's work.
