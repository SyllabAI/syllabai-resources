## Factors Affecting Bystander Effect

### **Factors Affecting Helping Behavior:**

### **Situational Factors:**

1. **Diffusion of Responsibility:**
   * Feeling less personally responsible with more people around.
   * Less likelihood to help in a larger crowd, assuming others will assist.
2. **Noticing the Event:**
   * Reduced awareness of events in large crowds.
   * Latané and Darley's (1969) experiment showed delayed response in group settings.
3. **Pluralistic Ignorance:**
   * Socially interpreting situations based on others' actions.
   * If no one helps, perceiving the event as non-emergent; following others if they assist.
4. **Cost of Helping:**
   * Higher costs (time, effort, danger) decrease likelihood of helping.
   * Helping may occur when victim's costs outweigh personal costs, often to avoid guilt.

### **Personal Factors:**

1. **Competence:**
   * Feeling capable influences the type of assistance provided.
   * Trained individuals more likely to offer direct help, like CPR.
2. **Mood:**
   * Greater likelihood of helping when in a positive mood.
3. **Similarity:**
   * Increased likelihood to help victims perceived as similar to oneself

While some personality factors play a role, bystander intervention is predominantly influenced by situational factors.
