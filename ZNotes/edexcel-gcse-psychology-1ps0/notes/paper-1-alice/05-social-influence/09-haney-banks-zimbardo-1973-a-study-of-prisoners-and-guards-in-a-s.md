## Haney, Banks & Zimbardo (1973) A Study of Prisoners and Guards in a Simulated Prison

### **Aims:**

1. Investigate the psychological impact of perceived power and authority within a simulated prison setting.
2. Understand how individuals conform to assigned roles in a controlled environment, specifically as prisoners or guards.
3. Examine the dynamics of interactions between prisoners and guards and their influence on behaviour.

### **Background:**

* The Stanford prison experiment aimed to explore the psychological consequences of power and authority in a prison context.
* Seeks to replicate and extend findings from the Stanford prison experiment to deepen understanding of psychological dynamics in such situations.

### **Sample:**

* Volunteer college students are randomly assigned roles as prisoners or guards.

**Procedure:**

* Conducted in a simulated prison environment with cells and appropriate uniforms.
* "Guards" were given authority, including batons; "prisoners" were arrested by actual police officers.
* Guards exhibited aggressive and authoritarian behaviours, leading to prisoner rebellion.
* Initially planned for two weeks, it was terminated after six days due to extreme psychological distress.

### **Results:**

* Participants quickly adapted to assigned roles, with guards becoming increasingly authoritarian and prisoners experiencing significant distress and rebellion.
* Demonstrated people's capacity to conform to assigned roles, even in a simulated environment.
* Unexpected and extreme behaviours led to early termination.

### **Conclusion:**

* Haney, Banks, and Zimbardo's study underscores the substantial impact of perceived power and authority on human behaviour in a simulated prison.
* Emphasises individuals' tendency to conform to assigned roles and the importance of ethical considerations in psychological research.
* Contributes to a better understanding of the psychological dynamics of power and authority in controlled settings.
