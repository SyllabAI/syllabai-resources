## Dweck’s Mindset Theory

**Ability:** what we can do

**Effort:** Doing better using determination

* Dweck introduced the idea of mindsets, which are a set of beliefs
* Two main mindsets are growth and fixed mindset
  * **Fixed Mindset:** the belief that abilities are fixed and are unchangeable
  * **Growth Mindset:** belief that there’s potential for improvement through practice and effort
* Teachers play a pivotal role in shaping a child’s mindset
* This can affect a child’s performance in several ways, such as refusal to take on challenges, low self-esteem and ability to improve.

**Supporting evidence:**

* **Mueller and Dweck (1998)**: praising students’ ability led to a fixed mindset and difficulty with coping with setbacks, compared to praising students’ effort which led to a growth mindset and preservering more

* **Yeager and Dweck (2012)**: low-achieving students with a growth mindset performed better compared to controls

* **Gunderson et al. (2013) (KEY STUDY)**: positive correlation (relationship) between process praise and incremental framework (belief ability can be changed/moulded)

|

**Ecological validity:** many studies were conducted in laboratories/controlled settings and utilised artificial/unusual tasks, which questions the extent these results can be applied to real-life scenarios

||
||

**Practical applications:** useful for teaching and parenting; educators and parents can help children learn to develop a growth mindset through recognising and praising children’s effort rather than outcome; overall contributing to a better society due to its benefits

|

**Questionable usefulness:** emphasises heavily on the individual’s mindset, which can be a major weakness; this reductionism could lead to less focus on the individual’s environment (quality of education, socioeconomic status, etc) that may be the contributing factor that allows or prevents them from trying

||
||

**Some consistency regarding children from different backgrounds:** Sisk et al. (2008) found some results that students from low socioeconomic backgrounds or who are academically at risk may benefit from mindset interventions

|

**Socially unethical:** constantly praising a child for their efforts rather than focusing on the quality of the outcome may cause more frustration than satisfaction; students can continue to work hard but still fail if no proper feedback is given for their work

||
||

**Contributes to positive psychology:** mindset theory sees failures and setbacks as an opportunity to learn rather than something that lowers one’s self-worth; positive implementations can be made such as coping with challenges

|

**Counter-evidence:** Bouchard and McGue (1981) found a positive correlation between genetics and intelligence in 111 studies; challenges the idea effort is what leads to ability

**Inconsistent evidence:** Sisk et al. (2008) conducted two meta-analyses, one between mindset and academic performance, another on mindset intervention and academic performance; both meta-analyses concluded weak effects
