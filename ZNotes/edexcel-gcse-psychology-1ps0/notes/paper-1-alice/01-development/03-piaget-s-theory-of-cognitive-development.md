## Piaget’s Theory of Cognitive Development

* Piaget proposed that children learn through schemas and mental frameworks for organizing and interpreting information.
* **Schema/Schemata:** Mental Representations of the world based on one’s own experiences.
* **Assimilation:** fitting new experiences into existing schemas
* **Accommodation:** when existing schemas are modified to make new experiences
* **Equilibrium: a** balanced state of mind reached when schemas can explain all an individual experiences.

**Practical application:** can be integrated into educational settings (such as teaching children according to their stage of development – age-appropriate teaching), supporting the idea of designing of classroom activities, contributing to societal benefit

|

**Subjective nature of the theory:** the theory was built on Piaget’s observations of his own children, and his own interpretations of open-ended responses from children he interviewed

||
||

**Evidence:** support from studies such as the Piaget and Inhelder (1956) ‘Three mountains’ task (KEY STUDY), confirming the theory’s claims, meaning there is (some) validity and reliability

|

**Cultural and social considerations:** the theory doesn’t consider the other factors that may affect the child's development; Dasen (1994) found Aboriginal children conserved much later compared to Piaget’s Swiss sample

||
||

**Qualitative nature:** the theory was built on an abundance of rich detail, which could further support the validity

|

**Small and biased samples:** Piaget’s samples were from well-educated, upper-class backgrounds, and replica studies utilised small sample sizes, which makes it difficult to confidently conclude the generalisability of the results

||
||

**Holistic nature:** highlights how children learn and change their schema/schemata (cognition) through interactions with their environment

|

**Difficult to study scientifically and objectively:** Piaget’s theory lacks defined, operationalised (measurable) variables (example: you cannot measure schema/schemata)

**Questionable reliability:** additionally, various studies that researched Piaget’s theories each had to define certain variables differently, causing different results to be found
