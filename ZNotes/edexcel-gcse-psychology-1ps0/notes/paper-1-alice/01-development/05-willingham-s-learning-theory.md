## Willingham’s Learning Theory

* Willingham's Theory emphasises the importance of factual knowledge in problem-solving and learning.
  * factual knowledge precedes skill
  * the importance of practice, and effort

### **Practice and effort**

* Practice and effort are essential for mastering knowledge and skills.
  * STM (short-term memory) benefits from practice
  * Problem-solving and creativity are crucial for success
  * STM involves practice which must be repeated for information to stick; with enough practice, the information will be fixed in the LTM (long-term memory)

### **Strategies to support cognitive, physical and social development**

* **Cognitive development:** proposed teaching strategies to aid in students’ learning
  * utilise new problems that align with a child's stage of development but still require effort

  * student’s abilities can change

  * consider other factors, not only developmental level, such as student failing to understand the task
* **Physical development:** requires practice and effort, which improves muscle movements (motor skills), and related this to brain processing
  * focus on movements and its suitability – in what order they must be carried out

  * practice movements in the required order so it becomes automatic

  * utilise conscious effort – make a physical/motor task harder, but still achievable with practice and effort
* **Social development:** the focus on practice and effort led to ideas on what strategies can be used to help children develop socially
  * building on the child’s ability to decentre (take other’s point of view)

    * *example:* ask the child “How would you feel if this happened to you?”

  * take advantage of social learning (learning through observing and imitating their behaviour); demonstrate appropriate/desirable behaviour in front of children

  * prevent impulsive behaviours by identifying what triggers them in the first place

    * *example:* phones in class

  * encouraging children to practice self regulation – controlling and limiting oneself from doing things they would want to do but do not have any benefit

    * *example:* spend time playing games instead of studying

  * delaying gratification, which means handing out rewards later instead of immediately, teaching the child to keep working and develop self-control

    * *example:* instead of rewarding them for completing one question, reward them only when they have completed one page of questions

**WEAKNESSES**

||
||

**Challenged Piaget’s ideas on cognitive development:** Willingham disagrees with Piaget’s claim that children can only take on the view of others (decentre) at 7 years old; he believed children can do this much earlier. Piaget later changes his view and agrees children can see other’s point of view at 18 months. This critique contributes to our understanding of children’s development

|

**Does not consider individual differences:** the theory attempts to overgeneralise proposed strategies work on everyone, when that is not the case; individual variations can affect development, such as neurodevelopmental disorders (dyslexia, ADHD, autism)

||
||

**Experimental evidence:** Repacholi and Gopnik (1997) showed that young children were not as egocentric as Piaget thought; they showed children at 18 months old were able to understand another person’s desires and emotions

|

**Low ecological validity:** results from experimental studies may have limited applicability to real-world settings due to its controlled setting and artificial tasks

||
||

**Practical implementations:** the theory has been widely used in various areas, such as education and parenting, which is a strength as it positively contributes to aiding children’s development

|

**May be difficult to study:** though the theory comes from many areas of cognitive science (such as neuroscience and memory), so while this holistic nature can be a strength as it shows its interaction between various areas, it makes it difficult for researchers to focus on a key area

||
||

**Scientific nature:** ‎based on cognitive science, which is considered an objective field of study, and supported by various laboratory experiments, which are conducted in controlled environments to ensure internal validity

|

**Contradicting claims and evidence:**

* despite the theory stating self-regulation can be learnt by all individuals through practice, it states genes and the environment (nature and nurture), which questions the initial statement; can self-regulation really be learnt through practice in all individuals?

* **Watts et al. (2018)** replicated the ‘Marshmallow experiment’, which tested children’s impulse control and delayed gratification, and found inconsistent results once family background was taken into account, suggesting upbringing plays a role in and affects speed of social development
