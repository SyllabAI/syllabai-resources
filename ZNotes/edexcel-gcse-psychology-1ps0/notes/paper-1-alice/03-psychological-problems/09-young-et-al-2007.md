## Young et al. (2007)

### **Aims:**

1. Investigate the efficacy of Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy (CBT) in addressing internet addiction.
2. Evaluate the sustainability of CBT effects on internet addiction over time.

### **Background:**

* Growing concern about internet addiction in the contemporary digital era.
* CBT has demonstrated effectiveness in treating various addictive behaviours.
* Assess the long-term effectiveness of CBT in diminishing internet addiction.

### **Sample:**

* 114 participants were selected from the Centre for Online Addiction
* Participants actively seeking treatment for internet addiction were recruited.
* The sample was longitudinally followed to examine the sustained effects of CBT.

### **Procedure:**

* Participants completed questionnaires gauging their internet addiction control throughout and post-CBT treatment.
* Standardised CBT procedures were employed for internet addiction treatment.
* Questionnaire data were analyzed to assess CBT's efficacy and its enduring impact on internet addiction.

### **Results:**

* Young et al. identified CBT as effective, with sustained effects observed over six months.
* Online CBT emerged as a viable and lasting treatment for internet addiction.
* Significant improvements in participants' control over internet use were revealed through the questionnaires.

### **Conclusion:**

* The study advocates for CBT's use in tackling internet addiction.
* Emphasizes the effectiveness and longevity of online CBT for this contemporary issue.
* CBT suggests that individuals can regain and maintain control over internet use.
