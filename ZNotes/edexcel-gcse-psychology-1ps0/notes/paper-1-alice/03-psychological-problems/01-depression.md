## Depression

**Unipolar depression** is characterized by a consistent and severe lowering of mood, pulling the emotional state in one direction, thereby impacting daily functioning. The International Classification of Diseases version 10 (ICD-10) labels unipolar depression as “depressive episodes”.

### **Main symptoms from International Classification of Diseases version 10 (ICD-10)**

* Persistent **sadness** or low mood
* Loss of interest or motivation
* **Fatigue** or low-energy
* **Other symptoms**
  * Disturbed **sleep**
  * Poor **concentration** or indecisiveness
  * Low **self-confidence**
  * Poor or increased **appetite**
  * Suicidal thoughts or acts
  * **Guilt** or self-blame
  * **Agitation** or slowing of movements
* At least 1 of the main symptoms present most of the time on most days for **2 WEEKS**

### **Incidence of depression overtime**

* **Martin Seligman (1988)** documented a tenfold surge in depression diagnoses compared to the 1940s
* **King’s Fund (2008)** anticipates a growth in diagnosed depression cases from 1.24 million in 2007 to 1.45 million by 2026 in England
* **Brandon Hidaka (2012)** attributes the heightened prevalence of depression to the stress associated with “modern living”, encompassing factors like sleep deprivation, poor diet, and increased social isolation
* **Jean Twenge et al. (2010)** observed a greater likelihood of depression diagnosis in young adults in 2007 compared to 1938
* **Liu Yi Lin et al. (2016)** establish a connection between increased social media usage and a heightened risk of depression in young American participants

### **How depression affects individuals and society**

* Depression may lead to higher suicide rates as individuals experiencing feelings of worthlessness and sadness seek an escape
* Workplace productivity is significantly affected, with the Health and Safety Executive estimating 9.9 million workdays lost in 2014-15 due to stress, depression, or anxiety related to work
* The increasing rates of depression escalate the cost of treatment, including expensive antidepressant prescriptions and the need for trained therapists for therapies like counselling
* In 2007, depression cost the National Health Service (NHS) an estimated £1.7 billion, potentially straining NHS resources and services
