## Learning Theory of Addiction

### **Classical Conditioning:**

* Learning through associations, where two or more occurrences become linked.
* Automatic triggering of one thing when the other is experienced.
* Positive associations with a substance or activity lead to the formation of addictive behaviours.

### **Operant Conditioning:**

* Repetition of behaviours due to positive consequences (rewards).
* Positively reinforced behaviour increases the likelihood of repetition.
* Negative reinforcement or consequences may discourage certain behaviours.
* Addiction behaviour reinforced by positive feelings increases the likelihood of repetition.

### **Social Learning Theory:**

* Learning behaviour by observing and modeling others, especially role models.
* Imitation of admired figures, potentially leading to addiction, e.g., mimicking a parent's smoking.

### **Strengths:**

* Assumption of behaviours being unlearned offers potential for addiction treatments.
* Operant Conditioning acknowledges the interaction of biological and external factors in addiction.
* Classical Conditioning explains relapses, as situations associated with addiction trigger urges.

### **Weaknesses:**

* Generally neglects the role of biological factors in addiction theories.
* Fails to explain why only a small percentage becomes addicted despite exposure to similar conditions.
* Social Learning Theory suggests discontinuation of addiction if role models avoid addiction.

### **Treatments for Addiction:**

### **Drug Therapy:**

* Medication aids detoxification effects and reduces withdrawal symptoms.
* Drugs combat cravings, such as methadone for opiates, nicotine patches or gums for smokers, and naloxone for alcoholics.
* Antidepressants address underlying issues like depression.

### **Strengths:**

* Supported by research evidence.
* Addresses short-term problems effectively.

### **Weaknesses:**

* Placebo similarities in effectiveness for some medications.
* Risk of dependency on certain drugs.

### **Cognitive Behaviuoral Therapy (CBT):**

* Two stages: Functional Analysis and Skills Training.
* Functional Analysis uncovers addiction sources, and Skills Training equips patients to avoid triggers by teaching them skills to cope with Addiction)
* Patient maintains a diary to discuss feelings in CBT sessions.

### **Strengths:**

* Empowers patients to control their behaviour.
* Backed by research evidence.

### **Weaknesses:**

* Requires patient commitment to treatment.
* Skill retention and application may vary among patients.
