## Peterson & Peterson (1959): Short-Term Retention of Individual Verbal Items

### **Background**

* We can hold information in our STM when we rehearse it over and over again
* Interference theory argues that forgetting happens when new information disrupts the retention of previous acquired material, and gets overwritten

### **Aim**

To investigate the true duration of STM by interfering the rehearsal process.

### **Sample**

* n = 24 university students
* All the students were enrolled in introductory psychology courses of Indiana University
* Participation in experiments were a course requirement

### **Procedure**

* All participants tested individually
* Each participant was read out a trigram (meaningless, three-consonant, non-recurring syllables) by the experimenter, intended to be rehearsed
* Immediately after the trigram was read out by the experimenter, then a 3-digit number was read out
* Participant had to repeat the 3-digit number that was read out and count backwards in 3’s or 4’s
* Participants had to recall the trigrams 8 times for each intervals of  3, 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18 seconds
  * in total, each participant experienced 48 trials

  * during these intervals, participants counted backwards

  * distinct trigrams were employed for each trial to reduce recognition
* There was a second variation of the experiment:
  * participant given time to rehearse the stimulus, either for 1 or 3 seconds

![Peterson and Peterson – procedure](../../../assets/f6625303118c-df06bd736f3741b71f7b15f2.webp)

### **Results**

* **Experiment 1:**
  * Longer counting backwards led to decreased accurate trigram recall
  * 80% correct recall after 3 seconds, dropping to less than 10% after 18 seconds
* **Experiment 2:**
  * Increased recall frequency compared to results of the first experiment
  * A similar decline in recall over time was observed

### **Conclusion**

* Supported the idea that STM information is lost through interference when rehearsal is prevented
  * shows the limited duration of short-term memory
  * findings reinforced the role of interference in forgetting
  * highlighted the importance of rehearsal in order for information to transfer from STM to LTM

**STRENGTHS**

**WEAKNESSES**

||
||

**Standardised procedure:** all participants were asked to do the same tasks (memorising trigrams, counting backwards in 3’s or 4’s and done between specific time intervals, and recalling the trigram seen initially), meaning this study can be replicated to test its reliability

|

**Lacks mundane realism:** memorising and recalling trigrams is not an everyday task nor a realistic way of utilising memory in real-life situations, which means the findings of the study may not reflect cases where we realistically use memory

||
||

**Trials were repeated:** each participant went through 48 trials, which meant across all participants, 1152 trials in total were conducted; this repetition found consistent results throughout all trials, meaning the study has very strong reliability

|

**Repetition of trials may lead to order effects:** each participant had to do 48 trials (which is a lot), which may lead to order effects (change in behaviour where a participant performs better due to familiarity or worse due to boredom); participants may be likely to experience fatigue effects (perform worse due to boredom), which may skew the reliability and validity of results

||
||

**Laboratory experimental design:** the research took place in the laboratory of Indiana University, suggesting high controls were put in place to isolate the IV (independent variable) and DV (dependent variable) from uncontrolled EVs (extraneous variables), which ensures only the IV has an effect on the DV, strengthening cause-and-effect conclusions

* Additionally, the use of meaningless and randomised trigrams, and ensuring each trigram shown was distinct from one another also further strengthens the cause-and-effect conclusions, as it avoids the possibility participants recall the trigrams due to putting their own personal meaning to them, or recall the trigrams due to similarity with the previous trigram

|

**Controlled setting does not reflect natural settings:** the highly-controlled laboratory setting may not represent where we normally use memory, so it may not represent the usage of memory in natural or familiar settings (like at home, school, workplace, etc), thus lacking ecological validity

||
||

**Practical application:** the study highlights the important role of rehearsal, and how any disruptions (interference) to this process inhibits the process of transferring information from STM to LTM, which in turn, leads to lesser information recalled; this means (for example) students revising for exams must ensure to avoid distractions

|

**Sample bias:** very small sample size (n=24), shared similar characteristics such as being university students at Indiana University enrolled in an introductory psychology course; this may limit its ability to cover a wider range of individual differences and the general population
