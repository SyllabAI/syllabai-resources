## Bartlett’s (1932): War of The Ghosts

### **Background**

Schema theory says people rely on their existing knowledge and cultural schemas to interpret and recall information. Bartlett, intrigued by this idea, explored how cultural schemas influence memory, particularly in the context of unfamiliar stories.

### **Aims**

To investigate:

* the nature of reconstructive memory using an unfamiliar story

* whether personal (cultural) schemas influence what is remembered from the story and how it is recalled

### **Sample**

* 20 British participants in his study (Cambridge University).
  * the participants were not familiar with Native American folk stories (War of The Ghosts)

### **Procedure**

* Participants read the War of The Ghosts (WOTG) folklore twice

* They were then required to recall the story using **serial reproduction** and **repeated reproduction**

* **Serial Reproduction:** technique involving participants retelling stories to each other, creating a chain

  *How it was implemented in this study:*

  * participant told to re-tell the story to the second participant

  * second participant re-tells the story heard to the third participant

  * chain goes on

* **Repeated Reproduction:** involves participants repeatedly retelling a story

  *How it was implemented in this study:*

  * participants asked to write out the story 15 minutes later

  * recall is tested after minutes, hours, days, months, and years later

### **Results**

* Participants exhibited changes and connections (rationalisation) in serial and repeated reproduction.
* Example: ‘Something black came out of his mouth’ transformed into “a man's dying breath” or “foaming at the mouth”
* Omissions occurred for unfamiliar or unpleasant parts of the story, particularly unfamiliar names of places
* Details became familiarised/simplified, such as ‘canoe’ became '“boat”, and ‘hunting’ became '“fishing”
* In repeated reproduction, rewrites often adhere to a similar form or the initial reproduction's outline (reproductions followed the same theme)

### **Conclusion**

* Memory is a reconstructive process in which individuals modify unfamiliar information to align it more closely with their schemas
* Memory is not accurate or can not be recalled fully
  * pre-existing knowledge and cultural understanding influence interpretation and reconstruction
    * through omission and simplifying of unfamiliar details

**STRENGTHS**

|

**WEAKNESSES**

||
||

**Can be considered ecologically valid:** Bartlett made his participants recall the story minutes, hours, days, months, and years later, which is an accurate representation on how we use memory and recall, which means findings can be applied and represent real-life scenarios

|

**Mundane realism is questionable:** the nature of the task may make the applicability of findings debatable because we do not utilise our reconstructive memory to recall/reproduce folklore stories with strange words and meaning; this means the findings may not represent an everyday situation where we use memory realistically

||
||

**Qualitative data produced, collected, and analysed:** participants had to recall details in their own words, and Bartlett analysed the themes to find the actual nature of reconstructive memory; these rich details can provide validity to the theory because the detailed reproductions and analysis provide a great deal of context on how reconstructive memory actually works

|

**Qualitative data is susceptible to bias:** Bartlett was not scientific or objective with his methods, as he analysed and determined what changed in each reproduction, meaning all conclusions were based off his subjective interpretation, and indicating the overall content of the research contains researcher bias and lacks proper validity

||
||

**Some standardisation maintained:** Bartlett kept some standardisation in his procedure, such as every participant read the same folklore and had to reproduce it after 15–30 mins, and recall it hours/days/months/years later, which means the study can be replicated to test for reliability

|

**Lacks standardised procedure to an extent:** participants were given the flexibility to read at their own pace, which may have affected accuracy of reproduction, leading to lower reliability and even impacting the validity of conclusions

||
||

**Evidence is consistent:** Bartlett replicated his research using different folklores and pictures, and found participants had the same tendency to omit and transform unfamiliar content when remembering, so this means the first researches have reliability due to its consistency, overall validating the theory of reconstructive theory

|

**Sample bias:** many argued that Bartlett only utilised Cambridge University students, which may cause confounding/participant variables such as being well-versed in English, which may have affected their ability to read and re-tell the story, compromising generalisability of results to non-English-speaking populations
