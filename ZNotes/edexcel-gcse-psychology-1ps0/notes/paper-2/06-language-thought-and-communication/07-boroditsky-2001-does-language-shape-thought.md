## Boroditsky (2001) Does Language Shape Thought?

**Background:**

* English speakers use horizontal metaphors for time (e.g., breakfast before lunch), while Mandarin speakers use vertical metaphors (e.g., lunch below breakfast).

**Aim:**

* Investigate if language shapes conceptions of time in Mandarin and English speakers.

**Experiment 1: Immediate and Long-term Impact of Spatial Metaphors**

* **Participants:**
  * 26 native English speakers and 20 native Mandarin speakers, graduate or undergraduate students at Stanford University.
  * Tested in English.
* **Procedure:**
  * Spatial prime questions (horizontal or vertical scenarios) followed by target questions about time.
  * Half spatiotemporal (before/after) and half purely temporal questions.
  * Response times recorded.
* **Results:**
  * Both groups responded quicker to spatiotemporal questions following horizontal primes.
  * English speakers:
    * Answered purely temporal questions faster after horizontal primes.
    * Answered spatiotemporal questions faster than purely temporal questions.
  * Mandarin speakers:
    * Answered purely temporal questions faster after vertical primes.
    * Answered spatiotemporal questions faster after horizontal primes.
* **Conclusion:**
  * Native English speakers tend to think horizontally about time (faster after horizontal primes).
  * Native Mandarin speakers tend to think vertically about time (faster after vertical primes).
  * Mandarin speakers maintained their time concept even when tested in English, showing the influence of native language on time perception.


|Strengths|Weaknesses|
|:---|:---|
|Experimental Method: use of controlled variables|Only studied time and didn’t consider any other concepts|
|The findings were all supported by the three experiments conducted, which means they are reliable and strengthen the results|Limited Generalisability; with potential bias with all the sample being from Stanford University|
||Doesn’t represent real-life thinking- with our thoughts usually not being primed to answer questions, so the study lacks validity.|
