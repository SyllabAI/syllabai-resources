## Concept of Self and Self-Concept

* **Self-concept** encompasses our perception of ourselves, influenced by others' responses and beliefs.
* According to American psychologist Michael Lewis, the self-concept evolves with age and is shaped by life experiences.
* **Lewis (1990)** categorizes the self into two components:
  * **Existential self:**
    * Emerges around two months old, preceding verbal expression.
    * It denotes fundamental self-awareness, recognizing separation from others and the environment.
    * A stable aspect of the self-concept sustained throughout one's life, evolving through interactions with the surroundings.
    * Example: A child begins to distinguish sensations, such as the impact of hitting a parent or a toy, versus hitting their leg, forming a concept of separation.
  * **Categorical Self:**
    * Develops around 18 months.
    * Children start defining themselves using categories like age or gender.
    * Categories expand to include roles like a friend or honest person.
    * Example: A child engaged in activities like football may identify strongly with that, and over time, other interests or roles may replace or complement the initial identity.
* **Carl Rogers' Theory of Self:**
  * Childhood experiences and interactions nurture the Rogerian self-concept.

  * **Consists of three elements:**

    1. **Self-image:** Perception of one's identity.
    2. **Self-esteem:** Evaluation of personal value.
    3. **Ideal self:** Aspiration for who one desires to become.

  * Incongruence arises when the ideal self diverges from the self-image, negatively impacting mental health.

  * Congruence occurs when the ideal self aligns with the self-image, creating a harmonious self-perception.
* **Self-actualisation** pertains to realizing one's potential.
* Rogers emphasises that self-actualization is attainable when the components of the self are congruent, posing a challenge when discrepancies exist.
