## Vohrs and Schooler (2008) The Value of Believing in Free Will

**Aim(s):** To investigate whether participants would cheat if they believed human behavior was predetermined.

**Experiment One:**

* **Procedure:**
  * Sample consisted of 30 undergraduate students (13 female, 17 male).
  * Participants individually entered the laboratory and were randomly assigned to conditions.
    * **Condition A (Anti-Free-Will):** Read a passage claiming free will was an illusion.
    * **Condition B (Control Group):** Read a passage discussing consciousness without mentioning free will.
  * After reading, participants completed two questionnaires assessing beliefs and mood.
    * Free will and determinism scale.
    * Positive and negative affectivity schedule.
  * Completed computer-based mental arithmetic problems with a rigged setup to measure cheating behavior.
* **Results:**
  * Anti-free-will condition showed weaker free will beliefs (mean score of 13.6) compared to the control group (mean score of 16.8).
  * Mood scores after reading passages did not differ.
  * Anti-free-will participants cheated more on mental arithmetic (mean score of 14.00 vs. 9.97).
  * Strong negative correlation between cheating and free will scores.
* **Conclusions:**
  * Weakening belief in free will increased cheating.
  * Exposure to anti-free-will content altered views and actions.
  * Raises concerns for moral behavior development if deterministic perceptions are promoted.

**Experiment Two:**

* Participants paid themselves more money for a task after reading deterministic statements.
* Showed active cheating behavior.

**Strengths and Weaknesses:**


|Strengths|Weaknesses|
|:---|:---|
|Controlled experimental design.|Small sample size.|
|Random assignment of participants.|Limited generalizability to broader populations.|
|Use of multiple measures (questionnaires, tasks).|Lack of ecological validity in rigged computer setup.|
|Replication in Experiment Two adds validity.|Potential demand characteristics in the setup.|
|Strong correlation between cheating and beliefs.|Results may not capture real-world moral behavior fully.|
