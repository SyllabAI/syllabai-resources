## Types of Sampling Techniques


|Sampling Technique|Definition|Strengths|Weaknesses|
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
|Volunteer Sampling|Participants voluntarily choose to be part of the sample, often leading to convenience and easy data collection.|Convenient and easy to implement. Quick data collection.|It may not represent the broader population. Volunteer bias: participants may differ from non-volunteers.|
|Opportunity Sampling|The sample consists of individuals who are conveniently available at the time of the study.|Convenient and easy to implement. Useful when access to a specific population is limited.|It may lack diversity. It may not be representative of the entire population.|
|Stratified Sampling|Population is divided into subgroups (strata) based on certain characteristics, and samples are randomly selected from each stratum.|Ensures representation from various subgroups. Provides more accurate results for specific subgroups.|Complex and time-consuming. Requires accurate classification of individuals into strata.|
|Random Sampling|Everyone in the population has an equal chance of being selected, ensuring a truly random and unbiased sample.|Eliminates researcher bias. Results in a representative sample if done correctly.|Challenging to implement with large populations. Time-consuming and resource-intensive. Not suitable for small populations where every individual is crucial.|
|Systematic Sampling|Regular pattern in choosing the sample, with a starting point selected randomly.|Simple and easy to implement. Suitable for large populations.|May introduce bias if there is an underlying pattern in the population. Potential for periodic patterns, leading to skewed results.|
|Cluster Sampling|Certain areas are chosen as sampling frames, and random samples are taken from these chosen areas.|Cost-effective for geographically dispersed populations. It is useful when a complete sampling frame is not available.|Potential for less precision compared to other methods and May result in less diversity within clusters.|
