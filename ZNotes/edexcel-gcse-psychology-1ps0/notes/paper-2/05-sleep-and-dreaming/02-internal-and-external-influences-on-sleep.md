## Internal and External influences on sleep

* Biological Rhythms: Regular contrasts in biological activity, including circadian and ultradian rhythms.
* Circadian Rhythms: 24-hour cycles influencing sleep-wake patterns.
* Ultradian Rhythms: Biological cycles shorter than 24 hours.

### Sleep Stages:

* Internal Influences (Endogenous):
  * Suprachiasmatic Nuclei (SCN):
    * Affected by internal and external factors.
    * Key role in setting biological clock.
  * Circadian Rhythms:
    * Govern sleep-wake patterns.
    * Evidence from animal and human studies.
  * Ultradian Rhythms:
    * Drives sleep cycles, including REM and NREM stages.
    * Involves hormones like melatonin and ACTH.
  * Melatonin and Pineal Gland:
    * Melatonin sets circadian rhythms.
    * Pineal gland produces melatonin.
* External Influences (Exogenous):
  * Light's Effect:
    * Primary zeitgeber affecting sleep-wake cycle.
    * Light exposure influences internal clock.
    * Jet lag and shift work disruptions.
  * Zeitgebers:
    * External cues synchronizing biological clock.
    * Entrainment theory (Duffy & Czeisler, 2009).
    * Light as a primary zeitgeber.

### Strengths and Weaknesses:

### Internal Influences of Sleep:

* Strengths:
  * Animal studies support SCN's role.
  * Evidence from human blindness (Miles et al., 1977).
  * Melatonin's role in controlling sleep (Chen et al., 2015).
* Weaknesses:
  * Generalization challenges from animal studies.
  * Variability in human blindness conditions.

### External Influences of Sleep:

* Strengths:
  * Practical applications supporting bodily clocks.
  * Light as the primary zeitgeber (Mistlberger & Skene, 2004).
* Weaknesses:
  * Inconsistent findings on the role of light.
  * Variability in studies on sleep-wake cycles (Siffre, 1975).
  * Unnatural settings affecting validity.
