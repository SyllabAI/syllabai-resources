## Activation Synthesis Theory (Hobson and McCarley, 1977)

* Proposed by **Hobson and McCarley** in 1977, the Activation-Synthesis Theory suggests that dreams are a result of random brain activation during REM sleep and the brain's attempt to synthesize or make sense of these random neural signals.

**Neurobiological Context:**

Understanding the neurobiological context is crucial for grasping how the brain generates and processes dream content. Neurons, the fundamental cells of the nervous system, play a central role in transmitting information. The key components include:

* **Neuron:** A cell in the nervous system responsible for processing and transmitting information through chemical and electrical signals. Responds to stimuli in the environment, contributing to neural activity.
* **Axon:** The 'cable' extending from a neuron's cell body to terminal buttons, facilitating the transmission of messages to other neurons, muscles, or glands.
* **Impulse:** The electrical signal, also known as action potential, travels from the cell body down the axon to the terminal buttons, releasing neurotransmitters.
* **Dendrites:** Structures that receive messages from other cells.
* **Terminal Branches:** Form junctions with other cells, facilitating communication.
* **Neurotransmitter:** A chemical released by an impulse at the terminal button, entering the synaptic gap.
* **Synaptic Gap:** The gap between the dendrites of one neuron and the next.
* **Synaptic Transmission:** The process wherein a neurotransmitter crosses the synaptic gap, influencing the dendrites of the next neuron.

### **Brain Signal Transmission:**

1. An electrical impulse, or action potential, is triggered from the cell body of one neuron, travelling down the axon to the terminal buttons.
2. At the end of the axon, a neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic gap located between neurons.
3. The neurotransmitter traverses the synaptic gap, reaching the dendrites of the next neuron and continuing the message.
4. If the receptors at the dendrites of the next neuron are receptive, they pick up the neurotransmitter.
5. The neurotransmitter triggers an electrical signal by altering the chemical balance at the receptor and then returns to the synaptic gap.
6. Receptors initiate an electrical impulse from the cell body, repeating the cycle as the signal travels down the axon.

**Key Components:**

* **Random Activation:**
  * Dreams originate from the brain's random neural activity during REM sleep.
  * No intentional or meaningful purpose behind dream content.
* **Sensory Blockade:**
  * During dreaming, the brain blocks external sensory input, ensuring that dream content is internally generated.
  * Allows for subjective, experiential dream scenarios.
* **Movement Inhibition:**
  * Neurons responsible for motor control are inhibited during REM sleep.
  * Explains the lack of physical enactment of dreams during sleep.
