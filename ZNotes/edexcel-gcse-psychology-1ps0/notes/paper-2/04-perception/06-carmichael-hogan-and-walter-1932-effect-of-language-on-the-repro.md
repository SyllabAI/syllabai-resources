## Carmichael, Hogan and Walter (1932) Effect of Language on the Reproduction of Visually Perceived Form

**Aims:**

* Investigate the impact of words used to describe an ambiguous object on participants' subsequent redrawings of the image.
* Specifically focus on the influence of language on how visual images are perceived, controlling for other potential influences.

**Procedure:**

* 95 participants divided into 3 groups (48 in Group 1, 38 in Group 2, 9 in Control).
* Visual display of 121 stimulus figures on a white cardboard disc.
* Words/phrases heard before the picture appeared for Groups 1 and 2.
* Group 1 heard the first version of the word list, Group 2 heard the second version, and the Control group saw pictures without hearing any words.
* Participants asked to draw the pictures after viewing and could see them again until clear.
* Group 1 and 2 took 2-8 showings, average of 3; Control group had an average of 4 showings.
* Researchers independently rated drawings on a 5-point scale for accuracy.

**Results:**

* Focus on drawings rated as 5 (most distorted).
* In Group 1, 74% resembled the shape described; in Group 2, 73% showed a strong resemblance; in the Control group, only 45% looked like items named in the word lists.

**Conclusions:**

* Description of a visual image affects how it is reproduced, indicating that words influenced participants' perception of the images.

**Strengths:**

* Inclusion of a control group to distinguish between the effect of word lists and the inherent ambiguity of the pictures.
* Independent ratings by researchers for accuracy and similarity to word list descriptions, enhancing reliability.

**Weaknesses:**

* Participants could view pictures multiple times, potentially influencing accuracy and making results unreliable.
* Ratings done by researchers, introducing potential bias, as they were aware of the study's aims; lack of blind assessment.
