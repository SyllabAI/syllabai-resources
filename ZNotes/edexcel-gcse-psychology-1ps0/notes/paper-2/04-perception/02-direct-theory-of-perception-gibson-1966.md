## Direct Theory of Perception (Gibson, 1966)

* **Optic Array:**
  * The optic array refers to the pattern of light that reaches the eyes from the environment. It includes all the visual information available to an observer at any given moment.
  * Gibson proposed that the optic array is a rich source of information for perception. Changes in the optic array, such as motion and patterns, provide immediate cues about the structure and layout of the environment.
* **Perception - Bottom-Up Processing:**
  * Bottom-up processing involves the analysis of sensory information from the environment to form perceptions. It starts with the basic sensory input and progresses to higher-level perceptual experiences.
  * Gibson's approach emphasizes that perception is driven by the information present in the environment. Instead of relying on pre-existing mental structures or internal representations, bottom-up processing involves direct sensory input for perception.
* **Invariant Features:**
  * Invariant features are stable properties of the environment that remain constant despite changes in the observer's perspective or conditions.
  * Gibson argued that organisms perceive invariant features, such as the constant ratios of object sizes and shapes. These invariant features provide a stable basis for perception even as the observer moves through the environment.
* **Affordances:**
  * Affordances are the action possibilities or opportunities for interaction that the environment offers to an organism. They are properties of objects that suggest how they can be used or manipulated.
  * Gibson introduced the concept of affordances to highlight that the environment provides direct information about how it can be utilized. For example, a chair affords sitting, and a doorknob affords turning.

### Application to Everyday Life

* Example: In everyday life, understanding the affordances of objects helps individuals interact with their surroundings more effectively. For instance, recognizing that a chair affords sitting allows a person to use it for that purpose. Navigating through spaces, understanding the layout based on optic array information, and perceiving stable features contribute to efficient daily activities.

### **Strengths:**

* Ecological Validity: Gibson's theory emphasizes studying perception in real-world contexts, enhancing ecological validity.
* Direct Perception: The idea of direct perception without the need for extensive cognitive processing aligns with the immediacy of everyday experiences.

### **Weaknesses:**

* Limited Explanation of Higher Cognition: Gibson's theory focuses more on lower-level processes, providing less insight into complex cognitive functions.
* Underemphasis on Learning and Experience: The theory underplays the role of learning and experience in shaping perception.
