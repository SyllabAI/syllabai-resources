## Surds

* Surds are irrational numbers that cannot be expressed as simple fractions and are often left in root form. Specifically, a surd is a root that produces an irrational number.

* Example: $\sqrt{2}$

### Simplifying Surds

* We can simplify surds by prime factorizing the number under the root and simplifying

* Example: $ \sqrt{72} =\sqrt{36 \times 2}= \sqrt{36} \times \sqrt {2} = 6\sqrt{2} $

### Operations with Surds

* ### **Addition and Subtraction**

  * We can only add or subtract surds when the value under its root is same (like terms)

  * Example : $3\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2} $

    * “Unlike surds” can’t be combined, for example:  $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ cannot be simplified further

* ### **Multiplication**

  * $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{a \times b}$

  * Example : $2\sqrt{3} \times 4\sqrt{5} = (2 \times 4) \sqrt{3 \times 5} = 8\sqrt{15} $

* ### **Division**

  * $\sqrt{a} \div \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{\dfrac{a}{b}}$
  * $\sqrt{36} \div \sqrt{6} = \sqrt{\dfrac{36}{6}} = \sqrt{6}$

### Rationalizing the denominator

* This process involves converting the denominator, which is originally in the surd form into a whole number by

  multiplying the numerator and denominator with a suitable surd

Example:

* Rationalize $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{2}}$

  * $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{2}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} $
  * $= \dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$

* Rationalize $\dfrac{3}{2+\sqrt{5}} $

  * $ \dfrac{3}{2+\sqrt{5}} \times \dfrac{2-\sqrt{5}}{2-\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{3(2-\sqrt{5})}{4-5}$
  * $ = \dfrac{6-3\sqrt{5}}{-1} = -6 +3\sqrt{5} $
