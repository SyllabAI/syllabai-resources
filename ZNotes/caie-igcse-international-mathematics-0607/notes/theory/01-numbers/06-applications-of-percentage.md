## **Applications of Percentage**

### **Expressing one quantity as a percentage of another**

* Divide the first quantity by the second and multiply by 100

* Example: Express 4 as a percentage of 20

  * So, $4 \div 20= \dfrac{1}{5}$

  * Then multiply by 100 = $\dfrac{1}{5} \times 100 = 20 %$

### **Increase and decrease in percentage**

$$
\boxed {\text{Percentage Change} = \dfrac{\text{Change in amount }}{\text{Original amount }} \times 100}
$$

### **Reverse Percentage**

* Finding the **original amount before** a percentage increase or decrease

$$
\boxed{\text{Original value} = \dfrac{\text{Final value}}{1\pm \frac{p}{100}}}
$$

* $p$ = change in percentage
* Use $+$ when there is a increase
* Use $-$ when there is a decrease

**Example**: After a 20% increase, the value is 600, find the original

$\text{Original value} = \dfrac{6000}{1+ \frac{20}{100}} = 500$

### **Percentage Applications in Finance**

**Simple Interest (SI)**

* It’s the extra amount paid or earned over time without compounding, and it’s calculated only from the original amount

$$
\boxed {SI = \dfrac{P \times R \times N}{100}}
$$

* $P$ = Principal amount (original amount)

* $R$ = Rate of intrest

* $N$ = No. of years

$$
A = P + SI
$$

* $A$ = Total amount

* $P$ = Principal amount (original amount)

* $SI$ = Interest amount earned or paid

**Compound Interest (CI)**

* It is the interest that grows on the principal amount (original amount) and the accumulated amount of the previous years

* It grows faster than simple interest

$$
\boxed {A = P\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^N}
$$

* $A$ = Total amount

* $P$ = Principal amount (original amount)

* $R$ = Rate of intrest

* $N$ = No. of years

$$
CI = A - P
$$
