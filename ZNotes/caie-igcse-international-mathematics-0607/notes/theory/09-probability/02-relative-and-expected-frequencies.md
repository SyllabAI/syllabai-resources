## Relative and Expected frequencies

### **Relative frequency**

* Shows how often an event happens compared to the total number of trials.

* Its an estimate of probability

$$

\boxed{\text{Relative Frequency} =
\dfrac{\text{Number of times Event E occurs}}{\text{Total number of trials}}}

$$

* Example:

  * Suppose you flip a coin 100 times and get 55 heads

  * The relative frequency of getting heads is: $\dfrac{55}{100} = 0.55$

### **Expected frequency**

* Also known as theoretical probability

* Expected frequency is the number of times an event is expected to occur based on its probability

* It is used when the probability is known

$$

\boxed {\text{Expected frequency} = \text{Probability of event} \times \text{Number of Trials}}

$$

* Example: If the probability of rain is 0.3 over 40 days:

  * Expected frequency = $0.3 \times 40 = 12$
