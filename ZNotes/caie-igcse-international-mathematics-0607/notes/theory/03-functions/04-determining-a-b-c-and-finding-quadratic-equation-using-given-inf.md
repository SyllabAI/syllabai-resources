## Determining $a,b,c$ and finding quadratic equation using given information

### **1. Given vertex and another point**

* Use the vertex to write the quadratic equation in $y= a(x-h)^2 + k$

* Substitute the values of vertex and $(x_1, y_1)$ in the equation,  expand and solve to find $a$

* Substitute the $a$ value in the equation to find $b$ and $c$ by expanding

* Example:

  * Given the vertex $(2, -3)$ and a point $(4, 5)$.

  * Using vertex form, $f(x)=a(x-2)^2-3$

  * Substitute the point $(4,5)$:

    $5=a(4-2)^2-3$

  * Simplify:

    $5=4a-3$

    $4a=8$

    $a=2$

  * $\therefore$ the quadratic equation is $f(x)=2(x-2)^2-3$

    If required, the expanded form is $f(x)=2x^2-8x+5$

### 2.**Given vertex and another point, where a=1**

* Do it normally like mentioned above by substituting the values of vertex in the equation , expand and solve to find $a$, 

  then use value of $a$ in the equation to find $b$ and $c$ by expanding

* Example:

  * Given the vertex $(1, 2)$ and a point $(3,6)$.

  * Using vertex form, $f(x)=a(x-1)^2+2$

  * Substitute the point $(3,6)$:

    $6=a(3-1)^2 +2$

  * Simplify :

    $6=4a+2$

    $4=4a$

    $a=1$

  * $\therefore$ the quadratic equation is $f(x)=(x-1)^2+2$

    If required, the expanded form is $f(x)=x^2-2x+1$

### **3. Given x-intercept and a point**

* Write the quadratic in factor form: $f(x)=a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)$

* Substitute the values and solve the system of equation to find $a$

* Then substitute $a$ and expand if required

* Example:

  * Given the x-intercepts $x=1$ and $x=3$, and a point $(0, 6)$

  * Since the x-intercepts are $1$ and $3$, the quadratic can be written as $f(x)=a(x-1)(x-3)$

  * Substitute the point $(0, 6)$:\
    $6=a(0-1)(0-3)$

    $6=3a$.

    $a=2$.

  * $\therefore$ the quadratic equation is $f(x)=2(x-1)(x-3)$.

    If required, the expanded form is $f(x)=2x^2-8x+6$.

### 4.**Given x-intercept and a point, where a=1**

* Solve normally like mentioned above, by substituting the roots (x-intercepts) and the point in factor form $f(x)=a(x-x_1)(x-x_2)$ and solving 

* Example: 

  * Given the x-intercepts $x=1$ and $x=4$, and a point $(2 ,-2)$

  * Since the x-intercepts are $1$ and $4$, the quadratic can be written as $f(x)=a(x-1)(x-4)$

  * Substitute the point $(2, -2)$:

    $-2=a(2-1)(2-4)$

    $-2=a(1)(-2)$

    $a=1$

  * $\therefore$ the quadratic equation is $f(x)=1(x-1)(x-4)$.

    If required, the expanded form is $f(x) = x^2-5x+4$
