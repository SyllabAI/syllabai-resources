## Magnification & Mathematics

- Magnification, M = $$\frac{\text{Image size (I)}}{\text{Actual size (A)}}$$

### Calculating the magnification of a photomicrograph or an image

- Measure the width of the cell in mm on the photomicrograph using
  a ruler

- Convert the measurement from millimeter to micrometer.

- Apply magnification equation, using given value of the actual
  size.

### Calculating magnification from a scale bar

- Measure the given scale bar using ruler

- Convert the measurement from millimeter to micrometer

- Apply the magnification equation

### Calculating the real size of an object from its magnification

- Measure the observed length of the image in mm using a ruler

- Convert the measurement from millimeter to micrometer

- Apply the equation to calculate the actual length, using given
  value of the magnification.

### Calculating the magnification of a microscope

- Check the magnification of the lens
- Check the magnification of the eyepiece
- multiply the two together

### When counting in grids

- count all full squares.
- For the partial squares, split the squares into half (this step is for extra accuracy), if that square is covered more than half then count the square as one. divide the number of partial squares by 2
- The final answer should be a whole number or 0.5
- Smaller grid size increases the accuracy of measurements.
- The calibrated eyepiece graticule can be used to measure the
  diameter of a certain area of field view. It can then be used to
  find area of field view.
- It can be used to measure the lengths of cells in certain areas.

### Improving the accuracy

* Take at least 5 measurements to increase the accuracy of results.
- $\text{error} = \frac{\text{No. of readings}\ \times \ \text{Half of smallest scale division}}{\text{Total reading}} \times 100$%
- $\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum of data}}{\text{No. of data}}$
  - Useful for replicated readings.
- $\text{Gradient} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$, where
  $\Delta y$ & $\Delta x$ are height and width of triangle.
- Draw a right-angled triangle from 2 points on a straight line graph or a tangent on the curve; Ensure that the triangle exceeds half of the graph.
- $\text{change} = \ \frac{\text{Final}\ –\ \text{Initial}}{\text{Initial}} \times 100$%
- It makes comparing easier by negating effects of differences in initial readings between samples.
- Error in a measurement is half of the value of
  the smallest division on the scale you are reading from.
- If you read from two positions in the scale, i.e., initial, and final reading, the error is multiplied by 2 because the error in measurements will occur twice

### Percentage uncertainty

$$\text{percentage of uncertainty} = \frac{\text{uncertainty}}{\text{actual measurement}} \times100$$
