## Common indicators
1. Effect of litmus:
  - In acidic $\rightarrow$ Turns Red
  - In alkaline\base $\rightarrow$ Turns Blue

2. Universal Indicator

| **pH** | **Description** | **Colour** |
| -------- | -------- | -------- |
| 0-3    | Strong acid    | Red     |
| 3-6    | acid  | Orange/yellow    |
| 7    | Neutral   | Green     |
| 8-11    | Alkaline    | Blue    |
| 11-14    | Strong Alkali   | Violet/purple    |

3. Cobalt Chloride paper 
  - In presence of no water $\rightarrow$ Blue 
  - In presence of water $\rightarrow$ Pink/mauve

4. Hydrogen carbonate indicator: 
  - original color is red
  - increase in CO2 $\rightarrow$ yellow color (it makes medium acidic as CO2 is an acidic gas)
  - decrease in CO2 $\rightarrow$ purple color (removal makes medium alkaline)

5. Methylene blue: Oxygen indicator:
  - In presence of Oxygen $\rightarrow$ blue color
  - No oxygen present $\rightarrow$ decolorises

6. Bromothymol blue pH indicator: 
  - it changes color over a pH range of 6.0(Yellow) to 7.6 (Blue)
  - it is a good indicator of dissolved CO2 and other weakly acidic solutions

7. Potassium permanganate: 
  - it’s a strong oxidizing agent. Dissolving in water to give an intense purple solution
  - concentrated sulfuric acid reacts with potassium permanganate forming an acidic solution
  - acidic solutions of permanganate are reduced to a faintly pink manganese(II)
