## Effect of inhibitors on $V_\text{max}$ and $K_\text{m}$

### **Competitive inhibitor**

* As the enzyme is more likely to bind to the inhibitor so it has higher affinity to the inhibitor so lower affinity to the substrate as affinity reduce so $K_\text{m}$ increases. $V_\text{max}$ is unchanged as when you add more substrates the reaction is reversible.

  * Affinity decreases (love for enzyme)

    * "Strength of attraction" between an enzyme’s active site and its substrate.

    * High Affinity: The enzyme and substrate "love" each other.

  * $V_\text{max}$ (amount of working enzymes) is unchanged

  * $K_\text{m}$ increases ( amount of substrate At half of the Vmax)

### **Non-competitive inhibitor**

* As the inhibitor binds to the allosteric site and disrupts the weak bonds so ruin the active site, it can no longer bind to the substrate so reduces the $V_\text{max}$, however even if you increase the concentration of substrate nothing changed so affinity is left unchanged so Km is also unchanged.

  * $K_\text{m}$ is unchanged

  * $V_\text{max}$ reduces

  * Affinity is unchanged
