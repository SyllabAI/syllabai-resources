## Mode of action of enzymes 

### **Lock and key Theory**

* Each enzyme has an active site which has its specific shape and is perfectly complementary to the substrate forming an enzyme-substrate complex.

* When products are released, active site is free and can be reused; this is called the lock-and-key mechanism its as shown.

  * E.g. Sucrase has a rigid shape that only fits sucrose. If you try to fit lactose in there, the shapes are not complementary and no Enzyme-Substrate complex forms

    ![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/206007cec95dafaba6da90d0.webp =685x)

### Induced fit mechanism 

* This is how enzymes actually behave.

* Where the enzyme is partially complementary to the substrate, so the enzyme moulds and folds to make it complementary and give an enzyme-substrate complex.

* Temporary hydrogen bonds hold the substrate to the active site of the enzyme.

  * E.g. Lysozyme (the enzyme in your tears that kills bacteria). As the bacterial cell wall enters the active site, lysozyme wraps around it physically stretching the bonds in the bacterial sugar chain until they snap.
