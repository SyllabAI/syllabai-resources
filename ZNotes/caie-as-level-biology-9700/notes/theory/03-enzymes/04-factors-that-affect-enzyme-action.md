## Factors that affect enzyme action:

1. ### Temperature

   ![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/d70f86f33c4b510c900214ee.webp =657x)

   * Before optimum temperature

     * Raising temperature up to optimum temperature increases the rate.

     * As molecules gain more kinetic energy and move faster.

     * So more collisions and increases the chances of successful collisions.

   * At optimum temperature

     * Enzyme works at its fastest rate.

     * Maximum number of successful collisions.

   * After optimum temperature

     * Rate slows down as enzyme starts to denature and loses its active site (hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, and hydrophobic interactions break **NOT** disulfide or peptide bonds).

2. ### pH

   * Each enzyme has a specific pH where it works at its maximum known as optimum pH.

   * If pH increases or decreases rate of reaction slows down as active site loses its shape.

   * Change in pH meaning change in concentration of hydrogen ions so change in charge of R group.

   * Weak bonds (hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, and hydrophobic interactions break **NOT** disulfide or peptide bonds).![Add a little bit of body text](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/a6a0e830c53d1e380fe81c6c.webp =653x)

3. ### Enzyme concentration

   * Increasing enzyme concentration will increase collisions so higher chances of successful collisions with substrate.

   * Increase number of enzyme substrate complexes formed.

   * Above a certain point enzyme no longer becomes limiting factor and graph levels off which means that all substrates are used up.

4. ### Substrate concentration

   * Increasing substrate concentration will increase collisions so higher chances of successful collisions with enzyme.

   * Increase number of enzyme substrate complexes formed.

   * Above a certain point substrate is no longer becomes limiting factor and graph levels off which means that all active sites are occupied.

5. ### Inhibitor concentration

   * Inhibitor interfere with enzyme activity and reduce the rate.

   * So increasing concentration of inhibitor reduces rate of reaction.
