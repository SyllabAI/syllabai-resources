## Mode of Action of Catalase and Amylase

### Effect of Catalase on Hydrogen Peroxide

* Catalase is an enzyme found in almost every living cell.

* Catalase catalyzes the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide($H_2O_2$), a toxic metabolic byproduct, into water ($H_2O$) and oxygen($O_2$), which are non-toxic.

* Progress of this reaction is measured by the rate of formation of products

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/80f29d6ea5e43c947b81e8ea.webp =672x)

While gas production can be measured by counting bubbles, collecting the volume of oxygen gas per unit time is more accurate.

**Why you should measure rate by volum**e:

* Bubbles vary in size, leading to inconsistent gas volume data.

* High probability of miscounting.

### Effect of Amylase on starch

**Experiment:**

* Add few drops of iodine into every well of the spotting tile

* In a set of test tubes label and add $3 cm^{3}$ of different concentrations of starch to every test tube.

* Add buffer solution to amylase to let pH=7.

* Place the amylase and starch tubes in a water bath at for 5 minutes to ensure they are at the correct temperature.

* Add $1 cm^{3}$ of amylase to the first test tube and start stopwatch.

* Every 10–30 seconds, use a pipette to take a small sample of the mixture and add it to a fresh drop of iodine on the tile.

* Record the time when the blue-black color no longer appears.

* Repeat for all starch concentrations.

* The progress of this reaction is measured by the rate of disappearance of substrate

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/297782d5a8bba8c115152198.webp =648x)

### Use of Colorimeter

* Measures the absorbance or transmission of specific light wavelengths through a solution to determine the concentration of a solute.

* You can measure the intensity of blue black obtained and plot a graph.

* Used due to different color judgement by eye.

* When using a colorimeter for this test, you must use a red filter because the blue-black starch-iodine complex absorbs red light most strongly.
