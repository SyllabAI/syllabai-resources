## $V_\text{max}$ and Michaelis–Menten constant ($K_\text{m}$)

### $V_\text{max}$

* Level at which all enzyme molecules bind to the substrates

  * Even if you add more substrate, the reaction won't go any faster because there are no free workers active sites left.

* **Maximum possible rate** of an enzyme-controlled reaction.

* Highest point where the curve levels off (the plateau).

### Michaelis–Menten constant ($K_\text{m}$)

* Used to compare enzymes’ affinity to the substrate

* Substrate level at which reaction rate is half of that of the $V_\text{max}$

  * $V_\text{max}$ used to derive $K_\text{m}$. Find $\frac{1}{2}$ $V_\text{max}$ then determine the substrate concentration at this rate. This substrate concentration is $K_\text{m}$.

  * $\frac{V_\text{max}}{2}$ = $\frac{1}{2}$ $V_\text{max}$

* The higher the affinity to the substrate the lower the substrate concentration needed so the lower the $K_\text{m}$

* Low $K_\text{m}$ = High Affinity

* High $K_\text{m}$= Low Affinity

* Think of it as a person if they like their job a lot they wouldn’t mind low pay ( high affinity for the job so low $K_\text{m}$, however if the person hates their job they would need a high pay from it.
