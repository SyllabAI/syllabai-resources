## Antibiotics

* Inhibit production of the cell wall and disturb cell membrane function

* Inhibit protein synthesis and affect protein activity

* Inhibit DNA synthesis

* Affect enzyme action

* 2 types :

  * **Bactericidal antibiotics** → kill bacteria directly.

  * **Bacteriostatic antibiotics** → stop/inhibit bacterial growth and reproduction, allowing the immune system to remove the bacteria.

### Antibiotic resistance and prevention

1. A large population of pathogenic bacteria contains genetic mutations.

2. When a patient takes antibiotics, non-resistant strains are killed, but any bacteria with a resistance mutation survives the selection pressure

3. Surviving bacteria reproduce rapidly through binary fission, passing on the resistance until an entire population is drug resistant

### Effect of antibiotic resistance

* **Treatment failure:** Antibiotics that previously killed bacteria may no longer work, making infections harder to treat.

* **Increased mortality:** Resistant infections can become more severe and increase the risk of death.

* **Longer hospital stays:** Patients may require longer treatment and hospitalization because infections take longer to control.

* **Higher healthcare costs:** More expensive antibiotics, additional tests, and longer hospital stays increase healthcare costs.

* **Limited treatment options:** Fewer effective antibiotics are available, making some infections increasingly difficult to treat.

* **Return of previously controlled diseases:** Diseases that were previously easily treated may become more common or difficult to control.

* **Spread of resistance:** Resistant bacteria can spread between people, communities, hospitals, and countries, making the problem widespread.

### Prevention of antibiotic resistance

* Use antibiotics only when prescribed by a qualified healthcare professional.
* Do not use antibiotics for viral infections, such as colds and flu, because antibiotics do not kill viruses.
* Complete the prescribed course of antibiotics as directed, rather than stopping early when symptoms improve.
* Do not share antibiotics with other people or use leftover antibiotics from a previous illness.
* Use the correct antibiotic and dose as prescribed, rather than choosing one yourself.
* Limit unnecessary antibiotic use in agriculture, since resistant bacteria can develop and spread from animals to humans.

### Effect of penicillin

* Prevent synthesis of cross-links between peptidoglycan polymers in the cell wall.

* Resistant bacteria produce penicillinase enzyme.

### How penicillin acts

* Blocks the enzymes called glycoprotein peptidases, which are essential for the development of bacterial cell walls.

* Bacteria secrete enzymes called autolysins that create tiny holes in their cell walls, allowing the wall to stretch and create new peptidoglycan cross-links to increase the size of the cell wall.

* Because penicillin prevents the formation of these cross-links, the cell wall weakens, and the cell absorbs water through osmosis and bursts

* Thus, penicillin only impacts bacteria during their growth.

**Why antibiotics don't affect human cells or viruses**

* Viruses are not affected by antibiotics because they:
  * Do not have peptidoglycan cell walls.

  * Do not have ribosomes.

  * Do not have their own metabolic machinery.

  * Use the host cell's machinery to reproduce.
* Human cells are generally not affected because antibiotics target structures/processes specific to bacteria:
  * Human cells do not have peptidoglycan cell walls.

  * Human cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S, unlike bacterial 70S ribosomes.

  * Humans have different metabolic pathways from bacteria.
* Therefore, antibiotics can selectively target bacteria without directly targeting viruses or human cells.
