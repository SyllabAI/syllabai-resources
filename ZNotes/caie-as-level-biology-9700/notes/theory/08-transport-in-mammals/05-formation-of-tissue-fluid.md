## Formation of tissue fluid

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/f949dc59bec8dab6eede9374.webp =791x)

* Arterial end (Ultrafiltration)

  * As blood enters capillaries from the arterioles.

  * When the heart pumps off creates a high hydrostatic pressure

  * Which is strong enough to make small molecules such as water, glucose, amino acids, ions, and oxygen out of capillary pores and into the surrounding spaces.

  * Larger components like red blood cells and most plasma proteins are to large to pass and in the blood.

* Interstitial space

  * Once outside, this fluid is now called **tissue fluid.**

  * It surrounds cells, allowing them to absorb nutrients while releasing waste into the tissue fluid

* Venous end (Reabsorption)

  * As fluid leaves the capillary, the hydrostatic pressure drops significantly.

  * However, the concentration of plasma proteins remains high, so it creates a strong blood osmotic pressure which draws water back into the capillary from the tissue fluid.

### Function of tissue fluid

* Delivers nutrients and oxygen.

* Waste removal.

* Serves as a medium for movement of hormones and chemical messengers to reach target cells.

Not all the tissue fluid returns to capillaries; some returns to lymphatic capillaries and once inside it's called lymph and it filters the fluid through lymph nodes

* Lymph nodes have a large number of lymphocytes and phagocytes
