## Bohr shift

* Displacement of oxygen dissociation curve to the right; meaning haemoglobin has lower affinity to oxygen due to the presence of a higher concentration of carbon dioxide or low PH.

* During respiration, CO₂ reacts with water to form carbonic acid, which dissociates to produce H⁺ ions.

* H⁺ ions bind to haemoglobin, causing a conformational change in its structure.

* This change reduces haemoglobin’s affinity for oxygen.

* Therefore, haemoglobin releases more oxygen to actively respiring tissues.

*  helps supply more oxygen where respiration is occurring rapidly.

* Partial pressure of oxygen is high in lungs, meaning it readily associates with oxygen to become fully saturated

  * But low in active muscle as there is a large release of oxygen so tissue receives the needed oxygen.
