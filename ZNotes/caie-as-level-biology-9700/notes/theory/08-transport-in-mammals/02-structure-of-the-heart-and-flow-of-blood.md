## Structure of the heart and flow of blood

### Structure of heart

* Atria have thin muscle walls as they don't need to pump at high pressure.

* Ventricles have thicker walls as they need to pump blood away from the heart.

* Pressure on the left side is greater than on the right side

* Septum prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood

### Flow of blood

1. The vena cava connects deoxygenated blood from the body's tissues and returns it to the right atrium. The blood then moves into the ventricle.

2. The pulmonary artery collects the deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle and moves it to the arterioles, then capillaries.

3. Gas exchange then happens, so oxygen goes inside the capillaries, and carbon dioxide moves inside the alveoli to be exhaled.

4. Oxygenated blood moves to the pulmonary vein and into the left atrium and then moves into the left ventricle.

5. The aorta pumps blood from left ventricle and pumps it to the rest of the body.
