# Cell structure

## Microscope in cell studies

### Making temporary preparations:

1. Cut a thin slice of the specimen.

   1. Using a sharp scalpel.

   2. It must be thin enough to show light to pass through it.

2. Place a drop of a suitable medium such as distilled water or a specific stain such as iodine or methylene blue onto the center of a clean slide.

3. Place the section of the specimen onto the drop of liquid (to prevent the specimen from drying out) using forceps ensuring it's flat and not folded.

4. Hold a clean coverslip at a 45 degree angle using forceps (to prevent trapping air bubbles) then gently lower it down.

5. Use a filter paper or paper towel to blot away any excess fluid leaking through the coverslip.

**When drawing:**

* Use a sharp HB pencil: as lines must be fine and sharp.

* Draw single, continuous, clear lines: avoid sketching, feathering, or using multiple broken strokes every line should be clean.

* No shading to represent  darkness, depth, or texture.

* Proportions and sizing: drawing must be large enough to occupy more than half of the space and show all the relevant structures.

* All label lines must be horizontal with no arrows and drawn with a ruler.

### Light microscope

* Uses a beam of visible light focused by glass lenses to illuminate the specimen.

* Low resolution of 200nm. Meaning 2 points closer than 200nm can't be distinguished.

* Magnification is typically up to $\times 1500$.

**Advantage of light microscope**

* Can see cell cycle happening in real time.

* Dyes can be used.

* All chromosomes can be seen.

**Disadvantages of light microscope**

* Lower resolution of only 200nm meaning 2 points closer than 200nm can't be distinguished.

* Lower magnification of only $\times 1500$

* Inability to view smaller organelles

### Electron microscope

* Uses a beam of high-energy electrons focused by electromagnets.

* High resolution of $0.5$ $\text{nm}$

* Magnification is typically up to $\times 500000$, which allows scientists to resolve fine cellular ultrastructure.

* Because air molecules scatter electrons, the entire column must be kept under a vacuum so living cells can't be viewed

* Samples must be stained using heavy metal salts. They deflect the electrons to create the contrast.

* Image output results is micrographs, which are monochrome.

Magnification is typically up to $\times 1500$
