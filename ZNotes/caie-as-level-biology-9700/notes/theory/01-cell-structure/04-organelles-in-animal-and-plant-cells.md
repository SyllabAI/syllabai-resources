## Organelles in animal and plant cells

### Cell Surface membrane

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/f5e90ffb5d0d87fef7fac885.webp =718x)

* Cell surface membrane has a thickness of 7.0 nm.

* Made up of phospholipids which have hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails which face each other so the core is hydrophobic.

* Heads are directed outwards to form bonds with the surrounding water molecules.

**Function of cell surface membrane:**

* Separates cell contents from the surrounding.

* For exchange of material.

* Enzyme catalyzed reactions.

* Cell to cell adhesion.

* Cell signaling.

* Cell recognition.

### Nucleus

* Largest organelle in the cell.

* Double-membrane-bound, about 10µm.

* Outer membrane is covered in ribosomes; inner membrane is smooth; the double membrane has nuclear pores.

* Nuclear pores are present which are gaps in the nuclear envelope that facilitate exchange between the nucleus and the cytoplasm.

**Function of nucleus :**

* The nucleolus is a tiny, rounded deeply stained structure that is responsible for the formation of ribosomes.

* Stores genetic material.

* Controls cell division.

* Controls metabolic reactions.

* **DNA packaging**

  * DNA is coiled around histone proteins.

  * Histone proteins prevent DNA from getting tangled, as histone proteins are positively charged while DNA is negatively charged.

  * DNA wraps around histone proteins to form nucleosomes.

  * Nucleosomes then condense even further and fold to form chromatin.

  * Chromatin folds and condenses further to form a chromosome.

### Golgi Body

* Formed by fusion of vesicles from rough endoplasmic reticulum.

* Stack of flattened single-membrane-bound sacs known as cisternae.

* Surrounded by vesicles.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/013d210a048f1a7911d77f50.webp =585x)

**Function of golgi body :**

* Main site for protein modification and packaging.

* Glycosylation is where a carbohydrate molecule is added to a protein molecule.

  * For example, adding a heme group to form hemoglobin.

* Forms lysosomes.

### Mitochondria

* Mitochondrion (when asked organelle write singular not plural).

* Double membrane-bound.

* Outer is smooth; inner is folded into cristae to increase surface area to volume ratio.

* The matrix consists of:

  * Circular loops of DNA

  * 70S ribosome

  * Enzymes

* **Some mitochondria may look more spherical than others due to:**

  * Different growth rates.

  * Different cut sections of the cell.

  * Different fluidity of membrane.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/3010d6efa832be65aa15c60d.webp =631x)

**Function**

* Site of aerobic respiration.

* Beta-oxidation of fats.

**Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)**

* ATP is a phosphorylated nucleotide it consists of the nitrogenous base adenine, the pentose sugar ribose, and 3 phosphate groups.

* Cellular respiration is the metabolic process by which cells break down nutrients to generate ATP.

  * Cellular respiration oxidizes glucose to release the chemical energy stored which is used to add a phosphate group to ADP, creating ATP.

* Energy is released by breaking ATP to ADP by hydrolysis.

  * Using water, producing ADP and $P_i$ and energy.

  * ${ATP}  +  {H}_2\text{O}  \rightarrow \text{ADP}  +  Pi  + \text{energy}$

  * Energy is released because the bonds in the product are more stable than the reactants

* ATP is needed for:

  * Active transport of ions against a concentration gradient across cell surface membrane.

  * Anabolic reactions such as protein synthesis.

  * Movement such as muscle contraction.

### Ribosome

* Smallest organelle in an animal cell.

* Each ribosome is made up of a large and a small subunit.

* Made of rRNA and proteins.

**Types of ribosomes:**

* 80S (22-25nm): outer nuclear membrane, Rough endoplasmic reticulum, free in the cytoplasm.

* 70S (18nm): bacterial cell, chloroplast, mitochondrion.

**Function**

* Formation of primary protein structure.

**When observing a specimen under the microscope, distinguish key organelles:**

* Nucleus: look for a large, dense structure with a double membrane and nuclear pores; it's usually the organelle that absorbs the most of the stain.

* Mitochondrion: identified by its double membrane, where the inner membrane is folded into **cristae,** increasing surface area.

* Chloroplast: look for grana connected by intergranal thylakoids.

* Ribosomes: appear as tiny, dense dots.

* Endoplasmic reticulum: identify whether it is smooth or rough by identifying the presence of ribosomes.
