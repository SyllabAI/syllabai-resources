## Magnification

* How much an image has been enlarged than the actual size.

* It does not have units e.g. $\times 10$.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/1190d68eea62a57734439bab.webp =610x)

### Worked out example:

Size of the image: 25mm

Magnification $\times 100000$

### Calculations:

* Actual size $= \frac{\text{image size}}{\text{magnification}}$

* $\frac{25 \times 1000}{100{,}000}$

* 25 is multiplied by 1000 as the size should be converted to $\mu\text{m}$.

## Resolution

* Ability of a microscope to distinguish between 2 separate points.

* 0.5nm for electron microscope and 200nm for light microscope.

* Electron microscope has a higher resolution due to shorter wavelength of light.

Limit of resolution

* Half the wavelength of radiation used to view the specimen.

### Eyepiece graticule

* Fitted inside of a microscope.

* Measured in eyepiece units (EPU).

* EPU’s represent different lengths so we have to calibrate the graticule.

* The stage graticule which is placed on the stage is used to calibrate the eyepiece graticule.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/57043c96126fef00e5ec2e19.webp =609x)

How to measure the EPU

* The stage micrometer is the top ruler (0.1mm for each division)

* Look where they match first at 10 then at 50 on the eyepiece graticule (40 EPU)

* $\frac{0.1}{40}$=0.0025mm

* 0.0025x1000=2.5$\mu\text{m}$

* Don’t forget to always convert to $\mu\text{m}$ in the end
