## Protein structure

1. ### Primary Structure

   * Linear sequence of amino acids held together in a polypeptide chain by peptide bonds.

   * Slight change can affect the protein structure.

   * Unique sequence of amino acids for each protein.

   * Think of it as a straight wire with no bending at all.

2. ### Secondary Structure

   * Arrangement of polypeptide chain from coiling or folding.

   * Held by hydrogen bonds (shown as a dashed line).

   ![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/d060fb36fc2c4c84f0bb7d91.webp =684x)

   * Producing alpha helix or beta pleated sheets.

   * Think of it as twisting a wire by coiling or folding.

   ![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/830fcb3ceecc28680f11b362.webp =735x)

3. ### Tertiary

   * Amino acid chain is folded further.

   * Think of it as twisting a long wire by both coiling and folding.

   * These bonds can be either hydrogen bonds or ionic bonds or disulfide bonds or hydrophobic interactions.

4. ### Quaternary

   * Only found in protein which contain more than one polypeptide chain such as hemoglobin and collagen.
   * Think of it as 1 or more wires coiled up together.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/38cee0687c427c2b0ec0a74c.webp =843x)

### Denaturation of protein:

* Loss of the 3D shape.

* Due to breaking of hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, and hydrophobic interactions.

* Peptide bonds and disulfide never break.

* Disulfide bonds may only break due to addition of reducing agent.
