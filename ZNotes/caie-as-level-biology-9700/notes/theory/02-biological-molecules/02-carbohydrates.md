## Carbohydrates

### Monosaccharides:

* $(CH_22O)_n$

* Pentose n=5

  * Ribose which is the sugar in RNA
  * Deoxyribose which is the sugar in DNA

* Hexose n=6

  * Glucose, galactose, and fructose

**$\alpha$ glucose**

* In $\alpha$ glucose, the hydroxyl group attached to the anomeric carbon/ carbon 1(C1) is positioned downwards.

  * This orientation is determined by the folding of the open-chain glucose molecule.

    * The -OH group on C1 and the -CH$_2$OH group on C5 are on **opposite** sides of the ring.

**$\beta$ glucose**

* In $\beta$ glucose, the hydroxyl group attached to C1 is positioned upwards.

  * This orientation is determined by the folding of the open-chain glucose molecule

    * The -OH group on C1 and the -CH$_2$OH group on C5 are on same side of the ring.

### Disaccharides

* 2 monomers join together via a **condensation reaction;** the bond that forms is called a **glycosidic bond.** One of the monomers loses an OH group while the other loses a hydrogen (H).

* They are made of 2 monosaccharides joined by a condensation reaction as a ++water molecule is los++t.

* Examples :

  * **Lactose** - made of a glucose and a galactose molecule.

  * **Maltose** - made of 2 glucose molecules.

  * **Sucrose** - made of a glucose and a fructose molecule.

### Polysaccharides

Polymers are synthesized via **condensation reactions**, where the formation of each **glycosidic bond** results in the loss of one **water molecule.**

* Example:

  * Starch - the energy storage for plants

    * Consists of Amylose and Amylopectin

  * Glycogen - the energy storage for animals

  * Cellulose - cell wall component

**Glycogen**

* Energy storage for animals.

* Has more 1-6 $\alpha$  glycosidic bond.

* Water insoluble so does not affect the osmotic pressure.

* Rapidly hydrolyzed by enzymes due to many terminals so allow for faster breakdown into glucose for immediate fuel for muscles.

**Cellulose**

* Unbranched polymer made of $\beta$ glucose monomers.

* 1-4 glycosidic bond

* Every other monomer is inverted 180° from the previous monomer.

  * Straight and unbranched

* Joined by many hydrogen bonds

  * Many straight chains of cellulose lie parallel to each other.

  * Many hydrogen bonds form between the hydroxyl groups of adjacent chains.

**Cell wall**

* Many straight chains of cellulose lie parallel to each other.

  * Many hydrogen bonds form between the hydroxyl groups of the adjacent chains.

  * The cross-linking the chains together makes a strong bundle called microfibril.

* Microfibrils have immense tensile strength.

  * In the plant cell wall these microfibrils are laid down in different orientation within a matrix of other polysaccharides such as hemicellulose and pectin.

  * This allows the cell wall to withstand high turgor pressure without bursting.

* Molecules associate to make **microfibrils**, which then bundle together to form **fibers**.

* These fibers make up the **plant cell wall**, providing structural support; the wall is **freely permeable** to water and solutes.
