## Globular and Fibrous Proteins

### Globular Protein

* Spherical protein.

* Water soluble.

* Hydrophilic R groups point outward, hydrophobic R groups point inwards.

* **Roles:** metabolic reactions due to its 3D structure.

* E.g. hemoglobin, antibodies, enzymes.

**Hemoglobin**

* Made up of 4 polypeptide chains  ( 2 are alpha globin and 2 are beta globin).

* Each polypeptide chain surrounds a heme group (contains iron).

* Iron helps hemoglobin to bind and release oxygen.

* Each heme group holds an oxygen molecule so a hemoglobin holds 4 oxygen molecules or 8 oxygen atoms.

  * Each of the prosthetic haem groups contain one $Fe^{2+}$ ion, which bind reversibly to one oxygen molecules.

  * A single haemoglobin holds a total of 4 listen molecules.

* Its structure allows for it to form hydrogen bonds with water.

**Function:**

* Binds to oxygen in lungs and transports them to tissues to be used for aerobic respiration.

* When oxygen binds to hemoglobin it forms oxyhemoglobin.

### Fibrous protein:

* Long parallel polypeptide chains with cross linkages that forms them into fibers.

* Water insoluble.

* **Roles:** structural functions.

* E.g. collagen, keratin.

**Collagen**

* Most common structural protein.

* Strong tensile strength as collagen molecules form fibrils which form fibers.

* Collagen molecules lie parallel to each other but are **staggered** so no weak points or any gaps this also increasing it’s tensile strength.

* Made up of 3 polypeptide chains.

* Every 3rd amino acid is glycine.

* Triple helices structure.
