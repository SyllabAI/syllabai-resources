## Phloem

A complex tissue in vascular plants responsible for the translocation of sucrose and amino acids (assimilates) from source to sink in the phloem sieve tube

**Source:** The site of synthesis, usually leaves during summer.

**Sink:** The site of utilisation, usually roots, flowers, or tubers.

### Adaptations of sieve tube element:

* Living tissue with peripheral cytoplasm with no nucleus which allows for translocation with no resistance.

* Has perforated end walls to allow for mass flow of sap solution from one element to another.

### Adaptations of companion cell:

* Many mitochondria to produce ATP as a source of energy for loading of source.

* Nucleus and gene coding for required proteins.

* Rough endoplasmic reticulum for protein synthesis.

* Plasmodesmata allow for diffusion of sucrose

### Loading of sucrose and mass Flow

* Companion cells actively load sucrose into the phloem using proton pumps and H⁺–sucrose co-transporter proteins.

* Proton pumps (H⁺-ATPases) in the companion cell membrane use ATP to actively transport H⁺ ions out of the companion cell into the cell wall/apoplast.

* This creates a high concentration of H⁺ ions outside the companion cell and establishes an electrochemical gradient across the membrane.

* H⁺ ions move back into the companion cell down their electrochemical gradient through specific H⁺–sucrose co-transporter proteins.

* The movement of H⁺ into the companion cell provides the energy to transport sucrose into the companion cell against its concentration gradient.

  * This is secondary active transport, because the co-transporter does not directly use ATP; it uses the H⁺ gradient created by the proton pump.

* Sucrose then moves from the companion cell into the sieve tube element through plasmodesmata, increasing the sucrose concentration in the phloem.

* The high concentration of sucrose lowers the water potential of the sieve tube.

* Water therefore enters the sieve tube from the adjacent xylem by osmosis.

* The entry of water increases the hydrostatic pressure inside the sieve tube at the source.

* At the sink, sucrose is unloaded from the sieve tube and is used or stored.

* Removing sucrose increases the water potential at the sink, causing water to move out of the phloem and back into the xylem by osmosis.

* This creates a hydrostatic pressure gradient between the source and sink.

* Phloem sap, consisting mainly of water and dissolved sucrose, moves by mass flow from the source (high hydrostatic pressure) to the sink (low hydrostatic pressure).
