## Xylem

**Transports mineral ions and organic compounds in water.**

* Has pits for sideways movement of water

* Has lignin in its walls, which is impermeable to water, so it can provide stability to the plant; that is the reason why xylem is always in the middle of the plant.

* Lignin impregnation occurs in spiral, annular, or reticulate patterns before complete lignification, allowing vessel elongation during plant growth.

* Has **NO** end walls, so water can travel up easily without resistance.

* Has no nucleus and organelles, so a larger volume of water can be taken up.

* Has cellulose lining, which is hydrophilic, allowing for **adhesion** between *cellulose molecules and water molecules*.

* While there's also **cohesion** *between water molecules,* causing a continuous column of water.

How change in diameter affects water cohesion and adhesion:

* Increasing diameter will cause a larger volume of water to be transported via cohesion.

* Narrower diameter means more adhesion.

### Water can take 2 pathways

* Apoplastic pathway:

  * Water flows faster through the apoplast pathway because it encounters relatively little resistance as it moves through the continuous cell walls and intercellular spaces.

    * Adhesion between water molecules and the cell walls helps water move through the narrow cell-wall spaces.

  * Water moves through the cell walls and intercellular spaces.

    * However, its movement through the apoplast pathway is blocked at the endodermis by the Casparian strip, which contains suberin and lignin.

    * The Casparian strip prevents water and dissolved ions from continuing through the cell walls and forces them to cross the cell surface membrane into the cytoplasm.

    * This diverts water into the symplastic pathway.

    * The symplastic pathway allows the plant to selectively control which ions enter the xylem.

    * The accumulation of ions in the xylem lowers its water potential, causing water to enter by osmosis and contributing to root pressure.

* Symplastic pathway

  * Can be through cytoplasmic pathway: water enters cytoplasm passing through cell surface membrane by osmosis.

  * Vascuolar pathway: passes through the vacuole by osmosis

* Offers resistance

Water moves across plasmodesmata down a water potential gradient.
