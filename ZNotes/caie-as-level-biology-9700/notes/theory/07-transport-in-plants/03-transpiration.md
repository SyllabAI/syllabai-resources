## Transpiration

Loss of water vapor as it evaporates from the spongy mesophyll cells into the  air spaces and diffuses down the water potential gradient to the stomata into the atmosphere

Why is Transpiration an inevitable consequence of gaseous exchange?

* Gas exchange happens when the stomata open to allow the entry of carbon dioxide and the exit of oxygen and water vapor.

* To reduce Transpiration

  * * Close the stomata.

    * Have a thick layer of waxy cuticle.

**Transpiration pull:**

* Water vapor diffuses out of the leaf by evaporation (transpiration). Water evaporates from spongy mesophyll into air spaces

* So more water will move out of mesophyll cells by osmosis via the symplastic pathway through cytoplasm or through cell wall by the apoplastic pathway

The greater the transpiration, the greater the water movement by transpiration this is called **transpiration pull**

Also known as capillary

Real life example: while drinking juice through a straw, the more you sip, the more juice flows through the straw (faster).

The Straw is the Xylem: The plastic straw acts exactly like the microscopic xylem vessels (tubes) running up a plant stem.

Your Mouth is the Leaf: When you suck air out of the straw, you create a vacuum (negative pressure).

### Cohesion-Tension Theory

**Cohesion**: hydrogen bonds form between water molecules, creating a continuous, unbroken column of water in the xylem.

**Adhesion**: hydrogen bonds form between water molecules and the hydrophilic cellulose walls of the xylem vessels, helping to support the water column against gravity.
