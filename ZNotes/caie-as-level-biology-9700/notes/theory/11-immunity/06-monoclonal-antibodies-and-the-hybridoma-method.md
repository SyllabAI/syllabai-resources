## Monoclonal antibodies and the hybridoma method

* Help in Diagnosis of disease

### Monoclonal antibodies

Monoclonal antibodies bind with high specificity to a single specific antigen.

Example (Cancer detection): Immobilized monoclonal antibodies specific to antigen of cancer cells are tagged with a color indicator.

**Treatment of Disease**

* mAbs (monoclonal antibodies) bind to and block specific receptors on cancer cells or toxins (e.g., blocking growth signal receptors on tumor cells).

* Drugs which treats the diseases is binded to mAbs(monoclonal antibodies).

* The antibody attaches specifically on target cancer cells, delivering the drug directly while sparing healthy tissue.

* mAbs (monoclonal antibodies) mark tumor cells so the body's natural killer cells or phagocytes can destroy them.

### Hybridoma method

* A mouse is injected with a specific antigen to stimulate an immune response.

* After a few weeks, B-lymphocytes (plasma cells) producing the desired antibody are harvested from the mouse’s spleen.

* The harvested plasma cells are fused with myeloma (cancerous B-cell) cells using a fusogen such as polyethylene glycol (PEG).

  * The fusogen causes the cell membranes to fuse, forming hybridoma cells.

* The mixture is cultured in HAT selective medium, where unfused plasma cells die naturally, and unfused myeloma cells are killed. Only successful hybridoma cells survive.

* Surviving hybridoma cells are separated into individual wells and screened to identify clones producing the desired antibody.

* Selected hybridoma cells are left to divide by mitosis and produce large quantities of pure monoclonal antibodies.
