## Vaccine programs to control infectious diseases

Vaccination programs operate on both an individual level and a population level to control, eliminate, or even eradicate infectious diseases.

* **Protects Vulnerable Individuals:** Herd immunity creates a protective barrier around individuals who cannot be safely vaccinated (e.g. newborns or those with severe allergies).

* **Threshold:** The percentage of the population needed for herd immunity depends on the pathogen's transmissibility. Highly contagious diseases like measles require around 95% coverage.

### Breaking the Chain of Transmission

Mass vaccination reduces the total reservoir of the pathogen in a community. With fewer infected individuals, the probability of an uninfected person coming into contact with a carrier drops.

### Eradication & Containment

* **Eradication:** Global, synchronized vaccination campaigns can completely eliminate a pathogen from the human population (e.g. eradication of **Smallpox** in 1980).

* **Ring Vaccination:** Used to contain localized outbreaks by vaccinating everyone in the immediate household and surrounding area of a confirmed case, effectively creating a buffer zone to stop further transmission.
