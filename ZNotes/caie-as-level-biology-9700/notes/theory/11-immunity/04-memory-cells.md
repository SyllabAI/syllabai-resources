## Memory cells

During the primary response, a small pool of B- and T-lymphocytes differentiate into memory cells and remain circulating in the blood and lymphatic system for long periods.

Upon re-exposure to the same pathogen:

* Memory cells recognize the non-self antigen immediately.

* Clonal selection and expansion occur far more rapidly.

* Plasma cells are produced much faster and produce a significantly higher concentration of antibodies for a longer duration.

* The pathogen is cleared before it can reproduce enough to cause symptoms (providing long-term immunity).
