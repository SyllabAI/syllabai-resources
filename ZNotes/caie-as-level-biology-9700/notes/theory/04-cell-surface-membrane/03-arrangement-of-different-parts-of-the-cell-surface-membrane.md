## Arrangement of different parts of the cell surface membrane

* **Cholesterol**

  * Found only in animal cells

  * Hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails

  * Provide mechanical stability: without it, the membrane may break

  * Regulate fluidity: At low temperatures, cholesterol prevents phospholipids from packing too closely together, which stops the membrane from becoming too rigid.

    * At high temperatures, cholesterol interacts with the hydrophobic tails to pull them together, which stabilizes the membrane and prevents it from becoming too "leaky."

### Factors affecting fluidity of the cell surface membrane:

* **Increase** in number of unsaturated fatty acids: causes an **increase in fluidity**, as they cause a kink because of carbon-to-carbon double bonds (C=C), so fatty acids are more separated.

* Length of phospholipid tail: as length **increases,** fluidity **decreases**.

* Temperature: as temperature **decreases,** fluidity **decreases**; that’s why there is a bacterium that responds and increases the number of unsaturated fatty acids.

  * As temperature decreases, kinetic energy decreases, so fluidity decreases.

  * To raise fluidity, bacteria increase the number of unsaturated fatty acids.

  * Fatty acid tail length decreases. Longer tails have more surface area, which means there are more hydrophobic interactions.

* Glycolipids

  * Form hydrogen bonds with surrounding water molecules to stabilize the membrane
