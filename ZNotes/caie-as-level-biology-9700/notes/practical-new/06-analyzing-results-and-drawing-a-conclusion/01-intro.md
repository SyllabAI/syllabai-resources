# **Analyzing results and Drawing a conclusion**

## Describing trends scientifically 

* When you have completed your experiment and gathered data, you need to be able to draw a reasonable conclusion. 

* This conclusion needs to describe the correlation between the two variables. This could be by describing the **trend** or stating how the variable aren’t related at all. 

* If possible, the best way to spot a trend or show its effect would be by plotting a graph, the shape of the graph shows the relationship between the IV and DV. 

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/29f0f0e8c6a2de3a3d15950d.webp =839x)

* Take (Y) as the dependent variable (variable being measured) and (X) as the independent variable (the variable that is changed by investigator) 

  * **Increasing:** The measured value (Y) increases as the variable being changed (X) increases, this shows a positive correlation between IV and DV.

  * **Decreasing:** The measured value (Y) decreases as the variable being changed (X) increases, this shows a negative correlation between IV and DV

  * **Constant:** The measured value (Y) remains approximately constant as the variable being changed (X) increases, the DV stays constant regardless of change in IV

  * **No relationship:** The measured value (Y) shows no clear relationship with the variable being changed (X), the value of DV varies with effect of IV so no clear correlation. 

  * **Increase then decrease:** The measured value (Y) increases to a maximum at a certain point of changed value (X) before decreasing, the DV peaks at a certain (optimum) value of IV.

  * **Decrease then increase:** The measured value (Y) decreases to a minimum at a certain point of changed value (X)before increasing, the DV reaches a minimum at a certain point of IV. 

  * **Levels off (plateau)**: The measured value (Y) increases to its maximum at a certain point of changed value (X) then plateaus off, showing that after a certain point, the increase of IV does not change the DV (likely from some sort of limiting factor)

  * **Rapid change:** The measured value (Y) increases very slightly before reaching a certain value of the IV (X), then shooting up. This may indicate that DV only shows significant results after a certain value of the IV.
