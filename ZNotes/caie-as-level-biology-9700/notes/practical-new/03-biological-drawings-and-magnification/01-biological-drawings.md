# Biological Drawings and Magnification

## Biological Drawings

* Often time, when scientists observe a biological specimen through a microscope, they may need to draw a rough diagram showing the arrangement and position of organisms or structures observed in the specimen or micrograph.

* Drawing a detailed diagram can require large amounts of skill and time, which is why to eradicate this issue scientists use plan diagrams to overall arrangement clearly and quickly.

* These rough diagrams are called (biological) **Plan diagrams.**

## Plan Diagrams

* To draw accurate plan diagrams, the rules you must follow are:

  * **Rule 1: Draw bold, continuous lines. No breaks or overlapping sketches.**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/94f09a240da96924c9f040cd.webp =455x)

* * **Rule 2: Do not draw any individual cells, instead just differentiate tissues with a line in between.**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/f095ce2befd3b3c5c6951a1f.webp =453x)

* * **Rule 3: The proportions of your drawings must match the micrograph**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/f97ef00ce33c05f2c4d07b4b.webp =455x)

* * **Rule 4: Do not shade or crosshatch even if the organism has a darker stain.**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/29946fe3856969c4149c0d53.webp =458x)

* * **Rule 5: No cells should be overlapping, each cells cell wall and cell membrane must be drawn.**

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/c80f477d1f403412a4e84836.webp =453x)
