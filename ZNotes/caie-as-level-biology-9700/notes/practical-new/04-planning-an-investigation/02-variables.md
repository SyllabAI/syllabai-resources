## Variables

* **Variables:** Factors that can be changed, measured or kept constant during an investigation. They include **independent, dependent and control variables**.

* ++Identifying variables++:

  - **Independent variables**: The variable that is manipulated or changed by the investigator.

    When planning the investigation, a range of values of the independent variables should be selected to observe its effect on the dependent variables.

  - **Dependent variables**: The variable that is measured by the investigator as an effect of the changing IV

  - **Control variables**: The conditions or variables that are kept constant through out the experiment

  - **Experiment example**: Investigating the effect of light intensity on photosynthesis

* example: **investigating the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis**:

  * **Experiment:** Measure the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed at different distances from a lamp.

## Choosing the values for independent variables

* When choosing your values for the IV you must remember:

  - Pick a minimum of 5 different values

  - The values must have an appropriate range to measure the effect of IV on DV

  - The intervals between values must be equal (Example 5,10,15)

**Method of changing the IV**

* The method used to alter the IV must be accurate and easy to measure. You may use:
