## Identifying Anomalies

* **Anomaly:** A result or data point that does not fit the overall pattern or trend of the other results.

* Some examples of anomalies are:

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/3e7f740dbf63512b299a8ba7.webp =903x)

* Anomalies are easily spottable because they are the only ones that stray slightly far from the line of best fit. 

* Anomalies can be caused by many factors such as experimental error, contamination of samples, equipment malfunctions etc. 

* To avoid them, you can repeat the experiment, this time more carefully to avoid errors. 

* In case there is a case where you do not have enough time to fix the error, do not remove it, instead check the original experiment and measurements for possible errors.

* If the data required for a calculation includes the anomaly, remove it and proceed with the calculation but make sure to clearly state that the anomalous result is not included.
