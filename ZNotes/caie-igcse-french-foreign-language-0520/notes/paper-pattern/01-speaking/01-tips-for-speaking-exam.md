# Speaking

## **Tips for Speaking Exam**

### **1. Know the Exam Format**

Understand the three parts of the speaking exam:

* **Role-play**: Respond to prompts in a real-life scenario.

* **Topic Presentation & Discussion**: Talk about a prepared topic for about 1–2 minutes.

* **General Conversation**: Answer questions on everyday topics.

### **2. Choose a Strong Topic for Your Presentation**

Pick a topic that you are passionate about and can speak fluently on. Make sure it aligns with IGCSE themes like family, school, holidays, or the environment.

### **3. Expand Your Vocabulary**

Use a variety of words and expressions, especially synonyms to avoid repetition. Example:

* Instead of always saying *intéressant*, use *captivant, fascinant, enrichissant*.

* Learn useful linking phrases like *D’une part…, d’autre part…* (On one hand…, on the other hand…).

### **4. Master Common Questions and Answers**

Prepare for typical questions like:

* *Parle-moi de ta famille.* (Tell me about your family.)

* *Que fais-tu pendant ton temps libre ?* (What do you do in your free time?)

* *Où aimerais-tu voyager et pourquoi ?* (Where would you like to travel and why?)

### **5. Speak in Full Sentences**

Avoid giving one-word answers. Instead of just saying *oui*, say:
NO – *Oui.*

YES – *Oui, j’aime beaucoup le sport, surtout le football, car c’est amusant et bon pour la santé.*

### **6. Use Connectors to Sound Natural**

* **Basic**: *et, mais, aussi, parce que* (and, but, also, because)

* **Advanced**: *cependant, par conséquent, en revanche* (however, therefore, on the other hand)

### **7. Conjugate Verbs Correctly**

Practice speaking in different tenses:

* **Present:** *Je vais à l’école tous les jours.*

* **Past:** *Hier, j’ai joué au football.*

* **Future:** *Demain, je vais regarder un film.*

### **8. Use the Conditional & Subjunctive for Higher Marks**

* *Si j’avais plus de temps, je voyagerais plus.* (If I had more time, I would travel more.)

* *Il faut que je fasse mes devoirs.* (I have to do my homework.)

### **9. Learn Useful Fillers to Avoid Hesitation**

Instead of saying “umm,” use:

* *Eh bien…* (Well…)

* *Voyons…* (Let’s see…)

* *Alors…* (So…)

### **10. Pronounce Words Clearly**

Common pronunciation mistakes to avoid:

* **"J'ai"** sounds like *zhay*, not *jai*.

* **Silent letters:** *beaucoup* is pronounced *boh-coo*, not *bo-coop*.

* **Avoid anglicized words:** *Restaurant* is pronounced *resto-ron*, not *rest-ow-rant*.

### **11. Record Yourself Speaking**

Listen to your own recordings to identify areas of improvement in fluency and pronunciation.

### **12. Practice with a Native Speaker or AI**

Try **language exchange apps** like HelloTalk or speak to a French teacher. You can also use AI tools like ChatGPT to simulate conversations.

### **13. Use Body Language & Facial Expressions**

Express confidence with good posture and eye contact, even if it’s a recorded exam.

### **14. Avoid Over-Memorization**

Memorizing an entire speech can make you sound robotic. Instead, memorize key phrases and speak naturally.

### **15. React to the Examiner’s Questions**

Instead of just answering, **engage in the conversation**:

* Examiner: *Tu aimes le sport ?*

* You: *Oui, j’adore le football ! Et vous, quel sport préférez-vous ?* (And you, what sport do you prefer?)

### **16. Practice Thinking in French**

The more you think in French, the less you’ll rely on translation, making your speech more natural.
