## **Strategies to Improve Listening Skills**

### **A. Before the Exam**

1. **Listen to French Daily:**

   * Podcasts: *Coffee Break French, InnerFrench, Learn French by Podcast.*

   * Music: Listen to French songs and read the lyrics.

   * Films & Series: Watch with subtitles, then try without.

2. **Practice Past Papers:**

   * Get used to different accents and speeds.

   * Learn common question types.

3. **Expand Your Vocabulary:**

   * Focus on **common themes** (travel, family, health, school, etc.).

   * Learn **synonyms** (e.g., *bon marché* = *pas cher* for "cheap").

4. **Improve Pronunciation & Phonetics:**

   * Learn **liaisons** (how words connect in speech).

   * Recognize similar-sounding words (*père* vs. *pair*).

### **B. During the Exam**

1. **Read the Questions First:**

   * Predict what information you need.

   * Underline keywords in the questions.

2. **Listen for Context:**

   * Identify who is speaking and where they are.

   * Pay attention to **tone and emotions**.

3. **Write Key Words While Listening:**

   * Jot down numbers, dates, names, and places.

   * Use abbreviations (*bcp* for *beaucoup*, *tjs* for *toujours*).

4. **Don’t Panic on Unfamiliar Words:**

   * Focus on words you recognize.

   * Use context to guess meaning.

5. **Take Advantage of the Second Listening:**

   * Confirm answers or fill in missed gaps.

   * Double-check for small details (negatives, tenses).

### **C. Common Traps to Avoid**

* **Negation:** Be careful with *ne... pas, ne... jamais, ne... plus* (e.g., *Je n’ai jamais visité Paris* means "I have never visited Paris").

* **False Friends:** Words that look similar to English but mean something different (*actuellement* = "currently," not "actually").

* **Distractors:** Speakers may correct themselves ("Il part à 16h... ah non, à 17h!").
