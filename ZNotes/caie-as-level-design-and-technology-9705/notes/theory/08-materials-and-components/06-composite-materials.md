foam board

|

foam board is a very lightweight and rigid material. It consists of a foam core sandwiched between two layers of paper or plastic. It's easy to cut with a craft knife and is dimensionally stable

|

It is used for model making, mounting displays, architectural models, and various hobby and craft projects

|

It is not easily recyclable

|

Sheets in various sizes and thicknesses.

||

foil-backed and laminated card

|

This composite is a card laminated with a thin layer of foil or plastic. The foil layer provides a barrier against moisture and gases, while also reflecting heat.

|

Its primary use is in food and beverage packaging, such as juice cartons (e.g., Tetra Pak) and blister packs for tablets

|

challenging to recycle because of its layered composition (paper, plastic, and aluminium). The different layers must be separated, which can be energy-intensive and not widely available at recycling facilities.

chipboard

|

Chipboard is an inexpensive and dense material. It is made from wood chips and particles bonded with resin. It is heavy, has a coarse surface, and is not as strong as other boards. It is also susceptible to water damage and can swell or disintegrate if exposed to moisture.

|

Due to its low cost, it's widely used in inexpensive furniture, kitchen cabinets, and as a subfloor for carpeting

|

Chipboard effectively uses wood waste and reduces the demand for solid wood. However, the resins used often contain urea-formaldehyde, which can release harmful volatile organic compounds (VOCs)

|

Large sheets in various thicknesses.

||
||

medium density fibreboard (MDF)

|

MDF is made from fine wood fibres. pressed with resin. This results in a dense, uniform, and very smooth surface. It is easy to machine, cut, and finish, and it does not have a grain, making it ideal for painting. However, it is heavy and has low moisture resistance.

|

Used for furniture (especially painted or veneered), cabinets, speaker boxes, and decorative mouldings

|

MDF uses a significant amount of wood waste, which is a positive environmental aspect. Like chipboard, it can contain formaldehyde resins, which pose a health and environmental concern

|

Large sheets in various thicknesses.

||
||

plywood

|

Plywood is composed of thin layers of wood veneer, or "plies," glued together with their wood grain rotated at 90 degrees to each other. This cross-laminated structure gives it exceptional strength, stability, and resistance to warping. It has a high strength-to-weight ratio.

|

Construction, furniture, flooring, and structural applications like sheathing and subfloors.

|

Plywood production uses whole logs, but the process is highly efficient, and it can be made from fast-growing, managed plantations. The resins used may contain formaldehyde, but low-formaldehyde alternatives are available.

|

Large sheets in a range of thicknesses and grades

||
||

blockboard

|

relatively lightweight yet dimensionally stable and strong, especially over long spans. It is also less prone to warping than solid wood. It has good screw-holding capabilities and is easy to cut and work with standard woodworking tools

|

frequently used for making furniture, such as shelves, wardrobes, and tables, as well as for interior doors, partitions, and wall panels

|

While it makes use of smaller, less valuable pieces of wood for its core, its environmental impact is similar to other manufactured boards that use resins containing formaldehyde. However, new formulations with low-formaldehyde resins are becoming more common.

|

large sheets of various sheets

glass fibre reinforced plastic (GRP)

|

strong for its weight, corrosion-resistant, and an excellent electrical insulator. It is also waterproof, durable, and can be easily moulded into complex shapes

|

marine industry for building boat hulls, in construction for roofing and panels, in the automotive industry for body parts, and for storage tanks and pipes due to its chemical resistance.

|

difficult to recycle because its thermosetting polymer matrix cannot be melted and reshaped. The material's durability and long lifespan, however, can reduce the need for frequent replacement

|

sheets, rods, bars

||
||

carbon fibre reinforced plastic (CFRP)

|

high strength-to-weight ratio, making it one of the strongest and lightest materials available. It is also very stiff, resistant to fatigue, and has a low coefficient of thermal expansion, meaning it maintains its shape across a wide range of temperatures

|

aerospace industry for aircraft components (e.g., wings and fuselages), high-end sports equipment (e.g., racing bicycles, golf clubs), and in high-performance automotive parts

|

it's very challenging to recycle

|

sheets, rods, pre-impregnated (pre-preg) fabrics
