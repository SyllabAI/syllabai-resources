polylactide (PLA)

|

A thermoplastic polyester known for being stiff, rigid, and having high strength. It is biodegradable and biocompatible. PLA has a low melting point

|

Widely used in 3D printing filament, disposable cutlery, food packaging, and medical implants

|

PLA is commercially compostable, can’t be composted at home

|

granules/pellets for moulding and extrusion, and as filament for 3D printers.

||
||

polyhydroxybutyrate (PHB), e.g. BIOPOL®

|

A polyester produced naturally by bacteria. It is a strong, rigid, and durable material with excellent barrier properties against moisture. It is also fully biodegradable and biocompatible.

|

biodegradable packaging, single-use bottles, medical implants, and drug delivery systems. One well-known brand is BIOPOL®

|

fully biodegradable

|

granules/pellets for moulding and extrusion.

||
||

plastic made from corn/potato starch

|

These plastics are soft, flexible, and often transparent. They have a low working temperature and poor moisture resistance.

|

disposable shopping bags, food packaging films, and compostable waste bags.

|

easily biodegradable and can be composted in both commercial and home composting systems. However, the production process can use land and resources that could otherwise be used for food crops.

|

granules for film blowing and extrusion, as well as finished products like thin films and sheets.
