## Material properties

#|
||

Material Property

|

Significance/ it’s importance in a product

||
||

hardness

|

This is a material's resistance to scratching, indentation, and abrasion. For products that need to withstand wear and tear or maintain a smooth finish, like tool blades or countertops, high hardness is essential

||
||

ductility

|

A material's ability to be stretched or drawn into a thin wire without fracturing. This is a vital property for electrical wires, such as those made from copper, where the material must be pulled and bent without breaking.

||
||

toughness

|

A material's ability to absorb energy before fracturing. It's the opposite of brittleness. Products that need to withstand sudden impacts or shocks, like car bumpers or safety helmets, require high toughness.

||
||

brittleness

|

The tendency of a material to fracture or break with very little or no plastic deformation. Materials like glass are brittle and will shatter easily under stress. This property is significant where a clean break is needed or where impact is not a concern.

||
||

elasticity

|

A material's ability to deform under a load and then return to its original shape when the load is removed. This is important for products that need to be flexible without permanent deformation, such as rubber bands or springs.

||
||

malleability

|

A material's ability to be hammered or rolled into thin sheets without cracking. This property is crucial for products like aluminium foil or sheet metal, as it allows for shaping and forming.

||
||

dimensional stability

|

The ability of a material to maintain its size and shape despite changes in temperature, humidity, or other environmental factors. This is critical for precision parts, such as those in a camera body or architectural models.

||
||

electrical conductivity

|

A material's ability to conduct an electric current. Metals like copper and gold have high electrical conductivity, making them ideal for wires and circuit board components. Insulators, on the other hand, have low conductivity, which is important for preventing electric shocks.

||
||

thermal conductivity

|

The ability of a material to transfer heat. Materials with high thermal conductivity, like copper and aluminium, are used for heat sinks and cookware. Low thermal conductivity is desired for insulators, like those used in building insulation or thermal flask linings.

||
||

corrosion resistance

|

A material's ability to resist deterioration caused by chemical reactions with its environment. This is a critical property for products exposed to moisture, chemicals, or the elements, such as stainless steel cutlery or outdoor pipes.
