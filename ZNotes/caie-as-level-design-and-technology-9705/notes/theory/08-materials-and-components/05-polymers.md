||

acrylic (PMMA)

|

transparent, stiff, and brittle plastic. It is often used as a lightweight, shatter-resistant alternative to glass

|

windows, skylights, display cases, lenses, and lighting fixtures

|

not biodegradable and is a petroleum-based product. However, it can be recycled, which helps to reduce the demand for new material and lessen its environmental footprint

|

Sheets, rods, tubes, and granules for moulding

||
||

nylon

|

strong, stiff, and tough engineering plastic with excellent wear and abrasion resistance

|

Bearings, bushings, gears, wear pads, and a variety of machine parts. It's also used for textiles like clothing, ropes, and fishing nets

|

not biodegradable and its production from petroleum has environmental consequences. However, it is durable and long-lasting, which means it doesn't need to be replaced often

|

Pellets, rods, sheets, and tubes.

||
||

polyvinyl chloride (PVC)

|

rigid, durable, and cost-effective plastic. It is highly resistant to chemicals and has good electrical insulation properties

|

Pipes and fittings for plumbing, window frames, electrical wire insulation, and flooring

|

Its production and disposal release toxic, chlorine-based chemicals, including dioxins, which are highly carcinogenic. It is not easily recyclable and is a major source of microplastic pollution

|

Rigid and flexible sheets, pipes, and granules

||
||

polystyrene (PS)

|

lightweight foam with excellent thermal insulation and shock-absorbing properties

|

Disposable cutlery, CD cases, and food containers (in its rigid form). As a foam (Styrofoam), it is used for protective packaging, insulation panels, and disposable cups

|

not biodegradable and can take hundreds of years to break down. Due to its lightweight and bulky nature, it's difficult and often uneconomical to recycle. It contributes significantly to plastic waste in landfills and oceans

|

Pellets, sheets, foam blocks, and granules.

||
||

polypropylene (PP)

|

lightweight, tough, and fatigue-resistant plastic, meaning it can withstand repeated bending without cracking

|

food containers, bottle caps, ropes, and car bumpers

|

recyclable plastic. Its low density means it uses less raw material per product, and its durability means products last longer

|

Sheets, films, rods, and pellets

||
||

high and low density polyethylene (HDPE) (LDPE)

|

Both are highly flexible and have good chemical resistance

|

HDPE is used for rigid items like milk jugs, shampoo bottles, and durable pipes. LDPE is used for flexible items like plastic shopping bags, squeeze bottles, and plastic wraps.

|

Both are recyclable and are among the most commonly recycled plastics. Recycling HDPE and LDPE significantly reduces the need for new petroleum-based production.

|

Pellets, sheets, films, and bottles

||
||

high impact polystyrene (HIPS)

|

It's easy to form, machine, and glue, impact resistant

|

Housings for appliances, toys, food packaging (e.g., yogurt cups), and point-of-sale displays

|

not biodegradable. While it can be recycled, its lower value and mixed composition can make this process difficult

|

Sheets, rolls, and pellets.

||
||

polycarbonate ( PC )

|

tough and transparent thermoplastic. High impact resistance

|

CD/DVD discs, safety glasses, machine guards, unbreakable windows, and riot shields

|

recyclable, PC recycling is not as widespread as for other plastics.

|

Sheets, rods, tubes, and pellets

||
||

acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS)

|

tough, rigid, and impact-resistant plastic

|

LEGO® bricks, car parts, electronic device housings, and 3D printing filaments

|

petroleum-based plastic. While technically recyclable, it is not widely collected or recycled in most municipal programs.

|

Pellets, sheets, and rods

||
||

polyethylene terephthalate (PET)

|

strong, lightweight plastic that is an excellent barrier to moisture and gases. It is transparent and shatterproof. It has a high strength-to-weight ratio

|

plastic bottles for beverages. It is also used for food containers, packaging films, and as a fibre for clothing

|

one of the most widely recycled plastics in the world. This high recycling rate helps reduce its environmental impact, though the initial production is energy-intensive

|

Pellets, preforms (for bottles), sheets, and films
