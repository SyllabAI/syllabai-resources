## Forming

* ### Vacuum Forming

  * A sheet of thermoplastic is heated until it's pliable.

  * It is then draped over a mould.

  * A vacuum is used to suck the air out from underneath.

  * This forces the plastic sheet to conform tightly to the mould's shape.

  * The mould is called a former.

  * Design and Construction of Formers

    * Formers are usually made from resistant materials (such as MDF, wood, or epoxy) and must be engineered with specific design features such as **draft angles** (typically 3° to 5°) so the formed plastic can be easily removed without sticking, **chamfered or rounded edges** to prevent the heated sheet from tearing or thinning excessively during stretching, and **small drilled vent holes** to allow trapped air underneath to escape fully.

* ### Line Bending

  * A strip heater heats a straight line on a sheet of thermoplastic (usually acrylic, PMMA).

  * Once the line becomes soft and pliable, the sheet can be bent to a specific angle.

  * This is used to create simple shapes such as display stands.
