## Analysis of products

Product analysis is essential to inform new specifications, estimate costs, and improve usability. When analysing an existing product, one must evaluate the following factors:

- **Function -** What primary and secondary purposes does the product serve? How effectively does it perform, and how might its mechanical or operational performance be improved?
- **Aesthetics -** What are its visual and tactile attributes (line, colour, shape, proportion, form, texture, and finish)? How do light and shade affect its visual appeal?
- **Ergonomics** - How do humans interact with the product? Consider anthropometric data (e.g., percentiles, reach, weight, height, grip size) and psychological interactions to ensure comfort and ease of use.
- **Types and Properties of Materials** **-** What materials (papers, boards, woods, metals, polymers, composites, smart/modern materials) were used? Consider mechanical and physical properties such as:
  * *Hardness*: Resistance to scratching, indentation, or wear.

  * *Toughness*: Ability to absorb impact energy without fracturing.

  * *Malleability:* Ability to be deformed plastically under compression without cracking.

  * *Ductility*:  Ability to be deformed plastically under tension without cracking.

  * *Tensile Strength*: Resistance to being pulled apart.

  * *Electrical/Thermal Conductivity*: How the material conducts electricity/heat, respectively.
- **Production Processes -** How was it manufactured? Analyse whether the product relies on wastage (machining, cutting), shaping (bending, casting), forming (vacuum forming), addition (welding, adhesives), or printing. Consider whether the scale of production is one-off, batch, or mass production.
- **Target Market** **-** Who is the product designed for? Analyse target demographics (age, gender), socio-economic backgrounds, cultural preferences, and specific user needs (including inclusive design for users with visual, hearing, or physical impairments).
- **Cost -** What resources, material choices, tooling, and labour were needed for production? How do these impact the final retail price and commercial viability?
- **Safety of the User -** What inherent features, guards, or warnings ensure the product is safe during operation? Consider sharp edges, pinch points, electrical insulation, and stability.
- **Quality Control (QC) & Quality Assurance (QA) -** What quality standards and checks were applied during and after production? This includes compliance with safety standards such as non-toxic surface finishes/paints (crucial for children's toys), electrical safety certifications (e.g., insulation, fusing), and dimensional tolerance checks.
- **Sustainability -** Is the product eco-friendly? Evaluate raw material extraction impacts, energy consumption during manufacture, repairability, ease of disassembly, and end-of-life disposal or recyclability.
