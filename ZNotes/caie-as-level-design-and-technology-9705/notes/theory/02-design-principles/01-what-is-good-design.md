# Design Principles

## What is good design?

A good design:

* Meets the needs, wants, or values of the user:

  * A good design solves a genuine human problem or fulfills a clear psychological desire. It aligns with the user's lifestyle, beliefs, and expectations rather than forcing them to adapt to awkward product limitations.

* Is innovative:

  * Innovation introduces novel technological features, structural efficiencies, or fresh functional perspectives that significantly improve how a task is performed.

* Is simple (less is more):

  * Simplicity eliminates unnecessary visual clutter, redundant features, and over-complicated mechanisms, making the product clean, logical, and easy to interact with.

* Is aesthetic:

  * It uses harmonious lines, balanced proportions, thoughtful color palettes, and tactile surface finishes to create an emotional connection and visual delight for the user.

* Is long-lasting:

  * Employs robust construction techniques and durable materials to withstand physical wear and tear, while utilizing timeless styling to prevent psychological obsolescence.

* Is environmentally friendly:

  * Considers the entire product lifecycle, minimizing energy consumption during use, utilizing sustainable or recycled materials, and designing for easy disassembly and recycling at end-of-life.

* Is safe to use:

  * Integrates inherent safety features, smooth radii on edges, secure electrical insulation, and non-toxic materials to protect users from accidental harm.

* Makes the product useful:

  * Prioritizes core functionality and ergonomic efficiency, ensuring the product executes its intended primary and secondary purposes reliably.

* Makes the product understandable:

  * Features intuitive interfaces, clear signifiers, and natural mapping so that users immediately understand how to operate the product without needing instruction manuals.
