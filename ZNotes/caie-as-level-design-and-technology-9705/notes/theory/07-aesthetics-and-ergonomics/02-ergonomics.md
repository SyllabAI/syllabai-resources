## Ergonomics

* Ergonomics is the understanding of how humans interact with environments, products, and systems.

* The goal is to design products that are safe, comfortable, and efficient for human use.

* To design ergonomically, you must have anthropometric data.

### Common anthropometric measurements include:

* Weight - Used for load-bearing capacity and material strength.

* Height - Overall body measurement.

* Knee height - Used for desk or chair height.

* Sitting height - Crucial for seating design and clearance.

* Body Mass Index (BMI) - A ratio of weight to height used to assess body mass.

* Body circumference (arm, waist, hip, and calf) - Used for sizing clothing and ergonomic grips.

* Waist-to-hip ratio (WHR) - Used to assess body shape and health risk.

### The interpretation and application of this data are crucial in design development.

* Designers design for a wide range of users, typically from the 5th to the 95th percentile of the population.

  * This data helps them determine the range of adjustability needed for a product (like a chair's height or a car seat's position) or to create different sizes of the same product.

* ### **Worked Example - Applying Anthropometric Data to Design an Adjustable Office Chair:**

  * *Step 1 (Data Gathering) -* Designers look up popliteal height (floor-to-knee joint distance while seated) and hip breadth from anthropometric data tables for the target population.

  * *Step 2 (Percentile Range Target) -* To accommodate 90% of users, the design targets dimensions from the 5th percentile female up to the 95th percentile male.

  * *Step 3 (Application) -*The chair's gas-lift height adjustment range is calculated so that its minimum seat height matches the 5th percentile female popliteal height (preventing legs from dangling), and its maximum height matches the 95th percentile male popliteal height plus shoe allowance, ensuring feet rest flat and pressure is evenly distributed across the thighs.
