## Ways to modify designs to make them more sustainable

### Material Modifications

* **Dematerialization -** Use only the essential minimum amount of material required to satisfy structural and functional specifications.

* **Sustainable Substitutions -** Replace traditional virgin materials with bio-based alternatives (such as starch-based bioplastics, bamboo, or sustainably sourced timber).

* **Recycled Content -**Incorporate post-consumer or post-industrial recycled plastics, metals, and glass to cut demand for virgin raw material extraction.

* **Local Sourcing -**Utilize materials available close to the manufacturing site to significantly reduce transport carbon footprints and logistical costs.

### Manufacturing and Production Modifications

* **Reducing the Number of Manufacturing Processes**

  * *Detailed Explanation -* Streamlining production by consolidating multiple discrete parts into a single moulded or cast component (e.g., replacing a multi-part riveted sheet metal housing with a single injection-moulded polymer shell). This eliminates secondary machining steps, reduces factory tooling wear, lowers overall factory energy consumption, and cuts assembly time and waste.

* **Process Efficiency -** Minimise the number of production steps, explore less energy-intensive manufacturing methods, and implement lean manufacturing techniques to curb factory floor waste.

### Product Longevity and End-of-Life Modifications

* **Making Products Easy to Disassemble or Separate**

  * *Detailed Explanation -* Designing with Design for Disassembly (DfD) principles. This involves replacing permanent adhesives, ultrasonic welding, or complex over-moulding with mechanical fasteners (such as screws, clips, and interlocking snap-fits). Furthermore, designers must avoid bonding dissimilar plastics or mixing metals, ensuring that components can be rapidly separated into pure material streams.

* **Standardised Components & Reusability -** Use universally available standardized components and design parts (such as rechargeable battery packs or electronic modules) that can be easily salvaged, tested, and reused in new products.

* **Material Labelling -** Clearly mark and emboss material identification codes (such as ISO recycling symbols and polymer acronyms like ABS or PP) directly onto products and packaging to aid rapid sorting and separation for recycling.
