# Sustainable Design

## Key Definitions for Sustainable Practice

* **Embodied Energy -** The total sum of all energy consumed throughout a product’s entire lifecycle before its actual use, including raw material extraction, refining, transport, and manufacturing processes.

* **Dematerialization -** The process of reducing the total quantity of materials, energy, and physical resources required to manufacture and deliver a product or service function (e.g., making products lighter, thinner, or digitally multi-functional).

* **Circular Economy -** An economic and industrial model aimed at eliminating waste and continuous resource depletion by keeping products, components, and materials in circulation for as long as possible through sharing, leasing, reuse, repair, refurbishment, and recycling.

## Responsibilities of designers and manufacturers

* **Designers**

  * Must specify products and packaging made from sustainable, renewable, or recycled materials and components.

  * Must design products that are robust, long-lasting, and easily repaired to prevent premature obsolescence.

  * Must ensure products can be easily disassembled at the end of their life for material recovery.

  * Must optimize material usage to consume as little raw resources as possible without compromising functional integrity.

* **Manufacturers**

  * Must practice sustainable sourcing of raw materials and ethical supply chain management.

  * Must maximize energy efficiency across factory operations.

  * Must minimize waste production and control factory emissions or pollution during manufacturing.

  * Must take extended producer responsibility (EPR) for managing their products after consumer use.
