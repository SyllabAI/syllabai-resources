## Life memberships

The full amount received for a life membership is **not treated as income immediately**.

This is because the club will provide services to the member for many years. Recognising the entire amount in the year it is received would overstate that year's income.

Instead, the amount is spread over the specified period.

For example, if a life membership costs $1,200 and the income is recognised over 10 years:

$$ \text{Annual income} = \text{Subscriptions received} - \text{Opening arrears} + \text{Opening prepayments} + \text{Closing arrears} - \text{Closing prepayments} $$

### Accounting entries for life memberships

When the payment is received:

**Debit Bank**

**Credit Life Membership Fund**

At the end of the financial year, the appropriate amount is transferred to income:

**Debit Life Membership Fund**

**Credit Income and Expenditure Account**

The question will normally specify the period over which the income should be recognised.

### Calculating life membership income

If the life membership fee is transferred to income equally over a number of years:

$$ \text{Annual income} = \text{Subscriptions received} - \text{Opening arrears} + \text{Opening prepayments} + \text{Closing arrears} - \text{Closing prepayments} $$

Then:

$$ \text{Total Life Membership Income} = \text{Life Membership Fee} \times \text{Number of Life Members} $$

Remember to include **new life members who joined during the year** when calculating the number of life members at the year end.
