## Sources of finance and fundraising

The main sources of finance available to clubs and societies include:

* Annual subscriptions

* Life memberships

* Donations

* Grants

* Bank loans and overdrafts

* Fundraising events

* Trading activities

### Annual subscriptions

Annual subscriptions are usually the **main source of income** for a club or society. Members pay them regularly in exchange for access to the organisation's facilities or services.

An increase in subscriptions may generate additional income, but it could also cause some members to cancel their memberships.

### Life memberships

A life membership involves a member making a **one-off payment** in return for membership for the rest of their life.

The member does not have to pay annual subscriptions after becoming a life member.

### Donations

Donations are one-off amounts given to the club or society.

There are two main types:

* **General donations** can be used for any purpose.

* **Restricted donations** must be used for the specific purpose stated by the donor.

For example, a donation may be restricted to paying for a building extension.

### Grants

Grants are amounts received from governments or other organisations.

Some grants are intended to cover several years. In this case, the entire amount is not treated as income immediately. The appropriate amount is recognised as income each year.

### Bank loans and overdrafts

Loans and overdrafts provide borrowed finance that must eventually be repaid. Interest is normally charged on the amount borrowed.

### Fundraising events

Clubs and societies can raise funds through events such as:

* Special dinners

* Quiz nights

* Tournaments

* Collection boxes

* Email appeals

* Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding involves using an online platform to obtain smaller donations from a large number of people for a particular purpose.

### Trading activities

A club or society may operate activities such as a **shop, café or bar** to generate additional income.

These activities are treated separately using a statement of profit or loss. Any resulting profit is transferred to the income and expenditure account.

### Evaluating sources of finance

When choosing a source of finance, consider both financial and non-financial factors.

**Cost:** Consider whether the finance has a cost and whether interest must be paid.

**Repayment:** A loan must be repaid, whereas subscriptions and donations generally do not.

**Cash flow:** Consider whether the finance provides cash immediately or generates regular amounts over time.

**Effect on the accounts**

Consider how the source of finance will affect:

* Income and expenditure account

* Statement of financial position

**Non-financial factors**

Consider whether the source of finance could affect:

* Membership numbers

* Quality of membership

* Reputation of the club or society

A recommendation should consider the advantages and disadvantages of each option before reaching a justified conclusion
