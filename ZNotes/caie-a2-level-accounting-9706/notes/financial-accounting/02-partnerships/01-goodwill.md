# Partnership

## **Goodwill (Theory)**

### **What is Goodwill?**

* ### Goodwill is an **intangible non-current asset** that represents the value of a business's reputation, customer loyalty, brand name, and other factors that allow it to earn higher profits than a normal business.

* It is the difference between the value of a business and the value of its identifiable net assets.

* **Goodwill = Value of business − Fair value of net assets**

### **Why is Goodwill Created?**

Goodwill is created because a business may have advantages that cannot be physically measured, such as:

* A good reputation

* Loyal customers

* A strong brand name

* Skilled employees

* Good location

* High-quality products/services

* Good relationships with suppliers

These factors allow the business to earn **higher profits than expected**.

### **Why is Goodwill Needed?**

Goodwill is needed because:

* It recognizes the **extra value** of a business beyond its physical assets.

* It helps determine the **true value of a business** when it is sold or when ownership changes.

* It ensures partners receive a fair share when there is a change in the partnership structure (e.g., admission or retirement of a partner).

### **Types of Goodwill**

#### **1. Inherent Goodwill**

**Inherent goodwill is the goodwill that naturally exists within a business due to its reputation and success.**

Examples:

* A famous brand name

* Loyal customers

* Excellent reputation

**Key point:**

* It is **not purchased** and does not appear in the accounting records.

* It is difficult to measure accurately.

#### **2. Purchased Goodwill**

**Purchased goodwill is goodwill that arises when one business buys another business for more than the fair value of its net assets.**

Example:

A business has:

* Net assets worth $100 000

* Purchase price = $130 000

Goodwill: $130 000 − $100 000 = **$30 000 purchased goodwill**
