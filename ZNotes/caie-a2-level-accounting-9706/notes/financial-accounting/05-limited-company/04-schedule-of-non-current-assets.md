# **Schedule of Non-current Assets**

## Schedule of non current assets

A schedule of non current assets is a **note to the statement of financial position** required under IAS 16.

It shows movements in:

* Cost or valuation

* Accumulated depreciation

* Carrying amount

for each class of non current asset.

## Cost or valuation

Start with the balance at the beginning of the year.

Then:

* Add additions or acquisitions.

* Subtract disposals.

* Add or subtract revaluations.

This gives the closing cost or valuation.

Closing Cost=Opening Cost\+Additions−Disposals±Revaluation

## Accumulated depreciation

Start with opening accumulated depreciation.

Then:

* Add the depreciation charge for the year.

* Subtract accumulated depreciation relating to disposed assets.

* Subtract accumulated depreciation relating to revalued assets.

This gives closing accumulated depreciation.

# Closing Accumulated Depreciation=Opening Accumulated Depreciation\+Depreciation Charge−Depreciation on Disposals−Depreciation Eliminated on Revaluation

# Carrying amount

The carrying amount is calculated as:

# Carrying Amount=Cost or Valuation−Accumulated Depreciation

The schedule should show carrying amounts at the **beginning and end of the year**.

## Treatment of transactions

**Addition**

Add the cost to the cost section.

No entry is made in accumulated depreciation when the asset is purchased.

**Disposal**

* Deduct the original cost.

* Deduct the accumulated depreciation relating to the asset.

**Upward revaluation**

* Add the increase in value to cost or valuation.

* Eliminate the existing accumulated depreciation.

**Downward revaluation**

* Deduct the decrease in value.

* Eliminate the existing accumulated depreciation.

**Depreciation**

Add the year's depreciation charge to accumulated depreciation.

## Revaluation

When an asset is revalued, its existing accumulated depreciation is **eliminated**.

The asset then appears at its new fair value with **zero accumulated depreciation** immediately after the revaluation.
