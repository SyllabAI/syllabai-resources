## Financial Statement

### Statement of profit or loss

The statement of profit or loss is prepared **after the manufacturing account**.

For the trading section:

* Use opening and closing **finished goods**.

* Use **goods at transfer value** instead of purchases.

* Include any finished goods purchased for resale.

* Calculate gross profit.

* Add factory profit.

* Adjust for the change in the provision for unrealised profit.

Factory expenses are already included in the cost of production, so only **non-production expenses** such as advertising and carriage outwards are included afterwards.

### Cost of sales

$$ \text{Cost of Sales} = \text{Opening Finished Goods} + \text{Goods at Transfer Value} + \text{Finished Goods Purchased} - \text{Closing Finished Goods} $$

Then:

$$ \text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Cost of Sales} $$

### Statement of financial position

The statement of financial position is prepared in the usual way.

The manufacturing business will have three types of inventory shown as current assets:

* Raw materials

* Work in progress

* Finished goods

The **finished goods inventory is valued at cost**, after removing any unrealised factory profit.

### Manufacturing profit

Manufacturing profit is the profit added by the factory to the cost of production.

It is transferred to the trading section of the statement of profit or loss.

The purpose is to treat the manufacturing and trading sections as separate activities and measure the performance of the manufacturing operation.

### Unrealised profit

Factory profit included in **unsold finished goods** has not yet been realised through a sale to an outside customer.

This profit must therefore be removed from the value of closing inventory.


Example: A small shop usually makes **Rs. 50,000 profit per month**, but suddenly claims it made **Rs. 5 million profit in one month** without any major change in sales. That would be an **unrealistic profit**.

The **provision for unrealised profit** removes the factory profit element from unsold inventory so that the inventory is stated at cost.

### Provision for unrealised profit

If factory profit is 20% of cost:

$$ \text{Unrealised Profit} = \frac{20}{120} \times \text{Closing Finished Goods at Transfer Value} $$

The same calculation is made for opening finished goods to find the opening provision.

If the provision **increases**, the increase is deducted in the statement of profit or loss.

If the provision **decreases**, the decrease is added.
