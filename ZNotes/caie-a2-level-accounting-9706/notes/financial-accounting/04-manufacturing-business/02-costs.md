## **Costs and inventory**

### Direct costs

Direct costs can be **directly linked to the production of the goods**.

The three types are:

### **Direct materials**

Raw materials used to make the goods, such as wood, nails, glue and paint.

### **Direct labour**

Wages paid to employees directly involved in making, assembling or packaging the goods.

### **Direct expenses**

Other costs directly linked to producing the goods, such as royalties or hiring specific tools or machinery.

### Prime cost

Prime cost is the total of all direct costs.

$$ \text{Prime Cost} = \text{Direct Materials} + \text{Direct Labour} + \text{Direct Expenses} $$

### Indirect costs

Indirect costs cannot be directly linked to a particular product.

They are also called **factory overheads** and are the costs of running the factory.

Examples include:

* Factory rent

* Depreciation of machinery

* Wages of supervisors or cleaners

* Factory heating and lighting

Only **factory-related expenses** are included as factory overheads. Office and selling expenses are not included in the cost of production.

### **Inventory in a manufacturing business**

A manufacturing business has three types of inventory because its goods can be at different stages of production:

* **Raw materials**: materials waiting to be used in production.

* **Work in progress**: goods that are partly completed.

* **Finished goods**: goods that are completely manufactured and ready for sale.

All three types have opening and closing inventory balances.
