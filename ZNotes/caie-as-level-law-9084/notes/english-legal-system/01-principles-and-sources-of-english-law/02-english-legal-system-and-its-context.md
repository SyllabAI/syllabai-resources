## English Legal System and its Context

### **Legal Systems Around The World**

1. **Civil Law (Codified)**

* Characteristics:

* It originated in continental Europe and Asia, reflecting Roman legal traditions.

* Laws are systematically codified into comprehensive statutes, providing a detailed and structured legal framework.

* Historical Context:

* The roots of codification trace back to the Roman Emperor Justinian's codification of laws in the 6th century.

* Example:

* The Napoleonic Code in France, introduced in 1804 under Napoleon Bonaparte, is an influential codification of civil law principles.

1. **Common Law**

* Characteristics:

* It developed in England and expanded through British colonial influence.

* Emphasises the role of judicial decisions and precedent, fostering a dynamic and evolving legal system.

* Historical Development:

* It emerged during the medieval period through royal courts' decisions, evolving from local customs to a unified legal system.

* Example:

* The English legal system, rooted in medieval and early modern England, has significantly shaped the legal systems of the United States, Canada, and other Commonwealth nations.

1. **Customary Law**

* Characteristics:

* Evolves from societal customs, traditions, and practices.

* It is often prominent in tribal or indigenous communities, where oral traditions and community consensus are crucial.

* Cultural Significance:

* Reflects the cultural diversity and historical practices of specific communities.

* Example:

* Indigenous legal systems in Native American tribes incorporate customary practices to address disputes and maintain community order.

1. **Religious Law**

* Characteristics:

* Derives legal principles from religious doctrines and sacred texts.

* Emphasises moral and ethical conduct, intertwining legal and religious norms.

* Cultural Impact:

* Shapes legal systems with a moral foundation.

* Example:

* Sharia law in Islamic countries draws extensively from the Quran and Hadith, establishing a legal framework aligned with Islamic teachings.

1. **Mixed Legal Systems**

* Characteristics:

* Combine elements of different legal traditions due to historical influences or colonisation.

* Reflect on the complexities of legal evolution in culturally diverse societies.

* Legal Hybridity:

* Often, it emerges in post-colonial contexts.

* Example:

* South Africa's legal system blends Roman-Dutch civil law and English common law, reflecting the historical influences of colonisation and diverse cultural backgrounds.

### **Adversarial and Inquisitorial Systems**

1. **Adversarial System**

* Characteristics:

* Emphasises an active role for opposing parties in presenting evidence and arguments.

* A neutral judge or jury must render decisions based on evidence and legal arguments.

* Legal Culture:

* Fosters a competitive and rights-oriented legal culture.

* Example:

* The adversarial system, prevalent in the United States, promotes the idea of legal disputes as contests where each side vigorously advocates its position.

1. **Inquisitorial System**

* Characteristics:

* It places greater responsibility on the judge to investigate and determine facts.

* Involves active judicial involvement in examining evidence and questioning witnesses.

* Legal Philosophy:

* Emphasises uncovering the truth as a primary goal.

* Example:

* Common in civil law countries like France and Germany, the inquisitorial system ensures a comprehensive and directed inquiry into legal matters.

### **The Rule of Law and its Application to Law Making, the Legal System and Substantive Law**

- D**efinition:**

* The principle that all individuals and institutions are subject to and accountable under the law promotes fairness, predictability, and justice.

- **Application to Law Making:**

* Ensures laws are clear, accessible, and apply equally to all, preventing arbitrary rule.

- **Application to the Legal System:**

* Requires a fair, impartial, and transparent legal system, fostering public trust.

- **Application to Substantive Law:**

* Mandates that laws protect fundamental rights and adhere to constitutional principles, preventing abuse of power.

### **The Difference Between Civil and Criminal Law**

1. **Civil Law**

* Purpose:

* Resolves disputes between private parties, seeking compensation or specific performance.

* Outcome:

* Monetary compensation, restitution, or injunctive relief.

* Example:

* Contract disputes, personal injury cases, family law matters.

1. **Criminal Law**

* Purpose:

* Punishes offences against the state, emphasising deterrence and societal protection.

* Outcome:

* Fines, imprisonment, or other penalties.

* Example:

* Theft, murder, assault, white-collar crimes.

### **The Relationship Between Law and Morality**

- **Law and Morality Distinct:**

* Legal systems may not always align with personal or societal moral principles, as laws often serve practical and societal objectives.

- **Ethical Considerations:**

* The relationship prompts ongoing debate about the moral underpinnings of legal decisions.

- Example:

* The legality of specific medical procedures, like euthanasia or assisted suicide, may differ from moral perspectives.

### **Law and Justice**

- **Justice in Legal Systems:**

* Strives for fairness, equality, and due process, ensuring that legal processes lead to just outcomes.

- **Legal Ethics:**

* Upholds principles of fairness and equity in legal proceedings.

- Example:

* A fair trial, where evidence is presented impartially, and legal procedures are followed, contributes to the pursuit of justice.

### **The Role of Law in Society**

- **Order and Stability:**

* Provides a framework for societal order and stability, offering predictability and resolution for disputes.

- **Social Contract:**

* Reflects the implicit agreement between citizens and the state.

- Example:

* Traffic laws contribute to road safety and order, preventing chaos and accidents.

### **The Importance of Fault in Civil and Criminal Law**

1. **Civil Law**

* Fault Standard:

* Based on concepts of negligence or breach of duty, where responsibility is assigned for harm caused.

* Legal Accountability:

* Requires individuals to be held accountable for their actions.

* Example:

* A driver causing an accident due to reckless behaviour is liable for damages.

1. **Criminal Law**

* Fault Standard:

* Requires a guilty mind (Mens rea) for conviction, emphasising personal culpability.

* Legal and Moral Accountability:

* Links legal consequences to moral culpability.

* Example:

* Murder charges require the intent to kill or cause serious harm, demonstrating the importance of proving criminal intent for a conviction.
