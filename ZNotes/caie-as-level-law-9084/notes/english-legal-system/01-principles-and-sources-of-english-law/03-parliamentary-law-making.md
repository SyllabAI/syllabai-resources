## Parliamentary Law Making

### **The Legislative Process from Green Paper to Royal Assent, Role of the House of Commons and the House of Lords in the process, Different Types of Bill**

The legislative process in the United Kingdom is a comprehensive journey from the initial proposal of a law to its enactment through Royal Assent. This process involves several stages: the participation of both the House of Commons and the House of Lords and the consideration of various bills.

 1. **Green Paper:**
    * Definition: The process often begins with issuing a Green Paper, a government document that presents proposals for discussion and consultation.
    * Purpose: It allows public input and stakeholder feedback before formal legislative drafting.
 2. **White Paper:**
    * Definition: After consultation and consideration of feedback, the government may publish a White Paper, which outlines specific policy proposals and serves as a basis for potential legislation.
    * Formalization: The White Paper signals a more concrete intention to legislate.
 3. **Drafting the Bill:**
    * Preparation: Legal experts draft the proposed legislation, turning policy proposals into a precise legal framework.
    * Scrutiny: The draft undergoes internal scrutiny within government departments and may involve input from legal advisors.
 4. **First Reading:**
    * Introduction: The bill is introduced to either the House of Commons or the House of Lords.
    * Formality: This stage is largely formal, with no debate on the bill's content.
 5. **Second Reading:**
    * Debate: Members of the respective house discuss the general principles and themes of the bill.
    * Vote: A vote is taken at the end of the debate to determine whether the bill should proceed.
 6. **Committee Stage:**
    * Detailed Examination: A committee of members scrutinizes the bill in detail, considering each clause and the proposed amendment.
    * House-Specific Committees: In the House of Commons, this often involves Public Bill Committees, while in the House of Lords, it is managed by the Committee of the Whole House.
 7. **Report Stage:**
    * Consideration of Amendments: The bill, along with any proposed amendments from the committee stage, is considered by the whole house.
    * Further Amendments: Further amendments may be made during this stage.
 8. **Third Reading:**
    * Final Review: The final version of the bill is debated.
    * Approval: Members vote on the overall content of the bill.
 9. **House of Lords/Commons:**
    * Consideration in the Other House: If the bill started in the House of Commons, it is sent to the House of Lords (and vice versa).
    * Similar Stages: The bill goes through similar stages in the second house: first reading, second reading, committee stage, report stage, and third reading.
10. **Consideration of Amendments:**
    * Ping-Pong: If the second house makes amendments, the bill returns to the first house. This process may go back and forth until an agreement is reached.
11. **Royal Assent:**
    * Formal Approval: Once both houses agree on the final version, the bill is sent to the monarch for Royal Assent.
    * Becomes Law: After Royal Assent is granted, the bill becomes law and is referred to as an Act of Parliament. Types of Bills:
12. **Public Bills:**
    * Definition: Affect the general public and cover a wide range of issues.
    * Examples: Criminal Justice Bill, Education Bill.
13. **Private Bills:**
    * Definition: Affect specific individuals or organizations.
    * Examples: Bills related to local government or specific businesses.
14. **Hybrid Bills:**
    * Definition: Contain elements of both public and private bills.
    * Example: High-Speed Rail (London - West Midlands) Bill.
15. **Money Bills:**
    * Definition: Solely concerns national taxation or public expenditure.
    * House of Lords Role: Limited to delaying for a maximum of one month.

### **Parliamentary Supremacy**

* **Parliamentary Supremacy**: Parliament is the supreme legal authority, and other authorities cannot override its laws.
* **Legal Validity**: Parliament's statutes precede common law and any conflicting constitutional provisions.

### **Influences on Parliament**

1. **Political Pressure:**
   * Party Politics: The political landscape and party agendas influence legislative priorities.
   * Coalition Dynamics: In coalition governments, negotiations between parties shape legislative decisions.
2. **Public Opinion:**
   * Referendums: Direct expressions of public opinion can influence legislative decisions.
   * Public Demonstrations: Mass movements may impact political decisions.
3. **Pressure Groups:**
   * Advocacy: Groups representing specific interests actively lobby parliamentarians.
   * Policy Influence: Successful pressure groups can significantly shape legislation.
4. **Media Influence:**
   * Public Perception: Media coverage can influence public opinion and political decisions.
   * Agenda-Setting: Media can set the legislative agenda by highlighting specific issues.
