## Police Powers

### **Stop and Search: ss1-7 Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE), PACE Code A, Misuse of Drugs Act 1971, Terrorism Act 2000**


1. **Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984 (PACE):**
   * Sections 1–7: Grant police officers the power to stop and search individuals under certain circumstances.
   * Purpose: To prevent and detect crime, ensuring the public's safety.
2. **PACE Code A:**
   * Guidance: Provides the legal framework and procedural guidelines for the exercise of stop and search powers.
   * Accountability: Emphasizes the need for transparency and accountability in using these powers.
3. **Misuse of Drugs Act 1971:**
   * Role: Authorizes stop and search powers specifically for offences related to controlled substances.
   * Target: Aimed at combating drug-related crimes.
4. **Terrorism Act 2000:**
   * Extension of Powers: Grants additional powers for stop and search in the context of counter-terrorism efforts.
   * Heightened Scrutiny: Due to the potential impact on civil liberties, such searches are subject to increased oversight.

### **Arrest: s24 PACE, as amended by Serious Organised Crime and Police Act 2005, PACE Code G**


1. **Section 24 PACE:**
   * Power to Arrest: Outlines the circumstances in which a police officer can arrest a person without a warrant.
   * Grounds for Arrest: Includes suspicion of committing an offence, intent to cause physical injury, or preventing a breach of the peace.
2. **Serious Organised Crime and Police Act 2005:**
   * Amendment: Changes were introduced to the arrest powers outlined in s24 PACE.
   * Focus: Emphasis on addressing serious organized crime and enhancing police capabilities.
3. **PACE Code G:**
   * Guidance: Provides detailed guidance on exercising arrest powers, ensuring consistency and legality.
   * Rights of the Arrested Person: Outlines the rights of individuals upon arrest, including the right to legal representation.

### **Detention of Suspects at Police Station: Time Limits (ss34-46 PACE, PACE Code C), Rights of the Detained Person (s56 PACE, PACE Code C), Appropriate Adult (PACE Code C), Interviews (s60 and s60A PACE, PACE Codes E and F)**


1. **Time Limits (ss34–46 PACE; PACE Code C):**
   * Initial Detention: Provides time limits for the initial detention of suspects without charge.
   * Extension: Allows for extensions under specific circumstances, subject to authorization.
2. **Rights of the Detained Person (s56 PACE; PACE Code C):**
   * Notification: Inform the detained person of their rights, including the right to legal advice.
   * Consultation: Ensures the detained person understands their rights and the reason for their detention.
3. **Appropriate Adult (PACE Code C):**
   * Role: Ensures the welfare of vulnerable detainees, such as juveniles or those with mental health issues.
   * Support: Assists the detained person in understanding their rights and the legal process.
4. **Interviews (s60 and s60A PACE; PACE Codes E and F):**
   * s60 PACE: Allows for the recording of interviews with suspects.
   * s60A PACE: Provides for the use of live links for the questioning of suspects.

### **Treatment of Suspects at Police Stations: Searches (s54 and s55 PACE, PACE Code C), Fingerprints (s61 PACE), Samples (s62 and s63 PACE), Physical Conditions of Interview Room (PACE Code C), Role of the Custody Officer (PACE Code C)**


1. **Searches (s54 and s55 PACE; PACE Code C):**
   * Purpose: Authorizes the search of detainees, clothing, and personal items.
   * Safeguards: Includes guidelines to ensure searches are conducted respectfully and lawfully.
2. **Fingerprints (s61 PACE):**
   * Authority: Grants the police the power to take fingerprints from detained individuals.
   * Identification: Aids in establishing the identity of the suspect.
3. **Samples (s62 and s63 PACE):**
   * Collection: The police can obtain samples, such as DNA, from detained individuals.
   * Forensic Evidence: Assists in the investigation and forensic analysis of cases.
4. **Physical Conditions of Interview Room (PACE Code C):**
   * Guidelines: Ensures that the physical conditions of interview rooms are appropriate and meet legal standards.
   * Comfort: Recognizes the importance of creating an environment conducive to fair and effective interviews.
5. **Role of the Custody Officer (PACE Code C):**
   * Responsibility: The custody officer is responsible for overseeing the welfare and rights of the detained person.
   * Decision-Making: Determining whether to authorize detention is crucial in safeguarding the detainee's rights.

### **Impact of the Breach of These Rules: Statements Obtained Through Oppression (s76 PACE), Exclusion of Evidence (s78 PACE)**


1. **Statements Obtained Through Oppression (s76 PACE):**
   * Definition: Statements obtained under oppressive conditions, such as through torture or inhumane treatment.
   * Inadmissibility: Such statements are deemed inadmissible in court.
2. **Exclusion of Evidence (s78 PACE):**
   * Discretionary Power: The court can exclude evidence if its admission would render the trial unfair.
   * Balancing Test: Considers the impact on the fairness of the proceedings against the public interest in admitting the evidence.

* In summary, the legal framework surrounding stop and search, arrest, and the treatment of suspects at police stations is governed by PACE and associated codes. The process is designed to balance the powers of law enforcement with the protection of individuals' rights. Breaches of these rules can lead to the exclusion of evidence, emphasizing the importance of adhering to legal procedures during the investigation and detention of suspects.
