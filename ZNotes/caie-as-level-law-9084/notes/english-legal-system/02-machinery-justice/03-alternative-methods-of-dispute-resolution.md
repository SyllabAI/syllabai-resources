## Alternative Methods of Dispute Resolution

### **Negotiation, Conciliation and Mediation**


1. **Negotiation:**
   * Process: Parties communicate directly or through legal representatives to settle.
   * Informality: Flexible and informal process.
2. **Conciliation:**
   * Process: A neutral third party assists the parties in settling.
   * Role: Facilitator, not decision-maker.
3. **Mediation:**
   * Process: A neutral mediator facilitates communication between parties to help them reach an agreement.
   * Voluntary: Parties retain control over the outcome.

### **Arbitration, Arbitration Act 1996, Scott v Avery Clauses**


1. **Arbitration:**
   * Process: Parties agree to submit their dispute to an independent arbitrator whose decision is binding.
   * Advantages: Confidential, quicker, and less formal than court proceedings.
2. **Arbitration Act 1996:**
   * Purpose: Provides a framework for conducting arbitration proceedings and enforcing arbitration awards.
   * Key Features: Party autonomy, appointment of arbitrators, and enforcement provisions.
3. **Scott v Avery Clauses:**
   * Definition: Contractual clauses that require parties to use a specific method of alternative dispute resolution (ADR) before resorting to litigation.
   * Enforceability: Courts generally uphold such clauses, emphasizing the importance of ADR in resolving disputes.
