## Civil Courts and Civil Process

### **Roles and Jurisdiction of Various Courts in the UK**


1. **Magistrates' Court:**
   * Role: Deals with minor criminal offences (summary offences), such as petty theft or public disorder.
   * Jurisdiction: Can impose limited fines and short custodial sentences.
2. **County Court:**
   * Role: Hears civil and family cases, including contract disputes, personal injury claims, and family matters.
   * Jurisdiction: Deals with cases of moderate financial value, typically up to £100,000.
3. **High Court:**
   * Role: Handles complex civil cases, including major contractual disputes, large personal injury claims, and judicial reviews.
   * Jurisdiction: No financial limit; wide-ranging authority.
4. **Court of Appeal:**
   * Role: Hears appeals from the High Court and certain tribunals.
   * Jurisdiction: Consists of both the Civil and Criminal Divisions.
5. **Supreme Court:**
   * Role: The highest court in the UK; hears appeals on important points of law.
   * Jurisdiction: Replaced the House of Lords as the highest appellate court.

### **The Woolf Reforms**

* **Overview**: Implemented in 1999, the Woolf reforms aimed to streamline and modernize civil litigation in England and Wales.
* **Key Features:**
  * Pre-Action Protocols: Encourage early settlement through communication before litigation.
  * Case Management: Emphasis on active judicial case management to control costs and ensure efficient progression.
  * Allocation and Fast Track: Introduced the concept of multi-track, fast track, and small claims to categorize cases based on complexity and financial value.

### **Pre-Trial Procedures**


1. **Pre-Action Protocols:**
   * Purpose: Encourage early communication, exchange of information, and settlement discussions before formal litigation.
   * Benefits: Aims to reduce the number of cases reaching court and promote cost-effective resolution.
2. **Case Management Conference (CMC):**
   * Purpose: A court meeting to discuss and plan the future progress of the case.
   * Key Elements: Timetables, disclosure of documents, expert evidence, and settlement discussions may be addressed.
3. **Disclosure:**
   * Purpose: Parties must disclose relevant documents to the other side.
   * Types: Standard disclosure, specific disclosure, and disclosure of electronic documents.
4. **Witness Statements:**
   * Purpose: Written statements by witnesses outlining the evidence they intend to give at trial.
   * Submission: Usually exchanged before trial.

### **Allocation of Cases - Small Claims, Fast Track and Multi-Track**


1. **Small Claims:**
   * Criteria: Cases where the financial value does not exceed a certain threshold (e.g., £10,000).
   * Procedure: Simplified, informal procedures; parties often represent themselves.
2. **Fast Track:**
   * Criteria: Cases of moderate financial value (e.g., £10,000 to £25,000) with straightforward legal issues.
   * Procedure: Streamlined process; shorter trial duration.
3. **Multi-Track:**
   * Criteria: Complex cases with a high financial value (e.g., over £25,000).
   * Procedure: Comprehensive and flexible process to accommodate complexity.

### **Appeals**

* Process: The losing party in a lower court may appeal to a higher court, seeking a review of the decision.
* Grounds for Appeal: Typically, errors of law or procedure, unfairness, or new evidence.
