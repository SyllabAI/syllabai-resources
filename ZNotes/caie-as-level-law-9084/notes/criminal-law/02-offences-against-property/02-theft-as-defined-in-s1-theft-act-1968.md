## Theft as Defined in s1 Theft Act 1968

### **Definition (s1 Theft Act 1967)**

* **Actus reus:**
  * Appropriation of property belonging to another.
  * Property includes money and all other property, real or personal, including things in action and other intangible property.
* **Mens rea:**
  * Dishonesty.
  * The intention is to deprive the owner of the property permanently.

**s2 – Dishonesty:**

* **Definition:**
  * A person's appropriation of property is dishonest if they do not believe they have the right to deprive the other of it.
  * Subjective test: Focuses on the individual's state of mind regarding dishonesty.

**s3 – Appropriation:**

* **Definition:**
  * Any assumption of the rights of an owner, including outright taking, moving, and even not returning borrowed property.
  * A broad concept that covers various ways of interfering with the rights of the owner.

**s4 – Property:**

* **Definition:**
  * Property includes money and all other property, real or personal, including things in action and other intangible property.
  * The definition is intentionally broad to cover a wide range of possessions.

**s5 – Belonging to Another:**

* **Definition:**
  * Property belongs to any person who has possession or control of it, or in the case of land not in the possession of any person, any person with an interest in it.

**s6 – Intention to Permanently Deprive:**

* **Definition:**
  * The defendant must intend to treat the property as their own to dispose of, regardless of the owner's rights.
  * This includes outright disposal, keeping the property, or using it to prevent the owner from recovering it.

**s7 – Sentencing:**

* **Penalties:**
  * Theft is a triable offence, which can be tried in the Magistrates' Court or the Crown Court.
  * The maximum sentence in the Magistrates' Court is seven years, while in the Crown Court, it is unlimited.
* **Aggravating Factors:**
  * The court considers various factors, including the property's value stolen, any aggravating factors, and the defendant's criminal history.
  * Aggravating factors might include the use of violence, targeting vulnerable victims, or being part of an organized crime group.
* **Mitigating Factors:**
  * Mitigating factors, such as remorse or cooperation with authorities, may be considered in sentencing.
  * The court aims to balance the severity of the offence with individual circumstances.

### **Summary**

* Theft (s1 Theft Act 1968):
  * Actus Reus: Appropriation of property belonging to another.
  * Mens Rea: Dishonesty and intention to permanently deprive.
* s2 – Dishonesty:
  * Subjective test of dishonesty.
* s3 – Appropriation:
  * Broad concept covering any assumption of the rights of an owner.
* s4 – Property:
  * Broad definition to include various forms of possessions.
* s5 – Belonging to Another:
  * Ownership is determined by possession, control, or interest.
* s6 – Intention to Permanently Deprive:
  * Intent to treat the property as their own, preventing the owner from recovering it.
* s7 – Sentencing:
  * Triable either way, with penalties ranging from fines to imprisonment.
  * Aggravating factors and mitigating factors are considered in sentencing.
