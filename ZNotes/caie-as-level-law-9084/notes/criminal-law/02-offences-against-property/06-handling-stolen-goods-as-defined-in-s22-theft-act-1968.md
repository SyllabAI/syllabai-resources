## Handling Stolen Goods as Defined in s22 Theft Act 1968

**Actus Reus:**

Handling stolen goods, as defined in Section 22 of the Theft Act 1968, encompasses several actions related to unlawfully dealing with property that has been stolen:


1. **Receiving, Retaining, or Disposing of Stolen Goods:**
   * This includes acquiring, holding, or getting rid of stolen property.
   * The actus reus is fulfilled when an individual engages in any of these actions with goods they know or believe to be stolen.
2. **Assisting in the Disposal of Stolen Goods:**
   * A person can be charged with handling stolen goods if they assist in disposing of such goods, even if they are not the primary recipient.
   * This extends the scope to those who contribute to the stolen property movement or transfer.

**Mens rea:**

The mens rea for handling stolen goods revolves around the knowledge or belief that the goods in question are stolen, coupled with the intent to dishonestly avoid the owner's rights by dealing with the stolen property:


1. **Knowledge or Belief:**
   * The defendant must be aware or believe that the goods they are dealing with are stolen.
   * This requirement emphasizes a level of awareness on the part of the individual, distinguishing inadvertent actions from intentional involvement.
2. **Intent to Dishonestly Avoid Owner's Rights:**
   * The handling must be done to avoid dishonestly ignoring the owner's rights in the stolen property.
   * This underscores the dishonesty in the defendant's actions, emphasizing an intention to undermine the rightful owner's interests.

### **Sentencing**

Handling stolen goods is considered a serious offence, and as such, it is classified as an indictable offence, necessitating trial in the Crown Court:


1. **Maximum Sentence:**
   * The maximum sentence for handling stolen goods is fourteen years' imprisonment.
   * This substantial penalty reflects the gravity of the offence and its potential impact on victims.
2. **Factors Considered in Sentencing:**
   * The court takes into account various factors when determining the appropriate sentence.
   * The nature and value of the stolen goods, the degree of the defendant's involvement, and their criminal history are all considered.
   * Aggravating factors, such as involvement in organized crime, may lead to more severe sentencing.

### **Summary**

* Actus reus:
  * It involves receiving, retaining, or disposing of stolen goods.
  * Handling goods known or believed to be stolen.
  * Includes assisting in the disposal of stolen goods.
* Mens rea:
  * Requires knowledge or belief that the goods are stolen.
  * Intent to dishonestly avoid the owner's rights by dealing with the stolen property.
* Sentencing:
  * The indictable offence was tried in the Crown Court.
  * Maximum sentence: Fourteen years' imprisonment.
  * Factors considered in sentencing include the nature and value of the stolen goods, the level of the defendant's involvement, and their criminal history.
