## Blackmail as Defined in s21 Theft Act 1968

### **Definition (s21 Theft Act 1968)**

* Blackmail is a criminal offence defined under Section 21 of the Theft Act 1968. It involves making unwarranted demands with menaces, intending to gain something for oneself or cause a loss to another.

**Actus reus:**

* Making Unwarranted Demands with Menaces:
  * Unwarranted demands involve seeking something to which the person making the demand has no reasonable claim.
  * Menaces refer to threats, whether express or implied, that would cause a reasonable person to fear for their safety or the safety of others.

**Mens rea:**

* Intent to Gain or Cause a Loss:
  * The defendant must have the intention to gain for themselves or cause a loss to another by making unwarranted demands with menace.

### **Sentencing**

* **Severity of Offense:**
  * Blackmail is a serious offence due to its potential for harm and manipulation.
  * It is an indictable offence, meaning it is tried in the Crown Court.
* **Maximum Sentence:**
  * The maximum sentence for blackmail is fourteen years imprisonment.
* **Factors Considered in Sentencing:**
  * The court considers various factors, including the nature and seriousness of the threats, the impact on the victim, any financial gain by the defendant, and the defendant's criminal history.

### **Extended Explanation**

* The mens rea for blackmail requires the defendant to have the intent to gain something for themselves or cause a loss to another. The essence of the offence lies in the wrongful intent behind making the demands. The defendant must be aware that their demands are unwarranted and accompanied by threats, and they must have the specific intention of gaining an advantage or causing a detriment.

* Given the severity of blackmail, it is classified as an indictable offence, meaning it is tried in the Crown Court. The maximum sentence for blackmail is fourteen years imprisonment. The court considers various factors in determining the appropriate sentence, including the nature and seriousness of the threats, the impact on the victim, any financial gain by the defendant, and the defendant's criminal history. Sentencing aims to reflect the gravity of the offence and the individual circumstances surrounding each case.
