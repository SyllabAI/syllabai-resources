## Burglary as Defined in s9 Theft Act 1968

### **Definition of s9 Theft Act 1968**

* Burglary is defined under Section 9 of the Theft Act 1968. There are two main categories: burglary under s9(1)(a) and (2), and burglary under s9(1)(b). Additionally, the Act outlines the concept of aggravated burglary under s10.

### **s9(1)(a) and (2) - Actus reus and Mens rea**

* **Actus Reus:**
  * Unlawful entry into a building or part of a building.
  * Trespassing with the intent to commit theft, grievous bodily harm (GBH), or criminal damage.
* **Mens rea:**
  * The defendant must intend to commit theft, GBH, or criminal damage at the time of entry.

### **s9(1)(b) - Actus reus and Mens rea**

* **Actus reus:**
  * Remaining in a building or part of a building as a trespasser.
  * Intent to commit theft, GBH, or criminal damage.
* **Mens rea:**
  * The defendant must have entered the building as a trespasser.

### **s9(3) - Sentencing**

* **Maximum Sentence:**
  * Burglary is a serious offence and is indictable only, meaning it must be tried in the Crown Court.
  * The maximum sentence for burglary is life imprisonment.
* **Factors Considered in Sentencing:**
  * The court considers factors such as the nature of the burglary, any harm caused, the defendant's criminal history, and any aggravating or mitigating circumstances.

### **s9(4) - A Building**

* **Definition:**
  * A building includes any inhabited structure, such as a house or even a temporary structure, if someone is present during the offence.

### **Aggravated Burglary as Defined in s10 Theft Act 1968**

* **Definition:**
  * Aggravated burglary involves committing burglary (s9) with the additional element of possessing a firearm or imitation firearm at the time of the offence.
* **Actus Reus and Mens rea:**
  * The actus reus and mens rea for aggravated burglary are the same as for burglary (s9).
  * The additional element is the possession of a firearm or imitation firearm.

### **Extended Explanation**

**s9(1)(a) and (2) – Burglary Actus Reus and Mens Rea:**

* Burglary under s9(1)(a) and (2) involves the actus reus of unlawfully entering a building or part of a building. This could be through physical force, threats, or any other means of unauthorized entry. The mens rea requires the defendant to intend to commit theft, GBH, or criminal damage at the time of entry.

**s9(1)(b) – Burglary Actus Reus and Mens Rea:**

* Burglary under s9(1)(b) involves the actus reus of remaining in a building or part of a building as a trespasser. The defendant must have entered the building as a trespasser, and the mens rea requires the intent to commit theft, GBH, or criminal damage.

**s9(3) – Sentencing:**

* Burglary is a serious offence, and being indictable only, it must be tried in the Crown Court. The maximum sentence is life imprisonment. The court considers various factors in sentencing, including the nature of the burglary, any harm caused, the defendant's criminal history, and any aggravating or mitigating circumstances.

**s9(4) – A Building:**

* "Building" is broadly defined under s9(4) and includes any inhabited structure. This can range from traditional houses to temporary structures if someone is present during the offence.

**Aggravated Burglary as Defined in s10:**

* Aggravated burglary involves the same Actus reus and Mens rea as burglary under s9 but with the additional element of possessing a firearm or imitation firearm at the time of the offence. This elevates the seriousness of the offence, and the maximum penalty remains life imprisonment.

### **Summary**

* s9(1)(a) and (2) – Actus Reus and Mens Rea:
  * Actus Reus: Unlawful entry into a building or part of a building.
  * Mens Rea: Intent to commit theft, grievous bodily harm (GBH), or criminal damage at the time of entry.
* s9(1)(b) – Actus Reus and Mens Rea:
  * Actus Reus: Remaining in or part of a building as a trespasser.
  * Mens Rea: Entered the building as a trespasser with the intent to commit theft, GBH, or criminal damage.
* s9(3) – Sentencing:
  * Maximum Sentence: Life imprisonment.
  * Factors Considered: Nature of the burglary, harm caused, criminal history, and any aggravating or mitigating circumstances.
* s9(4) – A Building:
  * Definition: "Building" broadly includes any inhabited structure, temporary or permanent, if someone is present during the offence.

**Aggravated Burglary as Defined in s10 Theft Act 1968:**

* Definition: Committing burglary (s9) with the additional element of possessing a firearm or imitation firearm.
* Actus Reus and Mens Rea: Same as burglary under s9, with the added element of firearm possession.
* Maximum Penalty: Life imprisonment.
