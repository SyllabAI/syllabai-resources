## Robbery as Defined in s8 Theft Act 1968

### **Definition (s8 Theft Act 1968)**

* Actus reus:
  * Theft.
  * And immediately before or at the time of doing so, and to do so, uses force on any person or puts or seeks to put any person in fear of being then and there subjected to force.
* **Mens rea:**
  * Intention to steal.
  * Knowledge or intention that force will be used to steal.

**Actus reus:**

* Theft:
  * The unlawful taking of property belonging to another to permanently deprive the owner of it.
* Use of Force or Threatening Force:
  * Force must be used or threatened immediately before or during the theft.
  * The force is applied to any person or is used to make any person afraid of being subjected to force.

**Mens Rea:**

* Intention to Steal:
  * The defendant must have the intention to steal the property.
  * This includes the intent to deprive the owner of the property permanently.
* Knowledge or Intention Regarding Force:
  * The defendant must either know that force will be used or intend that force will be used to steal.
  * Force is a crucial element that distinguishes robbery from simple theft.

### **Sentencing**

* **Severity of Offense:**
  * Robbery is a more severe offence than theft due to the added element of violence or the threat of violence.
  * The severity of the sentence reflects the increased harm caused by the use of force.
* **Triable Either Way:**
  * Robbery is a triable offence, meaning it can be tried in either the Magistrates' Court or the Crown Court.
* **Maximum Sentence:**
  * The maximum sentence for robbery is life imprisonment.
* **Aggravating Factors:**
  * Aggravating factors, such as the use of weapons, injuries to victims, or targeting of vulnerable individuals, may result in a more severe sentence.
* **Mitigating Factors:**
  * Mitigating factors, such as remorse or cooperation with authorities, may be considered in sentencing.
  * The court aims to balance the severity of the offence with individual circumstances.

### **Summary**

* Robbery (s8 Theft Act 1968):
  * Actus Reus: Theft with the use or threat of force.
  * Mens Rea: Intention to steal with knowledge or intention regarding using force.
* Actus Reus:
  * Involves the elements of theft and the immediate use or threat of force.
* Mens Rea:
  * Requires the intent to steal and knowledge or intention regarding using force.
* Sentencing:
  * Robbery is a more severe offence than theft.
  * Triable either way with a maximum sentence of life imprisonment.
  * Aggravating and mitigating factors are considered in sentencing.
