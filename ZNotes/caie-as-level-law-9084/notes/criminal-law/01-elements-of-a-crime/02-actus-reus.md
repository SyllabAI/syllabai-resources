## Actus reus

### **Actus reus as the Conduct Element of a Crime**

**Definition:**

* **Actus Reus:**
  * The physical act or conduct that constitutes a criminal offence.
  * It is one of the two essential elements of a crime, the other being mens rea (the mental element).

**Components:**

* **Voluntary Act:**
  * The act must be a voluntary, conscious movement.
  * Involuntary actions or reflex movements generally do not constitute the actus reus.
* **Possession:**
  * Possessing illegal items can also constitute actus reus (e.g., controlled substances, stolen property).
* **Result Crimes:**
  * Some crimes focus on the outcome (result crimes), where the act leads to a prohibited consequence.

### **Actus reus by Omission**

**Definition:**

* Omission:
  * Failing to act when there is a legal duty to act.
  * Imposing liability for not preventing harm or rendering assistance.

**Legal Duty to Act:**

* Special Relationships:
  * Certain relationships create a legal duty to act (e.g., parent-child, doctor-patient).
  * Failure to act in these relationships may lead to criminal liability.
* Contractual Obligations:
  * Contracts may impose duties, and a failure to fulfil these duties may constitute criminal omission.
* Statutory Duties:
  * Legislation can create duties to act in specific situations.

### **Causation**

**Definition:**

* **Causation:**
  * Establishing a causal link between the defendant's act and the prohibited consequence.

**Types:**

* **Factual Causation:**
  * The defendant's act must be a factual cause of the prohibited consequence.
  * The "but for" test is commonly used to determine factual causation.
* **Legal Causation (Remoteness):**
  * Even if the defendant's act is a factual cause, it must be legally sufficient to warrant criminal liability.
  * Intervening acts may break the chain of causation.

### **Extended Explanation**

**Actus reus as the Conduct Element of a Crime:**

* Actus reus is a crucial element in defining a criminal offence. It represents the physical act or conduct that constitutes the crime. For a criminal act to occur, the defendant must have voluntary and conscious movement. Possession of illegal items, such as controlled substances or stolen property, can also fulfil the actus reus element. In some cases, the focus is on the outcome (result of crimes), where the act leads to a prohibited consequence.

**Actus reus by Omission:**

* Actus reus by omission occurs when an individual fails to act in a situation where there is a legal duty to act. Special relationships, contractual obligations, and statutory duties can create this legal duty. For instance, a parent has a legal duty to protect their child, and a doctor has a legal duty to provide necessary medical care. Failing to fulfil these duties may result in criminal liability.

**Causation:**

* Causation directly links the defendant's act and the prohibited consequence. Factual causation is determined by the "but for" test, asking whether the consequence would have occurred "but for" the defendant's act. Legal causation assesses whether the defendant's act is legally sufficient to warrant criminal liability. Intervening acts may affect legal liability if they break the chain of causation. Causation is crucial for attributing responsibility for the prohibited consequence to the defendant's actions.

### **Summary**

* **Actus reus:**
  * The physical act or conduct component of a crime.
  * Complements the mental element (mens rea).
  * Includes voluntary acts, possession, and result crimes.
* **Actus reus by Omission:**
  * Failure to act when there's a legal duty.
  * The legal duty arises from special relationships, contractual obligations, or statutory duties.
* **Causation:**
  * Establishing a causal link between the defendant's act and the prohibited consequence.
  * Factual causation is determined by the "but for" test.
  * Legal causation assesses the sufficiency of the defendant's act to warrant criminal liability.
