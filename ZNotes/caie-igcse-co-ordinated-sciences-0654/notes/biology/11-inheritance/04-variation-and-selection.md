## Variation and selection

### **Variation**

* Observable differences between individuals of the same species.
  * **Genetic variation**: Differences caused by a difference in genes.
  * **Phenotypic variation**: Differences caused by a difference in either/both environment and genes.
  * **Discontinuous variation**: Traits with limited or no intermediates between two extreme values, usually due to genes only.
  * **Continuous variation**: Traits with a large range of phenotypes between two extreme values.

### **Mutation**

* A change in a gene or chromosome, the rate of formation of which can be accelerated by:
  * Chemicals such as tar in tobacco smoke
  * Ionizing radiation from X-rays
  * Ultraviolet radiation from sunlight

### **Selection**

| Natural selection | Artificial selection |
|----|----|
| Happens in nature on its own | This happens due to human intervention |
| Individuals with variations best suited to their environment survive longer and reproduce | Individuals that possess desirable variations to humans survive longer and reproduce |
| All that survive long enough pass on their alleles to their offspring | Only the most desirable individuals are picked to pass on their alleles to their offspring |
| Increases species’ chance of survival in the wild | No change or decreased chances of surviving in the wild |
| More variation leads to better adaptation and an increased likelihood of surviving the disease | Less variation, which leaves offspring susceptible to disease |
| - | Carried out over generations to improve animal or crop yield gradually |
| Resources are scarce and competition is harsh, leaving only the best adapted to survive and breed | - |
| Example: Some bacteria mutate to become resistant to an antibody, passing on these resistant genes to their offspring and thus making the antibody less effective | Example: Humans artificially select crops that quickly reproduce asexually, leading to poor variation for the chosen crop |

**Evolution**: The change in adaptive features of a species over many generations due to natural selection.

**Adaptation**: The process of species becoming more suited to their environment over generations.
