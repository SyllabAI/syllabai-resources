## The Impact of Migration on Societies

### Immigration/Emigration

* Migration involves people moving to live in another place, either within a country or across international borders. The focus is primarily on global migration, a timeless phenomenon

* **Key definitions related to migration**

  * Migrants move from one country to another to settle

  * Emigration refers to leaving the country of origin

  * Immigration refers to entering and settling in a new country

  * Migrants are both emigrants from one country and immigrants to another

* **Differences between internal and international migration**

  * Internal migration occurs within a country and is easier,  as no international borders are crossed

  * International migration involves greater legal and physical barriers

* **Historical patterns of migration**

  * Early humans migrated out of Africa and spread globally

  * Before the WW2, migration mainly flowed from Europe to colonies, linked to colonialism and the displacement of indigenous populations

  * Since the Second World War, migration has mainly been from former colonies to Europe and North America

* **Reasons for migration and how it is controlled**

  * Post-war migration has been driven by poverty and persecution in former colonies

  * Migrants are attracted by higher living standards in developed countries

  * Immigration policies control entry and often favour certain countries

  * Restrictions on African migration to Europe have increased the danger of unofficial routes

  * Migrants from developed countries usually migrate legally and obtain visas more easily

* **Typical patterns of migrants and settlement**

  * Most migrants are unmarried adult men due to fewer restrictions and risks

  * Men often migrate first and later bring family members

  * In some countries, such as the Philippines, married women migrate alone to support their families

  * Migrants often settle near existing communities from their home country

**4 Types of Migrant**

1. Economic Migrant

   * people who migrate to gain a higher standard of living

   * Migration is regulated by international agreements and national policies

   * Migrants usually need visas or work permits to enter and work in another country

     * Obtaining visas can be expensive and time-consuming

     * Applications are carefully checked; many are rejected

   * Embassies act as gatekeepers, controlling who can enter and work

2. Highly Skilled Migrant

   * Highly skilled migrants are workers who have expertise or qualifications that are in demand in a country’s economy

     * Developed countries target professionals such as doctors, engineers, and IT experts

     * They attract them with incentives like permission to bring family or citizenship eligibility

3. Refugees and Asylum Seekers

   * Refugees are people who flee their homes due to war, disasters, or persecution to find safety elsewhere

     * Many remain within their own country, often moving to areas with friends or relatives

     * International refugees often go to neighbouring countries with similar cultures, while others travel farther

   * Legal status of refugees

     * A refugee is someone whose asylum claim has been accepted

     * Asylum seekers are those applying for protection but not yet legally recognised

     * The UN collaborates with governments to provide support and solutions for refugees

4. Students

   * Students are a type of temporary migrant who move abroad to study

     * Many universities in developed countries, e.g., the UK, accept international students and benefit from higher tuition fees

     * Universities have some responsibility for students, so they are treated differently from other migrants

     * Students are expected to return home after completing their studies, but some stay on as highly skilled workers

     * This can lead to “brain drain,” where countries lose talented individuals who study abroad and remain

### Push and Pull Factors

Push factors are reasons for leaving a country, pull factors are reasons for moving to another country.

* **Push Factors:**

  * ++Poverty++: to get a better quality of life

  * ++Lack of opportunity++: to gain better life chances in education or employment

  * ++Famine and environmental problems++: to escape the effects of climate change

  * ++Violence and persecution++: to escape conflicts or discrimination based on religion/race/ethnicity

* **Pull Factors:**

  * ++Better education:++ to have better life chances through a better quality of education

  * ++Employment:++ more and better-paying work opportunities

  * ++Living standards++: to have higher livin++g standards++

  * ++Western Consumer Culture:++ Many people wish for the lifestyle of western countires as they have been socialised to see it as superior

  * ++Hope of Better Treatment:++ Many people believe, sometimes falsely, that they can adapt and will be treated better

* These factors explain migration to an extent, but it also depends on what the immigrants’ perspective is, which may be shaped by friends or family staying abroad in those countries

### Increasing Cultural Diversity

International migration has increased ethnic and cultural diversity in many countries, especially in W. Europe and N. America

* Societies with multiple ethnic groups are often described as multicultural

* Migrant groups may gradually blend with the host culture while retaining aspects of their original culture

* Children and grandchildren of migrants often develop hybrid identities, combining both cultures

  * e.g. Somali communities in Holland maintain language, religion, and traditions while integrating into Dutch society

### Economic Consequences

* Migration flows from poorer to wealthier countries, propelled by opportunities for education, work, and a better quality of life

  * Many emigrants are young and healthy, leaving an older population behind in their home countries

  * Internal migration within countries can create older populations in rural areas and more young people in cities

* **Impact on origin countries**

  * Loss of skilled workers, known as “brain drain,”

    * e.g., trained doctors may migrate to countries offering higher pay

  * While emigration benefits individuals, it reduces workforce and human capital in home country

* **Economic effects of migration**

  * Emigrants often send money back home, called remittances

  * Remittances provide foreign currency and support local economies, becoming a significant source of income for many countries

* **Economic consequences for countries receiving migrants**

  * Many developed countries need more workers because their populations are ageing

    * Migrants can fill gaps in skilled sectors like healthcare and tech, and in less popular jobs, such as seasonal work

    * For example, foreign‑born workers have driven employment growth in Germany’s economy recently, helping prevent job losses.

  * Migrants may be willing to accept lower wages, which helps employers fill certain roles, but can be unpopular with labour groups

  * Migrants make a positive contribution to the economy by increasing the labour force and tax revenues

    * Evidence suggests immigration has supported economic growth in Europe after the pandemic.

  * However, large numbers arriving at once can put pressure on housing, schools, and healthcare, leading to strain on local resources and resentment from some of the local population

### Negative Perceptions of Migrants

* Immigrants are sometimes met with suspicion, resentment, or hostility in host countries

  * Negative stereotypes can be spread by the media or political leaders

  * Example: Political campaigns in the US and Europe have sometimes focused on restricting migration to gain support

* **Public responses are complex**

  * Individual reactions can be positive when a migrant’s personal story is known

  * Large groups arriving quickly can trigger defensive reactions, especially if migrants have different languages, religions, or cultures

  * Example: Many Europeans welcomed Ukrainian refugees in 2022, showing context-based responses

* **Political and legal controls on migration**

  * Governments manage migration to allow skilled workers in and restrict others

  * Illegal migrants are often those travelling without the required documents, such as visas

  * Migrants from Africa and elsewhere may travel long distances, often using smugglers, to reach Europe

  * Those entering without legal status often face detention or deportation, with limited rights
