## Differences in Life Chances due to Age

### **Age and Education**

* Gender, ethnicity and social class are more relevant than age when evaluating life chances in education

  * However, within societies, many older people did not go to school

* The life chances of those who missed out on school will be negatively affected by this.

* The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted the education of many children and young people around the world

### Age and Employment

* Education is seen as the most important priority for children globally.

* In some countries, children aged 14+ can do **part-time work**

* In poorer countries, children often work to support the family: helping on farms, selling produce, or doing **household chores** like caring for siblings.

* **Working children** do part-time work alongside school and education to contribute to their famiy’s income. This is generally acceptable.

* **Child labour** is children working long hours for very low pay, and often no school. This isconsidered an abuse of rights.

* They often **miss school**, which **harms their development** and **violates their rights**.

* The worst cases of child labour involve children being **forced to work under abusive or dangerous conditions**, sometimes **as part of modern slavery**.

  * Banning child labour may harm families who rely on that income for survival.

* Older people may face **ageism** at work; harder to get promotions, seen as less adaptable or productive.

* In countries with **welfare systems**, people usually retire around 60–70 and receive pensions to replace lost income.

### Age and Health

* Improvements in healthcare and a decrease in diseases around the world have led to better life chances for children

* Children are being vaccinated more and more

* Diseases such as malaria and other infectious diseases still pose a threat and may harm a child’s education

* Poorer families may suffer from malnutrition

* Older people suffer from many more health problems and require more care

* Some may argue that younger children need more focus in healthcare, but some may counter that all people should get an equal chance

### Age and Housing

* Children’s housing is mainly influenced by **family factors** like **social class** and **ethnicity**

* Many older adults live alone after the death of a partner, which can affect their **independence** and **access to support**.

* Some older people live in **age-specific housing** with nearby health and support services; those needing more help may benefit from **care homes**, which can improve their **life chances** and quality of life.

### Age and Life Expectancy

* **Life expectancy** refers to the average number of years a person can expect to live.

* Life expectancy was lower in the past mainly because **many infants and children died**

  * Around **30 years in 1800**, compared to about **72 years today**.

* **Infant mortality rate (IMR)** is the number of babies who die per **100,000 live births**.

  * In the past, IMR was high due to **infectious diseases**, poor hygiene, and limited medical care.

* The **global IMR has declined sharply** over the last two centuries.

  * This decline has been caused by:

    * **Vaccinations** against preventable diseases.

    * Improvements in **nutrition, hygiene, and living standards**.

    * Better **antenatal and postnatal care**

* A decline in infant mortality has affected **family size and life chances**.

  * Families are more likely to have **fewer children**, as survival rates are higher.

  * Children with fewer siblings often have **better life chances** because:

    * More resources, such as food, education, and healthcare, are available per child.

    * Parents can provide more time and attention.

* Rising life expectancy has also changed experiences of **older age**.

  * In the past, people often retired with only a **short period of life** remaining.

  * Today, especially in developed countries:

    * Many people live into their **80s or 90s**.

    * Older people often remain **healthy and active** for much of retirement.
