## Impact of Global Ecological Issues on Societies

* Globalisation has increased ecological problems that worsen inequalities between and within societies

  * Environmental damage and climate change are largely caused by global economic activity

  * Poorer people and poorer countries are affected most, despite contributing least to the problem

### The Influence of Transnational Corporations (TNCs)

* TNCs are businesses that operate in many countries, employing workers, sourcing materials, and selling goods globally

  * Globalisation has increased their number, size, and power

  * Some are global brands, while others operate behind the scenes in sectors like mining and shipping

    * e.g., Coca-Cola, KFC, Domino’s, Unilever or lesser-known such as Maersk (shipping)

* **TNCs are part of the global capitalist system and aim to maximise profit**

  * Many promote environmental policies to protect their public image

  * However, their activities still cause environmental damage

* **Role of TNCs in developing countries**

  * Developing countries attract TNCs to create jobs and economic growth

  * To do so, governments may weaken environmental controls or planning laws

  * Low wages and poor working conditions reduce costs for TNCs

* **Impact on global inequality**

  * Environmental harm and exploitation are concentrated in developing countries

  * Profits mainly benefit TNCs and developed economies, reinforcing inequality

### Environmentally Damaging Actions by TNCs

* **Farming:** TNCs use fertile land to grow cash crops such as cocoa, coffee or tea

  * This leaves less land for food production

  * Farmers are given less fertile land

  * TNCs may use pesticides or chemicals that pollute the land

* **Plastics:** TNCs manufacture plastic or use it for packaging

  * This depletes natural resources

  * This also affects marine life, affecting the livelihoods of farmers

* **Fossil Fuels:** The use of oil, gas, and coal contributes to global warming

* **Mining**: mining can cause chemical pollution, soil erosion, and acidification of water bodies

* **Deforestation:** Logging and the clearance of land destroy forest ecosystems

  * This affects wildlife, reducing biodiversity and contributing to climate change

* **Fishing:** Large-scale fishing by TNCs reduces fish populations

  * This contributes to a loss in biodiversity

  * The livelihoods of fishermen are endangered

    * This may lead some to adopt a lifestyle of piracy

* **Consumer Lifestyle:** TNCs encourage a wasteful consumer lifestyle

  * depleting resources

  * causing environmental damage

* **Waste:** Waste produced by TNCs is often exported to developing countries

  * E-waste recycling releases toxic chemicals, harming health and polluting land and water

### Climate Change

* Human activity has caused climate change, which is widely recognised as a global threat

  * Carbon emissions from industry, energy production, and transport mainly drive global warming

  * Climate change affects regions unevenly and unpredictably, increasing social inequality

* **Changing rainfall patterns:** rainfall has become less predictable

  * Farmers cannot reliably know when to plant crops, affecting food production and livelihoods

* **Desertification:** land becomes barren due to soil erosion and climate change

  * Reduces land available for farming, threatening local communities’ food security

* **Loss of biodiversity:** species go extinct or become endangered

  * Disrupts ecosystems that people rely on for resources like food, medicine, and livelihoods

* **Extreme weather events:** storms and other weather events are stronger and less predictable

  * Causes damage to homes, crops, infrastructure, and can put lives at risk

### Pollution

Pollution results from the actions of TNCs, governments, and consumers

* It is a serious threat to health, reducing quality of life and life expectancy

* Poorer communities are most affected, as they cannot relocate to cleaner areas

* Pollution can be long-lasting (e.g., plastic in oceans) or temporary but damaging (e.g., oil spills)

**++Land Pollution:++** Land pollution harms plants, crops, and livestock that feed on contaminated vegetation

* Waste exported from developed countries to poorer nations can cause pollution and health issues, even when some is recycled

**++Water Pollution:++** Water bodies, such as lakes or oceans, can be polluted by substances like oil, chemicals and plastics

* In some places, people have to drink or use polluted water because there is no alternative

**++Air Pollution:++** is caused by emissions from factories and vehicle exhaust

* High pollution levels in cities (e.g., Lahore, Delhi, Shanghai) damage people’s health, causing breathing problems and increasing disease risk

* Small particles in polluted air affect the lungs and can lead to premature death; the WHO estimated 4.2 million deaths in 2016

### Urbanisation

* **Urbanisation:** an increase in the proportion of a population living in cities

  * Cities are usually defined as having over 100,000 people

  * Urbanisation is fastest in less developed countries, especially in Africa

  * City growth comes from rural–urban migration and high birth rates due to youthful populations

* **Drivers and impacts of city growth**

  * Cities attract people through jobs, education, and export industries

  * They draw in food, water, and resources from surrounding areas

  * This creates large ecological footprints, significantly affecting environments and lifestyles

* **Cities as magnets and drains**

  * Seen as magnets for innovation, economic growth, and opportunity

  * Also seen as drains that concentrate and waste natural and human resources

* **Informal housing in developing-world cities**

  * Poor urban residents often self-build housing due to a lack of affordable options

  * These settlements (e.g. shanty towns, favelas) are often on illegal land with no security of tenure

  * Governments may see them as a problem and attempt to remove or hide them

  * For residents, they provide cheap housing, access to work, and supportive community networks

* Despite these advantages, shanty towns have many problems

  * In LEDCs, living conditions for the urban poor are often worse than for the rural poor

* Conditions in shanty towns include:

  * Overcrowding, with many people sharing very small spaces

  * Contaminated water, such as clean piped water, is usually limited to wealthier areas

    * Poor residents often buy low-quality water or use unsafe sources

  * Lack of sanitation, with little or no sewerage provision

  * Risk of floods or landslides, as housing is often on unstable land

  * Indoor air pollution from cooking and heating, worsened by poor ventilation

* These conditions increase the risk of infectious diseases and other dangers, such as fires
