## Sociological Views of the Success of Attempts to Reduce Social Inequalities

### New Right Criticisms of the Welfare State/Underclass

* **Welfare states have been strongly criticised by conservative and right-wing politicians and writers**

  * These sociologists with this view are called the **New Right**. They argue that:

  * Welfare encourages a dependency culture

  * Welfare payments have become too generous, encouraging laziness

  * Welfare produces the underclass, who rely on welfare

  * Welfare is bad for the economy as it supports too many people

  * The welfare system can be abused, with some people fraudulently claiming benefits

    * Although this is more of a claim against fraud in general, as part of the system

  * The state interferes more than it should; some sociologists call this the ‘nanny state’

* Right-wing thinkers (New Right) argue that there is an **underclass**, a marginalised group at the bottom of society

  * They are seen as separate from the working class because many are unemployed or economically inactive

* The underclass is said to have **different values** from mainstream society

  * dependency on welfare

  * lack of work ethic

  * rejection of dominant norms

* The idea is **not new**, even though the term is recent

  * Karl Marx identified groups outside the working class in the 19th century

* **Marx referred to groups such as**

  * unemployed or displaced workers, casual and irregular workers, and those unable to work

  * the ***lumpenproletariat*** (e.g. criminals, vagrants, prostitutes), seen as detached from class struggle

* **New Right views differ from Marxism**

  * Marx saw these groups as shaped by capitalism, while the New Right blames culture and values

* The underclass is seen to be different from the working class in the following ways:

  * low income, unemployment, poor housing and poor education

  * social exclusion, likely unaware of rights and separated from society

  * Fatalists - they feel alienated from people and are suspicious of authority

  * dependency on the welfare state

* New Right American Sociologist Charles Murray defines the underclass by behaviour. For him, the signs of an emerging underclass are:

  * increasing dependency on welfare

  * Increasing illegitimacy (children of unmarried mothers)

  * rising crime, rising drug use

  * increasing antisocial behaviour

  * growing reluctance to get a job

* Left-wing sociologists have also used the term underclass, but they see people in it as victims of poverty, unemployment and wider economic forces rather than as being responsible for their situation.

* Many left-wing writers have stopped using the term because it has become associated with New Right ideas and is now seen as a negative and blaming label.

* Marxists reject the idea of an underclass altogether, arguing that it is simply the most disadvantaged section of the working class, especially those pushed out of work during economic downturns.

* From this view, poverty and social exclusion are caused by capitalism and inequality, not by people’s values or behaviour.

* Marxists and other critics argue that governments should reduce inequality through the redistribution of wealth and

  stronger welfare support to improve life chances and reintegrate excluded groups into society

### The Culture of Poverty and the Dependency Culture

* Debates about the underclass are closely linked to ideas about a culture of poverty and dependency culture.

* The culture of poverty refers to values that may develop among people in long-term poverty, such as focusing on short- term survival rather than planning for the future, which can make it harder to escape poverty.

* New Right sociologists argue that the welfare state encourages these values, because regular benefit payments allow people to survive without working.

* They claim this creates a dependency culture where individuals rely on the state instead of taking responsibility

* The New Right argues that paid work provides self-discipline, pride and self-worth, while welfare encourages laziness

* As a result, they argue that the welfare state actually traps people in poverty, especially those in the underclass, by discouraging work, saving and long-term planning.

### Marxist Criticisms of the Welfare State

* Marxists criticise welfare because it **softens the worst effects of capitalism**

  * This reduces anger and dissent among the working class, leading to less pressure for radical or revolutionary change

* Welfare makes capitalism appear less unjust than it really is

  * It creates the impression that inequality is being dealt with, hiding the exploitative nature of the capitalist system

* Welfare can be seen as a form of bribery

  * Limited support keeps workers compliant, making revolutionary change less likely

* Some Marxists argue that removing welfare would expose injustice

  * Extreme inequality would become clearer, which could increase support for the revolution

* Many left-wing writers still support the welfare state

  * They argue that a complete overthrow of capitalism is not possible

  * Welfare is the best available way to redistribute wealth and improve life chances

### **Criticisms of NGOs**

There are several problems associated with NGOs

* Aid may encourage a dependency culture, making people reliant on aid and encouraging laziness, not only for the recipients of aid, but the country’s government as well

* Sometimes the aid may be useless and not what is needed

  * Staff from developed countries may not understand the problems of developing countries, and may not be able to provide aid that is actually needed

* Some NGOs have been involved in scandals, such as  staff abusing or exploiting local people

* Marxists argue that aid is a tool for developed countries to control and exploit developing countries

  * Keeps developing nations dependent on charity for basic services like health and education

* Feminists argue that NGOs sometimes ignore how aid affects gender relations

  * For example, loans to women for small businesses may be opposed by husbands, as they change household dynamics

### **Feminist Criticisms of Equality Legislation**

* Feminists campaigned for laws to achieve gender equality, aiming to remove barriers in pay, workplace discrimination, and access to male-dominated jobs. However, passing laws alone did not fully solve inequality.

* **Laws introduced for equality**

  * Equal pay for equal work: women should earn the same as men for the same job

  * Ending gender discrimination: illegal to discriminate at work due to sex, e.g., promotions or pregnancy

  * Enabling women to work in male-dominated roles, e.g., the armed forces

* **Feminist criticisms of these laws**

  * Women still earn less than men on average

  * Women have limited access to male-dominated fields

  * Some laws are hard to enforce; employers can mask discrimination with other excuses

  * Legal action is costly and time-consuming; women may simply change jobs instead

  * Laws focus on employment but neglect broader issues like pregnancy and childcare
