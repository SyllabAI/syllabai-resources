## The Use of Legislation within Societies to Reduce Inequality

* Levels of inequality vary between countries. In modern industrial societies, inequality fell during much of the 20th century due to welfare states and governments

* At the end of the century, this trend reversed: inequality widened as top earners pulled further ahead, even though living standards at the bottom still improved because of economic growth.

* Wilkinson and Pickett (The Spirit Level, 2011) argue that inequality matters more than overall wealth. More unequal societies have worse outcomes in physical and mental health, education, crime, imprisonment, and social mobility.

* These negative effects impact all social groups, not only the poor, meaning greater equality benefits society as a whole.

* Governments often accept some inequality in meritocratic societies, as rewards are linked to talent and effort, but extreme inequality is seen as problematic

* Governments are expected to protect minimum living standards for the poorest through laws and welfare policies.

### **Progressive Taxation**

* This means taxing people with higher incomes more according to their wealth and income

  * For example, tax might bt 10% upto a certain amount and 15% for income more than that amount

* Not all taxes can be progressive, as taxes on goods (GST) are the same for everybody, affecting poor people more

* Wealthy people also use methods to avoid these taxes by hiring accountants and opening offshore accounts

### Redistribution of Wealth Through Benefits

* The government's income from taxes is used to fund govt departments, including the welfare state

* With progressive taxation, the government can redistribute wealth, providing welfare benefits to those in need, including:

  * People in poverty

  * Unemployed people

  * Those with a disability or sickness

  * Those who cannot work due to family responsibilities

### Minimum Wage

* This means companies can’t pay their employees less than a certain amount per hour

* This ensures everybody is paid enough to at least support themselves

* Supporters of a minimum wage say that it prevents poverty and reduces inequality.

* Opponents say it increases unemployment because companies would emрlоу more workers if they could pay them less

* Some companies use workarounds, such as classifying workers as independent contractors and not giving work benefits

### Equal Opportunities Laws

* These laws prevent discrimination against certain groups and make sure everyone has equal opportunities

* They discriminate on grounds of sex, age, religion, race, etc. illegal

* They ensure equality in applying for jobs, training, promotion, workplace treatment and job terminationn

* These laws give fair rights to workers and a responsibility for companies to uphold and enforce them

### Other Government Measures

* Governments reduce inequality by subsidising or providing free goods and services for poorer groups

* Price controls on basic goods such as bread or rice ensure food remains affordable and limit excessive profits.

* Welfare benefits, such as free bus travel and winter fuel payments for pensioners, help protect living standards.

* Support for children from low-income families, such as free school meals, improves equality of opportunity.
