## Different **Sociological Theories of Social Inequality**

### Labelling Theory

* The labelling theory is part of interactionism

  * It looks at society through a micro lens, not focusing on the wider impacts of gender, age, and ethnicity

  * It helps us understand how inequalities arise and are reproduced in micro events

* **Stereotyping**

  * Views held by individuals or portrayed through general media that present a set of assumptions about a social group

  * These can lead to discrimination and prejudice against certain groups, called **negative stereotyping**

* **Master Status**

  * When a person is labelled, and that label affects others’ perception of them

  * They may also start to think of themselves like that and internalise the behaviour

  * This is why some people face difficulty in rejecting that label or changing their behaviour

* **Self-fulfilling prophecy**

  * People who have been labelled may begin to act in certain ways, making the expectations of the label come true.

* Interactionist Howard Becker argued that people from powerless groups would be disadvantaged because people with power might label them based on stereotypes and treat them with bias

### **Marxism**

* Marxists believe extreme inequalities in poverty and wealth are consequences of capitalism

* They focus on social-class inequalities and believe all other inequalities to be a consequence of class

  * For example, a female may be disadvantaged ++because++ she is in the lower class

* They believe capitalism keeps the bourgeoisie richer and keeps the proletariat poor and dependent

  Some key theories of Marxism are:

* **Exploitation of the proletariat:** Marxists believe capitalism thrives in the bourgeoisie, exploiting the proletariat

  * They don’t pay the full value of the work the proletariat does, and hence make a profit

  * The proletariat is poor and has to make a living, so they have no choice but to accept

* **Ideological Control:** This states that the bourgeoisie manipulates the proletariat’s thoughts to make them believe that Capitalism is a fair system

  * They do this through the ISA (see Louis Althusser in Chapter 2), and spread propaganda, and make them believe that the bourgeoisie is on top because they earned it or deserved it

* **Poverty trap:** This is how Marxists explain that the bourgeoisie uses its power to keep the proletariat poor

* **Reserve army of labour**

  * Reserve army of labour means unemployed workers who can be used by employers to control the workforce.

  * Employers keep wages low because unemployed workers are ready to replace those who demand higher pay or better conditions.

  * Workers who form trade unions or challenge employers can be easily replaced.

  * It mainly includes low-skilled working-class people who are most likely to be unemployed.

  * These workers are hired during economic booms and laid off again when no longer needed.

  * Other groups, such as women, immigrants, young and older workers, can also be used when it suits the economy.

### **Feminism**

Feminists focus on the inequalities women face compared with men. e.g. more women in poverty than men. Feminists have the following theories

* **Division of labour:** refers to the traditional split of roles within the family.

  * Traditional division of labour placed women in unpaid domestic and caring roles and men in paid work.

  * This restricted women’s opportunities outside the home and reduced their financial independence.

* **Dual burden:** refers to having responsibility for both paid employment and most domestic and caring work in the home.

  * Arlie Hochschild described this as the “second shift”, where women work a job outside the home and then continue working through cooking, cleaning and caring after returning home.

  * This work is unpaid and often unrecognised, despite being essential for family life and society.

  * It restricts women’s opportunities for promotion, training and higher-paid jobs.

  * The dual burden increases stress, fatigue and work–life imbalance, affecting physical and mental health.

* **Triple shift**: refers to women carrying three roles at the same time: paid work, domestic labour, and emotional care.

  * Alongside jobs and housework, women are expected to manage the emotional well-being of the family, such as supporting children and partners.

  * This emotional labour is unpaid, taken for granted, and adds further pressure beyond the dual burden.

* **Horizontal and Vertical Segregation,** as explained previously

  * Men and women tend to be in different types of work (horizontal segregation)

  * Men tend to be at a higher level in the hierarchy in the same work organisation (vertical segregation).

### Functionalism

* Functionalists see inequality as a functional thing that is beneficial for the whole society.

* To best encourage the usage of people’s skills and talents, society has to reward some people more than others.

* Poverty reminds the rest of society of the **importance of values such as hard work** and honesty, and what happens if we don’t follow these values

  * The existence of poverty means that unpleasant or poorly paid jobs will be done

* It also increases social solidarity among people who are not poor

Functionalists use the following key terms:

* **Social Mobility** between social classes is important for society, according to functionalists

  * For example, the education system channels people into the careers that best fit them. People can be upwardly mobile through success in education.

* **Meritocracy**: Upward social mobility is when people move up the social class hierarchy, often through education, work, or talent.

  * In a meritocratic system, success depends on ability, effort, and talent, allowing those who work hard to improve their social position.

* **Legislative change:** If society doesn’t function as it should, it could become dysfucnctional

  * Functionalists would want society to adjust by bringing in new laws to ensure equal opportunities and meritocracy.
