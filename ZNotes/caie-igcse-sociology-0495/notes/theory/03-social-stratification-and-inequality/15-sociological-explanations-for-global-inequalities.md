## Sociological Explanations for Global Inequalities

### Marxist Views of Capitalist Exploitation

* **Marxist** view is based on the idea that inequalities between richer and poorer countries are caused by capitalism.

  * Capitalism is based on profit and private ownership, which creates inequality between social classes.

  * These class inequalities exist within countries and also on a global scale

  * Marxists argue that global inequality mirrors class inequality

    * Developed countries are the ruling class

    * Poorer countries are the working class

* **Exploitation as the key mechanism**

  * Richer countries use their economic and political power to exploit poorer countries

    * Poorer countries provide cheap labour, raw materials, and markets for richer countries

      * This exploitation benefits developed countries while limiting development in poorer ones

  * Marxists argue that this process began with colonialism

  * It continues today through global trade, investment, and multinational corporations

* **Dependency theory (neo-Marxist view):** capitalism creates long-term dependency between countries

  * Neo-Marxists argue that poorer countries are kept dependent on richer ones for trade, investment, and technology

    * Wealthier countries exploit those slightly less wealthy

    * These countries then exploit those below them

  * This creates a chain of dependency, similar to a hierarchy

  * As a result, the poorest countries struggle to develop independently and remain underdeveloped

### Colonialism

* Colonialism was the domination of LEDCs by more developed countries for economic, political, and cultural control

  * The exploitation of people and resources benefits the colonising country

  * Creates global inequalities that continue after independence

* **Historical context:**

  * Began \~500 years ago with European exploration and trade

  * Europeans crossed oceans, e.g., to the Americas, and established trading posts that became settlements

  * Trade was unequal: colonies supplied cash crops, raw materials, and labour (including slavery in Africa)

  * Indigenous populations suffered from violence, disease, and cultural disruption (e.g., Aztecs in Mexico)

  * European powers competed for territories, imposing borders without regard for ethnic or cultural divisions

* **Mechanisms of control:**

  * Local industries redirected or destroyed to supply colonisers’ economies (e.g., India’s cotton industry under Britain)

  * Indigenous populations forced into labour, taxes, and capitalist systems

  * Education and governance are restricted, limiting local skill development

  * Cultural influence imposed with changes to language, law, and administration

* **Socioeconomic impacts on colonised countries:**

  * **Economic dependency:** colonies forced to grow cash crops or provide raw materials, limiting domestic economic development

    * Many LEDCs remained reliant on exports to the ex-colonial power

  * **Poverty:** local populations had little access to land, wealth, or economic opportunities

    * Wealth and resources were extracted to benefit the colonisers

  * **Labour exploitation:** forced labour, low wages, and high taxes tied people to the colonial economy

    * Peasants and workers had little economic freedom

  * **Limited education and skills:** Colonial powers restricted schooling to a small elite

    * Post-independence governments often lacked trained personnel

  * **Social stratification:** colonial systems created hierarchies that favoured certain groups

    * This reinforced inequalities within colonies, often along ethnic or class lines

  * **Infrastructure for colonisers:** roads, ports, and cities built primarily to extract resources, not to benefit local populations

* **Post-colonial challenges:**

  * Economic dependence continues: reliance on trade, aid, and investment from former colonial powers

  * Governance struggles: lack of democratic tradition and administrative experience

  * Ethnic conflict and civil unrest persist due to imposed borders and social divisions (e.g., Pakistan and Bangladesh)

  * Cultural influence remains (e.g., English in Sierra Leone and the Gambia)

  * Neo-colonialism: former powers maintain influence through economics, politics, and culture

### Feminist Ideas of Patriarchy

* **Gender is a central factor in global inequality, alongside capitalism and colonialism**

  * The patriarchy systematically disadvantages women in many areas of life

  * International organisations, NGOs, and social movements focus on gender issues, but women are still far from achieving equality

* **Progress towards gender equality remains uneven, especially in work and education**

  * Women often lack financial independence due to limited access to well-paid jobs

  * While more girls attend school worldwide, some countries still show a gender gap in education

  * Measures to assess equality include: freedom of marriage and divorce, freedom of movement, access to education, household power, and reproductive choice

* **Capitalism reinforces the exploitation of women globally**

  * Young women are disproportionately employed in sweatshops in Southeast Asia and elsewhere to produce cheap clothing for developed countries

  * They are paid very little, often have poor working conditions, minimal training, and almost no job security

  * Wages may be taken by a male relative, limiting financial independence

  * Male managers dominate workplaces, reflecting patriarchal structures

* **Patriarchal structures limit the benefits of employment**

  * Even when women earn money, societal norms and male control often block true autonomy

  * The combination of capitalism and patriarchy means progress towards equality is slowed or partially blocked
