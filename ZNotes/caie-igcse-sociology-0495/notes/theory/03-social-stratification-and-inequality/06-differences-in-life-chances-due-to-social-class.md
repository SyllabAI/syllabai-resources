## Differences in Life Chances due to Social Class

* Social class is a little harder to define and classify

  * Marxists see society in only two classes: The wealthy **bourgeoisie** ruling class and the working **proletariat**

  * Most sociologists divide society into three classes: upper, middle and lower based on the following factors

    * Wealth

    * Occupation

    * Housing

    * Income

    * Education and qualifications

    * Lifestyle

    * Status

### Social Class and Education

* Different classes likely attend different types of schools

  * Schools in lower-class areas will have lower-class children

  * Govt. run schools educate mainly lower and middle-class children

  * Private schools charge parents and promise better quality education, so they educate mainly upper and some middle-class kids

* Lower-class children have lower educational achievement on average than middle or upper-class children

### Social Class and Employment

* Social class is often identified using occupation because it is strongly linked to income, social status, and living standards.

* Occupations can be ranked in a hierarch**y**, with higher-paid and higher-status jobs at the top and lower-paid jobs below.

* **Non-manual occupations** involve mainly mental or office-based work rather than physical labour.

  * This includes **the professions** (e.g. doctors, lawyers), which require high levels of education, have high status, and are well paid.

  * It also includes lower-status office jobs; these roles are often called **white-collar jobs** and are usually seen as middle- class.

* **Manual occupations** involve physical labour and practical skills, such as mechanics, miners, builders, and other trades.

  * These are often called **blue-collar jobs** and are typically associated with the working class.

* **Both manual and non-manual jobs can be further divided** based on skill level and the qualifications or training required, creating more detailed class distinctions.

* The upper class sits at the very top of the social class hierarchy, comprising extremely wealthy individuals.

  * Many do not need to work because their wealth is sufficient to support their lifestyle.

  * They often have cosmopolitan lifestyles,

* One part of the upper class is the **landowning aristocracy**, whose wealth and status are inherited, e.g., royal families.

* Another group is the **new rich** or **super-rich**, whose wealth comes from their own achievements rather than inheritance.

  * The super-rich include **celebrities** and **elite professionals**, such as major figures in sport, media, and entertainment.

  * Successful **entrepreneurs** who have founded and run global businesses

  * People at the very top of powerful professions, such as **senior politicians, judges, lawyers,**

  * This group is different from the aristocracy because their wealth and status are **achieved** *rather than inherited*

* The middle class includes people in a very **wide range of occupations**, from routine office jobs to highly paid professional roles

  * One group within the middle class is small business owners, including shopkeepers, landlords, and small farmers, who may be self-employed and have some economic independence.

  * Another group is professionals and managers (upper middle class), whose high income and social status come from education, qualifications, and training.

    * This upper middle class expanded in the 20th century with the growth of the welfare state and public sector jobs

  * The lower middle class works in a wide range of jobs, including routine clerical, administrative, and retail work, with lower income and status than the upper middle class, but still above manual workers.

* **These differences give the middle class better life chances in the following ways:**

  * They are usually safer and in hygienic environments like offices, unlike factory or mining jobs with higher health risks.

  * Incomes are higher, leading to a better standard of living and the ability to save

  * More chances for promotion, giving long-term career progression.

  * Working conditions and benefits are better, including paid holidays and sick leave.

### Social Class and Health

* **Health inequalities exist between social classes**

  * Living conditions matter, such as poorer housing, polluted areas and insecure or low-paid lower class

  * Working-class groups experience worse health outcomes, including higher risks of premature death, complications at birth and some cancers.

  * Low income limits healthy choices, such as affording nutritious food

  * Stressful and dangerous jobs, along with poverty, damage both physical and mental health

  * Access to healthcare is unequal: working-class areas often have fewer nearby health services

    * The cost of treatment or medicine can be a barrier where healthcare is not free.

### Social Class and Housing

* Middle-class people will live in better housing and are

  * properly heated

  * ventilated and airy

  * less pollution

  * better air quality

  * less crime

  * better aesthetic

  * more facilities

### Social Class and Life Expectancy

* The middle class has a higher life expectancy than the working class for many reasons

  * Working-class jobs are more dangerous

  * Working-class people may live in an unhealthy environment

  * Working-class people may not have enough money to afford paid healthcare

  * Working-class people may lack a healthy lifestyle with nutritious food and adequate exercise
