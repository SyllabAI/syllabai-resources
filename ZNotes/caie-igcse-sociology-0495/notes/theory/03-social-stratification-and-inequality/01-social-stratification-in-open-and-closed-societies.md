# Social Stratification and Inequality

## Social Stratification in Open and Closed Societies

* When different roles and tasks develop in a society, it becomes socially differentiated

* But when some roles and statuses are seen as superior to each other, the society becomes stratified into layers of social status, such as power and prestige. This is **social stratification**.

  * In open societies, you can change your social status or role through hard work and education. Modern industrial societies are an example of this. Stratification is constantly changing

  * Closed societies, however, do not provide this leverage. Your role is fixed and unchangeable, and usually you are born with it. Some traditional societies are like this.

* People are usually stratified by age, gender, ethnicity, but other forms are present

### Achieved and Ascribed Status

* Ascribed statuses are given by society, and we have almost no control over them

  * Age, sex, ethnicity, religion, and social class are ascribed at birth

  * However, one can change religion, and a person’s age constantly evolves

* Achieved statuses, however, are those we can change through choice or hard work

  * In open societies, we can change our social class and religion

* Occupational status is an achieved status

### Poverty

Poverty can be measured in many ways, such as **absolute** and **relative** poverty

* Absolute poverty is characterised by an individual's inability to access the necessities of life, including clean water, food, shelter, education, sanitation and access to information. The World Bank puts this as living on an income of $3.00 a day.

* Relative poverty is when someone is considered poor, taking into account the social context of others in their society. They may have the necessities of life, but they lack items that are essential to their society. They are poor in comparison to others only in their society

  * People likely to be in relative poverty are the disabled, orphaned, unemployed, immigrants/refugees or low-wage workers.

* Poor people usually face many risks, such as a lack of health facilities and are most affected by events such as wars and

  climate change

### Cycle of Poverty

* Poverty mainly comes from being born into a poor family, lack of opportunities, low wage labour

* The cycle of poverty is when a family has been in poverty for over three generations, and the factors that keep them poor stay the same

* It is nearly impossible for many to escape poverty as they lack opportunities, resources, and cultural or social capital. This is known as the poverty trap

* Poor people struggle, and most of their income goes toward trying to survive

  * They struggle to buy expensive goods or in bulk, and can’t spend much on fuel to travel for items

  * They buy secondhand items, which need to be replaced often

* Poverty can be explained by **poverty culture,** which is a set of values or habits keeping people poor, such as

  * low education

  * Bad future planning

  * desire for instant rewards

  * **fatalism**: the mindset that you cannot do anything about your situation

* The concept of a poverty of culture is controversial howeveras it suggests it’s their fault they are poor, when in reality such factors as education are not available to them.

### Wealth

* Wealth refers to ownership of assets that can be bought and sold to generate income.

* It is not limited to cash and includes a wide range of valuable resources

  * land

  * stocks and shares

  * valuable items such as art and jewellery

  * property and infrastructure

* Wealth is a major indicator of social class. People with high levels of wealth usually have a higher status, greater life chances, and more power in society.

* Some forms of wealth generate additional income or an increase in value over time.

  * Art and valuables may appreciate in value

* The very wealthy often do not need to work because their assets generate continuous income.

* Wealth is often inherited rather than earned through work.

### Power and Social Exclusion

* Inequality is not only about wealth but also about **power**

* Power is the ability to achieve goals even when others resist

* **Sources of power include:**

  * Occupation (police, judges, managers)

  * Government roles (law-making and policy control)

  * Social status, such as gender, age, class, and ethnicity

  * **Power differences can exist between:**

    * Men and women

    * Adults and children or the elderly

    * The middle class and the working class

    * Dominant and minority ethnic groups

  * **Power can be used:**

    * Directly through force or legal threats (job loss, prison, violence)

    * Indirectly, through manipulation or withholding information

  * Power allows some individuals or groups to **make others do what is expected**

  * **Direct power** involves the use or threat of force or punishment

    * Examples include losing a job, legal penalties, imprisonment, or violence

  * **Indirect power** is less visible and more subtle

    * Those with power may manipulate situations or control decisions

    * Information can be hidden or selectively shared to influence behaviour

  * Indirect power is often harder to challenge because it does not involve obvious threats

  * People with little power may experience **social exclusion**

    * Exclusion from rights, opportunities, and resources

    * Prevented from fully participating in society

    * Often ignored politically due to low influence

### Social Mobility

* Social mobility refers to **movement between social classes**

* It shows how open or closed a society is

* **Types of social mobility**

  * **Upward mobility**: moving to a higher class (e.g. working class → middle class)

  * **Downward mobility**: moving to a lower class (e.g. middle class → working class)

  * **Intergenerational mobility**: children end up in a different class from their parents

  * **Intragenerational mobility**: an individual changes class during their own lifetime

* **Mobility and types of society**

  * Social mobility occurs in **open societies but** is limited or impossible in **closed societies**

  * In a **meritocracy**, status is achieved through talent and effort, so high mobility is expected

* **Factors that increase social mobility**

  * Education and qualifications

  * Promotion and career progression

  * Marriage into a different social class

  * Changes in wealth (e.g. inheritance or winning money)

* **Patterns of mobility**

  * Upward mobility is more common than downward mobility

  * This is due to:

    * Decline in unskilled manual jobs

    * Growth of middle-class occupations

    * Increased access to education

  * Most mobility is **short-range**

    * Small movements between nearby classes, and there is often little real change in lifestyle or life chances

* **Marxist view**

  * Social mobility acts as a **safety valve, as it** reduces class conflict by making the system appear fair

  * People believe success is possible, even if inequality remains

* **Functionalist view**

  * Middle-class jobs are mainly filled by people from middle-class backgrounds because ability and intelligence are seen as inherited

  * This view is controversial and criticised by other sociologistsand critics  argue that talent exists across all classes but is not equally rewarded

### Meritocracy

* **Meritocracy** is the idea that individuals achieve their social position based on **talent, ability, and effort**

* Many modern, **open societies** claim to be meritocratic or aim to become so

* **Key features of a meritocracy**

  * Equal opportunities in **education** and **employment**

  * Success depends on how hard someone works and how capable they are

  * Social background should not determine outcomes

* **Meritocracy and inequality**

  * Meritocracies are **not equal societies**

  * Inequality of outcomes (e.g. income, status) is accepted

  * Supporters argue that this inequality is **fair** because everyone has the same chance to compete

  * Rewards such as high-paying jobs and wealth are seen as deserved

* **Criticisms of meritocracy**

  * They are **unfair**

    * It rewards people who already have advantages like natural ability or talent

  * Meritocracy often exists **in theory, not in reality**

    * Wealthy families pass on advantages to their children, such as private schooling and extra support

### Modern Slavery

* Early, closed societies were divided into **citizens and slaves**

* Slaves were treated as **property**, forced to work, and had no rights

* Legal ownership of slaves was abolished in most countries in the **19th century**

* **Modern slavery** still exists today, but in **hidden and informal forms**

  * Refers to situations where people are **controlled, exploited, and denied freedom**

* **Forms of modern slavery**

  * **Debt bondage**

    * People borrow money due to poverty

    * Forced to work to repay debt

    * Low wages + high interest make escape nearly impossible

  * **Human trafficking**

    * People are forced or tricked into work they did not choose

    * Often kept under strict control and unable to leave

  * **Forced marriage**

    * Individuals (often women or girls) are made to marry without consent

    * Expected to perform heavy domestic labour

    * Treated as servants rather than family members

* Modern slavery is often discovered only after **serious harm or death**

### The Caste System

* A **closed system of stratification**, historically found in India

  * **Status is ascribed and inherited at birth**, meaning individuals cannot change caste

* Caste controls key aspects of life:

  * **Occupation** – certain jobs are reserved for specific castes

  * **Marriage** – people must marry within their castes

* The system is justified through beliefs about **ritual purity and pollution**

  * Higher castes are seen as pure

  * Contact with lower castes is believed to cause pollution

* An example of a caste system is in Hinduism, with different castes such as:

  * Brahmins - priests, scholars

  * Kshatriya - warriors, rulers

  * Vaishya - farmers, merchants, traders

  * Shudra - labourers

  * Dalits - untouchables, 

* Although caste discrimination was **officially abolished in 1950**, caste still affects education, employment, marriage, and social status

* Social class has replaced them in many modern societies and is **more open**, allowing some movement

### Intersectionality

* Intersectionality is the idea that social class, gender, ethnicity, age (and other factors) overlap, shaping a person’s life chances and experiences of inequality.

* People with **multiple disadvantages** face greater barriers.

* Explains why people in the **same class** can have different outcomes.
