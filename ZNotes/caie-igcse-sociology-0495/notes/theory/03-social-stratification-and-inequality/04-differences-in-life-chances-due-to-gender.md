## Differences in Life Chances due to Gender

### Gender and Education

* In the past, only men were educated, as women were expected to be housewives and were supported by their husbands

* In many developing countries today, there is still a gap in girls’ education, as 2/3 of all illiterate people are female

  * As a result, in such societies, women can't receive qualifications and have worse life chances
  * In South Sudan, women face immense difficulty in gaining education, with the lowest female literacy rate in the world

* However, in countries where there is no such disparity, girls are on an equal level of academic achievement

### **Gender and Employment**

* Women have always worked, but their main expected role was unpaid domestic labour (housework and childcare), while men were seen as the main breadwinners responsible for family income.

* When women did paid work, it was often in low-paid, low-status jobs like cleaning and caring, which were undervalued

* In modern societies, women participate more in the workforce, and paid work has become a part of female identity.

* The role of housewife and mother still exists, but it is now often combined with paid employment rather than replacing it.

* As women’s role in paid work has increased, men are more likely to share housework and childcare

* Women face discrimination in employment and are more likely to be employed part-time, as well as taking long maternity leaves

  * Subsequently, they are paid less and may not receive adequate pension

* **Women's life chances are affected by paid employment in the following ways:**

  * Women earn less than men and are less likely to be financially independent.

  * Women are more vulnerable to poverty.

  * Women's careers are interrupted by maternal and family responsibilities

* There are **two main types of segregation** in workforces.

  * **Horizontal segregation** describes how men and women usually work in different fields. Women may work in lower-paid fields such as education, nursing, food preparation, while men occupy roles in IT, Law, Finance, and STEM

  * **Vertical segregation** means that even though men and women may work in the same workplace, men are usually placed higher in the workplace hierarchy than women and are subsequently paid more

* **More and more women were being employed by the end of the 20th century for several reasons:**

  * They wanted to break free of traditional gender roles and become independent

  * Economic factors lead to skilled jobs occupied by women being more available

  * Better access to contraceptive methods helped women in family planning

  * There were more and more idols for girls to look up to

  * The workplace attitude began to shift more to value women

  * Girls were being more socialised to see themselves in fields other than being a housewife

  * Changes in legislation prevented discrimination based on gender

* Even though women are largely employed, there is a **glass ceiling,** preventing women from fully achieving according to their qualification

  * Women are not taken seriously for promotions

  * Many males in higher positions have a sexist attitude

  * Many men at the highest level of a company may not want a different sex working with them

  * Some men are sceptical about the family responsibilities of women

### **Gender and Health**

* Men and women may suffer from different health problems at different rates

  * e.g., Alzheimer’s is more common in women

* Women around the world face a great risk to their health during pregnancy and childbirth

  * It is worse for younger, uneducated or poorer women

  * In LEDCs, a lack of access to proper health facilities may prove fatal

  * Annually, around **260,000 to 290,000 women die from pregnancy and childbirth-related causes**

* Another threat to mainly women is domestic violence and abuse in the home

* Men are at greater risk due to some male dominated job involving labour or dangerous environments, such as the military or coal mines

### Gender and Housing

* Gender doesn’t really affect housing like social class or ethnicity does

* Men and women usually live in families

### Gender and Life Expectancу

* Women live longer on average than men in most societies, around 5 years longer.

* **Possible reasons are:**

  * Men are usually in higher-risk situations, so they suffer from more accidental deaths

  * Men, in many societies, consume more alcohol, tobacco, and other drugs than women.

  * Men are more prone to risks in their work, such as in the military, mining, factories etc..

  * There are biological reasons for this difference in life expectancy as well
