# Education

## Formal and Informal Education

### Differences between Formal and Informal Education

**++Formal Education++**

* **Formal education** takes place in schools and colleges, is taught by trained teachers, and follows an agreed curriculum.

* **The development of formal education** was gradual and became widespread mainly over the past 150 years.

* **Resistance to formal education** existed in many societies: some political leaders feared educating the poor would challenge social order, while some traditional communities opposed schooling because it did not teach practical skills needed for work and family support.

* **T**hese views still exist in some parts of the world, particularly regarding the education of girls.

**++Informal Education++** refers to:

* Education outside the classroom - e.g. after-school activities

* **++Hidden Curriculum++**: Things children learn in school but are not part of the formal education curriculum e.g, they might learn the importance of punctuality or obedience to seniors

### The Official Curriculum: Testing, Syllabus Content and Subjects

* **Official curriculum** refers to the subjects taught in schools and their content

  * Becomes increasingly specialised as students progress through the education system

* **Primary education** focuses mainly on basic skills such as reading, writing and mathematics, with limited exposure to other subjects

* **Secondary education** involves a broader range of subjects

  * Subjects are usually taught by specialist teachers

* **Post-compulsory and higher education** involve narrowing subject choice

  * Students study fewer subjects, often choosing between arts and sciences, and usually focus on one main subject at university

* **++National Curriculum++** sets out the subjects and content that all learners of a particular age or standard must study

  * Ensures uniform teaching across schools

  * Often includes national tests to assess progress

    * Test results measure school performance

* **Purpose of the National Curriculum**

  * Allows governments to decide the skills and subjects needed for future citizens

  * Prioritises core skills such as reading, writing and mathematics

    * Once these are established, learners can study a wider range of subjects, such as:

      * Sciences, History, Geography, Literature, Religious Studies, technology, Art, Music, Drama, physical education, Foreign Languages

* **Advanced Education**

  * Additional subjects become available, such as sociology, economics, and more languages

  * Sociologists argue that all learners should study society and other cultures from a young age

* **Subject Content and Teaching**

  * Each subject has a defined range of content to be taught and learned

  * Teachers have some freedom to adapt lessons to class needs

  * In exam-assessed subjects, teachers must cover all syllabus content

* **Organisation of Knowledge**

  * Formal teaching assumes knowledge can be divided into labelled subjects (e.g., history, maths, science)

  * These divisions are **social constructions**, not natural

    * May prevent learners from seeing connections between subjects

    * Important information may fall between subjects and be neglected

### The Hidden Curriculum: Norms and Values, Punctuality, Conformity, Gender Roles

* Learning occurs through the school experience, not just formal subjects

  * Students acquire norms, values, beliefs, and attitudes through everyday school life

* School structure and organisation teach lessons implicitly

  * Schools are separate from the home, and classrooms are often arranged with the teacher at the front and students facing them

  * Conveys that the teacher is in control and the centre of attention

* Teacher-student interactions and assumptions also shape learning

  * e.g., punctuality teaches time management

**++Hierarchies++**

* Schools have a clear hierarchy with authority concentrated at the top

  * Adults (head teacher, senior teachers) control rules, organisation, and discipline

  * Learners occupy the bottom level and follow norms associated with their role

* Hierarchies teach students about authority and social roles

  * Reinforces obedience, respect for rules, and understanding of responsibilities

**++Other Features of the Hidden Curriculum:++**

* **Competitiveness**: Competitions and tests teach us to outperform rather than cooperate with others

* **Punctuality**: Students have to be on time for lessons, which helps, as many jobs and meetings have fixed timings

* Learners follow authority and conform to school rules through

  * formal (punishments, detentions) and informal (peer disapproval, teacher expectations) control

    * which teaches discipline, responsibility, and prepares them for adult life.

* **Community Spirit:** Teaches shared values through assemblies and uniforms

* Symbols such as flags or displays may communicate nationalist values or enforce certain behaviour
