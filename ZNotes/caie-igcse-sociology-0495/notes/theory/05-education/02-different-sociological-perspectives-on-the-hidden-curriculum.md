## Different Sociological Perspectives on the Hidden Curriculum

### Functionalism

According to functionalists, the hidden curriculum teaches norms, values, and skills that prepare learners for adult life

* Learners learn their role in society, how to cooperate with others, and what responsibilities they will have as adults

### Marxism

The hidden curriculum enforces social control, teaching working-class children to be passive, conformist, and accept low expectations

* Maintains ruling-class power by persuading the working class to accept ideas against their interests

* Working-class learners internalise failure as their own fault, while upper-class learners are taught to expect high-status roles

* The division of subjects limits understanding of how society works, helping to reproduce class inequality

### Feminism

The hidden curriculum reinforces gender roles, teaching boys and girls to conform to traditional stereotypes

* Boys and girls may wear different uniforms, study different subjects, or be treated differently by teachers

* This can lead to mistaken beliefs about what each sex is capable of and limit aspirations
