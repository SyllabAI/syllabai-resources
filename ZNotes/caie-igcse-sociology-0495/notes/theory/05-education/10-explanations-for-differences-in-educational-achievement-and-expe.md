## Explanations for Differences in Educational Achievement and Experience Based on Social Class

### Material Factors (e.g. Access to Digital Technology)

**Material Deprivation**

* Lack of money and resources prevents children from fully participating in education

* Causes children to achieve lower grades and have reduced chances of academic success

**Effects of Material Deprivation**

* An inadequate diet causes tiredness and poor concentration

* Cannot afford uniforms, stationery, sports kit, or school trips, leading to exclusion from activities

* Limited access to technology, such as laptops or the internet, makes homework and attending online lessons difficult

* Few educational resources at home, such as books and materials, limit self-study and slow progress

* Older children may work part-time to help the family income, leaving less time and energy for study

### Cultural and Social Factors

* Social factors like the cultural background of working-class learners may explain underachievement

* Called cultural, not material, deprivation because it comes from values and attitudes, not lack of money

* Some sociologists claim that certain cultural aspects of the working class may disadvantage educational achievement

**++The Working Class Attitude++**

* **Fatalistic Attitude**: working-class children may feel unable to control their own destiny, leading to thinking there is no point in studying

* **Immediate Gratification**: prefer short-term rewards over long-term benefits; may want to start earning money early rather than studying for future higher pay or status

* **Search for Thrills and Excitement**: lack of access to hobbies or holidays may lead boys in particular to deviant or criminal activities for excitement

* Parents may not value education, maybe because they had bad experiences at school themselves

**++Criticisms of this Theory:++**

* Evidence for a separate working-class culture is questioned; the term assumes that working-class culture is inferior

* Even if accurate, this culture may be the best way to cope with disadvantages when people have little control over their lives

* Most working-class parents value education and are ambitious for their children

* Some parents lack the knowledge and confidence of middle-class parents to demand extra support from schools

* Some parents are reluctant to contact schools due to their own negative experience

  * Schools may misinterpret this as a lack of interest in children’s education

### Cultural Capital

* Working-class parents may lack the cultural capital that middle- and upper-class parents have

* Concept developed by Pierre Bourdieu

* Middle- and upper-class learners have advantages from two sources:

  * Economic capital: parents can spend more on a good education

  * Cultural capital: includes tastes, values, and behaviour

**Cultural capital advantages**

* Familiarity with books and reading

* Visits to museums, concerts, and art galleries

* Understanding the importance of education

* Knowledge of how the education system works and how to use it to help children

* Working-class children have less cultural capital, which may lead teachers to view them less favourably

### Home and Community Factors

Home and local area may limit educational success

* Disadvantaged schools in poor regions: lower quality education, more social/behavioural needs, less funding

* Families may not be able to afford higher education, and children may limit their ambitions

* Loyalty to community or social class may discourage moving up the class ladder

* Lack of role models may lead children to see education as unimportant

* Crime, drugs, and gangs may interfere with education

Middle-class children are less affected; parents can provide books or tutors

### Linguistic Factors

* Basil Bernstein (1971) identified two language codes

  * Restricted code: informal, everyday speech with family/friends; limited vocabulary; meanings implied

  * Elaborated code: formal, used in schools and textbooks; meanings explicit; allows expression of complex and abstract ideas

* Middle-class children are more confident using elaborated code in school, giving teachers the impression that they are more able

* Restricted code is used by everyone and has its own strengths

* Middle-class advantage comes from being able to switch between restricted and elaborated code, allowing them to communicate effectively in both home and school contexts

* Working-class children may struggle with school tasks requiring the elaborated code, which can lower achievement

### Compensatory Education

* Aimed at raising the educational achievement of working-class learners

* Provides extra support to learners from disadvantaged backgrounds

  * Examples: extra funding for schools in poor areas, higher pay to attract good teachers

* Schemes offering free preschool places and support

  * Ensures children from disadvantaged families start primary school with basic literacy and social skills

### In-School Factors

* Explanations for working-class underachievement include factors inside school, not just outside school

* Michael Rutter et al. (1979) found that good schools improve life chances for all learners

  * Good schools are well organised with clear rules and expectations

* Tim Morris et al. (2021) found that enjoyment of school at age six positively correlates with exam achievement at 16, regardless of social class, gender, or ethnicity

* Schools serving poorer communities may provide lower-standard education due to:

  * Less funding

  * More social problems to manage

  * Poor behavioural standards

  * Low academic expectations

**Peer Groups and Subcultures**

* Peer groups can influence educational achievement

* Middle-class children often value education due to socialisation and background

* Groups with positive attitudes towards school support each other in learning

* This forms a **pro-school sub-culture**: a learner subculture sharing school values, norms of studying hard, and cooperating with teachers

**Anti-school Subcultures**

* Some working-class children rebel against school because they find it hard to achieve and fit in with middle-class rules

* Develop alternative values, attitudes, and behaviours opposing academic aims

* Formed to improve self-esteem and gain status within their peer group

* May include:

  * Truancy (skipping lessons or school)

  * Avoiding schoolwork

  * Cheating in homework or tests

  * Aggression towards teachers

  * Disliking or bullying pro-school students

  * Involvement in delinquency or serious crime outside school

### Selective and Private Education

* Children from different social backgrounds often attend different schools

* Middle- and upper-class children are more likely to attend private or selective schools

* Private schools help achievement because

  * Classes are smaller, so learners get more individual attention

  * Facilities and resources are better due to higher funding

  * Fee-paying parents mean schools provide high-quality education to satisfy them

  * A strong reputation and alumni network may help students get better jobs

* Selective schools help achievement in the following ways

  * Selected students have already shown academic potential, so teachers will have better expectations for them

  * There are likely to be more pro-school subcultures, so students are disciplined and encourage others to work hard

  * All learners are of a similar academic level, so teachers do not have to slow down to support challenged learners

### Labelling Theory

**++Introduction: Labelling and Educational Achievement++**

* Patterns of underachievement are shaped by meanings created within classrooms

* Learner progress i**++s i++**nfluenced by how teachers and learners see each other

* Evidence linking social class and achievement may lead teachers to expect working-class learners to underachieve

  * Low expectations reduce encouragement and academic support

  * This can contribute to working-class failure

**++Labelling Theory and Interactionism++**

* Labelling theory linked to interactionism

* Interactionism uses a micro-level approach

* Learners are not passive victims of material or cultural deprivation

* Focus on interactions between individuals within the school

* Teachers hold institutional power

  * Their interpretations of behaviour and potential shape interactions and outcomes

**++How Teachers Label Learners++**

* Teachers judge and classify learners

  * High-achieving, low-achieving, troublemakers, ideal learners, hardworking, lazy

* Labelling affects learner performance

* Early judgments influence all future interactions

  * Teacher expectations shape treatment

  * Treatment affects learner response and achievement

**++Howard Becker and the ‘Ideal Student’++**

* Teachers judge learners using non-academic criteria

  * Speech

  * Dress

  * Personality (cooperation, enthusiasm, politeness)

  * Conduct and appearance

* These criteria form the stereotype of the “ideal pupil”

* Social class strongly influences evaluation

  * Working-class learners are often seen as poorly motivated, lacking family support, or disruptive

  * Teachers may assume a lack of ability regardless of actual potential

* Middle-class learners are more likely to fit the ideal pupil stereotype

  * Enter school confident, fluent, and familiar with learning

  * Teachers assume greater potential and encourage achievement

**++Self-Fulfilling and Self-Negating Prophecies++**

* Teacher expectations influence learner outcomes

* Positive labels encourage effort and success

* Negative labels discourage effort and reinforce failure

* Streaming and setting institutionalised self-fulfilling prophecies

* Low placement may undermine confidence and limit achievement

**++Streaming and Setting++**

* Streaming places learners into fixed ability groups for all subjects

* Setting places learners into ability groups for specific subjects

* Both involve predicting success and failure

* Placement influences self-image

  * High streams develop confidence and high achievement

  * Low streams develop low self-esteem and see themselves as failures

* Learners in low streams may

  * Lose motivation

  * Become disruptive

  * Underachieve despite effort

**++Teacher expectations and achievement++**

* Teachers may be less ambitious for low-stream learners

  * Provide less information

  * Offer less challenging work

* Streaming and setting are closely linked to social class

  * A higher social class increases the chance of top placement

* These practices reinforce working-class underachievement

### Marxist Explanations

**Marxist Views on Education**

* Marxist explanations focus on social class and inequality in education

* Many ideas about underachievement are supported by Marxists

* Pierre Bourdieu is often described as a Marxist due to his concept of cultural capital

**Class Position (Raymond Boudon)**

* Educational choices and achievement must be understood in relation to social-class background

* Expectations differ by class

  * Middle-class learners may see failure to attend university as personal failure

  * Working-class learners may see entry into higher education as a major success

* Working-class learners may face social costs

  * May need to weaken ties with the community or friends who do not pursue higher education

* Occupational mobility matters

  * Working-class learners entering office or professional jobs may move further up than their parents

  * Middle-class learners entering professions may remain at the same class level as their parents

* Educational success should be judged according to class position, not absolute outcomes
