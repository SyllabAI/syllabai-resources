## Marxist Views of Education

### Socialisation and Social Control as Capitalist Ideological Control 

* Marxists agree that education socialises people, but into a capitalist/ruling-class value system, not shared values

* Schools teach people to accept their social position and hierarchy without challenge

* Working-class children are indoctrinated to become compliant workers:

  * Be punctual, work hard, accept low wages, and follow orders

  * Leads to false consciousness, unaware that they are being exploited

* Middle- and upper-class children attend elite/private schools, learning that they are deserving of privilege

* This ensures access to top universities and high-status jobs, reproducing ruling-class power and wealth

* Education thus maintains the class system over time, benefiting the bourgeoisie

### The Hidden and Official Curriculum

* Capitalist values are passed through the **official curriculum** and the **hidden curriculum**

* **Official curriculum**: separates knowledge into subjects, does not teach the true nature of society, discourages critical thinking

  * Subjects like sociology are limited, so most children are not encouraged to question society

* **Hidden curriculum**: teaches obedience to authority and acceptance of hierarchies, preparing children for the capitalist workplace

### The Middle-Class Culture in Schools  

* Schools reflect middle-class values, as teachers are mostly middle-class and reward middle-class attitudes and behaviour

* Working-class children may feel out of place, needing to act differently at school and at home

  * Some rebel with disruptive behaviour, others struggle academically

* Pierre Bourdieu: cultural capital - knowledge of culture, manners, behaviour, and school norms

* Middle-class families have more cultural capital, passing it to children to navigate middle-class schools 

### The Myth of Meritocracy 

* Marxists reject **meritocracy and social mobility**, arguing that schools **reproduce existing inequalities**

* Success depends more on **class background** than talent or effort

* Middle- and upper-class children gain advantages: private schools, tutors, books, resources

* Working-class children start at a **disadvantage** in competing for high-status jobs

* Meritocracy is therefore considered a **myth**

### Material Factors, e.g. Access to Digital Technology 

* Material factors disadvantage working-class children in education

* At home: limited access to digital devices, high-speed internet, books, and sometimes no private space for study

* At school: fewer resources, less equipment, and limited access to extra learning opportunities 

* These disadvantages reduce opportunities to achieve, reinforcing class inequality

### Setting by Ability 

* Setting and streaming: grouping learners by ability for classes or subjects; higher sets often get faster-paced teaching and more challenging material

* Marxists argue this is biased towards class: working-class learners are more often placed in lower sets, limiting opportunities and achievement

* Causes include material and cultural disadvantages (resources, support at home) and teacher stereotyping based on social class

* The potential of working-class children is underestimated, reinforcing social inequality
