## Global Differences in Educational Achievement and Experience

### Availability of Schools

* Accessible schooling is a relatively recent development in many countries.

  * Most people only had access to a few faith schools

* Building a national school system is challenging

  * Limited resources: money, teachers, teaching materials

  * Some countries still lack enough schools for all children

* Barriers for learners:

  * Long travel distances for rural/remote children

  * Very large class sizes (50+ learners per class)

* The quality of education can be poor due to a lack of basics:

  * Electricity, drinking water, toilets, and classroom ventilation

  * Books and teaching resources

  * Special equipment

  * Qualified teachers

### Poverty/Wealth

* Many children do not attend or complete school due to family poverty

  * Children may work on the farm or at home to support the family

* Free education exists in many countries, but some costs remain (uniforms, books)

  * For some families, it is more practical for children not to attend

* Poverty also affects health:

  * Poor diet and living conditions cause more illness, leading to missing school

  * Lack of electricity at home makes studying after dark harder

* Wealthier families can invest in extra education:

  * Private tutors or additional resources give children an advantage

  * Increases the likelihood of achievement regardless of natural ability.

### Access to Education for Girls

* Historically, schooling was mostly for boys; girls received little education

* In some developing countries, boys are still prioritised:

  * Families see boys’ education as an investment for their future careers and supportingthe  family

  * Girls may be expected to marry and join their husband’s family

* International efforts:

  * UN and member governments see girls’ education as a human right and essential for development

* Benefits of educating girls:

  * Improves the living standards, health, and nutrition of families

  * Delays marriage and reduces the number of children

  * Increases access to formal jobs and income

  * Educated women pass knowledge about health and nutrition to their families

### Class Sizes

* Smaller classes generally improve achievement as teachers can focus more on individual learners

* Large classes make it harder for teachers to support all learners

* Typical class sizes:

  * Industrialised countries: 25–30 learners in state schools

  * Private schools: smaller

  * Developing countries: often 70+ learners per class (UNESCO, 2016)

* Very large classes for young children can harm their early learning

### Norms, Values and Cultural Differences

* School attendance has only become normal worldwide relatively recently

* Some families or communities do not see school as important

  * Often, because the parents did not attend themselves

* Other families place a high value on education

  * Many see school and qualifications as the main way to improve life chances or escape poverty

### Access to the Internet / Digital Resources

* Schools worldwide vary in access to digital resources

  * Some have computers and good internet, so learners develop ICT skills and can research

  * Others have limited or no access

* COVID-19 pandemic affected education globally

  * Many schools closed for months

  * Learners without digital access often miss out on education

  * Schools with internet could provide online lessons and exams
