## Functionalist Views of Education

Functionalists believe schools perform vital functions that contribute to the well-being of society.

* **++Socialisation++**: Schools pass on the norms and values from one generation to the next.

* **++Social Control++**: Schools teach children to follow rules and learn to control their behaviour.

* **++Economic Function++**: The education system ensures that society has enough people with qualifications and skills to maintain the economy.

* **Selective Function**: By testing students and awarding qualifications, the education system ensures individuals can find work appropriate for them.

### Meritocracy and Social Mobility

* Societies rely on meritocracy to **allocate roles based on talent and effort**, ensuring the most capable fill important jobs.

* Schools apply meritocracy by **measuring achievement through universal standards** like exams, rather than ascribed status.

* This rewards students who **work hard and demonstrate ability**, linking effort to outcomes.

  Education, therefore, motivates learners to **develop skills and strive**, supporting social efficiency and fairness.

* Success in school provides **qualifications** that enable **upward social mobility**, reinforcing the meritocratic system.

### Role Allocation

* One function of education is to produce people with the skills and qualifications required by the economy.

* Meritocracy ensures the most skilled learners go into the fields that best match their skills, with specific qualifications or subjects required.

* Some careers (e.g., medicine, law) need further **university study**, while others require practical training or vocational skills.

* Education performs **role allocation** by assessing ability through **tests and exams**, guiding learners onto suitable paths

* High achievers may progress to **university and high-status jobs**, while others enter trades or lower-paid roles

  * Even low-status jobs are necessary, so that all required jobs are occupied

* When functioning well, the system matches the **number of qualified people to the number of jobs available**, producing the workforce society needs

### Value Consensus

* The education system **socialises children into a shared set of values**, making them feel like a part of society

* Schools create a **value consensus**, where most people adopt similar values

* Children learn shared values through **collective experiences**:

  * Being with other children, learning together, and assemblies

  * Singing the national anthem, seeing national symbols (flags, head of state photos)

* Schools act as a **bridge between family and society**

  * At home, children have **ascribed status** and are valued individually

  * At school, children must **achieve status** among peers, preparing them for adult work roles

### Equal Opportunities

* Functionalists argue that society and education are **meritocratic**, and they reward ability rather than background.

* Schools provide **equal opportunities**: all children follow the same curriculum and take the same exams.

  * Differences in outcomes reflect **ability or effort**, not social class.

* In a meritocracy, a working-class child with talent + effort can achieve as much as a privileged child

* Schools teach a **value system promoting meritocracy and fairness**, leading most people to accept the system as legitimate

* Learners who fail tend to **blame themselves**, reinforcing the idea that success is earned and the system is fair

### Standardised Testing

* Functionalist meritocracy and role allocation depend on **standardised testing** to compare all learners fairly.

* Exam boards (e.g., Cambridge Assessment) set **uniform exams** taken across schools and countries.

  * Grades and marks indicate who has performed well, allowing for fair comparison.

* Schools enable learners to be judged by **universal standards** and to **accept them**, which is essential for a modern industrial economy.

### Setting by Ability

* Learners have **different abilities**, so equal opportunity does not mean everyone should learn at the same pace

* Slowing teaching for slower learners may **hold back faster learners** and still not meet the needs of the weakest

* Functionalists support **setting by ability**: learners are grouped into classes based on ability

  * Different classes may study at **different paces** or cover **different material**

### Vocationalism

* Vocational education trains learners for specific trades or professions

* Functionalists see it as part of **role allocation**, ensuring essential jobs are filled

* Provides an **alternative to academic routes**, e.g., healthcare or vehicle maintenance

* Ensures society has **skilled people for the necessary work**
