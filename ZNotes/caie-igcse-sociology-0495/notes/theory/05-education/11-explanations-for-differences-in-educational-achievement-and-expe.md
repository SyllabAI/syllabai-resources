## Explanations for Differences in Educational Achievement and Experience Based on Ethnicity

### Institutional Racism

* Institutional racism refers to discrimination built into the policies and practices of institutions, often unconsciously

* Minority ethnic groups may be disadvantaged across institutions, including education

* Learners may react to perceived racism by rebelling against school authority

### Ethnocentric Curriculum

* Ethnocentrism occurs when the dominant group’s culture is treated as most important

* Can be based on the belief that minority groups should assimilate into the dominant culture

* Many education systems follow an ethnocentric curriculum

* Shown through teaching that prioritises the dominant culture

  * Minority languages and cultures are seen as less valuable

  * Literature and history of minority groups excluded

  * Religious practices and festivals of minorities are ignored or replaced by majority traditions

* **Effects of ethnocentrism**

  * Ethnic minority learners may lose interest because lessons seem irrelevant

  * Learners may view their own culture as inferior or unimportant, reducing confidence and engagement in education

  **Ethnocentrism without a dominant group**

  * Can exist in countries with a colonial history

  * Former colonies may retain the education systems of the colonising power

### Role Models

* Institutional racism limits minority ethnic representation in schools and society

* Few role models in teaching, politics, and high-status professions

* Learners may limit their ambitions due to the belief that opportunities are unequal

### Linguistic Factors

* Teaching in a non-native (often colonial) language can limit understanding and attainment

* Minority languages may be devalued and seen as incorrect, leading to negative labelling and unfair assessment

* Example: AAVE

  * Misjudged as ungrammatical

  * Shown to be a valid language (Labov)

* Bilingualism can be an educational advantage

### Cultural, Material and Social Factors

Ethnic background affects learners’ achievement through cultural, social, and material factors.

* Cultural:

  * Some cultures value academic success less; this may reduce learner motivation.

  * Cultural/religious values may clash with school norms, which slows progress.

* Social:

  * Dress, appearance, or speech can reinforce teacher stereotypes; a self-fulfilling prophecy.

  * Minority ethnic families may have less cultural capital, hindering achievement.

* Material:

  * Lack of money for resources or trips, which disproportionately affects working-class minority ethnic children.

### Home/Community Factors

* Louise Archer studied why children of Chinese background in UK schools outperformed other ethnic groups.

* Cultural values

  * Parents placed a high value on education, investing time and money regardless of wealth.

  * Children are socialised to prioritise educational success.

  * Parents frequently discussed schoolwork and career planning, giving children clear goals

  * Chinese children are told to “be the best,” not just “do your best,” leading to stronger motivation.

### In-school Factors, e.g. Pro- and Anti-school Sub-cultures, Ethnic Sub-cultures

* For schools to be meritocratic, all learners need equal opportunities.

* Institutional racism in schools

  * Discrimination in streaming and setting: minority ethnic learners may be placed in lower sets based on assumptions, not ability.

  * An ethnocentric curriculum favours the dominant culture.

  * Teacher assumptions: minority ethnic learners may be seen as low-achieving or labelled 'troublemakers' → self-fulfilling prophecy.

**++Pro- and Anti-School Sub-Cultures++**

* Subcultures influence achievement and can form based on ethnicity and social class.

* Tony Sewell (1997) – African Caribbean boys ('rebels')

  * Anti-school sub-culture, opposed rules, sometimes violent, highly aggressive masculinity

  * Behaviour is partly a response to racist teachers and wider school racism

* Complex anti-school subcultures

  * Máirtín Mac an Ghaill – 'Black sisters' (female African Caribbean & Asian learners in inner-city UK colleges)

  * Criticised school/college: streaming, teachers’ failure to recognise ability, ethnocentric curriculum

  * Despite criticism, prioritised education → worked hard and achieved success

  * Anti-school in attitude but pro-education in outcome

### Labelling Theory

**++Labelling and Self-Fulfilling Prophecies++**

* Teachers often label learners from different groups, creating assumptions about their abilities.

* Minority ethnic learners may be placed in lower streams or sets due to language differences or teacher stereotypes rather than actual ability.

**++African Caribbean Learners in the UK++**

* Higher proportions of African-Caribbean boys are excluded from schools compared to other ethnic groups.

* Initial behaviour from some boys led to an expectation of poor behaviour for all.

* Teachers often label boys as troublemakers, expecting them to achieve at a lower level.

  Labels can become a **master status**

  * A label becomes the most important identity in the eyes of others.

  * Internalised by the learner, believes it describes them accurately.

**++Labelling and High-Expectation Groups++**

* Chinese learners studied by Archer faced high expectations from teachers.

* High expectations had drawbacks

  * Little praise for success because it was expected.

  * Little support was provided when difficulties arose because teachers assumed self-sufficiency.

* Peer labelling also occurs

  * Non-Chinese peers saw Chinese learners as 'geeks'.

  * Some learners may reject the label by reducing effort, risking failure to gain peer approval.
