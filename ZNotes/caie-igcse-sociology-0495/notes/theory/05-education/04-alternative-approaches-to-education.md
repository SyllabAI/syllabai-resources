## Alternative Approaches to Education

### Online Learning

* Education is increasingly separated from physical schools, with more learning taking place online or at home through distance education rather than in classrooms.

  * This allows learners to study independently and suits adults, workers, or those without access to nearby schools.

* Distance education developed from early models like open universities, which used posted materials and broadcast lessons.

  * This evolved into **MOOCs (massive open online courses)**, which are free, highly interactive, and allow learners worldwide to discuss and learn together without meeting physically.

* Online learning became essential during the COVID-19 pandemic when schools closed globally.

  * Schools adapted by delivering live lessons, digital assignments, and online feedback, proving that large-scale online education can work.

* Online education increases access but depends heavily on digital resources.

  * Learners without reliable internet or enough devices, especially in families with multiple children, are disadvantaged.

* Online learning does not fully replace the social role of schools.

  * Many learners missed peer interaction and felt online contact could not substitute face-to-face socialisation.

### Homeschooling

* Homeschooling involves children being educated at home rather than in a formal school, usually by parents or hired tutors, giving families direct control over learning.

* The legality of homeschooling varies by country.

* In more flexible systems, homeschooling allows learners to follow their interests and learn at their own pace, rather than a fixed school timetable.

* During the COVID-19 pandemic, many parents temporarily educated children at home using work set by schools.

* Since the pandemic, some learners have continued homeschooling by choice.

  * Research suggests they prefer the autonomy, pace, and learning environment compared to returning to school.

**++Reasons for Homeschooling++**

* Parents believe that they can teach their children better than a school can.

* A learner may have a sickness/disability, making school a struggle

* Children who live in remote areas or who are constantly travelling may not be able to attend school

* Parents may disapprove of schools and some values they don’t want their kids to be taught

* Parents may want their children to avoid bullying/peer pressure.

**++Criticisms of Homeschooling++**

* Homeschooling reduces contact with same-age peers.

  * This can be seen positively because age-segregated peer groups are artificial and rare in adult life.

  * Greater interaction with adults may build confidence and maturity.

  * Learners are protected from negative peer pressure and bullying.

* Homeschooling limits wider social experiences.

  * Learners may miss forming long-term friendships that are a common part of school life.

  * Fewer opportunities to develop key social skills such as cooperation, teamwork, and dealing with people they dislike.

  * Limited exposure to people from different backgrounds may reduce tolerance and flexibility needed in adult society.

### Unschooling

* Unschooling is an alternative form of education where children control **what**, **when**, and **how** they learn.

  * Learning follows the child’s interests and pace rather than a fixed timetable or syllabus.

* Formal teaching and subject-based lessons are minimal.

  * Parents act as facilitators instead of traditional teachers, usually within a homeschooling setting.

* The focus is on developing the ability to **learn independently**.

  * Emphasis is placed on curiosity, problem-solving, and self-motivation rather than memorising set knowledge.

### Vocational Learning

* Vocational learning focuses on teaching **practical, job-specific skills** needed for particular occupations.

  * It is designed for learners who may not suit purely academic study and prefer hands-on learning.

  * The aim is to prepare learners directly for skilled work (e.g. training for trades or technical roles).

### Progressive Schooling

* Rejects traditional education assumptions; focuses on **learner-centred approaches** rather than rote memorisation.

* Emphasises **learning by doing**, problem-solving, and critical thinking to prepare learners for real-life situations.

* Develops **social skills** through group work, cooperation, and collaboration with peers.

* Encourages **responsibility, independence, decision-making, and contributing to the community**, rather than just following instructions.

* Examples include **Montessori schools**, which structure learning around activities chosen by learners, promoting self-directed learning and practical skills.
