## **Alternatives to the Family**

People also live in housholds that are not family, and some types are discussed below:

### Single Person Households 

* There are growing numbers of one-person households in many countries, such as in Europe but a very low number in Asian countries

* The are 4 major types of people living alone:

  * Old people whose children have moved and their partner has passed

  * Young adults who have moved out of their parent’s house but have not yet married

  * People who want to live alone such as women focusing on their career

  * People who have separated or divorced from their partner, usually men

* In Europe and North America, the number of singleperson households has increased as a result of greater equalityfor women, a decline in the pressure to marry and increased number of divorces.

### Shared Households 

* These households consist of people living together who are not related, and may share some rooms, the kitchen or the lounge

* This could be a group of university students looking to save money

### Friends as Family

* The friend groups performs the functions of a familly, providing support, for many people, especially those who are far away or do not have contact with family

* Some sociologists argue that people may rely more on friends:

  * They provide support for people seeking independence from their parents

  * They may provide greater emotional, practical support

  * Friendships with other unattached people may help divorced/separated people

* Young people rely on friends from the same age group

* Number of young people sharing a house has increased, mainly for economic reasons

* Friendhips are based on equality and mutual respect, unlike unequal family bonds

  * but the family provides functions that friends cannot, such as inheritance or care in old age
