## **Alternatives to Marriage**

### Cohabition

* Cohabitation: two people living together in a sexual relationship without marriage

  * Exists historically in many societies

  * Often accepted by communities as equivalent to marriage

* Last \~50 years: increasing acceptance

  * Especially in Europe and North America

  * Linked to changing social values

* Now widely seen as a normal and legitimate relationship type

* Fulcher and Scott’s (1999) 3 types of cohabitation:

  * **++permanent/long-term relationships++**, which have no formal ceremony

    * Linked to strong welfare states

      * The state provides economic security across the life course

    * Women are less economically dependent on male partners

      * Reduced the need for marriage as a formal economic contract

  * **++trial marriages,++** when a couple live together before marriage (common in countries such as the UK:)

    * The majority cohabit before marriage

    * Shows delayed marriage, not rejection

    * Economic reasons: higher education, low income, wedding costs

  * **++short-term relationships++** without commitment

    * Common among young couples

    * Financial motives: sharing rent and living costs

    * Used to test compatibility before marriage or children

    * Some cohabit with multiple partners sequentially

The increase in cohabitation helps to explain both the fall in marriage rates and the rising age of marriage

### Singlehood

* **Voluntary singlehood**

  * Chosen not to marry or cohabit

  * Women prioritise career; a secure income makes marriage a matter of choices

  * Socially acceptable to live alone or with friends/housemates

* **Post-divorce/separation**

  * Many live alone after a breakup

  * Social contact is important, and is aided by the internet/social media

    * Especially for those outside cities or with children

* **Involuntary or long-term singlehood**

  * Some never find a partner

  * Some prefer independence, keeping singlehood

### Lone Parents

Other than the death of the other parent, there are 2 other factors:

* **++Divorce/separation:++** Children fare better with one parent than in a family with arguments and an unhappy atmosphere

* **++Unmarried Parents:++** Many lone parents have never married

  * Women may choose to raise children alone

  * Easier due to the ability to work and welfare support

* **++Social Acceptance:++**

  * Lone parenthood is increasingly socially accepted

  * Children of lone parents are less likely to face stigma from peers

  * This is partly because divorce and other reasons for lone parenthood are now viewed as socially normal

    * and partly because the number of lone parents has increased

* Many lone parents eventually marry or remarry, forming two-parent families; lone parenthood is often temporary.

* Many children of lone-parent families have two living parents:

  * May live with one parent but maintain regular contact with the other

  * Some split time equally between parents

  * The frequency of contact and financial provision by the non-resident parent varies a lot

**++New Right View++**

* Lone parents are a social problem

  * Claim links to children’s educational underachievement, petty crime, and later unemployment

* **Counter view:** family type is not the main factor

  * Poverty is the key influence

  * Many social problems are associated with poverty; lone parents are more likely to experience them than two-parent families

* Not all children of lone parents have problems; some children in two-parent families do

### Civil Partnership

* Same-sex couples can legally marry in 31 countries (e.g., UK, Mexico, NZ)

* Other countries allow **civil partnerships**, giving same-sex couples similar rights to marriage

* Some countries do not legally recognise same-sex relationships, and may be socially disapproved or illegal

* Civil partnerships can also be available to opposite-sex couples as a legal alternative to marriage
