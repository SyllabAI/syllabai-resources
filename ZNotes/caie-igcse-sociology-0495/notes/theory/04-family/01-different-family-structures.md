# Family

## Different Family Structures

### Nuclear Family

* The nuclear family consists of two heterosexual adults in a long-term relationship living with their dependent children

* Sociological writing in the 20th century treated the nuclear family as the normal and ideal family type

  * Seen as the most common and preferred family form in industrial societies such as the UK and the USA

* Functionalists argued that the nuclear family was universal

  * In 1949, George Murdock claimed it was the basic family unit found worldwide, based on cross-cultural research across many societies

* Other family forms were viewed as variations built around the nuclear family

  * Extended families were argued to contain a core unit of parents and children

* Murdock’s claim of the nuclear family’s universality was widely criticised

  * Other family types became increasingly common from the 1970s onwards

### Extended Families

**Extended families** include more relatives than the nuclear family, usually living in the same household. There are many types of extended family:

* **++Vertically Extended++**

  * This is a family with more than two generations (usually including grandparents)

* **++Horizontally Extended++**

  * This is a family with aunts, uncles and cousins (people from the same generation as parents as children)

* **++Modified Extended++**

  * A family not in the same household, but sharing regular contact and mutual support

  * In industrial societies, extended families may exist without shared housing

    * Improved transport and communication allow relatives living far apart to stay in regular contact

    * When support and contact are maintained despite distance, this is described as a modified extended family

* **++Beanpole++**

  * Smaller families and longer life expectancy have produced a beanpole family structure

    * Fewer children mean fewer siblings, aunts, and uncles

    * Several generations may exist at once, but with few numbers in each

    * This is a vertically extended family with a tall but thin family tree

### Reconstituted Family

* A reconstituted family is formed when a person remarries after divorce or the death of a partner creating a new family unit

* Reconstituted families can involve:

  * One or both partners may have been married before

  * One or both partners may have children

  * Children from previous relationships may live in the new household or with another parent

* Remarriage creates new social relationships Including step-parents, children, siblings, and grandparents.

* Norms and roles are often unclear in reconstituted families

  * Step-parents may be unsure about their authority over their partner’s children

  * Children may be uncertain about how much authority a step-parent has#

### Same-sex family

* Same-sex families are formed when two adults of the same sex raise or adopt children
* In past, these families were not accepted in society
* Modern societies hold them to the same regard as opposite-sex families

### Lone Parent Family

* Lone-parent families: families where only one parent is present to raise the child/children

  Historical context:

  * Traditionally formed when one parent died, leaving the other to raise children alone

  * Often temporary, as a surviving parent might remarry → creating a reconstituted family

* Modern trends:

  * Since the mid-20th century, lone-parent families have increased in industrial societies

  * These reasons can explain the increase in this type of family since the 20th century:

    * increase in divorce rates

    * Women have greater financial independence due to more equality, so they do not need a man for income

    * More and more women choose to raise the child without the involvement of the father

### Empty Nest Family

* This is when adult children leave the home for marriage, education or employment, leaving parents alone

* Parents may stay in this family for a longer time due to an increase in life expectancy

* **++Boomerang Family++**

  * This is when adult children (called **boomerang children**) return to live with parents due to economic reasons

### Polygamous Family

* involves a man married to two or more women, or a woman married to two or more men

* Study according to the laws of your region, which is further explained in the types of marriage

### Childless Family

* Childless families: couples who do not have children

* Historically, women without children were stigmatised or pitied because motherhood was seen as their primary role

* Developments in medtech. allow previously infertile couples to have children

* Many childless families today are childless by choice

  * Reasons include the high cost of raising children, women prioritising careers, and concerns about the future world (e.g., climate risks)

### The Strengths and Limitations of Different Types of Family

* **++Nuclear Family++**

  * most widespread and functional type

  * geographically mobile - easy to move and relocate

  * provided role models and stability

  * Feminist Ann Oakley criticises this type because it can be oppressive to women

* **++Extended Family++**

  * Extended families provide support for raising children and caring for older generations

  * Modified extended families are becoming more common

* **++Lone-Parent Family++**

  * Lone-parent families are increasingly common and can successfully raise children despite being criticised by sociologists

  * Some parents believe a single-parent environment is better than a two-parent household with constant conflict or unhappiness

* **++Reconstituted Family++**

  * Reconstituted families face challenges as two existing families learn to live together

    * Many reconstituted families are successful

  * Children of divorced parents may split time between two families, widening their support network
