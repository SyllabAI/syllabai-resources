## **Explanations of Changing Patterns and Trends in Marriage, Divorce and Cohabitation**

### Marriage Trends

* Slightly more women than men are married

  * Women usually marry **older men**, creating a gap in younger ages that narrows after 40

* Marriage rates have declined in many countries

  * Long-term decline in the **UK and Australia**

  * Increase in countries such as **China, Russia and Bangladesh**

* Marriage now happens **later in life**

  * Rising average age at first marriage

  * Large differences between countries

* Divorce has become more common

  * Marriage is less permanent than before

* Fewer people remarry after divorce or widowhood

* Cohabitation has increased

  * Often long-term and may include **children**

  * Not included in marriage statistics

* Remarriage is more common than in the past

  * Often follows **divorce**, not death

  * Men are more likely to remarry

  * Creates **reconstituted families**

### Divorce Trends

**++Trends in Divorce Rates++**

* Divorce rates have risen sharply in many modern industrial societies

* Rates are higher globally than in the past

* Divorce rates vary widely between countries

  * Lowest rates occur where divorce is socially unacceptable

  * Strong religious or cultural restrictions reduce divorce

**++Legal Nature and Measurement of Divorce++**

* Divorce is the **legal ending of a marriage**

* Because divorce is a legal process, divorce rates can be recorded accurately

* However, marital breakdown is difficult to measure overall

  * Alternatives to divorce are not officially recorded

**++Alternatives to Divorce (Unrecorded)++**

* **Separation**

  * Partners stop living together

  * May lead to divorce, but not always

  * No reliable statistics available

* **Desertion**

  * One partner leaves the family

  * No accurate data collected

* **Empty-shell marriage**

  * Couple remain legally married and lives together

  * The relationship lacks intimacy or affection

  * Marriage exists in name only

**++Impact of Legal Changes++**

* Legal changes have made divorce easier and cheaper

* Divorce laws now allow separation where it was previously restricted or impossible

* The rise in divorce suggests that many couples would have divorced earlier if allowed

* Relaxed divorce laws have made divorce quicker and more accessible

**++Gender and Cultural Patterns in Divorce++**

* Divorce patterns differ by gender and culture

* In some Islamic marriages, men can end a marriage unilaterally

* In Europe, women are more likely to initiate divorce

* Women are less likely to remarry after divorce than men

**++Social Characteristics Affecting Divorce (Europe & North America)++**

* Those who marry young are more likely to divorce

* Middle-class people are less likely to divorce than working-class people

  * Reversal of the past, when only the wealthy could afford divorce

* People with strong religious beliefs are less likely to divorce

  * Catholicism forbids divorce and does not allow remarriage in the church

**++Changing Attitudes and Values++**

* Changing social attitudes have increased divorce

* Individuals prioritise personal happiness over traditional marital obligations

* Divorce carries less stigma than in the past

* Being unmarried or choosing to be child-free is more socially accepted

**++Secularisation and Marriage++**

* Secularisation has reduced commitment to marriage

* Religious beliefs play a smaller role in shaping behaviour

* Marriage is less likely to be seen as a lifelong religious commitment

* Civil marriages are more common than religious ceremonies

**++Women’s Financial Independence++**

* Women’s increased financial independence has contributed to divorce

* Women no longer rely on marriage for economic security

* Less willingness to accept traditional expressive roles and domestic labour

* Greater independence makes post-divorce survival more realistic

**++Life Expectancy and Divorce++**

* Longer life expectancy increases the likelihood of divorce

* Marriages last longer, including after children leave home

* Dissatisfaction can accumulate over time

* Some couples divorce to pursue new lifestyles or opportunities

**++Extra-marital Relationships and Technology++**

* Extra-marital relationships are often a factor in divorce

* Dating apps and online communication make meeting alternative partners easier

**++Wider Changes Affecting Family Patterns++**

* Changes in legislation have reshaped family patterns

  * Divorce laws relaxed

  * Same-sex marriage is legalised in some countries

* Cultural expectations have weakened

  * Reduced family and community pressure to conform

  * Individuals expect greater freedom to choose how they live

* The role of the internet

  * Online information increases awareness of lifestyle and relationship choices

  * Social media and dating apps make forming relationships easier
