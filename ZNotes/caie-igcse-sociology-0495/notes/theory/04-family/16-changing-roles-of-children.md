## Changing Roles of Children

### Definition of Childhood

* Children are people who have not yet reached adulthood

* The age of adulthood varies between societies

* Children’s lives are regulated by laws and are different from adult lives

  * e.g. schooling and restrictions on labour

* Adult rights and responsibilities are gained gradually, at different ages in different societies

### Rites of Passage

* Many traditional societies mark the transition from childhood to adulthood with a rite of passage

* For boys, this often involves separation from the family and initiation rituals

  * After the ceremony, they are considered full adult members of the community, regardless of age

### Gender and Adulthood in Traditional Societies

* For girls, biological events may mark adulthood

  * First menstruation often seen as entry into womanhood

### Childhood in the Past (according to Philippe Ariès)

* Children in medieval Europe were treated as small adults

* Worked with adults, dressed like adults, shared games, festivals, and punishments

* Not viewed as innocent or vulnerable so exposed death or sex

* Small size seen as suitability for work, not as a difference from adults

### Childhood in Modern Industrial Societies

* Children are under adult authority (parents, teachers)

  * Expected to be obedient and accept adult knowledge

* Daily life tightly controlled (activities, movement)

* Clear distinction between childhood and adulthood

* Childhood is socially and culturally constructed, not natural

* Increased control is a response to perceived risks

  * Less freedom of movement due to fears such as traffic and harm from adults

* Social control shapes the modern experience of childhood

### Children’s Rights and Power

* New laws and regulations give children greater protection and power

* Corporal punishment banned in many countries

* Most countries have signed the **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child**

  * Children have the right to be listened to

  * Children have the right to express their views

### Economic Burden/Cost

* In the past, children were an economic benefit, working early to support the family

* This still occurs in some African societies

* In modern industrial societies, children are an economic cost because:

  * Children cant do paid work to add to the family's income

  * Childcare, early education and school education are expensive despite state run insitituions

  * Parents are expected to provide higher living standards

  * Children put pressure on parents to buy various consumer goods such as toys, snacks

  * Children are dependent on their parents for longer

    * boomerang children may be a burden later on

### Child-Centredness

Modern industrial societies are **child-centred**, with children central to family life and society

* Childhood is viewed as a period of innocence, separate from adult work and responsibilities

* Industries produce specialised goods for children (clothes, food)

* Children exert consumer pressure on parents and they often comply

* Children have shifted from economic **producers** in the past to major **consumers** today

Societies have become more child-centred because:

* families are smaller, so each child gets more attention

* children spend more time with their parents

* standards of living have improved, so more money can be spent on children

* companies and advertising target children as consumers

* laws to protect children may pressure parents to be extra careful

* adults may be pressured to be ‘good parents’ with a range of media on this topic

### Boomerang Children

* Parent–child relationships often continue after age 18

* Increasing numbers of adult children live with parents in Europe and North America

  * Some leave but return due to housing or marriage costs

* Known as **boomerang children**; families are **boomerang families**

  * Results in a **full nest** rather than an empty nest

* Parents remain financially responsible for children for longer than in the past
