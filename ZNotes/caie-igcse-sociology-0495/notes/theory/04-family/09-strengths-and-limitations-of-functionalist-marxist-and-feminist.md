## **Strengths and Limitations of Functionalist, Marxist and Feminist Views of the Role of the Family**

#|
||

View

|

Strengths

|

Limitations

||
||

**Functionalist**

|

• Most people still live mainly in nuclear families, supporting the idea that they are common and socially normal.

• Recognises that families can benefit individuals and contribute positively to social stability

|

• Based largely on the experiences of middle-class white men, it ignores problems like abuse and violence

• Fails to recognise that non-nuclear families can perform functions equally well

• Seen as outdated and sexist, assuming women want domestic roles and men want limited involvement

||
||

**Marxist**

|

• Shows how the nuclear family supports and maintains capitalism

• Explains the importance of families in sustaining capitalist society

• Highlights the family’s role in consumerism

|

• Offers no realistic alternative, as families exist in all societies

• Overly focused on work and the economy

• Underplays the personal importance of family life

• Often neglects women’s oppression

||
||

**Feminist**

|

• Highlights ignored issues such as power inequalities, domestic labour, violence and abuse

• Challenges the idea that nuclear families benefit everyone

• Values family diversity without ranking family types

• Recognises positive social changes like divorce and cohabitation

|

• Many women report satisfaction in nuclear families and value motherhood

• Tends to see all male-female relationships as exploitative, ignoring love and mutual respect
