## **Changing Conjugal Roles**

### More Diverse Family Types

* Roles of partners (usually in marriage) were traditionally fixed with little mobility

* Increasing family diversity has changed conjugal roles

* In same-sex families, roles are often unclear or not identifiable in traditional terms

### Segregated to Joint Conjugal Roles

* **Functionalists** argue that families should have separate gender roles

  * women taking the **expressive** role (emotional and housekeeping)

  * men taking the **instrumental** role (economic work and decisions)

**++Dual-Worker Families++**

* Both partners have paid work

* Men increasingly do traditional “female” chores (cooking, cleaning); these men are called **“new men”**

* Some families have **role reversal**, where the woman works, and the man becomes a **househusband,** managing the home

**++Impact of Traditional Female Roles++**

* Traditional female roles in the family limit women’s opportunities at work.

* Responsibility for childcare often reduces the amount of paid work women can do, leading many to work part-time.

* Time taken out of paid work for childbirth and childcare can reduce access to training and promotion.

* Feminists therefore argue that conjugal roles should be joint rather than segregated.

**++Joint Conjugal Roles (Willmott & Young)++**

* Found a more equal division of domestic labour in UK families

* Based on joint conjugal roles rather than segregated roles

* Husbands and wives both contribute to family responsibilities, though some gender division remains

There are several possible reasons for the change towards more symmetrical families:

* **Privatisation of Nuclear Families:** Nuclear families became **privatised**, focusing life around the family home rather than work or community

  * Improved home comforts (heating, TV) supported this change

  * Privatisation encouraged a more equal partnership between spouses

* The **decline of the extended family** meant there was less pressure from other relatives to conform to their norms

* **Women have a higher status** in society, so men are more likely to treat them on an equal basis

* **Paid work gives women greater independence**, leading to more equality within the family (e.g., joint decision-making)

  * **Decreasing average number of children** allows women to work more and pursue careers

* Men and women can now **choose and negotiate roles** more easily

* **Labour-saving devices** reduce housework time and effort, making the home more attractive

**++Critiques of Equal Gender Roles in Domestic Labour++**

* Willmott and Young may be too optimistic about equality

  * Men often choose tasks that are less dull or repetitive (e.g., special meals)

  * Both genders may overestimate men’s contribution and underestimate women’s

  * Men still make most major family decisions 

* Some household tasks done by others:

  * Children may have chores

  * Families may employ nannies or staff; childcare often seen as women’s work, driving/other tasks as men’s work

* Research challenges:

  * Deciding which tasks to include and how to measure them

  * Different accounts from husbands and wives can contradict findings

### The Pivot/Sandwich Generation

* Middle-aged adults care for **both children and elderly parents** → called the **sandwich** or **pivot generation**

* Period is temporary but demands on time and energy can be intense

* Delayed childbirth increases overlap between caring for children and parents

* Caring responsibilities often fall mainly on women, reflecting traditional **expressive roles**
