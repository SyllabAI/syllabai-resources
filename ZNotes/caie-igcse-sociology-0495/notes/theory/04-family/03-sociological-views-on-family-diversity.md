## **Sociological Views on Family Diversity**

### The New Right View

* New Right thinkers argue society should return to the nuclear family because newer, diverse family types cannot perform key family functions

* Children are seen as needing to be raised by both a mother and a father

* According to the New Right, changes that have undermined family functions include:

  * increase in lone-parent families

  * easier divorce in some regions

  * cohabitation (seen as unstable and disruptive)

  * rise of feminism

  * Women working more often (disruptive to the development of children)

* The New Right links family change to social problems, including educational failure, rising crime and delinquency, and a dependency culture

* Welfare benefits are seen as discouraging work and encouraging reliance on the state, which interferes with the ‘natural’ family order

  * Disrupts gender roles and child-rearing

  * Produces dysfunctional families

* Lone-mother families are criticised

  * Boys lack a male role model for paid work, responsibility, and controlled behaviour

* New Right politicians support reducing welfare, discouraging women from working, and strengthening the male-breadwinner nuclear family

Criticisms from feminist sociologists (e.g., Pamela Abbott, Claire Wallace, Melissa Tyler):

* People have the right to live in varied family types: alone, same-sex couples, lone-parent, female-breadwinner households

* Different family types can successfully raise children

* Lone parents often struggle due to poverty, requiring more financial support rather than cuts to welfare

### The Postmodernist View

* Postmodernists view the growth of new family types positively

  * People are less constrained by traditional values, family, gender, social class, or religion

  * Individuals can make their own choices about living arrangements, whether in families or alternatives

* Criticisms of postmodernist views:

  * Some sociologists argue that diversity and choice are exaggerated

  * Many communities still expect conventional family patterns and roles

  * Those who deviate may face stigma or ostracism

  * Some people find too many options overwhelming and prefer traditional family models

### The Extent of Family Diversity and the Dominance of the Nuclear Family

* New Right and postmodernist views assume family diversity is greater now than in the past

* Actual diversity is highest in Europe and North America, but traditional family types remain common

* UK 2021 data:

  * Most common: opposite-sex married couple with no children (includes couples yet to have children or whose children have left home)

  * Second most common: opposite-sex married couple with dependent children
