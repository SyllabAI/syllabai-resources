## **Demographic Factors and their Effects on Family Life**

### Family Size and Birth Rates

* In the past, children were an **economic asset**

  * Parents supported children only briefly; by **age 6–7,** they worked and contributed

  * Children provided **labour when young** and **care in old age**

  * Larger families increase **income and long-term security**

  * This pattern still exists in some **less developed countries**

* In modern industrial societies

  * The birth rate has fallen dramatically

  * Mothers are having fewer children - falling fertility rate

There are multiple reasons for this:

* It is now expensive to raise children, since they can’t do paid work anymore, and instead depend on their parents for education

* There is a lower infant mortality rate, so parents have fewer children because they know they will survive

* Children are no longer essential for caring for older people

  * Pensions, investments, and health/social services allow older people to support themselves

* People can now control the number of children they have because contraception, especially the pill, is widely available

  * Greater use in the West due to secularisation and the decline of religious opposition

* A smaller family is easier to relocate, such as for a job

* Opportunities for women in paid work are restricted if they have many children

Some couples choose not to have children

* Focus on careers and shared leisure reduces time for children

* Less pressure from family to have children

* Couples who both work and have no children are called DINK families (‘double income, no kids’)

  * Often financially better off than couples with children

### Life Expectancy

As the birth rate has fallen, so has the death rate

* Life expectancy has increased worldwide over the last 200 years

  * Infant mortality (under 1 year) and child mortality (1–5 years) have fallen significantly

  * The average global life expectancy is now 73

Some reasons for the decline in global death rates include:

* improved access to running water

* advances in sewage disposal and sanitation, which prevent and treat diseases

* higher living standards, especially better diets

* fewer deaths related to work due to safer, healthier conditions

* better education and healthcare

* vaccination of children against diseases

Developing countries usually have higher death, birth, and fertility rates than developed countries

* Rates fall during industrialisation: death rate drops first → population grows → birth rate falls later

* High birth rates are linked to low maternal education, limited access to contraception, and high infant/child mortality

* As education improves and more children survive, birth rates decline

### Ageing Population

Ageing populations are increasing in some countries

* Life expectancy rises, and fewer children are born

* Older people form a higher proportion in developed countries; younger populations in developing countries

* Retirement age rising; state benefits support retired people

* Retirement can reduce status, especially for men; women’s status is increasingly linked to occupation

Many older people still rely on their children, especially daughters or daughters-in-law

* Historically, one reason for having children was to ensure care in old age

* The pivot generation (adults with young children) often spends significant time and resources caring for ageing parents

**++Consequences for Families with an Ageing Population:++**

* More older people live alone after a partner dies → rise in single-person households

* Grandparents play a greater role in supporting their children and grandchildren

* Adult children face a greater burden caring for ageing parents, sometimes even past retirement age

* Older people experience greater isolation and loneliness

* Some families return to extended family living → three generations in one household

### Having Children in Later Life

Women are having their first child later in life

* Pregnancies among women over 40 have doubled since the 1990s (UK)

* Health risks for mother and baby exist, but are reduced by medical advances

* New technologies allow childbearing at older ages

* Main reasons: pursuing education/career and greater financial security

* Downsides: older parents may have less energy and less support from their own ageing parents
