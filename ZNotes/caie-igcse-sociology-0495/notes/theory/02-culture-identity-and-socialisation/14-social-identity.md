## Age and Identity

### Age Groups and Roles

* Societies divide people into age groups differently

* Roles linked to age are socially constructed, not always based on biology

* Modern Western/industrial societies: long childhood, focus on socialisation before adulthood

* Older people usually retire at a legal age

* Traditional societies: children work with adults early, no formal retirement, work until physically able

**++Stages of Life in Traditional Societies++**

* Men’s lives are divided into: children, warriors, and elders

* Boys of similar age are grouped together

* Initiation into adulthood occurs at the same time

* Elders are seen as wise and knowledgeable. They are valued members and are often tribe leaders or act as advisors

**++Industrial Societies++**

* Longer childhood due to extended education and training before employment

* Childhood and adolescence focused on preparing for economic roles

* The retirement age is set legally

* Age roles are more formalised (school, work, retirement) than in traditional societies

* Social mobility valued: achieved status (education, career) is more important than inherited status

**++Generations and Age Cohorts++**

* **Generation**: members of the same age group, \~25 years apart

* **Generation gap**: differences in experiences and attitudes (e.g., teens see older people as out of touch, older people see teens as rebellious)

* **Digital natives**: young people who grew up with tech.

  * **Digital immigrants:** older people who may struggle with tech

* **Age cohort:** members born at the same historical period, shared experiences affect attitudes/opportunities

* Example: baby boomers in Europe/North America: 1960s social changes (wealth increase, Cold War, birth control, new tech, environmental concerns)

  * Large cohorts → targeted products (music, fashion)

  * Ageing cohorts → higher health/social service costs

**++Adolescence and Transition to Adulthood++**

* Adulthood used to be a single event (initiation ceremony).

  * Today, adolescence is a longer stage with peer pressure and status anxiety (e.g. teens striving for social approval).

* Modern societies emphasise achieved status over ascribed status

* **Peer groups:** support network, shared experiences, help manage pressures

**++Adolescence, Identity, and Peer Groups++**

* In past societies, adulthood was marked by a single event

* Example: initiation ceremonies

* In modern societies, transition takes many years

* Adolescence is the period between childhood and adulthood

  * Often linked to status anxiety

* Young people are pressured to achieve status

* Peer groups provide support and shared identity, leading to the formation of youth subcultures
