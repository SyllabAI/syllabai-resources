## Methods of Control Used by Formal Agencies in Achieving Social Conformity

### Law-Making

* Laws are rules made by the government to control behaviour and protect people’s rights.

* Laws differ between countries and in how they are enforced

* Governments can respond to social change by creating new laws

* Example: the rise of the internet has led to new laws on online activities

### Coercion

* **Coercion**: forcing someone to do something against their will, often through threats or force

* Can happen between individuals, e.g., blackmail for money

* Usually breaks laws or rules, but formal agencies can use it legally

* Examples:

  * Police using handcuffs, Tasers, or detaining suspects

  * Armed forces are using tear gas or water cannons against protesters

### Digital Surveillance

* Monitoring or recording people’s activities using digital technology

* Not new, but tech advances in the late 20th century increased and formalised it

* Examples in daily life:

  * CCTV cameras in cities

  * Phone and internet usage tracking

* Purpose/Impact:

  * Helps police respond quickly to crimes

  * Identify suspects and track movements

  * Governments may monitor online activity that threatens the law or the state

### Arrest

* Detaining someone suspected of committing an offence

* Usually, only the police can do this and must have valid reasons

* There are limits on how long a person can be held before being charged and going to court

### Sentencing and Imprisonment

* Court sentences vary by country, crime severity, and the offender’s record

* Sentences range from a discharge (offender released) for minor offences to imprisonment for serious crimes

* Some countries still use capital punishment (death penalty) for the most serious offences

### Religion

* Religious laws can act as a strong deterrent to committing ‘wrong’ behaviour

  * e.g., Sharia law in Muslim countries
