## The Debate between Consensus and Conflict Views of Social Control

### Consensus View: Functionalism

* Social control works best when people are persuaded that conforming is the right thing to do

* If persuasion fails, sanctions can act as an alternative route

* Consensus views, like functionalism, see informal social control as positive and vital for society’s stability

* Functionalists such as Emile Durkheim argue that societies need shared values to hold people together and prevent anomie

* **Anomie** is chaos that happens when there are no norms or values, leading to the breakdown of bonds between individuals and society

* Durkheim said societies need a collective conscience shared by nearly all people

### Conflict View: Marxism

* Marxism sees social control as a way for the ruling class to keep power over the working class

* Louis Althusser explained two ways this happens:

  * **Ideological State Apparatus (ISA):** schools, media, religion; convince people to accept norms that benefit the ruling class

  * **Repressive State Apparatus (RSA):** police, courts, armed forces; step in if ISA doesn’t work

* ISAs teach the working class that inequality is fair, e.g., children learn to respect and obey those born into high status

* If persuasion fails, the RSA enforces rules, e.g., protests may be first discouraged through the media, then controlled by the police or the military

* Marxism sees social control as a way for the ruling class to keep power over the working class

### Conflict View: Feminism

* Feminism views social control as a way men keep power over women in patriarchal societies

* Families reinforce women’s roles as mothers and housewives, limiting their choices

* Education guides girls towards certain subjects and careers, keeping women in traditional roles

* This control makes it harder for women to gain independence and equality, benefiting men
