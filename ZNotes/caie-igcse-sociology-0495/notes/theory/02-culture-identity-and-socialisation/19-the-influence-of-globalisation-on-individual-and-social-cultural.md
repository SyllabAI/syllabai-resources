## Globalisation

* **Globalisation** is the process by which the world is becoming increasingly interconnected.

* People and cultures around the world have more contact with each other and are more aware of each other.

* Globalisation involves changes in politics, economics and culture. Examples include

  * food, such as different cuisines andcooking techniques from different cultures, which are available everywhere

  * Global mass media, such as movies, music, news and TV shows from around the world are available.

  * global brand names associated with culture, such as KFC, Pepsi and Disney.

* The world is now often perceived as a **global village** with a **global culture**

* **Cultural Globalisation as Westernisation:**

  * Some sociologists argue that cultural globalisation mainly involves the spread of Western, especially American, culture

  * This suggests culture flows in **one direction**

  * Globalisation is seen as something **done by powerful countries to weaker ones**, not an equal exchange

* When globalisation is viewed negatively, it is called **cultural imperialism**

  * Cultural imperialism means powerful countries dominate others through **culture rather than direct political control**

  * This is compared to colonialism, but instead of armies and governments, control happens through media, values, and lifestyles

* Some see Western cultural influence as **beneficial** because it may spread ideas such as:

  * Human rights

  * Freedom and democracy

  * Gender equality

* Supporters argue that these values improve life chances and reduce oppression

* Others argue Western culture is **imposed**, not chosen

  * Local cultures, languages and traditions may be weakened or lost

  * Globalisation can lead to the **exploitation** of local cultures for profit

  * This creates cultural dominance without direct political rule

* Cultural globalisation has increased debates around **cultural appropriation**, which occurs when elements of a minority Historically oppressed cultures are adopted by members of a more powerful or dominant culture. This is often criticised when cultural practices are used without understanding their original meaning or context.

  Sociologists link cultural appropriation to **power inequalities**, arguing that dominant Western groups can adopt

  * cultural elements freely, while minority groups may face discrimination for the same practices.

  * **Example:** Western fashion brands selling traditional Indigenous patterns or symbols (such as Native American or Aboriginal designs) purely for profit, without crediting the culture or sharing economic benefits.

  * These arguments are connected to **colonial history**, as cultural appropriation is seen as a continuation of past exploitation, where cultures were stripped of meaning and value.

* Although new media and technology have spread globally, sociologists argue that this does not lead to complete **cultural homogenisation**. Instead, cultures adapt global influences in different ways.

  * The use of technology is shaped by **local culture, economic conditions and social organisation**, meaning globalisation produces varied outcomes

  * **In** parts of Africa, mobile phones are often shared and used by farmers to check market prices or spread information about weddings and community events.
