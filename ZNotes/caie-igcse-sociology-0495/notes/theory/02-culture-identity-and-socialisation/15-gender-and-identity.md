## Gender and Identity

### Gender Roles and Social Control

* In many past societies, gender roles were fixed and clearly defined

* Men and women were expected to follow strict norms and expectations

* Little opportunity to negotiate or challenge gender roles

* Gender roles shaped how people saw themselves, known as gender identity

* These expectations acted as a strong form of social control

### Functionalist View of Gender (Talcott Parsons)

* Parsons links gender roles to biology

  * Women give birth and breastfeed, leading to a closer bond with children

* In modern industrial societies, fathers are often absent due to paid work

* **++Women++** **->** *expressive* role: emotional care, nurturing children

* **++Men++** **->** *instrumental* role: paid work, financial support

* Functionalists argue that these roles are complementary and necessary

* Gender identity is seen as natural, universal, and biologically based

### Feminist Criticism of Functionalism (Ann Oakley)

* Oakley argues that gender roles are socially constructed, not biological

* Parsons’ ideas reflect American culture

* Uses cross-cultural evidence to challenge functionalism, such as the Alor in Indonesia

* Women are not closely tied to their children, but they still develop normally

  * Shows expressive role is not essential for family functioning

* Oakley argues these roles benefit men and reinforce patriarchy

### Cross-Cultural Evidence (Margaret Mead)

* Mead studied traditional societies in New Guinea

* Arapesh society, where men and women were peaceful and cooperative

* Chambri society, where women worked and made decisions, while men focused on appearance and domestic life

* Shows that gender roles vary between cultures and supports nurture over nature

* Gender identity is learned through socialisation

### Sex and Gender in Modern Industrial Societies

* **Sex** refers to biological differences, such as chromosomes

* **Gender** refers to social roles, expectations, and behaviour

  * Sex and gender are closely connected, but not the same

* Traditionally, societies assumed two sexes and two genders

* Male is linked to masculinity, female is linked to femininity

### Gender Diversity and Spectrum

* Not everyone identifies with the sex assigned at birth

  * Includes transgender, non-binary, and gender non-conforming people

* Some sociologists view gender as a spectrum

* People may identify at different points between male and female

* Hijra in South Asia is recognised as a third gender

* Two-Spirit people in Indigenous American cultures

* Shows that gender is culturally shaped

### Gender Roles in Modern Industrial Societies

* Gender roles have become more flexible over time

  * More women are entering paid employment and senior positions

  * Women increasingly expect financial independence

* More men take part in childcare and housework, an increase in stay-at-home fathers

  * Men form closer emotional bonds with children

* However, inequality and patriarchy still exist

### Masculinity, Femininity, and Hegemony

* Traditional masculinity is linked to strength and dominance, which is known as hegemonic masculinity

  * Men are expected to be breadwinners and emotionally strong

    * Failure to meet expectations is seen as weak or unmanly

* Femininity is linked to emotional sensitivity and dependence

* Sociologists now talk about multiple masculinities and femininities
