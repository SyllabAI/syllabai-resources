## Globalisation’s influence on individual and social/cultural identities

### Cultural Defence

* Some societies actively **resist globalisation** to protect their **national identity, values, and traditions**. This resistance is often a reaction against **Westernisation** and **cultural imperialism**.

  * **France** protects its national culture by limiting the availability of English-language music and television. This reflects **cultural nationalism**, where the state intervenes to preserve language and identity.

  * **Japan** promotes traditional customs such as kimono culture, tea ceremonies, and Japanese-language media through education and media policy. This reflects **selective globalisation**, where global influences are accepted but reshaped to fit local culture.

### Hybrid Identities

**++Globalisation and Cultural Flow++**

* Globalisation spreads ideas, trends, and lifestyles across the world, often seen as Western.

* This can put pressure on local traditions, making people feel their culture is being replaced.

* Western countries also adopt practices, foods, and philosophies from other regions.

**++Hybrid Cultures++**

* Globalisation blends cultural elements from multiple traditions.

* Music, fashion, and everyday habits can mix influences from different countries.

* These hybrid cultures are dynamic, rather than simply replacing old traditions.

**++Hybrid Identities++**

* People can combine aspects of different cultures into their personal identity.

* Migrants or people in multicultural areas may identify with more than one culture at once.

* This flexibility allows for unique lifestyles and values shaped by multiple cultural influences.

**++Cultural Change and Preservation++**

* Local cultures don’t vanish under globalisation

* Traditional practices may take on different meanings, like cultural festivals or rituals being shared with outsiders.

* Globalisation can enrich societies by keeping cultural exchange alive while maintaining roots.

### **++Culture, Tourism, and New Value++**

* Some cultural practices gain economic and social value through tourism.

  * For instance, a traditional dance might no longer be part of its original ceremony but is performed for visitors, creating income for the performers.

### Homogenization of Identities

**++Homogenisation vs Cultural Diversity++**

* Some sociologists argue that globalisation could create a single, worldwide culture.

* People everywhere may end up speaking the same language, eating similar cuisines, dressing similarly; this is called **homogenisation**.

* Homogenisation is the opposite of **cultural diversity**, where multiple distinct cultures coexist.

**++Americanisation++**

* In reality, the dominant influence in global culture is often American, a process called **Americanisation**.

* This spreads a consumer-driven lifestyle, encouraging people to buy goods they may not need or afford.

* Example: traditional diets are replaced by fast food or sodas linked to the American lifestyle.

**++Social and Environmental Impacts++**

* Americanisation can harm health, encourage materialism, and increase environmental damage due to heavy resource use.

* It may also lead to the **loss of local languages, traditions, and cultural practices**, reducing cultural diversity.
