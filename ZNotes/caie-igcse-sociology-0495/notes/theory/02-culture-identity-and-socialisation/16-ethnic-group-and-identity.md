## Ethnic Group and Identity

### Ethnicity as a Social Construct

* Ethnicity is socially constructed, meaning groups exist because people identify themselves and others as belonging to them

* Ethnic identity is based on shared culture, not biology

* Sociologists now prefer the term ethnicity over race, because race focuses mainly on physical differences

### Ethnic Diversity and Migration

* Modern industrial societies are more ethnically diverse due to migration

* Migration brings people with different languages, religions and traditions into the same society

* Some countries have many ethnic groups of similar size, while others have one majority group

### Majority and Minority Ethnic Groups

* A majority ethnic group is the largest or most powerful group in a society

* Minority ethnic groups are smaller and often have less power

* The majority groups dominate politics, business and institutions

* Minority groups may face discrimination and prejudice

* In societies with many similarly sized groups, power is more evenly shared

* In rare cases, a minority group controls power, such as white rule during apartheid in South Africa

### Ethnicity vs Race

* In the past, racism focused on physical traits like skin colour

* Today, cultural differences are more important in creating divisions, and this shift is why sociologists focus on ethnicity

### Cultural Features of Ethnicity

* Ethnic groups may differ in language or dialect and have different customs and traditions

* Religion is often an important symbol of ethnicity

* Shared ancestry and history strengthen group identity

* Food and dress are visible expressions of ethnic culture

### Hybrid Cultures and Mixed Identities

* When ethnic groups live together over time, cultures can mix

  * This leads to hybrid cultures, which mix elements from different traditions

* People from mixed ethnic backgrounds may identify with more than one culture and may choose how they express their identity

### Ethnic Identity, Pride and Discrimination

* Ethnic identity can be a strong source of pride and belonging

* Minority groups may experience inequality and exclusion

* Discrimination can damage self-esteem and identity

* New migrants often face suspicion or hostility from the majority

### Globalisation and Ethnic Identity

* Globalisation allows migrants to stay connected with their ethnic group

* Social media helps people maintain language, traditions and family ties,  strengthening ethnic identity even when living abroad

* Hybrid identities emerge when people blend cultures, e.g., British Pakistanis mixing British and Pakistani culture

### National Identity and Ethnicity

* National identity is closely linked to ethnicity

* Many believed globalisation would weaken national identity, but it has become stronger in recent years

* Nationalism has played a role in wars, political change and independence movements

### Nations as Social Constructions

* Benedict Anderson describes nations as “imagined communities”

* Members of a nation do not personally know each other, but feel connected

* National identity is especially important in diverse societies

  * It helps reduce loyalty to smaller ethnic or cultural groups

### Problems with National Identity

* Conflict can occur when groups reject national identity and want independence

* Artificial borders, especially in Africa, have divided ethnic groups

  * These divisions were created during colonial rule and still cause tension

### How States Promote National Identity

* Governments encourage national identity through symbols like flags and money

* A head of state acts as a national figurehead

* National anthems and ceremonies create shared meaning

* National holidays and rituals build unity

* International sports teams promote national pride

* State-controlled or national media spread national culture
