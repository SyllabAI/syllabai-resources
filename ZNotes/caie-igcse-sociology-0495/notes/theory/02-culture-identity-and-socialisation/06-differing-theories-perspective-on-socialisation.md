## Differing Theories’ Perspective on Socialisation

Each theory has its own views on the process and agencies of socialisation.

### Functionalism

### Inadequate socialisation

* Inadequate socialization in sociology refers to the failure of primary agents, particularly parents, to properly instill society’s core norms, values, and behavioral expectations in children

* Functionalists see socialisation as necessary for society to function properly

* Feral children show that, due to inadequate socialisation, normal human behaviour does not develop

* The family is the main agent of socialisation in functionalism because they teach basic norms and values needed to function in society

* Families that do not value education or rules may fail to socialise children properly, which can lead to poor academics and higher crime

### Social cohesion and value consensus

* Socialisation teaches shared norms and values across society that create social cohesion and a sense of belonging

* Value consensus allows people to agree on acceptable behaviour

* People can predict how others will act because values are widely shared

* In unfamiliar situations, behaviour is guided by underlying values

* Social cohesion encourages acceptance of rules and authority

* People accept failure or hardship as part of how society functions

* This shared belief helps maintain social stability and order

### Marxism

### Socialisation into Capitalist Ideology

* Marxists believe that socialisation has a function.

* However, they believe it favours the bourgeoisie only, based on capitalist norms and values.

### Reproduction of Social Class

* Marxists argue that socialisation helps capitalism survive by passing on capitalist norms and values, benefiting only the bourgeoisie rather than society as a whole

* Socialisation reproduces social class across generations

* Working-class children are taught that inequality is fair and deserved

* They learn to blame themselves for low status

* Bourgeois children are socialised to see themselves as superior

### Feminism

### Gender Role Socialisation into Masculinity/Femininity

* Feminists argue that socialisation maintains unequal power and status between the 2 sexes

* Gender roles are learned from an early age, not biologically fixed

* Children are socialised into expectations of masculinity and femininity

* These expectations shape behaviour, opportunities, and life chances

* Ann Oakley argued that gender roles are learned through 4 specific socialisation processes:

  * **Manipulation:** Parents may praise and encourage some activities while discouraging others. For example, a girl may be discouraged for liking sports and cars, while a boy is praised for it.

  * **Canalisation:** Parents **channel** their children into doing things they want. A boy may be encouraged to play football, while a girl may be brought dolls and make-up.

  * **Verbal appellations:**  Ways in which parents address their children. A girl may be called pretty, while a boy may be called handsome

  * **Different activities:** Boys and girls will be encouraged to take part in different activities. Girls may help their mother in the kitchen or style hair, and boys may be asked to do heavy tasks and help with DIY projects around the home

* Since the 1970s, gender socialisation has changed, with some parents trying to avoid stereotypes

* Avoiding gender roles is difficult because other agents of socialisation give conflicting messages

  * **Media** often reinforces gender stereotypes by focusing on male achievement and female appearance

  * **Peer groups** sanction children who do not meet gender expectations, e.g., boys who cry or girls who fight

  * **Education** may reinforce gender roles through subject choices and teacher expectations

* Feminists argue that gender socialisation maintains inequality in society

* Until gender role socialisation changes, inequality will continue to benefit men
