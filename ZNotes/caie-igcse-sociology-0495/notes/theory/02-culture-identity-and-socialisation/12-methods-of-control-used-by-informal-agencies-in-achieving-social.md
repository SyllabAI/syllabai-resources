## Methods of Control Used by Informal Agencies in Achieving Social Conformity

### Ostracism

* Ostracism is a form of informal social control where offenders are rejected or isolated by the community when they do  something seen as unacceptable

* Communities respond without using formal agencies, e.g., ignoring the offender or denying access to resources

* The aim is to restore stability in the community rather than punish the individual

* Ostracism was more common in the past, but it is still common in peer groups, etc

### Parental Rewards

* In families, parents use rewards and punishments to control behaviour

* Rewards include treats, privileges, and praise

* Praise encourages good behaviour and is more effective than punishment

### Social Media Likes

* ‘Likes’ on social media are a form of informal social control

* ‘Likes’ boost self-esteem, especially for teenagers

* They signal popularity and social approval

* People change their behaviour or opinions to gain more likes

* A few ‘dislikes’ can cause feelings of rejection, acting as a modern form of ostracism

### Religious Rewards/Sanctions

* Religious beliefs act as informal social control via fear of punishment after death

* Belief in heaven rewards good behaviour, and hell punishes bad behaviour

* These beliefs encourage people to follow laws and moral rules

* Belief in an all-seeing god removes the idea of getting away with crime

### School Punishments

* Schools display rules and have a clear system of rewards and sanctions

* Modern schools prioritise rewards because positive sanctions are more effective than punishment

  * Informal rewards include praise, positive comments, merit badges, and points

  * Formal rewards include certificates and exam success

* Informal negative sanctions include disapproval, verbal comments, isolation, and removal from class

* Formal punishments include detentions, contacting parents, confiscating property, and exclusion

* In the past and even in some countries today, corporal punishment is used

### Dismissal From a Job

* Workplaces have rules about punctuality, dress and conduct

  * Dismissal is the strongest negative sanction

* Laws exist to prevent unfair dismissal and may require a disciplinary hearing

* Other negative sanctions include loss of pay, closer monitoring, demotion,

* Rewards include promotion, pay rises, bonuses, commissions, and performance pay
