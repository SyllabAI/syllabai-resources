## Social Class and Identity

### Social Class

* Social class is a social construction; it’s not obvious from biology, unlike age, gender, or ethnicity.

* Being in a particular class affects your **life chances**, which means the opportunities you get in education, work, housing, and health.

* Sociologists don’t always agree on who belongs to which class, and people themselves might view their class differently from how others see it.

### Upper Class

* This is the small elite group that often has inherited wealth, exclusive education, and powerful positions.

* They show their status through **conspicuous consumption**, which is buying expensive things like houses and cars to show off.

* They also have **cultural capital**, meaning they know the tastes, manners, and ways of behaving that mark them as elite.

* Being in the upper class also involves power, influence, and being part of an exclusive social club.

### Middle Class

* The middle class is bigger and more mixed. It can be split into:

  * **Professional middle class**: doctors, lawyers, teachers, who are high-skill jobs

  * **Business/managerial middle class**: business owners or managers

* They also show their class through lifestyle, possessions, education, and the way they spend leisure time.

* People who have moved up from a lower class, known as **upward social mobility**, often try harder to show they’ve made it by their house, car,

* Over the last 100 years, the middle class has grown because living standards improved, and now it’s a big part of society.

### Working Class

* Includes manual workers, factory staff, construction workers, and some office workers.

* Historically, they had a **strong collective identity** because they worked in the same industries, lived in the same communities, and joined the same trade unions.

* There was a sense of pride in being working class

* Jobs often passed from fathers to sons, and people married within the same community.

* Over time, these communities have weakened because traditional industries closed, jobs disappeared, and the media often portrays the working class negatively.

### Social Mobility

* Moving up in class is **upward mobility**; moving down is **downward mobility**.

* Mobility affects identity because people may change the way they live or are seen socially.

* Showing off status symbols like cars, holidays, or houses is often part of proving new status after moving up.

### Identity and Culture

* Class affects your identity and culture, what you like, how you behave, and what you value.

* **Cultural capital** (Bourdieu) is how people use tastes, knowledge, and manners to signal their class.

  * Upper and middle classes use cultural capital to maintain status.

  * Working-class culture often focused on solidarity, pride, and resilience.
