# Culture, Identity, and Socialisation

## Society as a Product of Social Construction

**Social constructionism** in sociology is a theory proposing that reality, knowledge, and meaning are created through human interaction and social consensus rather than being inherent or objective.

### Culture

* Culture is a society’s whole way of life.

* It influences our lives completely

* It includes:

  * What and how we eat, with whom and when

  * How we dress

  * Language

  * How do we have fun or take a break

  * Where and how do we live

  * Our religious beliefs and superstitions

  * Our celebrations and festivals

* Society is made up of institutions, while culture dictates how these institutions should work

  * Social institutions are organized systems of social structures, norms, and behaviors designed to meet fundamental human needs and maintain social order

  * The five core, universally recognised institutions are **family, education, religion, government,** and the **economy**

* Each person has their own sense of identity; identity is a sense of who we are as individuals

* Identity is how we see ourselves, and how we think we are similar or different from others

* **++Norms++**

  * **Norms** are the set of behaviours society expects of us in different situations

  * There are many norms, and some are more observed than others, and every culture has its own norms

  * For example, you may bow as a greeting in Japan, but you kiss cheeks in France

  * Widely observed norms may become customs if they are accepted over time

  * Norms enforced by legal means become laws

* **++Values++**

  * **Values** are ideals of what is considered wrong or right

  * Many people have their own values, but values are shared across a society

* Values provide a moral framework for topics such as privacy, personal space and honour

  * Norms tell us how to act in a way that respects this framework

**++Norms and Values across Society++**

* Values and norms vary across society; this means they are socially constructed

  * This means they are created by society because we need to learn them and pass them down

  * In some places, burial is the only way, but in other places, people may prefer cremation

* Norms and values may be completely different across cultures; what one culture may find disgusting, another may find respectful

* They change over time, and vary greatly over different historical periods, another example of how they are relative and constructed

* Society is predictable due to them, but some people don’t conform, and society responds to them to try to make them conform.

* Symbols and language are another aspect of society, as they are sounds and shapes that carry meaning, such as a cross or a flower, and words like Salam or Guten Tag

### Status and Role

* **Status** is the position one has in a society

* One can have many statuses, for example,  you may be a friend, you may be a teenager, a student, a son, or an upperclassman

* Statuses can be ascribed or achieved

* **Ascribed status** is the one you are born with, such as gender, ethnicity, and family background

* **Achieved status** is the one you get by your actions, such as your age, your job, and your achievements

* **Role** is the set of expected behaviours or norms you have to follow with a particular status

  * You may follow these norms, or not

    * This impacts your status

### Stereotypes

* **Stereotypes** are an exaggerated set of ideas about a type of person or group

* People learn about the values and statuses of other people

* The media uses stereotypes to represent particular groups, so people may relate

* This leads stereotypes to be often misleading and false, and forms the basis for future social interactions

  * Example: Assuming an Indian person working as a tech customer support is a scammer who says, “Do not redeem it!”
