## Postmodernist Views of Identity as Chosen rather than Given

### From Modernity to Postmodernity

* The 20th century is described as the **modern period**, where society was organised around clear structures like class, family, work and nation.

* Earlier societies are described as **pre-modern**, with tradition and religion strongly shaping people’s lives

* Some sociologists argue that we have now entered a new stage called **postmodernity**, meaning that society has undergone significant changes.

* Postmodernists believe this is a break from how society used to function

### Key Features of Postmodern Society

* Postmodernists agree that **mass communication and digital technologies** are central to this new era

  * Media, the internet and social platforms shape how people see the world and themselves

* Information moves rapidly and globally, weakening the influence of tradition and authority

* There is no single dominant culture or set of values that everyone follows

### Identity in Modern Society

* In modern societies, identities are mostly **ascribed**, meaning they are given at birth or fixed by life stages

  * Gender, age and ethnicity strongly shaped who you were expected to be

  * Roles such as being a wife, husband, worker or parent came with clear expectations

* There was some choice, such as changing occupation, but overall identity was relatively stable

* People were expected to perform social roles in predictable ways

### Identity in Postmodern Society

* Postmodernists argue that identities today are more **fluid and flexible**

* Individuals have much greater **choice** in how they present and define themselves

* Identity is increasingly **constructed**, not given

* Digital technologies allow people to experiment with multiple identities, especially online

* This can be empowering, but it can also create uncertainty and confusion

### Consumption and Identity in Postmodern Society

* Postmodernists argue that identity today is shaped more by **consumption** than by production

* Modern societies were centred on **production** (what job you did and your role in the economy)

* Postmodern societies are **consumer societies**, where identity is built through what people buy, use and display

* Work is less central to how people define themselves

* People are more likely to describe themselves through lifestyle choices, brands and tastes

* Consumption becomes a way of signalling status, values and personality

### Identity Through Consumption

* People construct identities by choosing particular products, styles and experiences

* Owning certain brands, technologies, or fashions communicates who someone is (or wants to be)

* Consumption allows individuals to create **distinct lifestyles**, even if they have similar jobs

* Studying what people consume helps sociologists understand changing cultures and identities

### The Pick-and-Mix Society

* Postmodernists describe society as a **pick-and-mix** or supermarket-style society

* Individuals select different elements to build their identity, rather than inheriting a fixed one

* These choices can include:

  * hobbies and sports teams

  * fashion styles and diets

  * influencers, media preferences and beliefs

* Individuals decide how important each choice is

* Identities are no longer permanent; people can drop old choices and adopt new ones easily

### Lifestyles: Modern vs Postmodern

* In modern societies, lifestyles are strongly shaped by structural factors:

  * Gender influenced roles and expectations

  * Social class shaped education, work and lifestyle

  * Ethnicity influenced cultural norms and behaviour

  * Age defined what behaviour was seen as appropriate

* In postmodern societies, these links are weaker

* People appear to have greater freedom to cross boundaries and redefine themselves

### Increased Choice and Flexibility

* Postmodern society offers more opportunities to:

  * change careers

  * work remotely

  * relocate globally

  * Challenge traditional gender and age roles

* Young people can gain wealth or status outside traditional career paths

* Identity becomes something that is **actively chosen**, not passively received

### Criticisms of the Postmodern View

* Critics argue that postmodernists exaggerate how much choice people really have

* Access to consumer choice depends heavily on **wealth and income**

* Social class still limits lifestyle options for many people

* Digital access is unequal, creating exclusion from online identities

* Structural factors like class, gender, ethnicity and age still shape life chances

* Postmodernism is criticised for downplaying inequality and power differences
