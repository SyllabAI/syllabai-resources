## Resistance to Social Control through Protest Groups and Sub-cultures

* Subcultures are groups of people who act or believe differently from most of society, with their own style, values, and ways of behaving

* Deviant subcultures reject some of the main society rules but follow their own group’s rules

* Teenagers often form subcultures to show their identity while growing up

* These groups use informal control to make members follow their rules

* Even so, members usually follow key rules of society, like speaking the same language or respecting basic laws

### Protest Groups

* Protest groups are a type of subculture made up of people wanting social change

* They challenge existing norms and values in society on specific issues

* Common issues include voting rights, poverty, human and civil rights, and environmental issues

* Some protest groups operate across international borders

  * e.g., School Strike for Climate led by Greta Thunberg involved students in \~150 countries protesting climate change

* Such movements unite people with shared concerns, experiences, or identities

### Youth Subcultures

* Adolescence is a period of developing autonomy from parents

* Functionalists: subcultures give teenagers an outlet to express emotions, functional for individuals and society

* Peer groups may have norms/values slightly  different from wider society

* Youth can experiment with identities, styles, and even risky behaviour safely before adult responsibilities

* Youth sub-culture = group adopting a style/culture partly at odds with mainstream culture (e.g., gyaru, mods, hippies, punks, goths, emos, egirls)

**The media often portrays them as deviant or threatening**

* Subcultures provide alternative opportunity structures for status/respect when mainstream routes are blocked

* Neo-Marxists: youth subcultures reflect working-class resistance to social/economic constraints

* Most youth participate temporarily, leaving for adult roles, and readopting mainstream norms

* Some subcultures today are more about fashion than rebellion

* Historically, male-dominated female subcultures are less visible (McRobbie’s bedroom subcultures)

  * Today, youth subcultures are more gender-mixed due to changing gender roles and greater female freedom

### Digital Subcultures

* The internet and social media, like YouTube and Instagram, allow people who are far apart connect

* Online communities, forums and chat rooms help subcultures grow stronger

* People can join international communities with shared interests or values

  * Examples include fans of online games, bands, or influencers forming subcultures

* Online subcultures can link to protest groups through **clicktivism,** a.k.a. online activism

### Religious Subcultures

* Most religious beliefs follow the dominant culture, but some are subcultures

* Religious subcultures demand strong conformity to their own norms and values

  * Example: the **Amish**, who have distinctive rules about dress, technology, work and community life
