## Processes Used by Agencies of Socialisation

### Canalisation

* Children try to meet social expectations from an early age

* They learn by watching and imitating others

* Behaviour that gets rewarded is likely to be repeated

* Parents and others use reinforcement by applying rewards or sanctions

* **Canalisation** teaches gender roles by steering children toward certain activities, e.g., girls may be brought dolls while boys are brought cars

### Role Modelling

* Older children and adults can act as role models whom others try to copy

* Children imitate behaviour they see being praised or valued, such as politeness

* Role models can also come from the media, e.g., athletes, celebrities, or influencers

### Hidden Curriculum

* Schools teach more than subjects, including a hidden curriculum

* Children learn norms and values through school routines and rules

* They pick up lessons on respect, punctuality, conformity, and social status

### Media representations

* Plays a big role in socialisation, especially with TV, films, and social media

* Children may imitate behaviours seen in media, like violence, but they can also interpret messages critically with family and peer guidance

* Continues to socialise adults through news, showing acceptable behaviour and consequences for breaking rules

* Advertising teaches **consumer behaviou**r, encouraging the desire for unnecessary products

* Often presents **stereotypes** about groups like young people or immigrants, which can shape opinions

### Peer pressure

* Strongly influences socialisation, especially for adolescents who crave acceptance

* Teens may follow friends’ behaviour even if it conflicts with parents’ or school expectations

* Active peer pressure happens when peers demand behaviour to join/stay in a group, even bullying

* **Social conformity** is when individuals adjust their behaviour voluntarily to fit in and be accepted

* Peer pressure also occurs on social media, with likes, shares, and negative comments acting as positive or negative sanctions

### Religious Teachings

* Religious institutions and teachings are agents of socialisation

* Strong religious beliefs guide behaviour and shape values

* Major religions give clear instructions on how to behave, e.g., the Five Pillars of Islam, the Five Precepts of Buddhism

### Workplace Training

* Workplace training socialises employees into job expectations and workplace culture

* Training helps workers learn skills and communication skills

* Further training may be needed for promotions or a pay raise
