## The Social Construction of Our Identity

Social identity is the portion of an individual's self-concept derived from perceived membership in relevant social groups, such as nationality, race, religion, or occupation

* Our social identity is formed based on our interactions with other people

* We often see ourselves with a macro view, what role we have in wider society

* Stuart Hall argues that people are more unsure of their identities today, with more cultures intermingling.

### Nature or Nurture Debate:

* Sociologists argue whether our social traits and our idenity is formed through biological inheritance of genes or socialisation

* Biological determinists argue that we inherit traits from our families as genes, such as a tendency to be aggressive or friendly, greedy or caring.

  * This is the **Nature** view

* They say that our behaviour in social environments is governed by instincts and drives

* Sociological determinists (nurture) argue that genes may give a tendency to act, but we as conscious beings have total free will and can act as we choose, and that is how we were socialised, which determines this.

  * This is the **Nurture** view

* An argument they put is the example of feral children

  * Feral children are those who have had extremely little contact with humans or society since birth

  * They find adapting to human society difficult

  * Thus proving that nature cannot completely determine how we act

### The Relativity of Culture

Culture is relative - it varies greatly over places and times, and there is no universal culture.

* It is relevant in areas such as food, clothing, language, festivals, and dress.

  * Just consider our different cuisines, from Middle Eastern Shawarma, to Italian Pizza, Pakistani Biryani, Malay Nasi Lemak, American Hamburger and Japanese Karaage

* In the modern world, we have greater access and appreciation of other cultures.

* Due to advances in communication and travel, we are undergoing globalisation.

* Globalisation is the process by which cultures around the world are more aware of, interact with and influence each other.

* The main flow occurs from West to East

### Socialisation, Norms and Values

* Socialisation is the process through which a person learns the norms and values of a culture

* This is done by agencies of socialisation, such as the family, media, workplace, and religion.

  * Primary Socialization occurs from birth to late infancy, where the child learns the basics of interacting with other people and develops their personality, at the most impressionable phase, such as Family

  * Secondary Socialisation happens later in life when a person has to learn a new role and its norms, such as marriage or the workplace.

* When the norms or values of two agencies conflict, we can choose which ideas to follow or reject and hence play a part in our own socialisation.
