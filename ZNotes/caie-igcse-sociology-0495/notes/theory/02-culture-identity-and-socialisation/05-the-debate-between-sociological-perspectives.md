## The Debate Between Sociological Perspectives

### Consensus vs Conflict Views

Consensus theory, rooted in functionalism, posits that society thrives on shared values, harmony, and cooperation. 

Conflict theory, originating from Marxism, views society as a struggle for dominance between groups competing for limited resources, power, and inequality.

* **Social consensus** happens when most people share values and agree on wealth, status, and power, leading to a stable and harmonious society

* Shared values can come from religion, beliefs, or politics and are strengthened by shared practices

* **Social conflict** occurs when groups disagree on important issues like wealth, status, and power, creating instability

* Powerful groups may manipulate society, so others accept inequalities and do not challenge the system

* Some perspectives, like interpretivism, focus on small-scale (micro) interactions rather than overall views

* Interpretivists are sometimes criticised for ignoring wider issues like inequality and power

### Functionalism

* Functionalism is a theory based on consensus, seeing society as stable

* It asks what function each part of society performs to maintain stability:

  * Families socialise children into norms and values

  * Prisons remove lawbreakers temporarily to maintain social order

* Society is like a human body, where each organ has a role to keep the whole body

* Problems in one part of society can affect other parts

* Functionalism dominated early and mid-20th century sociology

* Talcott Parsons is the main sociologist associated with functionalism

### Marxism

* A conflict perspective that sees society divided by class struggle

* **Capitalism** is based on private ownership and profit, which creates **inequality**

  * The **bourgeoisie** owns wealth and property and controls power in society

  * The **proletariat** must sell their labour to survive and is not paid the full value of their work

* Social institutions benefit only the ruling class

  * Schools make working-class failure seem normal and deserved

  * The media distracts people from inequality by focusing on entertainment and unimportant problems

* Accept that institutions have functions

* Inspired by socialism and communist movements

* It supports radical change to end exploitation

* Karl Marx is the key thinker linked to this theory

### Feminism

* A conflict perspective focusing on **inequality between men and women**

* Society is seen as **patriarchal**, meaning men hold more power

* Patriarchy is a systemic social structure where men hold primary power, dominating in roles of authority, political leadership, economic privilege, and control over women’s lives.

* Feminists study gender differences, e.g., why women earn less despite having similar skills

* Both a sociological theory and a political movement

* Feminists argue that equality has improved, but is still incomplete

* Aims to benefit everyone, not just women

* **Liberal feminism** supports gradual change through laws and reforms

  * Liberal Feminists acknowledge the advances the movement has made and belive that laws and reform are the key to obtain equality

* **Radical feminism** argues that patriarchy is deeply rooted and still dominant

* **Marxist feminism** links gender inequality with class inequality
