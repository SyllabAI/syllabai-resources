## The Digital Self and Online Identities

### Digital technologies and everyday life

* Digital technologies (internet, smartphones) are now part of **everyday life** in modern industrial societies

  * They affect **culture, social relationships and identity**

* Many people now have a **digital self** alongside their real identity

* Use of digital technology involves **choice** - people decide how and whether to use it

* Every day life is **faster**

  * Smartphones allow instant access to information and communication

  * People are reachable at any time

* Communication is now **global**

  * Families can stay in daily contact across countries

  * Video calls allow people to take part remotely in events like birthdays and weddings

### Online identities and the digital self

* Online identities are **socially constructed**

* People choose:

  * What information to share

  * What to hide

  * whether to be honest or anonymous

* Individuals can:

  * present different aspects of themselves

  * create **multiple online identities** across platforms

* Differences in **gender, age and ethnicity** may matter less online

  * e.g., a 45-year-old Australian man can present himself as a 14-year-old Korean girl on social media

### Digital status and online behaviour

* Digital spaces have their own **status systems**

* Status may be based on:

  * owning the latest technology

  * online popularity (likes, follows, views)

* This creates new forms of **status competition and pressure**, especially for young people

### Virtual communities and global connections

* Online-only groups are called **virtual communities**

* People can find:

  * new friends

  * shared interests

  * romantic partners (e.g. online dating)

* These communities can become an important part of a person’s **identity**

* People may feel a sense of belonging without meeting face-to-face

### **Digital technology, migration and ethnicity**

* Digital communication helps migrants maintain:

  * family roles

  * ethnic identity

  * cultural practices

* Migrants can:

  * speak their language

  * follow traditions and celebrations

  * access ethnic media from their home country

* This supports **diaspora identities**

* Example: global Chinese communities connected through online media

### Support, protest and collective identity online

* Digital communities help people facing:

  * discrimination

  * prejudice

  * isolation

* People can:

  * share experiences

  * offer emotional support

  * organise protests and campaigns

* Online spaces strengthen **collective identity** and social movements

### Digital divides and inequality

* Not everyone has equal access to digital technology

* This is called the **digital divide**

* Divides may be based on:

  * income

  * age

  * gender

  * geography (lack of internet access)

* Those excluded miss out on:

  * social opportunities

  * education

  * employment chances

### Disinformation, algorithms and echo chambers

* The internet contains large amounts of **misinformation**

* Examples include conspiracy theories

* Algorithms shape **online content**

  * track user behaviour

  * promote similar content

* This creates **echo chambers**

  * People see the same ideas repeatedly

  * Alternative views are excluded

  * Beliefs can become more extreme

### Negative impacts on identity

* Online communication can negatively affect identity through:

  * body shaming

  * unrealistic beauty standards

  * cyberbullying

* These can damage:

  * self-esteem

  * mental health

  * sense of identity

* Spending more time online may reduce identities based on:

  * neighbourhood

  * local community

### Reality of digital communities

* Some sociologists argue that digital communities are weaker because:

  * People can leave easily

  * conflict is avoided rather than resolved

  * There is little long-term commitment

* Unlike real communities:

  * They may lack diversity

  * Individuals are less responsible for each other

### Children, vulnerability and surveillance

* Children are more vulnerable online because:

  * They may not understand manipulation

  * They struggle to judge information critically

* This is why some platforms have **age restrictions**

* Online activity is monitored through **digital surveillance**

  * by governments

  * by platform owners

* Surveillance can:

  * prevent crime

  * but also threaten privacy

  * be used against political dissidents
