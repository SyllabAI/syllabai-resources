## Sampling Frames and Techniques

* It is not feasible to study the whole target population, so a portion of it, called the sample, is selected.

  * **++Sampling Frame++**: A list of all or the majority of people in the target population

* Common sampling frames include electoral registers (a list of all adults eligible to vote) and school registers

* For some target populations, finding a sample frame is difficult or even impossible

* Samples are chosen to be representative, meaning they are a downsized version of the original target population.

### Methods of Choosing a Sample:

1. **Random Sampling**

   * Completely random - all names have an equal chance of selection

   * e.g. Lucky draw

   * Can be unrepresentative, as it might produce an uneven sample

   * The researcher has no control over the selection - no selection bias

2. **Systematic Sampling**

   * Regular pattern to choose

   * e.g., Every tenth name on a list

   * No selection bias

   * Potential for unrepresentativeness

3. **Stratified Sampling**

   * Sampling frame divided into ‘strata’, or sections

   * These sections are based on gender, ethnicity, and age

   * Then, samples are chosen from each stratum

   * This makes it representative as there will be correct proportions to the target population

   * Minimal selection bias

4. ### Snowball Sampling

   * Used when the target population is very specific, and there is no sampling frame.

   * You have to get one respondent who puts you in touch with other respondents, who in turn do the same, and so on.

   * e.g. Asking criminals to do so

   * More difficult to access groups can be reached

   * Less representative

5. ### Quota Sampling

   * When a researcher sets out to find people with specific characteristics

   * e.g Finding IGCSE students who use ZNotes.

   * Useful when there is no sampling frame

   * Fairly representative

   * Selection bias due to the personal choice of the researcher
