## Analysis and Evaluation of Research Choices

### Validity:

* The **extent to which the research findings accurately reflect reality**

* **Interpretivists** prioritise validity

* Comes from mainly **qualitative** methods, e.g., unstructured interviews.

* More **insight** can mean more **validity**

### Bias:

* Comes from **researchers’ values**

* **Positivists** argue that **bias must be completely avoided**

* **interpretivists** argue that **bias is unavoidable, as sociology is the study of people**

* Researchers should be **completely open** about any bias so readers can decide for themselves

* **Researcher imposition** is when the **researcher imposes their own values** on the research, such as gender, age, ethnicity, etc, because the researcher observes, **analyses or interprets data differently.**

* Some forms of bias include:

  * **++Interviewer Effect:++**  when the interviewer’s **sex, age, ethnicity, reaction to responses, etc., affect the answers given by the respondent.** For example, he may want to look good in the interviewer’s eyes or not to offend him.

  * **++Hawthorne Effect:++** the **unintended effects on the behaviour of people being studied because they know they are being observed,** because they want to give the researcher a particular impression of themselves. For example, students may behave differently around a teacher

  * **++Interviewer Bias++**++:++ occurs when a researcher's presence, appearance, or behavior, such as tone, body language, or leading questions, unintentionally influences a participant's responses

### Reliability:

* Reliability is the **extent to which the research findings can be reproduced when it is repeated under similar conditions.**

* E.g., science experiments, when repeated, will yield similar results. This makes more **scientific research reliable**.

* Research that uses **standardised methods**, e.g., closed/coded questions, is **more reliable as it can be repeated.**

* Comes from quantitative methods linked with positivist quantitative methods

### Representativeness and Generalisability:

* **Target populatio**n: the **whole group being studied**, to whom the findings can be applied

* **Sample**: **part of the target population** being studied

* **Sampling methods**: techniques used to select a sample

* **++Representative++**: The sample **accurately reflect composition** of the target population

* **++Generalizable++**: The findings can be **applied to the wider target population**

  * e.g., large-scale census/survey

Sampling Error: An error that occurs when using samples to make inferences about the populations from which they are drawn
