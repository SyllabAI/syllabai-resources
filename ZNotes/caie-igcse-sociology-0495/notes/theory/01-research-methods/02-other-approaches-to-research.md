## Other Approaches to Research

### Triangulation

* **Triangulation** is the use of **more than one method in sociology**, with *both* *positivist and interpretivist aspects.*

* It is used to *support* **quantitative data** *with* **qualitative examples.**

* Makes research both **valid** *and* **reliable.**

* Makes findings *balanced*.

* Example: A study on student behavio**u**r combining **surveys** (quantitative), **in-depth interviews** (qualitative), and **classroom observations** (qualitative) to get a complete picture, as shown in studies of social media's impact on learning.

### Longitudinal Studies

* Research carried out over a **long period of time on the same group or individuals** *to study change or development*.

* For example, checking on the *same participants* once *every year* for a period of thirty years

  #|
  ||
  
  Advantages

  |
  
  Disadvantages

  ||
  ||
  
  Shows change and development over time

  |
  
  Very time-consuming and expensive, difficult to get funding for

  ||
  ||
  
  Helps reveal long-term patterns

  |
  
  Eventually, participants might drop out, leading to bias; this is known as sample attrition

  ||
  ||
  
  Makes it possible to identify cause and effect

  |
  
  Hard to replicate, less reliable

  ||
  ||
  
  Highly valid; if participants will commit, they will give in-depth insights

  |
  
  Participants may change their behaviour because they know they are being observed; this is called the Hawthorne Effect
