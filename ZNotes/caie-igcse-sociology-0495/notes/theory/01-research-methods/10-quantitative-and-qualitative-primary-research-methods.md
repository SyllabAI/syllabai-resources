## Quantitative and Qualitative Primary Research Methods

### Surveys

* Large-scale methods with many respondents

* Produce quantitative data so preferred by positivists

* Includes questionnaires and structured interviews

### Questionnaires

* To be completed independently, without the presence of the researcher

* Commonly distributed by post, online or handed out

* Needs a large sample for a sufficient **response rate** - i.e., the number of people who fill in the questionnaire

* To increase the response rate, researchers may

  * Explain research in detail

  * Collect the questionnaire himself or provide an envelope

  * Remind those who haven’t responded

  * Short and easy questionnaire

  * Clear instructions

  * Use personal names to give a personal feeling

  * Money incentives

* **Good Questionnaire Design:**

  * As short as possible

  * Easy to understand and fill out

  * Sufficient number of options for respondents to express their views

  * Avoid difficult terms or technical ones

  * Avoid suggesting that a certain answer may be right or wrong

  * Neutral but friendly language

  * Provide clear instructions

  * Ask about what the respondent may know

### Interviews

* Researchers ask questions face-to-face to respondents

* Can be quantitative or qualitative

* Can be face-to-face, online, or over the telephone

* 4 main types you will study: **structured, unstructured, semi-structured, and group**

**++Structured++**

* Like a questionnaire - but verbal

* Closed questions with a limited range of answers

* Standardised set of questions

* Researcher can explain research and clear misconceptions - higher response rate

* More time-consuming - the researcher may need assistants

**++Unstructured++**

* Researcher only briefly prompts the respondent

* Participant will explain the answer in detail and talk freely

* Used as a qualitative method to gain understanding - verstehen

* Usually over an hour and in a relaxed setting

**++Group++**

* Carried out in a group with a large number of participants

* Usually structured, but can be more flexible and unstructured

* Used to find out the behaviour in a group and how people react to the opinions of others

* Researcher has to moderate and decide the degree of freedom of speech

* **++Advantages++**

  * Valid - gives a realistic social setting

  * Some people may raise an idea that others may challenge

  * A group setting may stimulate ideas

  * Broader range of view -  diversity

  * Practical in terms of time and money

* **++Disadvantages++**

  * Difficult to record/transcribe

  * Some individuals may dominate others and inhibit participation

  * Irrelevant discussion is possible

  * Interviewer needs to be skilled at moderating

* **Extra Tips**

  * You may study guidelines for conducting interviews if you want to take a more practical approach

  * You may also study question techniques

  * These topics are often provided in books, but are not officially part of the IGCSE 0495 Syllabus from 2025

### Observations:

**++Participant Observation++**

* Involves a researcher joining a group and living as they do

* Objective is to understand them and gain insight from their perspective

  **++Three Main Stages++**

  1. Getting In

     * Researcher needs a group to accept them; this can be achieved in two ways

     * **Covert** is when the researcher hides their identity as a researcher

     * **Overt** is when the researcher explains the research beforehand

     * Researcher may need to share some characteristics with the group, such as gender, ethnicity, or religion

  2. Staying In

     * Researcher needs to gain the trust and cooperation of the group

     * He has to observe carefully

     * May encounter problems while note-taking, especially if covert

     * May have to participate in acts they may not want to do

  3. Getting Out

     * Needs to leave without harming personal relationships

     * Needs to stay objective and impartial to write an unbiased report

**++Non-Participant Observation++**

* When a researcher studies a group but does not take part in the group themselves

* May produce quantitative data if the number of events is counted

* Eliminates the risk of the Hawthorne effect, the researcher getting connected to the group

* Researcher may need to avoid the Hawthorne effect via one-way glass or by cameras

* Researcher has to rely on their own interpretation, so there is a chance they might not understand some behaviour

**++Covert Method of Observation++**

* The group is unaware that research is occurring

* They may think that the researcher is a new member of their group

* **++Advantages++**

  * Used when deviant, e.g. criminal activity is to be studied

  * Reduces the risk of the Hawthorne effect

  * Highly valid

* **++Disadvantages++**

  * Permission to enter the group is not required

  * Researcher may have to engage in unsavoury acts to maintain cover

  * Researcher has to spend time and energy maintaining cover

  * Difficult to ask questions or take notes

  * Researcher has to deceive a  group - highly unethical

**++Overt Method of Observation++**

* Group is aware that research is taking place

* Whether the participant/non-participant, the researcher has to explain the purpose of the research

* **++Advantages++**

  * Researcher can ask questions and easily take notes

  * Researcher has to spend less time maintaining cover

  * Ethically correct

* **++Disadvantages++**

  * Risk of the Hawthorne effect

  * Not possible in all settings, as some groups may not accept a researcher, such as a criminal group.
