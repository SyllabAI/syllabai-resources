## **Analysis, Interpretation and Evaluation of Data from Qualitative and Quantitative Sources**

### Evaluating Qualitative Sources

* A sociologist needs to ask some **questions to evaluate a qualitative source and assess its validity.**

  * **Who** wrote the source? What social group was he from?

  * **Why** was the source produced?

  * Was it **meant to be read** by others?

  * **Was it possible for the author** to know about what they are describing?

  * Does the source **appear to be biased?**

  * Is the author’s view likely to **reflect the opinions of other people** at the time, i.e. Is it **representative**?

  * Do other sources either **contradict or corroborate** the source?

  * Did the author make it known h**ow the source should be interpreted?**

* ++Summaries of Studies:++

  * A summary is a common secondary source that allows a sociologist to get a good basis of knowledge on a research topic

  * A summary contains a condensed version of the information on a topic, with comments on research findings from other studies

  * These are not comprehensive and may need to be checked against a primary source for accuracy

### Evaluating Quantitative Sources

* Sociologists must choose the most accessible format to present statistical findings

* The different ways to present these statistics include:

  * Charts

  * Tables

  * Graphs

  * Diagrams

* As an IGCSE Sociology student, you must be able to interpret quantitative data presented in any form and answer the relevant question

* **Here are some tips :)**

**Step 1: Underline the keyword in the question**

* Identify command words such as most, least, highest, lowest, increase, and decrease

* This tells you what type of value you must find in the data

**Step 2: Find the relevant values in the data**

* Locate the row, column, bar, or line linked to the variable in question

* Compare only values that match the keyword

* Ignore trends, causes, or background information

**Step 3: Interpret the data literally**

* Select the category with the highest, lowest, largest, or smallest value

* Do not infer reasons or meanings beyond what is shown

* If values are equal, state that they are similar

**Step 4: State the answer clearly**

* Use one short, direct sentence

* Name the correct group, country, or year

* Do not explain or add extra detail unless asked
