## Ethical Issues

Since sociological research involves people, the research must consider moral issues when dealing with respondents.

Some of these guidelines include that the respondents. Many sociological organisations have their own guidelines.

**1)** must not be harmed - physically or mentally.

**2)** must give their informed consent,

**3)** must not be deceived

**4**) must have their privacy protected.

The researcher should also make sure that, when possible, the participant stays

* **++Anonymous++** - no personal information of the participant is revealed

* **++Confidential++** - it is not possible to identify a participant from his answers

### Informed Consent

* Participants must be aware of the purposes and publication of the research

* Participants must give their full consent whilst informed

* Participants have the right to refuse any question or request

### Privacy/Confidentiality

* Researchers must respect the participant’s privacy at all times

* Researchers should not ask personal questions

### Deception

* A researcher should not conceal or lie about the research

* Nor should he twist facts, or forget to provide information

### Prevention of Harm

* Participant should not face physical harm

* Participant should not face psychological harm, such as a disturbing question

* participants confidentiality and identity must be protected

### Legality

* Research must completely abide by the rules of the country in which it is being conducted.

* Misinformation and discrimination are legally unacceptable in some countries.

---

***Sociologists have to bear all of this in mind before, during, and after conducting research or analysing other researcher data to reach optimum levels of validity, reliability, and research authenticity.***
