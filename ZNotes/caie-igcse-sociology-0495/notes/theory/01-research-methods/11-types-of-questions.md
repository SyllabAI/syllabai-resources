## **Types of Questions**

Different techniques of research require different types of questions, such as interviews or questionnaires. The type of question you may want to use will depend on the kind of data you want to collect.

### **Open Questions**

* These are questions to which the respondent can reply freely in their own words

  * This allows researchers to gain detailed, insightful answers that provide validity

* Produce qualitative data

* Uses phrases such as “why?” or “ explain your answer “

* These questions may be used in unstructured and semi-structured interviews

  * These questions would be unusual in questionnaires, as respondents would not be willing to write long answers

* Example: "What are the biggest societal challenges you believe your generation faces, and why?"

### Closed Questions

* Questions that allow limited or set answers

* Respondent has to choose an answer from the provided list

* Generate quantitative data

* Each answer is assigned a value to produce statistical data

* One drawback is that the respondent may want to answer something that is not in the list provided

* Example: "Do you feel safe walking alone in your neighbourhood after dark? Answer Yes or No"

### Multiple-Choice Questions

* Type of closed question

* One statement, then a list of answers to choose from

* Often used in educational assessments with one answer labelled correct

* Example: "If you were a bicycle, what part would you be?"

  * **A.** The handlebars

  * **B.** The seat

  * **C.** The pedals

  * **D.** The wheels

### Scaled Question

* Type of closed question with a range of statements to choose from, such as:

  * strongly agree

  * agree

  * neutral

  * disagree

  * strongly disagree

* There is a debate about whether there should be an odd or even number of statements:

  * An odd number may lead to a respondent choosing the middle option in indecision

  * An even number may force a respondent to make a choice, even if he is actually neutral

* Example: “On a scale of 1 to 5, how confident are you in the government’s ability to keep you safe?”
