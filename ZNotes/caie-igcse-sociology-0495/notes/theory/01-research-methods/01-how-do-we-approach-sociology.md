# Research Methods

## How do We Approach Sociology

There are two main approaches to sociology:

### Positivism

* Founded by **Emile Durkheim**, this method relies on treating sociology as a **true science** and examining societies as a whole, known as the **macro view.**

* Propagated by **August Comte and Emile Durkheim** in the 19th century.

* **Views**

  * Social behavior is governed by **a set of social facts.**

  * Sociology should be treated with the **scientific method**.

* **Key Concepts**

  * **Reliability** is key: the extent to which the *research findings can be* **replicated** by doing the research under **similar circumstances.**

    * This allows for the *confirmation of results and* **accuracy.**

  * **Objectivity**: The researcher must remain **neutral** *and* **avoid bias** at all times.

* **Methods and Data**

  * Produces **quantitative data**—numerical *data.*

  * Positivists favour **experiments**.

  * They look for **patterns and trends** in data.

  * **Correlation**: when **two variables** *change at the same time.*

  * **Causation**: When a *change in one variable* has a **direct effect** *on another.*

**Example of a Positivist: Emile Durkheim:**

* He wanted to investigate the suicide rate of countries.

* He sought to identify **correlation and causation** in the data.

  * He saw that in cultures where social forces were strong, meaning how connected an individual was to religion in families, the suicide rates were much lower.

* He took a **large-scale, macro-view and looked for trends within quantitative data.**

### Interpretivism

* Developed by German sociologist **Max Weber.**

* **Approach**

  * Sociology is **not a pure science.**

  * Humans are **conscious, unpredictable** beings, and hence, sociology must be **approached differently.**

  * **Small-scale micro approach**: they study the *interactions between individuals and small groups.*

  * People do not always conform to society; *we are independent.*

  * People's ideas **change over time.**

* **++Key Concepts++**

  * They study the **meaning** *people give to their actions.*

  * They look at **understanding, motivation,** and **verstehen.**

  * **Verstehen**: **seeing** things from *another's point of view* and *truly understanding them*.

  * **Validity**: the extent to which a finding *represents reality and real life.*

  * **Subjectivity**: The *viewpoint and stance* of the researcher are *valued.*

  * Social reality is **embedded** in our actions—not *separate.*

* **++Data and Methods++**

  * Favour **qualitative data—descriptive** data—understanding *is more important.*

  * Favour **unstructured interviews and participant observation.**
