## Standardised Rates

* A Standardised Rate is a weighted average of rates in subgroups

* It is important to find Standardised Rates, as they take into account the different subgroups within a population which may affect the measurement of a rate.

* For example, A town with a large population of young women would inevitably have a higher Crude Birth Rate compared to a town with a low population of young women.

* Hence, to ensure that there is a fair comparison, Standardised Rates are used.

### Calculating Standardised Rates:

* To calculate Standardised Rates, we need the Standard Population of each subgroup.

* The Standard Populations usually sum to a total of 100.

* The formula for Standardised Rates is:

  $$

  \text{Standardised Rate} = \frac{\Sigma(Standard\ Population \times Crude\ Rate)}{\Sigma(Standard\ Population)}

  $$

* We will multiply each Crude Rate by its corresponding Standard Population and sum together the results of all the groups.

* Next, we will divide the Sum obtained by the Total Standard Population.
