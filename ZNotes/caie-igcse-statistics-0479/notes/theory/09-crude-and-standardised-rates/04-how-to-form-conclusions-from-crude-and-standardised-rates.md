## How to Form Conclusions from Crude and Standardised Rates

* The calculation of rates is useful to everyone.

* The management of a company would find it useful to know the rate of absences within each department, or the government would find it useful to know the rate of births in the country.

* However, Crude and Standardised Rates have situations in which they prove useful in reaching conclusions.

* The Crude Rate is useful for forming quick conclusions, analyzing trends over time, or comparing similar populations where subgroups within the population do not pose much bias to the rates.

  * For example:

    * The Crude Absentee rate is 10% for July; this allows the management to quickly reach an estimate regarding the number of employees who did not show up to work in the given month.

* The Standardised Rate is useful for more detailed analysis and to compare events that are greatly affected by subgroups in the population.

  * For example:

    * The Standardised rate of absentees is 8% for July; this accounts for the different types of departments.

    * Departments with a more stressful workload may have a trend of more frequent absences compared to departments with an easier workload.

* A High Crude Rate tells us that the event is widespread across the entire population.

  * For example:

    * A Crude Accident rate of 88%

* A Low Crude Rate tells us that the event is not widespread across the entire population.

  * For example:

    * A Crude Death rate of 2%
