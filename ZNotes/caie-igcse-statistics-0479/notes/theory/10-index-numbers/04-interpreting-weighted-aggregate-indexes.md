## Interpreting Weighted Aggregate Indexes

* A Weighted Aggregate Index is important as it takes into account the relative importance of each item rather than blindly using the Price Relatives as a source of comparison.

* A 30% increase in the cost of Electricity would undoubtedly put a greater burden on an electronics factory than a 50% increase in the cost of Salt.

* If we used an unweighted index, it would seem as if the Salt caused a greater spike in expenses, which would be untrue.

* If an item is used more, it will have a greater Weight, just like electricity has a greater weight than salt as it is used more.

* A higher weight means greater importance and effect, and a lower weight means less importance and effect.

### Limitations of Weighted Aggregate Indexes:

* Weighted Aggregate Indexes may not remain an accurate source of comparison if the base year is outdated.

* Weighted Aggregate Indexes assume any change in price to be due to inflation and do not account for the increase/decrease in quality of the product.

* Weighted Aggregate Indexes may not account for new products in the market that may substitute for older, outdated products.

### Effects of Changes in Weights:

* In Index Numbers, Weights are used to show the importance of different items in the data.

* When the weight of an item changes, so does its overall importance in the data.

* When an item has higher demand, it is given a higher weight
