## Line of Best Fit

* The line of best fit is not used solely for finding the correlation between the variables; it can also be used for extrapolation and interpolation.

* Interpolation is estimating a value within the range of values already given.

* Extrapolation is estimating a value outside the range of values already given.

### Finding the Equation of the Line of Best Fit:

* The equation of the line of best fit is found in the form of

  * $y = mx + c$

* Where:

  * $y$ = $y$ coordinate

  * $m$ = gradient

  * $x$ = $x$ coordinate

  * $c$ = $y$ intercept

* To calculate the gradient, it is always recommended to use ($\bar{x} , \bar{y}$) and another point on the line of best fit, such as ($a, b$)

* We can input these values into the formula for the gradient

  * $m = \frac{\bar{y} - b}{\bar{x} - a}$

* Now that we have the values of $y, x, m$, we can substitute them into the formula to find the value of c.

### For example:

* The value of ($\bar{x} , \bar{y}$) is (30 , 40)

* Another point B lies on the line of best fit at (25, 30)

* $m = \frac{40 - 30}{30 - 25}$

* $m = 2$

* $y = mx + c$

* $40 = 2 \times 30 +c$

* $c = -20$

### How to Estimate The Value of a Variable:

* If given the value of one variable, we can estimate the value of the other either using the line of best fit or the equation for the line of best fit.

### Assumptions Made When Estimating Values:

* While estimating values, we assume that the Relationship between the variables is strong enough that a line of best fit can be used as an accurate reference.

* The relationship between the variables is linear.

* There are no extreme values in the data.

* We must also ensure that our extrapolation follows some given limits:

  * Measures like length, age, etc. cannot be negative.

  * Measures like the weight of a Human will have a reasonable limit.

  * Percentages cannot exceed a hundred.
