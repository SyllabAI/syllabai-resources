1. ### Simple Random Sample:

   * We will choose 5 random numbers from 0-54 via a calculator or a random number generator.

   * For example, we choose 21, 31, 42, 07, 31, 24.

   * We will **NOT** take the number 31 twice, as it has been repeated. All chosen numbers must not be repeated.

   * So, our sample consists of residents 21, 31, 42, 07, and 24.

* To see if a sample is representative in terms of multiple strata, we cross-check the ratio of the strata in the sampling frame and the sample obtained.

  * To find out if our sample is representative in terms of gender, we can find the ratio of females to males in the population and in our sample.

    * $\frac{22}{55}= 0.4$

    * $\frac{33}{55}= 0.6$

    * So the ratio for female to males in the population should be 4:6, or 2:3

    * Our sample contains 2 females (07, 21) and 3 males (24, 31, 42)

    * As this ratio aligns with the ratio of males and females in the population, we can say that our sample is representative in terms of gender.

1. ### Systematic Sample:

   * To take a systematic sample of sample size 5, we will first divide the population into 5  equal portions.

   * For this, we will divide the population by the sample size.

   * $\frac{55}{5}= 11$

   * So we have 11 members of the population per group, meaning our population has been divided into groups 00-10, 11-21, 22-32, 33-43, 44-54.

   * A random number will be selected from the first group, and every 11th number after that first selection will be chosen for our sample.

   * For example, if we get 05 as our first choice. The other numbers chosen will be 16, 27, 38, and 49.

   * To check if our sample is representative in terms of gender, we will follow the same method used before.

2. ### Stratified Sample:

   * To take a stratified sample, we must first divide the population according to its proportions.

   * If we need to obtain a sample stratified by gender, we will determine the number of males and females that the sample must include to make it representative.

   * For this, we will use a formula

   * $\frac{\text{category 1}}{\text{population size}} \times \text{sample size}$

   * $\frac{22}{55} \times 5=2$

   * Therefore, our sample must include 2 females randomly selected out of the 22.

   * $\frac{33}{55} \times 5=3$

   * And it must contain 3 males randomly selected out of the 33.
