## How to Scale Data for Comparison

* If we are given data of 2 distinct categories, it is difficult to compare them.

* For example, 2 friends want to compare their exam scores; however, both gave seperate exams.

* In order to make a fair comparison, we can scale both datasets to a set Mean and Standard Deviation.

### Formula:

* To scale data, we use the following formula:

  * $\frac{x - \overline{x}}{\sigma} = \frac{x_2 - \overline{x_2}}{\sigma_2}$

* Where:

  * $x$ = Orignal Mark

  * $\overline{x}$ = Orignal Mean

  * $\sigma$ = Orignal Standard Deviation

  * $x_2$ = Scaled Mark

  * $\overline{x_2}$ = Scaled Mean

  * $\sigma_2$ = Scaled Standard Deviation

* Above, we are given some data regarding the marks of 2 friends, Max and Leo, in 2 separate examinations.

* We cannot compare the raw data given as they both have given different exams; hence we must scale their marks.

* For this example, we will scale their marks to

  * Mean of 50

  * Standard Deviation of 5

* Hence:

  * $\frac{x - \overline{x}}{\sigma} = \frac{x_2 - \overline{x_2}}{\sigma_2}$

  * $\frac{47 - 53.5}{3.8} = \frac{x_2 - 50}{5}$

  * $x_2 = 41.45$

* Max’s Scaled Mark is $41.45$

  * $\frac{x - \overline{x}}{\sigma} = \frac{x_2 - \overline{x_2}}{\sigma_2}$

  * $\frac{39 - 45.2}{2.7} = \frac{x_2 - 50}{5}$

  * $x_2 = 38.52$

* Leo’s Scaled Mark is $38.52$

* As $41.45 > 38.52$, Max performed better than Leo on his exam.

### Standardising Data to a Mean of 0 and Standard Deviation of 1:

* To Standardise data to a Mean of 0 and a Standard Deviation of 1, we use the following formula:

$$

z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}

$$

* Where:

  * $z$ = Standard Data Value

  * $x$ = Original Data Value

  * $\mu$ = Mean of the Dataset

  * $\sigma$ = Standard Deviation of the Dataset

* So, to find Max’s Score Standardised to a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1:

  * $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$

  * $z = \frac{47 - 53.5}{3.8}$

  * $z = -1.71$
