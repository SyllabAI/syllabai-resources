## How to Find Quartiles and Percentiles using Cumulative Frequency Diagrams

* We can easily find the value of any percentile with the help of a cumulative frequency polygon or curve.

### How to find the Percentile of a Quartile:

* To find the value of a Quartile, we must first convert it into a Percentile.

* For example:

  * The Lower Quartile represents the $\frac{1}{4}th$ point of the data.

  * Hence, to convert into a percentile:

    * $\frac{1}{4} \times 100$

    * $= 25th$ Percentile

* The Percentile values of each Quartile are fixed and will always be:

  * LQ = $25th$ Percentile

  * Median = $50th$ Percentile

  * UQ = $75th$ Percentile

### How to find the Value of a Percentile:

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/709edae11bb59a60a5cf5836.webp =773x)

* To find the value of the Upper Quartile of a dataset through a cumulative frequency curve:

  * First, we will convert the Quartile into a percentile:

    * UQ = $75th$ Percentile

  * Next, we will find the location of the 75th Percentile by using the formula:

    * $\frac{x}{100} \times f$

    * Where $x$ is the percentile and $f$ is the total frequency of the dataset.

  * Hence:

    * $\frac{75}{100} \times 150$

    * $= 112.5$

  * The value of the UQ will be found at the 112.5th frequency

  * We will use a ruler and pencil to read the curve and find the value.

  * Therefore, the Value of the UQ is estimated to be $46$.

### **Estimating Frequencies in Given Parts of the Distribution via Cumulative Frequency Curve:**

* We may be asked to find the frequency in a certain part of the Frequency Distribution.

* For example, find the frequency between values 10 and 50:

  * First, we have to find the corresponding frequencies for the values 10 and 50.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/379eac31103bcda301468947.webp =782x)

* We have found the frequencies to be:

  * CF at 10 = 5

  * CF at 50 = 130

* To find the frequency between the 2 values, we can simply find the difference between their obtained frequencies

  * $130 - 5$

  * $= 125$

* Hence, the frequency between 10 and 50 is 125.
