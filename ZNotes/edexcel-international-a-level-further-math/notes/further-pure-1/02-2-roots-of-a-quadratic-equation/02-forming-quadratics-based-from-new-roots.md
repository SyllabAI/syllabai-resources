## Forming quadratics based from new roots

* you may be asked to create new quadratics from new roots
  * use the 2 roots of the new quadratic given to calculate the sum and product of roots
  * substitute the final values into a quadratic equation and multiply all terms by the denominators until all terms in the quadratic are integers
* these identities are often used when calculating the sum and product of the roots
  * $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = (\alpha + \beta)^2 - 2\alpha\beta$
  * $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = (\alpha + \beta)^3 - 3\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta)$

---

**Example Question**

* the roots of the equation $8x^2 + 2x -3 = 0$ have roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Form a quadratic equation with **integer** coefficients which have roots: $\frac{1}{\alpha}$ and $\frac{1}{\beta}$

**solution**

* identities:

  * $\alpha + \beta =\frac{-1}{4}$ and $\alpha\beta = \frac{-3}{8}$

* find the sum of roots

  * $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta}$

  * $\frac{\alpha + \beta}{\alpha\beta}$

  * $\frac{-1}{4} / \frac{-3}{8} = \frac{2}{3}$

* find the product of roots

  * $\frac{1}{\alpha} * \frac{1}{\beta}$

  * $\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}$

  * $\frac{8}{-3}$

* substitute into quadratic

  * $x^2 + (\frac{-b}{a})x + \frac{c}{a}$
  * $x^2 + \frac{-2}{3}x + \frac{8}{-3}$
  * $3x^2 - 2x + 8$
