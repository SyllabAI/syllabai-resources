## Solving quadratic equations

- Quadratic equations with complex numbers as solutions exist as a conjugate pair; if the first solution is complex number $z$, the other solution is its complex conjugate number $z^*$
- If we are given the first root of a quadratic with no real solutions, we can use this information to find the original quadratic equation where the first root is a solution

**Example question**
- *Given $\alpha$ = 8 - 5i is a root of a quadratic equation, find the original quadratic equation*
  - $\beta = 8 + 5i$ ($\beta$ is the conjugate pair for $\alpha$) 
    - $(z - \alpha)(z - \beta) = 0$
    - $(z - (8 - 5i))(z - (8 + 5i)) = 0$
    - $(z - 8 + 5i)(z - 8 - 5i) = 0$
    - $z^2 - 8z - 8z + 64 - 5i(z) + 5i(z) - 8(5i) + 8(5i) - 5i^2 = 0$
    - $z^2 - 16z + 64 - 5(-1) = 0$
    - $z^2 - 16z + 69 = 0$


## Solving cubic and quartic equations
- cubic equations have 2 possible outcomes when solving
  - one root is real while the other 2 are a complex conjugate pair
  - all 3 roots are real
- quartic equations have 3 possible outcomes when solving
  - all 4 roots are real
  - 2 roots is a complex conjugate pair while the other 2 roots are real
  - all 4 solutions are 2 complex conjugate pairs
