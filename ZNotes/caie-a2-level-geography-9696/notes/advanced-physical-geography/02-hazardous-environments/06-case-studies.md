## Case Studies

### **Tectonic Movements**

* **Haiti - 2010**

  * On 12 January 2010, an Earthquake recorded as 7.0 on the Richter Scale occurred 25km south-west of Port-au-Prince at a depth of just 13km.

  * Aftershocks were recorded as 5.9 on the Richter Scale.

  * About 300,000 people were killed, 250,000 injured and 1 million displaced.

  * Cuba is located on the Gonave microplate, a small strip of the Earth’s crust squeezed between the North American
    and Caribbean tectonic plates, between the Septenrional Fault Line and the Enriquillo-Plantain Garden Fault Line.

  * The Casualties were due to inadequate construction of buildings in Port-au-Prince. Most of the residents here live under tin-roofed shacks on unstable, steep ravines.

  * The UN managed to provide food for only 200,000 people whilst releasing USD 10 million from its emergency fund alongside USD 13.7 million from European countries.

  * Following the Earthquake, plans were made to rescue, rehabilitate and reconstruct the country. However, the international community has failed to lift Haiti out of poverty, corruption and violence and much progress has not been made despite the USD 4 billion given. A debt of USD 1.5 billion has further weighed down Haiti’s economy.

* **To-hoku, Japan - 2011**

  * In 2011, an Earthquake of magnitude 9.0 on the Richter Scale was recorded off the east coast of Japan. The epicentre was 70km east of To-hoku at a depth of 30km.

  * It was the most powerful earthquake ever to hit Japan, and the fourth most powerful since 1900.

  * The earthquake caused a Tsunami and generated waves in excess of 12m which killed thousands and damaged a large portion of the Sendai region.

  * The Tsunami hit the coast just 10-30 minutes after the earthquake and the damage from the tsunami was much greater than the earthquake as the sea walls were much shorter than the waves that hit the coastline and thus rendered useless.

  * There were 16,000 deaths, with 2500 missing and 250,000 displaced.

  * More than 125,000 buildings com\[pletely collapsed and a further 1 million were damaged. There was widespread structural and fuctional damage to transport and infrastructure facilities. 10% of Japan’s fishing ports were damaged.

  * The Fukushima Nuclear Disaster was a direct result of the 2011 Earthquake Tsunami whereby 3 reactor cores overheated and suffered partial meltdowns, releasing radioactive material and displacing 150,000 people.

  * Aid organisations donated around USD 1 billion in emergency relief. The long-term economic impact included the reconstruction of destroyed facilities, with a cost of USD 122 billion.

### Mass Movements

* **Hong Kong landslides (1972)**

  * In June of 1972, a series of major landslides hit several apartment complexes and squatter complexes were wiped out with at least 156 casualties.

  * The Landslides had been caused by waterlogged soils due to the landfall of Typhoon Rose in August 1971 alongside other heavy rainstorms hitting Hong Kong on the days prior to the landslides.

  * **Sau Mau Ping landslide**

    * On June 18, a landslide occurred and buried the squatter village of Sau Mau Ping, destroying 78 huts and killing 71, most of whom were children.

    * Staff and fathers of the students at an adjacent college started rescue operations, later assisted by 600 other rescue personnel.

  * **Shiu Fai Terrace landslips**

    * 3 Landslips occurred on June 16th. They were not as big as the Po Shan Road Landslide but had affected a much greater area and several were killed.

  * **Po Shan Road landslide**

    * On June 16, 1972, several slips and cracks were noticed in the retaining wall, but no action was taken due to heavy rainfall in the region alongside mud blocking the Po Shan Road.

    * On June 17, a slip occurred over the whole width of the cut slope at the southern face of Inland Lot 2260 on Po Shan Road. Residents were urged to leave their houses and apartments around the IL 2260 surrounding Po Shan Road as a precaution.

    * By June 18, mud was observed falling on the nearby Conduit Road, and cracks continued to appear in nearby retaining walls. It quickly became apparent that a major landslide was about to occur and nearby apartments were evacuated whilst Po Shan Road was cordoned off to the public.

    * Around 5 pm, the same day, the first of 2 slips occurred when a mass of rocks, mud and vegetation broke free and slid down the side of IL 2260 breaking through a retaining wall and completely blocking Conduit Road. No casualties were recorded.

    * Sometime after 8 pm, 18 June 1972, a disastrous landslide occurred at a hillside above Po Shan Road starting at IL 2260 and, in less than ten seconds, destroyed several houses and retaining walls. The force of the slide was so strong that it completely swept away a 6-storey building and completely destroyed another 12-storey apartment block, with the top 4 storeys of an adjacent unoccupied building being torn off in the process. The slide continued downhill and several flats at Robinson Road and Babington Path had collapsed.6-storey building completely swept away

    * There were 67 casualties, with 20 injuries in less than a minute. Rescue efforts were made slow and hampered due to destruction of the transport network, the lack of lighting, the heavy rainfall and the continuous slides that continued to occur afterwards.

  * Several other casualties have been recorded in major landslides in Hong Kong since 1972, however they have never exceeded 5 in each accident.

  * Hong Kong has since regulated construction of buildings on its hilly terrain, installed drainage tunnels and strategically installed barriers to protect from landslides.

  * The Landslides were determined to have been caused by:

    * Poor, unregulated construction of apartment complexes and buildings on hilly terrain.

    * Typhoon Rose brought intense rainfall for several days in 1971, damaging the retaining walls. Further heavy rainfall in the days before the accident had only made the situation much worse.

    * Buildings and roads built on man-made steep slopes were unregulated.

    * Ground-water conditions were much higher than normal contributing to the waterlogging of soil and a reduction in shear-stress upon the increase in soil saturation. As a result, a slope failure took place.

### Volcanic Hazards

* **Lake Nyos, Cameroon**

  * Lake Nyos is nearly 2 km wide and over 200 metres deep.

  * In August 1986, a huge volume of gas escaped from the lake and swept down into neighbouring valleys for a distance of up to 25 km. The ground-hugging clouds of Carbon Dioxide gas were up to 50 metres thick and travelled at speeds of over 70 km/h.

  * Around 1700 people suffocated, 3000 cattle died and all other animal life in the area was killed by asphyxiation. The only people who escaped the tragedy were sleeping on upper levels of houses. Plant-life, however, was not affected.

### Tsunamis

* **Boxing Day Tsunami, December 26, 2004, Indonesia**

  * On 26 December 2004, around 8 am, an earthquake of Magnitude 9.2-9.3 struck with its epicenter off the west coast of Aceh, in northern Sumatra, Indonesia. The earthquake caused a massive Tsunami with waves up to 30m high.

  * The Sunda Trench slipped by 20m (30km deep) and triggered 9.0 earthquake for 4 minutes.

  * Each 1.5m of coast was hit with 1000 tonnes of water. The wave destroyed Northern Sumatra and the Nicobar Islands. It hit areas of Indonesia, Sri Lanka, India and Thailand.

  * The 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami and Earthquake is the deadliest tsunami in history, the deadliest natural disaster in the 21st Century, the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Asia, and one of the deadliest natural disasters in recorded history.

  * There was a total of 227,898 casualties, with 42,789 missing and 1.7 million people displaced. ⅓ of the dead were reported to be children.

  * £50m was provided in aid (where food and shelter were the top priority). A major proportion of the aid was provided in the form of sanitation facilities to contain the spread of diseases resulting from the tsunami. The World Food Programme provided food to nearly 1.3 million people affected by the tsunami.

  * The lack of tsunami early warning systems in 2004, constant communication failures between governments and vulnerability of the local public were a major factor for the high casualty count. Locals were unaware of tsunami warning signs and did not recognise them before the waves hit. Most cities consisted of high population densities close to the coast where mangrove forests had been cut down for tourism and as such the coast and been deprived of the natural buffer to absorb the wave’s energy.

  * Since the Tsunami occured on a Sunday morning, most locals were at home and tourists at the beach. The wave travelled miles inland, flattening roads, destroying roads and erasing infrastructure which further hampered rescue operations.

  * As a result of the Tsunami, Early Warning Systems were established with Sirens and Alerts and underwater deep sea cable sensors for earthquakes.

  * Public Education Awareness plans and Emergency Drills were practiced and implemented alongside the construction of Tsunami shelters and establishment of mangrove forest rehabilitation programmes.

* **2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami**

  * On 11 March 2011, just after 2:45 pm, an undersea earthquake of Magnitude 9.0-9.1 occurred in the Pacific Ocean, 72 km (45 mi) east of the Oshika Peninsula of the Tōhoku refion of Japan. It lasted approximately six minutes and caused a massive tsunami.

  * The earthquake was the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Japan. It triggered powerful tsunami waves that reached heights of up to 40.5 meters in Miyako, and which, in the Sendai area, traveled at 700 kmph and up to 10 km inland.

  * Residents had less than 10 minutes of warning and more than 100 evacuation sites were washed away. The tsunami was followed by snowfall that hindered rescue efforts. There were 19,759 casualties, with 284 firefighters dead, 6,242 injured and 2553 missing. As of 2015, 228,863 people of the 400,000 displaced continued to live away from their homes.

  * The earthquake and tsunami caused the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Disaster. 3 nuclear reactor faced meltdowns, and radioactive water was discharged into the waters of Fukushima affecting and putting the lives of over 100,000 people at risk.

  * The estimated economic damage amounted to over $300 billion, making it the costliest natural disaster in history

  * The death toll of the tsunami was primarily due to the height of the waves and the force of with the travelled inland, overwhelming coastal defenses. A large portion of the deceased were people of ages 65\+. The tsunami had occurred so fast that many locals were unable to make it to evacuation sites.

  * Since the Tsunami, Japan has invested in higher seawalls with improved resident awareness and tsunami drills. The disaster has led to an increased investment in undersea, real-time sensor technology to track seismic activity and wave elevation.

### Large-Scale Atmospheric Disturbances

* **Hurricane Katrina, 2005**

  * Hurricane Katrina was one of the most devastating natural disasters in U.S. history, making landfall on August 29, 2005 as a Category 3 Hurricane.

  * On August 25, the Hurricane was of Cat 1 in winds of 80mph.
    On August 26, the Hurricane reached Cat 2 with winds sustaining 96-110mph.
    On August 27, the Hurricane reached Cat 3 with winds sustaining 111-130mph.
    On August 28, the Hurricane reached Cat 5 with winds \~160mph.
    Finally, on August 30, the Hurricane got back to the Tropical Storm Rating.

  * Nearly 80% of New Orleans was submerged, with some areas under 15 to 20 feet of water. The flooding was mainly caused by 53 breaches in the federally built levee system which could not sustain the shear stress from the rainfall and thus breached, allowing billions of gallons of water from Lake Pontchartrain and Lake Borgne to pour into the city.

  * There were 1,392 casualties with 7,543 injuries and 135 missing. More than 1.5 million people were displaced with 100,000 trapped in the city.

  * Hurricane Katrina produced a storm surge of up to 28 feet. The catastrophic failure of the levee system alongside a delayed federal response to the accident and an economic loss of up to USD 160 million brought about efforts to prevent future storm surges, with the construction of bigger, stronger levee systems and taller floodwalls

* **Cyclone Bhola, 1970**

  * The 1970 Cyclone Bhola is the deadliest tropical cyclone ever recorded. On November 12, 1970, it made landfall in East-Pakistan (Modern Day Bangladesh) and India.

  * It originated from the remnants of the Tropical Storm Nora in the South China Sea, and re-intensified over the Bay of Bengal before traveling northward. It made landfall during a period of high tide which greatly amplified its destructive power.

  * Massive storm surges of up to 10 metres were recorded in areas of low-lying islands in the Ganges Delta with high population densities and no elevated ground. The Storm made landfall during the night, and many people were sleeping in their homes at the time. Storm devastation was further enhanced by the complete failure of the Pakistani Government to communicate warnings to local residents despite knowing of the approaching storm.

  * There were up to 500,000 casualties and 1.5 million displaced. 85% of the homes in the affected area were wiped away, with over 280,000 cattle lost and 65% of the regions fishing capacity lost. Perceived criminal negligence by the Pakistani Government further hampered rescue efforts which were delayed and a slow relief response was provided. The 1970 Cyclone Bhola is one of the reasons for political instability in the region and the liberation of Bangladesh in 1971.

  * According to the World Bank, 4.7 million people were affected by Cyclone Bhola with over 400,000 buildings damaged and 115,000 fishermen impacted. Pre-existing poverty and the lack of infrastructure meant that the damage was estimated to be USD 330 million.

  * The disaster influenced the creation of Cyclone preparedness programmes where 76,000 volunteers provide early warnings to remote villages. More than 200 elevated concrete shelters were constructed, and the adoption of Early Warning Systems such as the Doppler Radar, public awareness programmes and mangrove forest restoration only enhances preparedness and safety during future storm events.

### Small-Scale Atmospheric Disturbances

* **1974 Super Tornado Outbreak**

  * The 1974 Super Outbreak was one of the most violent and widespread tornado events in recorded history. On April 3, 1974, over a period of 18-24 hours, 148 tornadoes struck 13 U.S states and Ontario, Canada. The 1974 Super Tornado Outbreak held the record for the most tornadoes in a single outbreak until the 2011 Super Outbreak.

  * The Outbreak consisted of powerful storms, 30 of which were characterised as F4-F5, with 6 F5 tornadoes in 1 day. The tornadoes carved a combined damage path of over 2,500 miles and at one point, 15 separate active tornadoes were on the ground.

  * There were 355 casualties with 6,000 people injured and up to 30,000 displaced.

  * The outbreak caused approximately USD 600 million in damage with over 27,000 buildings damaged or destroyed.

  * Preventative methods include the modernisation of the Doppler radar network in the 1990’s alongside widespread public awareness and school safety drills. The 1974 Super Tornado Outbreak is one of the reasons for the discovery of meteorological downbursts and the refinement of the Fujita (F) Scale by Dr. Ted Fujita.

* **2011 Super Tornado Outbreak**

  * The 2011 Super Tornado Outbreak is the largest and costliest tornado outbreak ever recorded. On April 25, 2011, over a span of 72 hours, 362 confirmed tornadoes across 21 states were reported with the outbreak peaking on April 27 where 216 tornadoes touched down in a 24-hour period.

  * The Outbreak featured 15 violent tornadoes, of which 4 were characterised as EF5 with winds exceeding 200mph and bring widespread devastation.

  * There were 348 casualties with 3,100 people injured and 350,000 displaced.

  * More than 15,000 homes, businesses and farm buildings were destroyed with over 23,000 buildings either damaged or destroyed. Economic damages totaled approximately USD 12 billion and forests were stripped of vegetation leaving behind scars in the soil.

  * Preventative methods include the implementation of impact-based warnings and public awareness programmes.
