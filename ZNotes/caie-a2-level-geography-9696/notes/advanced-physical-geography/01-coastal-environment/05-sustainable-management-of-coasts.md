## Sustainable Management of Coasts

### Shoreline Management Plans

* SMPs are used in England and Wales for every littoral cell, so that each stretch can be managed, and the resultant (and future) impacts on adjacent cells can be monitored

**Objectives**

* Define flooding and erosion risks to lives and the natural environment over the next century.

* Identify best policies for managing risks, and the impacts of those policies.

* Inform planners, developers and locals of risks.

* Comply with international and local nature laws.

* Highlight areas of absent coastal knowledge.

* Provide an action plan.

**Principles**

* **Hold the line:**
  * Maintain/repair current coastal defences.

  * Becomes more expensive with time, as continual building needed
    due to rising sea levels
* **Advance the line:**
  * Build new coastal defences on the seaward side of current ones.

  * Possible land reclamation – very expensive and global warming risk.
* **Managed realignment:**
  * Allow coastline to move in/out from the land in a managed way (with SMPs).
* **No active intervention:**
  * Make no investment in the coastline and allow natural processes to take over. Cheapest strategy but threatens livelihoods.

**Conflicts:**

* Coastal environments pose a barrier to management strategies
  * **Urbanisation/transport:**
    * Land-use changes remove habitats; dredging is visually unappealing and poses health risks to humans/fish (heavy metals); water abstraction/disposal causes water pollution.
  * **Agriculture:**
    * Land reclamation removes habitats; water abstraction lowers groundwater table inducing salt water intrusion; fertilisers cause eutrophication.
  * **Tourism/Recreation:**
    * Land-use changes remove habitats; water abstraction lowers the groundwater table encouraging salt water
      intrusion; water/waste disposal triggers water pollution and eutrophication.
  * **Fishing:**
    * May lead to overfishing and therefore an unsustainable reduction in fish stocks if not regulated; fishing kit left over pollutes; fish farms introduce alien species to the area.
  * **Industry:**
    * Land-use changes remove habitats; extraction of natural resources and expulsion of chemicals causes pollution and eutrophication; river impoundment/barrages increase erosion.

### Problems Facing Sustainable Management

* **Sustainable management:** Must consider 3 pillars, meet the needs of the current while not compromising the future.
  * **Environmental sustainability:**
    * Conserve ecosystems, reduce and prevent pollution, maintain aesthetics.
  * **Economic sustainability:**
    * Allow communities to continue to make a living, protect assets (buildings, factories) from erosion, cost is balanced by benefits.
  * **Social sustainability:**
    * Maintain (or improve) living standards, communities and livelihoods.

### **Coastal Defenses:**

* Coastal Defense covers protection against coastal erosion and flooding by the sea.

* **Cost and Benefit Analysis:**

  * A systematic approach to estimating the strengths and weaknesses of alternatives.

* **Costs:**

  * Cost of building, Maintenance, Limited Access to the coast, Reduced recreational value, Economic cost, Unattractive, Prone to Erosion, Reduced accessibility.

* **Benefits**:

  * Protection of infrastructure, Employment, Peace, High land prices Hard Engineering:

* **Seawalls:**

  * Large-scale concrete horizontal walls designed to reflect wave energy.

  * Able to withstand severe waves.

  * Easily made in areas of high density.

  * Expensive with a lifespan of about 30-40 years.

* **Curved Seawalls:**

  * Large-scale concrete curved walls designed to reflect wave energy.

  * Able to Withstand severe waves.

  * Easily made in areas of high density.

  * Expensive with a lifespan of about 30-40 years.

  * **Withernsea:** Wave return wall part of £6.3m scheme, holds the line but limited length due to cost.

* **Gabions:**

  * Rocks held in wire mesh cages absorb wave energy.

  * Cheaper than seawalls and revetments and are flexible.

  * Disrupt longshore drift, can rust and burst. They have a lifespan of 5 years.

  * **Skipsea:** Landowner has employed own cages. While protecting his own land, erosion now occurs at greater rates either side of the gabion baskets.

* **Groynes:**

  * Wooden barriers perpendicular to the coast at the sea to prevent longshore drift.

  * They are cheap to build and widen the beach.

  * They are unappealing and do not have a long lifespan.

  * **Hornsea:** 2 major concrete groynes at a cost of £5.2m with others build and maintained. Down-drift now experiences greater erosion – Great Cowden Farm lost 45 years earlier than expected.

* **Rock armour:**

  * Large rocks at the base of a cliff to absorb wave energy.

  * They are cheap to build.

  * Unattractive can can only be built on a small-scale.

* **Revetment:**

  * Sloping walls parallel to the coast designed to absorb and dissipate wave energy.

  * Easily made and cheaper than sea walls, however their lifespan is limited.

* **Rip-Rap:**

  * Large boulder placed parallel to the coast to create a ramp.

  * They have low maintenance costs but are expensive.

  * **Easington:** home to a gas terminal for 25% of the UK’s gas. £4.5m rip-rap barrier at the base of cliff.

* **Cliff drainage:**

  * The removal of water from rocks in the cliff.

  * Cost-effective.

* **Cliff grading:**

  * Lowering of slope angle to make a cliff safer.

  * Useful on clay but uses large amounts of land and is impractical.

* **Soft Engineering:**

  * **Offshore reefs:**

    * Waste materials (e.g old tyres) are weighed down to reduce the speed of oncoming waves.

    * Low technology and relatively cost effective.

    * Long-term impact unknown.

  * **Beach Nourishment:**

    * Sand pumped from seabed to replace eroded sand.

    * Looks natural and attractive.

    * Expensive and is a short-term solution.

    * **Withernsea:** An attempt made as part of £6.3m scheme.

  * **Sand Dune conservation:**

    * Natural erosion of sand and flooding prevention.

    * Attractive and increases access to the beach.

    * Maintenance is required and reduced public access to the beach.

  * **Cliff Re-grading:**

    * The relocation of material for a gentler slope.

    * It is more stable and can maintain natural appearance once repopulated.

    * It is disruptive during construction to locals and wildlife.

    * **Mappleton:** £2.1m investment with EU funding to reduce slumping and build new visitor facilities (car park toilets). In 2002 signs of heavy basal scour, threatening success.

  * **Planning Controls:**

    * No development or urbanisation in areas under risk.

    * Relatively cheap and effective in the long run.

    * Uses a wide area of land, off limits to construction and expansion of a settlement.

    * **Spurn Head:** Management strategy abandoned in 1995 by Holderness Borough Council as repeated winter storms washed over the head of the spit.

  * **Do Nothing:**

    * You do absolutely nothing.

    * Cost-effective.

    * Erosion leads to coastline retreat, endangering settlements and lives. Political implications are present.

  * **Shelters:**

    * Shelters along coastlines or around rivers may be located in areas prone to flooding.

    * Bangladesh - Over 2000 shelters located around the 3 main rivers.

  * **Embankments:**

    * Raised sections around coastlines that prevent ocean water from spilling onto land.

    * Cheap to maintain with a reduced flood risk.

    * If breached, flood water may remain trapped on land and threaten life.

    * Bangladesh storm surges.

    * **Netherlands:** ½ underwater, so embankments built to withstand 1200 year flood event.

    * **Ganges River, Bangladesh:** In 1991 a 6m storm surge killed 140,000 when embankments were breached.

  * **Offshore reefs:**

    * Dropping large waste materials (car shells, tyres) offshore to reduce wave energy.

    * Low-technology, little cost involved.

    * Unknown long-term environmental effects.

  * **Managed retreat (sustainable):**

    * Allow the coastline to retreat or breach defences in certain areas, so saltmarshes can develop.

    * **C**heaper than strengthening existing sea wall, saltmarsh provides a feeding ground for birds.

    * Sacrificed agricultural land, compensation must be paid, new sea walls may be needed, disruptive initial works.

    * **Medmerry, West Sussex, UK:** £28m scheme to protect 350 homes, 2 holiday parks and critical national infrastructure from 1000-year floods. New defences 2km in-land from previously held strong in severe storm weather in 2013 and 2014.

  * **Barrages:**

    * Dam-like structure (hard-engineering) on estuaries or inlets, to create a freshwater lake behind it, and to prevent saltwater mixing with inland fresh water.

    * Encourage land reclamation, temporary barriers
      are most flexible, can produce HEP

    * Changes saltmarshes into freshwater – destroying bird’s feeding ground, interferes with Salmon migration, expensive, prevents ship navigation.

    * **Rance Barrage, France:** 240MW facility on the coast of Brittany, 750m long with 24 turbines. Copes with maximum tidal range of 13.5m.

### **Case Study**

* **Holderness Coast:**

  * The Holderness Coast is located on the east coast of England and extends 61 km from Flamborough in the north to Spurn Point in the south.

  * It is a boulder-clay coast that.

  * It is one of Europe’s fastest eroding coastlines with an average annual rate of around 2 m lost to the sea.

  * 5 kms of land and 29 towns/villages lost since Roman Times.

  * Holderness coast is made up of soft boulder clay that has slowly eroded away.

  * Spurn point provides evidence of longshore drift on the Holderness Coast. Roughly 3% of the material eroded from the Holderness Coast is deposited at Spurn Point to form a spit.
