## Management of a Tourist Destination

**The Lake District, United Kingdom**

**Background**

* **Size:** England’s largest national park, 2362km2, 600,000 acres large, 36 miles by 40 miles tall
* **Location:** North-west of England, 70 miles from Manchester and Liverpool, 26 miles of coastline
* **Trees:** 12% covered by woodland, 12,000 acres broadleaf woodland, 9,500 acres managed by Forestry Commission, 620 acres owned by National Park
* **Climate:** Max: 20 degrees - 6.8 degrees, Min: 12 degrees - 1 degrees, Ambleside 2000mm rainfall, Seathwaite 3552mm rainfall
* **Population:** 40,500 people (18 per km2), 24,500 dwellings (68% owner-occupied, 42% rented, 24% second homes)
* **National Park Aims:** To be an inspirational example of sustainable development
* **Ownership:** National Park owns less than 4%, rest is owned by National Trust, United Utilities, Forestry Commission and private owners
* **Employment:** 1% unemployment, 1 in 5 of economically active are self-employed, 15,000 people work in tourism, 2,500 people work in farming
* **Geographical attractions**
  * **3 highest mountains:** Scafell Pike 978 meters, Scafell 964 meters, Helvellyn 950 meters
  * **3 largest lakes,** Windemere (10 miles long) 14.8km2 , Ullswater 8.9km2, Derwentwater 5.5km2
* **Things to do**
  * 3,100km right of way paths
  * 48 routes for people with limited mobility
  * Guided walks, cycles and boats
  * Canoeing, kayaking, steam cruises
  * Historical sites (stone circles, Roman remains and Tudor manor houses

**Growth**

* **2018:** £1.48 billion spent, 19.5 million tourist visitors, 28.6 million tourist days
* **Growth over time:** 5% year on year growth, 46 million visitors expected in 2035, 14.8 million visitors in 2012
* **Reasons for growth**
  * Social media (44% of business owners say it has had positive affect)
  * Rise in desire for a luxury holiday without having to go abroad
  * M6 nearby
  * Ease of accessibility in a motor car
* **Infrastructure**
  * 89% of people arrive by car
  * Narrow, winding roads
  * Poor/unused public transport
  * Heavy boat use for lake travel
  * M6 nearby
  * Heysham port 30 miles away
* **History**
  * Beatrix Potter owned property and land in the Lake District,  used profits from Peter Rabbit books to buy land to preserve
  * Prior to the rise of cheap holidays abroad, the Lake District was an immensely popular destination for people all over the UK to holiday to

**Impacts of Tourism:** ‘a victim of it’s own success?’

**Environmental**

* **Positives**
  * 87% of people do stick to footpaths, limiting their reach - but this does not spread out the load
  * If National Park powers increase, the environment can be managed in a much more sustainable way
  * 10mph boat speed limit imposed on Windemere - reducing swash and erosional effects of boats disturbing water and wildlife
  * Organisations such as Friends of the Lake District promote conservation and sustainable tourism - to combat economy led tourism
  * Calls for Ambleside bypass to reduce congestion pressures on small town
  * Tree preservation orders prevent certain areas of forestry from being cut down and used for others
* **Negatives**
  * 4 million people walk an average of 6km each year - causing intense path erosion (grown 4x in size)
  * 89% of visitors come by car, causing air and noise pollution + congestion on narrow roads not built for excessive car numbers
  * Ministry of Defence flyovers cause severe noise pollution multiple times a week
  * Litter
  * Landscape blotting from car parks and shopping centres
  * Oils and fuels from boats pollute waters
  * Carrying capacity is exceeded in summer months - evidenced by congestion and varying types of pollution

**Economic**

* **Positives**
  * Visitors provide 38,000 full-time job posts (total tourism jobs 65,000)
  * Big businesses such as Hayes garden Centre (1.5million visitors) bring many people to the area.
  * Aviation enthusiasts may come to the Lake District just to see RAF flyovers
  * 1000s of cars per day need to be parked - an opportunity for pay-and-display car parks
* **Negatives**
  * High average house prices, £255,000 with average income £27,000
  * Seasonal interest as high rainfall in winter >100mm/month puts people off
  * Service decline as businesses not sustainable due to only seasonal interests, or even less than seasonal where 2nd homeownership >20%
  * Overall tourism number fell by 0.5% in 2018 but revenue up 3.7% to £3bn
  * If National Park powers increase, income will fall

**Social**

* **Positives**
  * Low unemployment rate of 1%
  * Elderly dependents account for 30% of the population
  * Haile has seen its population rise steeply by 400 in last 50 years
  * Attracting visitors allows education on important environmental issues
  * 6 local authorities and 8 housing associations set up to provide affordable homes
* **Negatives**
  * Significant lack of affordable housing - low house price is £183,000 for an income of £14,200.
  * 17.7% second homeownership. >20% affects village sustainability (51% in Coniston).
  * Loss of sense of community as the area becomes increasingly money-driven
  * Troutbeck is a ghost town due to 2nd homeownership

**Management Strategies/Solutions:** the Lake District has been described by a journalist as “a car park with puddles”

**Poor footpaths:** ‘Fix the Fells’ (Helvellyn) repair footpaths

* **Advantages**
  * Stone pitching (digging stones vertically into the ground) -hard-wearing, traditional technique, suitable on steep gradients
  * Reseeding (fertiliser and grass seed mix) - hard-wearing, low maintenance, only £20 per metre, comfortable and blends in well
  * Education may reduce footpath wear
* **Disadvantages**
  * Stone pitching - may encourage more walkers, £100 per meter, can be uncomfortable and requires stones
  * Reseeding - requires skilled drivers and machinery, can’t be used on 15-degree slopes, may not fully establish quickly
  * Education requires good communication

**Congestion:** land use zoning

* **Advantages**
  * Reduces conflicts in land use between loud and violent users and environmentalists
  * Encourages the spreading out of activities (honeypot sites are dispersed, reducing congestion)
* **Disadvantages**
  * Spreading out services and activities will reduce an area's appeal and groups of people may not want to be moved around

**Congestion:** one way

* **Advantages**
  * Cheap enough to do, as only some signage and road markings need to be changed
  * Has the capacity to reduce two-way traffic volume, so flow should increase
* **Disadvantages**
  * Frustrates locals as they will not like change
  * RTCs as people adjust

**Congestion:** Hawshead bypass

* **Advantages**
  * Provide a direct route that doesn’t cut through busy town centres, alleviating pressure and congestion
  * Higher speed limits allow greater traffic flow and locals/tourists happy as traffic volume reduced in towns
* **Disadvantages**
  * Requires large amounts of land. Therefore, plenty of opposition from environmentalists due to the damage to habitats and lost wildlife
  * Cost an awful lot of money so the cost of the project may not be fully justified

**Congestion:** parking restrictions (Hawkshead 2 hours)

* **Advantages**
  * May involve the provision of more parking spaces too
  * **Resident-only** passes will reduce cars parking on pavements/roadsides/popular places which directly contributes to congestion
* **Disadvantages**
  * Hard to police (would require constant surveillance)
  * Car parks require expensive refitting with technology and expansion projects need large capital and space/land

**Congestion:** public transport (‘Drive less, see more’)

* **Advantages**
  * £7 million funding granted in 2012 for 3-year ‘Drive Less, See More’ scheme
  * Unifies BBBB network throughout the national park to provide unified non-car travel
  * Aims to reduce 11,000 CO2 tonnes
* **Disadvantages**
  * Requires a unified payment system, good frequency, standard experience across operators and a benefit to the locals - all of which are hard to achieve for success

**High visitor numbers:** toll roads

* **Advantages**
  * Forcing people to pay has the potential to deter some visitors if placed along busiest entry roads to National Park
  * May create extra revenue for other management projects
* **Disadvantages**
  * If cost is too high, visitor numbers will drop significantly and tourism will reduce to critical levels in the Lake District - job loss occurs
  * If priced too low, the scheme will not deter most, and no extra income produced

**High visitor numbers:** increase product TAX

* **Advantages**
  * Earns extra revenue for small businesses and local economy, slightly higher prices, or a ‘Lake District Tax’ to be applied on certain days may act as a deterrent for tourists
* **Disadvantages**
  * Scheme is potentially unfair if taxation only occurs on certain days, if too high of a percentage, people will stop buying products and the area will earn a bad reputation

**2nd homes:** mandatory occupation time

* **Advantages**
  * Ensures that residents use the home as their primary residence, directly fighting the 2nd home issue
* **Disadvantages**
  * Hard to enforce, as how do you check if someone occupies a home for exactly half a year or more?

**2nd homes:** locals only housing (cheap council rent)

* **Advantages**
  * Could be focussed on young people/families, via a permit or grant scheme
  * Would address the issue of 2nd home buyers purchasing all properties
* **Disadvantages**
  * Scheme may be discriminatory, or hard to manage - may be easy to fiddle paperwork when proving someone has lived somewhere for a set amount of time

**Seasonal jobs:** farming diversification

* **Advantages**
  * Allows farms to earn income through farming during that season, and tourism during the height of that season
  * Farm shops, tours and animal experiences provide jobs and revenue
* **Disadvantages**
  * Doesn’t solve a problem for those without farms
  * Seasonal employment is still an issue

**Property prices:** build more low-cost homes (Hawkshead rent)

* **Advantages**
  * Offer exclusively to locals, helps reduce house prices of £255,000 average
* **Disadvantages**
  * Expensive for local council to do with little funding
  * Demand/supply may not be met, and would require good maintenance to keep good

**Honeypot management:** pedestrianised zones

* **Advantages**
  * Pedestrianizing honeypot sites (Ambleside/Keswick) eliminates cars and risks to humans
  * Area becomes calmer, less air pollution and more people walking in/out of shops
* **Disadvantages**
  * Lack of travel to an area may reduce revenue and footfall
  * Area may lose honeypot status

**Boating lakes:** Windemere 10mph limit

* **Advantages**
  * Reduced erosional damage caused by swash, noise pollution and increased safety
* **Disadvantages**
  * Ruins the fun for motorboats, may reduce tourism is boat leisure not fun
