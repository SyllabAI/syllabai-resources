## Motivators at Work

### **Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivation**

* **Intrinsic (internal):** motivation that comes from within by the actual performance of the task rather than the consequences of it.
  * It includes factors like enjoyment, sense of satisfaction, feeling of contribution, achievements, etc.
* **Extrinsic (external):** motivation from consequences of the task, e.g. money, promotion and bonuses.

### **Reward Systems**

* It can include pay, bonuses, profit sharing and performance-related pay.
  * **Pay** can be linked to performance- the harder you work or, the faster you complete tasks, the higher pay you will receive.
  * **Bonuses**: additions to salaries.
  * **Profit sharing:** a percentage of the profit is shared by all the workers. It gives a sense of belonging and increases motivation.
* There are different views of monetary rewards are successful in improving productivity, i.e.:
  * A paper by **de Waal and Jansen (2011)** summarizes a number of research findings.
    * They cited studies demonstrating that over half the growth in productivity in Chinese State industries could be attributed to the use of bonuses (Yao, 1997) and studies demonstrating the positive effects of performance-related pay (Belfield & Marsden, 2005).
    * However, de Waal and Jensen also include contradictory evidence as they cite studies which demonstrate that in organisations with very high inequalities, there is also a very high turnover of staff, which was also true in another study.
    * This suggests that the costs to the low performers outweigh any gains in productivity shown by the high performers.
    * There were also other studies where there was no relationship between the size of bonus payments and performance.
  * Research in many organisations in the UK and Holland found no relationship between bonus payments and performance.

### **Non-Monetary Rewards**

* Includes praise, respect, recognition, empowerment and a sense of belonging.
* Reward and recognition are different.
  * **Rewards** are promised from the start; this affects your extrinsic motivation as you work for a salary.
  * **Recognition** is not promised from the start but rather when a worker is recognised for their contributions or achievements. E.g., a letter of thanks from the employer for a job well done, ‘employee of the month’, etc.
* Achievement of a difficult task or completion of a task can lead to a feeling of empowerment.
* **Rose (1998)** estimates that around 75% of organisations in the UK had some form of non-monetary recognition scheme, which was mostly in organisations with customer contact.
* Such schemes can highlight desired behaviours and, through recognition, create role models for others to imitate, and they are often low-cost as well.
* Evidence also suggests that staff turnover can be positively affected.

### **Evaluation**

* This might help you find what motivates you and what job you want to aim for.
* It will also be helpful to organisations as they would find out what motivates their workers.
* It can be considered a **behaviourist** approach to motivation as it considers the effect of certain outcomes (reinforcements) on behaviour rather than focusing on cognitive aspects.
* Lack of empirical (observational) evidence, but still, all these ideas can be tested experimentally to find the effectiveness of different rewards.

### **Individual vs Situational**

* There are individual differences; what motivates one person won’t motivate the other.
* Someone working in a highly creative environment is more likely to be motivated by non-monetary rewards compared to a competitive business environment.
