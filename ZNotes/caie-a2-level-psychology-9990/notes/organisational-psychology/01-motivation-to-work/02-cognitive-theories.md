## Cognitive Theories

### **Goal-Setting Theory (Latham and Locke, 1984)**

* Locke and Latham based their theory on Ryan's (1970) claims, which stated that “human behaviour is affected by conscious purposes, plans, intentions, tasks, etc”.
* According to Locke (1980), goal setting is a key motivator for people to work hard and improve their performance.
* Specific goals are more effective than vague goals as they are challenging and will compel workers to work harder.
* **5 key principles for goal setting theory (4C F):**
  * **Clarity:** goals should be clear, specific, unambiguous and measurable.
  * **Challenge:** should be relevant and linked to rewards.
  * **Complexity:** should be achievable within a specific time period.
  * **Commitment:** must be understood and accepted.
  * **Feedback:** must involve feedback on task progress and achievement.
    * Feedback allows one to identify weaknesses and find more efficient, easier and quicker ways to achieve the goal.
    * Feedback should be positive and constructive, focusing on the strategies used.
    * It should also involve reflection by the individual rather than an authority's feedback.
  * **SMART Goal:**
    * **S**pecific
    * **M**easurable
    * **A**ttainable
    * **R**elevant
    * **T**imescale
* The final method of goal setting is ‘Backward Goal Setting’, in which the individual works backwards to determine the most appropriate way of reaching this goal.

### **VIE (expectancy) Theory (Vroom,1964)**

* This theory states that workers are rational, and their decisions are guided by logical thought processes where potential costs and rewards play a significant role.
* He believes that performance can be influenced by factors such as knowledge, skills, experience and individual characteristics like personality, different ambitions and goals.
* **According to Vroom, workers can be motivated if:**
  * There is a clear relationship between the effort and performance.
  * The favourable performance is rewarded.
  * The reward satisfies a need.
  * The desire to satisfy the need is strong enough to make the effort worthwhile.
* **He proposed the equation:**

Motivation = expectancy  *instrumentality*  valence

* **Expectancy** is the perception of how effort relates to performance and the worker’s confidence level in their capability of achieving the task.
  * It can be modified using additional resources or training and supervision.
* **Instrumentality** is the perception of how much effort will be rewarded and whether workers believe that they will be awarded. It is positively affected if the management ensures the reward is given as promised.
* **Valence** is the perception of the strength/size of the reward as well as the extent to which this reward is needed or wanted.

### **Equity Theory (Adams, 1963)**

* Application of social psychology where **Social Exchange Theory** predicts that people do a cost-benefit analysis before doing a task.
* It states that workers want fair treatment and expect pay, status and recognition to equal the effort. However, if we perceive others as treated better, the perceived inequality will lead to demotivation.
* Moreover, workers can bring certain inputs (I) to the job like skills, qualifications, energy, enthusiasm and effort.
  * It may be perceived input (Ip) or actual (Ia).
* In turn, workers expect outcomes (O) from their jobs like pay, recognition, involvement and other benefits.
  * It may be a perceived outcome (Op) or an actual (Oa).
* The key proposal of the theory is that workers compare themselves to others to check the **equity** (fairness) of their positions.
* Inequity, however can lead to low motivation and has two types:
  * **Underpayment:** when you are not paid as much as you deserve or as someone else in the same post.
    * To bring it to equity, you might increase the outcomes of your job, e.g., ask for a raise or decrease your input.
    * Alternatively, you can change your cognition by concluding that the comparison made was inappropriate and should instead compare yourself with someone else. Or distort the way the inputs and outcomes are perceived.
* **Overpayment:** when you are being paid more than you deserve or compared to someone else in the same post.
  * You may increase input by working harder, which will unlikely decrease your outcomes, such as a pay cut.
  * Alternatively, the same cognitive reasoning and distortion can be used as underpayment.

### **Evaluation**

* All three theories support the individual explanation
* The theories have high **ecological validity** but will be difficult to generalise to all organisations due to the many different variables involved in each.
