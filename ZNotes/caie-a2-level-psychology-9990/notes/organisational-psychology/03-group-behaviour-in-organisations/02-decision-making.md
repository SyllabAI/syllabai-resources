## Decision-making

### The decision-making process (Wedley and Field, 1984)

* Wedley and Field propose that managers should be encouraged to pre-plan the decision making process as once started, it is difficult to stop.
  * Pre-planning includes: type of leadership to use, whether to involve others, how to gather information, who to contact etc.
* **Stages of (rational problem solving) decision making:**
  * Identify the problem/opportunity
  * Formulate your objective(s)
  * Familiarise yourself with the problem
  * Generate alternative solutions
  * Evaluate the alternatives
  * Choose the best alternative
  * Implement the alternative
  * Follow up with the evaluation of the results
* A decision support system (DDS) is a computer based system that supports decision making.

### Group think (Janis, 1971) and strategies to avoid groupthink

* A psychological phenomenon within a group wherein the desire for harmony in the group results in an irrational decision making outcome.
* **Symptoms of group think (Janis 1971):**
  * **Illusions of invulnerability:** members of the group believe that they can do no wrong and can never be in any sort of trouble. This leads to optimistic thinking about outcomes and encourages risky behaviours.
  * Unquestioned beliefs
  * **Rationalising**: the group members ignore warning signs and assume everything will be okay.
  * **Stereotyping:** if anyone tries to raise concern or questions about the risky decision, they will be ignored and labelled as part of the 'outgroup.'
  * **Self-censorship:** members are less likely to listen to their own doubts as it appears that no one else has any doubts, thus everyone is convinced there’s nothing to worry about. It is similar to ‘pluralistic ignorance’ seen in bystanders – since no one is responding, there’s no real emergency.
  * **Mind guards:** even if we are worried, we hide our worry. If everyone does this, it can lead to risky behaviours.
  * **Direct pressure to conform** to those that disagree – they are made to appear as though they are being disloyal and traitorous by asking questions.
* **Benefits of group think:** in large groups, decisions, tasks and projects are completed quickly and efficiently.
* **Causes:**
  * When group members are similar to one another
  * Presence of an extremely charismatic leader
  * High levels of stress
  * Morally challenging situations
* **Strategies to reduce the risk of group think:**
  * Give members the opportunity to express themselves
  * Break up members into smaller independent groups
  * The leader should not state their views at the start of a discussion
  * Have someone take the role of the ‘devil’s advocate’

### Cognitive limitations and errors (Forsyth, 2006)

* **Sins of Commission:** misuse of information in the decision making process. This may involve:
  * **Belief perseverance:** using information that is known to be inaccurate
  * **Sunk cost fallacy:** members may remain committed to a flawed plan due to the time/money already invested in it
  * **Extra evidentiary bias:** choosing to use information despite being told to ignore it
  * **Hindsight bias:** falsely overestimating the importance of past knowledge/experience
* **Sins of Omission:** overlooking key information.
  * **Base rate bias:** overlooking basic relevant info
  * Attribution errors are made when members make decisions based on inaccurate assessments of an individual’s behaviour
* **Sins of Imprecision:** relying too heavily on heuristics (shortcuts) that oversimplify complex decisions.
  * **Availability heuristic:** over reliance on information that’s readily available
  * **Conjunctiva bias:** failure to consider relationships between events
  * **Representativeness heuristic:** heavy reliance on decision making factors that appear meaningful but are actually misleading.
