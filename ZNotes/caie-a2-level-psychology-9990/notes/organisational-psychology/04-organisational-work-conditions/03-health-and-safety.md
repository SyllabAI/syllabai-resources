## Health and safety

### Accidents at work: errors and accidents in operator-machine systems (Nagel, 1988)

### **Background**

* A popular case of human error happened at the Three Mile Island power plant in the USA in 1979.
* An employee left an alternate feedwater pipe switched off, leaving the reactor to overheat.
* It took 2 hours and 18 minutes to find the fault as over 1600 gauges had to be examined to find the fault.
* However, other than human error, there were several errors in the system itself eg.  a colour sometimes represented safety whereas in another part it meant danger.
* Since then, machines have been made more user friendly, operators take regular breaks and systems have been tried and tested as emergency procedures.

### Reducing accidents at work (Fox et. al, 1987)

* Investigated the use of token system to reward workers for not having accidents or injuries for a specified amount of time.
* The study was conducted at two open pit mines using the same mining procedures and their associated product processing plants in USA.
* The two mines used the same mining procedures and were of similar sizes.
* Prior to the study, the number of absences from work due to injuries was significantly higher than the national average.

### **Sample**

* Participants are the employees in the two mines including office and clerical workers, engineers, managers, custodial, maintenance and production workers.
* Employees in first mine were ultimately 214 Employees in second mine were 501 in 1983.

### **Procedure**

* **Workers were divided into four groups based on the numbers of working days lost due to injury:**
  * **G1**- workers in least hazardous jobs (office workers)
  * **G2**- foremen, shift supervisors, technicians, engineers and surveyors
  * **G3**- mechanics, labourers, maintenance workers and operators of bulldozers, etc
  * **G4**- workers in most hazardous jobs (electricians, scraper operators and fuel and lobe workers)
* **Workers were given ‘trading stamps’ along with their pay if:**
  * They didn’t suffer lost time injuries or injury that required doctors’ attention during the month.
  * They prevented damage to property
  * If they prevented another person from injury
  * If their entire group prevented injuries (group reward)
  * As the difficulty of the job increased, so did the number of trading stamps.
* **No stamps due to:**
  * Days lost due to injury. E.g day or two’s work resulted in no stamps for a month.
    * As the number of days lost increased, so did the duration of no stamps for a maximum of 6 months.
  * Damage to property (loss of stamps for a max of 12 months)
  * Failing to report an injury (loss of stamps for 1 month).
  * The group would lose group rewards for as many months as the individual lost his individual awards.
* Stamps could be ‘spent’ at local stores and could be exchanged for anything from a huge range of merchandise.
* The miners had to record some data including the frequency of accidents and total numbers of days lost (severity).
* In addition, direct costs of injuries and accidents were also recorded along with the cost of trading stamps to calculate a cost to benefit ratio.

### **Results**

* There were significant decreases in the number of days lost in both mines in the first year.
* Costs of accidents and injuries in both the mines declined by approximately 90%.

### **Conclusion**

* Days lost from work, accidents and injuries decreased and no deaths or permanent disabilities occurred at either mine during the token economy.
* Anecdotal evidence suggests that these stamps were greatly appreciated by all workers regardless of being sceptical in the beginning.

### Safety promotion campaigns (Cowpe, 1989)

* At time of research, chip-pan fires were a major cause of domestic fires in the UK and had heavy associated costs.

### **Aim**

* To test the effectiveness of the campaigns shown on tv which was measured using fire brigade statistics (quasi-experiment).
  * Two 60 sec advertisements were produced showing cause of fire (overfilling and leaving unattended) and actions to contain them.

### **Procedure**

* Two strategies were considered for Cowpe’s advertising campaign.
  * **Prevention strategy**: involved an advert showing how to avoid chip-pan fires. E.g: don’t overfill the pan with oil, don’t leave the pan unattended.
  * **Containment strategy**: people would be educated about how to deal with fires.
* The prevention strategy was rejected as it would be unsuccessful and ignored (‘this won’t happen to me’) whereas the containment strategy would raise awareness and be effective.

### **Results**

* There was an overall 12% drop in fires along with higher levels of awareness and recall of the adverts for extended periods.

### **Conclusion**

* Adverts were effectiveness in reducing in chip-pan fires however the effectiveness of either strategy in the advert was unknown.
* Cowpe suggested that the adverts increased knowledge and confidence but the combination of the two strategies helped reduce fires, save money and save lives.

### **Evaluation**

**Fox et al**

* Application of token system is simple **behaviourist** which offers consistent and predictable rewards to desired behaviour (safety training).
* Its very useful as the number of accidents reduced and the worker safety increased although it is argued that this approach is over-controlling and **unethical**.
* The study shows that the way the working environment (**situation**) is created can have significant effects on the behaviour of workers.

**Cowpe**

* As the study used an experimental approach, the cause and effect were easily measured but it was difficult to control extraneous variables.
* There was high ecological validity as the participants were members of the general public who watched tv adverts. However, it’s impossible to tell who was affected and whether the advert specifically helped prevent a fire.
* Over using media campaigns reduce effect and awareness as people become desensitised to the message
