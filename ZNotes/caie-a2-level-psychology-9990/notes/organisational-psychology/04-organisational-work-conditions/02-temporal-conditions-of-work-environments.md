## Temporal conditions of work environments

### Shift work (Pheasant, 1991)

* Many people work in ‘shifts’, meaning their working pattern does not involve the same work pattern every week.

**Types of shifts:**

* **Day shift:** typically between 6am-2pm.
* **Afternoon/twilight shift:** typically between 2pm- 10pm.
* **Night shift:** typically between 10pm-6am.
* Shift work should be managed efficiently to avoid negative effects.

**Pheasant identified two main approaches:**

* **Rapid rotation shifts:** frequent shift changes.
  * **Metropolitan rotas**: pattern of 2 day shifts + 2 afternoon shifts + 2 night shifts + 2 days off. As this is an 8-day pattern, it moves one day ahead each week.
  * **Continental rotas** pattern of 2 day shifts + 2 afternoon shifts + 3 night shifts + 2 days off followed by 2 day shifts + 3 afternoon shifts + 2 night shifts + 3 days off.
* **Slow rotation shifts:** infrequent changes e.g: day shifts for multiple weeks then night shifts for multiple weeks.
  * This allows circadian rhythms (physical, mental, and behavioural changes that follow a daily cycle) to adapt to one shift without feeling forced or causing health problems.

### The effects of shift work on health (Knutsson, 2003)

* Knutsson reviewed the effects of shift work highlighting the relationship between working at night or a shift work pattern and specific medical disorders.
* According to him, shift work doesn’t affect longevity.
* Two studies directly compared mortality rates between day and night workers.
  * The study conducted in UK showed no significant difference and the one conducted in Denmark showed a small increase in relative death risk for shift workers.

**The specific disorders and conditions include:**

* **Gastrointestinal disease:**
  * More common in shift workers than day workers.
  * Mostly constipation and diarrhoea experienced by night shift workers.
  * Peptic ulcers are common for regular shift workers like drivers, factory workers etc.
  * Risk of duodenal ulcers are doubled in shift workers.
* **Cardiovascular disease:**
  * Physical working conditions like chemicals, noise and vibrations along with psycho-social factors such as stress and work schedules can contribute to cardiovascular diseases.
  * Multiple studies support relationship between shift work and cardiovascular disease.
* **Cancer:**
  * Increased risk of breast cancer in women who work night shifts.
  * Study conducted with nurses, flight attendants and radio and telegraph operators.
  * However, exposure to other carcinogens cannot be controlled.
  * No conclusive evidence on increased risk of cancer due to shift work.
* **Diabetes and other metabolic disturbances:**
  * Certain substances have higher concentration during night work like potassium, uric acid, glucose and cholesterol which can increase metabolic disturbances.
  * Some studies show higher BMIs and risk of diabetes in those working shift work.
* **Pregnancy:**
  * Existing relationship between shift work and low birth weight as well as shift work and premature birth.
  * Increased risk of miscarriage among shift workers.
* **Exacerbation of existing disorders:**
  * Biological processes can be interrupted by shift work indicating that taking medicines can be more complex during shift work.
  * Taking same dose at same time can cause different effects due to differences in internal body clock.
  * Sleep deprivation can also affect existing disorders such as frequency of seizures experienced by epileptics and frequency of asthma attacks.

### Shift work and accidents (Gold et al., 1992)

* Gold et al. conducted a self-administered questionnaire in Massachusetts regarding shift work, sleep and accidents in 1986.

**Sample**

* 878 nurses
* Day and evening shift work was grouped together as it doesn’t show to disrupt circadian rhythms.
* Other groups were night shift workers and ‘rotators’ (who change patterns frequently).

**Procedure**

* Questions asked:
  * Whether they worked variable shifts or same shift (asked about current week, previous 2 weeks and following week on the number of day, evening or night shifts worked)
  * Sleep and wake times for all shifts and for days off
  * Quality of sleep
  * Use of alcohol
  * Use of prescription or non-prescription medication
  * Sleeping aids
  * Times they had ‘nodded off’ (fallen asleep) at work (in the past week)
  * Times they had ‘nodded off’ while driving to and from work (in the past year)
  * Accidents, errors and ‘near-miss’ accidents in the past year: includes driving accidents, medication errors, job procedural errors and work-related personal injuries that could be attributed to sleepiness.

**Results**

* Out of 878 questionnaires, 687 returned. Mean age of sample was 33.9 years.
* Rotators and night shift workers had fewer hours of sleep than day/evening workers
* 92% of day/evening nurses,6.3% night nurses obtained regular anchor sleep. (4 or more hours of sleep during the same clock hours every night).
* Anchor sleep disruption was experienced by 49% of day/evening workers, 94% of rotators and 2.9% of night nurses.
* Night nurses were 1.8 times more likely to report poor quality sleep than day/evening nurses and rotators were 2.8 times more likely to report poor quality sleep than day/evening workers. Night nurses and rotators were twice as likely to use medications to help them sleep.
* Nodding off on the night shift occurred at least once a week in 35% of rotators, 32% of night nurses and 20% of day evening workers who worked the occasional night shift, in contrast, only 2.7% of day/evening nurses and 2.8% of rotators reported any incidences of nodding off on day or evening shifts.
* When compared to day/evening nurses, rotators were 3.9 times as likely and night nurses 3.6 times as likely to nod off while driving to and from work in the previous year.
* Rotators reported twice as many accidents as day/evening nurses including factors like length of time working, age and use of alcohol.
* Results are consistent with laboratory demonstrations of the effects of sleep deprivation and the disruption of circadian rhythms considering increased cognitive errors.
* These principles of work schedule will improve health and safety for nurses and patients

### **Evaluation**

**Issues and debates**

* **Nature versus nurture debate-** shift work might interfere with a person’s natural circadian (daily) rhythm and affect them negatively.
* All researches are very useful applications. Workers will be informed about these risks and can work accordingly to minimise risks.

**Research methods**

* The study by Knutsson was a correlation which doesn’t imply causation; hence, an experiment would lead to more valid results but would raise ethical issues
* The study by Gold relies heavily on self-reports and can be described as **quasi-experimental** as he was able to compare two naturally occurring groups, thus creating an **independent variable**.
  * He was not able to randomly allocate participants to these different groups meaning that there may be many differences between them explaining the results.
  * However, the research findings are supported by laboratory experiments into short-term sleep deprivation and cognitive errors.
  * There might be reporting bias and a longitudinal study might be more effective as it will track errors and accidents that can be correlated with shift work patterns.
