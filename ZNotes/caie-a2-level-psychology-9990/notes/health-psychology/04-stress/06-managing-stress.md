## Managing Stress

### **Medical Techniques (Biochemical)**

* Commonly used as a quick and easy way to deal with stress, especially in short term
* Anti-depressants that are most widely prescribed are **selective
  serotonergic reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)** such as Prozac
  * These work by regulating serotonin levels in the brain to
    elevate mood and have been used to treat panic disorder, GAD,
    and OCD
* **Benzodiazepines** are used to treat anxiety and work by releasing
  inhibitory transmitters
  * **Inhibitory Transmitter** – makes brain less aroused, more calm
    and less anxious
* **Kahn et al. (1986)**
  * I gave a group of patients benzodiazepines as well as a placebo to a control group
  * They followed 250 patients for eight weeks
  * They found that benzodiazepines reduced stress significantly
    more than the placebo

### **Psychological Techniques: Biofeedback (Budzynski et al., 1969)**

* **Biofeedback**: a technique in which electromechanical device monitors physiological processes such as heart rate or muscle tension
* The device reports the information
* The information enables the person to gain voluntary control over
  these processes through operant conditioning
* **Principles of biofeedback:**
  * The patient has electromyography (EMG) electrodes attached to
    the skin surface over a particular muscle
  * The patient hears a tone with a pitch proportional to the
    electromyographic activity in a muscle group
  * As the patient gets better, they have to maintain their
    relaxation level.
* **Budzynski et al. (1969)** wanted to assess the effect of biofeedback in reducing tension headaches
* Tension headaches are associated with sustained contraction of the scalp and neck muscles
* Each participant had electrodes attached to their head, one inch
  above the eye across their forehead
* There were three conditions, and each of the 15 participants were randomly allocated to one of these conditions
* The experimental group were told that the pitch of the tone would vary with the level of muscle tension on the forehead
* Control groups were told to relax deeply, especially the forehead
  muscle
* They were told that the constant tone should help them relax and
  deeply as possible and to do this in silence
* The results were gathered over five sessions, and the mean level of muscle tension
* They found that participants in the feedback group saw a significant reduction in muscle tension over five sessions

### **Psychological Techniques: Imagery (Bridge, 1988)**

* Involves the systematic practice of creating a detailed an attractive and peaceful setting or environment
* There is no single correct way to use imagery for stress relief
* Used to promote relaxation, which can lower blood pressure and reduce other problems related to stress
* **Bridge et al. (1988)** wanted to see if stress could be alleviated in patients being treated with radiotherapy for early breast cancer using randomized control trials (RCT) lasting for 6 weeks
* A sample of 139 women at Middlesex Hospital, London
* Patients completed the Leeds General Scales Measure, which gives the
  severity of depressive and anxiety symptoms in patients who have not
  receive a primary diagnosis of an affective illness
* They also completed a mood scale, the profile of mood that uses 65 items to assess depression, vigour, fatigue, tension, anger or confusion
* Participants were randomly allocated into either three conditions,
  **relaxation, relaxation plus imagery** or a **control condition**
* There were no significant differences in the Leeds General Scales but the mood disturbance score on the profile of mood states differed significantly from the predicted way
* This indicates that relaxation positively affected mood state and
  that this effect was further increased when relaxation was combined
  with imagery

### **Preventing Stress (Meichenbaum, 1985)**

* Proposed Stress Inoculation Therapy which follows three stages:
  * **Conceptualisation**: education emphasising the development of a warm, collaborative relationship through which a careful assessment and problem reconceptualization are finished.
  * **Skills acquisition and rehearsal**: coping strategies, as well as the patient learns new skills to apply in stressful situations. These can include relaxation and emotional regulation skills
  * **Application and follow through**: focuses on transferring coping mechanisms to real life and prevent relapse
