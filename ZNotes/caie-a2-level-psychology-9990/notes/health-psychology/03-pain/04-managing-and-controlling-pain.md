## Managing and Controlling Pain

### Medical Techniques

- Surgical treatments are used whenever medications do not relieve
  pain

- Such treatments can involve cutting nerve pathways or making lesions
  in certain areas in the brain

- Surgical treatment is usually recommended for people with terminal
  illness

- There are also several physical therapies that can be used to ease
  and manage pain

<!-- end list -->

- These include:

  - **Manual Therapies** such as massages

  - **Mechanical Therapies** such as ultrasound

  - **Heat Treatments** such as microwave diathermy

  - **Cold Treatments** such as ice packs

  - **Electrotherapy** such as electrical nerve stimulation

<!-- end list -->

- **Biochemical treatments** such as analgesics and painkillers can be
  used to manage pain.

- An analgesic or pain killer is any member of a group of drugs used
  to achieve analgesia

  - **Analgesia** – relief from pain

- Analgesics act in various ways on the central nervous systems

- The type of medicine to use depends on the location of the pain

- All painkillers have side effects

- For pain associated with inflammation and headaches, paracetamol or
  anti-inflammatory pain killers.

- Pain caused by sensitive nerves can be treated with depression
  medications

- The most popular chemical treatment is aspirin.

- Aspirin and other similar drugs such as ibuprofen have three
  therapeutic actions:

  - Against pain

  - Against inflammation

  - Against fevers

- They work on the damaged tissue causing the pain

- They also have no known effect to the nervous system

- The only drawbacks of aspirin is the number of side effects such as
  gastric irritation and bleeding, as well as deafness

- Opiates inhibit pain messages from raveling to the brain

- They close the gate, preventing pain signals from getting to the
  brain

- Morphine and similar drugs such as fentanyl are the strongest
  painkillers

- Some medications come in a patch but they all work in similar ways

- Opiates can only be prescribed after consultation with a general
  practitioner and dosages would be closely monitored

### Psychological techniques: cognitive strategies

- There has been a growing acceptance of the value of psychological
  interventions

- These can include relaxation, biofeedback, cognitive coping skills,
  mental imaging and counselling

- **Cognitive redefinition** is where an individual attempts to alter
  their thinking to replace the thought of apprehension

- This approach is related to distraction therapies

- Cognitive redefinition involves the patient replacing pain-related
  thoughts with more positive thoughts

- **Attention management** is often included in cognitive behavioral
  treatments (CBT)

- Patients may have different responses to attention strategies and
  individual differences in what diversion techniques are effective as
  pain relief methods

- The core method of attention diversion to divert attention from pain
  by refocusing or directing attention to something unrelated to pain

- According to the Gate Control Theory, attention diversion can help
  close the gates and reduce the perceptions

- Deliberately using imagery, attention diversion and mindfulness to
  cope with pain can take many forms

- Patients suffering from chronic pain reportedly claim that engaging
  in an enjoyable activity is the most effective method of diversion

<!-- end list -->

- **Morley, Shapiro and Biggs (2004)** developed a training program
  which taught patients how to use attention diversion strategies such
  as:

  - Usage of imagery and mindfulness: creating a vivid and pleasant
    image to concentrate

  - Basic attention management: brief relaxation, internal and
    external attention focus

  - Pain coping strategies: coping self-statements, signal breathing

  - Discussion on dealing pain and relationship to pain and its
    chronic nature

  - Attention diversion and mindfulness: focus on the details of the
    experience, mindfulness, breathing exercises

  - Intense pain and flare-ups

  - Pain transformation images

<!-- end list -->

- In the first time period until a three month follow up, pain
  intensity reduced significantly

***<span class="underline">Alternative Techniques</span>***

- **Acupuncture**

  - Is derived from Ancient Chinese medicine

  - Fine needles are inserted into certain sites of the body for
    therapeutic or preventative purpose

  - Western acupuncture is the use of acupuncture after a medical
    diagnosis

  - Research has shown that acupuncture can stimulate nerves under
    the skin and in muscle tissue

  - This leads into the production of pain-relieving substances such
    as endorphins

  - Traditional acupuncture is based on the belief of Qi

  - **Qi** is said to be a ‘life force’ that flows through the body
    in channels called meridians

  - When Qi cannot flow freely in the body, sickness can result

  - They believe that acupuncture can restore the flow of Qi

<!-- end list -->

- **Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS)**

  - Battery-operated device that has leads connected to sticky pads
    called **electrodes**

  - When the machine is switched on, the electrodes passes small
    impulses to areas of the body where you experience muscle pain

  - These impulses can reduce pain signals passing along nerves and
    can help the individual relax

  - It is also suggested that electric impulses can stimulate
    endorphins

  - Health care professionals have reported that TENS can help some
    people, although it depends on the individual and the condition
    being treated

### **Issues & Debates:**

- Methods of controlling pain must not be reductionist

- For example, the biomedical approach explains and treats pain in
  a simplistic way

- The most useful application that comes from our understanding of
  pain relief will come from combining both physiological and
  psychological techniques.

- Cognitive techniques are the most useful, as patients can apply
  these techniques anywhere.
