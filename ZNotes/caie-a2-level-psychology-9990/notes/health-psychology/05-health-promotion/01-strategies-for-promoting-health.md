# Health Promotion

## Strategies for Promoting Health

* This is a report of a study conducted by **Cowpe (1989)**
* Chip-pan fires were the major cause of domestic fires and accidents
  in 1976-1984
* Approximately 1/3 of all domestic fires were caused by chippans
* This study aimed to test the effectiveness of an advertising campaign warning people about Chip pan fires
* This can be understood as a quasi-experiment
* Two sixty-second advertisements involving the usage of chip pans were made, as well as advertisements dedicated to avoiding chip pan fires.
* These advertisements were screened on UK Television between 1976 and 1984
* Ten regions were shown the advertisements
* The effectiveness of these campaigns was measured through the usage of fire brigade statistics.
* Cowpe reports an overall 12% decrease in fires (7-25% depending on
  area)
* High awareness levels and recall of the adverts existed for a
  considerable time
* He concluded that the advertising campaign proved effective, as shown by the reduction of chip pan fires.

### **Fear Arousal (Janis and Feshbach, 1953)**

* Aimed to test if a level of fear would have a behavioural consequence in relation to tooth-brushing
* Four groups consisting of 50 students each were shown a film about
  dental hygiene.
* Depending on their group, they either received a strong fear
  message, a moderate fear message or a low fear message involving
  tooth decay
* An additional control group screened a video involving the functions of the human eye
* Questionnaires were also administered, asking how tooth-brushing behaviour had changed
* 76% of the high fear group worried (42% increase after watching the
  video) about tooth decay
* 46% of the low-fear group felt worried (a 24% increase after watching the video) about tooth decay
* 28% of the high-fear group reportedly changed habits after being exposed to the video
* 50% of the low-fear group changed their tooth-brushing habits after being exposed to the video
* They concluded that minimal fear arousal was an effective method of
  promoting health

### **Yale Model of Communication**

* **Hovland et al. (1953)** conducted many studies at Yale University which helped to form a model of persuasive communication.

<!-- end list -->

* This model focuses on three aspects of communication:
  * **The Communicator**: this aspect focuses on credibility, as well as sources for the message given. This can be achieved through using academic advice, appropriately qualified people, as well as personal experiences and trustworthiness (e.g. group-based drug therapies led by ex-drug users)
  * **The Communication**: This focuses on the arousal given by the
    message promoted.
  * **The Audience**: This focuses on considering the proper
    demographic and type of issue that is being promoted.

### **Health Belief Model**

* States that people are motivated by fear to protect their health
* **Leventhal (1967)**
  * Demonstrated the health belief model with an experiment
    involving cigarette smokers
  * Participants who were exposed to high fear appeal involving watching a film of a lung surgery changed their intentions regarding smoking more than those exposed to moderate fear appeal

### **Issues and Debates:**

* **Yale Model of Communication**
  * Has good **application to everyday lives** as they can model strategies that marketing campaigns can employ
* **Janis and Feshbach’s Fear Arousal Model**
  * It has good **application to everyday life** as it can model strategies that can be created, although it is best used for addictive negative health behaviours such as smoking and drinking
* Individual and Situational Explanation

<!-- end list -->

* All strategies must tackle both **individual and situational
  factors** as people could adhere to these strategies depending on
  what target group the strategy aims to promote healthy behaviours.
  Factors such as age and behaviour must be considered so that these
  theories become deterministic.
* Application to everyday life:
  * Both Fear Arousal and the Yale Model of Communication can be used to create strategies that marketing companies and practitioners can use to change health behaviours.
  * Prevention of illness is a more effective way to deal with
    illnesses
  * Campaigns that promote healthier lives (such as encouraging exercise) help not only an individual but the entire medical community.

### **Providing Health Information (Lewin, 1992; 2002)**

* Giving patients information to make them informed decisions could be a good way to promote healthy behaviours.
* **Lewin (1992)** wanted to find out if home-based programmes would
  reduce psychological distress in making decisions
* 176 patients who had suffered a heart attack to either a self-help
  program or informed counselling
* The self-help programme consisted of a heart manual given to
  participants
* The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale assessed psychological adjustment, was better in the rehabilitation group at one-year
* They also had less contact with general practitioners during the following year, and significantly fewer were readmitted in the first six months
* Clinically anxious or depressed patients had the most significant
  improvements.
* The cost-effectiveness of the home-based programme is yet to be
  compared with a hospital-based program
