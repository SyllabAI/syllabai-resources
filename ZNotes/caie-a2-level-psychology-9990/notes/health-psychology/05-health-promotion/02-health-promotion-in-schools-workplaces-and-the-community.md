## Health Promotion in schools, workplaces and the community

### **Schools (Tapper et al., 2003)**

* Developed a “Food Dudes” program that aimed to promote healthy
  eating in schools
* This program focuses on three aspects:
  * **Taste Exposure**: Increasing the exposure to a new food group
    over a period of weeks
  * **Modelling**: Certain factors, such as imitation and observational learning makes modelling more effective
  * **Rewards**: Positive reinforcement for eating the food, as well as rewards for implying the importance of healthy eating behavior

<!-- end list -->

* The programme is implemented by staff through:
  * Six Food Dudes episodes
  * A set of Food Dudes rewards
  * A set of letters from the Food Dudes that praised the children
  * A Food Dudes home pack to encourage children to eat fruits and
    vegetables at home
  * A staff manual and briefing video to help teachers implement the
    programme correctly
  * A set of education support materials to help teachers meet
    curriculum targets with the Food Dudes
* The main intervention phase lasted for 16 days
* During the intervention period, children were exposed to episodes of
  the Food Dudes and listen to their teacher, who read the Food Dudes
  letters
* Children were also rewarded with Food Dudes stickers and prizes if
  they tasted fruits and vegetables
* Both teachers and parents evaluated the program.
* Children were enthusiastic about the curriculum work using the Food
  Dudes
* Even after four months, the children were still eating significantly
  more fruit and vegetables, compared to before the programme.

### **Workplaces (Fox et al., 1987)**

* Implemented a token economy on an open pit mine to reduce accidents
* Workers from two mines, the Shirley Basin and Navajo Coal mines
  participated
* The number of workers in the Shirley Basin mine was 197 when the data collection began (1972)
* The number of workers in the Navajo Coal mine was 450 workers when data collection began (1975)
* Stamps were rewarded to employees if:
  * They worked without lost time and injuries
  * They were in a workgroup in which all the workers had no lost time injuries
  * They were not involved in equipment-damaging accidents
  * They have adopted safety suggestions
  * And if they show unusual behavior that prevents accidents
* Stamps were removed if:
  * A worker or group gets injured
  * Causing equipment damage
  * Failing to report accidents

<!-- end list -->

* The number of days lost without accidents, as well as costs for
  accidents were recorded
* Both the number of days lost due to accidents as well as the costs of covering accidents reduced significantly after the token economy has been implemented

### **Communities (Farquhar et al., 1985)**

* Focuses on the **Stanford Five City Multifactor Risk Reduction
  Project** (FCP)
* Long-term field study assessing the feasibility and effectiveness of
  community-wide health education directed at lowering cardiovascular
  disease risks

<!-- end list -->

* The FCP is originally planned to include:
  * Six years of education
  * Four independent (cross-sectional) population surveys
  * Four surveys of the cohort
  * Continuous surveillance for CHD episodes

<!-- end list -->

* Population surveys provide a major source of data relating to CVD risk
* Randomly selected samples from 12-74 years old were used

<!-- end list -->

* Cities involved include Monterey and Salinas (treatment cities),
  Modesto, San Luis Obispo and Santa Maria (control cities)

<!-- end list -->

* The main goals of the population surveys include:
  * To develop or adopt appropriate measurement instruments and
    procedures for the survey
  * To hire, train and supervise staff to operate the centres
  * To establish survey centres in each of the four communities
  * To identify and recruit randomly selected samples from each
    community with an objective of achieving at least a 66% response
    rate
  * To conduct surveys in a manner that provides accurate and
    reliable data, and to maintain an effective relationship with
    general public and healthcare professionals

<!-- end list -->

* As well as surveys, physiological measures were used. These include:
  * Body height and weight
  * Blood pressure by two methods (mercury manometer and
    semi-automated machine)
  * Resting heart rate
  * Non-fasting blood samples analyzed for plasma thiocyanate (a measure of smoking rate)
  * Expired air carbon monoxide
  * Urinary sodium potassium and creatinine (index of prior sodium
    chloride intake)
  * Low-level bicycle test (measure of fitness)

<!-- end list -->

* Short term impact saw knowledge of CVD risk factors increase in both treatment and control groups
* Improvement in the treatment group was more noticed
* There is a significant decrease in blood pressure and cholesterol
* Net decreases in resting heart rate favoured intervention participants
* Dietary saturated fat intake declined significantly in women, but
  not in men for the 24-hour diet recall
* Long-term impact found that both CHD and all-cause mortality risk
  scores were maintained in intervention cities while levelling out in
  control cities
* A follow-up in 2014 found that frequent, regular, systematic contact with media professionals influencing newspaper coverage of health-related topics

### **Issues and Debates:**

* At Schools
  * Tapper used **children as participants** in her study on promoting healthy eating in schools. Researching about healthy eating in childhood can support positive adult health behaviours. It must be important that the parents are also informed

<!-- end list -->

* At the Workplace
  * Fox et al. used a **longitudinal study** that allowed the
    effectiveness of the token economies implemented in both mines

<!-- end list -->

* At communities
  * Farquhar uses a **longitudinal study** to identify the long-term effects of implementing mass media campaigns in communities to which is still being measured as of 2014.
* For all methods of health promotion
  * All methods of promoting health listed have great **application to everyday life** as it provides evidence to support the implementation of mass health campaigns to encourage healthy behaviours
