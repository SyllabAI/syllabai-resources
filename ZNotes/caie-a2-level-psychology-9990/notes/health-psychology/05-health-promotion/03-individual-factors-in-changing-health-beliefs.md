## Individual Factors in Changing Health Beliefs

### **Unrealistic Optimism (Weinstein et al, 1980)**

* Investigated unrealistic optimism about future life events
* Unrealistic optimism: an error in judgment where people tend to believe that they are invulnerable and expect others to be victims of misfortune

### This research attempted to test **six hypotheses**:

* People believe that negative events are less likely to happen to
  them than to others and vice versa for positive events
* The more undesirable the negative event is, the stronger the
  tendency that one’s own chances are less than average
* The greater the perceived probability of an event, the stronger
  the tendency for people to believe that their own chances are
  greater than average
* Previous personal experience with an event increases the
  likelihood that people will believe that their own chances are
  less than average
* When a stereotype exists of a particular type of person to whom
  a negative event is likely to happen, people would tend to
  believe that their own chances are less than average
* In the first study, **258** college students estimated how much
  their own chances of experiencing 42 events differed from the
  chances of their peers
* Instructions from the questionnaire stated:
  * *“Compared to other students – same sex as you – what do you
    think are the chances that the following events will happen to
    you?”*
* There were 18 positive and 24 negative life events cited in the form
* Beneath the description of each event, there was a 15-point rating
  scale in which participants had to choose their relative probability
  of this happening to them
* The lowest possible choice was 100% less than average, as this
  indicated a probability of 0
* Overall, participants rated their own choices to be above average for positive events and below average for negative events
* The present data provide evidence of unrealistic optimism for both
  kinds of events
* Results showed that despite supporting all six hypotheses, different
  factors managed to govern responses to positive and negative events
* For negative events, optimism and perceived controllability had an
  impact on participants’ judgements
* When an event was judged as controllable, a stereotype existed in
  participants’ minds of the kind of person to whom the event
  generally occurred

### **Transtheoretical Model (Prochaska et al, 1997)**

* Suggests that individuals follow a six-stage model that involves the changing of health behaviour
* The six stages include:
  * **Pre-contemplation**: People do not intend to take action in
    the foreseeable future (next six months)
  * **Contemplation**: People intend to start healthy behaviour in the foreseeable future (usually the next six months). However, people may still feel ambivalent about changing their behaviour.
  * **Preparation**: People are ready to change their actions within the next thirty days. They start to take small steps towards behavioural change and believe that changing their behaviours would make them healthier.
  * **Action**: The person has changed his behaviour within the last six months and intends to keep moving forward with that behaviour
  * **Maintenance**: The person has sustained his behavioural change in a while, usually more than six months, and intends to maintain the behaviour change going forward.
  * **Termination**: The person has no desire to continue the unhealthy behaviour and is sure that they will not relapse.

<!-- end list -->

* Processes of change are the actual activities that can be done in
  order to move through different stages of interventions
* There are ten stages of intervention:
  * **Consciousness-raising**: Increases awareness about the causes, consequences and cures for a particular problem behaviour
  * **Dramatic relief:** Initially produces increased emotional experiences followed by reduced effect if appropriate action can be taken
  * **Self-re-evaluation**: Combines both cognitive and affective assessments of one’s self-image with and without a particular habit
  * **Environmental re-evaluation**: The belief that one can change
    the commitment and recommitment to act on that belief
  * **Social Liberation**: Requires an increase in social opportunities to demonstrate the new, positive behaviours
  * **Counter-conditioning**: Requires the learning of healthier
    behaviours that can become substitutes for problem behaviours
  * **Stimulus control:** Removes cues for unhealthy behaviours and
    prompts healthier alternatives
  * **Contingency management:** Provides consequences for taking
    steps in a particular direction
  * **Helping relationships:** Develop relationships with caring, trust, openness and acceptance for healthy behaviour change

<!-- end list -->

* To help individuals progress through these stages, practitioners
  must need to understand the processes of change.
* **Prochaska et al. (1992)** believe that the future of health promotion programmes lie with stage-matched, proactive and interactive interventions.

### **Health Change in Adolescents (Lau, 1990)**

* A longitudinal study aimed to explore the sources of stability and change in young adults’ health beliefs
* The data collected in this study was part of a larger piece of
  research of students enrolled at **Carnegie Mellon University** for classes ending in 1983
* Out of 1,106 students who were sent questionnaires, **947** of these provided data, which included responses from students and their parents
* The data collected via questionnaires over a period of three years
  were collated to investigate six questions:

<!-- end list -->

1. Is there a link between the health beliefs of parents and students
   when they initially leave home for college?
2. How do parents exert their influence on children?
3. How much do young adults’ health beliefs change during the first
   years of their college life?
4. How strong is the association between the health beliefs of the
   young adults and their peers at this time?
5. How do peers exert their influence on these young adults?
6. How strong is parental influence compared to peer influence?

<!-- end list -->

* The results found that there was a substantial change in the performance of health behaviours during the first three years of College
* Parents’ influence is much more significant than that of peers
* Lau suggested that the direct modelling of behaviour is the most powerful influence on health beliefs

### Issues and Debates:

* **Application to everyday life**: Understanding the individual factors that can identify who could change their behaviours following a health promotion campaign
* There can be **cultural bias** in the individual factors that
  can influence a person’s change in health behaviours
