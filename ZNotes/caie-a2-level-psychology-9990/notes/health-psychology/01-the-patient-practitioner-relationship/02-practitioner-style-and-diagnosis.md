## Practitioner Style and Diagnosis

### **Practitioner style: Doctor and patient-centred (Byrne and Long, 1976; Savage & Armstrong, 1990)**

* Focused on the interactions between patients and practitioners

#### **Byrne and Long (1976)**

* Investigated the interactions between patients and practitioners
  * Tape recorded and analysed about 2,500 medical consultations in
    several countries
  * They found that each practitioner tended to use a consistent style for all patients being treated
  * Most of the styles were classified as either **doctor-centred**
    or **patient-centred**
  * They commented that few doctors could reflect on the dynamics and process of the consultation
  * From the tape recordings, they recognised six phases that
    doctors went through in this consultation process:
    * Establishes a relationship with the patient
    * Attempts to discover the reason for the patient’s attendance
    * Conducts a verbal or physical examination or both
    * The doctor, the patient or both of them consider the
      condition
    * The doctor, and occasionally the patient, details further
      treatment
    * Ends the consultation

* Most of the 2,500 consultations underwent this process
  * **Doctor-centred** consultations tend to ask closed questions
    that require brief answers such as ‘yes’ or ‘no’
  * They also focused on the first problem the patient gives
  * Doctor-centred doctors also tend to ignore any other problems
    that the patient might have had
  * **Patient-centred** doctors had a less controlling role
  * They tend to ask open-ended questions such as ‘Can you describe
    the situation when pain occurs?’
  * These questions allow the patient to relate more information and
    introduce new facts that can be pertinent
  * They also avoided medical jargon and allowed the patient to participate in decision-making

#### **Savage and Armstrong (1990)**

* Compared the effect of directing and sharing styles of consultation
  * They used randomised allocation to conditions to test how
    satisfied patients felt when undergoing these approaches
  * A **random sample** of **359 participants** between the ages of
    16 to 75 presenting any symptoms were eligible
  * Each patient was asked for their consent to have their
    consultation recorded
  * Patient satisfaction was measured by two questionnaires that asked about the quality of communication in the consultation and any thoughts immediately and one week later
  * From 200 results, they found that both consultation styles had
    high ratings
  * However, directing consultations had reported more satisfaction
    with their doctor’s explanation
  * This implies that authoritarianism and certainty are elements of the doctor’s style that satisfy the patients the most.

### **Practitioner Diagnosis: Type I and II Errors**

* **Type I Error**: the practitioner diagnoses a patient as sick when he is actually healthy
* **Type II Error**: the practitioner diagnoses an ill person as healthy
* Type I and II errors may occur at any point throughout a patient’s
  illness from screenings, consultations and treatment stages
* **Screenings** involve cheap tests that are given to large
  populations, many of whom will not manifest any clinical indications
  of disease, such as smears
* **Testing** is a much more expensive and mostly invasive procedure that is given to those who manifest clinical indications of disease and are most frequently applied to confirm a suspected diagnosis.

### **Disclosure of Information (Robinson & West, 1992)**

* Investigated whether computerization leads to greater disclosure compared to paper questionnaire
* Recruited 33 male and 36 female participants from a Genito-urinary
  (GU) clinic in England
* Ages ranged from 15 to 49 years, with a mean of 27 years
* They were randomly assigned to either computer interview or paper
  interview
* It consists of a comparison of the data collected from the computer
  interview and the patient questionnaire
* Due to withdrawal and computer errors, 37 participants are in the
  computer condition and 32 in the paper condition
* Participants were asked to complete their case histories through
  either a computer or paper-based questionnaire
* After this, the patients underwent a consultation with their doctor,
  complete with a physical examination
* Data collected were from the doctor’s interview, with three specific
  data:
  * Number of symptoms reported
  * Number of reported previous attendances at the GU clinic
  * Number of sexual partners the patient had in the last 12 weeks
* Found that both paper and computer interviews yielded more symptoms
  than doctor’s interview
* The mean number of sexual partners increased in the computer condition
* Data is not statistically significant
* Computer interviews will elicit more information from patients than
  paper questionnaire with regard to personal symptoms
* Suggests that patients are unwilling to disclose all their symptoms
  within a consultation
* Computer-based interviews are elicited to support the doctor’s diagnosis

#### Evaluation:

* Byrne and Long
  * The usage of **tape recordings** only recorded the usage of
    verbal cues. This **ignores non-verbal cues** such as bodily
    gesture and interpersonal skills
  * As there was a large amount of recordings used around the world,
    Byrne and Long’s study is **generalizable** to different
    populations
  * As all consultations are recorded, the researchers can gather a
    lot of **qualitative data** to be analysed, but it can be too
    **subjective** and reduced **reliability**
* Savage and Armstrong
  * This research used an **independent measures design.** This
    means that participants are in either of two conditions. This
    may introduce **confounding variables** as other patients may
    prefer using computers over paper as a form of disclosing
    information

#### Issues and Debates:

* For both Byrne and Long & Savage and Armstrong’s studies
  * The findings for Byrne and Long, as well as Savage and Armstrong
    have **relevance to everyday life,** suggesting that doctors
    should be aware of their communication style when dealing with a
    patient
  * These studies display **individual and situational**
    explanations on why patients can prefer different styles of
    their doctors’ consultation styles
  * There is also **cultural bias** when considering the findings of communication techniques. Some cultures could prefer a direct approach when handling a doctor. Both studies were conducted in the West.
