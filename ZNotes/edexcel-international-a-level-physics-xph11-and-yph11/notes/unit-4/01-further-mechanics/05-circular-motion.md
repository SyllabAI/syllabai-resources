## Circular Motion

* Sometimes, when doing calculations for circular motion, we make the assumption that an object is a perfect circle.
* For example, we assume that the orbits of planets are perfect circles, even though, in reality, they are elliptical.

**How do we divide angles?**

* 1 degree is divided into 60 arcminutes
* One arcminute is divided into 60 arcseconds

We would use radians instead of degrees for circular motion calculations in physics.

Recall the formula

$s=rθ$

where $θ$ is the subtended angle in radians, $s$ is arc length, and $r$ is radius.

The value of $s$ when it travels one revolution of the circle is $2πr$ (circumference of the circle)

$θ  = \frac{s}{r}$

$θ  = \frac{2πr}{r}$

$= 2π $ radians

**Radians** is the angle at the centre of the circle whose arc is equal in length to the radius.

**Angular displacement** is the vector measurement of the angle through which something has moved.

### **Derivation of Angular Velocity**

$$

ω = \frac{θ}{t}

$$

Assuming that the angular displacement is 2π, or a full circle

$ω = \frac{2π}{T}$

$ω= 2πf$

We can also derive another formula for angular velocity using $s=rθ$ and $v= \frac{s}{t}$

Firstly rearrange $v= \frac{s}{t}$ to $s= vt$

Then equate $s=rθ$ to $s= vt$

$\frac{rθ}{t} = v$

$\frac{θ}{t} = \frac{v}{r}$

From $ω = \frac{θ}{t}$, we can now substitute $ω$ into the equation

∴ $ω = \frac{v}{r}$
