# Further Mechanics

## Impulse

* **Impulse is the change in momentum, denoted as:**

$$

I = F\Delta t = \Delta  p = mv – mu

$$

$I$ = impulse (N s)

$F$ = force (N)

$t$ = time (s)

* $\Delta p$ **= change in momentum (kg m/s)**

$m$= mass (kg)

v= final velocity $(m s^{–1})$

$u$ = initial velocity $ (m s^{–1})$

* The unit for impulse is $Ns$,
* Impulse is a vector quantity because it involves force. Thus, it acts in the same direction as the force.

**Example Scenario:**

* The force from a bike's brakes slows the bike by changing its momentum. We call this force "impulse." The longer you hold the brakes, the more your bike's speed decreases. It's like gently coming to a stop. This tells us that impulse quantifies the effect of the force acting over time.
