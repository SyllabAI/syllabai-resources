## Gravitational Potential

* **Gravitational potential** at a point is the work done per unit mass to move an object from an infinite distance to that point in the field.

* Gravitational potential is always negative. This is because, as a mass is brought towards another mass, its g.p.e. decreases. Since g.p.e. is zero at infinity, it follows that anywhere else, g.p.e. and potential is less than zero, i.e., negative.

* Gravitational potential can be calculated at a distance $r$ from a mass $M$ using the equation:

$$

V_{grav} = -\frac{GM}{r}

$$

Have you ever wondered how the equation for gravitational potential energy came about?

$$

G.P.E. = mg\Delta h

$$

The gravitational field strength tells us how quickly the potential changes over distance.

$$

g = \frac{dV_{grav}}{dx}

$$

For small distances, $g$ does not change significantly, so we can use the actual distance change

$g = -\frac{\Delta V_{grav}}{\Delta x}$

$gΔx = -\Delta V_{grav}$

$E = m\Delta V_{grav}$

$E = mg\Delta x$

$$

E_p = mg\Delta h

$$
