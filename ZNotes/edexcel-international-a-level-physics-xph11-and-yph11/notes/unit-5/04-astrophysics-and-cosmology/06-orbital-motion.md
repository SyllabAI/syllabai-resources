## Orbital Motion

* For an object orbiting a planet, such as an artificial satellite orbiting the Earth, gravity provides the centripetal force which keeps it in orbit. Therefore,

$$

\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^{2}}{r}

$$

Cancelling off $m$ gives us:

$$

v^2 = \frac{GM}{r}

$$

* This equation allows us to calculate, for example, the speed at which a satellite must travel to stay in a circular orbit. Since the masses get cancelled out, this means that all satellites, whatever their masses, will travel at the same speed in a particular orbit

### **Kepler’s Law**

$$

v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}

$$

substitute in the equation for $v^2$ that we derived earlier

$$

\frac{GM}{r} = \frac{4\pi^{2} r^2}{T^2}

$$

Make $T^2$ the subject by rearranging:

$$

T^2 = (\frac{4\pi^{2}}{GM}) r^3

$$

Thus

$$

T^{2} \propto r^3

$$
