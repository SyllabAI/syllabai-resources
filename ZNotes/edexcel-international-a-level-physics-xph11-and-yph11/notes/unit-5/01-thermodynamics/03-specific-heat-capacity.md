## Specific Heat Capacity

* The energy required to raise the temperature of a $1 kg$ substance by one degree **Kelvin** is known as its Specific Heat Capacity.
* Different materials have different specific heat capacities because they have varying molecular structures.
* e.g. water - 4200 $J kg^-1^ °C^-1 $, hydrogen - 14300 $J kg^-1 °C^-1$

**Calculations:**

* We will be using the formula $E=mcΔθ$ quite often in this topic.
* $E$ = the energy transferred to a material
* $m$ = the mass of the substance
* $Δθ$ = the change in temperature of the substance
* $c$ = the specific heat capacity of the substance

Make sure to continuously convert all the values into SI units before performing calculations

Example:

How long does a 1kW kettle take to raise the temperature of 20g of water by 27°C?

Worked Solutions:

$E=mcΔθ$

$E=Pt$

$Pt=mcΔθ$

$(20÷1000)(4200)(27)=(1000)t$

$t= 2.27 seconds$

Assumption: No heat is lost to the surroundings
