### **Independent Variable**

* The concentration of lipase enzyme

### **Dependent Variable**

* The final pH after 5 minutes

### **Constant Variables**

* Volume of milk used

* Initial pH of milk

* Temperature

* Time for reaction to occur

* Volume of lipase solution used in each test

* Type and source of milk

* Type of pH indicator

### **Method**

First, prepare five lipase solutions with concentrations of 10%, 7.5%, 5%, 2.5%, and 0% (distilled water), labelling them A, B, C, D, and E respectively. Then, set up five test tubes or beakers with the same labels and add 5 cm³ of milk along with five drops of a pH indicator, such as phenolphthalein or universal indicator, to each one. Next, add equal volumes—such as 1 cm³—of the corresponding lipase solutions to the appropriate test tubes and gently swirl them to mix. After that, leave the mixtures to stand for five minutes before observing and recording the final pH of each solution. Finally, repeat the entire experiment at least twice, calculate the average results, and record your findings in a table.

### Safety Precautions

Wear safety goggles and a lab coat at all times. Avoid direct contact with the lipase solution. Dispose of all solutions according to laboratory guidelines.
