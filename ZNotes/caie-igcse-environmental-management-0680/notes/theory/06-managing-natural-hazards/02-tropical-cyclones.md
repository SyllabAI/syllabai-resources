## Tropical Cyclones

* **Tropical cyclones** are large areas of very low pressure with wind speeds of over 119 km h-1

  * Cyclones are found in the Indian Ocean and Australia

  * Hurricanes in the Atlantic Ocean and eastern Pacific Ocean

  * Typhoons in the western Pacific Ocean

* These are all the same weather features and are often referred to as tropical cyclones.

### **Requirements for the tropical cyclones to occur:**

* Ocean surface temperature of at least 27°C. Warm water provides the energy to increase evaporation, which rises and condenses, releasing huge amounts of energy.
* Ocean depth of at least 60m deep.
* These conditions occurring between latitudes of 5° and 20° north and south to
  have sufficient Coriolis effect (rotation of earth), making the air spin. Oceans are too cold at higher latitudes.
* Very little wind shear (change in wind speed or direction). It allows the vertical development of the storm, if wind shear too strong, it can stop it.

### **Characteristics of tropical cyclones**

* A tropical cyclone can be up to 800 km in diameter and up to 20 km in height.
* Last for about a week, moving 10-15 km h-1, rotating anticlockwise around the eye (calmest part of the storm) in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
* Cloudy sky, increase in wind speed, rain with sunny intervals
* Air pressure falls, wind speed continues increasing to over 119 km h-1. Large cumulonimbus clouds form and very heavy rain falls. This is the eyewall or vortex.
* In the eye of the storm, the sky is clear, wind and rain is light and the temperatures are warm.
* After the eye passes, cumulonimbus clouds form again, with the return of heavy rain and strong winds.
* Wind speed and rainfall decreases. Sunny intervals. Tropical cyclones create the following hazards:
  * Structural damage to building because of strong winds

  * River flooding and landslides, due to heavy rainfall

  * Storm surges can lead to flooding in low-lying coastal areas, the intense low pressure can raise sea levels and strong winds can push waves up to 5 m high inland.
