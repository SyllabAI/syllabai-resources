## Flooding

* Flooding is when the discharge of a river exceeds the capacity of the river's channel. When this happens, the river overflows the bank and covers the adjacent floodplain.

  * **Discharge**: the volume of water passing a measuring point or gauging station in a given time, measured in cubic metres per second (cumecs)

### **Physical causes:**

**Weather** -

* **Heavy intense rainfal**l can exceed the infiltration capacity of the soil, leading to increased overland flow.

* **Steady prolonged rainfall** can saturate the soil, cause the water table to rise, reducing infiltration capacity.

* **Rapid snow melting** while the ground is still frozen, can cause overlands to flow

**Previous weather -**

* **Antecedent soil moisture**: the amount of moisture present in the soil before a rainfall event.

* The more saturation, the increased chances of overland flows because of less infiltration.

**Soil and rock type -**

* Impermeable rocks and soils (e.g. granite, clay) have low infiltration capacity and percolation rate, leads to greater overland flow.

* **Percolation**: the vertical movement of water from the soil into the underlying rock

**Land relief -**

* Steeper slopes lead to faster overland flow with little infiltration time.

**Storm surges, tsunamis -**

* Flooding of low-lying coastal areas.

### **Human causes:**

* **Deforestation** - Reduces interception and infiltration.

* **Cultivation** - Ploughing down rather than across slopes increases the water flow.

* **Urbanisation** - Concrete and tarmac are impermeable surfaces

* **Climate change** - Global warming may lead to rising sea levels and more rainfall in some areas.
