# Differentiation

## Introduction

* Differentiation helps us find the **gradient of the tangent**, a line which touches the curve at a **single point**.

 ![Screenshot 2024-08-30 at 3.07.29 PM.png](../../../assets/f01cd744863c-66d166662b98872d0ae71ef98f2785be76c03b47c0348c41.07)

Using the formula for finding the gradient, we get

$$
m = \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

Where $h$ is a small number that is greater than 0.

* As $h$ becomes smaller and approaches 0 (but never reaches it), we get the gradient of a tangent to a point on the curve.

From this, we can deduce

$$
f\prime(x) = tan(\theta) = m
$$

* Where $f\prime(x)$ represents the **first derivative**, or the result obtained by differentiating $f(x)$ once.




### **Notation**

It is denoted by many notations:

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = f\prime(x) = y\prime
$$

* Where $\frac{dy}{dx}$ implies a function $y$ has been differentiated with respect to the variable $x$.
  * It can also be written as $\frac{d}{dx} ~ y$, where $y$ is the function being differentiated.



### **Basics of Differentiation**

**<center>Power rule </center>**

$$

\frac{d}{dx} ~ x^n = n \times x^{n - 1}  \quad [\text{For any real number} ~ n ~ \cancel{=} ~ 0]
$$

**<center>Sum and Difference rule</center>**

$$

\frac{d}{dx} ~  f(x) ~ \pm ~ g(x) = \frac{d}{dx} ~ f(x) ~ \pm ~ \frac{d}{dx} ~  g(x)
$$

**<center>Scalar multiple rule</center>**

$$

\frac{d}{dx} ~ a \times f(x) = a \times \frac{d}{dx}~  f(x) \quad [\text{Where}~  a ~\text{is a constant number}]
$$

**<center>Constant rule</center>**

$$

\frac{d}{dx} ~a = 0 \quad [\text{Where} ~ a ~ \text{is a constant number}]
$$
