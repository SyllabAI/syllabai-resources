## Substitutions

Many questions may ask you to solve unique equations, which may not resemble a quadratic at first glance. These can be solved by making a clever substitution, by the use of a **variable**, and then factorising it to solve for the values of $x$.

The table below shows an **example** of possible substitutions that one can use

| Equation | Substitution |
|----|----|
| $ax^4 + bx^2 + c$ | $u = x^2$ and $u^2 = x^4$ |
| $ax^6 + bx^3 + c$ | $u = x^3$ and $u^2 = x^6$ |
| $ax + b\sqrt{x} + c$ | $u = \sqrt{x}$ and $u^2 = x$ |
| $ax^{\frac{2}{3}} + bx^{\frac{1}{3}} + c$ | $u = x^{\frac{1}{3}}$ and $u^2 = x^{\frac{2}{3}}$ |
| $ax^{-2} + bx^{-1} + c$ | $u = x^{-1}$ and $u^2 = x^{-2}$ |


---

**<center>Example 1</center>**

Solve the equation $x^4 - 5x^2 + 4 = 0$

**<center>Answer</center>**

Let $u = x^2$, such that $u^2 = x^4$ and $x = \pm \sqrt{u}$

Using this substitution, we get $u^2 - 5u + 4 = 0$. This quadratic equation can be factorised easily.

$$
(u - 4)(u - 1) = 0
$$

$$
u = 4 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2
$$

$$
u = 1 \implies x^2 = 1 \implies x = \pm 1
$$

**<center>Example 2</center>**

Solve the equation $6x + \sqrt{x} -1 = 0$

**<center>Answer</center>**

Let $u = \sqrt{x}$, such that $u^2 = x$ and $x = u^2$

Using this substitution, we get $6u^2 + u -1 = 0$. This quadratic can be factorised easily.

$$
(3u - 1)(2u + 1) = 0
$$

$$
u = \frac{1}{3} \implies \sqrt{x} = \frac{1}{3} \implies x = \frac{1}{9}
$$

$$
u = \frac{-1}{2} \implies \sqrt{x} = \frac{-1}{2} \implies x = \frac{1}{4}
$$
