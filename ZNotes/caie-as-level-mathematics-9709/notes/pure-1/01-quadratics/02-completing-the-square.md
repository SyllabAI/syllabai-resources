## Completing the square

Completing the square allows us to write a quadratic, $ax^2 + bx + c$, in the form $p(x + q)^2 + r$. This is useful for a variety of reasons, which we will see later on.

* Given $ax^2 + bx + c$, we can complete the square using the formula:

$$

a\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 + ~ c ~ - \frac{b^2}{4a}
$$

* Comparing coefficients in the expansion of $(x + a)^2$ is another way to complete the square.


---

**<center>Example</center>**

Express $3x^2 + 9x + 5$ in the form of $p(x + q)^2 + r$, where p, q and r are constants.

**<center>Answer</center>**

First we identify our coefficients, $a = 3, b = 9, c = 5$. Now we can substitute in our values into the formula:

$$

3\left(x + \frac{9}{2(3)}\right)^2 + 5 - \frac{9^2}{4(3)}
$$

Which simplifies to

$$

3\left(x + \frac{3}{2}\right)^2 - \frac{7}{4}
$$

* You can verify your answer by expanding it and comparing it with your original quadratic.
