## Trigonometry identities

$$

tan(\theta) = \frac{sin(\theta)}{cos(\theta)}
$$

$$

sin^2(θ) + cos^2(θ) = 1

$$


---

**<center>Example</center>**

Prove the identity $\frac{cos^2(x) -sin^2(x)}{cos(x)} + \frac{1}{cos(x)} ≡ 2cos(x)$

**<center>Answer</center>**

$sin^2(x)$ can be written as $1-cos^2(x)$

$$

\frac{cos^2(x)-(1-cos^2(x))}{cos(x)}+ \frac{1}{cos(x)}
$$

$$

\frac{2cos^2(x)-1}{cos(x)}+ \frac{1}{cos(x)}
$$

$$

\frac{2cos^2(x)-1 + 1}{cos(x)}
$$

$$

\frac{2cos^2(x)}{cos(x)} \implies 2cos(x)
$$
