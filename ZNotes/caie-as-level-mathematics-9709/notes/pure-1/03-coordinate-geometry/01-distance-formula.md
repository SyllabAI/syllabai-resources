# Coordinate Geometry

## Distance Formula

* The distance formula helps us find the distance, or length, between two points.

Let us consider two points, on the same linear line, $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$. The lines $x = x_2$ and $y = y_1$ can be drawn, resulting in a **right angled triangle** with the line $AB$ as the **hypotenuse**.

![](../../../assets/9a343f9b1b79-66b5f1c8a9896e1061bc0d859e29338b7d21fa7a4c34d45b.11)

The length of the **base** is obtained from $x_2 - x_1$ and the **height** is obtained from $y_2 - y_1$.

* Using Pythagoras theorem, we can derive the **distance formula** for two points, $A$ and $B$, as:

$$

AB = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}
$$

### Mid-point formula

* The mid-point formula helps us find a point, usually denoted by $M$, which is **equidistant** from two other points.The mid-point of two points can be found using:

$$

M\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)
$$

---

**<center>Example 1</center>**

The distance between points $A(0,4)$ and $B(a, 1)$ is 5 units. Find the value of the **positive integer** $a$.

**<center>Answer</center>**

Using the distance formula:

$$
AB =\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}
$$

We can substitute our values in

$$

5^2 = (a − 0)^2 + (1 − 4)^2​
$$

$$

a^2 = 16 \implies a = 4
$$

**<center>Example 2</center>**

A line segment, joining points $A(2, -3)$ and $B(4, a)$ has a midpoint $M(b, -2)$. Find the values of $a$ and $b$.

**<center>Answer</center>**

Using the mid-point formula

$$
M = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)
$$

The $y$ value of the mid-point can be represent as

$$

y_M = \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}
$$

$$
-2 = \frac{-3+a}{2}
$$

$$
a = -1
$$

The $x$ value of the mid-point can be represented as

$$
x_M = \frac{x_1+x_2}{2}
$$

$$
b = \frac{2+4}{2}
$$

$$

b = 3
$$
