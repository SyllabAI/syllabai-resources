## Equations of lines

### **Equation of line parallel to $y$-axis**

* The equation of a line parallel to the $y$-axis can be written in the form $x = a$.
  * All points on this line have the coordinate $P(a, y)$.

![](../../../assets/6b3829144477-66bce3975ba8be0c409d85749e5edfbdb41330511d5eb9fb.03)

### **Equation of line parallel to $x$-axis**

* The equation of a line parallel to the $x$-axis can be written in the form $y = b$.
  * All points on this line have the coordinate $P(x, b)$.

### **Equation of a straight line**

The equation of a straight line is given by

$$

y = mx + c
$$

* $m$ represents the gradient.
* $c$ represents the $y$-intercept $(0, c)$. It can also be looked at in terms of a **vertical translation**.

### **Point-slope formula**

The point-slope formula is **another way** to find the equation of a straight line.

* Consider a general point $P(x , y)$, which can be **any point** on the line, and a **known point** $Q(x_1, y_1)$ which the line passes through.

<center>The gradient, m, can be found using the formula</center>

$$

m = \frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1}
$$

<center>From this, we find the point-slope formula by making (y - y_1) the subject of the formula</center>

$$

(y - y_1) = m(x - x_1)
$$

---

**<center>Example</center>**

Find the equation of the straight line that passes through the points (-5, 3) and (-4, 1).

**<center>Answer</center>**

$$
m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \frac{1 - 3}{-4-(-5)}
$$

$$
m =-2
$$

$$
y = -2x + c
$$

Now we can substitute any point in this equation to find the value of the $y$-intercept c

$$
1 = -2(-4) + c
$$

$$
c = -7
$$

So our final answer is

$$

y = -2x - 7
$$
