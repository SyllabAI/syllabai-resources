## Perpendicular bisector

* A perpendicular bisector is a line that bisects another line **perpendicularly**, at a 90 degree angle, at its **midpoint**.



---


**<center> Example </center>**

Two points have coordinates $A(5, 7)$ and $B(9, -1)$. Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the line $AB$.

**<center>Solution</center>**

The gradient, $m_1$, of $AB$ can be calculated using

$$

m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} 
$$

$$

m = \frac{-1 - 7}{9 - 5}
$$

$$

m = -2
$$

We can also find the gradient of the perpendicular, $m_2$, using

$$

m_1 \times m_2 = -1
$$

$$

-2 \times m_2 = -1 \implies m_2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$

So we have a line equation, for the perpendicular bisector, in the form

$$

y = \frac{x}{2} + c
$$

To find the value of the $y$-intercept, $c$, we can use the fact that the perpendicular bisector **must** intersect the **midpoint** of the line $AB$.

$$

M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2} \right)
$$

$$
M = \left( \frac{5 + 9}{2}, \frac{7 - 1}{2} \right) 
$$

$$

M = \left(7, 3 \right)
$$

Using these coordinates into our line equation, we get

$$

3 = \frac{7}{2} + c \implies c = - \frac{1}{2}
$$

So the equation of the perpendicular bisector is

$$

y = \frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{2}
$$
