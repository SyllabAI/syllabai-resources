## Introduction to radians

* The radian is the angle formed when the **arc length** is the **same** as the **length** of the **radius**.
  * It is also denoted as a “rad”.
* Consider a circle with its radius, $r$, equal to 1
  * An arc length of $2 \pi$ would have an angle of $360^\circ$ degrees, due to the circumference being $2 \pi r$.
  * Half of its circumference, or an arc length of $\pi$, would have an angle of $180^\circ$ degrees.

### Converting between units

* Using the result, $180^\circ = \pi$, we get these useful ratios
  * 1 radian = $\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$ degrees
  * $1^\circ$ degree = $\frac{\pi}{180^\circ}$ radians

These ratios can help as convert from degrees to radians, or vice versa, as shown in the table below.

| Converting from | Formula used |
|----|----|
| Degree to Radian | $\theta \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ}$ |
| Radian to Degree | $\text{radian} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$ |


---

**<center>Example 1</center>**

Convert $360^\circ$ degrees to radians.

**<center>Answer</center>**

$$

360^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180^\circ} = 2 \pi ~\text{radians}
$$

**<center>Example 2</center>**

Convert $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians to degrees.

**<center>Answer</center>**

$$

\frac{\pi}{2} \times \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} = 90^\circ ~ \text{degrees}
$$
