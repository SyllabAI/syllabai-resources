## Types of Business Organisations

1. **Sole trader**: A business that is solely run by one person who makes all the decisions.


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Low start-up cost|Owner has unlimited liability|
|Quicker decision making|Limited finance to run or start business|
|Sole trader can have all profits generated|Sole trader may lack essential skills needed to run the business|
||Enterprise does not have a separate legal entity|

(**Note**: **Unlimited Liability** occurs when a company goes into debt, and the owner(s) have to sell personal belongings to pay their debt or losses off. 

**Limited liability** occurs when a company has debt, and the owner(s) have to pay the amount they invested in the company.)

2\.**Partnership**: A Business run by 2 to 20 people who share and divide work, decision-making, rewards, and losses.


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Partners can equally divide the profits and keep them|Owners have unlimited liability|
|Work and decision-making can be equally divided amongst the partners|Chances of conflicts or potential differences may arise|
|Easy to start up|Slower decision making|
|Access to wide range of skill and information|Business has no independent legal status|
|More number of partners, more capital raised|Business does not have a sperate legal entity|

3. **Limited Company**- A business which generates capital by selling shares. There are two types of limited companies which are :

- Public limited Company- Shares are issued to the public through the stock market.
- Private limited company- Shares are usually sold to families or friends.

(**Note** - Shareholders are people who have invested in a company and in return, gotten shares, which is a percentage of ownership of the company)


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Limited liability|Difficult to set up (lot of legal paperwork)|
|A lot of capital can be raised|All affairs or accounts of business have to be shared for the public to see|
|Company has separate legal entity|Firm may become too large to be managed well|
||Selling too many shares can lead to owners losing their control over the business|
|Business has separate legal entity||

4. **Cooperative**: A business which is managed and owned by people who use its services or are a part of its operations. There are two types:

- **Producer cooperative**: Owned and run by manufacturers who come together and sell their products.
- **Worker cooperative**: Owned and run by the workers themselves.
- **Consumer cooperative**: Owned and run by the users of cooperatives, the goods or services.


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Owners have limited liability|Limited capital|
|Has a separate legal entity|Members may not have the necessary skills needed to run the cooperative|
|Less chances of conflict as it follows collective principles (Decisions are made with agreeance of all members, and work is equally divided)||
|Has a separate legal entity||

5. **Franchise**: a business where a company can get the rights to use an established business name and image as well as sell their products.

(**Note** - **Franchisee** is a company that gets the rights to use another company's name. **Franchisor** is the existing business that gives the right.)


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Great chances of success because a brand and the products are well known|Franchisee will have to run company based on rules and controls set by franchisor|
|Banks will be more willing to give loans|Franchisee has to pay for the license of the right to use a brand, which has a substantial cost|
|The franchisor is responsible for the design and layout of the store as well as the staff training.|The franchisee has to pay the franchisor a percentage of earnings.|

6. **Social Enterprise**: Business which is aimed at helping the community. There are two types:

- **Not-for-Profit**: Produces or provides their own goods and services. Profits made are reinvested to grow businesses and provide services for the betterment of society.
- **Charity**: Gathers funds through donations or fundraising.


|Advantages|Disadvantages|
|:---|:---|
|Helps enhance the community|Limited access to capital|
|Higher rate of employee satisfaction (they know they are working for a good cause)||
