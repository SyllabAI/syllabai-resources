## Manufactured Boards

* Not all wood sizes can instantly be cut up from a tree, depending on how you cut a tree, waste product will be produced.
* By using this waste product, we can make boards of the desired sizes
* These boards can be used instead of full timber, providing a more affordable solution.
* There are many different types of boards (depending on how they are manufactured), we will cover the main 5:

| Name of Board | Description |
|----|----|
| Blockboard | The centre core is made of soft wood strips and joined together with an adhesive. Covered with Plywood on either side for looks and strength. Sometimes finished off with a veneer. |
| Chipboard | Small bits of wood bonded together with a resin and put under a press to make the board strong. It is weaker than a Blockboard and comes in a preset thickness, however it is more affordable. Sometimes used for indoor furniture when it is covered with a finish. |
| Hardboard | It is made from wood fibres that are placed under pressure until they come together. Due to that, it is smooth on one side and rougher on the other. It is commonly used in indoor furniture. |
| Medium Density Fibreboard (MDF) | MDF is very common, however it is not used in school workshops due to the fact that it produces toxic dust when worked with. It is made of very fine wood dust pressed with a resin. It is easy to work with. It is considerably strong due to the present resin agent. |
| Plywood | It is made of layer timber with bisecting grain. Layers are bonded together with a resin and pressed into boards. A range of different plywood grades are available. |

### **Advantages of Using Boards**

* They are **cheaper** than 100% timber.
* They are available in a **range of sizes.**
* **Environmentally friendly** as they use wood waste product.
* They **do not split** along the grain.
* Readily available with **finishes** (such as paint or veneer).
