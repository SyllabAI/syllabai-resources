## Plastics

### **Introduction**

* Plastics are a **new group of materials** discovered only in the **1940s**; now, they overtook the world and even pose an environmental issue.
* Plastics are made from **the upper fraction of petroleum.**
* **Please note that plastics are almost guaranteed to appear on the exam (for paper 3)**

### **Thermoplastics**

**Nylon:**

* Nylon is a **synthetic polymer**; it is a fibre.
* It is **shiny** (or can be made to be), which is why it is commonly used for **different clothing types.**
* Nylon, being a **fabric**, has a very **high elastic limi**t; this is another reason it is used in clothing.
* Nylon is a fibre that has a VERY high tensile strength
* Overall, it is a fabric that can resist wear, tensile forces, **and high temperatures.** This gives it benefits over other fabrics.
* Excellent **Insulator**.
* It can be cast through **injection moulding**

**Polyethylene:**

* Polyethylene is split into 2 categories: **Low density (LDPE)** and **High density (HD**PE).

  **Low Density (LDPE):**
* **Moisture resistant**, which makes it a good plastic to use for **food packaging and plastic bags.**
* Very **common** in everyday life.
* **Soft** and **Pliable**
* **Good Chemical Resistance**
* Uses:
  * Playground slides
  * Plastic Containers
  * Battery Cases

  **High Density (HDPE):**
* Has a **stronger tensile strength than LDPE**
* Physical properties **vary vastly** depending on the moulding used
* Unlike LDPE, it is **not see-through**
* **Low-temperature resistance**
* Uses:
  * House Wrap
  * Toys
  * Flexible HDPE Pipes

### **Polyethylene Terephthalate (PET)**

* PET is probably the **most common plastic** around us (or known by us)
* Part of the polymer family
* It is **very strong** and **light,** which makes it easy to transport
* Large **range of temperature**  (Approximately -60 → 130)
* Doesn’t break or shatter
* **Has been proven safe to use in contact with food and beverages**
* Uses:
  * Plastic Bottles
  * Sailcloth
  * Food packaging/ Containers

### **Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC)**

* Cheap
* Acid and Alkali resistant
* Discolour in strong UV light
* **High density** when compared to other thermoplastics
* Toxic when heated due to the production of fumes
* Brittle below 0 degrees
* Flame retardant
* High Tensile and compression forces
* Uses:
  * Piping
  * Heat Shrinking Tubes
  * Fake leather

### **Acrylic (PMMA)**

* Usually the most common plastic in **school workshops**
* Can be in sheets
* Opaque, translucent, transparent (Can be made into any 3)
* Can be dyed into any colour
* Weather Resistant
* Resistant to most acids and alkalis
* Relatively Cheap
* Uses:
  * Small projects are done in school
  * Picture frames
  * Window replacements (instead of glass)

### **Polystyrene (PS)**

* Commonly known as **Styrofoam**
* Can be **recycled** but **not efficiently**
* One of the more **toxic** plastic types
* Can be treated (polished)
* Cheap
* Good insulator
* Poor chemical resistance
* Poor weather resistance
* Brittle
* Uses:
  * Toys
  * Cutlery
  * Electric housings
  * Light Diffusers
  * CD cases

### **Polypropylene (PP)**

* It is one of the more common plastics **available on the market**
* Strong and can withstand **significant temperatures**
* Low density
* Very similar to PE but stronger
* Low cost (cheap)
* Poor resistance to fuels
* High permeability to gases
* Brittle below 0 degrees (freezing)
* Uses:
  * Pipes
  * Packaging/Films
  * Rope/Netting
  * DVD cases

### **Acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene (ABS)**

* High chemical resistance
* High fatigue resistance
* High strength, stiffness and rigidity
* Tough and Ductile at low temperatures
* Used very commonly in **3D printers**
* Uses:
  * Gardening Tools
  * 3D printer filling
  * Pipes and Fittings
