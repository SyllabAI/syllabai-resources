## Reforming and Deforming

* It means changing the shape of a product or material without adding or removing extra material
* It is used in industry to form difficult and tough-to-make shapes
* Below is a table that shows different deforming processes:

| Process name | Material | Description | Uses |
|----|----|----|----|
| Steam bending | Hardwoods | Bits of hardwood are heated in a steam chamber until they can bend enough. Then, they are shaped/wrapped around a product | Wooden furniture |
| Lamination | Hardwoods | Sheets of veneer (thin wood) are layered with a strong adhesive. They are then pressed together to form a stronger single-layer | Wooden furniture |
| Vacuum forming | Thermoplastics | Thermoplastic sheets are heated, and a former is then put below the plastic sheets. The air below is blown out, and the sheets take the shape of the former | Suitcase colorings, Food packaging |
| Blow molding | Thermoplastics | Tubes of plastics are heated and fed through a mold. The air is blown into the tubes, forcing it to expand and form against the mold | Plastic milk bottles |

* **Below is a table of the reforming processes:**

| Process Name | Material | Description | Uses |
|----|----|----|----|
| Injection molding | Thermoplastics | Plastic beads are heated and injected into a special mold to form complex shapes | Toys, low-cost consumer goods |
| Extrusion | Thermoplastics and metals | The material is heated and pushed through a form to create longer parts | Pipes, tubes, rods |
| Sand casting | Metals | Non-ferrous metals are melted and poured into a special mold made from sand | Engine blocks, gas & water valves |
| Die casting | Metals | The metal is melted and forced into a hardened tool steel mold | Toys, Engine parts, Airplane models |
