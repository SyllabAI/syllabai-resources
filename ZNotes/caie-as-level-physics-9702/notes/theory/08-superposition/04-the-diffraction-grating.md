## The diffraction grating

![ChatGPT Image Sep 28, 2026, 04 55 02 PM](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/b11c1d6c019fcd2f8cf486a4.webp =800x)

A **diffraction grating** consists of a large number of **equally spaced, parallel slits**. When monochromatic light passes through it, the waves interfere and produce **sharp bright maxima** at specific angles.

The positions of the bright maxima are given by:

$$
\boxed{d\sin\theta = n\lambda}
$$

* Where $d$ = distance between successive slits =  $\frac{1}{N}$

* $N = $ number of slits per meter

* $\theta =$ angle from horizontal equilibrium

* $n=$ order number

* $\lambda =$ wavelength

### Finding wavelength

When we rearrange the previous equation we get - $\lambda = \frac{d\sin\theta}{n}$

If the grating has **$N$ lines per meter** then it becomes $d = \frac{1}{N}$

If the grating has **$N$ lines per millimeter** then it becomes $d = \frac{1}{N(10^3)}$

this gives $\lambda = \frac{1\sin\theta}{Nn}$

when $d$ isnt given this can be used

### Factors that affect intensity of fringes

For a given grating and wavelength, the **number of slits illuminated does not change the angle** of a particular maximum.

Instead, increasing the number of slits makes the bright maxima:

* **sharper**

* **narrower**

* **more clearly separated**

* generally easier to measure accurately.

So: **number of slits affects sharpness of maxima**

### Order of maxima

* n=0: **central maximum**, θ=$0^\circ$

* n=1: **first-order maximum**

* n=2: **second-order maximum**

* n=3: **third-order maximum**, etc.

Higher orders occur at larger angles.

However, an order is only possible if: $\boxed{n\lambda \le d}$

Therefore - $\boxed{n_{max} \le \frac{d}{n}}$

The maximum possible order is the largest integer satisfying this condition.

### Worked example

A grating has 600 lines $mm^{-1}$. A second-order maximum occurs at $32.0^\circ$. Find wavelength

$d = \frac{1}{N} = \frac{1}{600}\ \text{mm} = 1.67\times10^{-6}\ \text{m}$

using - $d\sin\theta=n\lambda$

$\lambda=\frac{d\sin\theta}{n}$

$\lambda =\frac{(1.67\times10^{-6})\sin32.0^\circ}{2}$

$\lambda =\frac{(1.67\times10^{-6})(0.530)}{2}$

$\boxed{\lambda=4.43\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}}$

$\boxed{\lambda=443\ \text{nm}}$
