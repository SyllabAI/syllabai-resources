## Gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy

### Gravitational Potential Energy (G.P.E)

The energy that a mass has or is stored due to it’s position/height in a gravitational field

* Formula is applicable when gravitational field is uniform

* Formula : $E_P$ = $mgh$

  * $E_P$ - Gravitational Potential Energy

  * $m$ - mass of object

  * $g$ - gravitational field strength

  * $h$ - height of object

* Formula for change in energy : ∆$E_P$ = $mg∆h$

  * $∆h$ - change in height

  * ∆$E_P$ - change in Gravitational Potential Energy

* Derivation : $W = Fs$ & $w = mg = F$

  $\therefore W = mg.s$

  $s$ in direction of force $= h$ above ground

  $\therefore W = mgh$

### Kinetic Energy (K.E)

Energy a mass/object posses due to it’s movement/velocity.

* Formula : $E_k$ = $\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$

  * $E_k$ - Kinetic Energy

  * $v$ - velocity

* It gives KE at any instant given the current velocity

* Formula for change in kinetic energy : $\Delta E_k = \frac{1}{2}m(v^2-u^2)$

  * ∆$E_k$ - change in kinetic energy

  * $v$ - Final velocity

  * $u$ - Initial velocity

* Derivation : $W = Fs$ & $F = ma$

  $\therefore W = ma.s$

  $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as \Longrightarrow as = \ \frac{1}{2}(v^{2} - u^{2})$

  $\therefore W = m.\frac{1}{2}\left\( v^{2} - u^{2} \right\)$

  $\therefore W = \frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$ (If the object starts from rest (u = 0), W = ½mv², which defines the kinetic energy possessed at speed v)

### Conservation of energy ( G.P.E. & K.E. )

* When mechanical energy is conserved, the total (G.P.E. \+ K.E.) remains constant, even though G.P.E. converts into K.E. (or vice versa) as the object moves.

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/65a9d2f49b174f937b7b152d.webp =547x)

Initial G.P.E \+ K.E = Final G.P.E \+ K.E

* At initial position - G.P.E = $mg∆h$ & K.E = 0

* At final position - G.P.E = 0 & K.E = $\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$

  $mg∆h$ \+ 0 = 0 \+ $\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$

  $mg∆h$ = $\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$

  $g∆h$ = $\frac{1}{2}v^{2}$

  $2gh$ = $v^2$

  $\boxed{v=\sqrt{2gh}}$

  * $v$ - velocity at final position

  * $g$ - acceleration due to gravity

  * $∆h$ - difference in height between initial and final position
