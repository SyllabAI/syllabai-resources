## Resistance and resistivity

### Resistance & Ohms Law

Potential difference per unit current

* Resistance opposes current and they are inversely proportional

* Unit - Ohms (volt per ampere)

* **Ohm’s Law** - The current in a component is proportional to the potential difference across the component when physical conditions (e.g. temp) stay constant

* so we can tell that V is proportional to I & R is the constant but , this only applies when it’s an ohmic conductor ( a material that follows ohm’s law )

* We can get the formula $V=IR$

  * V - voltage

  * I - current

  * R - resistance

* We can rearrange the formula $V=IR$ to give $R=\frac{V}{I}$

### Resistance , Length & Area

* As area increases resistance decreases as there is higher electron flow because a larger cross-section gives more parallel paths for charge carriers, reducing opposition to flow

* And resistance is proportional to length

* This gives the equation - $R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$

  * $\rho$ = resistivity (constant for the same material at constant temperature; unit: $\Omega \text{m}$)

  * $L$ = length

  * $A$ = cross-sectional area

Example Sum

A metal wire has a potential difference of 6.0V across it and a current of 0.50A. The wire has a length of 2.0m and a cross-sectional area of $(1.0\times10^{-6}\mathrm{m^2})$.

**(a)** Calculate the resistance of the wire.

**(b)** Calculate the resistivity of the metal.

**Ans**

**(a)** $V=IR$

$R=\frac{V}{I}$

$R=\frac{6.0}{0.50}$

$\boxed{R=12,\Omega}$

**(b)**

$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$

$\rho=\frac{RA}{L}$

$\rho=\frac{(12)(1.0\times10^{-6})}{2.0}$

$\boxed{\rho=6.0\times10^{-6}\Omega\mathrm{m}}$

**(Non-ohmic Conductor)**

* As current increases, the filament gets hotter.

* Hence resistance increases and current increases at a decreasing rate.

* Non-linear relation between V and I

* Resistance increases as current increases

**Why resistance increases as current increases?**

* Low current (Low temp.)

* High current (High temp.)

**As current increases:**

* Voltage ↑

* Current ↑

* Temperature ↑

* Vibration of ions ↑

* Collision of ions with electrons ↑

* Resistance ↑

**Explanation:**

As current increases, temperature of the filament increases, causing more collisions between ions and electrons and resistance increases.

**(Negative Temperature Coefficient)**

**(Non-ohmic Conductor)**

* Low temp. (High R)

* High temp. (Low R)

**As temperature increases:**

* Voltage ↑

* Current ↑

* Temperature ↑

* Released electrons - ↑

* More charge carriers

* Resistance ↓

**Explanation:**

As temperature increases, more electrons are released into the conduction band and resistance decreases.

**(Non-ohmic Conductor)**

* Low light intensity (High R)

* Bright light (Low R)

**As light intensity increases:**

* Light intensity ↑

* Photons absorbed ↑

* More electrons released

* More charge carriers

* Resistance ↓

* Current ↑ (for same voltage)

**Explanation:**

As light intensity increases, more electrons are released and resistance decreases so current increases (for the same voltage).
