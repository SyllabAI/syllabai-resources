## Elastic & Plastic Behaviour

### Stress-Strain Graph

![c8338c44 e8b3 4012 9dc9 e07c96b1f6f5](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/bea25fcc07404f1dbaa0f7f2.webp =846x)

* Elastic deformation - when the load is removed, the object can and will return to its original natural length

* Plastic deformation: when the load is removed, the object can’t and will not  return to its original length

* Elastic limit: the point beyond which the object does **not return to its original length** when the load is removed

  * Plastic deformation is beyond the elastic limit

### Force-Extension Graph

![7cb5b22d 2eee 4584 9a55 aaa33452fa0f](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/35a7eeb1838e006b3c04d53d.webp =860x)

Area under the graph when force is proportional to extension is $A = \frac{1}{2}Fx$

Elastic potential energy has the formula $E_P = \frac{1}{2}Fx$ showing that area under the graph is work done & is equal to elastic potential energy

* As we know $F=kx$ so $E_P = \frac{1}{2}kx^{2}$ if we substitute $kx$ into first equation of $E_P = \frac{1}{2}Fx$
