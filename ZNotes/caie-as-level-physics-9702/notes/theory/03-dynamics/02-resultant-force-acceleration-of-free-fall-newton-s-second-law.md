## Resultant Force, Acceleration of free fall & Newton's Second Law

* When ***air resistance*** (mentioned in detail below under types of forces) ***is 0, the acceleration of free fall is only influenced by gravity*** (remember this for terminal velocity).

* When a force is applied to an object, it accelerates in the same direction as the applied force, so ***force is proportional to acceleration***.

  * Note: If there’s more than one force, resolve them to get the resultant force—either by simple addition/subtraction or using trig identities (refer to the kinematics chapter for resolving vectors using the triangle or parallelogram law).

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/bc0306dc70cd16a23b7a5f94.webp =338x)

* So, when a planet has a higher *g*, so the acceleration of free fall is automatically higher.

* But your mass is always constant, which leads to the formula

  * $\boxed{F=ma}$

* This gives: **Newton’s Second Law: Resultant force is directly proportional to the product of mass and acceleration.**

* In paper, for Newton’s second law question, write: **Resultant force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum**

### Worked example

A car of mass 1500 kg is travelling at 12$ms^{-1}$ . The engine provides a forward force of 5000 N, while air resistance and friction together provide a backward force of 1400 N.

**a)** the resultant force on the car
**b)** the acceleration of the car

Answer:

a) $F_{resultant}$ = 5000-1400 

$\boxed{F = 3600}$

b) $F=ma$

$a$ = $\frac{F}{m}$

$a$ = $\frac{3600}{1500}$

$\boxed{a=2.40ms^{-2}}$
