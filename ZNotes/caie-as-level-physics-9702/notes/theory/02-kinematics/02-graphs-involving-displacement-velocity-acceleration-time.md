## Graphs involving displacement, velocity, acceleration & time

### Displacement-Time graphs

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/d428daede3400a7aa35ba7d2.webp =846x)

* Gradient of a displacement time graph is velocity

* So the y-intercept shows initial displacement

* Straight line shows constant velocity so 0 acceleration

* Curved lines tend to show increasing or decreasing velocity hence shows acceleration or deceleration

* To find (instantaneous) velocity on a curve draw a tangent to the specific time

### Velocity-Time Graphs

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/bb979caeee42a30d12b90ec4.webp =813x)

* Gradient of graph is acceleration

* Area under graph is displacement

* Positive straight line is positive uniform acceleration

* Positive curve is non-uniform acceleration

* Negative straight line is uniform deceleration

* Negative curve is non-uniform deceleration

* y-intercept is initial velocity

* To find the instantaneous acceleration when it’s non-uniform, draw a tangent to the point and use a right-angled triangle to find the gradient of the line.

### Acceleration-Time graphs

![](https://images.znotes.org/allimages/c034110dcfad78c610cf3cc3.webp =832x)

* When acceleration reaches 0 due to presence of air resistance, it’s because resultant force is 0.

* y-intercept is initial acceleration

* x-intercept is time taken to reach terminal velocity
