# Government Microeconomic Intervention

## Reasons for Government Intervention in Markets

## ++Non-Provision of Public Goods++

A public good causes **market failure**. Because private firms cannot charge consumers, they have no profit incentive to provide the good, meaning the government must step in to provide it directly (financed through taxation).

Public goods are defined by three key characteristics:

* **Non-excludability:** Once provided, it is impossible (or prohibitively expensive) to stop non-payers from benefiting from it (e.g., street lighting, national defense).

* **Non-rivalry:** Consumption by one individual does not reduce the quantity or quality available for others to consume. The marginal cost of providing the good to an extra user is zero.

* **Non-rejectability:** Individuals cannot opt out of consuming the good even if they want to (e.g., you cannot reject the benefit of clean air or national defense).

**The Free-Rider Problem:** Because the good is non-excludable, rational consumers will wait for others to pay for it so they can consume it for free. This breakdown in the price mechanism is why the private sector will not provide public goods.

## ++Merit & Demerit Goods++

Merit and demerit goods are types of **private goods** (they are both excludable and rival). They cause **partial market failure** primarily due to imperfect information (information failure) and the existence of externalities.

**Merit Goods**

* **Definition:** Goods that the government determines are better for consumers than they themselves realize, meaning they will be **under-consumed and under-produced** in a free market.

* **Imperfect Information:** Consumers suffer from short-termism (myopic behavior). They underestimate the long-term private benefits of consuming the good.

* **Positive Externalities:** Consumption generates third-party benefits (e.g., an educated individual is more productive, boosting the wider economy).

* **Result:** The free market produces too little at a high price. The government intervenes (via subsidies, direct provision, or regulation) to increase consumption. *Example: Education, healthcare.*

**Demerit Goods**

* **Definition:** Goods that the government determines are worse for consumers than they themselves realize, meaning they will be **over-consumed and over-produced** in a free market.

* **Imperfect Information:** Consumers overestimate the short-term benefits and ignore the long-term private costs (e.g., health issues).

* **Negative Externalities:** Consumption generates third-party costs (e.g., passive smoking healthcare costs burdening the state).

* **Result:** The free market produces too much at a low price. The government intervenes (via indirect taxes, negative advertising, or bans) to reduce consumption. *Example: Tobacco, alcohol.*
