# **Basic economic ideas and resource allocations**

## Classification of goods and services

## Free Goods

* Definition → A good that is not scarce and therefore does not require an allocation mechanism.

  * Scarcity → Does NOT apply.

  * Quantity → Available in sufficient amounts to meet demand.

* Demand → Equal to supply when the price is zero.

* NO Cost of Consumption → This is what differentiates free goods from private/economic goods.

* Examples → Sunshine, air, seawater, etc.

## Private Goods

* Definition → A good that is relatively scarce and therefore needs to be allocated to a specific use through an allocative mechanism.

  * Scarcity → Does apply.

* Alias → Economic Good.

* 2 Essential Qualities → Rival and Excludable.

* Rival → When one person consumes the good, the quantity available for others decreases.

* Excludable → A price is charged for the good, meaning those who are unable or unwilling to pay can be prevented from consuming it.

* 2 Types of Private Goods → 1) Merit Goods and 2) Demerit Goods.

## Public Goods

* Definition → Goods that are both non-rival and non-excludable.

* Opposite of Private Goods → Non-rival and non-excludable.

* Examples → Street lighting, police and law, national defence, etc.

* Non-Rival → Consumption by one person does not reduce the amount available to others.

* Non-Excludable → It is not possible to prevent individuals from using the good.

  * Impossible to Prevent → Others from consuming the good.

* Non-Rejectable → Certain public goods cannot be refused or avoided by individuals (not all public goods).

## Free Rider Problem

* Definition → A situation where individuals who have not paid for a good cannot be excluded from consuming it.

* Free Rider → A person who has little or no incentive to pay for a public good because they can still benefit from it without paying.

* Main Outcome → It is usually impossible to charge a price for public goods.

## Non-Rejectability

* Definition → The concept that certain public goods cannot be refused or avoided by individuals.

* Examples → Police force, national defence, etc.

* Does NOT Apply to All Public Goods → For example, education, as individuals can choose not to use the education provided.

## Market Failure

* Private Goods → Generally supplied through markets because producers can earn profits by charging consumers for their use.

* Public Goods → Often underprovided by the market because producers lack a profit incentive.

  * Reason → Non-rivalry and non-excludability make it difficult to charge consumers.

* Main Outcome → Public goods generally need to be provided by the State/Government.

## Quasi-Public Goods

* Definition → Goods that have characteristics of both private and public goods, making them difficult to classify as either one.

* Characteristics → A combination of the features of private and public goods.

* Examples → Public transportation, roads, parks, libraries, etc.

* Reason → They may be accessible to many people but can become rivalrous and may be partially excludable.

## Merit Goods

* Definition → A private good that is likely to be underproduced and underconsumed in a market economy.

* Reason → Consumers may not fully understand the possible benefits of consuming the good (benefits are underestimated).

  * Imperfect Information → Consumers and producers may lack the complete information required to make rational decisions.

  * Outcome → This reduces the level of economic efficiency.

* Characteristics → Rival and excludable.

* Examples → Education, public libraries, healthcare, museums, etc.

## Merit Good Provision

* Governments → Have 2 options:

  1. Subsidise the good.

  2. Provide it free of charge to consumers.

* Example – Education → Free provision helps ensure that access to education is not restricted only to individuals who can afford to pay.

### Main Takeaways from Graph

* Reason for Underconsumption and Underproduction → Consumers do not fully recognise the benefits of merit goods.

* Treatment → The government intervenes to increase the consumption and production of merit goods.

  * Examples → Education, healthcare, etc.

## Demerit Goods

* Definition → A private good that is likely to be overproduced and overconsumed in a market economy.

* Reason → Individuals may not fully understand the harmful effects of consuming too much of the good (imperfect information).

* Characteristics → Rival and excludable.

* Examples → Cigarettes, alcohol, etc.

## Demerit Good Discouragement

* Governments → Use measures such as taxation to reduce the consumption of demerit goods.

* Taxation → Increases the price of demerit goods, which leads to a fall in demand.

  * Example – Cigarette Demand → Lower cigarette consumption can benefit the individuals consuming them.

* Price Elasticity of Demand → Determines how effective taxation will be in reducing the consumption of demerit goods.

  * Inelastic Demerit Goods → Consumers may continue consuming similar quantities despite higher prices, particularly when addiction is involved.

### Main Takeaways from Graph

* Reason for Overproduction and Overconsumption → Consumers do not fully recognise the harmful effects of demerit goods.

* Treatment → The government intervenes to reduce the consumption and production of demerit goods.

  * Measure → Taxation.

## Information Failure

* Definition → A situation in which individuals lack complete information to make well-informed decisions about their behaviour.

* Alias → Imperfect Information.

* Key Element in Private Goods → It contributes to market failure in both merit and demerit goods.

  1. Merit Goods → Underconsumption and underprovision occur because consumers are unaware of their potential benefits.

  2. Demerit Goods → Overconsumption and overprovision occur because consumers are unaware of their potential harmful effects.

## Treatment for Information Failure

* Government Intervention → The government can encourage or discourage the consumption and production of goods.

  * Merit Goods → Encourage consumption and production.

  * Demerit Goods → Discourage consumption and production.

* Government's Aim → To correct market imperfections and improve resource allocation.

* Misallocation of Resources → Occurs when a good is not produced or consumed in the quantities required for an efficient allocation of resources.
