## Price Elasticity of Supply

* Definition → Measures how responsive the quantity supplied of a product is to a change in its price.

* Formula →

PES= %change in quantity supplied/ %change in price 

* PES \> 1 – Elastic Supply → Quantity supplied changes proportionately more than price, as firms can increase output quickly at relatively low cost.

* PES \< 1 – Inelastic Supply → Quantity supplied responds relatively little to a price change because increasing production may be difficult or costly.

* PES = 0 – Perfectly Inelastic Supply → The quantity supplied remains fixed, regardless of changes in price.

* PES = ∞ – Perfectly Elastic Supply → Firms are willing to supply any quantity demanded at a given price.

### Example: Price of Wheat Increases

* Price Increase → 15%

* Quantity Supplied Increase → 20%

* Calculation →

PES=20%15%=1.33PES=\\frac\{20\\%\}\{15\\%\}=1.33PES=15%20%​=1.33

* Magnitude → 1.33, which is greater than 1.

* Conclusion → Supply is relatively price elastic.

Note: PES is generally positive, so its magnitude is used to determine the degree of elasticity.

## 2. Factors Influencing Price Elasticity of Supply

### 1) Time Scale

* Short Run → Supply tends to be price inelastic because firms have limited time to increase production.

* Long Run → Supply tends to be price elastic because firms have more time to adjust their production capacity and resources.

### 2) Spare Capacity

* Full Capacity → Supply is relatively price inelastic because firms have little or no unused resources available to increase output.

* Spare Capacity → Supply is relatively price elastic because firms can use existing unused resources to expand production.

### 3) Level of Stocks

* Storable Goods → Supply tends to be price elastic because firms can store goods and release additional stock when prices rise.

* Perishable Goods → Supply tends to be price inelastic because goods cannot be stored for long periods without deteriorating.

### 4) Flexibility of Factors of Production (FOPs)

* Flexible FOPs → Supply is relatively price elastic because resources can be transferred between different uses to increase production.

* Fixed FOPs → Supply is relatively price inelastic because resources cannot easily be moved or adjusted to meet higher demand.

### 5) Barriers to Entry

* High Entry Barriers → Supply is relatively price inelastic because new firms face difficulties entering the market.

* Low Entry Barriers → Supply is relatively price elastic because new firms can enter the industry more easily and increase market supply.

# Interaction of Demand and Supply

## 3. Market Equilibrium

* Definition → The point where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, determining the equilibrium price (P\*) and equilibrium quantity (Q\*).

* Price Stability → At equilibrium, there is no tendency for price to change because the quantity consumers wish to buy matches the quantity producers wish to sell.

* Market Disequilibrium → Occurs when quantity demanded and quantity supplied are unequal.

### Types of Market Disequilibrium

* Surplus → Occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded, resulting in excess supply.

* Shortage → Occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied, resulting in excess demand.

## 5. The Price Mechanism (Invisible Hand)

* Definition → The price mechanism is the process through which changes in prices influence the decisions of consumers and producers, helping allocate scarce resources.

* 3 Main Functions → Rationing, signalling and incentivising.

### A) Rationing Function

* Definition → Prices help distribute scarce goods among consumers by influencing their willingness and ability to purchase them.

* Increase in Price → Reduces the quantity demanded, helping allocate limited resources among consumers.

* Example → As available airline seats become scarce, ticket prices may rise, discouraging some consumers from purchasing tickets and rationing the remaining seats.

### B) Signalling Function

* Definition → Prices communicate information about changes in market conditions to consumers and producers.

* Price Changes → Indicate where resources are becoming scarce or where consumer demand is increasing.

* High Prices → Signal to firms that consumers may want more of a product, encouraging producers to consider increasing output.

### C) Incentivising Function

* Definition → Prices encourage consumers and producers to change their behaviour in response to market conditions.

* High Prices → Provide an incentive for firms to increase production because potential profits may rise.

* Low Demand → May lead to lower prices, reducing the incentive for firms to produce additional output.
