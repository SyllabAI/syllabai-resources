# The Price System & the Microeconomy

## **Demand & Supply Curves**

## Effective Demand

Definition: Effective demand refers to the quantity of a good or service that a consumer is both willing and financially able to purchase at different prices over a given period.

* Desire vs Demand: Wanting a product alone does not constitute demand; the consumer must also be able to afford it.

* Example: If someone wants to buy a Ferrari but cannot afford it, this is only a desire, not effective demand.

* Key Point: Effective demand requires both willingness and purchasing power.

## 2. Demand

Definition: Demand represents the relationship between the price of a product and the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy over a particular period.

* Law of Demand: For most goods and services, a rise in price causes quantity demanded to fall, while a fall in price causes quantity demanded to rise.

* Demand Curve: A graphical representation of the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

* Ceteris Paribus: All other factors influencing demand are assumed to remain unchanged.

* Individual Demand: The demand of a single consumer for a particular product.

Example: Tom's demand for tomatoes shows how much he is willing and able to purchase at different prices, assuming other demand factors remain constant.

\- Important: Without the supply curve, the market equilibrium price and quantity cannot be determined.

## 3. Market Demand

Definition: Market demand refers to the combined quantity of a product demanded by all consumers in a market at different prices.

* Calculation: Add the quantities demanded by every individual consumer at each price.

* Purpose: Shows the relationship between market price and the total quantity demanded.

## 4. Supply

Definition: Supply refers to the quantity of a good that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at different prices over a given period.

* Individual Supply: The relationship between the price of a product and the quantity supplied by an individual producer.

* Law of Supply: For most goods and services, price and quantity supplied have a direct relationship.

  * Price rises → Quantity supplied rises.

  * Price falls → Quantity supplied falls.

* Supply Curve: A graph showing the relationship between price and the quantity producers are willing and able to sell.

* Ceteris Paribus: Other factors affecting supply are assumed to remain constant.

* Equilibrium: Both demand and supply curves are required to determine the equilibrium price and quantity.

### Explanation of the Law of Supply

* Higher prices encourage producers to supply more because selling the product becomes potentially more profitable.

* Without the demand curve, the equilibrium price and quantity cannot be established.

## 5. Market Supply

Definition: Market supply is the total quantity of a product that all producers in a market are willing and able to supply at different prices.

* Calculation: Add the quantities supplied by all individual producers at each price.

* Purpose: Illustrates the relationship between market price and total quantity supplied.

## 7. Determinants of Demand

Definition: Conditions of demand are non-price factors that influence the quantity consumers are willing and able to purchase at each price.

* A change in any determinant of demand shifts the demand curve either to the right or to the left.

* Price is not the only factor affecting demand.

### 1) Income

* Normal Goods: Goods for which demand increases as consumer income rises and decreases as income falls.

* Inferior Goods: Goods for which demand decreases when income rises and increases when income falls.

  * Reason: When income decreases, consumers may switch to cheaper alternatives.

### 2) Price of Other Goods

Goods may be related as substitutes or complements.

A) Substitutes

* Goods that can be used in place of one another.

* Example: Coffee and tea.

* If the price of tea rises, consumers may switch to coffee, increasing the demand for coffee.

* If the price of tea falls, demand for coffee may decrease as consumers choose tea instead.

B) Complements

* Goods that are normally consumed together.

* Example: Cars and fuel.

* If the price of cars rises, demand for cars falls, which may also reduce the demand for fuel.

### 3) Tastes and Preferences

* Consumer preferences influence the demand for goods and services.

* Products that become more appealing to consumers are likely to experience higher demand.

* Advertising: Can change consumer preferences and increase or decrease demand for a product.

### 4) Speculation

* Consumers may purchase goods in anticipation of future price increases, hoping to resell them at a profit.

* Examples: Houses, shares and antiques.

* If consumers expect prices to rise, current demand is likely to increase.

### 5) Size, Age, Gender and Population

* Population Size: A larger population generally increases demand for most goods and services.

* Age and Gender: Changes in demographic composition affect demand for products aimed at particular groups.

* Examples: Products designed for young people, elderly individuals, males or females.

### 6) Distribution of Income

* More Equal Income Distribution: May increase demand for normal goods.

* Greater Income Inequality: May increase demand for luxury goods among higher-income groups and inferior goods among lower-income groups.

* Key Point: The effect depends on how income is redistributed and the types of goods being considered.

## 8. Determinants of Supply

Definition: Conditions of supply are non-price factors that affect the quantity producers are willing and able to supply at each price.

* A change in these conditions shifts the supply curve to the right or left.

* Price is not the only factor determining supply.

### 1) Costs of Production

* Increase in Costs → Supply decreases because production becomes more expensive.

* Producers are willing and able to offer less output for sale at each price.

* Examples: Higher wages, more expensive raw materials and increased costs of components used in phones.

### 2) Availability of Resources

* Greater Resource Availability → Supply increases as producers gain access to more inputs.

* Example: Discovery of additional copper deposits may enable technology industries to produce more electronic chips.

### 3) Climate and Weather

* Weather conditions affect industries that depend on the climate, particularly agriculture.

* Favourable Weather → Can increase agricultural output and supply.

* Adverse Weather → Can damage crops and reduce supply.

* Other Affected Industries: Construction and tourism.

### 4) Technology

* Improved Technology → Increases supply by making production more efficient.

* Technological progress can lower production costs, allowing firms to produce more at each price.

* Example: Advanced machinery may increase the production capacity of electronic chip manufacturers.

### 5) Government Regulation

* Government regulations may be introduced to protect workers, ensure health and safety, and promote consumer welfare.

* Examples: Minimum wage laws, equal pay requirements and workplace safety legislation.

* Higher Regulation → May reduce supply if it increases production costs or reduces the efficiency of factors of production.

### 6) Taxes and Subsidies

* Indirect Taxes → Increase production costs, causing supply to decrease.

* Subsidies → Lower production costs, encouraging firms to supply more output at each price.

## 10. Shifts in the Demand Curve

A change in any condition of demand causes the demand curve to shift, depending on whether demand increases or decreases.

* Rightward Shift → Increase in Demand: Consumers are willing and able to purchase more at every price.

* Leftward Shift → Decrease in Demand: Consumers are willing and able to purchase less at every price.

## 11. Shifts in the Supply Curve

A change in any condition of supply shifts the supply curve, depending on whether producers can supply more or less at each price.

* Rightward Shift → Increase in Supply: Producers are willing and able to offer more output for sale at every price.

* Leftward Shift → Decrease in Supply: Producers are willing and able to offer less output for sale at every price.
