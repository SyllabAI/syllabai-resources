## Frustration

### **Type of Frustrating Event**


1. **Impossibility of Performance:**
   * Definition: Performance becomes objectively impossible.
2. **Supervening Illegality:**
   * Definition: Performance becomes illegal after the contract is formed.
3. **Change of Circumstance Making Performance Pointless:**
   * Definition: Even if not impossible or illegal, a fundamental change makes performance pointless.

### **Limitations on the Doctrine of Frustration**


1. **Contractual Provision:**
   * If the contract has provisions for such events, frustration may be limited.
2. **Inconvenience or Additional Expense:**
   * Frustration is not accepted solely due to inconvenience or additional expense.
3. **Foreseen or Reasonably Foreseeable Event:**
   * If the event was foreseeable or foreseen, frustration may not apply.
4. **Self-Induced:**
   * Frustration won't apply if the event leading to frustration is self-induced.

### **The Effect of Frustration at Common Law**

* The contract is automatically discharged.
* Parties are excused from future performance.
* Both parties are generally restored to their pre-contractual positions.

### **The Effect of Frustration under the Law Reform (Frustrated Contracts) Act 1943, s1(2) and s1(3)**

* s1(2): Money paid before the frustrating event is recoverable.
* s1(3): Where a party has obtained a valuable benefit before the frustrating event, that party must pay for the benefit.

### **Summary**

* Types of Frustrating Events:
  * Impossibility of performance.
  * Supervening illegality.
  * Change of circumstance making performance pointless.
* Limitations on Frustration:
  * Contractual provision.
  * Inconvenience or additional expense.
  * Foreseen or reasonably foreseeable events.
  * Self-induced events.
* Effect at Common Law:
  * Automatic discharge of the contract.
  * Parties excused from future performance.
  * Restoration to pre-contractual positions.
* Effect under the Law Reform (Frustrated Contracts) Act 1943:
  * s1(2): Recovery of money paid before frustration.
  * s1(3): Payment for valuable benefits obtained before frustration.
