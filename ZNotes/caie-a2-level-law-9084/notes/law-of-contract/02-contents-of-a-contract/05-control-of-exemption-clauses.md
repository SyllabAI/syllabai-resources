## Control of Exemption Clauses

### **Common Law**

* Rules of Incorporation: Exemption clauses must be effectively incorporated into the contract. This can be through signature, notice, or consistent course of dealing.
* Contra Proferentem Rule: Any ambiguity in the wording of exemption clauses is interpreted against the party seeking to rely on them.

### **Statutory**

* **Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (Business to Business Contracts Only):**
  * s1(3) Definition: Covers any provision excluding or restricting liability.
  * s2 Negligence Liability: Liability for negligence cannot be excluded.
  * s3 Liability Arising in Contract: Cannot exclude liability for breach of contract unless reasonable.
  * s11 Reasonableness Test: The overall fairness and reasonableness of the clause are assessed.
* **Consumer Rights Act 2015 (Trader and Consumer Contracts s61):**
  * s2 Definition: Defines consumer contracts and traders.
  * s31 Exclusion of Liability Goods Contracts: Unfair terms excluding liability for faulty goods are not binding on consumers.
  * s57 Exclusion of Liability Service Contracts: Unfair terms excluding liability for faulty services are not binding on consumers.
  * s62 Requirement for Contract Terms to be Fair: All terms must be fair and transparent.
  * s65 Negligence Liability: Cannot exclude or restrict liability for death or personal injury resulting from negligence.
  * s68 Requirement for Transparency: Terms must be transparent and easy to understand.

### **Summary**

* Common Law:
  * Rules of incorporation ensure the effective inclusion of exemption clauses.
  * The Contra proferentem rule applies if the clause is ambiguous.
* Statutory: Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (Business to Business Contracts Only):
  * s1(3) Definition covers clauses limiting liability.
  * s2 prohibits excluding liability for negligence.
  * s3 restricts exclusion of liability for breach of contract, subject to reasonableness.
  * s11 assesses the overall reasonableness of the clause.
* Consumer Rights Act 2015 (Trader and Consumer Contracts s61):
  * s2 defines consumer contracts and traders.
  * s31 and s57 limit the exclusion of liability for faulty goods and services.
  * s62 mandates fair and transparent contract terms.
  * s65 restricts the exclusion of liability for death or personal injury from negligence.
  * s68 requires transparency in contract terms.
