## Consideration

### **Nature and Function**

* **Definition**: Something of value exchanged between parties to a contract.
* **Function**: It distinguishes a contract from a gift and is a key element for its enforceability.
* **Essential Characteristics:**
  * It must be something of value.
  * Must move from the promisee.

### **Sufficiency and Adequacy**

* General Rule: The law does not concern itself with the adequacy of consideration; it only requires sufficiency.
* Exceptions: In some cases, gross inadequacy might indicate duress, fraud, or undue influence.

### **Past Consideration**

* General Rule: Past consideration (something already done) is not valid consideration.
* Exception: If parties agree that past actions are considered part of the current bargain.

### **Performance of Existing Duties**

* General Rule: Performing an existing contractual or public duty is not a valid consideration.
* Exception: If there's a promise to do more than the existing duty, it can be a valid consideration.

### **Past Payment of Debt**

* General Rule: Part payment of a debt is not a valid consideration for the discharge of the whole debt.
* Exception: If there is a genuine dispute about the debt, part payment may be a valid consideration.

### **Promissory Estoppel**

* Definition: A legal principle preventing a party from returning on a promise if the other party has relied on it to their detriment.
* Requirements:
  * Clear and unequivocal promise.
  * Reliance on the promise.
  * Detriment suffered due to reliance.
  * Injustice if the promise is not enforced.

### **Summary**

* Nature and Function:
  * Something of value exchanged.
  * Distinguishes contracts from gifts.
* Sufficiency/Adequacy:
  * The law concerns sufficiency, not adequacy.
  * Exceptions for gross inadequacy indicating duress, fraud, or undue influence.
* Past Consideration:
  * Generally not a valid consideration.
  * Exception if parties expressly agree.
* Performance of Existing Duties:
  * Performing existing duties is not a valid consideration.
  * Exception if there's a promise to do more.
* Part Payment of Debt:
  * Part-payment is generally not a valid consideration for the whole debt.
  * Exception if there's a genuine dispute about the debt.
* Promissory Estoppel:
  * It prevents going back on a promise.
  * Requires an explicit promise, reliance, detriment, and injustice if not enforced.
