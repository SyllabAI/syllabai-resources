## Capacity (Minors Only)

### **Reason for Limitation and Possible Reform**

* Reason for Limitation: Minors are presumed to lack the legal capacity to enter into binding contracts due to their vulnerability.
* Possible Reform: There have been discussions about reforming the law to grant minors more contractual capacity or allowing certain contracts to be binding on minors.

### **Categories of Contracts**


1. **Necessaries:**
   * Definition: Contracts for goods and services necessary for a minor's reasonable requirements.
   * Enforceability: Generally, minors are bound by contracts for necessities, and these contracts are enforceable against them.
2. **Beneficial Contracts of Service (Education, Training, and Employment):**
   * Definition: Contracts providing education, training, or employment opportunities for the minor's benefit.
   * Enforceability: Generally, these contracts are enforceable against minors, recognizing their potential benefit.
3. **Voidable (Continuing Obligations):**
   * Definition: Contracts not falling into the categories above.
   * Enforceability: Minors can choose to avoid these contracts, making them voidable. However, they must disaffirm the contract during their minority or within a reasonable time after reaching the majority.

### **Remedies Against Minors**


1. In Equity:
   * Doctrine: Minors can be held accountable for restitution in equity if they have received benefits under the contract.
2. Minors’ Contracts Act 1987, s2 and s3:
   * Provisions: The Act allows minors to be bound by contracts for necessities and contracts that benefit them, but other contracts remain voidable.
3. Limits:
   * Minors’ Protection: Legal limits on enforcing contracts against minors aim to protect them from potentially exploitative or unfair agreements.
