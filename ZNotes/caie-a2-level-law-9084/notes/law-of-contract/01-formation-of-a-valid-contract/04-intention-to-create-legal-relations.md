## Intention to Create Legal Relations

### **Reason for Requirement**

* Purpose: The legal principle determining whether an agreement creates a legally binding contract.
* Importance: Distinguishes social/domestic agreements (presumed not legally binding) from commercial agreements (presumed legally binding).
* Ensures: Clarity and predictability in contract enforcement.

### **Presumption and Rebuttal in Commercial and Social/Domestic Agreement**


1. Commercial Agreements:
   * Presumption: Strong presumption in favour of legal intent.
   * Rationale: Parties engaging in business transactions are presumed to intend legal consequences.
   * Rebuttal: Possible, but requires clear evidence that parties did not intend legal enforcement.
2. Social/Domestic Agreements:
   * Presumption: Presumed not to have legal intent.
   * Rationale: Social and domestic agreements are often based on trust and informal arrangements.
   * Rebuttal: Possible, but the burden of proof is higher; clear evidence of an intention to be legally bound is required.

### **Summary**

* Reason for Requirement:
  * Distinguishes legally binding contracts from non-binding agreements.
  * Ensures clarity and predictability in contract enforcement.
* The presumption in Commercial Agreements:
  * The strong presumption in favour of legal intent.
  * Reflects the business nature of commercial transactions.
* Rebuttal in Commercial Agreements:
  * Possible but requires clear evidence of no intention to be legally bound.
* Presumption in Social/Domestic Agreements:
  * Presumed not to have legal intent.
  * Reflects the informal and trust-based nature of such agreements.
* Rebuttal in Social/Domestic Agreements:
  * Possible but requires a higher burden of proof.
  * Clear evidence of an intention to be legally bound is necessary.
