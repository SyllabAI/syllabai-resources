## Offer and Acceptance

### **Principles and Evidence**


1. **Offers:**
   * Definition: A clear, specific proposal made by one party to another.
   * Characteristics: Intention to be bound, definite terms, and communicated to the offeree.
   * Evidence: Written or verbal statements, conduct, or actions indicating an intention to enter a contract.
2. **Invitation to Treat:**
   * Definition: An invitation for others to make offers, not a binding offer itself.
   * Characteristics: Advertisements, displays of goods, and auction sales are often invitations to treat.
   * Evidence: Context, language, and surrounding circumstances determine if it's an offer or invitation to treat.
3. **Counter Offers:**
   * Definition: A response that alters the terms of the original offer.
   * Effect: Terminates the original offer and becomes a new offer.
   * Evidence: Communication of revised terms by the offeree.
4. **Requests for Information:**
   * Definition: Seeking additional details before accepting an offer.
   * Effect: Generally, it is not a counteroffer but an inquiry.
   * Evidence: Communication seeking clarification or more information.
5. **Termination:**
   * Definition: Withdrawal or lapse of an offer.
   * Methods: Revocation by the offeror, rejection by the offeree, expiration of a time limit, or conditions not met.
   * Evidence: Communication of revocation or rejection.
6. **Acceptance:**
   * Definition: Unconditional agreement to the terms of the offer.
   * Characteristics: Must be communicated unequivocal and in the manner the offer prescribes.
   * Evidence: Clear and unambiguous communication of acceptance.
7. **Application of the Rules to Standard Form Contracts, Auction Sales, and Contracts by Tender:**
   * Standard Form Contracts: Offer is often in terms of the standard form; acceptance is typically by performance.
   * Auction Sales: Bid is an offer; acceptance is the fall of the auctioneer's hammer.
   * Contracts by Tender: Invitation to submit offers; acceptance occurs when the tender is selected.

### **Summary**

* Offers:
  * Clear, specific proposals.
  * Intention to be bound, definite terms, and communicated.
* Invitation to Treat:
  * Not a binding offer but an invitation for others to make offers.
  * Context, language, and surrounding circumstances determine its nature.
* Counter Offers:
  * Alters the terms of the original offer.
  * Terminates the original offer and becomes a new offer.
* Requests for Information:
  * Seeking additional details before accepting an offer.
  * Generally, it is an inquiry, not a counteroffer.
* Termination:
  * Withdrawal or lapse of an offer.
  * Revocation, rejection, expiration, or unmet conditions.
* Acceptance:
  * Unconditional agreement communicated in the prescribed manner.
  * Unambiguous communication.
* **Application of Rules:**
  * **Standard Form Contracts:**
    * Offer often in terms of the standard form; acceptance by performance.
  * **Auction Sales**:
    * A bid is an offer; acceptance is the fall of the hammer.
  * **Contracts by Tender:**
    * Invitation to submit offers; acceptance when the tender is selected.
