## Common Law

### **Purpose and Nature of Damages**

* Purpose: To compensate the innocent party for losses from a breach of contract.
* Nature: Compensatory, not punitive.

### **The Measure or Calculation of Damages**


1. **Expectation Loss:**
   * Definition: Aims to put the innocent party in the position they would have been in if the contract had been performed.
2. **Reliance Loss:**
   * Definition: Compensates the innocent party for losses incurred in reliance on the contract.
3. **Non-Pecuniary Loss:**
   * Definition: Covers losses that are not financial, such as emotional distress.

### **Limitations on Recovery**


1. **Causation:**
   * The breach must be the cause of the loss.
2. **Remoteness:**
   * Losses must have been foreseeable at the time of contracting.
3. **Mitigation:**
   * The innocent party is required to take reasonable steps to minimize losses.

### **Evaluation of the Use of the Remedy of Damages**

* **Advantages:** Provides financial compensation; flexible; widely recognized.
* **Disadvantages**: May not fully compensate for non-pecuniary losses; does not restore the specific performance of the contract.

### **Summary**

* Purpose and Nature of Damages:
  * Purpose: Compensation for losses.
  * Nature: Compensatory, not punitive.
* The Measure or Calculation of Damages:
  * Expectation Loss: Aims to restore the innocent party to the position if the contract was performed.
  * Reliance Loss: Compensates for losses incurred in reliance on the contract.
  * Non-Pecuniary Loss: Covers non-financial losses like emotional distress.
* Limitations on Recovery:
  * Causation: The breach must be the cause of the loss.
  * Remoteness: Losses must have been foreseeable.
  * Mitigation: The innocent party must take reasonable steps to minimize losses.
* Evaluation of Damages:
