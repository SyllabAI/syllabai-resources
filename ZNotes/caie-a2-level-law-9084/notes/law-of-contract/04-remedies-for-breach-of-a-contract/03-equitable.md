## Equitable

### **Purpose of Equitable Remedies**

* Purpose: To achieve fairness and justice when damages are an inadequate remedy.
* Nature: Aimed at correcting or preventing injustice rather than compensating for loss.

### **Nature of Equitable Remedies**


1. Specific Performance:
   * Definition: Requires the breaching party to perform the contractual obligations as agreed.
2. Specific Restitution:
   * Definition: Restores the innocent party to their position before the contract.
3. Rescission:
   * Definition: Cancels the contract, treating it as if it never existed.
4. Injunction:
   * Definition: Court order preventing a party from doing a particular act.

### **Limitations on their Award in Contract Law**


1. **Discretionary:**
   * Courts have discretion in awarding equitable remedies.
2. **Availability:**
   * It is not always available; it depends on the nature of the breach.
3. **Inadequacy of Damages:**
   * Typically awarded when damages are inadequate.

### **Evaluation of the Use of Equitable Remedies**

* Advantages: Tailored to the circumstances; can prevent injustice.
* Disadvantages: Complexity; not always available; may not fully compensate.

### **Summary**

* Purpose of Equitable Remedies:
  * Achieve fairness and justice when damages are inadequate.
* Nature of Equitable Remedies:
  * Specific Performance: Enforces performance of contractual obligations.
  * Specific Restitution: Restores the innocent party to the pre-contract position.
  * Rescission: Cancels the contract.
  * Injunction: Prevents a party from certain acts.
* Limitations on Award:
  * Discretionary: Courts have discretion.
  * Availability: Not always applicable.
  * Inadequacy of Damages: Typically awarded when damages are insufficient.
* Evaluation of Equitable Remedies:
  * Advantages: Tailored to circumstances; can prevent injustice.
  * Disadvantages: Complex; not always available; may not fully compensate.
