## Assault

### **Definition and Elements**

* **Definition**: Assault, in tort law, is the intentional causing of another person to apprehend the infliction of immediate and unlawful force. It's important to note that assault in tort is distinct from its criminal counterpart, and it focuses on the threat or anticipation of physical harm rather than the actual infliction.
* **Elements Required for Liability:**
  * **Intent**: The defendant must intend to cause the claimant to apprehend immediate and unlawful force. This requires a voluntary and deliberate act.
  * **Apprehension of Immediate Force**: The claimant must reasonably apprehend the immediate and unlawful application of force. This involves the claimant believing they are about to be harmed.

### **Actionable Per Se**

* Assault is often considered actionable per se, meaning that the claimant does not need to prove actual damage or harm. The very act of causing someone to reasonably fear immediate harm is sufficient for a claim of assault.

### **Actions Constituting Assault**

* **Words and Conduct**: Assault can be committed not only through physical actions but also through words and conduct that create a reasonable apprehension of immediate harm. For example, raising a fist, brandishing a weapon, or making explicit threats can all constitute assault.
* **Silence**: In certain situations, silence or a failure to act can also amount to assault if it creates a reasonable belief in the claimant that immediate harm is imminent. This is particularly relevant when there is a duty to act, and the failure to act induces apprehension of harm.

### **Possible Reforms**

* **Technological Assault**: Considering advancements in communication technology, reforms might explore how threats conveyed through electronic means, such as social media or messaging apps, fit into the assault framework.
* **Expansion of Actions**: Reforms could consider expanding the types of actions that can constitute assault, especially in light of evolving societal norms and potential new forms of threat.

### **Summary**

* Definition and Elements:
  * Assault is the intentional causing of apprehension of immediate and unlawful force.
  * Elements include intent and the claimant's reasonable apprehension of immediate harm.
* Actionable Per Se:
  * Assault is often actionable per se, requiring no proof of actual harm.
* Actions Constituting Assault:
  * It can be committed through physical actions, words, or conduct inducing fear.
  * Silence, in certain circumstances, can also constitute assault.
* Possible Reforms:
  * Addressing technological aspects.
  * Considering an expanded scope of actions constituting assault.
