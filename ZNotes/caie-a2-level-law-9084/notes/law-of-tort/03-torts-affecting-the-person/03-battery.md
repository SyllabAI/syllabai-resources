## Battery

### **Definition and Elements**

* **Definition**: In tort law, battery refers to the intentional and direct application of unlawful force to another person. It involves physical contact with the claimant and can occur without causing injury.
* **Elements Required for Liability:**
  * **Intent**: The defendant must have the intention to apply force. This requires a voluntary and deliberate act.
  * **Unlawful Force**: The force applied must be unlawful, meaning it is unjustified or consented to.

### **Actionable Per Se**

* Like assault, battery is often considered actionable per se. This means that the claimant does not need to prove actual damage or harm; the act of the intentional application of force is sufficient for a claim in battery.

### **Force and Hostility**

* **Meaning of Force**: Force in the context of battery can range from a light touch to a more aggressive act. The key is that there must be a physical connection between the defendant and the claimant.
* **Relevance of Hostility**: Battery does not require actual hostility between the parties. Even if the defendant had no ill will or hostility towards the claimant, the intentional application of force can still give rise to liability.

### **Defences**

* **Consent**: If the claimant consents to the application of force, this can be a valid defence against a battery claim. However, the consent must be genuine, informed, and not obtained under duress.
* **Self-Defence**: A defendant may argue that they were acting in self-defence, applying force to protect themselves from an imminent threat. The force used, however, must be proportionate to the perceived threat.
* **Necessity**: In some cases, a defendant may justify the application of force on the grounds of necessity, arguing that it was necessary to prevent greater harm.

### **Possible Reforms**

* **Modern Forms of Contact**: Considering technological advancements, reforms might address physical contact in virtual spaces, such as online harassment or virtual assaults.
* **Consent and Informed Consent:** Reforms could focus on ensuring that consent is genuinely informed, especially in situations where power imbalances might impact the voluntariness of the consent.

### **Summary**

* Definition and Elements:
  * Battery is the intentional and direct application of unlawful force.
  * Elements include intent and the application of force.
* Actionable Per Se:
  * Battery is often actionable per se, requiring no proof of actual harm.
* Force and Hostility:
  * Force can range from a light touch to a more aggressive act.
  * Hostility is not required for a battery claim.
* Defences:
  * Consent, self-defence, and necessity can be valid defences against a battery claim.
