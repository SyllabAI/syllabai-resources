## Defences

### **Violenti Non-Fit Injuria**

* **Meaning of Consent**: Volenti non-fit injuria is the legal principle that states that if someone willingly and with full knowledge consents to a particular risk or harm, they cannot later claim that they have suffered an injury due to the defendant's actions. This is particularly relevant when individuals willingly expose themselves to certain risks.
* **Importance of Knowledge and Understanding:** For this defence to apply, the claimant must consent and fully understand the risks involved. This emphasizes the importance of informed consent, especially in contexts such as medical treatment, employment, and sports where individuals voluntarily expose themselves to potential harm.
* **Application in Cases of Employment, Medical Treatment, and Sport**: In employment, individuals may consent to certain risks associated with their job. In medical treatment, patients must often provide informed consent before procedures. In sports, participants may accept the inherent risks associated with the activity.

### **Contributory Negligence**

* **Nature of a Partial Defence**: Contributory negligence is a partial defence where the claimant's own negligence contributes to the harm they suffer. While it doesn't completely absolve the defendant, it can reduce the damages awarded.
* **Apportionment of Blame and Reduction of Damages**: The court assesses the degree of fault on both sides and apportions the blame accordingly. The percentage of contributory negligence then reduces damages awarded to the claimant.

### **Other Defences**

* **Inevitable Accident**: This defence asserts that the harm suffered by the claimant was due to an unavoidable accident for which the defendant cannot be held responsible.
* **Act of God**: Refers to events beyond human control, such as natural disasters, for which no one can be held liable.
* **Statutory Authority**: If the defendant's actions were legally authorised, they may be protected from liability.
* **Illegality**: If the claimant's actions leading to the injury were illegal, they may be barred from seeking compensation.
* **Necessity**: In certain situations, acting out of necessity to prevent greater harm can be a valid defence.

### **Possible Reforms**

* **Enhancing Informed Consent:** Reforms may focus on ensuring that consent is genuinely informed, particularly in power imbalances, such as employment contracts or medical treatments.
* **Contributory Negligence Thresholds**: Reforms could involve reconsidering the thresholds for contributory negligence and how they are applied, especially in cases where the claimant's actions may be seen as a response to external pressures.

### **Summary**

* Volenti Non-Fit Injuria:
  * Consent and full knowledge are crucial.
  * Applies to employment, medical treatment, and sports.
* Contributory Negligence:
  * Partial defence reducing damages.
  * Apportions blame based on fault.
* Other Defences:
  * Inevitable accident, Act of God, statutory authority, illegality, and necessity.
  * Each defence has specific conditions and implications.
* Possible Reforms:
  * Enhancing informed consent.
  * Reviewing contributory negligence thresholds.
