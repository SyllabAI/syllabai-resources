## Duty of Care

### **The Neighbour Principle**

* **Origin and Significance**: The concept of duty of care, as articulated in the Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932) case, is often attributed to the famous "neighbour principle" formulated by Lord Atkin. This principle has since become foundational in establishing the duty of care in negligence cases.
* **Neighbour Definition**: Lord Atkin's expansive definition of a "neighbour" encompasses not just those in immediate physical proximity but anyone whose well-being could reasonably be foreseen to be affected by one's actions.
* **Universal Applicability**: The neighbour principle represents a broad and inclusive approach to duty of care, emphasizing the interconnectedness of individuals within society.

### **The Modern Three-Part Test (Caparo Test)**

* **Introduction and Evolution**: The Caparo Industries plc v Dickman (1990) case marked a pivotal moment in the evolution of the duty of care concept. The judgment introduced a more structured three-part test to determine the existence of a duty of care, addressing concerns about the potential expansiveness of the neighbour principle.
* **Foreseeability of Harm**: The first element requires assessing whether harm was reasonably foreseeable. This involves considering whether a reasonable person in the defendant's position would have foreseen the potential for harm to the claimant.
* **Proximity of Relationship**: The second element examines the proximity of the relationship between the parties. Proximity encompasses physical closeness and other factors indicating a close and direct connection.
* **Fair, Reasonable**: The third element introduces a normative aspect, evaluating whether it is appropriate, just, and reasonable to impose a duty of care in the circumstances. This element injects a sense of balance and equity into determining duty of care.

### **Importance of Policy Considerations**


1. **Balancing Individual and Societal Interests**: Determining duty of care is not merely a mechanical application of legal principles; it involves a delicate balancing act. Courts must weigh the interests of individuals seeking redress against broader societal considerations.
2. **Factors in Policy Considerations:** Courts consider various factors, including the potential for a floodgate of litigation, the social and economic implications of imposing a duty, and the broader public interest.
3. L**imitations Based on Policy**: While recognizing the importance of protecting individuals from harm, the law limits the duty of care based on overarching policy considerations.

### **Summary**

* The Neighbour Principle:
  * It originated in Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932) with Lord Atkin's neighbour principle.
  * Defines "neighbour" broadly to include anyone foreseeably affected by one's actions.
  * Emphasizes the interconnectedness of individuals within society.
* The Modern Three-Part Test (Caparo Test):
  * Caparo Industries plc v Dickman (1990) introduced a structured three-part test.
  * Elements: Foreseeability, Proximity, Fairness.
  * Represents a more nuanced and refined approach to establishing duty of care.
* Importance of Policy Considerations:
  * Balancing individual interests and societal concerns.
  * Factors considered: Potential floodgate of litigation, social and economic implications, and public interest.
  * Imposes limitations on the duty of care based on overarching policy considerations.
