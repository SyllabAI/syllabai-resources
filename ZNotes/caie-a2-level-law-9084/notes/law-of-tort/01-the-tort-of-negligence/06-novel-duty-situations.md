## Novel Duty Situations

### **Pure Economic Loss and Liability for Negligent Misstatement**

* **Pure Economic Loss**: This involves financial harm without any accompanying physical damage. Generally, the law has been cautious in recognizing claims for pure economic loss to prevent an open floodgate of litigation.
* **Liability for Negligent Misstatement**: An exception to the general rule on economic loss is the recognition of liability for negligent misstatement. This occurs when there is a special relationship between the parties, and the defendant provides information that is relied upon by the claimant to their detriment.

### **Liability of Nervous Shock**

* **Scope of the Duty**: Liability for nervous shock, or psychiatric injury, arising from negligence is recognized in certain circumstances. Typically, a duty arises when the defendant should have foreseen that their conduct could cause psychiatric harm to the claimant.
* **Restrictions on the Scope of Duty**: Courts impose restrictions, such as the requirement for a close relationship between the parties and proximity regarding witnessing or being directly involved in a traumatic event.
* **Policy Considerations**: Courts balance the need for compensation with the potential for opening the floodgate of claims, emphasizing the importance of foreseeability, proximity, and reasonable limitations.

### **Possible Reforms**

* **Pure Economic Loss**: Reform in this area may involve carefully delineating circumstances where claims for pure economic loss are permissible, possibly considering factors like reliance and assumption of responsibility.
* **Liability for Nervous Shock**: Reforms might focus on clarifying the criteria for establishing liability, ensuring a balance between compensating genuine harm and preventing excessive litigation.

### **Summary**

* Pure Economic Loss and Liability for Negligent Misstatement:
  * Pure Economic Loss: Generally not recoverable to avoid a floodgate of litigation.
  * Negligent Misstatement: Recognized in special relationships where information is provided and relied upon to the claimant's detriment.
* Liability for Nervous Shock:
  * Scope of the Duty: Recognized when psychiatric harm is foreseeable in certain circumstances.
  * Restrictions: Close relationship and proximity requirements.
  * Policy Considerations: Balancing compensation needs with the potential for excessive claims.
* Possible Reforms:
  * Pure Economic Loss: Consideration of circumstances allowing recovery.
  * Liability for Nervous Shock: Clarification of criteria for liability, balancing compensation and prevention of excessive claims.
